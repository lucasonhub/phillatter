# Sovereign European AI Built on Permissively Licensed Open Models

**Autores:** Dirk de Pol

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract This paper examines the conditions under which a European organization can adopt a permissively licensed language model, develop it further within a confidential environment, retain control over its model weights and training materials, and reduce dependence on NVIDIA hardware and other concentrated infrastructure suppliers\. The analysis combines a targeted review of model releases and license terms, an assessment of European Union and national legal requirements, a threat\-model\-based security architecture, and a staged procurement and development roadmap\. The study forms part of the broader intellectual and research context of Omegacene, an interdisciplinary project examining the implications of artificial intelligence, technological transformation, and the changing relationship between human and machine agency\. Within this context, AI sovereignty is treated not merely as a question of model ownership or infrastructure procurement, but as a problem of institutional control, verifiable accountability, and the capacity to make consequential technological systems independently assessable and governable\. The central finding is that model licensing, regulatory compliance, operational security, and supply\-chain sovereignty are separate but interdependent dimensions\. Apache License 2\.0 generally permits confidential modification without an obligation to publish derivative artifacts\. This permission does not establish the lawfulness of training data, guarantee freedom from third\-party rights, or exempt a downstream organization from applicable AI, data protection, cybersecurity, or procurement law\. The European Commission’s compute\-based guidance on modifications of general\-purpose AI models provides a useful planning indicator, but it does not constitute a statutory exemption or a security threshold\. Technical transparency can improve auditability but cannot prove the absence of malicious behavior\. Confidential execution, access controls, artifact signing, and model\-extraction defenses reduce particular risks without providing absolute protection\. Hardware independence is likewise multidimensional: replacing NVIDIA with another supplier does not eliminate dependence on non\-European fabrication, memory, interconnects, firmware, or design tools\. Advanced fabrication already exists within the EU, but the evidence reviewed does not establish a complete, independently controllable European accelerator supply chain\. The paper proposes three complementary model\-development tracks, a four\-zone security architecture, measurable procurement requirements, corrected compute estimates, and a risk register\. Its principal contribution is a framework for making sovereignty claims testable by translating broad aspirations into explicit technical, legal, operational, and supply\-chain criteria\. In the wider Omegacene\.com context, this approach offers a practical basis for evaluating whether control over AI systems is substantive, demonstrable, and resilient, rather than merely asserted through licensing choices or national affiliation\. Keywords: digital sovereignty; general\-purpose AI; Apache License 2\.0; AI Act; confidential model development; model weights; trade secrets; confidential computing; export controls; semiconductor supply chains; EuroHPC; public procurement\.
