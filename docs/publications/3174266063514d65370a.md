# The final rescue step in failed airway management: handedness is included in front\-of\-neck access guidance but has not been measured

**Autores:** Daniele Salvatore Paternò; Luigi La Via; Francesco Piccitto; Basilio Davide Calà Lesina; Rossella Moltisanti; Antonio Putaggio; Emilia Concetta Lo Giudice; Sorbello Massimiliano

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Non\-invasive respiratory support fails in a predictable minority of critically ill patients, and when it does, the escalation pathway may terminate in the cannot\-intubate\-cannot\-oxygenate scenario and emergency front\-of\-neck access \(eFONA\)\. The 2025 Difficult Airway Society guidelines describe this final step in unprecedented detail, and in doing so, make handedness an explicit variable\. The operator&\#x27;s side of approach is determined by hand preference, with the non\-dominant hand performing laryngeal stabilisation while the dominant hand cuts, and a deliberate hand swap is required before bougie insertion\. These are reasonable prescriptions, but the guidelines themselves state that little evidence supports them or many other steps of the technique: no study has compared eFONA performance between the dominant and non\-dominant configuration\. The assumption embedded in the guidance, i\.e\., that the operator can choose the optimal side, is least likely to hold precisely where eFONA is most often needed: at the bedside in intensive care and emergency departments, where access is constrained by bed position, ventilator circuits and lines\. We argue that handedness in eFONA is a measurable and currently unmeasured determinant of performance and that simulation is the only setting in which it can be studied\. Thus, we outline an ongoing within\-participant randomised study\.
