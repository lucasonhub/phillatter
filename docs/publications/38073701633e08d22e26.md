# The Generalization Gap in Machine\-Learning\-Based State\-of\-Charge Estimation for Lithium\-Ion Batteries: Why Accuracy on a Benchmark Is Not Accuracy in the Field

**Autores:** Habeeb Kareem olawale; Olamilakan Nafiu Olamilakan; Yusuf Abdullazeez Babatunde; Quadri Kokoroowo

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

State\-of\-charge \(SOC\) estimation is the core function of every lithium\-ion battery management system \(BMS\), and over the past decade deep learning models — feed\-forward networks, long short\-term memory \(LSTM\) networks, and their variants — have repeatedly reported estimation errors low enough to rival, and sometimes beat, the physics\-based Kalman filter methods that preceded them\. This article argues that this comparison is frequently misleading, because most reported deep learning accuracy figures are measured on drive cycles, temperatures, and even battery cells drawn from the same distribution as the training data, while a deployed BMS must estimate SOC accurately for cells, temperatures, and usage patterns it has never seen\. Drawing on the foundational Kalman\-filter work, the deep learning SOC literature, and transfer\-learning studies that explicitly test cross\-condition performance, this article states the generalization problem precisely, offers an opinion on why it persists despite being well documented, and proposes reporting and design practices intended to close it\.
