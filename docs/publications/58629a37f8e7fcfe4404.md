# Electroencephalography Changes in Emotional Recognition: A Signal Processing Approach Using Wavelet Features

**Autores:** Beulah Snowin; Philo Hazeena; Lakshmi S\. Narasimhan

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: motion recognition using electroencephalography \(EEG\) has gained significant attention in the fields of affective computing and human\-computer interaction due to its ability to capture real\-time neural responses\. EEG signal variations associated with emotional states can be analysed using wavelet\-based signal processing within the arousal\-valence framework\. Purpose: This study aims to analyse EEG signal variations associated with emotional states using a wavelet\-based signal processing approach within the arousal\-valence framework\. Methods: A prospective observational study was conducted on 48 healthy participants\. Emotional states were induced using image, video and music stimuli categorised under the arousal\-valence model\. EEG signals were recorded using the standard 10\-20 electrode placement system\. The acquired signals were pre\-processed and analysed using discrete wavelet transform \(DWT\) to extract time\-frequency features, including energy and entropy\. Statistical analysis was performed to evaluate hemispheric differences and gender\-based variations\. Results: Differences in EEG activity were observed between the left and right hemispheres across multiple emotional states \(p &lt; \.05\)\. Video and music stimuli produced stronger and more distinguishable EEG responses compared to image\-based stimuli\. Negative emotions demonstrated higher discriminative patterns than positive emotions\. Gender\-based variations were also observed in EEG responses\. Conclusion: Wavelet\-based EEG feature analysis provides meaningful and discriminative information for emotion recognition and may provide a robust feature set for future machine learning\-based emotion recognition systems\. Further studies incorporating classification algorithms and larger data sets are required to validate their predictive performance\.
