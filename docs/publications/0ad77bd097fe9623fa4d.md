# Olfactory Epithelium as a Model for Human Neural Regeneration

**Autores:** Ziyad Matrouk Almeshrigi

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The olfactory epithelium is a privileged region in terms of neurogenesis and repair mechanisms that can be studied through its regeneration capability at any age\. The present article provides an insight into the current understanding of the regenerative potential of the olfactory epithelium from multiple angles, including cellular composition and diversity, modes of regeneration, molecular regulators, comparative and experimental approaches, and therapeutic implications\. The review focuses on the value and limitations of the olfactory epithelium as a model for studying human neural regeneration and discusses the existing evidence from studies of human tissue, animal models, single\-cell technologies, and stem\-cell biology\. It aims to provide a comprehensive view of the regenerative processes by describing the function of particular cells and the modes of their interactions in the context of the entire system\. The findings on the functional diversity of olfactory sensory neurons and supporting cells and their connections with stem cells, growth factors, and biomaterials are presented\. The article also highlights the differences between the regenerative response in various animal models and the response of the human olfactory epithelium to injury\. Finally, the review concludes with a discussion of the therapeutic implications of the study of olfactory epithelium regeneration\. The study provides a valuable insight into the mechanisms and points to the importance of additional preclinical and clinical research in multiple areas related to neural regeneration\. Moreover, it highlights the complexity of extrapolating the results from animal experiments to humans and the uniqueness of the human olfactory epithelium, which may hinder the translation of the findings and require additional considerations\.
