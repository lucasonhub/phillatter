# ORBITAL SELECTION FOR SPACE\-BASED DATA CENTERS: A COMPARATIVE MULTI\-PHYSICAL AND TECHNICAL PERFORMANCE TRADE\-OFF AMONG LEO, DAWN\-DUSK SSO, AND GEO

**Autores:** Dafnhê Thor Castro de Martins Barros; Felipe Augusto Trancoso Fernandes

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The scaling of terrestrial data centers dedicated to massive artificial intelligence workloads faces severe electrical power grid saturation and evaporative cooling water constraints\. Orbital Data Centers \(ODCs\) offer a compelling sustainable paradigm by harvesting continuous extraterrestrial Air Mass Zero solar irradiance \(1,361 W/m²\) in the space vacuum\. However, orbital trajectory selection decisively dictates boundary conditions for thermal stress, radiation damage, and data throughput\. This paper establishes a rigorous analytical and multi\-physical comparative evaluation among three candidate trajectories: Conventional LEO \(500 km, 53°\), Dawn\-Dusk SSO \(700 km, 98\.2°\), and Equatorial GEO \(35,786 km, 0°\)\. The investigation focuses strictly on four technical axes: launch mass decomposition and dry specific weight \(kg/kW\); 10\-year subsystem lifetime reliability under Total Ionizing Dose \(TID\), battery fading, and Coffin\-Manson thermal fatigue; sustained AI compute throughput \(orbital PUE and thermal throttling\); and communications performance \(RTT latency and RF/FSO daily throughput\)\. Coupled physical modeling and annual 365\-day orbital propagations demonstrate that Dawn\-Dusk SSO constitutes the optimal design alternative: it slashes specific launch mass to 48\.24 kg/kW \(a 28\.5% saving relative to LEO and 35\.4% relative to GEO, with compute IT hardware explicitly modeled at 10\.0 kg/kW\), confines eclipses to brief seasonal solstice windows \(281 days/year 100% eclipse\-free and max 18\.8 min eclipse\), retains 88\.5% battery capacity over 10 years, preserves BGA solder joint reliability at 96\.8%, achieves an orbital PUE of 1\.15 with 98\.8% sustained throughput, and delivers an agile 9\.5 ms geometric RTT \(21\.5 ms end\-to\-end\) with 150\.2 TB/day downlink volume per 100 Gbps optical node over polar ground stations\. Saaty&\#x27;s canonical AHP evaluation \(CR = 0\.000 &lt; 0\.10\) ratifies SSO as the superior regime with a composite score of 0\.5158\.
