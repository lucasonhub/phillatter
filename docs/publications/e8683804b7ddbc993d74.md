# Proximal Remote Sensing of Oil Palm Basal Stem Rot Using Deep Learning Vision Algorithms and Ground\-based Images

**Autores:** Wan Siu Hong; Jonathan Yong Chung Ee; Jin Yuan Chan; Mohamad Izzuddin Anuar; Mahmud Iwan Solihin

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Basal Stem Rot \(BSR\), caused by Ganoderma boninense, threatens oil palm productivity in Southeast Asia\.This study develops and benchmarks a low\-cost remote sensing framework for BSR severity diagnosis from smartphone\-captured RGB trunk images, evaluating two pathways for future field deployment\.The first pairs YOLO detection with a Random Forest classifier: YOLO26n gave the best accuracy\-to\-cost trade\-off among three nanovariants for localizing BSR symptoms \(mushroom, rot, white\-button\), and the end\-to\-end YOLO26n\+RF pipeline achieved 0\.83 accuracy and macro\-F1 of 0\.79 across Healthy, Mild, Moderate, and Severe classes\.Rot was consistently the hardest symptom to localize, owing to its diffuse, boundary\-ambiguous appearance\.The second pathway trained four image classifiers \(EfficientNet\-B0, MobileNetV2, Swin\-Tiny, and ConvNeXt\-V2\-Tiny\) directly on severity labels, with ConvNeXt\-V2\-Tiny performing best \(0\.83 accuracy, 0\.83 macro\-F1\)\.Both pathways performed well at severity extremes but struggled with Moderate, reflecting visual overlap between stages\.SHAP analysis identified rot\-area ratio and fruiting\-body count as top predictors\.These offline results show accurate BSR diagnosis is potentially achievable from smartphone imagery, laying groundwork for future on\-device deployment\.
