# Self\-transcendent emotions and global identity in the context of a global threat in Chile: concurrent associations, temporal stability, and longitudinal invariance

**Autores:** Lander Méndez; Anna Wlodarczyk; Javier Torres‐Vallejos; María José Mera\-Lemp; Alberto Amutio

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The present study analyzed the psychological mechanism by which perceived threat in the face of a global phenomenon influences social identification with superordinate categories, such as global identity, considering the role of self\-transcendent emotional experience, measured by self\-transcendent emotions\. Using a two\-wave half\-longitudinal design \(T1\-T2\) conducted in Chile \( N = 224\), structural equation modeling \(SEM\) and path analysis with bootstrapping were applied\. The results showed that perceived threat was positively associated with self\-transcendent emotions, and that self\-transcendent emotions, in turn, was associated with higher global identity levels at both measurement points\. The concurrent indirect effects were statistically significant at T1 and T2 \(β = 0\.21, β = 0\.20, respectively\); however, the cross\-lagged model did not provide evidence of significant prospective effects between perceived threat, self\-transcendent emotions, and global identity, once the temporal stability of the constructs was controlled for \( p &gt;0\.05\)\. The longitudinal scalar invariance of the global identity construct \(ϕ = 0\.67\) allowed for the comparison of latent means between both measurements, highlighting a slight but statistically significant decrease in global identification over time \( d = −0\.15, p = 0\.014\)\. The findings suggest that, although global identity appears to weaken modestly over time as the threatening situation persists, the self\-transcendent emotional experience constitutes a resilient psychological mechanism for maintaining identification and a sense of belonging to a human community in the face of global threats\.
