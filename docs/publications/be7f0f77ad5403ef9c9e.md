# Risk\-Set Misalignment Inflates Apparent Performance in OBE\-Oriented Landmark Temporal Prediction

**Autores:** Ya Zhao; Yang XU; Juan Huang; Yang Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Temporal event prediction models are widely deployed in outcome\-based education \(OBE\) and other domains where timely intervention depends on reliable prospective estimates\. However, the trustworthiness of reported performance hinges on whether the evaluation cohort is aligned with the prospective decision context\-specifically, whether subjects whose outcomes have already been realized are excluded at the prediction landmark\. This paper presents a systematic evaluation framework that formalizes landmark temporal prediction as a risk\-set alignment task, in which eligibility, feature availability and future outcomes are rigorously anchored to a common time point\. Using 32,593 enrolment records and over ten million activity logs from the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset \(OULAD\), we evaluate logistic regression and histogram gradient boosting at five weekly landmarks under both a prospective landmark\-aligned risk set and a retrospective diagnostic cohort\. The results reveal a striking discrepancy: aligned ROC\-AUC ranges from 0\.634 to 0\.679, whereas the retrospective cohort yields 0\.872 at week 12\-a difference driven by simultaneous changes in population, prevalence and outcome composition\. Event\-time stratification confirms that already\-resolved events are substantially easier to classify than future events \(ROC\-AUC 0\.905–0\.966 vs\. 0\.623–0\.683\)\. These findings demonstrate that risk\-set misalignment inflates apparent discrimination and compromises the trustworthiness of temporal prediction models in OBE and analogous prospective decision settings\. We provide a reproducible reporting protocol to support comparable evaluation across studies\.
