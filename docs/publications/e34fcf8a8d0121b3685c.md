abstract: 'Once a regularity has formed within a physical system, what makes it genuinely
  active at a given moment? How can its degree of formation be compared? How does
  it begin, continue, increase, decrease, cease, or migrate across time? Can a specified
  dynamical condition be reached from a given state? When do two already established
  regularities genuinely interact? And how can an organization composed of multiple
  regularities persist, fail, undergo substitution, and recover? The Law\-Dynamics
  Group\-Form develops a formal theory of how established regularities actually operate\.
  Its starting point is not an abstract rule suspended outside the physical system,
  but a regularity already borne by concrete structure\. The problem is then to determine
  how that regularity enters current operation, acts, changes across time, contributes
  to the dynamics of other regularities, and participates in persistent multi\-regularity
  organization\. The thirteen form\-texts constitute five connected but non\-substitutable
  theoretical domains\. First, the framework separates the standing of a regularity
  from its current action\. A Law may already be established without acting at every
  moment\. Necessary and sufficient directions for Law\-action are developed separately,
  together with a multidimensional Degree regime for comparing the extent to which
  an established Law has been sedimented, replayed, made available, and dynamically
  established\. Second, cross\-temporal change is made into a formal dynamical object\.
  LawDyn is not defined as a mere difference between measurements at two times, but
  as an actual change of the same Law across a lawful trajectory segment\. This permits
  onset, continuation, cessation, increase, decrease, and migration to be distinguished,
  followed by a mathematical system, a reachability regime for dynamical targets,
  and a Sedimentation\-Field operating substrate in which these structures can actually
  run\. Third, the framework examines continuity across occurrences and historical
  source\. Identity of physical roots does not automatically determine Law\-side pairing,
  and Law\-side pairing does not by itself settle the general criterion of Law identity\.
  The theory separately asks which actual segment of history bore the first establishment
  of a Law and how far source can be determined when more than one historical explanation
  remains admissible\. Fourth, a theory of direct event\-level interaction between
  Laws is established\. Co\-presence, shared medium, correlated change, and generic
  physical influence do not by themselves constitute inter\-Law interaction\. Direct
  interaction requires that an already established duty of the source Law actually
  enter the target Law’s dynamical event and make an event\-level contribution conforming
  to that duty\. Necessary structure, mathematical representation, and a sufficient
  direction are developed separately\. Fifth, the analysis is extended from individual
  Laws and pairwise interaction to multi\-Law organization\. Multiple Laws may bear
  different node roles while repeated event\-level interactions bear interaction roles\.
  Such an organization may then face member loss, replacement of bearers, changes
  in degree, interruption of interaction, and recovery\. The framework therefore distinguishes
  fixed from substitutable realization, single trajectories from trajectory families,
  persistence from recovery, and ordinary stability from stronger guarantees over
  lawful successor trajectories\. The theory applies to physical systems in which
  regularities are formed through experience and borne by physical structure\. In
  neuroscience, this includes experience\-dependent plasticity, neural dynamics, learned
  stable pathways, current activation, functional migration, circuit interaction,
  and the stability and recovery of multi\-circuit organization\. In cognitive science,
  it bears on how experience, habit, memory, perception, and behavioral organization
  use established patterns to structure later activity\. In artificial intelligence,
  it provides a formal basis for distinguishing trained structure, runtime action,
  degree, cross\-temporal dynamics, migration, interaction, and multi\-structure stability
  rather than identifying the mere presence of parameters with current operation\.
  In physics and complex\-systems research, it advances regularity from static description
  to a dynamical object that can be physically borne, change along trajectories, contribute
  to events, interact with other regularities, and participate in stable organization\.
  The central question is therefore not only what a regularity is, but a more explicitly
  dynamical one: **how does an established regularity actually enter the running of
  a world?**'

How does it begin, continue, increase, decrease, cease, or migrate across time? Can a specified dynamical condition be reached from a given state? When do two already established regularities genuinely interact? And how can an organization composed of multiple regularities persist, fail, undergo substitution, and recover? The Law\\\-Dynamics Group\\\-Form develops a formal theory of how established regularities actually operate\\\. Its starting point is not an abstract rule suspended outside the physical system, but a regularity already borne by concrete structure\\\. The problem is then to determine how that regularity enters current operation, acts, changes across time, contributes to the dynamics of other regularities, and participates in persistent multi\\\-regularity organization\\\. The thirteen form\\\-texts constitute five connected but non\\\-substitutable theoretical domains\\\. First, the framework separates the standing of a regularity from its current action\\\. A Law may already be established without acting at every moment\\\. Necessary and sufficient directions for Law\\\-action are developed separately, together with a multidimensional Degree regime for comparing the extent to which an established Law has been sedimented, replayed, made available, and dynamically established\\\. Second, cross\\\-temporal change is made into a formal dynamical object\\\. LawDyn is not defined as a mere difference between measurements at two times, but as an actual change of the same Law across a lawful trajectory segment\\\. This permits onset, continuation, cessation, increase, decrease, and migration to be distinguished, followed by a mathematical system, a reachability regime for dynamical targets, and a Sedimentation\\\-Field operating substrate in which these structures can actually run\\\. Third, the framework examines continuity across occurrences and historical source\\\. Identity of physical roots does not automatically determine Law\\\-side pairing, and Law\\\-side pairing does not by itself settle the general criterion of Law identity\\\. The theory separately asks which actual segment of history bore the first establishment of a Law and how far source can be determined when more than one historical explanation remains admissible\\\. Fourth, a theory of direct event\\\-level interaction between Laws is established\\\. Co\\\-presence, shared medium, correlated change, and generic physical influence do not by themselves constitute inter\\\-Law interaction\\\. The central question is therefore not only what a regularity is, but a more explicitly dynamical one: \*\*how does an established regularity actually enter the running of a world?\*\*
