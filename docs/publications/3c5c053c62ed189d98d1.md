# A Correlational Study of Gender Differences in Aggressiveness and Stress Coping Strategies among Kyrgyz Youth

**Autores:** Hena Fatma; Tinatin Zhakshylykova; Ahmed A\. Moustafa; Kotra Krishna Mohan

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examined the relationship between aggressiveness and stress coping strategies among Kyrgyz youth, with particular attention to gender differences\. The sample included 102 participants aged 18 to 26 years, recruited through randomly distributed online surveys\. Aggression was assessed using the Russian adaptation of the Buss\-Perry Aggression Questionnaire \(BPAQ\-24\), measuring four dimensions: physical aggression, verbal aggression, anger, and hostility\. Coping strategies were evaluated using the Russian adaptation of the Brief COPE inventory, assessing maladaptive and emotion\-focused coping styles\. The Correlation analyses indicated that physical aggression was positively associated with verbal aggression \(r = \.42, p &lt; \.01\), anger \(r = \.51, p &lt; \.01\), and hostility \(r = \.47, p &lt; \.01\)\. Verbal aggression was correlated with anger and hostility\. Anger showed strong associations with hostility\. Higher levels of aggression were positively related to maladaptive coping strategies \(r = \.22, p &lt; \.05\), while correlations with emotion\-focused coping were weaker and non\-significant \(r = \.12–\.15\)\. These findings highlight that aggression and coping strategies are moderately associated among Kyrgyz youth, with maladaptive coping particularly associated with higher aggression\. Gender differences were observed, with stronger associations among male participants\. The results suggested that further research on additional psychosocial factors within the Kyrgyz socio\-cultural environment\.
