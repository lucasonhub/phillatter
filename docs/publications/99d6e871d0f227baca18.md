# LTM: Latent Transition Based In\-Context Memory for Vision\-Language\-Action Models

**Autores:** Qixin Sun; WenLong Gao; Xiansheng Chen; Zhongyuan Wang; Si Liu; Pengwei Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Robotic manipulation often requires remembering interactions that are no longer visible\. We introduce Latent Transition Memory \(LTM\), which stores history as compact latent transitions between consecutive observations\. Instantiated on $π\_\{0\.5\}$ as LTM\-$π$, our method uses a frozen latent action encoder, a lightweight adapter, and causal KV caching for incremental inference without memory\-specific semantic annotations or external reasoning\-model calls\. With DreamDojo transitions, LTM\-$π$ achieves 58\.54% success on RoboMME and 80\.5% on LIBERO\-Mem, performing best among baselines without external reasoning models or memory\-specific annotations\. Ablations support the contribution of transition content beyond temporal cues and the efficiency of delayed memory admission\. Scaling RoboMME demonstrations tenfold improves success from 57\.67% to 87\.50%, while a Cup Swap diagnostic highlights the role of process supervision in temporal composition\. Under a 200\-token memory budget, success remains 84\.00%, versus 87\.50% without compression\. Finally, real\-robot counting success improves from 11\.25% to 82\.50%\. Together, these results demonstrate the effectiveness, scalability, and efficiency of latent transition memory, highlighting its capacity for complex temporal information, bounded\-context operation, and real\-world robotic control\.
