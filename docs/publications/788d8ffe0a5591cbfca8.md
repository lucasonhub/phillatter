# Development and Comparative Analysis of AI\-Based Classification Models for Mitigating Email\-Related Cyber Risks

**Autores:** Mehmet Tepeli

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Corporate email systems are a fundamental component of businesses&\#x27; communication and business processes, but they also bring significant cybersecurity risks such as spam and phishing attacks\. Especially as digitalization increases, the accurate and rapid detection of email\-based threats faced by businesses has become a critical requirement\. This research presents a comparative performance analysis of machine learning and transformer\-based deep learning models for the automatic classification of spam, normal, and phishing messages\. A large\-scale, three\-class dataset consisting of 479,466 English email records was used in the study\. Traditional machine learning algorithms such as Support Vector Machines \(SVM\-LinearSVC\), Logistic Regression \(LR\), Naive Bayes \(NB\), and Decision Tree \(DT\) models, as well as transformer\-based BERT, RoBERTa, and ELECTRA models, were evaluated\. The performance of the models was analyzed using accuracy, precision, recall, F1\-Score, ROC\-AUC, and confusion matrix metrics\. In addition, statistical comparisons, phishing\-specific security errors, cost\-sensitive scenarios, and computational efficiency were evaluated to provide a more comprehensive assessment of model performance\. Transformer\-based models generally achieved stronger overall and class\-specific classification performance, while conventional machine learning models offered substantially lower computational requirements\. BERT achieved the highest overall performance, with 98\.84% accuracy and a Macro F1\-Score of 0\.9827, while RoBERTa produced statistically comparable results\. Security\-oriented and computational analyses further showed that the model with the highest aggregate performance was not necessarily optimal for all deployment scenarios, highlighting the importance of considering misclassification consequences, computational requirements, email traffic volume, and available infrastructure in model selection\. It is considered that the research contributes to the literature on email security and AI\-based cyber risk management through models developed using a large\-scale, three\-class dataset, diverse AI architectures, and security\-oriented evaluation criteria\.
