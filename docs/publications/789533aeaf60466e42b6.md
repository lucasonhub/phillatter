# “Figure out who I am as a person and then go from there”: youth insights on humanizing education

**Autores:** Dena N\. Simmons; Shauna L\. Tominey; Miriam Miller; Meiko Lin

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Over the past decade, there has been a surge in mental health challenges among adolescents\. While social and emotional learning \(SEL\) programs are positioned to bolster young people&\#x27;s social, emotional, and academic success and mental health, too few SEL programs center adolescent development in the present context, largely ignoring the realities and needs of Black, Indigenous, and People of Color \(BIPOC\), LGBTQ2SIA\+, and students with disabilities\. To fill this gap and need for transformative SEL, we hosted a series of focus groups with fifteen BIPOC middle schoolers to ask what they need to belong and thrive at school, given our belief that the youth most marginalized carry the genius to transform schools for every student\. In this paper, we share their insights on how educators can create learning environments that ensure their safety, honor their humanity, cultivate joy, and foster their social, emotional, and academic well\-being through transformative SEL and liberatory teaching practices\. Youth shared easy\-to\-implement examples of how educators can nurture relationships with students, create safe and caring classroom environments, humanize education, and advance social justice and civic engagement\. Based on what we learned from youth, we urge SEL program developers and educators to start by discovering who young people are and what they need to flourish socially, emotionally, and academically, then to use what students share to inform instructional practice, create and select academic content, guide interactions, and build communities where students feel a sense of trust, belonging, and care\.
