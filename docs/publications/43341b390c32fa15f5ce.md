# Artificial intelligence in credit markets: Algorithmic lending, financial inclusion, and regulatory arbitrage

**Autores:** Podong Song; Shutong Wu; Janson Partab

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The present study investigates the dual role of artificial intelligence \(AI\) in simultaneously advancing financial inclusion and facilitating regulatory arbitrage\. We analyze a cross\-country panel dataset of 42 economies from 2012 to 2022 using system Generalized Method of Moments \(GMM\) and two\-stage least squares \(2SLS\) estimation to resolve endogeneity\. Our findings reveal that a one standard deviation increase in our novel AI Lending Intensity Index \(ALII\) significantly expands credit access, raising the financial inclusion index by an average of 8\.4 to 11\.2 percentage points\. However, this inclusion dividend is highly asymmetric\. Heterogeneity analyses demonstrate that inclusion gains are heavily concentrated among agile FinTech\-affiliated lenders \( β = 0\.138\) and emerging low\-income economies \( β = 0\.162\), whereas traditional banks use AI primarily for defensive cost reduction\. Crucially, the inclusion effect is strongest in jurisdictions with weaker supervisory capacity, where micro\-level tracking confirms that algorithmic lenders engage in regulatory arbitrage\. Tighter domestic rules prompt platforms to unbundle operations, expanding off\-perimeter shell subsidiaries from 2 to 11 and shifting over half of their asset routing cross\-border\. Furthermore, using asymmetric Generalized Extreme Value \(GEV\) link modeling, we prove that unregulated AI lending introduces severe macro\-prudential risks, accelerating credit cycle volatility along a heavy\-tailed Frechet distribution profile \( ξ = 0\.241\)\. This systemic amplification is successfully cushioned only in economies with comprehensive macro\-prudential frameworks\. These findings carry direct implications for building technology\-neutral regulations, setting algorithmic accountability standards, and coordinating cross\-border supervision\.
