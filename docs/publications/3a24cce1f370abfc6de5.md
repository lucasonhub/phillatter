# Governing Epistemic Risk in Data\-Driven Decision\-Making: A Conceptual Framework Integrating Data Governance, Platform Power, Algorithmic Governance and Information Integrity, with Application to Kenya

**Autores:** Robert Wanyama Kilwake; Roselida Maroko Ongare

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Data\-driven decision\-making increasingly depends on long socio\-technical chains in which data is collected, integrated, modelled, ranked and circulated before it reaches a decision\-maker\. Existing governance instruments focus mainly on safety, rights and compliance, and existing scholarship treats data governance, platform power, algorithmic governance and information integrity as separate problems\. As a result, the reliability and legitimacy of the knowledge on which decisions rest — their epistemic quality — remains largely ungoverned\. This conceptual paper addresses that gap\. Using an integrative literature review and a model\-type conceptual design, it synthesises datafication theory, platform capitalism, algorithmic governance and social epistemology into a single data\-to\-decision lens\. It contributes \(i\) a taxonomy of thirteen epistemic risks located along the data\-to\-decision lifecycle; \(ii\) the Epistemic Risk Governance Framework \(ERGF\), which maps four governance pillars onto six actionable governance dimensions; \(iii\) seven testable propositions, including a weakest\-link principle of epistemic governance; and \(iv\) a comparative analysis showing where the ERGF extends the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, the UNESCO Recommendation and Kenya’s emerging data and AI governance instruments\. The framework is illustrated through Kenyan decision contexts and accompanied by a design science agenda for its empirical evaluation\.
