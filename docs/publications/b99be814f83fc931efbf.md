# Fujie, Naoto: The Operational Shape of Brassierian Scientific Realism and Its Conditions of Transformatio Scientific Paradigm Transitions, Xenoscientific Operational Shapes, the Stellarization of Scientific Operational Shapes, and the Non\-Privilege of Epistemic Progress

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This article critically reconstructs Ray Brassier’s scientific realism by distinguishing the epistemic conditions of warranted knowledge from the material and operational configurations through which scientific inquiry becomes possible\. Rather than claiming that nonconceptual causal operations refute conceptual mediation, it examines how the conditions enabling observation, experimentation, rational revision, and scientific\-image construction arise, persist, transform, become unavailable, or are reactivated\. Through engagements with Sellars, Brandom, Kant, Hegel, Kuhn, and contemporary philosophy of science, the paper examines the nonidentity and possible causal coupling of conceptual justification, experimental implementation, historical retention, and downstream physical effects\. It develops an operational account of scientific paradigm transitions without treating paradigms as universal prerequisites for scientific change or equating successful intervention with epistemic entitlement\. The stellarization of scientific operational shapes is introduced as a conditional research proposal concerning heterogeneous scientific operations capable of retaining, transferring, interrupting, and reactivating experimental capacities across changing configurations\. Comparisons with active inference, constructor theory, autonomous laboratories, and contemporary AI\-for\-science research delimit the proposal’s explanatory scope\. Previously executed synthetic experiments, including a separate 12,000\-world comparison of changing correction conditions, are evaluated as exploratory computational evidence rather than tests of Brassier’s epistemological necessity\. The paper does not claim to refute conceptual mediation or establish a new scientific law\. Its contribution lies in clarifying the conditions under which epistemic qualification, operational feasibility, historical transmission, and consequential scientific intervention become coupled, separated, or reorganized\. \(direct link \)
