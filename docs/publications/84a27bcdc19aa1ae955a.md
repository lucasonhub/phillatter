# Ultrasound cycloplasty: dynamic efficacy and long\-term safety in glaucoma patients from northwest China

**Autores:** Ping Ge; Rui Shi; Qing\-Qing Mu; Rui\-Xue Zhang; Xiangxiang Yang; Yuan He

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose To comprehensively evaluate the dynamic efficacy of UCP in treating different types of glaucoma patients in Northwest China, systematically analyze treatment outcome variations across follow\-up time points, and assess long\-term safety profiles Methods In a 1\-year follow\-up study, 24 patients \(30 eyes\) with various types of glaucoma were enrolled and underwent UCP treatment \(10 sectors or 8 sectors\)\. Follow\-up visits were conducted at different postoperative time points \(1 day, 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, and 1 year\)\. Follow\-up assessments consisted of IOP measurements, IOP reduction from baseline, surgical success rate, 24\-hour IOP fluctuation, pain score improvement, and complications\. Furthermore, the correlation between IOP at the final follow\-up and baseline IOP was evaluated\. Results 1\. The overall IOP in all patients significantly decreased after UCP compared to preoperative levels \( P &lt; 0\.01\)\. 2\. UCP demonstrated significant long\-term IOP\-lowering effects in patients with various types of glaucoma, with a 59\.91% reduction at 1 year postoperatively\. The efficacy was particularly notable in patients with primary angle\-closure glaucoma, achieving a surgical success rate of 96\.67% at 1 year\. 3\. Preoperative baseline IOP showed a positive correlation with IOP reduction at 1 year postoperatively \( r s = 0\.641, P &lt; 0\.01\), indicating that higher baseline IOP was associated with greater IOP reduction\. 4\. UCP significantly improved IOP fluctuation, with the mean 24\-hour IOP fluctuation decreasing from 10\.6 preoperatively to 5\.3 postoperatively \( P = 0\.001\)\. 5\. UCP exhibited acceptable safety, with significant reductions in pain scores \( P &lt; 0\.05\) and no severe complications such as hypotony or macular edema observed during follow\-up\. Conclusion 1\. In glaucoma patients in the Northwest population, UCP demonstrates significant long\-term and dynamic efficacy—the higher the baseline IOP, the more pronounced the IOP\-lowering effect, with outstanding performance in stabilizing IOP fluctuations\. 2\. UCP exhibits acceptable safety and controllable complications\. Postoperative IOP stabilizes by 3 months, allowing for gradual withdrawal of medications, making it a reliable treatment option for glaucoma\.
