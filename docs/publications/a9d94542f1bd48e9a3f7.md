# Shaping the future as we bet

**Autores:** Mirko Daniel Garasic

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract The rapid ascent of decentralized prediction platforms such as Polymarket signals more than a regulatory or financial controversy\. This article argues that while prediction platforms represent a hyper\-concentrated, automated case study, they instantiate a broader infrastructural condition of late technocapitalism: the hyperstitional collapse between representation and execution\. Drawing on Nick Land’s accelerationist framework, the sociology of financial markets, and digital epistemology, we contend that prediction markets—much like conventional opinion polling, forward guidance, and algorithmic models—do not merely reflect probabilities but actively produce the futures they claim to forecast\. However, whereas traditional polling operates through diffuse, human\-mediated feedback loops \(e\.g\., strategic voting and bandwagon dynamics\), prediction markets intensify this operational logic by coupling real\-time financial incentives, capital\-weighted aggregation, and automated execution\. We examine this hyperstitional condition across three registers: epistemological, ontological, and ethical\.
