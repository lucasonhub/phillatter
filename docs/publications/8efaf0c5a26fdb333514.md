# The roadmap of quantum many\-body physics : from exact diagonalization to machine learning

**Autores:** Yilun Gao

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

The study of interacting quantum many\-body systems is constrained by the exponential growth of the Hilbert space with system size, which limits both numerical simulations and theoretical understanding\. This thesis explores how the interplay between disorder and interactions shapes localization and ergodicity in quantum many\-body systems, using the exact diagonalization method, and how machine learning can serve as an alternative approach for studying these systems\. We first investigate the many\-body localization problem in a one\-dimensional SU\(2\)\-invariant random antiferromagnetic Heisenberg chain using exact and sparse matrix diagonalization methods\. By analyzing energy level gap\-ratio statistics, participation ratio, entanglement entropy and the entanglement spectral parameter as a function of disorder strength under di\!erent random coupling distributions, we identify clear qualitative di\!erences from the widely studied random field Heisenberg chain\. For system sizes up to L = 24, signatures of localized behaviour are much less pronounced than in the case of the random field model\. Motivated by the limitations of conventional numerical methods, we examine machine learning as a scalable paradigm for many\-body physics\. Using the Bose\- Hubbard model in one and two dimensions as a benchmark, we test HUBBARDNET, a deep neural network architecture, by replacing standard stochastic gradient descent with adaptive moment estimation \(ADAM\), adjusting the learning rate for improved optimization, and introducing physics\-informed output activations that can resolve extremely small wave\-function amplitudes\. This approach yields ground\-state energy errors reduced by orders of magnitude and wave function fidelities exceeding 99%, remaining robust across four decades of interaction strength and over superfluid, Mott\-insulating, and quantum chaotic regimes\. Beyond energy prediction, the trained models accurately reproduce generalized inverse participation ratios and multifractal dimensions for ground and excited states, capturing localization, delocalization, and multifractality across the spectrum, with excited\-state training explicitly guided by fractal dimensions that encode the multifractal properties of the wave functions\.
