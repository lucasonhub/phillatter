# Feasibility of diffuse reflectance spectroscopy for tumor border detection in colorectal cancer surgery

**Autores:** Joris T\. Hepkema; Isis Boeije; Arend G\. J\. Aalbers; Brechtje A\. Grotenhuis; Marinke Westerterp; Freija Geldof; Behdad Dashtbozorg; Theo J\. M\. Ruers

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Adequate distal resection margins \(DRM\) in colorectal cancer surgery are critical for oncological safety while preserving sphincter function\. Current intra\-operative DRM assessment methods are limited or have major drawbacks, leaving surgeons without real\-time guidance\. Diffuse reflectance spectroscopy \(DRS\) offers a potential solution using rapid optical feedback on tissue characteristics\. In this study, continuous measurements were performed on freshly excised colorectal specimens using a broadband DRS system \(400–1600 nm\)\. A binary classification approach was applied to discriminate between non\-tumor and tumor locations\. Multiple feature extraction strategies were evaluated in combination with different classifiers and hyperparameter tuning was performed\. A total of 39,741 spectra \(22,451 non\-tumor, 17,290 tumor\) from 86 patients were included\. Using the first derivative of the spectra paired with a radial basis function \(RBF\) support vector machine achieved good results, reaching a mean Matthews Correlation Coefficient \(MCC\) of 0\.76 ± 0\.27 across patients\. Sensitivity and specificity were 0\.93 ± 0\.13 and 0\.88 ± 0\.17, respectively\. This study demonstrates the feasibility of using DRS combined with machine learning to distinguish tumor from non\-tumor regions in colorectal cancer surgery\. Future work should focus on prospective validation of distal margin assessment and exploring probe designs with deeper penetration to enhance intra\-operative applicability\.
