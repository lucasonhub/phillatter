# Grassini Grimaldi, Alessandro: My Private Rosetta Stone: A Fragment of Akragas, the Cognitive World of Ancient Greece, and the Neurodivergent Mind That Could Not Let Go — The Greeks, the Romans, and the Architecture of Western Consciousness

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This monograph uses a personal encounter with a fragment of ancient limestone from Akragas as an entry point into the cognitive, philosophical and material inheritance of the Greek and Roman worlds\. The object functions as a “private Rosetta Stone”: not as a device for translating scripts, but as a means of examining how touch, material form, memory and neurodivergent cognition can mediate historical understanding\. The work moves from Akragas and Magna Graecia through Greek architecture, mythology, theatre, philosophy and language, and then considers the Roman transformation and preservation of the Greek inheritance\. It proposes a broad distinction between a Greek mode of cognition oriented toward interior inquiry, philosophical questioning and tragic ambiguity, and a Roman mode oriented toward exterior organisation, law, institutional durability and civic structure\. Western intellectual culture is interpreted as an continuing interaction between these two modes rather than the achievement of either in isolation\. The monograph also develops an autobiographical argument about embodied and haptic cognition\. It considers whether material contact, sensory attachment and neurodivergent patterns of attention can constitute forms of historical knowing that precede or supplement explicit conceptual understanding, while distinguishing personal recognition and cultural inheritance from conventional archaeological proof\. \(direct link \)
