# From pixels to prevention: Can artificial intelligence transform the endoscopic management of colorectal neoplasia?

**Autores:** Muhammad Abdul Haseeb Khan; Abdullah Saeed; Hafiz Hanzala Tufail; Bakhtiar Ameer Tahir; Ehtezaz Khan Ali Zai

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In this literature narrative review of artificial intelligence \(AI\) technologies for colorectal endoscopy \(PubMed/MEDLINE, EMBASE, and Cochrane Library, January 2015 to June 2026, with supplementary hand\-searching\), the &quot;pixels\-to\-prevention&quot; paradigm \-image interpretation → detection → characterization → clinical decision → treatment quality → surveillance → prevention \-is employed to examine whether the well\-evidenced performance characteristics of AI can actually be tied to effective cancer prevention rather than merely listing out the different types of applications of AI that were reported in previous reviews\.Colorectal cancer prevention is strongly connected to colonoscopy but is limited by lesion miss rates, endoscopist variability, and interpretation of the results into management\.Computer\-aided detection enhances the adenoma detection rates and reduces the lesion miss rate, but the higher adenoma detection rates is just a surrogate and does not directly contribute to cancer prevention: AI\-assisted colonoscopy prefers the detection of diminutive and non\-neoplastic lesions rather than advanced neoplasms\.This review traces the evolution from computer\-aided detection to computer\-aided characterization and optical diagnosis, highlighting their recent applications for quality control of resection, prediction of recurrence development, and individualized surveillance based on risk stratification where we distinguish advances in metrics of performance from clinical utility \(interval colorectal cancer, unneeded resections, surveillance burden\)\.The review considers the questions related to the problem of false positives, algorithm bias, dataset variability, lack of external validation, automation bias, interpretability, workflow, and regulation\.Asking ourselves how intelligent endoscopy could help us going beyond the discovery of lesions through the use of artificial intelligence, we propose an agenda for research that focuses on validation and patient outcome\.
