# Maternal knowledge, clean water availability, and hygiene practices associated with diarrhea among children under five in south sorong, indonesia

**Autores:** Agustina Mallisa

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Diarrhea remains an important public health problem among children under five, particularly in remote settings where access to safe water and adequate hygiene practices may be limited\. Maternal knowledge and household environmental conditions can influence the occurrence of diarrhea in young children\. This study aimed to examine the associations between maternal knowledge, clean water availability, maternal hygiene practices, and diarrhea among children under five in the working area of Kais Community Health Center, South Sorong Regency, Indonesia\. Methods: A quantitative analytical observational study with a cross\-sectional design was conducted in June 2026\. The study included 43 mothers of children under five selected through total sampling\. Data were collected using questionnaires and observation checklists\. Univariate analysis was used to describe participant characteristics and study variables, while associations were assessed using the Chi\-square test\. Results: Among respondents, 55\.8% had adequate knowledge, 60\.5% had access to clean water, and 53\.5% reported inadequate hygiene practices\. Diarrhea was identified in 32\.6% of children under five\. Significant associations were found between diarrhea and maternal knowledge \(p &lt; 0\.001\), clean water availability \(p = 0\.003\), and maternal hygiene practices \(p = 0\.003\)\. Conclusion: Maternal knowledge, access to clean water, and hygiene practices were significantly associated with childhood diarrhea\. Strengthening maternal health education, safe water access, and household hygiene practices should be prioritized in community\-based diarrhea prevention programs in remote areas\.
