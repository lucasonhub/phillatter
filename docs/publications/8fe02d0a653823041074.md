# GIS\-based spatial prioritization of natural\-hazard exposure and recorded COVID\-19 case counts in Lemery, Batangas, Philippines

**Autores:** Jovito P\. Permante; Erwin Rafael Cabral; Florina Padilla\-Santiago

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Local governments often maintain separate natural\-hazard maps and health\-surveillance records but lack a transparent method for identifying areas where these concerns overlap\. This study applied a Geographic Information System \(GIS\) sieve\-mapping approach to prioritize the 46 barangays of Lemery, Batangas, Philippines using 2,505 confirmed COVID\-19 cases and ten natural\-hazard layers covering seismic, landslide, coastal, flood, and volcanic hazards\. QGIS was used to convert hazard classes into ordinal scores, adjust scores according to the proportion of barangay area affected, apply predefined weights, and generate a composite ranking\. The decision premise was that barangays with high absolute COVID\-19 case counts and substantial mapped hazard exposure may warrant greater preparedness priority\. A balanced 50/50 weighting between the health and natural\-hazard domains was used as an a priori screening scenario, not as evidence of equal contributions to disaster loss\. Natural\-hazard weights reflected qualitative local frequency information rather than probabilistic loss estimates\. The additive combining rule summed the scaled health component and weighted, area\-adjusted hazard scores without modelling interactions or joint probabilities\. Bukal recorded the highest absolute number of confirmed COVID\-19 cases, while District IV, Wawa Ibaba, Palanas, Mataas Na Bayan, and Sambal Ilaya received the five highest composite priority scores\. The resulting ranking represents relative planning priority rather than infection risk or probabilistic disaster risk\. Future work should incorporate population normalization, sensitivity analysis, vulnerability and exposure indicators, hazard interactions, and finer\-resolution data\.
