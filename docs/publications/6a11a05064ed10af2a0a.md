# Land surface model snow formulation sensitivity and a solar\-zenith\-angle snow covered area scheme evaluated across the western United States

**Autores:** R\. M\. Maxwell

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate representation of snow water equivalent \(SWE\) remains a central challenge for land surface and integrated hydrologic models\. This work expands the ParFlow\-CLM snow model with several new parameterizations, including a formulation that modulates fractional snow covered area \(fSCA\) by solar zenith angle\. I evaluate these parameterizations across 220 site\-years from the SNOTEL observation network, spanning three experiments and 58 configurations that represent the major snow parameterization choices in the literature, including three albedo schemes, five rain\-snow partitioning methods, and band\-separated snow aging formulations\. A systematic sensitivity analysis shows that fSCA dominates SWE prediction accuracy, exceeding the combined effects of albedo scheme, rain\-snow partitioning, snow aging, and soil moisture\. Static fSCA corrections improve peak SWE but introduce a trade\-off with melt timing\. The new solar\-zenith\-angle\-modulated fSCA scheme resolves this trade\-off by using seasonal solar geometry to transition between snow\-preserving accumulation and snow\-exposing ablation conditions\. Across all configurations, the SZA\-fSCA scheme achieves the best balance of peak accuracy and melt timing \(NSE 0\.79, melt bias of \-1 day\), surpassing reanalysis products and default process\-model benchmarks and approaching LSTM performance \(NSE 0\.82\) when combined with forcing bias correction\. These advancements provide physics\-based snow options that place ParFlow\-CLM among other widely used land surface and snow models in sophistication\. Improved representation of snow water equivalent supports more accurate integrated continental hydrologic simulation over the United States\.
