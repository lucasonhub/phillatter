# Cholinergic inputs to motoneurons in the spinal cord augment focal muscle spasticity in rodents

**Autores:** Patrik Meglić; Petra Šoštarić; Eirini Tsape; Melpomeni Galani; Ana Knezović; Laura Restani; Marco Pirazzini; Ornella Rossetto; Gareth Brian Miles; Laskaro Zagoraiou; Ivica Matak

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background and Purpose Cholinergic premotor inputs to motoneurons support high levels of motor output when required\. However, the possible role of these inputs in pathologically elevated muscle tone has not been characterised\. To address this, we examined the modulatory effect of spinal acetylcholine \(ACh\) on experimental spastic paralysis\. Experimental Approach Rats were injected with tetanus neurotoxin \(TeNT, 33–100 pg, im\) into gastrocnemius to induce sustained rigid hind‐paw extension and resistance to passive ankle flexion\. The effects of spinal ACh on experimental hypertonia were probed by lumbar intrathecal application of the AChE inhibitor neostigmine \(NEO\) or a muscarinic M 2 receptor antagonist \(AQ‐RA 741\)\. Effects of NEO on extracellular ACh were measured in the facial nucleus after TeNT \(33 pg\) or botulinum toxin A \(BoNT/A, 2 U\) injection into the rat whisker pad\. The intensity of TeNT‐evoked hindlimb hypertonia \(10 pg, im\) was assessed in mice with genetic ablation of cholinergic C bouton source interneurons \(V0 C interneurons\)\. Key Results NEO augmented the severity of TeNT‐evoked hypertonia, whereas AQ‐RA 741 attenuated it\. Local perfusion of NEO caused a BoNT/A‐sensitive increase in extracellular ACh concentration\. The involvement of cholinergic C boutons was corroborated by reduced hypertonia following TeNT challenge \(10 pg, im\) in V0 C ‐ablated mice\. Conclusions and Implications Our results suggest that spinal cholinergic transmission, associated with vesicular ACh release and M 2 signalling at C boutons, exacerbates experimental spastic paralysis\. We reveal a new role for endogenous spinal ACh, with implications for developing new therapeutic approaches to treat spasticity and hyperkinetic movement disorders\.
