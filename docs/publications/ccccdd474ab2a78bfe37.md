# Zhao, Xiangbin: The Structure and Dynamics of Intention: A Formal Theory of Intentional Organization, Action, and Directional Switching

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

What makes a physical system capable of forming its own directions of action? Why does an internally generated impulse follow one path rather than another, and how can such directionality arise from the system’s organization without an additional controller that reads probabilities and makes decisions? What distinguishes an established intention from its current action, the switching of its directions, and changes in its dynamics? These questions stand at the intersection of consciousness science, cognitive science, neuroscience, artificial intelligence, and the study of dynamical systems\. This work develops a systematic formal theory of Intention within On Self\-Manifestation\. It establishes Intention as a probabilized Law Ecology driven by source drive within the Intention\-Domain\. In its constitution, Intention is an organization of physically borne Laws; in the relative generation of its directions, it is a probability organization; and in its relations of breaking and altering established holding tendencies, it is a Principle organization\. Law, Probability, Principle, and Intention are therefore not four separate mechanisms assembled together, but different structural aspects of the same organization\. The theory derives four jointly necessary and sufficient structural conditions for Intention: a formally established probability organization within the Intention\-Domain; an endogenous source\-drive relation whose continuation is borne by that organization; a one\-to\-one correspondence between probability dimensions and Desire\-Gates; and a tuning\-command interface beyond each Desire\-Gate\. The probability dimensions are borne by Law and share a common Continuation\-Position, while their relative generation is borne by the organization itself\. Directional activity is thus explained through the structure and dynamics of the system, without positing a separate internal observer or decision\-maker that reads probability values\. Intention can be structurally established without currently acting, switching, producing behavior, or entering conscious manifestation\. The twenty chapters develop this account through four connected formal sequences and eight further investigations\. Chapters 1–12 establish necessary conditions, mathematical representations, and sufficient conditions for Intention qualification, Intention\-Action, Desire\-Gate switching, and Intention\-Dynamics\. Each of these four objects receives a bidirectional structural characterization within its specified semantic and working domain\. Action, switching, and dynamics are distinguished rather than collapsed into a single process: switching does not simply imply action, action does not imply switching, and dynamics is not a temporal series of action events\. Chapters 13–20 extend the theory into inversion, determination, limits, construction, reachability, source attribution, stability, and cross\-realization invariants\. The inversion analysis establishes what can be identified about Intention\-Dynamics under a specified observation protocol and why observable outcomes do not uniquely determine internal dynamics\. The determination framework distinguishes positive, negative, and undecided verdicts, together with a separate out\-of\-domain status where applicable\. The limit analysis characterizes structural persistence, hard boundaries, and degeneration beyond those boundaries\. The construction analysis demonstrates the operation of existing Intention structures in a finite reference specification, while the reachability analysis establishes legal paths and negative certificates relative to declared initial conditions and transition rules\. The theory also distinguishes the first occurrence of Intention qualification from the actual historical source responsible for its formation\. Source attribution requires both an actual formation link and a legally constrained counterfactual sufficiency test; it cannot be reduced to the earliest event, the last condition to become satisfied, or an isolated necessary cause\. The stability analysis distinguishes persistence of an original qualification segment, persistence of a fixed bearer, and continuity at the existence level, while separating claims about individual trajectories, trajectory families, and all legal trajectories\. The final study establishes theory\-neutral directional invariants across realizations and formulates their relation to Law\-bearing and Self\-Manifestation\-Field structures\. The central contribution of this work is a unified formal theory of Intention grounded in structural qualification, dynamic organization, and explicit criteria of determination\. It establishes the conditions under which Intention holds, distinguishes its qualification from its operation, characterizes directional switching and dynamical change, and develops rigorous methods for analyzing its identifiability, limits, reachability, historical sources, and stability\. By connecting physical organization, probabilistic directionality, and intentional activity within one formal system, the theory offers a new foundation for investigating intention and agency in biological and artificial systems\.
