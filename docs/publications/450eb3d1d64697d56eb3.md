# Reproductive Identity \- Language, Reason, and Artificial Intelligence after Hegel and Marx \- Augustine’s Psychological Trinity, Analytic Philosophy, and Ecological Realism

**Autores:** Matti Vesa Volanen

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This article reconstructs a history of language, reason, and practical activity from Augustine through Kant, Hegel, Marx, the European linguistic turn, and analytic philosophy in order to formulate the concept of reproductive iden tity\. Reproductive identity is not sameness, a fixed inner essence, or the final conceptual possession of the world\. It is the historically open, materially conditioned work through which embodied individuals and communities sustain, repair, and develop the relations that enable their continuation\. Augustine’s psychological trinity supplies an early grammar of memory, understanding, and will or love; Kant clarifies the conditions of judgment and practical reason; Hegel understands concept and reality as mutually realised in activity; and Marx translates this movement into the communal metabolism through which human beings reproduce life in and through nature\. The linguistic turn and an alytic philosophy provide indispensable accounts of disclosure, difference, reference, truth, use, and normativity\. The section on analytic philosophy distinguishes formal validity from situated inquiry and reconstructs practical reason as both normatively articulated and causally efficacious\. Neither tradition, however, can settle the material and in stitutional conditions of the forms of life in which language works\. The final sections develop ecological democracy and politically subordinate artificial intelligence as institutional consequences of the argument\. Artificial intelligence is treated not as a sovereign subject but as a reversible public infrastructure whose legitimacy depends on ecological admissibility, traceability, contestability, repair, and the common reproduction of internal and external nature\.
