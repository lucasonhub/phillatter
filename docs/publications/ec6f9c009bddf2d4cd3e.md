# Zohrevandi, Talaye &amp; Niami, Morteza: Institutional Continuity and Existential Foreclosure in Marriage: A Daseinsanalytic Sociological Synthesis

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Marriage occupies a paradoxical position within human existence: it is simultaneously a relationship, a legal arrangement, an economic partnership, a social identity, and, frequently, the institutional framework within which parenthood becomes possible\. Yet the persistence of marriage as an institutional form does not guarantee the persistence of the living relation between the people who inhabit it\. This article develops a conceptual synthesis of existentialphenomenological \(Daseinsanalytic\) and sociological perspectives on marital distress, arguing that the central problem is not marriage as such but a process we term existential foreclosure: the gradual transformation of a contingent relational possibility into an experienced ontological necessity\. Drawing on Heidegger&\#x27;s account of thrownness, Being\-with, and everydayness; Goffman&\#x27;s dramaturgical theory of role performance; Marcia&\#x27;s developmental construct of identity foreclosure; and Giddens&\#x27;s and Bauman&\#x27;s sociological accounts of intimacy under late and liquid modernity, we propose a two\-level model in which structural role\-extraction and existential identity\-fusion reinforce one another\. We treat a recent, terminologically related set of unreviewed preprint constructs, &quot;ontological leakage,&quot; the &quot;dynamic mask,&quot; and &quot;institutional monopoly,&quot; as a parallel formulation worth noting for terminological comparison rather than as a validated theoretical foundation, and we ground the argument instead in peer\-reviewed literature\. We close with implications for a Daseinsanalytic clinical stance and directions for future empirical research\. Keywords: marriage; existential foreclosure; Daseinsanalysis; identity foreclosure; dramaturgical theory; late modernity; phenomenology of relationships \(direct link \)
