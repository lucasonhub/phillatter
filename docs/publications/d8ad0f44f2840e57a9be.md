# Minamikata, Juza: Attribution Transfer in Consciousness Research: A Local Bridge Restraint

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Consciousness research frequently transfers evidential support across distinct analytical positions, including neural activity, representational content, conscious manifestation, phenomenal unity, and subject attribution\. This paper formulates these transitions as an attribution\-transfer problem and develops the Local Bridge Restraint \(LBR\) as a domain\-specific methodological framework\. LBR requires evidence to remain indexed to the analytical position for which it was obtained unless a position\-specific bridge warrant and its conditions are independently supplied\. The framework distinguishes content attribution, conscious\-manifestation attribution, phenomenal\-unity attribution, and subject attribution, and treats bridge composition and retrospective projection as further inferential operations rather than automatic extensions of prior evidence\. To make the framework empirically usable, the paper introduces a minimal dynamic formation configuration combining local neural states with currently effective relations and applies it to binocular rivalry\. The analysis distinguishes content\-sensitive measures, evidence for conscious manifestation, and task\-specific spatial perceptual coherence, while resisting the identification of spatial coherence with phenomenal unity\. LBR is not proposed as a new mechanism or general theory of consciousness\. It is a local operationalization of a broader non\-inheritance constraint designed to identify where evidential scope is preserved, where a bridge is required, and what would justify stronger attributional transitions\. \(direct link \)
