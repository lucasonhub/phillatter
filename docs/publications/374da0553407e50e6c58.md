# Interpretation of the 2026 clinical practice guideline on polycystic ovary syndrome and the gut microbiota: From evidence to clinical translation

**Autores:** Fangfang Dai; Tailang Yin; Liangshan Mu; Rong Li

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Polycystic ovary syndrome \(PCOS\) is a heterogeneous reproductive, endocrine, and metabolic disorder, and the gut microbiota has emerged as an important focus of mechanistic and translational research\. The 2026 clinical practice guideline on PCOS and the gut microbiota was developed to bridge the gap between rapidly evolving microbiome research and uncertainty regarding its clinical application\. It addresses nine core questions and provides 15 recommendations covering disease association, microbiome testing, probiotics and prebiotics, dietary interventions, fecal microbiota transplantation, interactions with pharmacologic therapy, metabolic surgery, and the gut\-brain axis\. This article provides a focused interpretation of these recommendations, emphasizing the clinical implications of recommendation strength, the distinction between research\-oriented microbiome analyses and routine diagnostic testing, the adjunctive role of microbiota\-targeted interventions, and the need to distinguish mechanistic plausibility from demonstrated clinical benefit\. Implementation should remain embedded within established PCOS management, with lifestyle modification, evidence\-based pharmacotherapy, reproductive care, and mental health support as the foundation\. Microbiota\-targeted strategies should be individualized according to evidence maturity, strain or product specificity, patient phenotype, safety, and feasibility\. Future priorities include standardized microbiome methodologies, multicenter prospective studies, adequately powered randomized trials, clinically meaningful reproductive outcomes, and long\-term safety assessment\. This interpretation aims to support appropriate clinical application while avoiding premature translation of preliminary microbiome findings into routine practice\.
