# Women’s Representation and Digital Paratexts in the Pirelli Calendar: A Framing Approach \(1964–2024\)

**Autores:** Öznur NALÇINKAYA

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

As digital environments diversify, brands increasingly construct their public image not only through what is shown but also through official materials that guide how those signs should be read\. First published in 1964 for promotional purposes, the Pirelli Calendar has become an iconic case that makes visible aesthetic and ideological shifts in the representation of women within visual culture, while being continuously reframed through layers of digital paratext\. This study examines the transformation of women’s representation in the Calendar between 1964 and 2024 by focusing on the relationship between visual codes—nudity, gaze/orientation, pose and agency, and Goffman\-inspired indicators—and corporate digital paratexts, including official promotional statements, interview excerpts, and webpage descriptions\. Using purposive sampling, three images were selected from each chosen edition, yielding a corpus of 30 visuals; six accessible official paratext texts were also included\. The visuals were analyzed through visual content analysis and semiotic reading, whereas the paratexts were examined via a framing approach\. Findings indicate that an erotic/objectifying frame dominated from 1964 through the 1990s, while, from the 2010s onward, “empowerment,” “authenticity,” and “diversity” emerged as central themes in official digital discourse\. Yet certain patterns associated with the male gaze persist, and the relationship between corporate paratext and visual representation alternates between alignment and tension across different editions\. The study discusses how postfeminist “empowerment” rhetoric is re\-framed and circulated digitally within brand storytelling\.
