# Toward a sensory\-gradient framework for neuroinclusive secondary\-school design: an integrative review and contextualization for a tropical Nigerian setting

**Autores:** Omoniyi O\. Olagunju; Ayomide Teslim Ogundele

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Neuroinclusive school‐design research identifies environmental qualities such as acoustic control, sensory zoning, transition spaces, retreat provision, and spatial predictability, yet how these features interact across campus, building, and room scales remains underdeveloped, particularly in sub\-Saharan African contexts\. Methods This integrative review tested and refined a pre‐existing Sensory Gradient proposition for secondary‐school design and contextualised it for Ota, Nigeria\. Following Whittemore and Knafl&\#x27;s methodology, a systematic database search was combined with supplementary other‐methods identification, and study selection was reported using PRISMA 2020 principles\. Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection, ERIC, PsycINFO, MEDLINE/PubMed, and the Avery Index to Architectural Periodicals were searched to 15 July 2026\. Across the two identification streams, 73 reports were assessed and 26 evidence sources were included\. Primary studies and reviews were critically appraised, and a hybrid deductive\-inductive narrative synthesis and abductive audit tested whether elements of the original framework should be retained, modified, rejected, or added\. Results The evidence did not support a single high‐to‐low stimulus axis\. Environmental demands varied across acoustic, visual, movement, social, thermal, and other sensory dimensions, while predictability/legibility operated as a cross‐cutting characteristic and preferred stimulation differed among learners\. The framework was therefore reformulated as a multidimensional demand‐profile network in which learner‐environment fit, transition magnitude, predictability/legibility, and perceived control shape regulatory experience and participation\. Evidence was strongest for autistic learners, with comparatively limited direct evidence for ADHD, dyslexia, and dyspraxia\. Discussion The resulting Sensory‐Gradient Framework is evidence‐informed and testable rather than empirically validated\. Its initial contextual application to Ota highlights trade‐offs among passive ventilation, daylight, acoustic control, resource constraints, and institutional practice, and provides a basis for participatory design and post‐occupancy evaluation in Nigerian secondary schools\.
