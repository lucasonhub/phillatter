# Role of Diet in Drug Pharmacokinetics and Therapeutic Outcomes: An Overview

**Autores:** 

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Diet is an important but frequently underestimated determinant of drug pharmacokinetics and therapeutic response\.Food can influence the absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination of drugs through changes in gastrointestinal physiology, gastric pH, gastrointestinal transit, bile secretion, intestinal blood flow, drug solubility, transporter activity, and drug\-metabolizing enzymes\.The magnitude and direction of these effects depend on the physicochemical characteristics of the drug, pharmaceutical formulation, meal composition, quantity of food, timing of food intake, and individual patient characteristics\.High\-fat meals, for example, may increase the bioavailability of some poorly soluble drugs, whereas minerals and other food components can reduce absorption through complex formation\.Certain foods and beverages, particularly grapefruit, can alter intestinal cytochrome P450 and transporter activity and consequently modify systemic drug exposure\.Dietary vitamin K is clinically relevant in patients receiving warfarin, while foods containing high concentrations of tyramine may interact with monoamine oxidase inhibitors\.Enteral nutrition can also interfere with the absorption of selected medications\.Recent research has expanded the concept of food\-drug interactions to include food texture, viscosity, volume, and interactions involving the gut microbiome\.Regulatory agencies increasingly emphasize systematic assessment of food effects during drug development because changes in drug exposure can influence efficacy, adverse effects, and treatment safety\.A comprehensive understanding of diet\-drug interactions is therefore essential for individualized medication counseling, rational prescribing, medication safety, and optimization of therapeutic outcomes\.
