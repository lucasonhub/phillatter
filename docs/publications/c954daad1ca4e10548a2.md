# Space weathering age estimation of chondritic asteroids using machine learning

**Autores:** Lakshika Palamakumbure

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Asteroid surfaces are continuously modified by space weathering \(SW\), a combination of solar\-wind irradiation and micrometeorite bombardment that progressively alters their spectral and physical properties\. Understanding the timescales and mechanisms of these processes is essential for interpreting the surface evolution of airless bodies and reconstructing their evolutionary histories\. This work investigates asteroid surface evolution by integrating machine\-learning approaches with laboratory\-simulated SW data and spectroscopic observations of individual asteroids and asteroid populations\. Machine\-learning models, including an ensemble regression model and Support Vector Regression \(SVR\), were developed and trained using reflectance spectra of laboratory\-irradiated silicate materials representative of chondritic asteroid surfaces\. The models capture the non\-linear relationship between spectral properties and SW age and were applied to spacecraft observations of the near\-Earth asteroids \(25143\) Itokawa and \(433\) Eros, as well as large\-scale photometric and spectroscopic datasets from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey \(SDSS\) for main\-belt asteroid populations\. This approach enables SW ages to be estimated consistently across individual asteroid surfaces and statistically large asteroid populations\. The results demonstrate substantial variability in surface maturity both within individual asteroids and among asteroid populations\. Itokawa exhibits highly heterogeneous SW ages, ranging from approximately 103 years in recently exposed regions to more than 109 years in mature terrains, consistent with repeated resurfacing associated with regolith mobility, impacts, and rotational processes\. In contrast, Eros exhibits a more spatially uniform and mature surface, with typical SW ages of approximately 0\.4–2\.8 Gyr, suggesting a greater contribution from long\-term micrometeorite\-driven maturation\. At the population level, asteroid families show broad and asymmetric SW\-age distributions, with systematic differences between spectral maturity and dynamical family ages\. These results demonstrate that SW age characterizes the maturity and exposure history of the currently observed surface rather than the time elapsed since asteroid or family formation\. The analysis further indicates a systematic variation in apparent SW age with heliocentric distance\. Near\-Earth asteroids generally exhibit younger surface ages, reflecting frequent resurfacing associated with impacts and planetary encounters, whereas inner main\-belt asteroids tend to show more mature surfaces\. Outer\-belt populations display broader and, in some cases, younger apparent SW\-age distributions, reflecting differences in micrometeorite flux, collisional activity, and resurfacing histories\. These observations indicate that asteroid surface evolution is controlled by the competing effects of spectral maturation and surface renewal rather than by a simple monotonic increase in weathering age\. Consequently, asteroid surfaces can occupy different evolutionary states, ranging from relatively mature and quasi\-steady conditions to highly heterogeneous and episodically renewed states\. Overall, this work demonstrates the potential of machine learning to extract information on surface maturity from asteroid reflectance spectra and to bridge laboratory simulations with remote\-sensing observations\. By providing a scalable approach for estimating spectral SW ages across large datasets, the methodology offers a framework for investigating SW evolution across diverse asteroid populations and potentially other airless planetary surfaces\.
