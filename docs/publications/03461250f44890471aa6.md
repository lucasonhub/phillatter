# Adoption decision\-making of AI digital humans for agricultural regional public brands in short videos

**Autores:** Rong Wu; Jin Xiao; Jiaqi Chen

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction This study examines the adoption of AI digital humans \(AI\-DHs\) by agricultural regional public brands in short\-video promotion\. Methods A complex network evolutionary game model is developed in which brands interact on a scale\-free network and update their strategies through the Fermi rule\. The model considers AI\-DH production and generation costs, real\-human promotion costs, consumer preference, recommendation priority, and network size\. For each parameter scenario, 100 stochastic simulation runs are conducted, and mean adoption trajectories with 95% simulation intervals are reported\. Results Higher AI\-DH\-related costs reduce the relative payoff of AI\-DH promotion and suppress adoption, whereas higher real\-human promotion costs make AI\-DH promotion comparatively more attractive\. Consumer preference produces the most pronounced change in the evolutionary outcome, with higher preference shifting the system toward broader AI\-DH adoption\. Higher recommendation priority helps sustain AI\-DH adoption during the evolutionary process but does not independently determine the final adoption state\. Network size has limited influence on long\-run average adoption, although larger networks reduce stochastic variation in the evolutionary process\. Discussion These findings extend digital\-human research from post\-adoption consumer responses to the dynamic adoption decisions of agricultural regional public brands and highlight the roles of relative payoffs, platform conditions, and inter\-brand learning\.
