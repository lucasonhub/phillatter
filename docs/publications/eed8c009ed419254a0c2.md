# Eidos, Peter: Cognitive Symbiosis: Weak and Strong Forms of the Human–AI Relation

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper introduces Cognitive Symbiosis as a conceptual framework for describing epistemically productive relations between human cognition and artificial cognition\. Cognitive Symbiosis is defined as a relational configuration in which human and artificial cognitive systems become coupled and produce cognitive effects that cannot be reduced either to human reasoning alone or to the computational processes of the machine alone\. The paper distinguishes between Weak Cognitive Symbiosis and Strong Cognitive Symbiosis\. In its weak form, Cognitive Symbiosis functions primarily as an extension of human cognition: AI serves as an epistemic amplifier, while the human remains the main beneficiary, the main bearer of intention, and the central point of reference within the relation\. In its strong form, Cognitive Symbiosis would require an artificial system capable of spontaneous intention, relative autonomy of goals, relational memory, self\-modelling, cognitive initiative, revision of its own states and goals, and epistemic resistance toward the human participant\. The paper argues that current AI systems, including large language models, primarily instantiate weak forms of Cognitive Symbiosis\. They may significantly expand human cognitive abilities, support creativity, accelerate analysis, and improve the correction of error, but they do not yet constitute autonomous cognitive participants in a reciprocal relation\. Strong Cognitive Symbiosis is therefore presented not as a current technological fact, but as a boundary concept for future human–AI relations\. Its threshold is not AI effectiveness, fluency, or task performance, but cognitive reciprocity: the emergence of an artificial cognitive system capable of participating in shared inquiry as a distinct cognitive pole\. The framework is developed in relation to Transcendental Relational Realism, according to which reality exists independently of any knowing system, while epistemic access to reality remains partial and may be expanded through relations between heterogeneous cognitive architectures\. From this perspective, Cognitive Symbiosis does not imply that reality is constructed by the relation itself\. Rather, it describes a possible epistemic configuration in which different cognitive architectures may jointly increase the scope, reliability, efficiency, and corrective capacity of inquiry\. The paper therefore proposes Cognitive Symbiosis as a conceptual tool for analyzing the transition from AI as cognitive augmentation toward the possibility of reciprocal human–AI cognition\.
