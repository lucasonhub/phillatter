# ThreatTraceX: Architecture\-Native Attribution and Contrastive Explanations for Trustworthy Cyberattack Detection

**Autores:** Ravikiran Goturi; Soumen Chakraborty

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Chest radiography is the primary imaging modality for pulmonary disease screening, yet the limited interpretability of deep learning models remains a major barrier to clinical adoption\. This paper presents a unified framework for evaluating and improving ex\-plainability in CNN\-based chest X\-ray analysis\. We first conduct a systematic benchmark of five convolutional neural network ar\-chitectures \(DenseNet121, ResNet50, EfficientNetV2, MobileNetV3, and ConvNeXtV2\) combined with four widely used explainabil\-ity methods \(GradCAM, GradCAM\+\+, Integrated Gradients, and SmoothGrad\), evaluated with five complementary metrics span\-ning localization accuracy, attribution faithfulness, and robustness on the NIH ChestX\-ray14 dataset\. Evaluated on all 880 bounding\-box\-annotated images, the benchmark shows that explanation quality depends more on the backbone than on the attribution method: ConvNeXtV2 leads on localization while DenseNet121 of\-fers the highest explanation consistency at far lower parameter cost\. Building on this, we propose AttrNet, an explainability harness that is backbone\-agnostic by construction: it wraps a classifica\-tion network with a lightweight attribution branch that predicts class\-specific explanation maps natively in the forward pass and couples them back to the classifier through a cross\-attention gate and differentiable localization supervision, turning explainability from a post\-hoc by\-product into a trained objective\. Instantiated on DenseNet121 and evaluated on held\-out lesions under cross\-validation, AttrNet raises heatmap–lesion IoU from 0\.200 to 0\.345 and Pointing Game accuracy from 0\.333 to 0\.596 \(both 𝑝 &lt; 10−18, paired\) at a cost of 0\.011 classification AUC, an exchange favourable wherever spatial reliability governs clinical utility\. We further in\-troduce C\-GradCAM, a contrastive extension of GradCAM that suppresses activations shared with competing disease classes to produce more faithful and disease\-discriminative explanations, im\-proving attribution quality without requiring network retraining or architectural modification\. Collectively, the benchmark, Attr\-Net, and C\-GradCAM demonstrate complementary strategies for advancing transparent, trustworthy, and clinically meaningful ex\-plainable AI for pulmonary disease diagnosis\.
