# Process–microstructure–property relationships in additive manufacturing of high\-entropy alloys: recent advances, challenges and future perspectives

**Autores:** Ranjit Kumar Dehury; Vinit S\. Gupta; Yogesh G\. Joshi; Maria Dasan Thomas Victor

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

High Entropy Alloys \(HEAs\) are a new class of materials that have attracted significant attention due to their unique compositional complexity and exceptional mechanical, thermal, and functional properties\. Conventional HEA fabrication methods are often constrained by challenges in achieving compositional homogeneity, precise microstructural control, and the production of complex geometries\. In recent years, additive manufacturing \(AM\) has received great attention as a promising approach to enable HEA fabrication due to its ability to manufacture near\-net\-shape components, produce non\-equilibrium microstructure, reduce material waste, and enhance design flexibility\. This review summarises recent advances in AM\-based fabrication of HEAs and discusses the effect of feedstock characteristics, particularly powder and wire quality, on processability and final component properties\. Recent findings on microstructural evolution, phase formation, grain morphology, mechanical behaviour of additively manufactured HEAs and reinforced HEAs, and the corresponding mechanical performance are also reviewed\. Furthermore, current challenges related to defect formation, residual stresses, scalability, phase control, and process optimisation are highlighted\. Finally, the role of machine learning in alloy design and optimisation of process parameters, as well as future research directions for accelerating the development of AMed HEAs, are discussed\.
