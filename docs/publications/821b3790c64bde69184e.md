# Artificial Intelligence in Drug Discovery and Development: Current Advances and Future Perspectives

**Autores:** Kartik Deshmukh\*, Shubham Kanawade, Sakshi Dengle, Gaytri Dalvi, Sandhya Nivdunge

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Developing a new medicine takes years of research and requires many decisions along the way\. Researchers must identify a promising biological target, find or design a suitable compound, test its safety, and determine whether it benefits patients\. Artificial intelligence \(AI\) is beginning to support each of these steps\. Machine\-learning systems can analyse large biological and chemical datasets, while newer generative models can suggest molecules for further study\. AI can also help predict drug properties, identify possible new uses for existing medicines, and improve aspects of clinical\-trial planning\. These developments are encouraging, but they need to be viewed realistically\. A computer\-generated prediction is only a starting point: it must be tested in the laboratory and, ultimately, in people\. AI systems can also be affected by incomplete or biased data, may not work well on unfamiliar problems, and can be difficult to interpret\. This review discusses how AI is being used across drug discovery and development, what its current strengths and limitations are, and what will be needed for its responsible use\. The central message is that AI is most valuable when it works alongside experimental science and clinical expertise—not as a replacement for them\.
