# Corrosion and Wear Behaviour of Eggshell\-Derived CDHA\-GNP Coatings on Ti\-6Al\-4V and Ti\-6Al\-7Nb Alloys by Low\-Temperature Pack Cementation

**Autores:** Chandra Shekar Beera; Ramanaiah Nallu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Titanium alloys are widely used for orthopaedic and dental implants, yet surface wear and electrochemical degradation remain important performance concerns\. This study evaluates eggshell\-derived calcium\-deficient hydroxyapatite \(CDHA\) and 0\.5 wt\.% graphene nanoplatelet \(GNP\)\-reinforced CDHA coatings deposited on Ti\-6Al\-4V and Ti\-6Al\-7Nb by low\-temperature pack cementation at 800 °C for 2 h under argon\. Surface morphology, phase constitution, composition, Raman features, and supporting surface characteristics were examined\. Under dry sliding conditions, the CDHA\-GNP\-coated specimens exhibited lower specific wear rates than the untreated, oxidized, and CDHA\-coated specimens for both Ti\-6Al\-4V and Ti\-6Al\-7Nb alloys\. A further reduction in specific wear rate was observed under saline sliding in 0\.9 wt\.% NaCl, demonstrating the favourable wear response of the GNP\-containing coatings under both sliding environments\. SEM examination of the worn surfaces showed comparatively reduced surface damage and more compacted wear regions for the CDHA\-GNP coatings\. These observations are consistent with the lower material removal observed for the GNP\-containing coatings and indicate their improved resistance to sliding wear under both dry and saline conditions\. While the potentiodynamic polarization in 3\.5 wt\.% NaCl\. For the CDHA\-GNP condition, I\_corr decreased from 8\.822 to 5\.275 µA cm⁻² for Ti\-6Al\-4V and from 7\.974 to 4\.489 µA cm⁻² for Ti\-6Al\-7Nb, corresponding to reductions of 40\.2% and 43\.7%, respectively\. The corresponding corrosion rates decreased from 0\.0782 to 0\.0467 mm y⁻¹ and from 0\.0692 to 0\.0390 mm y⁻¹, representing reductions of 40\.3% and 43\.6%\. The results demonstrate the measured wear and electrochemical responses under the investigated laboratory conditions, while adhesion, fatigue, long\-term stability, and biological performance require further validation\.
