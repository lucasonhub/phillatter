# Students’ experiences of supplemental instruction and self\-reported academic performance in Physics within the bachelor of radiography and extended curriculum programme

**Autores:** Nape Matheko Phahlamohlaka; Vukani Siphesihle Ncwane

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Supplemental instruction \(SI\) in higher education is a co\-curricular activity that supports student learning and improves academic performance in high\-risk modules at the South African University of Technology\. However, there is limited empirical evidence regarding SI and student academic performance in modules such as Physics\. This study aimed to examine students’ satisfaction with SI facilitation, differences in self\-reported academic performance, and factors associated with performance among students participating in SI in the Physics module of the Bachelor of Radiography \(BRad\) in Diagnostics and Extended Curriculum Programme \(ECP\)\. Methods A quantitative, retrospective, observational research design comparing two sequential self\-reported assessment categories among students participating in SI was used\. A convenience sample of 183 students was approached from an eligible population of 350\. Data were collected from April to June 2025 using structured questionnaires available in paper\-based and online formats\. Assessment grades were self\-reported\. Data were analysed using descriptive and inferential statistics with a 95% confidence interval, and significance was set at p &lt; 0\.05\. The findings were interpreted through Vygotsky&\#x27;s sociocultural theory of learning\. Results Of the 183 students approached, 81\.42% responded \( n = 149\), after one age\-based exclusion, 148 were included in the analysis\. Among the 108 participants with matched Test 1 and Test 2 data, the median self\-reported grade category was one category higher in Test 2 than in Test 1 \(95% CI: 0–1; Z = −5\.941, p &lt; 0\.001\), with a large effect size \( r = 0\.57\)\. Lecture attendance was moderately associated with Test 1 grades \(rho = 0\.336, p &lt; 0\.001\) and weakly associated with Test 2 grades \(rho = 0\.239, p = 0\.012\)\. Supplemental instruction attendance showed a weak association with Test 2 grades \(rho = 0\.191, p = 0\.048\)\. Furthermore, 91 respondents \(70\.5%; 95% CI: 62\.0%–77\.8%\) agreed that the SI content was aligned with the Physics lecture material\. Conclusion This study found favourable student satisfaction with SI facilitation and positive perceptions of alignment between SI sessions and scheduled Physics lecture content\. The results support the continued provision of SI as a student\-support strategy in the Physics module for the BRad programme and ECPs at the South African University of Technology\.
