# Is China’s GDP undervalued? A digital economy model

**Autores:** Wang Weijia

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Transactions in a digital economy generate data that firms accumulate but national accounts do not price\. Within the model developed here, that omission implies measured GDP understates output\. We adapt the recursive data\-economy framework of Farboodi and Veldkamp \(2026\) to China and construct a bottom\-up measure of digital investment from the itemized asset disclosures of 5,120 listed firms\. The model does not identify a unique asset perimeter, so we carry two measurement boundaries throughout rather than designating one as the headline\. For 2024 the adjustment is 1\.60% of GDP, or 2\.16 trillion RMB, under a broad boundary that includes telecommunications network assets, and 0\.60%, or 0\.82 trillion RMB, under a narrow boundary that excludes them\. These are alternative measurement boundaries, not statistical bounds\. On listed firms alone, before any extrapolation to unlisted firms, the broad\-boundary figure is 0\.91%\. Because the knowledge stock is not identified without forecast\-error data, the broad\-boundary figure carries a calibrated scenario range of 0\.77–4\.07%, which reflects alternative calibrations rather than sampling uncertainty\. Every figure is conditional on a normalization of the mapping between measured digital investment and the value of data\. A validation audit of the classifier uncovers measurement defects that are not specific to China\.
