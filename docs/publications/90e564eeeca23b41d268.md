# Sirohi, Naipal Singh: Swayamasti: From “I Am” to “I Can Think”

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper introduces Swayamasti as a philosophical\-existential formulation centered on the primacy of being over thought: “I am; therefore, I can think\.” The formulation is proposed as a complementary reversal of René Descartes’ famous cogito ergo sum—“I think, therefore I am\.” Rather than treating thought as the starting point for establishing existence, Swayamasti begins with the immediate recognition of existence and regards thought, questioning, exploration, and knowledge as possibilities arising from that existence\. The paper does not seek to deny or establish the existence of God\. Instead, it argues that the question of God logically and experientially arises after the recognition of one’s own existence\. If God exists, human existence may be understood as participating in or emerging from a greater reality; if God does not exist, the immediate fact of one’s existence remains the starting point for inquiry\. Thus, existence is treated as epistemically prior to metaphysical interpretation\. The concept is then examined in dialogue with Western philosophy, modern scientific inquiry, consciousness studies, and Indian philosophical traditions, particularly the Vedic and Upanishadic notions of sat \(being\), ātman, and brahman\. The Upanishadic statement aham brahmāsmi \(“I am Brahman”\) is interpreted not as an initial assertion of personal divinity but as a possible culmination of an inquiry that begins with the simple recognition “I am\.” Swayamasti therefore represents an open philosophical trajectory: Being → Thinking → Questioning → Exploring → Knowing → Self\-inquiry → Possible realization of ultimate reality\.
