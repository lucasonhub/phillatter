# Where Does the AI Time Dividend Go? Generative AI, Work Intensification, and Work\-Life Balance

**Autores:** Dong Yao

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Generative artificial intelligence \(GenAI\) can accelerate knowledge\-work tasks, but task\-level efficiency does not necessarily translate into shorter working hours or improved employee welfare\. This article proposes an AI time\-dividend allocation framework that separates the creation of saved time from its subsequent use\. Drawing on the job demands–resources perspective, it examines a resource pathway through perceived time savings and work–life balance alongside a competing demand pathway through work intensification\. The reported three\-wave observational survey comprises 472 complete cases\. Standardized ordinary least squares models with HC3 robust standard errors indicate that GenAI use intensity is associated with perceived time savings \(β = \.495, p &lt; \.001\)\. Time savings is positively associated with work–life balance \(β = \.255, p &lt; \.001\), whereas work intensification is negatively associated with work–life balance \(β = −\.443, p &lt; \.001\)\. Reported bootstrap indirect effects are positive through time savings \(0\.126, 95% CI \[0\.079, 0\.176\]\) and negative through time savings and work intensification \(−0\.036, 95% CI \[−0\.059, −0\.015\]\)\. Performance pressure is positively associated with intensification, but its proposed interaction with time savings is not statistically significant\. The findings are consistent with competing allocation pathways; given the observational design, they should not be interpreted as causal effects\. The article identifies time\-dividend allocation as a consequential dimension of AI\-enabled work design\.
