# Internet\-use frequency, leisure\-time exercise, and offline social interaction among older adults in China: rural–non\-rural differences

**Autores:** Zhengyuan Xu; Doheung Lee; Changliang Yan

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Internet use may be associated with more active later life, but these associations may differ between rural and non\-rural settings and between exercise and social interaction\. We examined whether Internet\-use frequency was associated with leisure\-time physical exercise and offline interaction with friends and neighbors among older adults in China\. Methods This cross\-sectional analysis included 2,284 adults aged 60 years or older from the in\-person 2023 Chinese General Social Survey\. Weighted regression models adjusted for sociodemographic and health covariates and province; standard errors were clustered by county/district code\. Results The adjusted overall associations of Internet\-use frequency with exercise \(B = 0\.066; standardized β = 0\.062\) and offline social interaction \(B = 0\.115; standardized β = 0\.094\) were small\. The Internet\-use frequency × rural\-residence interaction for exercise was negative \[B = −0\.170, 95% CI \(−0\.256, −0\.084\), p &lt; 0\.001\]: the conditional association was positive among non\-rural respondents \(B = 0\.168\) and near zero among rural respondents \(B = −0\.002\)\. The corresponding interaction for offline social interaction was not statistically significant \(B = −0\.050, p = 0\.488\)\. Discussion Associations between Internet\-use frequency and measured offline activities varied by activity and residence\. Cross\-sectional, frequency\-based measures do not establish causal pathways or explain the contextual difference\. A weighted ordinal\-logit sensitivity analysis retained the negative exercise interaction, although its evidence was weaker than in the linear model\.
