# Niami, Morteza: The Ontology of the Unrepeatable The Principle of Ontological Non\-Repetition: Event Identity, Recurrence, and the Metaphysics of Actual Occurrence

**Autores:**

## Descrição do feed

The metaphysics of events raises a problem that is easily obscured by ordinary talk of repetition\. We say that an event occurred again, that the same event recurred, or that history repeated itself\. Yet such formulations leave open a fundamental metaphysical question: what makes two occurrences numerically distinct events, and under what conditions, if any, could one and the same event occur twice? This article develops a specialized account of event identity and numerical non\-repetition\. Its central thesis is the Principle of Ontological Non\-Repetition: no actual event can occur twice as numerically the same event\. Recurrent events are distinct actual occurrences that may be related by a shared type, structural similarity, causal pattern, description, function, or other repeatable determination\. The argument is therefore stronger than the ordinary type\-token distinction\. A type can have indefinitely many tokens, but the numerical identity of an actual occurrence cannot itself be instantiated more than once without ceasing to be numerical identity\. The article examines the distinction between numerical identity and qualitative identity, event types and event tokens, occurrence and re\-identification, recurrence and re\-instantiation, and possibility and actuality\. It then considers the implications of the thesis for major positions in analytic metaphysics, including Davidson&\#x27;s event ontology, Lewis&\#x27;s account of identity and similarity, perdurantism, endurantism, and haecceitism\. The argument does not depend on any particular theory of temporal ontology\. Perdurantism can distinguish events by temporal parts; endurantism can distinguish them by persistence conditions; haecceitism can ground primitive thisness\. None of these positions, however, entails that one and the same actual occurrence can numerically occur twice\. The article further distinguishes recurrence from re\-actualization, return from reversal, and re\-identification from repetition\. It concludes that what is repeatable in reality is not the actuality of an occurrence itself but the structures, properties, relations, descriptions, and possibilities that can be instantiated by distinct occurrences\. In this sense, the unrepeatability of actuality is not the negation of repetition but the metaphysical condition under which repetition can be intelligibly described\.
