# Data\-driven multi\-parameter collaborative optimization of microstructure and electrical performance for inkjet\-printed silver circuits

**Autores:** Hao Zhang; Zhiqiang Yan; Liangsheng Jia; Yanning Yang; Zhiqiang Yan

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Inkjet printing \(IJP\), as a rapidly advancing additive manufacturing \(AM\) technology, offers a promising approach for the conformal fabrication of circuits\. Nevertheless, the quality of circuits is heavily affected by process parameters and substantially determines the service performance of functional components\. Unlike conventional experimental trial\-and\-error methods, this work adopts data\-driven methodologies to accurately predict process parameters from measured data\. Initially, a full\-factorial experimental design was implemented to fabricate printed specimens\. Subsequently, the correlations between process parameters\-namely substrate temperature, printing speed, delay time, and number of layers and quality metrics, including line width \(LW\), line resistance \(LR\), and line thickness \(LT\), were modeled using long short\-term memory \(LSTM\), bidirectional LSTM \(BiLSTM\), and sparrow search algorithm\-enhanced BiLSTM \(SSA\-BiLSTM\) models\. The main and interactive effects of parameters on line quality were then quantified and analyzed\. Furthermore, the non\-dominated sorting genetic algorithm II \(NSGA\-II\) was utilized to identify and verify optimal parameter sets that minimize LW and LR while maintaining desirable LT performance\. The results reveal that the SSA\-BiLSTM model consistently outperforms both LSTM and BiLSTM models across all output responses, attaining the highest coefficients of determination \(R 2 \) and the lowest root mean squared errors \(RMSE\) for LW \(R 2 = 0\.9466\), LR \(R 2 = 0\.9782\), and LT \(R 2 = 0\.9287\)\. The optimized process parameters—substrate temperature of 68 ℃, printing speed of 1238 mm/min, 5 layers and an 8 ms delay time—proved to be both effective and reliable\. Overall, the proposed data\-driven framework significantly improves the manufacturing performance of IJP for microelectronic devices\.
