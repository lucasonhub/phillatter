# Design challenges and enabling technologies for ultra\-high\-speed electric drive systems

**Autores:** Yingshuai Liu; Dongdong Li; Hua Li; Xintong Li

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Ultra\-high\-speed electric drive systems are a key route toward higher power density in electric vehicles, aerospace propulsion, and industrial applications, with series\-production traction machines now exceeding 20,000 r/min and laboratory prototypes approaching or exceeding 100,000 r/min\. This mini\-review examines the design challenges and enabling technologies of ultra\-high\-speed machines, organized around five engineering questions: how high electrical frequency reshapes loss mechanisms and winding design, how centrifugal and fatigue loading constrain rotor structures, which bearing technologies remain viable at extreme speeds, how windage and cooling limit thermal management, and how power electronics and control must adapt\. Findings are synthesized from peer\-reviewed journal literature, with particular attention to retaining\-sleeve material selection, rotor stress and balancing, active magnetic bearing systems, windage loss scaling, and direct oil cooling trade\-offs\. Because rotational speed alone does not define the operational limits—tip speed, electrical frequency, bearing DN values, and rated power are equally decisive—the review frames each topic around its underlying physical constraint rather than a nominal speed threshold\. By identifying the conflicts among electromagnetic performance, mechanical integrity, and thermal stability, and by separating mature industrial practice from laboratory demonstrations, this review aims to provide a focused reference for researchers and engineers developing next\-generation high\-speed electric propulsion systems\.
