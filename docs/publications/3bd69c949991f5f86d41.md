abstract: 'The Core Emotion Framework (CEF) models human adaptation as ten functional
  affective operators distributed across Head, Heart, and Gut centers. Using a 100
  item, scenario based instrument administered via Prolific to an international sample
  (N = 826), we conducted a Phase 1 validation of the ten operator structure, focusing
  on internal consistency, factor structure, and convergent evidence from observational
  work. The scale assessed each operator across ten heterogeneous everyday scenarios
  (e.g., interpersonal conflict, crisis, opportunity evaluation, ambiguity, endings,
  feedback, novel environments, spatial navigation, delayed arrival), with items randomized
  within Google Forms and an embedded attention check; only participants who passed
  the check were retained, yielding a complete dataset with no missing responses.
  Internal consistency was high for nine of the ten operators, with Cronbach’s alpha
  coefficients ranging from good to excellent (Boosting α = 0.86, Achieving α = 0.83,
  Arranging α = 0.82, Expanding α = 0.82, Deciding α = 0.80, Calculating α = 0.78,
  Appreciating α = 0.77, Constricting α = 0.75, Accepting α = 0.74), and marginal
  but acceptable for Sensing (α = 0.69). The comparatively lower reliability of Sensing
  is theoretically expected: Sensing is a single operator whose expression is less
  explicitly represented in everyday language and whose activation frequently fuses
  with other operators in person‑specific ways. When such fusion occurs, individuals
  tend to report the dominant co‑activated operator rather than the Sensing component
  itself, producing greater contextual heterogeneity in item responses without implying
  multidimensionality. A ten factor confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) on the 100
  indicators yielded a large but interpretable chi square (χ²(4805) = 14,660.86, p
  <.001). The Root Mean Square Error of Approximation indicated excellent absolute
  fit (RMSEA = 0.0499), while incremental indices were substantially depressed (CFI
  = 0.68, TLI = 0.67, GFI = 0.59, AGFI = 0.58). This divergence is consistent with
  known behavior of CFI/TLI in high dimensional, correlated factor models: the ten
  operator structure involves 100 items, 4,805 degrees of freedom, and moderate inter
  operator correlations, and the scenario based design introduces residual covariance
  that a simple structure CFA cannot capture, inflating the baseline model and mathematically
  deflating incremental fit indices without implying structural failure. In high dimensional
  models with correlated latent variables, incremental fit indices such as CFI and
  TLI are mathematically biased downward because the baseline (null) model becomes
  artificially strong, inflating the denominator of the incremental fit calculation
  and suppressing the resulting values. Although these covariance patterns are consistent
  with the CEF prediction of operator fusion, they do not constitute direct evidence
  of fusion; rather, they indicate that a strict simple structure CFA is mathematically
  mismatched to a system in which operators are theoretically expected to share variance.
  Exploratory factor analysis (EFA) with ten extracted factors further clarified the
  latent structure. One factor (F1) formed an “executive engagement” cluster, with
  strong loadings from Deciding and Arranging and moderate contributions from Calculating,
  Achieving, and Constricting, indicating a coherent action readiness dimension rather
  than isolated center specific silos. Another factor (F6) was defined almost exclusively
  by Accepting items across scenarios, supporting a stable latent dimension consistent
  with its theoretical role as a baseline attractor, while a separate factor (F8)
  captured expansion oriented items (e.g., Expanding across scenarios), reflecting
  a relational–cognitive broadening construct. Factor correlations were moderate,
  aligning with the CEF’s prediction that operators are distinct but not independent,
  and that fusion and co activation are structural features rather than psychometric
  defects. Qualitative reflections at the end of the survey showed that participants
  experienced the instrument as mapping their own adaptive patterns under uncertainty,
  conflict, endings, and environmental change, often reporting that they became aware
  of how they “approach unknown situations,” “widen [their] scope,” or “adapt different
  aspects of [their] mental skills in different situations.” These self reports converge
  with independent observational evidence documenting repeatable signatures of operator
  dominance (chronically high, easily up scaled operators), suppression (under activated,
  difficult to increase operators), and fusion (entangled operators that cannot be
  cleanly separated in experience or language), which match the mechanical predictions
  of the CEF’s operator architecture. Taken together, the Phase 1 results support
  the ten operator structure as a coherent, functionally meaningful measurement model:
  most operators show strong internal consistency, the CFA indicates excellent absolute
  fit despite depressed incremental indices in a high dimensional, correlated system,
  and EFA reveals interpretable clusters consistent with the Head–Heart–Gut architecture
  and the roles of Deciding and Accepting. The scenario based design and the observed
  factor correlations are consistent with a structural constructivist view in which
  operators maintain identity while dynamically co activating and fusing across contexts.
  We outline implications for subsequent confirmatory work using categorical estimators
  and fusion sensitive ESEM, and invite independent researchers to reanalyze the openly
  archived data and test the CEF’s predictions about operator dominance, suppression,
  and entanglement across diverse populations and methods. Keywords Core Emotion Framework;
  somatic operators; emotional regulation; embodied cognition; neural crest development;

  operator fusion; quantum like cognition; Kuramoto oscillators; groove synchrony
  model; somatic protocols; emotional agility; detangling protocol; scenario based
  assessment; affective neuroscience; characterological rigidity; somatic strengthening.

# Bulgaria, Jamel: Quantitative Validation, Clinical Application, and Somatic Strengthening: The Psychometric and Bioenergetic Future of the Core Emotion Framework

## Descrição do feed

The Core Emotion Framework \(CEF\) models human adaptation as ten functional affective operators distributed across Head, Heart, and Gut centers\. Using a 100 item, scenario based instrument administered via Prolific to an international sample \(N = 826\), we conducted a Phase 1 validation of the ten operator structure, focusing on internal consistency, factor structure, and convergent evidence from observational work\. The scale assessed each operator across ten heterogeneous everyday scenarios \(e\.g\., interpersonal conflict, crisis, opportunity evaluation, ambiguity, endings, feedback, novel environments, spatial navigation, delayed arrival\), with items randomized within Google Forms and an embedded attention check; only participants who passed the check were retained, yielding a complete dataset with no missing responses\. Internal consistency was high for nine of the ten operators, with Cronbach’s alpha coefficients ranging from good to excellent \(Boosting α = 0\.86, Achieving α = 0\.83, Arranging α = 0\.82, Expanding α = 0\.82, Deciding α = 0\.80, Calculating α = 0\.78, Appreciating α = 0\.77, Constricting α = 0\.75, Accepting α = 0\.74\), and marginal but acceptable for Sensing \(α = 0\.69\)\. The comparatively lower reliability of Sensing is theoretically expected: Sensing is a single operator whose expression is less explicitly represented in everyday language and whose activation frequently fuses with other operators in person‑specific ways\. When such fusion occurs, individuals tend to report the dominant co‑activated operator rather than the Sensing component itself, producing greater contextual heterogeneity in item responses without implying multidimensionality\. A ten factor confirmatory factor analysis \(CFA\) on the 100 indicators yielded a large but interpretable chi square \(χ²\(4805\) = 14,660\.86, p &lt;\.001\)\. The Root Mean Square Error of Approximation indicated excellent absolute fit \(RMSEA = 0\.0499\), while incremental indices were substantially depressed \(CFI = 0\.68, TLI = 0\.67, GFI = 0\.59, AGFI = 0\.58\)\. This divergence is consistent with known behavior of CFI/TLI in high dimensional, correlated factor models: the ten operator structure involves 100 items, 4,805 degrees of freedom, and moderate inter operator correlations, and the scenario based design introduces residual covariance that a simple structure CFA cannot capture, inflating the baseline model and mathematically deflating incremental fit indices without implying structural failure\. In high dimensional models with correlated latent variables, incremental fit indices such as CFI and TLI are mathematically biased downward because the baseline \(null\) model becomes artificially strong, inflating the denominator of the incremental fit calculation and suppressing the resulting values\. Although these covariance patterns are consistent with the CEF prediction of operator fusion, they do not constitute direct evidence of fusion; rather, they indicate that a strict simple structure CFA is mathematically mismatched to a system in which operators are theoretically expected to share variance\. Exploratory factor analysis \(EFA\) with ten extracted factors further clarified the latent structure\. One factor \(F1\) formed an “executive engagement” cluster, with strong loadings from Deciding and Arranging and moderate contributions from Calculating, Achieving, and Constricting, indicating a coherent action readiness dimension rather than isolated center specific silos\. Another factor \(F6\) was defined almost exclusively by Accepting items across scenarios, supporting a stable latent dimension consistent with its theoretical role as a baseline attractor, while a separate factor \(F8\) captured expansion oriented items \(e\.g\., Expanding across scenarios\), reflecting a relational–cognitive broadening construct\. Factor correlations were moderate, aligning with the CEF’s prediction that operators are distinct but not independent, and that fusion and co activation are structural features rather than psychometric defects\. Qualitative reflections at the end of the survey showed that participants experienced the instrument as mapping their own adaptive patterns under uncertainty, conflict, endings, and environmental change, often reporting that they became aware of how they “approach unknown situations,” “widen \[their\] scope,” or “adapt different aspects of \[their\] mental skills in different situations\.” These self reports converge with independent observational evidence documenting repeatable signatures of operator dominance \(chronically high, easily up scaled operators\), suppression \(under activated, difficult to increase operators\), and fusion \(entangled operators that cannot be cleanly separated in experience or language\), which match the mechanical predictions of the CEF’s operator architecture\. Taken together, the Phase 1 results support the ten operator structure as a coherent, functionally meaningful measurement model: most operators show strong internal consistency, the CFA indicates excellent absolute fit despite depressed incremental indices in a high dimensional, correlated system, and EFA reveals interpretable clusters consistent with the Head–Heart–Gut architecture and the roles of Deciding and Accepting\. The scenario based design and the observed factor correlations are consistent with a structural constructivist view in which operators maintain identity while dynamically co activating and fusing across contexts\. We outline implications for subsequent confirmatory work using categorical estimators and fusion sensitive ESEM, and invite independent researchers to reanalyze the openly archived data and test the CEF’s predictions about operator dominance, suppression, and entanglement across diverse populations and methods\.
