# Art education participation and self\-actualization among Chinese college students: the mediating role of creative self\-efficacy and the moderating role of openness to experience

**Autores:** Kaidi Ma; Jingbo Sun

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Self\-actualization, the full use and development of one&\#x27;s talents and potentials, is a central aim of humanistic conceptions of wellbeing, and universities are increasingly expected to serve as meaningful contexts that nurture such growth\. Art education has been proposed as one vehicle for this mission, but the psychological mechanisms linking art education participation to self\-actualization remain poorly understood\. Drawing on self\-efficacy theory and self\-actualization theory, this cross\-sectional study examined whether creative self\-efficacy mediates the relationship between art education participation and self\-actualization, and whether openness to experience moderates this indirect pathway\. A total of 671 Chinese college students \(54\.8% women; M age = 19\.9 years, SD = 1\.3\) from three universities in Shandong Province completed measures of art education participation \(the self\-reported frequency of involvement in five university art domains\), creative self\-efficacy, self\-actualization, and openness to experience\. Correlational analyses showed that art education participation was positively associated with creative self\-efficacy \( r =\.52\) and self\-actualization \( r =\.38\)\. Moderated mediation analysis \(PROCESS Model 7, 5,000 bootstrap samples\) revealed that creative self\-efficacy mediated the effect of art education participation on self\-actualization \[indirect effect = 0\.172, 95% CI \(0\.132, 0\.214\), 47\.8% of the total effect\], and that openness to experience moderated the first stage of this pathway \(interaction = 0\.09, p = 0\.005\); the index of moderated mediation was significant \[0\.040, 95% CI \(0\.008, 0\.076\)\], with the indirect effect increasing from 0\.132 at low to 0\.212 at high openness\. These findings indicate that art education participation is associated with self\-actualization partly through students&\#x27; strengthened beliefs in their own creative capabilities, particularly among students high in openness to experience\. Because the design was cross\-sectional, these findings are framed as potential applications that require experimental or longitudinal evaluation rather than as established intervention guidance: universities may consider structured aesthetic education experiences as one resource for supporting students&\#x27; eudaimonic wellbeing\.
