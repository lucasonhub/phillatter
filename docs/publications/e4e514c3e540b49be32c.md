# Selective Fuzzy Bytetrack: An Association\-Level Fuzzy Enhancement For Multi\-Object Pedestrian Tracking

**Autores:** B\. B\. Aboud; Maytham Alabbas; H\. Z\. Neima

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose: This study aims to improve pedestrian identity preservation in crowded multi\-object tracking by enhancing ByteTrack&\#x27;s association stage without modifying the detector\. The focus is on reducing incorrect detection\-to\-track assignments under occlusion, overlap, and trajectory crossing\. Design/Methodology/Approach: Selective Fuzzy ByteTrack with a fixed YOLOX detector was employed\. The method processes clear associations using the original Byte Track procedure, while evaluating ambiguous matches through a Mamdani fuzzy inference system\. The fuzzy decision considers detection confidence, association cost, and ambiguity level\. The method was evaluated on the MOT17 training split with 21 sequence variants using TrackEval under the same detector and tracking threshold\. Research Limitation: The evaluation is limited to the MOT17 training split and a fixed YOLOX detector\. Additional datasets and detectors are required to confirm generalisability\. Findings: The proposed method improves HOTA, AssA, IDF1, IDP, and AssPr while reducing false positives and identity switches\. However, its conservative association strategy increases false negatives and slightly decreases MOTA\. Overall, the results indicate better identity preservation and association quality without modifying the detector\. Practical Implication: The method can be integrated into existing ByteTrack\-based systems without retraining or replacing the detector, making it suitable for crowded pedestrian\-tracking applications\. Social Implication: Improved identity consistency can support more reliable crowd\-monitoring and video\-analysis systems, provided that privacy and responsible\-use requirements are considered\. Originality/Value: The study introduces selective fuzzy reasoning specifically for ambiguous ByteTrack associations, improving identity consistency while preserving the original detection framework\.
