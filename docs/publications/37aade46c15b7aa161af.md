# Enhanced Multi\-Branch Attention Network for Toxicity Risk Prediction in Late\-Stage Drug Development

**Autores:** Izaz Ullah; Saman Fatima; Inzamam Ul Haq; Sakin Jan; Muhammad Talha

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Late\-stage toxicity can lead to substantial attrition in drug development, motivating computational methods that can prioritize compounds for further safety assessment\. Recent work such as Trialblazer has demonstrated the utility of molecular fingerprints and bioactivity\-derived features for this task\. In this study, we develop an Enhanced Multi\-Branch Attention Network that combines Morgan fingerprints, MACCS keys, and physicochemical descriptors through separate feature\-processing branches followed by attention\-based fusion\. The training procedure incorporates focal loss, label smoothing, regularization, weighted sampling, and stratified 10\-fold cross\-validation\. The reported experiments use 1,841 compounds for model development and an independent test set of 339 compounds\. The reported ensemble test performance is ROC\-AUC = 0\.910 and MCC = 0\.525, with the remaining performance measures retained exactly as obtained from the experimental implementation\. The comparison with Trialblazer is presented as a benchmark comparison, while the lower recall of the proposed model is explicitly discussed as an important trade\-off\. The results suggest that the proposed multi\-representation architecture provides a potentially useful screening aid for late\-stage toxicity prediction, while experimental validation and careful interpretation remain necessary\.
