# Flood pulse attributes of the upper Amazon River floodplains: spatiotemporal patterns and sensitivity to digital terrain model selection

**Autores:** Juan Sebastián Lozano; Ayan Santos Fleischmann; Daniel Gann; Clinton N\. Jenkins; Paulo C\. Olivas; Elizabeth P\. Anderson

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Flood extent, depth, duration, and connectivity are key attributes of the seasonal flood pulse in Amazon floodplains, but characterizing them consistently remains difficult in forested, low\-relief terrain\. We characterized these attributes across the floodplains of an \~1,800 km reach of the upper Amazon River, from the Marañón–Ucayali confluence \(Peru\) to the Japurá–Solimões confluence \(Brazil\)\. We combined radar\-altimetry water heights with three digital terrain models \(DTMs\): ANADEM, FABDEM, and MERIT DEM, to produce monthly flood layers for 2018–2023\. We then assessed how DTM selection affects flood attributes, compared our outputs with independent flood products, and described spatial and temporal patterns across the corridor\. DTM selection had little influence on upstream sections but caused large differences in central and downstream floodplains\. Maps using MERIT generally yielded greater depth and longer duration than those based on ANADEM and FABDEM, while extent differences were small and inconsistent across sections\. Pixel\-level maps further showed that depth and duration divergence among DTMs is spatially heterogeneous within sections, even where section averages appear stable\. All three DTMs agreed that maximum connectivity was larger in the central sections and smaller upstream and downstream\. Maximum flood extent matched well with a multi\-study flooding consensus, but depth comparisons against a local high\-accuracy DEM benchmark and duration comparisons with an independent hydroperiod product showed low agreement\. Averaged across DTMs, flood extent increased downstream, while depth and duration peaked in the central corridor, between the Javari and Putumayo/Içá confluences\. Compared with 2018, floods in 2019–2023 were shorter, shallower, and smaller in extent, consistent with documented hydroclimatic variability in the basin\. These results offer both an uncertainty framework for DTM\-based flood characterization in tropical floodplains and a six\-year georeferenced dataset of flood attributes along the upper Amazon, ready for use in hydrological, ecological, and conservation research with guidance on confidence and limitations\.
