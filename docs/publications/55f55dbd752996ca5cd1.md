# Public relations AI practices: Neoliberalism, field theory, and professional dispositions within an AI\-mediated habitus

**Autores:** Kyle Harris

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Public relations practitioners increasingly turn to artificial intelligence \(AI\) bots and agentic systems to enhance decision\-making, build campaigns, and support stakeholder research\. The growth in AI use, however, reflects pressures of neoliberalism influencing everyday practice\. Neoliberalism is an ideology that embodies market ideals and a sense of technological inevitability\. Within neoliberalism, practitioners may believe they are exercising a degree of autonomy in decision\-making, while actually navigating a field pre\-structured by market logic and efficiency goals\. This article leans on primary theorists Foucault and Bourdieu to explore these AI practices\. Interviews with practitioners \( N = 22 \) indicated that neoliberal patterns actively circulate within practitioner AI deployments\. They are made apparent in narratives around AI as compulsory to remain competitive, as a mode of self\-optimization, and as an efficiency and productivity multiplier\. Yet findings revealed the importance of field dispositions \(e\.g\., professional judgment, context, responsibility\) within practitioners’ neoliberal habitus\. These dispositions reflect the need for a unified approach — fusing human oversight, responsible risk management, and formalized governance — that may bolster professional work\. This article concludes that, amid the absence of regulatory frameworks, field dispositions serve an important, even necessary, role for public relations AI implementation\.
