# Multi\-source data integration and ensemble machine learning analysis of the role of Corilagin in regulating endoplasmic reticulum stress and alleviating intestinal ischemia reperfusion injury in mice

**Autores:** Zhiyang Wang; Qin Han; Lijie Pang; Siyang Zheng; Xin Wang; Bin Tian; Kun Li; Bin Li; Feng Qiu; Yunpeng Diao; Ruqiang Yuan

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Intestinal ischemia–reperfusion injury \(IIRI\) is driven by oxidative stress, excessive inflammation, Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress \(ERS\), and disruption of the intestinal epithelial barrier\. Corilagin, a natural ellagitannin with antioxidant and anti\-inflammatory activities, has shown protective effects against IIRI; however, its cross\-species and multitarget mechanisms remain unclear\. Methods This study investigated the protective mechanisms of Corilagin against IIRI by integrating Corilagin\-related targets, IIRI\-associated genes, and an ERS gene set\. Differential expression analyses of GSE37013 and GSE96733 were combined with an ensemble machine\-learning framework comprising 15 algorithms to prioritize core genes\. Molecular docking and 100\-ns all\-atom molecular dynamics simulations using the AlphaFold\-predicted full\-length human NRF2 structure were performed to assess the predicted Corilagin\-NRF2 interaction\. A mouse IIRI model was used to validate selected molecular and histological findings by histology, RT\-qPCR, Western blotting, and immunohistochemistry\. In IEC\-6 cells subjected to OGD/R, NRF2 immunofluorescence and siRNA\-mediated knockdown of Nrf2 or Abcg2 were used to further evaluate the involvement of NRF2 and ABCG2 in Corilagin\-mediated protection\. Results Intersection analysis identified 472 genes shared among Corilagin\-related targets, IIRI\-related genes, and ERS\-associated genes\. Enrichment analysis highlighted stress\- and injury\-related pathways, including PI3K\-Akt signaling\. Ensemble machine learning prioritized 12 core genes across human and mouse datasets, with ABCG2, IL10, and PRKCZ emerging as prominent candidates\. Docking and 100\-ns MD simulations supported persistent Corilagin retention within the predicted NRF2 binding region, while post\-relaxation MM/GBSA analysis indicated favorable interaction energetics\. In vivo , Corilagin pretreatment reduced inflammation and oxidative stress\-related changes, restored apical ABCG2 and tight\-junction protein expression, improved mucosal morphology, and altered CHOP expression\. In IEC\-6 cells, Nrf2 or Abcg2 knockdown weakened Corilagin\-mediated preservation of Occludin, Claudin\-1, and ZO\-1 under OGD/R, supporting NRF2–ABCG2 involvement in this protective response\. Conclusion Corilagin may alleviate IIRI by modulating ERS\-related molecular changes and preserving the intestinal epithelial barrier with NRF2 and ABCG2 contributing to a candidate regulatory module involved in this protective response\. These findings provide computational and experimental support for further investigation of Corilagin as a potential intervention for IIRI and illustrate the value of combining multi\-source data integration with ensemble machine learning to characterize the multitarget pharmacology of natural products\.
