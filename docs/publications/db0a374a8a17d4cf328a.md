# Sociology in Latin America: Institutional consolidation, enduring tensions, and emerging challenges

**Autores:** Juan Pedro Blois

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This essay examines the contemporary intellectual and institutional landscape of sociology in Latin America\. Following a period of relative consolidation, the discipline has gained greater institutional robustness across the region, though significant differences persist between and within national contexts\. Drawing on recent scholarship on national sociological fields alongside available data on programs, funding, and research output, the essay advances two main arguments\. First, Latin American sociology is better understood as a differentiated space marked by varying degrees of autonomy vis\-a\-vis global academic hierarchies, rather than as a homogeneous periphery defined by a simple North–South divide\. Second, contrary to narratives equating academic and nonacademic professionalization with depoliticization, a significant share of sociological work in the region has remained strongly attuned to social problems, thereby sustaining diverse forms of public engagement\. Looking ahead, the essay identifies three key challenges: political polarization, technological change, and the capacity to contribute to international theoretical debates\.
