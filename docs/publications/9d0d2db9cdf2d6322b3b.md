# Enhancing Long\-Tailed Macaque Pest Identification Using a Two\-Stage YOLO\-EfficientNet Pipeline

**Autores:** Hammami Rifqi Darmawan; Finki Dona Marleny; Ayu Ahadi Ningrum

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Long\-tailed macaque \(Macaca fascicularis\) infestations have reduced corn production from a potential 7–9 tons to only 4–5 tons per growing season \(±40%\), while conventional deterrence methods such as loud noise and shooting have proven ineffective\. This study developed an Internet of Things \(IoT\)\-based pest repellent system using audiosonic waves, an HC\-SR501 sensor, and an ESP32\-CAM module, incorporating YOLOv8 and EfficientNet\-B0 to replace the previously used YOLOv3 Tiny model, with the aim of improving detection accuracy and distinguishing long\-tailed macaques from humans\. Since population density at the partner&\#x27;s farmland was insufficient to construct an adequate dataset, image data were collected at Kembang Island, South Kalimantan, which serves as a natural habitat supporting a large and observable population of long\-tailed macaques, thereby enabling the acquisition of visually representative and diverse training data\. A total of 3,083 images were annotated using Label Studio under two classes \(human and long\-tailed macaque\), then divided at an 80:10:10 ratio without augmentation\. The YOLOv8n model was trained for 50 epochs, after which validation detections were cropped into 920 images to fine\-tune EfficientNet\-B0 over 10 epochs\. Evaluation results show that YOLOv8 achieved a precision of 0\.959, recall of 0\.965, mAP50 of 0\.979, and mAP50\-95 of 0\.723, while EfficientNet\-B0 reached validation accuracies of 98\.4–100% with no significant overfitting\. The two\-stage detection\-classification pipeline was successfully tested end\-to\-end on 121 field images, demonstrating substantial performance improvements over the prior model and contributing toward IoT and AI\-based smart agriculture solutions for effective, sustainable corn field protection\.
