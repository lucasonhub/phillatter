# Nutritional diversity and bioactive potential of thirteen wild edible insects in India: implications for sustainable food and nutrition security

**Autores:** Lobeno Mozhui; L\. N\. Kakati; Neha Trivedi; Aditya Kumar Tanwar; Mukesh Kumar Dhillon; Bendang Ao; Pranay Punj Pankaj; Rita Khatri Chhetri; Jasha Momo H\. Anãl

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Edible insects are increasingly recognized as sustainable alternative food resources owing to their high nutritional value, diverse bioactive compounds, and low environmental footprint\. However, comprehensive nutritional characterization of many wild edible insect species remains limited\. Therefore, this study evaluated the nutritional composition, amino acid and fatty acid profiles, bioactive properties, and multivariate nutritional relationships of 13 wild\-harvested edible insect species traditionally consumed in Nagaland, India\. Methods Proximate composition, total phenolic content, antioxidant content, amino acid composition, and fatty acid profiles were determined via standard analytical methods\. Spearman’s correlation analysis, principal component analysis \(PCA\), and chord diagram visualization were employed to examine associations among nutritional variables and species\-specific nutritional characteristics\. Results Across all 13 species, protein content ranged from 18\.90 to 63\.91%, while the lipid content varied from 4\.31 to 35\.08%\. The total phenolic content ranged from 2\.80 to 13\.89 mg GAE g −1 , and the antioxidant content ranged from 2\.01 to 45\.11 mg AAE g −1 \. Eight essential and nine nonessential amino acids were detected across all species, with leucine and proline as the predominant amino acids\. Fatty acid profiling revealed myristic, palmitic, stearic, palmitoleic, oleic, and linoleic acids as the major fatty acids, with monounsaturated fatty acids constituting the dominant lipid fraction \(46\.28–56\.90 μg 100 g −1 \), followed by saturated \(29\.69–42\.50 μg100 g −1 \) and polyunsaturated fatty acids \(0\.11–20\.12 μg 100 g −1 \)\. Strong positive Spearman’s correlations were observed between carbohydrate and phenolic content \( ρ = 0\.782\) and carbohydrate and antioxidant content \( ρ = 0\.784\)\. Phenolic and antioxidant contents were also positively correlated \( ρ = 0\.629\)\. Multivariate analyses, including principal component analysis and chord diagram visualization, revealed distinct species\-specific nutritional profiles\. Provespa barthelemyi larvae presented relatively high concentrations of essential and nonessential amino acids and oleic acid\. In contrast, Oecophylla smaragdina and Omphisa fuscidentalis were more strongly associated with elevated phenolic and antioxidant contents\. Conclusion Our findings demonstrate significant interspecific variation in proximate composition, amino acid and fatty acid profiles, phenolic and antioxidant content, underscoring the importance of species\-specific nutritional characterization when selecting edible insects for nutritional enrichment in value\-added product development and their potential contribution to sustainable food systems and future food security\.
