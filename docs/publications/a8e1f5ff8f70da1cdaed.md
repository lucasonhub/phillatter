# Estimating tree height and timber volume in the Cerrado using UAV\-based photogrammetry and digital canopy model

**Autores:** Tamilis Rocha Silva; C\. E\. L\. da Fonseca; Edson Eyji Sano; Roberto Shojirou Ogata; J\. B\. C\. Araujo; Mariane Rodrigues Ferreira; Carolina Arantes; José Felipe Ribeiro

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Aim of study: This study aimed to estimate and compare the accuracy of tree height and timber volume of four Cerrado forest tree species using a digital canopy height model \(CHM\) derived from unmanned aerial vehicles \(UAV\)\-based photogrammetric data and field height measurements\. Area of study: The research was conducted on a rural property undergoing productive recovery with native tree species, located in the Central Plateau of the Cerrado biome, Brazil\. Material and methods: A UAV equipped with a multispectral camera was used to acquire a digital CHM of the study area\. This data was then compared to field measurements of diameter at breast height \(DBH\) and total height of 143 individual trees from four species: Anadenanthera colubrina \(Vell\.\) Brenan, Astronium urundeuva \(M\. Allemão\) Engl\., Astronium fraxinifolium Schott\., and Plathymenia reticulata Benth\. Main results: The tree heights obtained from the UAV\-based multispectral camera were statistically similar to those measured manually using a graduated ruler, showing a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0\.81 and a root mean squared error \(RMSE\) of 1\.22\. The volume estimates showed an even higher correlation of 0\.97 and an RMSE of 0\.01\. Research highlights: UAV\-based photogrammetric data provides a fast and accurate method for estimating the height and volume of native Cerrado tree species, demonstrating its significant potential for application in forest inventories and management practices\.
