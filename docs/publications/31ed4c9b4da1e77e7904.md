# Beyond Compliance: A Reflexive Thematic Analysis of Healthcare Workers&\#x27; Job Satisfaction through Perceptions of Organizational Justice in an Indonesian Hospital

**Autores:** Muhammad Krisnanda Candra Mahkota; Rusmalia Dewi; Arumwardhani Nusandari

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This qualitative study explores how healthcare workers perceive organizational justice and how these perceptions shape their job satisfaction in an Indonesian hospital\. Using a reflexive thematic analysis guided by the Gioia methodology, this study examined lived experiences of fairness, leadership responsiveness, and trust in the hospital workplace through in\-depth interviews with five healthcare workers across radiology, pharmacy, medicine, and nursing\. The analysis produced five second\-order themes organized under three aggregate dimensions: perceived imbalance between contribution and outcome, erosion of organizational trust, and psychological dissociation with a recovery\-oriented orientation\. Findings indicate that uneven workload distribution, limited leadership presence during periods of strain, and repeated unmet expectations progressively erode employees&\#x27; trust, even as employees continue to perform their professional duties competently\. Despite this erosion, participants consistently expressed hope for organizational repair rather than intentions to leave, suggesting a reservoir of goodwill that hospital management can still draw upon\. The findings extend organizational justice theory by illustrating a process\-oriented, rather than static, model of justice perception in a hierarchical, collectivist healthcare setting\. This study discusses practical implications for hospital leadership, including transparent workload allocation and responsive crisis communication\. Keywords: Healthcare workers; Job satisfaction; Organizational justice; Reflexive thematic analysis; Trust
