# Fremstedal, Roe: “Kierkegaard’s ‘Naked Abstract Self’: Phenomenal, Transcendental, Narrative, or Ironic Self?”

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Kierkegaard Studies Yearbook\_ 30 \(1\):71\-95\. 2026This article engages with historical and contemporary debates on the self by discussing the “naked abstract self” in Kierkegaard’s The Sickness unto Death\. Pace Patrick Stokes, it shows that the “naked abstract self” cannot be a minimal self, which favors phenomenology over narratology\. Instead, this self requires higher\-order motives, autonomy, imagination, despair, and wantonness that stems from German Idealism and Romanticism historically\. The paper offers clear textual evidence for a Fichtean reading of the “naked abstract self,” arguing that Kierkegaard uses Jena Romanticism and Hegel to transform Fichte’s “I = I” into an ironic, aesthetic self that anticipates contemporary discussions of wantonness\. \(direct link \)
