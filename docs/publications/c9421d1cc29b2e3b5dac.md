# Governing brain\-computer interfaces as embodied autonomous neural systems: an ethical framework for neurorobotic governance in education

**Autores:** Jing Gao; Dong Lin; Jianfeng Hu

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid development of neurorobotic systems, particularly non\-invasive brain\-computer interfaces \(BCIs\) deployed in educational contexts, raises urgent ethical questions about child protection and societal impact\. Neurorobotic systems acquire biological signals through embodied sensors, process them via brain\-inspired algorithms, and actuate physical responses in closed\-loop architectures that directly affect vulnerable learners\. We propose Neuro\-Adaptive Learning Design \(NALD\) as a mechanism to translate the capabilities of such neurorobotic systems into ethical safeguards across five functional stages \(Sense, Analyze, Decide, Intervene, and Evaluate\) that characterize embodied autonomous neural systems\. Building on Ethics by Design, we introduce Governance by Design \(GbD\), incorporating ethical mandates into the technical architecture of neurorobotic systems\. The Ethical NALD \(E\-NALD\) framework maps 15 concrete safeguards onto NALD stages, grounded in the Belmont Report and operationalized through the UNESCO 2025 Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology and the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child\. By positioning educational BCIs within the broader context of neurorobotics, this study evaluates the potential societal impact of deploying embodied autonomous systems in compulsory learning environments and offers a governance framework that bridges the gap between abstract neuroethics principles and enforceable technical requirements for neurorobotic technologies\. The framework addresses the unique vulnerabilities of children as primary users of neurorobotic systems, including neurodivergent learners who may be simultaneously the most likely beneficiaries and the most at\-risk populations\.
