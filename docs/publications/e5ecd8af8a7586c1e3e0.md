# Fabbroni, Claudio: Representational Realism is not the Default Position of Cognitive Neuroscience

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Review of Philosophy and Psychology\_\. 2026Mental representations figure prominently in cognitive neuroscience, and this prominence is often taken to imply an underlying commitment to robust representational realism: the view that representations are real neural entities bearing determinate content grounded in their physical properties\. The present paper challenges this assumption on two fronts\. First, I argue that robust realist approaches face principled limitations, such as overgeneralization, and struggle to accommodate the diversity and context\-sensitivity of neuroscientific data\. Second, I claim that robust realism is neither required nor typically presupposed in scientific practice, as researchers rarely adopt such strong ontological commitments\. Moreover, I present an alternative position, representational pragmatism, which treats representations not as discrete entities but as idealized epistemic artefacts, namely modelling tools that enable intelligibility, guide experimentation, and support prediction\. I contend that this approach is more consistent with both neuroscientific practice and empirical data\. To illustrate these points, I articulate a set of widely accepted constraints on representational attribution and examine varitel semantics, a leading contemporary robust realist framework\. I argue that it fails to meet these desiderata and cannot reconcile its commitments with empirical findings\. This, I maintain, exemplifies a broader difficulty of robust realism: the fallacy of misplaced concreteness, whereby methodological abstractions are mistaken for literal features of neural architecture\. Finally, I defend representational pragmatism as a more viable and scientifically grounded stance that also fares better on representational desiderata\. I conclude that if there is a “default scientific position”, it appears more pragmatist than robust realist\. \(direct link \)
