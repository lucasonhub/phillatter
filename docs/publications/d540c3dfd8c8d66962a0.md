# Chuang, Yihsin &amp; Guest, Olivia: Beyond Empirical Success: Evaluating Theoretical Virtue Across the Computational Modeling Chain

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Computational models in psychology are typically judged by a single standard: empirical fit\. Unfortunately, fit is insufficient, even misleading as a sole criterion, as the same result can arise from fundamentally different situations: theoretically faithful models, mathematically flexible ones, miscalibrated operationalizations, or implementations that compute something other than intended\. Distinguishing between these is vital\. Our evaluative metatheoretical account recognises that models stand in three distinct relations: a\) toward the theory whose causal commitments it purports to capture, b\) toward the specification and implementation it purports to compute, and c\) toward the phenomenon it purports to explain\. Each relation can succeed or fail independently, governed by criteria local to that stage\. Importantly, relations are ordered such that theoretical content is transmitted or lost, and losses at earlier transitions are irrecoverable downstream\. What a model&\#x27;s empirical success implicates about its theoretical commitments, therefore, depends on whether transitions are secured\. Some success or failure profiles support explanatory claims; others only prediction; others establish theoretical coherence without empirical contact\. Our account diagnoses what a given model shows, replacing the implicit assumption that empirical success transfers automatically to theoretical understanding with an explicit conditional rendition of what models can and cannot establish\. \(direct link \)
