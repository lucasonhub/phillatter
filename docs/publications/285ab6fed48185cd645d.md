## Descrição do feed

\_AI and Society\_:1\-14\. forthcomingThis article provides a critical assessment of the state of the field by systematically examining the literature at the intersection of artificial intelligence and art history\. The study is based on a corpus of 180 publications obtained from 11 different academic databases and finalized through a multistage selection process\. We analyzed the corpus in terms of historical development, methodological orientations, and knowledge production patterns\. The study argues that AI\-assisted art history research cannot be explained solely by the use of technical tools; it must also be considered as socio\-technical processes in which visibility, authority, and knowledge production are reorganized\. Thus, we introduce the concept of “algorithmic canonization” that serves as an analytical framework helping to examine the effects of AI systems on art history\. The findings reveal a significant asymmetry in the examined literature\. While Asia is mostly associated with data production and technical applications, institutional structures centered in Europe and North America are more visible in terms of conceptual framework building and theoretical discussions\. On the other hand, the study argues that this situation cannot be explained solely through the West–Asia dichotomy, and that Asia harbors different centers and spheres of knowledge production\. Consequently, first, the article positions Asia not as a comparative object, but as an analytical lens that reveals the inequalities in the knowledge production order\. Second, it opens a discussion toward addressing the relationship between artificial intelligence and art history in more pluralistic and critical ways\. \(direct link \)
