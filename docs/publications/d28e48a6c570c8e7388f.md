# A personalised, occupancy\-aware recommendation system for residential shower water conservation using high\-resolution smart meter data

**Autores:** Sareh S\. Naserizadeh Ghamsari; Rodney Anthony Stewart; Hong Zhang; Khoi Anh Nguyen

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Residential water consumption represents a significant opportunity for more efficient and sustainable urban resource management, with showering accounting for the largest share of indoor household demand\. Conventional conservation strategies commonly rely on static population benchmarks that overlook household behavioural variability and demand context, leading to inefficient targeting of conservation interventions\. This study develops a personalised, data\-driven decision\-support framework that transforms high\-resolution smart\-meter data into adaptive, rule\-based water conservation recommendations\. Using 180,772 shower events from 203 Australian households, the framework integrates event\-level behavioural profiling with non\-intrusive occupancy inference, in which density\-based clustering of each household&\#x27;s shower events is mapped to a fuzzy occupancy range that preserves uncertainty rather than reducing household context to a single estimate\. Adaptive decision thresholds are constructed through a hybrid three\-layer mechanism combining household\-specific behavioural baselines, occupancy\-adjusted scaling, and a data\-derived efficiency envelope, enabling equitable recommendation triggering across households with different demand conditions\. A recommendation engine then integrates behavioural, contextual, and technical signals, complementing rather than replacing hardware\-based conservation measures such as water\-efficient showerheads\. Occupancy inference placed the verified household size within the inferred range for 90\.2% of 61 validation households\. A counterfactual simulation that assumes full compliance with every triggered recommendation indicates an analytical upper bound of approximately 21% reduction in total shower volume, concentrated in high\-consumption events while routine behaviour remains largely unchanged \(3% median duration reduction\); about two\-thirds of the simulated saving arises from flow normalisation and one\-third from duration capping\. These savings are simulated rather than observed, and field validation with real users is required before they can be read as achievable outcomes\. By integrating behavioural analytics, occupancy\-aware contextualisation, and adaptive decision logic within a unified framework, this study advances residential water conservation towards scalable, interpretable, and demand\-sensitive interventions that support cleaner production, resource efficiency, and sustainable consumption\.
