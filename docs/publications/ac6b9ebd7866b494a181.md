# A correlational study of animation narrative style preferences, viewing behavior, and audience experience

**Autores:** Guofeng Hu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction The association between animation narrative styles and the audience’s psychological experience is a critical question in media psychology\. Grounded in Uses and Gratifications \(U&amp;G\), this cross\-sectional correlational study examines how narrative preferences are associated with viewing experience\. Methods Through a survey of 615 animation viewers, we evaluated how complex/non\-linear, coherent/linear, and visual/expressive preferences correlate with cognitive engagement and flow experience\. K\-Means cluster analysis was utilized for audience segmentation\. Results Two significant “preference\-experience” pathways emerged: a positive correlation between a preference for complexity and cognitive engagement, and a strong correlation between a preference for coherence and flow experience\. Furthermore, viewers were clustered into Analytical Viewers, Casual Plot\-Followers, and Indifferent Viewers\. Cognitive engagement exhibited a statistical indirect association between complexity preference and flow experience\. Discussion These findings supply empirical evidence for understanding audience selection behaviors and offer evidence\-bounded implications for content creation and platform recommendation strategies, highlighting an eudaimonic and a hedonic gratification path\.
