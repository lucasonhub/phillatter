# The Object as a Constitutive Element in Sait Faik Abasıyanık’s Short Story “Bulamayan”

**Autores:** Ayşenur Arslan Yıldız

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Fiction criticism has changed over time\. In earlier periods, critics focused mainly on the author\. Later, they began to focus more on the text itself\. Text\-centered readings first examined the basic concepts of narratology\. After that, they started to pay attention to smaller elements inside the narrative\. One important development in contemporary literary studies is the new understanding of the “object” in fiction\. In traditional criticism, objects usually stayed in the background\. However, in recent theoretical approaches, the object can act as a “constitutive agent\.” It can start the narrative, shape the plot, and influence the fictional world\. This study analyzes the story “Bulamayan” from Son Kuşlar \(1952\) by Sait Faik Abasıyanık\. The analysis uses an object\-focused micro\-critical method\. The package shapes the plot, affects the character’s existence, and controls the rhythm of the story\. The study also examines the idea of “différance\.” For most of the story, the package remains closed\. The analysis connects this process to the “peacock” metaphor, which helps explain the moments when the narrative slows down and speeds up\. The object is also important because it shows physical control over the character and creates a strong focus in space\. At the end of the story, the package is opened, and a model boat appears\. This boat changes from a concrete object into something that exists mainly in the narrator’s mind\. This transformation reflects Sait Faik’s unique poetics\. He rebuilds the outside world through his inner perspective\. In conclusion, the study shows that the relationship between the object’s presence and its mystery divides the text both structurally and thematically\. When the object finishes its role and disappears, the narrative energy turns into a more internal and reflective process\. By focusing on the object, this study offers a different perspective from traditional thematic readings and highlights Sait Faik’s modernist narrative technique\.
