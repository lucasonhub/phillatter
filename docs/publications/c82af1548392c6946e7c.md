# Persistent regional asymmetry with apparent late\-record convergence in Eurasian\-North American snowmelt trends

**Autores:** Caleb G\. Pan; Sydney Baratta; Jorge Gonzalez; Sean Griffin; Kristofer Lasko; Jeremy Johnston; Peter B\. Kirchner; Shad O&\#x27;Neel; Jinyang Du

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Northern Hemisphere snowmelt phenology has shifted earlier over the past 37 years, but Eurasia and North America have followed markedly different trajectories\. Using a random forest framework fusing passive microwave brightness temperatures, ERA5\-Land reanalysis, and static landscape predictors, we produce annual 6\.25 km outputs of main melt onset date \(MMOD\), snow off date, and snowmelt duration \(SMD\) across the Northern Hemisphere seasonal snow domain from 1988\-2025, trained on more than 13,000 in situ observations\. Over the full record, median MMOD and snow off trends are roughly four to five times stronger in Eurasia \(\~\-2 days decade⁻¹\) than in North America \(\~\-0\.4 days decade⁻¹\), while SMD shows little systematic change in either continent, indicating that the dominant signal is a shift in timing rather than melt\-season length\. A 15\-year moving\-window analysis shows that this full\-period contrast obscures a converging trajectory: Eurasia developed a robust earlier\-melt regime by the early 2000s, while North America&\#x27;s trends were weak or even reversed in early decades before becoming more negative in recent windows\. Disaggregating by snow regime reveals that this apparent hemispheric convergence is largely an artifact of buffering within area\-dominant boreal forest and tundra regimes, where trends weaken or stabilize over time, while smaller seasonal snow regimes, including prairie, montane forest, and ephemeral snow, exhibit stronger, often asymmetric, accelerations toward earlier melt, with the most coherent advances concentrated in Eurasia\. These results indicate that hemispheric\-scale convergence in snowmelt timing masks substantial, regime\-dependent asymmetry, with direct implications for runoff timing and water availability in climatically sensitive seasonal snow environments\.
