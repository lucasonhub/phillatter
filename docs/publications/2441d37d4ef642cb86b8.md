# An IoT digital twin framework for smart waste and energy management optimazation in Maiduguri

**Autores:** Aishatu Ibrahim Birma; Abdulmuminu Shamsudeen; Raihanatu Mohammed Hamid

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid pace of urban development in cities like Maiduguri, Nigeria, brings significant operational challenges to municipal waste collection and energy distribution\. This study presents a six\-layer Digital Twin \(DT\) architectural framework designed for resource\-constrained urban environments that integrates resilient Internet of Things \(IoT\) sensing, machine learning \(ML\) forecasting, and agent\-based co\-simulation\. During a six\-month pilot deployment, data was obtained from 10 smart waste bins with ultrasonic sensors, servo actuators, and GSM modules, and 3 distribution\-node energy meters\. To predict bin fill levels and energy demand without future information data leakage, time\-series forecasting models including Long Short\-Term Memory \(LSTM\) networks, Random Forest, and XGBoost were evaluated using 5\-fold rolling\-origin cross\-validation\. The LSTM model gave the best predictive accuracy \(R²=0\.92, RMSE = 4\.8%\)\. The forecasted horizons were fed into an AnyLogic agent\-based simulation with Ant Colony Optimisation \(ACO\) for dynamic collection vehicle routing\. Across 30 stochastic simulation runs, the proposed DT framework achieved an 18\.2% pm 1\.4% reduction in total collection distance \(and 15\.4% pm 1\.1% reduction in fuel consumption\), a 41\.8% pm 3\.1% reduction in potential waste overflow events, a 10\.3% pm 0\.8% reduction in fleet CO₂ emissions, and an 11\.6% pm 1\.1% improvement in peak load\-balancing efficiency relative to the current static baseline\. The architecture uses local buffering in EEPROM to ensure data continuity even when network connectivity is intermittent\. The results provide a proof\-of\-concept for moving from reactive municipal scheduling to data\-driven decision making in infrastructural fragile environments\.
