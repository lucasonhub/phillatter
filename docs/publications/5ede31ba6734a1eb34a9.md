# A clinical prediction model for 90\-day functional dependence after minor stroke/TIA with exploratory pharmacogenetic associations: a post\-hoc analysis of the INSPIRES trial

**Autores:** Yaping Wang; Yaping Wang; Baizhu Li; Hongyi Yan; Lingling Jiang; Yuesong Pan; Yilong Wang; Yilong Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background and purpose Dual antiplatelet therapy \(DAPT\) reduces stroke recurrence after minor stroke/TIA, yet a clinically meaningful proportion of patients remain functionally dependent at 90 days\. We developed an interpretable clinical prediction model to define a high\-risk subgroup, and separately explored pharmacogenetic associations within this subgroup to generate hypotheses for precision secondary prevention\. Methods In this post\-hoc analysis of the INSPIRES trial \(4,439 patients\), LASSO logistic regression selected predictors for 90\-day functional dependence \(mRS &gt; 2\)\. Model performance was assessed by discrimination, calibration, and decision curve analysis\. In a genotyped subcohort, CYP1A2 rs762551 and POR rs1057868 were evaluated for outcome association and enrichment in the high\-risk subgroup\. Results Logistic regression achieved the highest AUC point estimate \(AUC = 0\.727\) among the four models evaluated \(AUC range: 0\.495–0\.698; 95% CIs overlapped across models\); it was selected as the final model for its interpretability and calibration, rather than for statistical superiority\. Key predictors were admission NIHSS, age &gt;65 years, and diabetes\. The highest\-risk quartile \(25% of patients\) carried a disproportionate outcome burden, with no effect modification by antiplatelet strategy \( p = 0\.793\)\. CYP1A2 CC \(23\.65% vs\. 16\.28%; Pearson χ 2 p = 0\.0031\) and POR TC \(54\.58% vs\. 46\.22%; Pearson χ 2 p = 0\.0105\) genotypes were enriched in functionally dependent patients; CYP1A2 showed no significant allele\-dosage trend \(trend p = 0\.0821\), whereas POR demonstrated a significant allele\-dosage trend \( p = 0\.0032\)\. Conclusion This clinical prediction model identified a high\-risk subgroup accounting for most poor outcomes after minor stroke/TIA\. Exploratory analyses identified CYP1A2 and POR variants enriched in this subgroup, generating pharmacogenetic hypotheses for future investigation\.
