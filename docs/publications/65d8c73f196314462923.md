# Berkla, D\.: The \_Other\_ Cut: Measuring Recognition as a Controlled Difference, in One Worked Case

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper is the third in the \*Not Less, But Other\* series and steps toward the worked demonstration left open by the preceding essay\. When one animal turns to look where another is looking, its response may track the other’s observable orientation, or it may be explained by simpler cues such as motion, salience, or proximity\. The paper states this distinction through controlled, design\-matched comparisons and reads a small set of comparative\-cognition studies through them\. In a well\-controlled chimpanzee gaze\-following case, responses continue to track a human experimenter’s orientation when simpler salience\-based explanations are constrained, and the method returns recognition at group level\. In a shelter\-dog object\-choice study, silent distal performance remains at chance once the proximity advantage is removed, and the method returns detection; a macaque study is treated as supporting background rather than as a second clean negative case\. The result is deliberately narrow\. The paper does not claim to disclose what an animal experiences, nor does it establish recognition across species\. Its contribution is to show that the structure\-first posture developed in the preceding essays can produce a positive, measured structural verdict while declining the unsupported inference from observable structure to presence\. Series note: This is the third paper in the series beginning with \*Not Less, But Other\*\.
