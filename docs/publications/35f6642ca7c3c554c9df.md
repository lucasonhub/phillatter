# Intratumoral co\-activation of ERV transcription and RNA virus\-sensing is associated with radiotherapy\-induced immune response and abscopal effect

**Autores:** Stella Logotheti; Elif Yildiz; Sarah Hasan; Viktoriia Bulanova; Elpida Theodoridou; Stefan Kuhn; Thorsten Stiewe; Stephan Marquardt; Athanasia Pavlopoulou; J\. Seco

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Radiotherapy \(RT\) transforms tumors into in situ vaccines that trigger antitumor immunity\. However, the relevant pathways remain largely unelucidated, limiting clinical translation\. Herein, we developed a hybrid dry\-lab/wet\-lab approach integrating systems\-level immune phenotypes following homogenous or spatially fractionated RT \(SFRT\) with transcriptomic profiling of irradiated tumors\. By combining feature extraction with machine\-learning, including multilayer perceptron modeling, we ranked predictors of immune infiltration and patient survivability for each RT type\. RT induced coordinated upregulation of viral RNA\-binding proteins \(OASes and RIG I\-like receptors\) along with endogenous retrovirus \(ERV\) RNAs predominately 400–800 bp long, which may serve as their ligands\. During abscopal responses, OASes and ERV transcripts were co\-induced and shared between irradiated and distant tumors\. SFRT triggered earlier activation of OAS signaling versus homogenous RT\. Our findings highlight ERVs as underappreciated contributors to RT\-induced antitumor immunity and their interactions with intratumoral cytosolic sensors as potential novel biomarkers in precision radio\-oncology\.
