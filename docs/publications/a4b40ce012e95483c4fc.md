# AI\-integrated DNA nanobiosensors for neuroinflammation: biomarker detection, intelligent analysis, and translational perspectives

**Autores:** Madhavi Porwal; Rishabha Malviya; Phool Chandra; Sathvik Belagodu Sridhar; Javedh Shareef; Tarun Wadhwa; Balamurugan Balusamy

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Neuroinflammation is significantly involved in the pathogenesis of different neurological disorders such as Alzheimer&\#x27;s, Parkinson&\#x27;s, and multiple sclerosis\. In many cases, traditional diagnostic methods are not sensitive or specific, nor capable of providing real\-time monitoring of the condition, necessitating advanced detection methods\. This review focuses on the utilisation of artificial intelligence\-driven DNA nanobiosensors for sensitive detection of neuroinflammation and therapeutic monitoring\. The literature was reviewed narratively to explore the developments in DNA nanotechnology, biosensors, and biomarkers for neuroinflammation, along with the inclusion of AI in data analysis, with a focus on aptamer\-based biosensors, DNA origami, Electrochemical and Optical biosensors, CRISPR\-based detection methods, and their combination with AI\-assisted data analysis\. It emphasised the growing biosensing technologies, such as aptamer\-based sensors, DNA origami, electrochemical and optical biosensors, and CRISPR\-based detection tools\. DNA nanobiosensors are highly specific and sensitive to neuroinflammatory biomarkers, such as cytokines and miRNAs\. Real\-time signal processing, pattern recognition, and real\-time monitoring with enhanced diagnostic power are achieved by incorporating artificial intelligence\. Nevertheless, issues such as fabrication complexity, data reliability, and clinical validation remain to be tackled\. However, implementation in routine clinical practice is hindered by fabrication complexity, data reliability, data reproducibility, and clinical validation\. In future, it is important to develop standardized manufacturing, clinically relevant validation in samples, scalable and reproducible sensor platforms, and robust AI models with appropriate external validation\.
