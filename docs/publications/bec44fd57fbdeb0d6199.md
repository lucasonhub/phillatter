# Haugaard Christensen, Sonja: The Cosmological Worldview of Einstein &amp; Whitehead \- Through the Lens of Neurotheology

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-19 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Https://Academia\.Edu/Resource/Work/179200995\_\. 2026This essay establishes a rigorous, interdisciplinary framework reconciling the cosmological worldviews of Albert Einstein and Alfred North Whitehead through the analytical prism of neurotheology\. Moving beyond the historical oversimplification that frames Einstein as a mechanical materialist, the text evaluates his perspective as a committed Spinozan pantheism anchored in the macroscopic, geometric harmony of General Relativity\. Utilizing Whitehead’s Process and Reality \(1929\) and the neurotheological foundations established in The Brain as a Transducer of Spirit \(Christensen, 2026\), the study demonstrates that the fundamental divergence between these two master thinkers lies in a clash between a static, Parmenidean cosmos and a dynamic, Heraclitean flux\. Crucially, the introduction of the classical Stoic concept of the Logos provides a non\-anthropomorphic framework for understanding physical equilibrium, wherein individual or mental misalignment results in an automated biological self\-punishment managed by the chronic hyperactivity of the brain’s ego center, the Default Mode Network \(DMN\)\. Rather than promising absolute insight into the unmeasured ninety\-five percent of the universe \(Dark Matter and Dark Energy\), this paper frames this cosmic sector as a frontier of potentiality, wherein the down\-regulation of the DMN serves as a universal biological transducer that allows human biology to interface with the living, qualitative dimensions of nature\. Ultimately, by contextualizing Einstein’s profound reverence for the historical, pulsating figure of the Nazarene against his rejection of ecclesiastical dogma, the essay demonstrates how process physics and Stoic reason gracefully unite empirical science with the energetic reality of human feeling\.
