# ASSESSMENT OF AVOIDABLE MORTALITY IN UKRAINE: ADAPTATION OF AN INTERNATIONAL METHODOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK

**Autores:** Lekhan Vn; Mykola I\. Zaiarskyi

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction\. Assessment of avoidable mortality is widely used to characterize potentially avoidable health losses and certain aspects of health system performance\.Aim\. To develop and pilot a reproducible methodology for adapting the approach of the Organisation for Economic Co\-operation and Development and European Statistical Office \(OECD/Eurostat\) to the assessment of avoidable mortality using official statistical data from Ukraine\.Materials and methods\. The OECD/Eurostat methodology \(2022 version\), including the list of preventable and amenable causes of death, corresponding ICD\-10 codes, and an age limit of 0–74 years, was used as the methodological basis\. An algorithm was developed to harmonize data from the State Statistics Service of Ukraine for 2018–2020, including classification of harmonization mapping types, direct age standardization using the OECD 2015 Standard Population, assessment of statistical uncertainty, and sensitivity and robustness analyses under the Base\-case and Extended harmonization scenarios\.Results\. Of the 93 OECD/Eurostat harmonization units, 81 showed full or practically full correspondence with the Ukrainian data, whereas 12 had methodological limitations\. In 2020, under the Base\-case scenario, the total avoidable mortality rate was 467\.1 per 100,000 population, exceeding the reference levels reported for the OECD and European Union \(EU\), but falling within the range observed in some Central and Eastern European countries\. Ischemic heart disease accounted for 50\.6% of deaths classified as total avoidable mortality in the Base\-case scenario\. For 2018–2020, the preventable\-to\-amenable mortality ratio was 1\.15 in the Base\-case scenario and 1\.38 in the Extended scenario, compared with 1\.81 in the EU\. The male\-to\-female ratio for total avoidable mortality was 2\.44 and 2\.50, respectively\. Inclusion of harmonization units with methodological limitations increased the total avoidable mortality rate by 14\.6%, with the largest increase observed for preventable mortality \(\+26\.6%\), whereas amenable mortality increased by 4\.9%\.Conclusions\. The developed algorithm yielded reproducible and epidemiologically plausible estimates of avoidable mortality, demonstrating its applicability to official Ukrainian mortality data\. Absolute estimates, particularly for preventable mortality, were sensitive to the harmonization scenario, whereas the main structural patterns and sex differences remained robust\.
