# Neo\-Banking Transformation: Institutional and Customer Readiness in Sri Lanka

**Autores:** Chameli Navodya; Methsara Hirushan; Kuvindi Jayawardane; Poornima Weerasinghe; Gayithri Niluka Kuruppu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study investigates the determinants of neo\-banking adoption in Sri Lanka by integrating the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology \(UTAUT\) with the Task\-Technology Fit \(TTF\) model while examining the moderating role of perceived risk\. A mixed\-methods design was adopted, combining qualitative interviews with senior banking and fintech professionals and a survey of 200 banking customers\. Quantitative data were analysed using PLS\-SEM, while institutional insights were analysed through thematic analysis using NVivo\. Results indicate that effort expectancy and task characteristics significantly influence behavioural intention, whereas performance expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions were insignificant\. Perceived risk emerged as the strongest predictor and moderated the relationships with effort expectancy and task characteristics\. The findings contribute to digital banking adoption literature by demonstrating how risk perceptions reshape technology adoption mechanisms in emerging economies\. Practically, the study highlights the importance of user\-centric design, regulatory clarity, and institutional readiness for successful neo\-banking implementation\.
