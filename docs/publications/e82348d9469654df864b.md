# Fixed compliance costs and producer selection in regional food production: evidence from China&\#x27;s 2006 agricultural product quality and safety law

**Autores:** Xi Feng; 毕军涛; Mengwei Yang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Food\-safety law protects consumers, but the mandatory standards, input restrictions, record\-keeping, and pre\-sale testing it imposes are largely fixed costs that fall hardest on the smallest producers\. Whether that incidence survives aggregation, that is, whether output shed by marginal producers is absorbed by survivors or genuinely lost to a region, remains unsettled\. We study China&\#x27;s 2006 Agricultural Product Quality and Safety Law, a nationally synchronous mandate, across 284 prefecture\-level cities from 2001 to 2018\. A continuous\-intensity difference\-in\-differences design links predetermined pre\-law exposure to post\-law changes in per\-capita animal\-source food output, measured at the point of production rather than consumption, treating 2006 as a transition year and 2007 as the first full year of implementation\. In the baseline specification, more exposed cities produced roughly 5% less animal\-source food after implementation, with the contraction emerging over the post\-law period\. Because exposure is built from a pre\-law outcome level, we re\-estimate the relationship on a window excluding the years used to measure exposure; the negative gradient persists\. Three findings locate the burden\. The contraction covers the activities the statute regulates, animal\-source food, grain, and primary\-sector value added alike, and is absent from secondary and tertiary activity, making a general regional decline less consistent with the observed pattern\. Within animal\-source food, it concentrates in aquaculture, the most compliance\-intensive segment\. Further, it weakens as baseline farm scale rises, a gradient that persists under province\-by\-year fixed effects and under year\-specific trends in baseline economic structure, both of which absorb the average exposure gradient entirely\. Because the design works on regional quantities rather than producer histories, it identifies output a region no longer produces rather than the departure of identified producers\. The findings are therefore consistent with uniform safety obligations having uneven production\-side incidence across regions with different producer structures, which points to shared compliance infrastructure as a way to ease fixed compliance burdens without weakening substantive safety standards\.
