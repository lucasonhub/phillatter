# The Effect of Using AI in Learning on Critical Thinking Skills Through Students’ Digital Literacy

**Autores:** Sutarsi Suhaeb; Nurfauziah; Nur Athiyyah Fadhilah

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The increasing use of generative AI in university learning has raised important questions regarding its contribution to students’ cognitive development\. Although applications such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude can facilitate personalized and efficient learning, their educational value may be constrained when students lack sufficient digital literacy\. To investigate these relationships, this study employed a quantitative cross\-sectional survey involving active university students with prior experience using generative AI for learning\. Data were collected through a structured five\-point Likert questionnaire and examined through Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling \(PLS\-SEM\)\. The analysis involved evaluating the measurement properties of the constructs, examining the structural model, and estimating both direct and mediated effects\. The findings confirmed satisfactory measurement quality for AI use, digital literacy, and critical thinking\. AI utilization was positively related to students’ critical thinking, but its direct effect was comparatively small\. A considerably stronger association was observed between AI utilization and digital literacy\. In turn, digital literacy was positively associated with critical thinking\. The mediation results further indicate that the contribution of AI utilization to critical thinking operates partly through students’ level of digital literacy, suggesting that the development of digital competencies is an important pathway connecting AI\-supported learning with higher\-order cognitive skills\. This suggests that the contribution of AI to students’ critical thinking is strengthened when students possess adequate digital literacy\. Therefore, higher education institutions should integrate digital literacy development into AI\-supported learning, particularly by strengthening students’ abilities to verify information, recognize potential algorithmic bias, and regulate their use of AI\. In this way, AI can function as a tool that supports intellectual development rather than replacing students’ critical thinking processes\.
