# Neuromorphic Spin\-Torque Nano\-Oscillators and Coupled Magnonic Networks: Nonlinear Dynamics, Frequency Entrainment, and Edge Reservoir Computing

**Autores:** Harshit Sharma, Rajnish Singh, Dr\. Vishal Shrivastava, Dr\. Vibhakar Pathak , Er\. Robil Varshney

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Edge artificial intelligence, real\-time wireless classification, and high\-frequency time\-series forecasting demand hardware architectures capable of processing complex temporal dynamics at ultra\-low power levels without conventional digital clock bottlenecks\. Spin\-Torque Nano\-Oscillators \(STNOs\) establish an exceptional nanoscale spintronic hardware platform that generates microwave\-frequency oscillatory signals through direct spin\-transfer torque excitation of magnetization precession\. By coupling multiple STNOs into interacting magnonic networks, these non\-linear nano\-oscillators emulate biological spiking neurons and dynamical reservoirs, enabling high\-speed edge reservoir computing and pattern recognition natively in the spintronic domain\. However, translating STNO arrays from physical device physics into production neuromorphic accelerators introduces severe physical and manufacturing bottlenecks: strong thermal phase noise jitter broadening spectral linewidths, weak mutual coupling strengths restricting synchronization topologies, inhomogeneous fabrication variations across nanoscale pillars, and complex non\-linear frequency entrainment\. This paper delivers an exhaustive, mathematically formal technical investigation and empirical benchmark of neuromorphic spin\-torque nano\-oscillators and coupled magnonic networks\. We formalize magnetization dynamics via the Landau\-Lifshitz\-Gilbert\-Slonczewski \(LLGS\) equation with STT active damping modulation, derive Adler equations for mutual phase synchronization and frequency entrainment, and model spin\-wave bus propagation\. Furthermore, we construct an enterprise SPICE and hardware\-in\-the\-loop testbed evaluating 64\-node STNO neuromorphic arrays across spoken digit recognition and real\-time wireless anomaly detection\. Our empirical findings demonstrate that our synchronized magnonic network achieves a 97\.4% classification accuracy on spoken digit recognition while operating at gigahertz frequencies within an ultra\-low power envelope of 42 nanowatts per oscillator—outperforming digital recurrent neural networks by over three orders of magnitude in energy efficiency\. Finally, we formulate open challenges in thermal noise suppression, all\-magnonic transistorless logic, and scalable 3D magnetic interconnect integration\.
