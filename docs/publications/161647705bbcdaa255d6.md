# Beyond single\-element toxicity: chromium\-salinity interactions, food\-chain transfer and mitigation in sugarcane agroecosystems

**Autores:** Gowtham Sornam; Sugumaran Melkumaramangalam Palani; Parameswari Ettiyagounder; Kalaiselvi Thangavel; Saravanan Naaganoor Ananthan; Elayarajan Maharajan; Selva Kumar Manikkam; Sassikumar Datchinamoorthy; Sinduja Murugaiyan; Priyatharsini Sengottaiyan

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Chromium contamination in sugarcane soils is often combined with co\-metals and salinity caused by tannery effluent, electroplating effluent, paper mill wastewater, vinasse fertigation and filter cake\. Hence, single element toxicity assessment does not provide realistic assessment of its exposure\. The present review summarizes research on chromium behaviour along the soil, root, stalk and cane juice in sugarcane\. Cr mobility depends on soil pH and redox potential \(Eh\), while salinity adds an additional control through ionic strength and chloride complexation\. Sequential extraction and speciation analysis prove that bioavailable fraction is more important for plant toxicity than total Cr concentration\. Cr\(VI\) enters roots via sulphate and phosphate transporters via mimicking oxyanions and this mechanism differs from that of other metal ions that use ion channels, a route that salt\-induced PHT1 expression could plausibly modulate, although this has not been tested in sugarcane\. In proxy species, combined Cr and salinity exposure raises reactive oxygen species production and membrane damage beyond the level induced by either stress alone, whereas in sugarcane the downregulation of PEP carboxylase, pyruvate orthophosphate dikinase and photosystem proteins has so far been documented only under salinity applied by itself\. Whether the two stresses interact on C4 carbon assimilation in cane remains untested\. Root\-to\-stalk transfer of Cr is low at most sites examined, but not at all of them, and measurable Cr reaches cane juice at concentrations that have driven hazard quotients above unity in two of the four field studies assessed\. Bagasse biochar, biochar with nZVI, halotolerant Cr\-reducing microorganisms and water management based on redox reactions all reduce Cr bioavailability, but field experiments in cane are lacking\. Factorial experiments investigating Cr behaviour depending on salinity gradient, juice level speciation and field trials in ratoons are required\.
