# Evaluating learner satisfaction and confidence in a precision agriculture MOOC: a cross\-sectional study in Palestinian higher education

**Autores:** Walid Khalilia; Hazem Horoub; Hani Iwidat; Faiz Abdelhafid; Radoslav Židek; Ľubomí­r Belej; Zuzana Palková

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Precision agriculture \(PA\) increasingly requires graduates who can work with sensor\-based, geospatial and data\-driven technologies, yet access to specialised training remains uneven in many under\-resourced higher\-education contexts\. Although massive open online courses \(MOOCs\) offer a potentially scalable response to this gap, empirical evaluation of precision\-agriculture MOOCs and of MOOC\-based learning in Palestinian higher education more broadly remains limited, so evidence on learners&\#x27; satisfaction and confidence in this specific educational context is needed\. Methods This study addresses that gap through a cross\-sectional, questionnaire\-based evaluation of learners participating in a precision agriculture MOOC developed within the BENEFIT Erasmus\+ project for Palestinian higher education; the design does not support causal inference\. A questionnaire administered following learners&\#x27; participation in the MOOC was completed by 62 of 69 invited learners \(response rate 89\.9%\)\. The course\-evaluation instrument comprised eight satisfaction items and nine confidence\-related items rated on a 0\-4 Likert scale; five negatively worded items were reverse\-scored for composite analyses\. We report descriptive statistics, one\-sample tests against the scale midpoint, a paired comparison of the two composites, and correlation analysis, with Spearman&\#x27;s ρ as a non\-parametric sensitivity check\. Results Learners reported positive overall satisfaction \(M = 2\.74, SD = 0\.57\) and positive overall confidence \(M = 2\.54, SD = 0\.40\), both significantly above the scale midpoint \(both p &lt; 0\.001, d &gt; 1\.2\)\. Agreement was strongest for perceived MOOC design quality \(82\.3%\), course enjoyment \(75\.8%\), confidence in mastering content \(75\.8%\), and confidence in passing an examination \(75\.8%\)\. Satisfaction and confidence were strongly and positively correlated \[ r = 0\.746, 95% CI \(0\.610, 0\.839\), p &lt; 0\.001; ρ = 0\.757\]; because both constructs were measured concurrently by self\-report within the same cross\-sectional survey, this association cannot establish the direction of the relationship or a causal link between the two constructs\. Satisfaction was significantly higher than confidence \(mean difference = 0\.20, p &lt; 0\.001, dz = 0\.53\)\. A single item\-perceived slide information density\-did not differ significantly from the neutral midpoint \( p = 0\.742\), and given this non\-significant result and a high proportion of neutral responses \(51\.6%\), is presented as an area that may warrant further, exploratory review rather than a confirmed design weakness\. Discussion The results suggest that a structured instructional\-design approach was associated with positive learner perceptions in this agriculture MOOC, while cognitive\-load management in slide design may merit further, cautious attention in future course iterations\.
