# A monotone redundancy\-aware decision\-burden method for explainable human oversight in human–robot collaboration

**Autores:** Seyma Yaman Kayadibi

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract This study develops a monotone pairwise redundancy\-aware Artificial Age Score \(AAS\) for event\-level decision burden in human–robot collaboration \(HRC\)\. Six normalized inputs describe safety and operational burden, with illustrative weights and conceptual overlaps\. Logarithmic evidence is aggregated with a symmetric pairwise discount\. Sufficient conditions guarantee coordinatewise monotonicity, additive contributions provide computational traceability, and an independent gate preserves detected mandatory\-review conditions\. On 13,762 held\-out non\-mandatory Sobol profiles, the method yielded a receiver operating characteristic area under the curve \(ROC AUC\) of 0\.892 \(95% bootstrap interval 0\.887\-−0\.897\) and balanced accuracy of 0\.800 \(0\.793\-−0\.806\) against a synthetic oversight policy, versus 0\.880 and 0\.788 for redundancy\-free AAS 0 \. Pairwise AAS produced no decreases in 1,179,648 coordinatewise checks, versus 3,484 for the ratio comparator\. Measurement\-error tests used 300 noise runs and 54 signed\-offset settings without threshold retuning\. In all 15 noise conditions, pairwise AAS retained higher mean score\-only balanced accuracy, whereas AAS 0 had higher mean gate\-plus\-score balanced accuracy\. Under an all\-input $$\-0\.05$$ offset, pairwise rank correlation remained 0\.9896, but 20\.97% of score decisions changed and 5\.19% of clean mandatory profiles were missed\. Thus, rank stability and non\-override do not ensure decision stability or correct sensing\. The findings establish mathematical properties and synthetic behavior, not empirical calibration, field effectiveness, or operator understanding\. Real HRC and human\-factors validation remain necessary\.
