# Physics\-informed neural operators for rapid electric field prediction in transcranial magnetic stimulation of healthy and tumor subjects

**Autores:** Philipp Moser; Fabian Greifeneder; Dominik Freinberger; Gernot Reishofer; Pia Ritter; Louis Frank; Stefan Thumfart; Sascha Freigang; K Mahdy Ali

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate and rapid prediction of transcranial magnetic stimulation \(TMS\)\-induced electric fields is essential for personalized clinical applications, from individualized therapy in major depressive disorder to presurgical functional mapping in neuro\-oncology\. Yet conventional finite element methods are often too computationally expensive for interactive use\. Existing deep learning surrogate models partly address this limitation but are largely constrained to fixed voxel\-grid representations that cannot naturally accommodate the irregular geometry of subject\-specific head meshes\. We present the first physics\-informed neural operator \(PINO\) for subject\-specific prediction of vector TMS electric fields, built on a mesh\-native dual\-encoder architecture that jointly encodes head anatomy and coil configuration\. By incorporating a curl\-based regularization term aligned with the curl\-free nature of the TMS secondary field, the PINO model promotes physical consistency as a soft regularizer alongside data\-driven supervision\. Trained on 75 subjects from the IXI dataset, the framework was evaluated across four independent held\-out test datasets, including healthy subjects from two additional MRI cohorts and, for the first time in learning\-based TMS electric\-field prediction, tumor patients\. The resolution\-agnostic formulation generalizes across subjects, coil types, and spatial resolutions without retraining, predicting vector electric fields on approximately 300,000 gray matter volume nodes in under 100 ms on a consumer\-grade GPU\. Across all test datasets within the focality\-50% region, the PINO model achieved median center\-of\-gravity errors of 0\.39–0\.43 mm, RMSE of 0\.12–0\.16 V/m, and mean angular deviations of 6\.7°–7\.3°, with performance within or below the error ranges reported by recent deep\-learning approaches\. At the selected 5% physics\-loss weighting, curl loss decreased by 9\.1% relative to the data\-only model, while RMSE and MAD increased by only 1\.6% and 1\.3%, respectively\. These results suggest that physics\-informed neural operators provide a scalable technical foundation for rapid gray\-matter electric\-field approximation, supporting future studies on real\-time electric\-field\-guided TMS workflows
