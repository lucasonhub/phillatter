# Stilwell, Phil: ✶ The Ontic Snap as a Cognitive Transition: Abductive Commitment and the Phase\-Transition Hypothesis

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

An interpretation can be available, plausible, and even more credible than its competitors without yet organizing an agent&\#x27;s experienced situation\. What changes when it begins to do so? This paper develops the ontic snap as a transition into operative commitment: a content acquires a defeasible default role in specified expectations, recognition, judgment, or conduct\. The account locates that transition within a continuing relation between inductive learning, abductive interpretation, and feedback\. Its distinctive concern is the change in an interpretation&\#x27;s functional standing, rather than the broader account of everyday world\-formation developed in the companion book\. Three claims are separated: a conceptual distinction between considering and relying on an interpretation; a psychological hypothesis that some commitments coordinate multiple downstream processes; and a stronger dynamical hypothesis that some transitions involve changes between persistent states\. Neither a categorical report nor a sudden experience establishes a neural or thermodynamic phase transition\. A minimal probabilistic formulation separates comparative support from commitment and action, while a dynamical example specifies what a phase\-transition proposal would additionally require\. Experimental findings about statistical learning, expectation, and reporting constrain the proposal without validating it as a whole\. The paper identifies competing models, measurement problems, and outcomes that would count against its empirical extensions\. Its contribution is a disciplined account of what must be explained when a possibility becomes a working premise about the world\. \(direct link \)
