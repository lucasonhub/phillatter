# Case\-level audit of thyroid ultrasound and cytology deep learning: label conflicts, split design, and shortcut\-sensitive performance in a public archive

**Autores:** Behnam Kiani Kalejahi; Mohammad Javad Rajabi

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The measured performance of thyroid imaging artificial intelligence depends on how the analytical population is assembled, how images are partitioned, whether non\-anatomical image content is informative, and which reference labels are used\. We audited a public multimodal thyroid archive at case level and examined what its reported performance does and does not demonstrate\. The released archive of Pang et al\. \(387 ultrasound images; 1,332 fine\-needle aspiration cytology blocks\) was audited for exact and near\-duplicate images and for contradictory labels, and a frozen case\-level partition was built\. Five ImageNet\-initialised backbones and their mean\-probability ensemble were evaluated by group\-stratified five\-fold cross\-validation and on the frozen test set, with thresholds selected on validation data and case\-clustered 95% confidence intervals\. Revision\-stage control analyses comprised an identical\-code comparison of image\-level and case\-grouped folds; retraining with a heuristically detected sonographic sector or its surround removed, for all five backbones and the ensemble; and a metadata\-only model without a convolutional network\. Frozen ultrasound models were then reassessed on TN3K, against its distributed benign/malignant labels, and on DDTI, for concordance with TI\-RADS categories; both datasets had been accessed during earlier analytical development\. Nine ultrasound images appeared under both benign and papillary carcinoma labels \(19 files\) and were excluded\. Under case\-level cross\-validation, the cytology ensemble reached an AUROC of 0\.958 \(95% CI 0\.938–0\.975\) and the ultrasound ensemble 0\.768 \(0\.716–0\.819\)\. Image\-level folds, despite 99\.8% within\-fold case contamination, changed AUROC by −0\.002 \(−0\.021 to 0\.019\) and \+0\.019 \(0\.008 to 0\.032\) for the two tested backbones\. After removal of the detected sonographic sector, the ultrasound ensemble retained an AUROC of 0\.690 \(0\.630–0\.747\), a paired change of −0\.078 \(−0\.148 to −0\.013\); with only the detected sector retained, it reached 0\.760 \(paired change −0\.008, −0\.054 to 0\.039\)\. Sector detection fell back to a fixed central box in 24\.2% of frames\. A metadata\-only model using frame geometry, global intensity and border statistics reached 0\.749 \(0\.692–0\.804\) for ultrasound\. In post\-exposure reassessment, the ensemble reached 0\.700 \(0\.661–0\.741; image\-level interval\) on TN3K and over\-predicted malignancy \(calibration\-in\-the\-large −0\.49\), and showed no concordance with DDTI TI\-RADS grouping \(0\.518, 0\.436–0\.605\)\. This case\-level audit of a public thyroid archive showed contradictory labels, partition\-sensitive evaluation, and substantial discriminative signal both after removal of a heuristically detected sonographic region and in acquisition metadata alone, so performance on this archive cannot be interpreted as exclusively lesion\-based\. Post\-exposure reassessment on external datasets showed limited and endpoint\-dependent transport of the internal models\.
