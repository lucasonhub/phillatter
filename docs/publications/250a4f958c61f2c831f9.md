# Case Report: Intentional massive melatonin ingestion \(450 mg\) in an adolescent female

**Autores:** Sepehr Miran; Bahar Amiri; Peyman Astaraki; Arian Karimi Rouzbahani

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Over\-the\-counter \(OTC\) melatonin is widely perceived as benign, yet its unregulated, high\-dose formulations create an accessible means of self\-harm for adolescents, and reports of intentional overdose have risen alongside a well\-documented surge in pediatric exposures\. We report a 16\-year\-old female who intentionally ingested 450 mg of melatonin \(90 × 5 mg tablets\), a 90\-fold supratherapeutic dose, and synthesize this case against the published literature on melatonin overdose, suicidality, and toxicokinetics\. She presented 2\.5 h post\-ingestion with marked somnolence \(Glasgow Coma Scale 13/15\) and heart rate of 62 beats/min\. Venous blood gas showed a mild, respiratory\-compensated metabolic acidosis \(pH 7\.34, HCO3 15\.6 mmol/L\), with mild hyponatremia and stress leukocytosis\. Hepatic, renal, and coagulation studies and a 12\-lead electrocardiogram were normal, and a comprehensive toxicology screen was negative, consistent with an isolated exposure \(Poison Severity Score Grade 1, Minor\)\. She was medically cleared within 14\.5 h of ingestion but, given persistent suicidal ideation on psychiatric evaluation, was transferred to inpatient psychiatric care at 20\.5 h post\-ingestion\. This case, together with a review of published case reports, cohort data, and toxicokinetic evidence, indicates that massive isolated melatonin ingestion typically produces self\-limited sedation, consistent with saturable hepatic CYP1A2\-mediated clearance, alongside mild, transient metabolic derangement without organ injury\. Population\-level and case\-level evidence converge, however, on a critical message for clinicians: low physiological toxicity does not indicate low psychiatric risk, and every adolescent presenting after intentional OTC chronobiotic ingestion warrants formal psychiatric evaluation regardless of how benign the medical course appears\.
