# Comparative efficacy of different mouthrinses on oral hygiene and gingival health in patients undergoing fixed orthodontic therapy: A non\-randomized comparative clinical study

**Autores:** Dr Karuna Najiar; Karamdeep Singh Ahluwalia; Vijay Agarwal; Hari Narayan Choudhary; Harsheeta Narhari Trivedi

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Fixed orthodontic appliances make oral hygiene maintenance challenging, as brackets, bands, and wires create plaque\-retentive areas that promote gingival inflammation, enamel demineralization, and other periodontal problems\. Adjunctive chemotherapeutic mouthwashes are commonly recommended to help control plaque during orthodontic treatment\.Aim and Objectives: To compare the efficacy of chlorhexidine versus fluoride mouthwash on periodontal status and extrinsic staining in orthodontic patients\.Materials and Methods: Sixty patients \(aged 15–35 years\) were allocated, using a non\-random clinical allocation procedure to two groups \(n = 30 each\): Group I received chlorhexidine mouthwash and Group II received fluoride mouthwash, both used once nightly for 21 days\. Institutional Ethics Committee approval was obtained\. Plaque, gingival, and stain indices were recorded at baseline \(T0\), day 10 \(T1\), and day 21 \(T2\)\. Intragroup and intergroup comparisons used paired and independent t\-tests \(p &lt; 0\.05\)\.Results: Both chlorhexidine and fluoride mouthwashes showed significant reductions in plaque accumulation and gingival inflammation over time \(p = 0\.001\)\. From T0 to T2, plaque scores decreased by 50\.4% with chlorhexidine and 41\.6% with fluoride, with a significant intergroup difference \(p = 0\.008\)\. Gingival scores decreased by 61\.3% and 50\.3%, respectively \(p = 0\.001\)\. In contrast, stain scores increased by 60\.1% with chlorhexidine and decreased by 24\.7% with fluoride \(p = 0\.001\)\.Conclusion: Both mouthwashes improved plaque and gingival indices over 21 days\. Chlorhexidine showed greater reductions in plaque and gingival scores but was associated with increased extrinsic staining, whereas fluoride showed a more favorable staining profile\. These findings should be interpreted in view of the non\-randomized design, absence of a formal sample\-size calculation, and the short follow\-up period\.
