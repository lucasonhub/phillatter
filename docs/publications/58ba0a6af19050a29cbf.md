# Surviving the Storm: A Qualitative Study of Caregivers&\#x27; Challenges in Family‐Based Treatment for Adolescent Anorexia Nervosa

**Autores:** Natasha Allen; Amy Kercher; Liesje Donkin

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OBJECTIVE: Family\-based treatment \(FBT\) is the recommended first\-line outpatient treatment for adolescent anorexia nervosa \(AN\) in Aotearoa New Zealand \(NZ\), but places substantial responsibility on parents/caregivers\. Although caregiver burden in FBT is increasingly recognized internationally, in\-depth qualitative work situated in the NZ service context is lacking\. This study explored how caregivers experienced participating in FBT for adolescent AN in NZ\. METHOD: Twelve caregivers participated in semi\-structured interviews between May and July 2025\. Interviews were analyzed within an interpretive description framework using reflexive thematic analysis\. RESULTS: Three themes were developed: confronting a life\-threatening illness within an under\-resourced and often unresponsive healthcare system; implementing an overwhelming, isolating treatment with limited guidance; and living with the lingering psychological toll long after FBT concluded\. Caregivers described experiences relating to caring for someone with AN and supporting them through treatment as consistent with trauma\-implicated stressors, although traumatic stress was not formally assessed\. DISCUSSION: These findings show how systemic, structural, and relational factors intersect to shape caregivers&\#x27; experiences and highlight the need for better\-resourced, trauma\-informed adaptations of FBT that position caregivers and young people as co\-central partners in care\.
