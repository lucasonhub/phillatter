# Emojis as socio\-emotional cues in EFL learning: a narrative conceptual review of affect, relatedness, and well\-being

**Autores:** Shamim Akhter; Abdulrazaq Alzahrani; Rabindra Dev Prasad Prasad; Musarat Shaheen

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Emojis can modify the emotional and relational interpretation of digital messages, but their contribution to learner well\-being in English as a Foreign Language \(EFL\) education remains uncertain\. This narrative conceptual review brings together purposively selected research on emojis, educational communication, and language learner emotions\. It distinguishes direct emoji evidence in EFL from emoji research in other settings, earlier emoticon studies, and contextual evidence on digital learning without an isolated emoji component\. Self\-determination theory provides the principal account of how perceived support might influence relatedness and motivation; digital semiotics explains cue interpretation, while broaden\-and\-build theory and a multidimensional view of well\-being delimit possible affective processes and distal outcomes\. The proposed model connects an emoji and its accompanying text to appraisals of communicative intent, warmth, appropriateness, and competence\. These appraisals may influence affect and relatedness, with subsequent implications for willingness to communicate and engagement\. Culture, familiarity, message purpose, sender identity, and relationship history condition these links\. Selected studies support specific effects on interpretation, impressions, self\-efficacy, or immediate task performance; they do not establish that emojis produce enduring well\-being or resilience in EFL learners\. The review develops testable propositions, considers adverse and null effects, and offers context\-sensitive pedagogical implications\. Its contribution is a bounded account of how small instructional cues might matter and what evidence is required to establish their consequences\.
