# Home\-based telerehabilitation after CABG: higher objectively verified adherence and 12\-week functional outcomes in a single\-center observational cohort

**Autores:** Yan Liu; Xugang Wang; Cuihua Wang; Minjuan Zhang; Manman Kong; Meng Wang; Jingjing Ma; 劉慧俐

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Home\-based telerehabilitation \(HBTCR\) may address access barriers to center\-based cardiac rehabilitation \(CBCR\) after coronary artery bypass grafting \(CABG\)\. However, real\-world comparative evidence on objectively verified adherence and functional recovery, with careful confounding control, remains sparse\. This observational cohort study compared session completion and 12\-week functional outcomes between HBTCR and CBCR in post\-CABG patients using routine clinical data\. Methods This single\-center observational cohort screened 288 patients and identified 195 potentially eligible post\-CABG patients \(February 2024–February 2025\)\. After excluding 28 patients with unresolved rehabilitation exposure, 167 patients \(56 HBTCR, 54 CBCR, 57 usual care\) formed the propensity\-score estimation cohort, adjusted for 25 baseline covariates with generalized propensity scores and overlap weighting\. The primary complete\-case analysis included 105 patients \(35 per group\) with complete 12\-week follow\-up\. The primary outcome was objectively verified session completion \(% of 36 prescribed sessions\)\. The key clinical outcome was 12\-week VO 2 peak, analyzed via weighted ANCOVA\. Results are reported as adjusted differences with 95% CIs\. Results After weighting, covariates were well balanced \(all SMDs &lt;0\.10\)\. HBTCR showed significantly higher objectively verified session completion than CBCR \(87\.7% vs\. 78\.5%; overlap\-weighted difference 9\.1 percentage points, 95% CI: 4\.4 to 13\.7; P &lt; 0\.001\)\. The adjusted HBTCR–CBCR difference in 12\-week VO 2 peak was small and not statistically significant \(0\.17 mL/kg/min, 95% CI: −0\.50 to 0\.84; P = 0\.616\)\. Both active rehabilitation groups showed substantially larger 12\-week VO 2 peak gains than usual care \(adjusted differences &gt;4\.0 mL/kg/min\)\. Conclusion Among post\-CABG patients who chose HBTCR or CBCR in routine practice, those choosing HBTCR had higher objectively verified session completion and comparable observed 12\-week improvement in VO 2 peak\. These findings support HBTCR as a feasible option for patients who prefer or require home\-based rehabilitation because of work, travel, or scheduling constraints\. Formal equivalence was not tested, and the clinical meaningfulness and cost\-effectiveness of this delivery model warrant further evaluation\.
