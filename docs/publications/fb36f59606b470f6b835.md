# 1\-Methylhistidine attenuates early myocardial inflammation in type 3 cardiorenal syndrome through modulation of the p105/p50 NF\-κB axis

**Autores:** Jiakun Zhang; Qian Wang; Xingyi Tu; Han Zhou; Shihui Wang; 李晶洁

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Type 3 cardiorenal syndrome \(CRS3\) is characterized by cardiac injury or dysfunction secondary to acute kidney injury \(AKI\), yet the contribution of AKI\-associated metabolic remodeling to early myocardial inflammation remains poorly understood\. Here, paired serum and myocardial untargeted metabolomics identified 1\-methylhistidine \(1\-MH\) as a metabolite elevated in both compartments, with the highest variable importance in projection score in serum\. Exogenous 1\-MH improved the E/A ratio and attenuated myocardial inflammation and terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick\-end labeling \(TUNEL\) positivity in rats with CRS3\. Similar protective effects were observed in a separate post\-ischemic cohort in which 1\-MH was administered immediately after renal reperfusion\. In CRS serum\-exposed H9c2 cells, 1\-MH reduced reactive oxygen species\-associated fluorescence, malondialdehyde content, and intracellular Fe 2\+ levels\. Transcriptomic profiling showed that 1\-MH suppressed inflammatory signaling, accompanied by changes in oxidative metabolism\. Integration of these transcriptomic signatures with target prediction prioritized NFKB1/p105, while ensemble docking identified recurrent candidate interaction regions within p105\. Cellular thermal shift assays showed a concentration\-related increase in p105 thermal stability following 1\-MH treatment\. 1\-MH increased p105 abundance while reducing total p50 abundance and nuclear p50 accumulation\. p105 overexpression increased p50 abundance and the expression of inflammatory transcripts and proteins suppressed by 1\-MH\. These findings demonstrate that 1\-MH attenuates early myocardial inflammation in CRS3 and reduces oxidative stress\-related changes in CRS serum\-exposed H9c2 cells\. These functional results support modulation of the p105/p50 NF\-κB axis and identify p105 as a molecular link between AKI\-associated metabolic remodeling and myocardial inflammatory signaling\.
