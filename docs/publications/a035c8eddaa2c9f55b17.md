# AI Literacy, Attitudes and Perceived Technological Competence as Correlates of Pre\-Service Teachers’ Beliefs About the Professional Relevance of AI: A Latent Mediation Analysis for Sustainable Teacher Education

**Autores:** Mesut Tabuk; Barış Çetin

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Preparing pre\-service teachers to use artificial intelligence \(AI\) critically and equitably is part of the quality\-education agenda of Sustainable Development Goal 4\. Within a framework informed by the theory of planned behaviour, this study examined how AI literacy is associated with pre\-service teachers’ belief that AI will play an important role in the teaching profession, and whether a gender difference in this belief runs through attitudes and perceived technological competence\. Data from 493 pre\-service teachers at one Turkish faculty were analysed with a latent structural equation model for ordinal indicators\. Within this model, the association between self\-reported AI literacy competence and the belief ran predominantly through positive attitude \(indirect β = 0\.20\) and secondarily through perceived competence \(β = 0\.07\)\. Ethical understanding was associated with stronger concerns about AI \(β = 0\.30\)\. Women held a weaker belief than men \(standardised difference = −0\.28\), a difference statistically accounted for mainly by lower positive attitude, with weaker evidence for perceived competence\. Coefficients were stable across specifications\. Because the design is cross\-sectional and single\-institution, the associations are not causal and require replication; they suggest that equitable AI preparation may be better targeted at positive experience with AI and perceived competence than at demographic categories\.
