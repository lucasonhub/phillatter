# A Classroom Intervention Study to Assess the Potential of a 360\-Degree Virtual Reality Learning Environment to Enhance Conceptual Understanding and Engagement in Physics

**Autores:** Eliane Merki; Andreas Lichtenberger; Volker Eisenlauer; A\. Vaterlaus; Sarah Isabelle Hofer

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract The use of individual student laptops and tablets in K\-12 classrooms enables large\-scale implementation of innovative learning technologies\. Theories of multimedia and immersive learning suggest that immersive features can influence cognitive and affective learning processes\. Therefore, 360\-degree virtual reality learning environments \(VRLEs\) represent a promising area for further investigation\. This study compares an interactive desktop\-based low\-immersive 360\-degree VRLE with a traditional paper\-and\-pencil approach to investigate effects on students’ conceptual understanding of Galilean transformations of uniform motion in position\-time graphs and on student engagement\. Both conditions worked with multiple representations within a Predict\-Observe\-Explain framework to support active learning\. A total of 313 students \(176 female, 137 male\) from the advanced track of upper secondary school participated in the pre\-post classroom study\. Conceptual understanding was assessed using the Inventory for Galilean Transformation of Uniform Motion in Position\-Time Graphs\. Students in both conditions improved their understanding, but no significant difference between conditions was observed\. However, students using the VRLE reported a stronger inclination to perform well\. These findings suggest that 360\-degree VRLEs may support certain aspects of self\-reported student engagement related to effort, but further research is needed to better understand how learning outcomes can be improved\.
