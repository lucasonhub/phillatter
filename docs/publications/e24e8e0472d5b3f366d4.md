# Recent advances in hybrid treatment technologies for pharmaceutical removal from complex water matrices

**Autores:** Ana Hayat; José L\.S\. Duarte; Carmen M\. Domínguez; Aurora Santos; Salvador Cotillas

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The widespread occurrence of pharmaceuticals in aquatic environments poses significant environmental and health concerns, largely due to their persistence and the limited removal efficiency of conventional wastewater treatment systems\. This review critically examines advanced oxidation processes \(AOPs\) and their hybrid configurations for the removal of pharmaceuticals from complex water matrices, including municipal, hospital, and pharmaceutical industry effluents\. Conventional AOPs, such as ozonation, Fenton, photocatalysis, and electrochemical oxidation, exhibit high degradation potential but are constrained by operational limitations and matrix effects\. Hybrid systems that combine multiple AOPs or integrate them with biological, membrane, or adsorption processes demonstrate enhanced performance through synergistic mechanisms\. A targeted mapping of the recent literature is also used to contextualize the growth of integrated treatment approaches and the uneven distribution of research among wastewater matrices\. Despite promising results, challenges related to scalability, cost, and environmental sustainability persist\. Future research should prioritize validating with real wastewater, ensuring long\-term operational stability, and conducting comprehensive techno\-economic and environmental assessments to enable the practical implementation of hybrid technologies\.
