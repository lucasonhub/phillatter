# Johnson, Jamison Todd: Jays and Photons \- A Hypothetical Consideration of the Presence of Light

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This is a small question, asked inside my Coherent Existence framework: What is light to the Jays, the moments of experience a life is made of? Does light carry a Trace between perspectives, or is it one itself? My functional writes each perspective as an intensity and a facing, a size that holds and a phase that turns, and classical optics writes a light field the same way\. Three plain facts of physics rhyme with what the framework says\. The absolute phase of a single field cannot be observed, while the relative phase between two fields is physical and does the work of interference\. A resonant cavity keeps only the modes that come back into agreement with themselves\. And the living body gives off an ultraweak light that is measured, real, and metabolic\. I set out three readings of that rhyme and weigh them\. The humble reading takes light as a channel between perspectives and asks almost nothing physics has not granted\. The heavy reading says the Jays are light, and I decline it: it inherits the decoherence objection, which the replies from quantum theories of mind have not settled, and biophoton coherence is unproven\. The third reading, the one I defend, sits between them\. At human scale the senses register almost everything by way of the electromagnetic field and carry it to the nerve by that field, and the body doing the collecting is held together, atom to atom, by the same field\. So “light observing light” is earned as a statement about the physics of perception, taking light to mean the field it is a ripple in, with no claim that consciousness is light\. The series’ proposed thesis, one consciousness of which every mind is a perspective, is named and kept apart from it\. I keep the seam between physics and my reading visible, and I end with what survives it: a resemblance, offered as a rhyme and not a proof\. \(direct link \)
