# Real\-world clinical outcomes under intravenous and subcutaneous esketamine protocols for treatment\-resistant depression: a multicenter, retrospective study

**Autores:** Gilberto Sousa Alves; Humberto M\. M\. Dos Santos; Samira D Damasceno; LAURA MARIANE SILVA MACIEL; Otavio Andre Farias Sousa da Silva; Ciro Sousa de Moura Fe; Jilmario Gomes de Santana; Mariélia Barbosa Leal de Freitas; André Férrer Carvalho; Dennison Monteiro

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Esketamine is a promising therapeutic option for treatment\-resistant depression \(TRD\)\. Intravenous \(IV\) administration is the most extensively studied parenteral route\. However, subcutaneous \(SC\) administration may offer simpler, more scalable delivery\. Multicenter real\-world evidence comparing IV and SC routes of administration remains limited\. Objective To report clinical outcomes and tolerability of parenteral esketamine protocols for TRD at three Brazilian advanced\-care centers\. Methods This multicenter retrospective chart review study \(2023–2025\) included 72 outpatients aged 18–65 years who received esketamine 2 to 3 times weekly via IV \(n = 26; 6 sessions\) or SC \(n = 46; 12 sessions\) administration\. Data were analyzed in RStudio 2026\.01\.0 Build 392 using R 4\.5\.2\. The primary outcome was categorical response on the Beck Depression Inventory\-II \(BDI\-II\) at the shared 6\-session milestone and at each protocol\-specific endpoint\. Secondary outcomes included continuous changes in BDI\-II and Beck Anxiety Inventory \(BAI\) scores in the full sample, tolerability and documented adverse events, and exploratory factor analysis of baseline symptom dimensions\. Results After six sessions, BDI\-II response distribution favored the IV protocol, although the between\-group difference was not statistically significant \(Pearson’s chi\-square = 5\.661; p = 0\.059; odds ratio, 3\.06; 95% CI, 1\.22–8\.00\)\. At the protocol\-specific endpoints, categorical BDI\-II response did not differ significantly between groups \(Pearson’s chi\-square = 1\.227; p = 0\.541; odds ratio, 1\.44; 95% CI, 0\.57–3\.73\)\. No serious adverse events or adverse\-event\-related discontinuations were reported; dissociation was more frequent with IV than with SC treatment \(53\.8% vs\. 13\.0%; p = 0\.001\), whereas nausea and mild transient blood pressure increases did not differ significantly\. BDI\-II factor analysis identified cognitive\-affective and somatic\-neurovegetative dimensions\. Conclusions The IV protocol showed a more favorable BDI\-II response distribution at the shared 6\-session milestone, whereas SC esketamine achieved statistically comparable categorical outcomes at its 12\-session protocol\-specific endpoint\. Route selection should consider speed of response, tolerability, dosing, operational complexity, and access\. SC esketamine may be a feasible option where simplicity, tolerability, and access are priorities\. Results should be interpreted with caution because of the retrospective, observational study design\.
