# Automated detection of inflammation in foot thermographic images of patients with diabetes acquired in an uncontrolled clinical environment

**Autores:** Riki Wartakusumah; Hiroshi Noguchi; Saldy Yusuf; Kanae Mukai; Mayumi Okuwa; Makoto Oe

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

AIMS: Quantitative methods for detecting diabetic foot inflammation remain limited, though localized skin\-temperature elevation is a promising early indicator\. Because background variability can hinder AI\-based foot identification, we evaluated AI\-driven thermal imaging for diabetic foot inflammation under uncontrolled clinical backgrounds and assessed whether curtain\-based background control and grayscale conversion improved detection\. METHODS: Cross\-sectional study design was conducted in Makassar, Indonesia, 100 plantar thermal images from 50 diabetic patients were acquired with and without a curtain, preprocessed \(sensitivity, 1\.5; emissivity, 0\.98\), and converted to grayscale\. Expert\-identified inflammatory hotspots were annotated and used to train a YOLO11n model with 5\-fold cross\-validation, evaluated using accuracy, precision, sensitivity, specificity, ROC\-AUC, IoU, and case\-error analysis\. RESULTS: With a curtain, color\-map images achieved sensitivity of 0\.773, versus 0\.455 without one\. Grayscale images performed consistently across conditions, with best results in the mixed dataset \(accuracy, 0\.980; precision, 0\.976; sensitivity, 0\.932; specificity, 0\.994; AUC 0\.966 vs\. 0\.886 for color\-map\)\. Correctly detected cases showed IoU &gt; 0\.9\. Low thermal contrast and small region of interest were the most frequent failure categories\. CONCLUSIONS: AI\-driven thermal imaging detects diabetic foot inflammation under uncontrolled conditions\. A curtain improved color\-map detection, but grayscale imaging provided robust, curtain\-independent performance, supporting its use for clinical screening\.
