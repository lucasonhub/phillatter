# A hybrid machine learning–physics framework for predicting electronic properties of Mg\-doped bulk and two\-dimensional ZnO in data\-sparse regimes

**Autores:** H S Dibbo; M\.A\.I\. Nahid

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Predicting composition\-dependent properties of doped semiconductors remains challenging due to data scarcity and the band\-gap underestimation associated with standard density functional theory \(DFT\)\. In this work, a hybrid machine\-learning \(ML\) and physics\-informed framework is developed for Mg\-doped bulk and two\-dimensional ZnO\. A Gradient Boosting Regressor is used for band\-gap prediction, and an empirical post\-processing calibration of approximately 1\.4 eV is applied to bring the PBE\-derived predictions closer to the experimental band\-gap scale\. Particular attention is given to the low\-doping regime, where training data are sparse\. Composition\-dependent bowing coefficients were first calculated using the ZnO and MgO band\-gap endpoints and the corresponding alloy band gaps\. A quadratic fit was then extrapolated into the low\-doping region, yielding a representative bowing coefficient of approximately 4\.9 eV around 5 at% Mg\. This value was held fixed for the final 0–12 at% Mg band\-gap calculations and combined with the calibrated ML output through a composition\-dependent weighting scheme\. Without bowing correction, the calibrated ML predictions show a slight band\-gap increase in bulk ZnO, whereas the band gap of two\-dimensional ZnO remains nearly constant at low Mg concentrations before decreasing at higher concentrations\. After applying the bowing\-based correction, the low\-doping predictions show a clearer band\-gap increase and reasonable agreement with reported experimental values\. Formation energy exhibits a non\-monotonic dependence on Mg concentration\. The proposed framework provides a computationally efficient approach for composition\-dependent prediction in data\-limited doped semiconductors\.
