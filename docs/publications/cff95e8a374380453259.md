# Right Answers, Wrong Reasons: Perception Errors in Multimodal Chain\-of\-Thought and the Blind Spot of Outcome\-Based Rewards

**Autores:** Haosong Hu

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Multimodal large language models increasingly answer visual questions through explicit reasoning chains, and the chains are typically fluent\. Evidence shows they can nonetheless be perceptually wrong—misreading values off a chart&\#x27;s axis, miscounting geometric primitives, or citing objects absent from the image—while the final answer lands correct anyway\. This survey organizes that evidence around a Process–Outcome Consistency \(POC\) framework: a 2×2 over process soundness and outcome correctness whose key cell, the compensated error \(flawed process, correct outcome\), is invisible to every metric and training signal that scores only the answer\. We trace the evidence for perception errors inside coherent chains, formalize why outcome\-based supervision—verifier\-reward reinforcement learning, outcome reward models, answer\-filtered rejection sampling—cannot select against compensated errors \(the reward insensitivity principle\), and review the resulting signatures: reward overoptimization, reward hacking, and reasoning that grows more articulate without growing more grounded\. We map mitigation onto five leverage points—upstream perception, inference\-time process shaping, training\-signal redesign, post\-hoc verification, process\-aware evaluation—and propose reporting compensated\-error and evidence\-grounding rates alongside accuracy\. We close on an asymmetry favoring multimodal settings: visual process errors are checkable by mundane tooling, making faithful multimodal reasoning an unusually tractable target for process supervision\.
