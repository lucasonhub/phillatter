# Johnson, Jamison: On Transmitting \- On the Round Trip of a Signal — Emitted, Modulated by Reality, Received, and Returned

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

What happens to all the data your senses collect every second? This lyric\-philosophical essay treats a person as both a receiver and a signal, and models experience as a round trip — emission, modulation by everything co\-present, reception through one&\#x27;s own facing, and return — built on the author&\#x27;s coherent\-existence functional, which it identifies as an intensity\-weighted form of Kuramoto&\#x27;s order parameter\. A loop that returns in phase builds; one that drifts decays into a lesson\. The essay&\#x27;s central guess is that a felt moment is made where two waves cross, a handshake in the form of Wheeler and Feynman&\#x27;s absorber theory and Cramer&\#x27;s transactional interpretation, offered as resonance rather than mechanism\. It asks, without predicting an answer, whether many lives returning to one Center would share a faint common carrier, and places the question of a designer as an open boundary condition\. It closes on scripture: paratērēsis in Luke 17:20–21, the two readings of entos hymōn, Thomas&\#x27;s inside\-and\-outside kingdom, and Elijah&\#x27;s still small voice, read as the prophets&\#x27; instruction to go quiet enough to hear\. \(direct link \)
