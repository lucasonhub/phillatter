# Stilwell, Phil: ◉ The Immateriality Slide \- Conscious Experience, Embodied Affect, and the Misclassification of the Spiritual

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Popular Christian apologetics sometimes caricatures physicalism as the claim that persons are merely &\#x27;molecules in motion\.&\#x27; The contrast is meant to do more than identify a metaphysical disagreement\. It suggests that love, rational inference, moral seriousness, awe, and religious experience would be unreal or untrustworthy if physically realized, then treats their evident depth as support for an immaterial soul or spiritual realm\. This paper argues that the move equivocates among four different claims: an experience is first\-personal, it is not exhausted by one vocabulary, it is physically realized, and it is evidence for an ontologically independent source\. None entails the next without argument\. The positive case is cumulative\. Conscious availability changes under anesthesia and neural injury; affect and salience change under pharmacology; the organization of access and report changes with neural connectivity; mystical\-type states can be occasioned by drugs and shaped by expectation; personal prayer recruits ordinary social\-cognitive systems; and religious interpretation varies with learned models and cultural framing\. These findings do not deductively prove identity theory, eliminate the explanatory gap, or show that every religious experience is mistaken\. They do show systematic intervention\-sensitive dependence\. That pattern is more expected if cherished human states are achievements of embodied organisms than if their first\-person character itself supplies public evidence of independence from embodiment\. The conclusion is therefore calibrated but consequential\. Irreducibility is not independence, higher\-level reality is not a separate substance, and value is not a residue of ignorance\. A dualist or religious account may remain possible, but it must supply a discriminating bridge or evidence beyond the phenomenology it shares with embodied alternatives\. Explanation does not empty experience\. It locates the conditions under which experience becomes possible, vulnerable, and valuable\.
