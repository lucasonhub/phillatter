## Resumo

Background Headache disorders impose a substantial burden across Africa and West Asia, where limited access to healthcare may increase reliance on complementary and alternative medicine \(CAM\)\. The current study explored clinicians’ perspectives on CAM use, safety concerns, and research needs across countries with different income status\. Methods A cross\-sectional web\-based survey of 123 neurologists and headache specialists with at least 5 years of experience in headache management from 37 countries was carried out\. They were classified into Low/Lower\-Middle Income Countries \(LIC/LMIC\) and Upper\-Middle Income Countries/High Income Countries \(UMIC/HIC\) groups in accordance with the World Bank income classifications\. Results Of 123 participants \(median age 42 years; 50\.4% males\), 52\.8% were from LICs/LMICs\. Clinicians reported that CAMs were frequently used in both groups, and there was no significant difference in overall frequency of CAM use \( p = 0\.116\)\. Clinicians in both groups perceived migraine as the most common headache type for which patients used CAM\. However, significant differences in CAM modalities were observed\. Acupuncture \(79\.3% vs\. 47\.7%, p &lt; 0\.001\), massage \(86\.2% vs\. 63\.1%, p = 0\.004\), yoga \(56\.9% vs\. 20\.0%, p &lt; 0\.001\), and supplements \(93\.1% vs\. 66\.2%, p &lt; 0\.001\) were reported by the clinicians as significantly higher in UMICs/HICs, whereas spiritual healing was significantly higher in LICs/LMICs \(70\.8% vs\. 46\.6%, p = 0\.006\)\. Clinicians reported that patients in LICs/LMICs were significantly more likely to consult CAM prior to seeking medical care \(89\.2% vs\. 67\.2%, p = 0\.003\), and perceived cost as a major factor influencing CAM use \(81\.5% vs\. 27\.6%, p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Conclusion CAM appears to be commonly encountered in headache care across Africa and West Asia according to clinician reports\. Although its use is shaped by cultural beliefs, accessibility, and perceived safety, concerns over unregulated products, herb\-drug interactions, and delayed conventional medical treatments were also highlighted\.
