# Molecular chaperone\-like TIGR04500 family proteins guide graspetide biosynthesis

**Autores:** Ling Hu; Wei Huang; Ji‐Wu Huang; Jing Zhang; Xiaohui Qiu; Botao Cheng; Lifeng Pan; Wen Liu

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

TIGR04500 family proteins share architectural features with the prokaryotic chaperones trigger factor and SurA, but their functions have remained unknown\. Here, we demonstrate that TIGR04500 proteins act as dedicated, non\-catalytic tri\-domain partners in the biosynthesis of graspetides, a family of ribosomally synthesized and posttranslationally modified peptides \(RiPPs\) that possess ester/amide linkages formed between residue side\-chains\. The partner action of TIGR04500 proteins largely depends on their distinct N\-terminal domains, which can interact with family\-defining ATP\-grasp enzymes to form a catalytic complex\. The remaining central multi\-helix and C\-terminal domains share structural similarities with trigger factor and SurA, and thus enable TIGR04500 family proteins to function in a manner similar to these molecular chaperones for peptide substrate recognition\. Genome mining reveals a number of conserved multi helix\-containing proteins/domains that could be involved in the biosynthesis of many families of RiPPs using a similar molecular mechanism, suggesting a common, chaperone\-like function in RiPP biosynthesis\. Graspetides are ribosomally synthesized and post\-translationally modified peptides\. Here, TIGR04500 family proteins are shown to guide graspetide biosynthesis as non\-catalytic chaperone\-like partners through a likely widespread recognition mechanism\.
