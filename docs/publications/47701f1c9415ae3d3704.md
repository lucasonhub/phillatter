# Sparse Transformer: a Controlled Testbed for Mechanistic Interpretability

**Autores:** Anton Korznikov; Alexey Dontsov; Elena Tutubalina

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Mechanistic interpretability aims to decompose neural network computations into human\-understandable features, yet progress is limited by the absence of ground\-truth units to evaluate against\. We address this with a Sparse Transformer \(ST\): a 5\-layer language model trained on TinyStories whose wide, TopK\-sparse MLP forces neuron specialization\. We show that ST neurons are highly interpretable: they outperform a loss\-matched dense transformer by 43% on AutoInterp, and circuits of a median of 12 neurons suffice to reproduce individual model predictions\. This testbed allows us to directly compare feature learning methods: Transcoder, which approximates the MLP input\-output mapping, recovers ST&\#x27;s neuron vectors with 52\.6% higher cosine similarity than Sparse Autoencoder, which only reconstructs outputs\. Furthermore, the ST enables a controlled study of polysemanticity: distilling its sparse MLP into a dense counterpart reveals how compression leads to feature superposition\. These results show how a known reference basis sharpens evaluation and understanding, a role we hope the ST can serve for the field\.
