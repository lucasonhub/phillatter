# A Privacy\-Preserving Decentralized Partition\-Autonomous Planning Method for Interconnected Multi\-Microgrid Distribution Systems Driven by Federated Proximal Policy Optimization

**Autores:** Yao Duan; Ran Cheng; Chong Gao

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

INTRODUCTION: The rapid integration of distributed renewable energy and ICT infrastructure has made interconnected multi\-microgrid distribution systems essential for low\-carbon power grids, yet centralized coordination exposes sensitive operational data to privacy risks, including eavesdropping and membership inference attacks under 5G and edge computing environments\.OBJECTIVES: This study resolves the tension between data privacy protection and decentralized collaborative planning by developing a unified federated reinforcement learning framework with formal privacy guarantees\.METHODS: A privacy\-preserving decentralized partition\-autonomous planning method driven by federated proximal policy optimization is proposed\. The network is divided into autonomous units, each performing local proximal policy optimization on local observations\. A proximal term constrains update steps against Non\-IID drift\. A time\-period adaptive differential privacy budget mechanism dynamically adjusts noise according to gradient sensitivity, complemented by a Shamir secret sharing\-based secure aggregation protocol forming dual\-layer protection\.RESULTS: Experiments on the Baran\-Wu 33\-node test system show daily operational cost within 3\.0% of the centralized benchmark, per round communication reduced by approximately 80% to 9\.6 MB, voltage compliance at 98\.4%, and membership inference attack AUC suppressed to 0\.521DISCUSSION: The adaptive privacy budget reduces utility loss by 3\.2 percentage points under strict privacy constraints versus fixed schemes, while secure aggregation maintains reliability under one PAU dropout with only 0\.38% deviation\. CONCLUSION: The proposed framework provides a feasible technical path for secure collaborative planning of large\-scale interconnected multi\-microgrid systems under practical ICT constraints\.
