# Bridging molecular and heterogeneous catalysis: metallolinker\-assembled and post\-synthetically metalated covalent organic frameworks for photocatalytic CO2 reduction

**Autores:** Yuan‐Hui Zhong; Lai‐Hon Chung

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Photocatalytic carbon dioxide reduction \(PCO 2 RR\) represents a promising approach for solar\-to\-chemical energy conversion and carbon utilization, yet catalyst development remains limited by the trade\-off between the molecular precision of homogeneous complexes and the robustness of heterogeneous materials\. Metallolinker\-assembled covalent organic frameworks \(COFs\) address this challenge by integrating well\-defined metal coordination sites into crystalline porous architectures, combining tunable catalytic environments with framework stability and recyclability\. Beyond acting as catalytic centers, metallolinkers regulate light harvesting, charge separation, CO 2 adsorption, and local reaction environments through synergistic interactions with the surrounding framework\. This review highlights recent advances in metallolinker\-assembled COFs for PCO 2 RR and, for direct comparison, in the related family of post\-synthetically metalated COFs, emphasizing how coordination chemistry, electronic structure, topology, and pore microenvironments govern catalytic activity and product selectivity\. The review discusses key strategies, including metal\-site modulation, linker engineering, donor–acceptor coupling, dual\-site construction, and microenvironmental regulation, to elucidate structure–function relationships in CO 2 activation and conversion\. Remaining challenges, including operational stability, sacrificial\-agent\-free systems, C 2\+ product formation, mechanistic understanding, and scalable fabrication, are critically examined\. Emerging opportunities in computational design, operando characterization, multifunctional frameworks, and device integration are further discussed to advance metallolinker\-COFs toward practical solar\-driven carbon conversion technologies\.
