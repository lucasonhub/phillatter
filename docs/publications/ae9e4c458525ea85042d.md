# A machine learning framework for stage\-structured predator–prey systems with fear effect: Sparse Identification, Gaussian Process Regression, and bifurcation analysis

**Autores:** G\. Ramraj; T\. Poornima

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper presents a comprehensive machine learning framework for the analysis of a stage\-structured predator–prey system incorporating the fear effect, intra\-specific competition among juvenile predators, and maturation delay\. The three\-species model, comprising prey \( N \), juvenile predators \( P 1 \), and mature predators \( P 2 \), is governed by a non\-linear ordinary differential equation system in which the prey growth rate is modulated by a fear factor 1/\(1\+ kP 2 \)\. We first establish the existence and local stability of the coexistence equilibrium E \* = \(16\.02, 16\.09, 32\.19\) and rigorously identify a supercritical Hopf bifurcation at a critical fear intensity k crit ≈ 0\.067, beyond which the equilibrium becomes unstable and a stable limit cycle emerges\. Five machine learning methods are deployed and benchmarked: \(i\) Sparse Identification of Non\-linear Dynamics \(SINDy\) with a generic polynomial library, which recovers the predator equations exactly \( R 2 = 1\.0\) and approximates the prey equation \( R 2 = 0\.9997\); \(ii\) Dynamic Mode Decomposition \(DMD\), whose linear assumption yields poor reconstruction \( R 2 = −2\.885 for P 2 \), demonstrating the inadequacy of linear methods for this non\-linear system; \(iii\) Gaussian Process Regression \(GPR\) with a periodic–RBF composite kernel, achieving RMSE values of 1\.92, 1\.03, and 0\.84 from sparse noisy observations; \(iv\) a physics\-constrained function approximation for recovering population trajectories from incomplete data; and \(v\) a polynomial surrogate ODE model for derivative field approximation\. Additionally, the effect of maturation delay τ on system stability is examined through delay differential equation analysis with a fourth\-order Runge–Kutta integrator, revealing delay\-induced destabilization and transitions to limit cycle dynamics at τ ≥ 10\. All numerical results are independently verified and all performance metrics are computed from actual analyses\. The framework provides a rigorous, reproducible methodology for applying modern data\-driven methods to stage\-structured ecological models\.
