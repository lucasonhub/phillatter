# Designing Teaching Materials with GenAI: Perceptions of Cognitive Load and Learner Competencies in Higher Education

**Autores:** Cristina Navas Romero; GANNA ZAKHAROVA; Francisco D\. Guillén‐Gámez; Sara Cebrián Cifuentes

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Generative artificial intelligence \(GenAI\) is increasingly used to design instructional materials in higher education, yet limited evidence exists on students’ perceptions of the cognitive demands and learner\-related competencies involved\. Drawing on Cognitive Load Theory, this study examined university students’ perceptions of GenAI\-supported instructional material design according to gender, age, and admission grade\. A quantitative, non\-experimental, ex post facto study was conducted with 451 university students enrolled in education\-related undergraduate and master’s programmes\. The instrument assessed perceived usefulness, beliefs concerning metacognitive practices, prior knowledge, and problem\-solving strategies, and perceived intrinsic, extraneous, and germane cognitive load\. Participants generally reported positive perceptions\. Female students reported significantly higher scores on the metacognitive skills, prior knowledge, and problem\-solving skills dimensions, although effect sizes were small\. These differences should be interpreted cautiously, given that female students represented 82% of the sample\. No statistically significant gender differences were detected in the cognitive load dimensions\. Age showed only a weak negative association with extraneous cognitive load, whereas admission grade was positively associated with metacognitive scores in both gender groups and with prior knowledge and problem\-solving scores among female students\. The findings describe students’ beliefs about the practices and cognitive demands associated with GenAI\-supported material design and may inform training in the critical review and pedagogical adaptation of AI\-generated teaching materials\.
