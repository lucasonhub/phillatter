# Beyond Cash: The Future of Financial Transactions and the Role of Financial Technology\- A Bibliometric Analysis

**Autores:** Ritesh Krishna Srivastava; Shweta Srivasatava

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The evolution of Financial Technology \(FinTech\) has completely transformed the nature of the financial transactions landscapeby shifting economies, out of cash and towards digital\-enabled ecosystems\. The study will offer an extensive bibliometric reviewto investigate the intellectual framework, thematic development, and research trend in the FinTech field\. According to theinformation obtained through the Scopus database, the paper applies performance analysis, science mapping, and thematicanalysis using such complex tools as Bibliometrix \(Biblioshiny\) and VOSviewer\. According to the study, academic publication isgrowing enormously, particularly, since 2020, as a result of heightened digitalization and the in9luence of the COVID\-19 pandemic\. Trends of citation are revealing the tightening of the knots of basic knowledge and the new research directions are being formed\. The literature search by the number of citations helped to verify that the most popular models of technology adoption were Technology Acceptance Model \(TAM\) and Uni9ied Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology \(UTAUT\)\. Trust, perceived risk, and behavioral intention were also important in terms of FinTech adoption\. The thematic mapping has revealed underlying themes as those of banking, financial services and financial literacy and emerging themes of financial inclusion, sustainability and digital payments\. Moreover, science mapping and co\-citation analysis shows that the field of interest is highly structured and interconnects, including theoretical backgrounds and methodological accuracy and practice\-based research\. Overall, the study indicates that FinTech studies are shifting to theory\-driven investigation and a more multidimensional and effects\-oriented sphere\. They also offer valuable knowledge to scholars, practitioners, and policy\-makers and indicate the primary directions of further research, namely, in the fields of regulation, cybersecurity, and inclusive digital finance\.
