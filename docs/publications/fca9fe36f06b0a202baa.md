# Boether, Olivier: The Improbable Witness: Contingency, Embodiment, and the Fittedness of Human Existence

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This treatise examines what it is to be a human being by taking seriously an ordinary yet arresting experience: the astonishment a person may feel at existing at all\. Beginning from the recognition that any particular individual is radically contingent, the essay argues that three capacities jointly constitute the distinctive human mode of being: embodied agency, the disposition to build and test theories about the world, and the reflexive power to know oneself\. Drawing together phenomenology, the enactive tradition in cognitive science, contemporary work on predictive processing, and philosophical accounts of agency and personal identity, the treatise contends that the felt sense of being perfectly fitted to the world is a genuine datum in need of explanation rather than a self\-evident truth\. It defends the claim that this fittedness is better explained by observation selection effects together with the co\-constitution of organism and world than by an inference to deliberate design, and it treats the manifest imperfections of human cognition and anatomy as consistent with, rather than fatal to, a sense of wonder\. The conclusion holds that to be human is to be an improbable witness: a self\-aware, world\-engaging animal whose very unlikelihood grounds, rather than undermines, the meaning it finds in its condition\.
