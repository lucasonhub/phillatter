# The Ministry of The Interior’s Regulatory Initiatives Regarding the Administration of Ottoman Prisons \(1880–1897\)

**Autores:** Faysal Uzkan; Besim Özcan

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In the Ottoman Empire, the changes and transformations that took place in various state institutions following the Tanzimat were also strongly felt in the legal sphere\. Developments in this area significantly impacted the administration of prisons\. Moreover, with the increasing prevalence of imprisonment, prisons came to the forefront not only as spaces that restricted freedom but also in terms of their administrative, legal, financial and physical aspects\. The central government intervened directly in these areas through the regulations it enacted\. This study evaluates the initiatives undertaken by the Ministry of the Interior between 1880 and 1897 to transform prisons into a more institutionalized, systematic and controllable structure\. The content of these initiatives is evident from official documents recorded in the Ottoman archives\. During the aforementioned period, the Ministry of the Interior undertook numerous initiatives regarding prisoners’ rights, inspection activities, the duties and responsibilities of prison officials, public order issues and daily basic needs\. These measures aimed to establish a standardized operational framework across prisons nationwide\. In particular, the central government’s reformist structure was sought to be implemented in the provinces through general circulars sent to administrators nationwide\. Additionally, these documents were used to enforce oversight and disciplinary mechanisms\. These efforts can be evaluated as a reflection of centralization efforts on prison administration\. In conclusion, although economic shortages and physical deficiencies across the country hindered the success of the reforms, the initiatives of the Ministry of the Interior are considered quite significant in terms of demonstrating the institutional perspective of the state center\.
