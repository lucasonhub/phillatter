# La Inteligencia Artificial como mediación pedagógica: una mirada crítica al fortalecimiento de la competencia argumentativa en la educación básica secundaria\.

**Autores:** Ronald García Villamizar

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid incorporation of Artificial Intelligence \(AI\) into education calls for reflection on its pedagogical meaning, particularly regarding the development of higher\-order thinking skills such as argumentation\. This article aims to analyze, from a theoretical and critical perspective, the conditions under which AI can become a pedagogical mediation rather than merely a tool for strengthening argumentative competence among secondary school students\. The work is developed through a review and discussion of recent research on generative AI, writing, critical thinking, and technological mediation in educational contexts\. The findings show that the educational value of AI does not depend on its technical capabilities but on the pedagogical intention guiding its use: when AI is used to challenge ideas, request counterarguments, and demand justification, it can act as an interlocutor that stimulates thinking; when it replaces the student&\#x27;s own textual construction, it displaces precisely the capacity it is meant to develop\. Risks related to cognitive dependency, unequal access, and academic integrity are also identified, requiring teacher guidance and critical digital literacy\. As a limitation, this analysis is theoretical and does not yet include original empirical evidence, which will be developed in the master&\#x27;s research from which this article originates\. Its originality lies in framing AI\-based pedagogical mediation as a triangular relationship between student, technology, and teacher, and in linking this framework to argumentative competence in secondary education\. It is concluded that the educational challenge is not teaching students to use AI, but forming students capable of critically dialoguing with it, questioning, contrasting, and arguing their own positions\.
