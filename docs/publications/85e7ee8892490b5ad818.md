# Perceived cross\-cultural competency and purchase intention in foreign\-influencer live streaming commerce: an extended theory of planned behavior perspective

**Autores:** Qingqing Liu; Kavitha Balakrishnan; Mokhtarrudin Bin Ahmad

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Foreign influencers are playing a growing role in China&\#x27;s live\-streaming commerce industry\. Yet, little is known about how consumers respond to foreign influencers’ ability to communicate and adjust across cultures\. To address this gap, the present study draws on the Theory of Planned Behavior \(TPB\) and distinguishes between two consumer perceptions: Intercultural Communication Competency \(ICC\) and Intercultural Adaptability \(ICA\)\. It examines the relationship between each perception and Purchase Intention \(PI\)\. The study also considers whether this relationship operates through three key TPB factors—Attitude \(ATT\), Subjective Norm \(SN\), and Perceived Behavioral Control \(PBC\)\. Survey data from 450 eligible consumers in Zhejiang Province, China, were analysed using partial least squares structural equation modelling\. ICC was positively associated with PI directly and indirectly through all three TPB constructs\. ICA had no significant direct association with PI but showed significant specific indirect associations through ATT, SN, and PBC\. ATT was the strongest indirect pathway for ICC, whereas SN was the strongest for ICA\. The findings differentiate communication\-based and adaptation\-based routes into consumer decision\-making and show how culturally relevant perceptions of foreign communicators can be incorporated as antecedents within TPB\. Because the data are cross\-sectional and were obtained from a convenience sample of topic\-interested consumers in Zhejiang Province, the findings represent conditional associations within this specific sample and should not be generalised to all Chinese consumers\.
