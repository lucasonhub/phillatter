# Zhou, Chang\-Peng &amp; Fonseca, Anderson: The Boundary Problem for Group Minds: A Possible Answer at the Physical Level

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-19 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Groups can coordinate, process information, and behave as cognitive agents without thereby constituting a conscious subject\. This paper approaches group consciousness through the problem of conscious individuation: what sustains the boundary between one subject and another? It argues that subject boundaries are partly grounded in the architectural asymmetry between communication within a system and communication across its boundary\. Communication architecture, rather than information volume alone, helps explain why systems remain separate, partially overlap, become nested, or potentially fuse\. We develop this argument through cases involving Block&\#x27;s Nation\-Brain, craniopagus twins, colonial invertebrates, and split\-brain patients, and relate it to existing accounts of subject combination\. The proposal does not explain why consciousness exists or establish that any group is conscious\. Its more limited claim is that the organization of communication is relevant to where the boundaries of conscious subjects are drawn\.
