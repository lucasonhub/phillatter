# Creating common ground: integrating transformative agroecology and extension research in undergraduate education

**Autores:** Karen L\. Nordstrom; Victor M\. Izzo; Emily Hoyler; V\. Ernesto Méndez

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper examines the Agroecology Extension \(AX\) summer Research Fellowship at the University of Vermont, a 5\-year USDA\-funded undergraduate program that intentionally integrated extension research with agroecology education within a land\-grant university context\. Drawing on predominantly qualitative evaluation of five cohorts \(2021–2025, n = 47\), we identify enabling and constraining pedagogical factors for creating common ground between two domains that remain institutionally separated at most land\-grant universities\. The study contributes to the emerging signature pedagogy for sustainable food systems education by documenting how three interconnected pedagogical dynamics—integrating political agroecology with extension, centering equity as a sustained commitment, and supporting participatory knowledge co\-creation with students—shaped program design and evolution across 5 years\. Findings demonstrate that transformative education at the intersection of agroecology and extension requires explicit engagement with the political dimensions of agroecology and extension history, relational infrastructure for equity work, and deliberate structures for co\-learning\. This case study offers practical pedagogical insights for land\-grant universities seeking to develop sustainable programs that bridge agroecology and extension while preparing graduates to address complex agricultural and food systems challenges\.
