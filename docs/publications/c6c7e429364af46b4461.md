# Corporate Governance Practices in Ai\-Powered Technologies: A Perspective of Engineering Students

**Autores:** Stephanie Grace V\. Cortes

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial Intelligence \(AI\) has become increasingly integrated into educational, professional, and technological environments, creating opportunities for innovation while raising concerns regarding transparency, accountability, ethical responsibility, privacy, and governance\. Despite the growing use of AI\-powered technologies among university students, limited research has examined how engineering students perceive corporate governance practices associated with these technologies\. This study explored the perceptions of undergraduate engineering students regarding corporate governance in AI\-powered technologies\. A descriptive quantitative research design employing a cross\-sectional survey approach was utilized\. Data were collected from ninety\-two \(92\) undergraduate engineering students at Cebu Institute of Technology–University \(CIT\-University\) using a structured questionnaire\. Frequency counts, percentages, weighted means, and ranking procedures were used to analyze the data\. The findings revealed that respondents demonstrated positive awareness of corporate governance principles in AI systems, particularly regarding ethical responsibility, accountability, transparency, and data governance\. The respondents also expressed a generally favorable level of trust in organizations utilizing AI\-powered technologies, although concerns regarding privacy protection, data security, and responsible AI implementation remained evident\. Among the governance dimensions examined, accountability received the highest rating, followed by ethical governance and transparency\. Furthermore, the results showed that engineering students generally practiced responsible AI use, particularly in avoiding technologies that violate ethical and privacy standards\. Large Language Models \(LLMs\), such as Gemini, ChatGPT, and Claude, were identified as the most commonly used AI\-powered technologies among the respondents\. The study concludes that engineering students recognize the importance of responsible AI governance and view accountability, ethical responsibility, and transparency as essential components of trustworthy AI systems\. These findings may contribute to the development of governance policies, educational initiatives, and responsible AI practices within academic and technological environments\.
