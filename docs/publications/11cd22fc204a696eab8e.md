# Physical exercise and mobile phone addiction in college students: negative emotions and coping styles as chain mediators

**Autores:** Shuwen Guo; Wenxia Tong; Xingbo Zhou; Sen Wang; Panyi Shen; Shuqiao Meng

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Considering the significant risks that widespread mobile phone addiction poses to the holistic health of college students, further research is essential\. This article systematically explores correlational mechanisms linking physical exercise engagement to mobile phone addiction\. Furthermore, it evaluates how Negative Emotions and Coping Styles function as mediators in this relationship\. Methods A convenience sampling approach was employed to recruit 3,283 undergraduates across the first 3 years of college\. Data collection took place between April and May 2025, during which participants self\-reported their engagement in physical exercise, problematic mobile phone use patterns, psychological distress \(manifested as depression, anxiety, and stress symptoms\), as well as their preferred coping styles\. All responses were gathered through the Wenjuanxing online survey platform\. For the statistical analysis, SPSS 29\.0 was utilized to compute descriptive statistics and bivariate correlations\. Furthermore, to evaluate the sequential mediation effect, we adopted Hayes&\#x27; PROCESS macro \(version 4\.1, Model 6\), implementing a bootstrapping approach with 5,000 iterations to generate bias\-corrected confidence intervals for the indirect pathways\. Results Students showed low physical exercise \(M = 18\.49 ± 14\.90\) and variable mobile phone addiction \(M = 35\.40 ± 14\.78\)\. Physical exercise negatively correlated with addiction \( r = −0\.83\), negative emotions \( r = −0\.39\), and negative coping styles \( r = −0\.39\), but positively with positive coping \( r = 0\.31\)\. Negative emotions positively predicted addiction \( r = 0\.618\)\. Mediation analysis showed physical exercise exhibited a correlational link with lower addiction levels \(direct effect = 21\.54%\) through three distinct indirect correlational pathways\. The path through negative emotions was dominant \(72\.45%\), while coping\-related paths accounted for 1\.41%−4\.6%\. The independent mediating contribution of positive coping styles was slightly larger than that of negative coping styles; the dominant explanatory power across both models stemmed from the pathway of negative emotions\. Conclusions Correlational results preliminarily suggest that physical exercise may be negatively associated with college students&\#x27; mobile phone addiction through correlational pathways including sequential serial paths involving negative emotions and two types of coping styles\. The present correlational data could provide empirical reference for developing sports\-based mental health interventions\.
