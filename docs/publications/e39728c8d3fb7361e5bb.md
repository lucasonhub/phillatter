# Four Quality\-Control Failure Modes in Public Wireline\-Log Archives: Measured Effects and a Reproducible Workflow, With Examples From Wyoming and Kansas

**Autores:** Emmanuel Kobina Gyasi; Precious Kamsiyo Ejikeme; Elvis Gyamfi; Somtochukwu Vivian Onuegbu

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Public wireline\-log archives are widely reused in petrophysical and machine\-learning studies, yet the processing steps required to make them analytically usable are rarely reported\. We processed 1,210 logs from the Teapot Dome archive in Wyoming, together with a 300\-well pilot drawn from 29,089 Kansas Geological Survey records, and found four processing failures, each of which produced an error larger than the signal it was supposed to resolve\. Unit conventions varied inside single archives: bulk density in kg/m³, transit time in µs/m and neutron porosity in percent sat alongside conventional units\. When a consolidated\-rock sonic transform was applied to whole boreholes, the secondary porosity index rose from −0\.181 in the shallowest depth decile to \+0\.019 in the deepest, monotonically apart from a single reversal between the sixth and seventh deciles \(Kendall τ = 0\.956, p = 5\.5 × 10⁻⁶\), a trend set by compaction and not by the rock\. Assuming one matrix mineral across a mixed interval gave negative density porosity in 23\.0% of samples\. It also inflated apparent secondary porosity by 29 to 46 percentage points\. In one of three wells within a single formation, tool calibration differed from its neighbours by enough to misassign mineralogy\. Pore\-type attribution itself proved fragile\. Applied to identical data, two defensible matrix\-assignment schemes flagged 21\.2% and 39\.2% of samples respectively, and each pointed to a different well as the source of the signal\. Photoelectric factor, which could settle the question independently, was logged in only 14 of the Wyoming files, whereas close to a third of the Kansas pilot carried it\. We release an open Python workflow that detects each failure mode, applies the corresponding correction, and reports the magnitude of that correction at every stage\. Our view is that log\-based pore\-type attribution should not be treated as reliable unless PEF or a neutron log corrected for lithology is available\.
