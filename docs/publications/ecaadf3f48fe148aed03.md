# Financial Self\-Efficacy as a Predictor of FinTech Adoption and Financial Planning Behavior

**Autores:** Rosananda Oktala; Fernando Africano; Dewi Fadila; Ogi Rifansyah

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examines the disconnect between high digital payment adoption and long\-term financial planning in an emerging urban economy by investigating whether Financial Self\-Efficacy predicts this gap directly or indirectly through FinTech Adoption\. Using survey data collected from 149 urban residents of Palembang, Indonesia, comprising 54\.4% students and 45\.6% workers, the conceptual framework was evaluated using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling \(PLS\-SEM\) in SmartPLS 4\. The analysis was complemented by the Heterotrait\-Monotrait ratio \(HTMT\) for discriminant validity assessment, PLSpredict, and Multigroup Analysis \(PLS\-MGA\) with Measurement Invariance of Composite Models \(MICOM\) testing\. The results show that Financial Self\-Efficacy significantly predicts FinTech Adoption and has a strong direct effect on Financial Planning Behavior\. In contrast, FinTech Adoption does not significantly predict Financial Planning Behavior when Financial Self\-Efficacy is controlled for, resulting in a non\-significant indirect effect through FinTech Adoption\. Furthermore, the multigroup analysis reveals that the predictive effect of Financial Self\-Efficacy on FinTech Adoption is nearly twice as strong among students as among workers\. These findings suggest that digital convenience alone does not automatically translate into long\-term financial discipline\. Instead, psychological capability, particularly Financial Self\-Efficacy, serves as an important factor linking technology adoption and financial planning behavior, with its predictive strength varying across occupational groups within an emerging urban population\.
