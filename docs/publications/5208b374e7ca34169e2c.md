# A COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS OF CLINICAL AND DEMOGRAPHIC PREDICTORS IN DEEP VEIN THROMBOSIS : A CROSS\-SECTIONAL STUDY

**Autores:** 

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The public health community faces a major challenge because of the lack of detailed comparison data from multicenter cross\-sectional studies on Deep Vein Thrombosis \(DVT\)\.This is especially true when trying to identify which clinical and epidemiological factors influence the likelihood of DVT\.Our goal in this research is to compare how these factors impact the occurrence of DVT across different groups\.The Bavarian Thromboembolic Risk \(BATER\) study included over 221 women aged 18 to 55, recruited from RVS hospital in Chittoor city\.Their data was collected and analyzed as part of this cross\-sectional study\.The participants who made their completion of providing their standardized questionnaires by collecting the information about demographics, reproductive history, lifestyle, comorbidities and the known genetic and clinical risk factors of DVT\.The researcher aimed to determine the incidence rates of DVT and investigate how various demographic and clinical factors predict the risk of developing this condition\.The study found that the risk of DVT significantly increases if a first\-degree relative has experienced DVT or if the individual has a personal history of DVT\.Conversely, common genetic markers\-such as Factor V Leiden, prothrombin, antithrombin, protein C, and MTHFR mutations\-did not show any statistically significant association with DVT risk\.The findings suggest that genetic factors are less predictive than clinical and demographic criteria, particularly a personal or family history of DVT\.This research indicates that clinical profiling should be prioritized over genetic testing for the prevention of DVT\.
