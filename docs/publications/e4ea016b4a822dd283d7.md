# Age\-specific agreement between Hi\-DongDong and UTAP\-2 articulation assessments in Korean children aged 2–13 years

**Autores:** Jinju Lee; Sejin Kwon; Jin Young Ko; Yulhyun Park; Jaewon Lee; Ju Seok Ryu; Jee Hyun Suh

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Digital approaches to articulation assessment are increasingly proposed to expand access to speech\-language evaluation, yet the comparability of speech performance elicited by digital and conventional word lists across age groups remains insufficiently understood\. Methods This study assessed age\-specific agreement between Hi\-DongDong word list, a novel digital articulation assessment instrument, and the Urimal Test of Articulation and Phonology 2 \(UTAP\-2\), the standard reference in South Korea, in 505 typically developing Korean children aged 2\-13 years\. Each child&\#x27;s audio recordings were scored by three independent speech\-language pathologists on both instruments\. Agreement for consonant accuracy measures\-Percentage of Consonants Correct \(PCC\), its revised form, and their totals\-and for whole\-word correctness was evaluated using intraclass correlation coefficients \(ICC\), Bland\-Altman bias and limits of agreement \(LoA\), and linear mixed\-effects models\. Results Inter\-rater reliability was excellent for all metrics \(ICC ≥ 0\.995\)\. At the population level, no significant instrument\-by\-age or instrument\-by\-age² interactions were observed, indicating that the age\-related developmental trajectories did not differ significantly between Hi\-DongDong and UTAP\-2, although PWC showed a significant main effect of instrument, with lower scores for Hi\-DongDong than UTAP\-2 at the mean\-centered age\. Individual\-level agreement, however, was strongly age\-dependent\. Low ICC values at ages 9 and 12 co\-occurred with narrow LoA and ceiling\-compressed scores, identifying them as artifacts of variance restriction rather than true disagreement\. In contrast, ages 4 and 7 showed low ICC with wide LoA, reflecting genuine measurement divergence during periods of rapid or transitional phonological development\. Among school\-age children \(7 years and older\), bias was negligible \(within ±1 percentage point\) and LoA were narrow \(approximately 8\-24 percentage points\), whereas preschool\-age children showed substantially wider individual\-level LoA\. Hi\-DongDong additionally sustained discriminative sensitivity for both consonants and vowels\-including diphthongs\-across a wider developmental range than the monophthong\-only UTAP\-2\. Discussion These findings support the use of Hi\-DongDong as a complementary articulation word\-list assessment across development, while underscoring that the present study evaluated clinician\-scored agreement between word lists rather than automated diagnostic performance\.
