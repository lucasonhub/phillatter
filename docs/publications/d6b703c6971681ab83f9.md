# Xiao, Yuxuan: Toward a Theory of AI Epistemic Dynamics: Recursive Contamination and Epistemic State Transitions in Large Language Models

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Large language models are increasingly used in sustained interactions rather than isolated prompts\. Existing research has examined failures such as hallucination, sycophancy, and factual error, but less attention has been given to how such failures can become connected through changes in the epistemic state of context over time\. We develop AI Epistemic Dynamics, a conceptual framework for analyzing this temporal dimension of LLM interaction\. Central to the framework is the Epistemic Context State \(ECS\), which characterizes the epistemic state of the context on which subsequent generation depends\. We distinguish four idealized states: Stable, Uncertain, Contaminated, and Self\-Reinforcing\. We then introduce Recursive Epistemic Contamination \(REC\) to explain how a low\-quality claim \(LQC\) may contribute to further LQCs that become part of the context and alter subsequent generation\. At the scale of sustained interaction, we call this process Micro\-REC\. We extend the same abstract recursive logic to Macro\-REC, in which contaminated epistemic outputs may circulate through wider informational environments and alter the conditions of later knowledge production\. This framework recasts AI reliability as a dynamic rather than purely static epistemic problem\. It explains how otherwise distinct local epistemic failures can become connected through path\-dependent and recursively reinforced trajectories of degradation\. AI Epistemic Dynamics thereby provides a basis for empirical research on epistemic\-state transitions, recursive degradation, corrigibility, and the long\-term reliability of AI systems\.
