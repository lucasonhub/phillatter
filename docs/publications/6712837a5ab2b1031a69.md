## Descrição do feed

What is consciousness, and where does experience begin? A system can discriminate a million shades of colour and be no more advanced, in the relevant sense, than one that discriminates two; what marks a step up is a new kind of distinction, not more of the same\. This chapter analyses consciousness as the detection of distinctions and orders its forms by a categorical transition principle: each level is reached by detecting a category of difference that the level below could not register\. The ladder has nine rungs, from bare registration through sentience — the level at which a registered difference becomes available to thought, and so is felt — to the detection of the self against all else, to distinctions among one’s own detections, to reflection\. The chapter places the boundary of experience at availability to thought and offers, in place of a solution to the hard problem, an identity a critic can doubt: feltness is availability to thought\. He explains why the doubt arises and names a question prior to the hard problem, the boundary question, of where in the ladder experience begins\. The chapter’s second result is architectural\. The mental has two axes: content, what a state registers, and orientation, how the state stands toward what it registers — for or against, drawn or repelled\. Value lies on the second axis\. The partition beneath it, determination or nothing, is derived from the contrastive structure of content; the orientation axis rests on a constructed premise the chapter states and the Premise Register carries, that a determination’s only further division is by felt orientation\. Empathy and cruelty are shown to be one capacity run in opposite directions, since both register another’s state and differ only in the orientation of the current, a result the book’s ethics and theology both inherit\. Memory, language and agency are turned away as criteria of consciousness rather than markers of its levels\. Four rival theories are read through the framework\. Global workspace theory is complemented by it; predictive processing is contested; integrated information theory and panpsychism \(Goff, Mørch\) are engaged in an appendix as rivals to the chapter’s interface account, on which experience is the interface between a modality and what it registers, and the modality’s absence is the absence of the field\. Block’s distinction between phenomenal and access consciousness is answered by separating availability in kind from an episode of access; Prinz, Tye, Frankish and Panksepp are placed on the ladder\. The chapter closes with an inventory of what the rest of the book uses — the two\-axes architecture and the existence of valence — and with a testable prediction staked in the appendix: that phosphenes evoked by cortical stimulation are experienced under restricted attention, where a global\-workspace account predicts their absence — a test the book describes as well\-posed and not yet adjudicated, with the design that would settle it\. The partition is graded derived, the orientation axis constructed, and the positive proposals constructed with live rivals named\. The chapter is candid that its criteria for the boundary of experience sit in an appendix no later result uses\. This paper is Chapter 13 of Dyology: On Diversity and Equality by Robin Mains \(2026\); the two\-axes architecture built here is the footing of the book’s metaethical side\.
