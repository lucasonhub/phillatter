# Re\-thinking Energy Markets in Times of the Energy Transition : Implications for Market Design and Regulation

**Autores:** Lisa Hanny

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Modern energy systems are undergoing a profound transformation\. The traditional model of centralized power plants delivering electricity to largely passive consumers is giving way to a decentralized and participatory landscape\. Rooftop solar panels, electric vehicles, storage devices, and smart household technologies are shifting the flow of energy and information, creating new opportunities for market participation and trading products such as flexibility services\. At the same time, these developments introduce unprecedented complexity: balancing variable renewable generation, coordinating millions of distributed energy resources, and ensuring grid stability are challenges that demand re\-thinking existing system and market structures\. In this context, energy flexibility emerges as a key mechanism, enabling participants to actively shape supply and demand, respond to market signals, and integrate low\-carbon technologies\. In this sense, electricity consumers or &quot;prosumers&quot; become active market participants, providing flexibility services that stabilize the system while benefiting economically\. Yet flexibility alone is insufficient without clear incentives, reliable monitoring, and transparent reward structures embedded in a suitable market design\. Digital technologies provide the critical bridge between market design goals and real\-world behavioural changes\. They help operationalize incentive schemes, monitor the behaviour of a growing number of market participants, and make economic rewards, e\.g\., from flexibility provision, tangible\. Hence, these technologies transform energy from a passive commodity into an interactive service, allowing markets, regulators, and consumers to interact in small time intervals and exchange a broad portfolio of energy\-related goods and services\. This dissertation explores how decentralization, sustainability, and digitalization intersect to reshape energy systems\. It highlights how digital technologies can operationalize incentives, enhance system resilience, and make energy markets more transparent, efficient, and participatory\. Ultimately, it argues that the energy transition is not just a technical challenge, but a societal one requiring an interdisciplinary consideration of policy goals and regulations as well as digital technologies\.
