# Belief\-sensitive norm nudging for household tap water use: an indirect\-pathways argument

**Autores:** Adriano Angelucci; Alberto Pirni

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Norm\-based nudges are increasingly used to promote environmentally responsible behavior, including more sustainable water use\. It is widely recognized that such interventions should be informed by prior survey\-based research\. A more specific and often neglected question, however, concerns what those surveys should actually measure\. This article addresses that question in the case of household tap\-water use\. We argue, first, that social norms often influence behavior not only directly but also indirectly, through mediating constructs such as personal norms, attitudes toward the behavior, and perceived behavioral control\. Second, drawing on the Reasoned Action Approach and related work on mediated norm effects, we argue that these constructs are grounded in underlying behavioral, normative, and control beliefs\. Accordingly, surveys designed to inform norm\-based interventions should not focus primarily on self\-reported behavior alone, but should also identify the belief patterns through which normative influence is likely to operate in a given population\. On this basis, we propose a belief\-sensitive survey blueprint for household tap\-water use\. The paper&\#x27;s contribution is therefore not to advance a new general theory of norm nudging, but to clarify how an indirect\-pathways perspective can inform the design of more context\-sensitive and belief\-sensitive water interventions\.
