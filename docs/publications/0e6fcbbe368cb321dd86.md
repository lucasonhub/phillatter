# Relationship Between Osteoarthritis and Metabolic Diseases: A Bibliometric and Visual Analysis

**Autores:** Yunping Weng; Junyan Wu; 谢水兰; Zhang Yumin

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background/Aims: To map global research on osteoarthritis \(OA\) and metabolic diseases, separately examining individual metabolic diseases and metabolic syndrome \(MetS\), and identify gaps and emerging frontiers\. Materials and Methods: Web of Science Core Collection publications \(2009–2025\) were retrieved on February 22, 2026\. Co\-authorship and keyword co\-occurrence networks, Louvain clustering \(modularity and silhouette evaluation\), burst detection, and keyword\-cluster visualization were performed\. Betweenness centrality assessed country collaboration\. Chord diagrams and temporal streamgraphs mapped pairwise disease co\-study patterns and trajectories\. GLP\-1 receptor agonist \(GLP\-1RA\) trends were assessed using 24 predefined terms\. Results: We included 10,217 articles from 1582 journals by 38,665 authors\. Annual output rose 4\.4\-fold between 2009 and 2025\. The United States \(33\.4%\), China \(15\.0%\), and the United Kingdom \(9\.3%\) were the leading contributors\. Ten major themes were identified: progression, arthroplasty, physical activity, inflammation, prevalence, Mendelian randomization, meta\-analysis, knee OA, body mass index, and hip\. Obesity and type 2 diabetes were the most frequently co\-studied conditions\. Obesity showed the strongest sustained growth, whereas nonalcoholic fatty liver disease \(NAFLD\) received limited attention\. Inflammation and risk were the strongest burst terms\. OA\-related GLP\-1RA publications increased 9\.7\-fold after 2021\. Conclusion: OA\-metabolic disease research is expanding rapidly\.
