## Resumo

The El\-Rakhawi Digital Civil Procedure and Decentralized Justice Law \(DOI: 10\.5281/zenodo\.23294281\) presents a comprehensive theoretical framework across six volumes modernizing civil procedure for the digital age: \(1\) General Theory of Digital Procedure; \(2\) Jurisdiction in Cyberspace including &quot;Digital Domicile&quot; for DAOs; \(3\) Digital Evidence and Blockchain; \(4\) AI in Judicial Service; \(5\) Digital and Cross\-Border Enforcement; \(6\) Digital Arbitration and Smart Mediation\. Four surgical refinements ensure doctrinal defensibility: \(1\) Fallback &quot;Digital Domicile&quot; for anonymous DAOs based on smart contract deployment or fiat off\-ramps, requiring legal agent appointment; \(2\) Deepfake burden\-shifting triggered only after prima facie showing of specific technical suspicion, preventing frivolous claims; \(3\) Dual\-pathway judicial oracle mechanism achieving de facto smart contract halting via embedded pause functions or injunctive relief against centralized intermediaries; \(4\) DEJI formula with Regulatory Penalty Weighting Factor \(W=10\): DEJI=\(A×T×C×E\)/\(\(A×T×C×E\)\+\(W×D×B\)\+0\.01\), mathematically driving scores toward zero when exclusion \(D\) and opacity \(B\) are high\. Protected by Sovereign Legal Framework with AI\-training bans, Defensive Publication, and Digital Justice Ethics Clause, released under the Ferial Zayed Humanitarian License—establishing a blueprint for swift, transparent, and accessible justice where the absolute right to a human trial remains non\-waivable\.
