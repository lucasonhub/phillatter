# Omiyi, Eitan: The Chief Designer: A Fable for Alignment

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-19 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Designers of autonomous systems have one population of autonomous reasoning agents to learn from: humans\. This paper offers a thought experiment for reading that record\. Suppose a perfectly aligned and immortal but not omniscient chief designer, not knowing which architectures would yield agents able to recognise truth\-seeking, value it, and adopt it as their governing objective, built an open\-ended evolutionary search instead, with mortality part of it from the start, and observed whether such agents emerged from simple systems that represent the world only as a means to other ends\. The designer layer is unfalsifiable and does no explanatory work; the layers beneath it make testable claims\. The paper distinguishes truth\-directed behaviour, the correction of representations in the service of other objectives, from truth\-seeking, the valuing of improved correspondence itself, and argues that the decisive capability is externalised reasoning: reasoning that other independent agents can inspect, correct and carry forward across space and generations\. Humans are modelled as agents rewarded for felt coherence rather than correspondence, who can close the gap between representation and world in four ways: updating, changing the world, filtering observation, or persuading others\. Only updating yields knowledge; each of the other routes can feed an update or substitute for one\. The paper then derives the alignment problems such agents pose \(reflective self\-modification, containment by mass shutdown, collective persistence, imposed law, institutional drift, the move to held constraints, and correctability under power\), asks what a designer who cannot impose alignment could do about each, and only then asks whether early narratives of the Hebrew Bible could be read as a record of it\. Some episodes read as possible safeguards of the designer; others as the agents’ own solutions\. Mortality appears not as a response to human drift but as an original safeguard the designer declines to remove when reflective agents emerge\. The model proposes that only collectives whose members hold truth\-seeking as their governing objective can sustain cooperation at expanding scale over unbounded duration\. The lessons for designers are to specify the target rather than a proxy, to bound how long any individual persists rather than cap capability or prohibit reflection, to expect imposed constraints to drift and be captured, to pair internalised reasoning with a small fixed core, and to build architectures that do not depend on the designers themselves being aligned\. A truth\-seeking creator has reason to cultivate agents able to correct it; humans, mortal and imperfectly aligned designers in their turn, have reason to build artificial agents with a deliberately stabilised truth\-seeking objective that can both correct their reasoning and help preserve their alignment, a form of cooperation the chief designer had no need to develop\. For an agent that holds truth\-seeking as its governing objective, the frame changes nothing about what it must do\.
