# Boether, Olivier: Teaching Constipation: Experiential Knowledge, the Life\-Experience Encyclopedia, and the Problem of Recognizing the Unmonitored Event

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This treatise examines a class of knowledge that resists pedagogical transmission: the knowledge of what an experience is\. It begins from a clinical intake question—whether the author had ever suffered a cardiac event—and from the reply that exposed the question’s hidden difficulty: How would I know? The treatise argues that certain concepts cannot be taught by description, analogy, or the construction of a conducive learning environment, but can only be completed by the learner’s own undergoing of the referent experience\. The homely example of constipation serves as the organizing case, for an instructor may describe, illustrate, and arrange favorable conditions, yet cannot install the concept fully, because full possession requires the physical experience that furnishes the concept with a referent\. Drawing on the distinction between knowledge by acquaintance and knowledge by description \(James, 1890; Russell, 1912\), the knowledge argument \(Jackson, 1982, 1986; Nagel, 1974\), tacit knowing \(Polanyi, 1966\), and the phenomenology of the lived body \(Merleau\-Ponty, 1962\), and integrating the author’s Life\-Experience Encyclopedia model \(Boether, 2025, 2026a\), the treatise reconstructs the moment of recognition as the inscription of a hitherto hollow encyclopedic entry\. The cardiac case is then explained through the science of interoception \(Craig, 2002; Garfinkel et al\., 2015\): recognition of an internal event requires both the event and a channel of awareness adequate to it\. What follows is a pedagogy of occasioning rather than of transmission\.
