# Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons exposure in humans and reproductive health outcomes: An umbrella review

**Autores:** Yue Wang; Yanlin Chen; Lili Chen; Dong Binhua

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons \(PAHs\) are a public health threat, but evidence linking PAHs exposure to adverse reproductive health outcomes remains unclear\. Methods: PubMed, Embase, and Web of Science were searched from inception to April 17, 2024, for systematic reviews and meta\-analyses of observational studies on PAHs exposure and reproductive outcomes\. Methodological quality was assessed using A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews \(AMSTAR\)\. Random\-effects models generated pooled estimates, without conversion among beta coefficients \(β\), odds ratios \(OR\), and risk ratios \(RR\)\. Evidence credibility was evaluated using evidence\-class criteria and the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation \(GRADE\)\. Results: Five articles reporting 15 associations were included\. Four \(26\.7%\) were significant: bladder cancer in aluminum production workers \(RR = 1\.26, 95% CI= 1\.16, 1\.36\) and iron and steel foundry workers \(RR = 1\.36, 95% CI= 1\.14, 1\.62\), birth weight reduction associated with PAHs DNA\-adducts \(OR = 1\.02, 95% CI = 1\.01, 1\.03\), and offspring attention deficit hyperactivity disorder \(ADHD\) associated with prenatal PAHs exposure \(RR = 1\.73, 95% CI = 1\.15, 2\.58\)\. Dietary PAHs were not associated with birth length \(β = \-0\.04, 95% CI = \-0\.09, 0\.01\) or birth weight \(β = \-5\.65, 95% CI = \-16\.35, 5\.06\)\. Overall, the certainty of evidence was generally low or very low\. Conclusion: Occupational and prenatal PAHs exposures may be associated with adverse outcomes, but evidence was low or very low certainty, and most birth\-size outcomes showed no significant pooled associations\.
