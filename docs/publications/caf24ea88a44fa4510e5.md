# Intellectual Property Protection of Purely AI\-Generated Content: Rethinking Authorship, Inventorship, and the Case for International Standards

**Autores:** Paul Barnabas; Abdulrahman Hassan Litalawe

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid growth of generative and autonomous artificial intelligence \(AI\) systems has unsettled the traditionally anthropocentric foundations of intellectual property \(IP\) law\. This article examines the extent to which existing intellectual property rights \(IPR\) frameworks, namely copyright, patent, and industrial design law, can accommodate works, inventions, and designs that are created exclusively by AI systems without direct human intervention\. Adopting a doctrinal legal research methodology, the article reviews primary legal materials, including statutes, international conventions, and case law from several jurisdictions, alongside secondary scholarly literature, to assess how the requirements of authorship, inventorship, originality, and moral rights have been interpreted in the context of purely AI\-generated content\. The discussion is reinforced by a comparative analysis of jurisdictional responses in the United Kingdom, the European Union, the United States, Australia, South Africa, Indonesia, Hong Kong, China, and the United Arab Emirates\. The central argument advanced is that traditional IPR laws were designed around human creativity and therefore struggle to extend coverage to autonomously generated content\. While a few jurisdictions have experimented with liberal judicial interpretation or sui generis protection, these efforts remain fragmented, inconsistent, and largely reactive rather than systematic\. The article concludes that an internationally coordinated framework, whether through reform of existing treaties or the negotiation of a dedicated IP\-AI instrument, is necessary to balance innovation incentives, economic interests, and the public interest in access to AI\-generated content\.
