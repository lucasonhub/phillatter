# ‘Some of it&\#x27;s fucked, some of it&\#x27;s useful’: Young men&\#x27;s experiences navigating masculinity content on TikTok

**Autores:** Krista Fisher; Lauren Powell; Emily C\. Lewis; Jasleen Chhabra; Vivian Gerrand; Zac E\. Seidler

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Masculinity content on TikTok is rapidly reshaping young men&\#x27;s identities, health and relationships, yet how young men perceive and navigate this content remains poorly understood\. This qualitative study explored the perspectives of 142 young men aged 16–25 \(Mage = 18\.85, SD = 2\.64\) from Australia, the United Kingdom and the United States\. Thematic analysis of open\-text responses generated two themes: A Blueprint for Masculine Self\-Development, capturing young men&\#x27;s simultaneous aspiration toward, and critique of, masculinity self\-improvement narratives; and Navigating Manosphere Ideologies, reflecting how young men engage with, interrogate or reject masculinist ideologies embedded within mainstream content\. Findings reveal a dynamic process of critical appraisal, weighing perceived benefits against ideological harm\. This carries significant implications for health promotion and digital policy: Evidence\-informed approaches are needed that leverage young men&\#x27;s desire for self\-improvement, address unmet psychosocial needs, encourage accountability and embed digital literacy and ideological safeguarding into digital places of meaning\.
