# Infralimbic\-hippocampal activity pattern associated with Pavlovian extinction of cocaine\-context associations

**Autores:** José Manuel Pérez\-Berlanga; Ana María Pérez\-Cano; Fabiola Ávila‐Gámiz; María del Carmen Gómez\-Roldán; Luis J\. Santín; David Ladrón de Guevara‐Miranda

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Extinction of cocaine\-context associations is a valuable strategy to suppress conditioned drug seeking and relapse vulnerability\. Prior work has mapped the contribution of multiple corticolimbic regions to the retrieval of instrumental cocaine\-context extinction, yet the neurofunctional profile supporting its Pavlovian counterpart remains untested\. In this study, we combined c\-Fos mapping with multivariate analysis to determine whether extinction expression of a cocaine\-induced conditioned place preference \(CPP\) is supported by a specific neurofunctional signature\. Mice underwent CPP conditioning followed by either forced extinction \(EXT, n = 9\), home\-cage withdrawal \(No\-EXT, n = 6\), or a saline procedure without drug exposure \(SAL, n = 7\), and c\-Fos expression was tracked during a subsequent CPP retest\. EXT animals both reduced CPP during retesting and displayed more c\-Fos⁺ nuclei in the infralimbic cortex \(IL\) and the ventral CA1 region of the hippocampus relative to No\-EXT and SAL mice, as well as increased c\-Fos in the dorsal CA1 compared to No\-EXT animals\. No group differences were found regarding c\-Fos expression in the prelimbic cortex, dorsal and ventral striatum, basolateral amygdala, and other hippocampal subfields\. Furthermore, a Principal Component Analysis indicated that CPP extinction, together with c\-Fos induction in IL and dorsal and ventral CA1, loaded onto a single dimension accounting for \~62\.5% of shared variance, suggesting that these variables integrate a specific neurobehavioral profile\. These findings identify an infralimbic\-hippocampal activity pattern associated with cocaine\-context extinction, providing a basis for future investigation of potential interactions between these structures\.
