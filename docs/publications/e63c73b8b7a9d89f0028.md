# Compliance\-as\-Code: Automating Evidence Collection with Human\-in\-the\-Loop Review

**Autores:** Rahul Muralinath

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Security and compliance frameworks generally require proof that a control functions continuously, not that it was configured correctly once\. Most organizations still produce that proof by hand \- a captured screenshot, a spreadsheet entry, a forwarded confirmation email \- a method built for a slower era of infrastructure change and poorly suited to systems provisioned, modified, and redeployed through code on a near\-continuous basis\. This article examines an architectural alternative: embedding evidence generation directly into the internal developer platform that already governs provisioning and deployment, so a control&\#x27;s operating state is captured as a byproduct of normal platform activity rather than reconstructed after the fact for an auditor\. Written from a platform\-engineering vantage point \- grounded in experience building golden\-path developer tooling and platform\-embedded policy enforcement \- rather than from compliance practice, its central claim is narrower than &quot;automate compliance&quot;: evidence gathering belongs at the platform layer, but the determination that gathered evidence actually satisfies a control&\#x27;s intent should remain a deliberately structured human decision\. Recent empirical work on infrastructure\-as\-code security posture and pipeline misconfiguration rates supports continuous, machine\-gathered evidence over periodic manual sampling; separate research on human oversight of automated recommendations complicates the assumption that adding a human reviewer automatically improves decision quality, and that complication shapes how the review layer proposed here is designed\. The article closes by considering what it takes, organizationally, to move a compliance function that has long operated on manual habit toward this platform\-centered model\.
