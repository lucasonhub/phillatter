# Machine Learning for Drought Analysis and Forecasting: A Critical Review of Data Fusion, Extreme\-Event Sensitivity, Explainability and Generalization

**Autores:** Ritik Mallik; Dayal Kumar Behera; Satarupa Mohanty

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Machine learning is increasingly applied to drought monitoring and forecasting, but studies differ widely in how drought is defined, what data are used, and how models are validated, which makes reported performance difficult to compare directly\. Objectives: To explain why reported machine\-learning drought\-model performance varies across definitions, data sources and validation schemes, and to assess whether recent studies adequately address rare severe events and cross\-domain transferability\. Method: Journal articles published January 2018 to August 2026 were reviewed, starting from a 70\-record high\-relevance subset of a 359\-record discovery export and extended through citation chaining\. Publisher and DOI verification produced a final evidence base of 55 articles: 48 primary studies and 7 reviews\. Target definition, input data, model family, validation design, metrics, class\-imbalance treatment, explainability and external testing were compared across studies\. Findings: Reported performance varied with the modelling task rather than with any single algorithm: Random Forest reached 97\.7% AUC\-ROC for agricultural\-drought mapping in Queensland, SVM reached average R²=0\.90 for hydrological drought in Algeria, and a CatBoost stacking model reached R²=0\.9065 for remote\-sensing\-based drought monitoring\. These scores are not directly comparable because targets, data and validation protocols differ\. Recent studies increasingly test cross\-product and cross\-region transfer, use SHAP/LIME explainability, and adopt extreme\-aware losses, yet class\-wise reporting of severe and extreme drought, ordinal\-aware evaluation, and leakage\-resistant temporal validation remain inconsistently applied across the 48 primary studies\. Novelty: The review jointly evaluates predictive accuracy alongside rare\-event sensitivity, ordinal drought severity, leakage\-resistant validation, explainability, uncertainty and cross\-domain generalization, criteria largely absent from earlier machine\-learning drought reviews\. Keywords: Drought Forecasting, Machine Learning, Remote Sensing, Explainable AI, Extreme Drought
