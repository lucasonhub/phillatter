# Raneses, Andaran: 20kHz: Notes for a Dying World

## Descrição do feed

20kHz: Notes for a Dying World is a work of philosophical non\-fiction by independent researcher Andaran Raneses\. Written in 19 fragments, the book presents a unified critique of four structures that shape human experience: civilization, language, morality, and identity\. Its central thesis is that the self — the &quot;I&quot; — is not an origin of consciousness but a byproduct: a mnemonic device created by awareness to label, store, and recall sensation\. The work introduces two original ontological propositions: 1\. The Ontology of Failure: Being is not the result of creation ex nihilo, but of a failure to remain nothing\. In a state of emptiness, the law that would forbid emergence does not exist, as forbidding requires something to exist\. Consequently, laws, time, and physical structures are defined as long\-enduring residues of their own annihilation\. Time is redefined as a law itself, and its death as a state of absolute structural freezing where no change remains to be counted\. 2\. Experience as Refusal: Qualia is not an epiphenomenon added to neural function\. It is identified as the function itself — the movement of 1 refusing 0 to maintain distinction from nothingness\. Pain is theorized as the necessary boundary condition that prevents being from collapsing into indistinguishability with non\-being\. Problem\-creation in human behavior is reinterpreted as a mechanism for boundary\-perception\. The text develops these propositions into an ethical framework: &quot;Awareness belongs to no I\. There is only awareness experiencing\. Let ethics be this: do not harm that which experiences\.&quot; The distinction between Object \(that which does not move\) and Effect \(that which moves but is unseen, like wind, time, and evolution\) is introduced to support this ethic\. The final fragments use a phenomenological case study — &quot;Naura&quot; — to illustrate a consciousness whose &quot;I&quot; function has not stabilized, and concludes with a concept of &quot;comfortable silence&quot; as a non\-escapist relation to emptiness\. This book contains no doctrine, self\-help prescription, or ideological affiliation\. It is intended as an independent contribution to contemporary discussions on the hard problem of consciousness, the ontology of time, and existential analysis\. Intended Audience: Readers in philosophy, psychology, consciousness studies, and critical theory; graduate\-level researchers and general readers interested in existential inquiry outside institutional frameworks\.
