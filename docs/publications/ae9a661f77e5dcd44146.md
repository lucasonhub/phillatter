# REPURPOSING OF DRUGS IN THE AGE OF PRECISION MEDICINE: APPROACHES, TECHNOLOGIES, AND CLINICAL APPLICATIONS

**Autores:** Peddaboina Usha Rani; Kotla Sahithi; Tekumatlai Gowthami; Kandi Subhashini; Anitha Sadula

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Drug repurposing, also known as drug repositioning or reprofiling, involves investigating existing drugs for uses beyond their original indications\. Unlike traditional drug discovery, it utilizes existing information on pharmacology, pharmacokinetics, toxicology, clinical experience, and production, thereby reducing uncertainties in drug development\. Advances in computational biology, systems pharmacology, artificial intelligence \(AI\), high\-throughput screening \(HTS\), and omics sciences have transformed repurposing from an observation\-based approach into a systematic strategy\. Computational methods can identify drug–disease interactions through molecular targets, biological pathways, gene\-expression profiles, and clinical phenotypes, which can be validated through experiments, data, and prospective studies\. Integration with precision medicine enables approved drugs to be evaluated in patients selected by specific molecular or clinical features, while biomarker\-based stratification may identify beneficiaries, particularly in heterogeneous disorders\. Electronic health records and registries further provide real\-world data for detecting treatment signals and evaluating drug effectiveness and safety in clinical practice\. However, successful clinical implementation faces challenges, including incomplete or biased databases, computational prediction errors, and confounding and selection biases in clinical observations\. Previous approval for one indication does not eliminate the need for indication\-specific evaluation, as repurposing may require dose adjustment, additional safety testing, and clinical trials\. Limited diversity in clinical databases may also restrict treatment generalizability\. This paper reviews major repurposing approaches, including predictive modelling, transcriptomic analysis, screening, organoids, clinical data mining, and real\-world evidence, emphasizing their integration with precision medicine and the criteria for successful clinical implementation\.
