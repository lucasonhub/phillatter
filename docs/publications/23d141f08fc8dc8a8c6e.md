# Women’s Protests and Secularisation in Poland: Study of Religious Views of Protesters

**Autores:** Piotr Paweł Laskowski; Ewa Dąbrowska\-Prokopowska; Michalina Trochimowicz; Konrad Talmont\-Kamiński

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Poland is one of the most Catholic societies in Europe\. However, in recent years, the country appears to have entered a period of rapid secularisation\. At the same time, the Catholic Church has worked closely with a conservative government to promote reactionary social policies, resulting in large\-scale social unrest\. We surveyed members of a Facebook group involved in organising women’s protests and compared those results to government data on religion in Poland in order to determine the relationship between the protests and secularisation\. The results show that many of the most active participants in the protests were nonreligious well before the recent events, with those events only playing a relatively minor role in shaping their view of the Church\. Retrospective questions about religiosity indicate that the unrest primarily revealed existing secularisation, rather than causing a move further away from faith\. The youngest protesters may prove to be the exception to this rule, as secularisation appears to occur mainly before individuals reach the age of 25\. At the same time, while the studied group constitutes something of an atheist avantgarde, the very broad support for the protests reveals that the Church has lost its ability to control public opinion in Poland, reflecting a much broader rift between Church and Polish society\.
