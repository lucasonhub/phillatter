# Naoto, Fujie: The Body Without Commands: Fourteen Operational Principles and the Emergence, Transformation, and Dissolution of the Concept of the Body

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper develops The Body Without Commands as a framework for reconstructing embodied organization without presupposing a single commanding center, fixed bodily identity, or privileged endpoint of bodily control\. Xeno\-Embodied Askesis is articulated through fourteen operational cuts that reorganize posture, balance, gait, breathing, sensory weighting, tension, movement initiation, whole\-body coordination, and long\-duration bodily sedimentation\. Pre\-operational generation concerns the conditions under which bodily distinctions become available before they are stabilized as explicit commands, intentions, or representations\. Extinctional non\-privilege rejects the assumption that the disappearance of one bodily organization establishes the disappearance of bodily possibility as such\. Embodied access generation tracks how new sensorimotor and postural configurations open previously unavailable forms of discrimination, coordination, and action, while access redesign concerns deliberate reorganization of the pathways through which bodily states become reachable\. Reality penetration identifies cases in which bodily reconfiguration propagates beyond immediate experience and alters later movement, environment coupling, habit, social interaction, or technological interface\. Bounded nonrequirement designates cases in which a previously necessary bodily route, posture, command structure, or coordination pattern ceases to be required within a limited configuration without becoming universally dispensable\. The broader claim is that the body is better understood as a dynamically reconfigurable field of operational access than as a single command\-governed unity\. \(direct link \)
