# Practice and dual impact of an undergraduate\-graduate collaborative learning model in pharmaceutical education

**Autores:** Weiyan Hu; Shuda YANG; Ju Li; Xiaomi Yang; Lanchun Zhang; 于浩飞; Lu Lu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Traditional hierarchical teaching silos undergraduate and graduate education, hindering cross\-level knowledge interaction and students’ comprehensive competency development\. How to integrate learners at different academic stages to boost interactive learning and educational quality has long been a difficult issue in pharmaceutical pedagogy\. This study constructed and implemented an undergraduate\-graduate collaborative learning model to tackle these drawbacks\. Methods This study adopted a non\-randomized quasi\-experimental design with intact classes\. Fixed collaborative groups were formed in a Pharmaceutical Botany and Pharmacognosy course to complete a semester\-long inquiry\-based project\. Intervention effectiveness was evaluated via multi\-source data, including single\-group self\-reported questionnaires \(intervention group only: n = 42 undergraduates\), objective final examination score comparisons \(experimental n = 42 vs\. control n = 48\), and thematic analysis of standardized reflection journals and semi\-structured interviews\. Comprehensive statistical indicators, including group means, standard deviations, mean differences, 95% confidence intervals, exact p \-values, and Cohen&\#x27;s d effect sizes, were supplemented for all comparative analyses\. Normality and homogeneity of variance assumptions were fully verified to ensure analytical rigor\. Results Subjective questionnaire findings are exclusive descriptive self\-reported outcomes of the intervention group: undergraduates reported prominent self\-reported improvements in literature retrieval \(88\.1%\) and problem\-solving \(85\.7%\), while graduates reported favorable growth in knowledge integration \(93\.3%\) and academic communication skills \(93\.3%\)\. Objectively, the experimental group outperformed the control group in final scores \( p &lt; 0\.05\) and comprehensive analysis questions scores \( p &lt; 0\.01\)\. Qualitative thematic analysis identified peer role model guidance and tiered knowledge bidirectional transfer as key potential mechanisms underlying the collaborative learning effects\. Conclusions This collaborative learning model is associated with the formation of a mutually beneficial cross\-grade learning community, correlates with reduced hierarchical barriers and shows potential positive associations with improved learning outcomes for both undergraduates and graduates\. The model exhibits promising application potential for optimizing teaching quality and cultivating mentoring competencies in pharmaceutical education; however, scalability and generalizability remain tentative rather than empirically verified universal effects, and no definitive causal effects can be established due to quasi\-experimental design limitations\.
