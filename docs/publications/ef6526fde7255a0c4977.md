# Growth–recovery decoupling in the timing of geomagnetic disturbances across five solar cycles

**Autores:** J\. A\. Wanliss

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The timing of geomagnetic disturbances depends on energy accumulation and releases within the 8 magnetosphere, and shows how that balance is organized by solar cycle\. We decompose every 9 negative excursion of the 1\-min SYM\-H index over 1981–2026 \(78,096 events at the median 10 threshold, spanning solar cycles 21 through 25\) into a growth lifetime τg, from downward threshold 11 crossing to the first attainment of its minimum, and a recovery lifetime τr, from minimum to recovery 12 crossing\. Both durations follow finite\-range power laws with distinct exponents, μg = 1\.079 ± 0\.005 13 and μr = 1\.305 ± 0\.004\. The recovery exponent is larger at every threshold and in every yearly 14 window\. Under the standard first\-attainment convention the exponents respond oppositely to solar 15 activity: μg rises with sunspot number \(r = \+0\.59, p = 7 × 10\-5 in independent windows\) but μr is 16 statistically flat\. The solar cycle influences the temporal width over which the scaling law extends\. 17 The solar cycle is therefore imprinted on low\-latitude disturbance timing selectively: through the 18 growth exponent and through the temporal cutoff scales of both channels, but not through the 19 recovery exponent\. The exponent ordering survives alternative estimators, independence treatments, 20 and an agnostic treatment of tied minima, and the undivided excursion recovers the single threshold\-21 robust burst\-lifetime law reported previously \(Wanliss and Weygand, 2007\)\. The apparent invariance 22 of bursts arises because the recovery channel controls the tail\. Growth and recovery timing are 23 therefore decoupled\. 24
