# Training the Next Generation of Future Surgical Residents: A Scoping Review and Proposal for an Undergraduate Curriculum From the Italian Polispecialistic Society of Young Surgeons

**Autores:** Gianmario Edoardo Poto; Marianna Peruffo; Manfredi Giuseppe Anzaldi; Andrea Castaldi; Gianmarco Elia; Giulia Misasi; Giulia Rovero; Martina Lenzi; Elisa Bassan; Francesca Gigola; Domenico Soriero; Giosuè Giordano Incognito; Gennaro Melone; Roberto Cirillo; Marco Angrisani; Maria Rosaria Mastrangeli; Alessandro Coppola; Michele Maruccia; Gaetano Gallo; Arcangelo Picciariello; Alessandro Pasculli; Ludovico Carbone

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

AIM: The transition from medical school to surgical residency is marked by a persistent gap between theoretical instruction and clinical readiness\. In Italy, variability in training programs across centers creates persistent gaps in residents’ medical education\. Scientific societies are asked to identify current training gaps and define the core competencies required for effective surgical training\. We propose an undergraduate curriculum to support last\-year medical students in Italy\.METHODS: A scoping review of the literature focusing on structured surgical curricula for undergraduate medical students was conducted\. A suggested “curriculum” was introduced at the 2025 Congress of the Italian Polispecialistic Society of Young Surgeons \(SPIGC\) in Bari, Italy\.RESULTS: The SPIGC curriculum includes seven domains: \(1\) soft skills: communication, informed consent, and interdisciplinary collaboration; \(2\) clinical and diagnostic skills: outpatient clinic management and perioperative care; \(3\) hygiene, prevention, healthcare systems; \(4\) technical skills; \(5\) emergency and trauma surgery; \(6\) perioperative management, integrating Enhanced Recovery After Surgery \(ERAS\) protocols, nutritional assessment, and infections; \(7\) evidence\-based medicine and recommendations\. The SPIGC curriculum highlights the importance of simulation labs, hands\-on workshops, and online learning, while providing clear pathways for fellowships or masters\. It also introduces artificial intelligence \(AI\) systems as a modern learning tool, while highlighting the importance of their safe and responsible use\.CONCLUSIONS: The SPIGC curriculum may provide a structured plan to improve surgical education for medical students in Italy and to align it with international standards\. Its acceptability and impact on training quality and patient outcomes remain to be evaluated through future consensus and prospective studies\.
