# Silin, Valerii: Embodied Biographical Alignment \(EBA\): A Developmental Alignment Hypothesis Based on Embodiment, Biography, Finitude, and Experience Integration

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_\. 2026Current alignment methods largely attempt to transmit human intent through preferences, rules, constitutions, oversight, demonstrations, or learned representations\. This paper proposes a complementary developmental hypothesis: some human values may be difficult to acquire robustly without temporally extended, embodied, socially embedded, and finite experience\. Embodied Biographical Alignment \(EBA\) is an experimental architecture in which multiple autonomous agents live bounded biographies, accumulate episodic and autobiographical memory, form durable relationships, encounter irreversible consequences, undergo controlled cognitive aging, and later contribute biographical experience to a more capable source system\. The central requirement is transformative rather than archival integration: the target is not a source system that merely possesses records of finite lives, but one whose internal organization has been persistently altered by their integration\. EBA does not assume machine consciousness, nor claim that experience automatically produces morality\. It instead defines a testable question: whether biography, embodiment, finitude, and longitudinal social participation improve value generalization, calibrated restraint, respect for autonomy, corrigibility, and resistance to simplistic proxy optimization beyond text\-only or preference\-only baselines\. We position EBA relative to embodied and enactive cognition, lived\-experience approaches, lifelong\-agent memory, progress alignment, machine finitude, and scalable oversight\. A prior\-art scan finds substantial overlap in individual components but no exact match for the complete architecture identified here\. We define falsifiable hypotheses, ablations, an integration test, ethical constraints, and a limited extension for alignment to a changing human population\. \(direct link \)
