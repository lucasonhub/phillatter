# Guarded Block Transfers for Polytope Distance: Linear Convergence with Explicit Constants and Gap\-Certified Screening for SVM Training

**Autores:** Mayank Gupta

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Hard\-margin SVM training is the distance problem between two convex hulls, which the Triangle Algorithm \(TA\) solves with a certified duality gap\. We propose \\emph\{guarded block transfers\}: $k$ Mitchell\-\-Demyanov\-\-Malozemov \(MDM\) transfers per scan are aggregated into one exact line search, guarded by a comparison with the single best transfer at a cost independent of the number of points $n$, and replaced by an away step when the leading transfer is capacity\-clipped\. On the Minkowski\-difference polytope, where the objective is $1$\-strongly convex, we prove global linear convergence with explicit constants and no combinatorial swap\-step bounds; the per\-step contraction is $k$\-independent and only the drop\-count bound grows with $k$, so aggregation is provably safe while the saving in scans is measured, not proved\. Since the TA certificate is the Frank\-\-Wolfe gap divided by the current distance, gap\-safe screening applies, and we bound the iteration after which it has removed every zero\-weight point off the optimal faces by $O\\\!\\left\(\\tfrac\{k\}ρ\\log\\tfrac\{1\}τ\\right\)$ up to logarithmic factors in the geometry, $ρ$ the rate constant and $τ$ the optimal\-face margin\. On five LIBSVM benchmarks, with fairer baselines than our submission, the solver matches the baselines to four or five digits wherever both converge, ties LIBSVM on sparse\-support hard margins and beats our blended pairwise conditional gradients \(BPCG\)\. But our full\-cache sequential minimal optimisation \(SMO\) code ties or beats it on kernel $L\_2$ margins, LIBLINEAR&\#x27;s dual solver is up to $4\.5\\times$ faster on the separable $L\_2$ margin, and our solver is one to three orders of magnitude slower than LIBLINEAR&\#x27;s Newton solver on dense\-support $L\_2$ margins and than LIBSVM on a nearly touching kernel hull\. Its edge is the certificate, not speed\.
