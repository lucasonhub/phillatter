# Securing Digital Governance: A Doctrinal and Empirical Analysis of the Legal Framework Governing e\-Government System Protection in Tanzania

**Autores:** Mary Juma

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Tanzania has moved much of its public administration onto shared digital platforms, among them the Government Electronic Payment Gateway \(GePG\), the Government Enterprise Service Bus \(GovESB\) and the e\-Office system\. The legal protection of these platforms rests on Part VII of the e\-Government Act \[Cap 273 R\.E\. 2023\], supported by the Cybercrimes Act, the Electronic Transactions Act and the Personal Data Protection Act\. This article asks whether that framework secures government systems in practice or only on paper\. It combines doctrinal analysis of the statutes, as consolidated in the Revised Edition of 2023 and amended in 2024, with qualitative data from semi\-structured interviews with thirty respondents in Morogoro Region, comprising ICT officers, administrators, users, members of the public and legal practitioners\. It draws on Information Systems Security Theory, Deterrence Theory and Protection Motivation Theory\. The article finds that the statutes are broad in coverage but thin in enforceable detail\. Sections 37 and 38 of the e\-Government Act impose security duties on public institutions without an institutional sanction, a timeline for remedial action, or a defined threshold for reporting incidents, while criminal liability under section 57 falls on individuals even where the underlying failure is budgetary or structural\. Responsibility is spread across the e\-Government Authority, the Tanzania Communications Regulatory Authority and its national CERT, the Personal Data Protection Commission and the police, with no statutory rule on who leads when an incident touches all of them\. Courts still apply the electronic evidence rules unevenly, and a thin treaty base limits the practical reach of Tanzania&\#x27;s extraterritorial jurisdiction\. The article argues that effectiveness depends less on enacting further statutes than on precision, enforceability and coordination, and it proposes targeted amendments, an incident coordination protocol, standardised forensic rules and ratification of the United Nations Convention against Cybercrime, which Tanzania signed in October 2025\.
