# The Subject in the Era of the Anonymous Text

**Autores:** Martin Sjardijn

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Foreword To better understand the meaning and scope of language in light of structuralism—the school of thought whose foundations were laid by pioneers such as Ferdinand de Saussure and Roman Jakobson—I take the philosophy of Paul Ricœur as my guide\. By mirroring his vision of the subject, discourse, and the hermeneutic detour via artificial intelligence against current developments surrounding Large Language Models, a new perspective emerges on how meaning comes into being today\. Whereas classical structuralism primarily conceived of language as a closed, anonymous system of signs and grammatical relations, Ricœur already demonstrated that meaning truly comes to life in the concrete utterance: in the moment when a speaking subject relates to another and to the world through language\. The stormy rise of generative AI models, which seamlessly generate coherent sentences and poetic constructs based on vast quantities of human text, seems at first glance to be the ultimate proof of the structuralist thesis that language can speak itself\. Through a dialogue with and via AI, I test Ricœur&\#x27;s critique of that view\. This inquiry focuses on the question of where the machine processing of language structures ends and where human, ethical, and lived meaning\-making remains indispensable\.
