# Cu 2 ⁺\-responsive hyaluronic acid nanoplatform for targeted curcumin delivery and suppression of osteolysis\- and metastasis\-related phenotypes in lung adenocarcinoma

**Autores:** Jian\-Gang Chen; Ao Xu; Yu\-Sheng Tang; Zhen Yang; Shi\-Bo Gao; Jiang\-an Tang; Yu\-Chen Song; Yan Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Bone metastasis is a major cause of morbidity and mortality in lung adenocarcinoma and is closely associated with epithelial–mesenchymal transition \(EMT\) and tumor\-derived osteolytic factors\. Although curcumin exhibits promising anti\-tumor and anti\-metastatic activities, its poor solubility and low bioavailability limit its therapeutic application\. In this study, a Cu 2 ⁺\-responsive hyaluronic acid nanoplatform modified with compound 1 \(1\-HA\) was developed for targeted curcumin delivery \(1\-HA@Cur\) and Cu 2 ⁺\-responsive fluorescence sensing\. The physicochemical properties, Cu 2 ⁺ sensing performance, and biological effects of 1\-HA@Cur were systematically evaluated under in vitro conditions using A549 lung adenocarcinoma cells\. Compared with free curcumin, 1\-HA@Cur exhibited enhanced antiproliferative activity and induced a significant dose\-dependent reduction in cell viability\. Enzyme\-Linked Immunosorbent Assay \(ELISA\) analysis demonstrated that nanoparticle treatment significantly decreased the secretion of parathyroid hormone\-related protein \(PTHrP\), a key mediator involved in osteolytic signaling\. Furthermore, qRT\-PCR analysis revealed that 1\-HA@Cur modulated EMT\-associated molecular changes, characterized by reduced Vimentin expression and increased E\-cadherin expression\. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that 1\-HA@Cur effectively suppresses lung adenocarcinoma cell growth and attenuates osteolytic\- and metastasis\-related phenotypes in vitro \. This multifunctional nanoplatform integrates targeted curcumin delivery with Cu 2 ⁺\-responsive fluorescence sensing, providing a potential strategy for lung adenocarcinoma therapy and copper\-ion\-associated microenvironment analysis, while further validation in more complex biological models is required\.
