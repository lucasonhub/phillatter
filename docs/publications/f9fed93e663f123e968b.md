# MJO Predictability: Its Sources and Limits

**Autores:** Meriem Krouma; Yakelyn R\. Jauregui; Boualem Khouider; Brandon W\. Kerns; Daehyun Kim; Hyemi Kim; Kirsten J\. Mayer; Pallav Ray; Bo‐Wen Shen; Frédéric Vitart; Yuntao Wei; Chidong Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract The Madden–Julian Oscillation \(MJO\) is the dominant mode of intraseasonal variability in the tropical atmosphere and a central source of subseasonal\-to\-seasonal \(S2S\) predictability\. Despite decades of progress, theoretical understanding of its forecast horizon remains elusive and contested\. This workshop\-inspired review synthesizes insights on MJO predictability, highlighting advances in theory, modeling, and machine learning applications, while critically assessing persistent challenges\. We revisit the layered concepts of intrinsic, potential, and practical predictability, with intrinsic predictability set by the flow itself, potential predictability defined by idealized initial conditions, and practical predictability constrained by real observations, data availability, and forecasting methodologies\. We examine the physical sources and statistical approaches underlying prediction skill, and evaluate current metrics and verification methodologies, emphasizing that evaluation metrics must be responsive to user needs and that observing systems should be designed in concert with model requirements\. We then assess limits imposed by physical processes such as MJO initiation and the Maritime Continent propagation barrier, as well as their potentially unclear modification under climate change\. Finally, we outline future research opportunities—spanning observations, hybrid modeling, and user\-centered forecasting—and argue for a more integrative prediction framework in which dynamical, statistical, hybrid, and machine\-learning approaches are treated not as competitors but as complementary components of a unified MJO forecasting system, while identifying open questions that will shape the next decade of MJO research\.
