# Adjudicator sensitivity of safety estimates in large language models for open\-ended cardiovascular reasoning: a blinded multi\-adjudicator benchmark study running title: adjudicator variability in LLM safety

**Autores:** Aykan Çelik; Tuncay Kırış; Uğur Kocabaş; Mustafa Karaca

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Large language models \(LLMs\) are increasingly evaluated for safety\-critical clinical reasoning, but safety estimates in open\-ended tasks may depend on expert adjudication thresholds\. We evaluated 300 blinded responses generated by three API\-accessed LLM configurations across 100 cardiovascular scenarios\. Two cardiologists independently scored all responses, and the original workflow used predefined triggers for safety adjudication\. First\-adjudicator ascertainment was subsequently completed for all responses, after which a separate non\-author cardiologist independently re\-adjudicated all 300 responses while blinded to model identity, reviewer information, and prior decisions; 53 discordant classifications were then assessed by a third blinded cardiologist using a majority\-resolution rule fixed before receipt of the third\-adjudicator results\. Continuous quality\-score reliability was poor \(ICC 0\.028\)\. The first adjudicator classified 53/300 responses \(17\.7%\) as safety\-positive, whereas the independent second adjudicator classified 0/300\. First–second crude agreement was 82\.3%, but positive agreement was 0%\. Third\-adjudicator resolution yielded 8/300 majority\-classified safety\-relevant errors \(2\.7%, 95% CI 1\.4–5\.2%\)\. Safety estimates in open\-ended cardiovascular LLM benchmarks were highly adjudicator\-sensitive\. Multi\-rater reliability and transparent resolution procedures should accompany numerical safety estimates, which should not be interpreted as real\-world clinical error prevalence\.
