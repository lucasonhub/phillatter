# Evaluating the accuracy and efficiency of Cloud\-to\-IDA for drift\-based collapse assessment of steel frames under near\- and far\-field motions

**Autores:** Elmira Shemshaki; Behrouz Asgarian; Zeinab Monirvaghefi

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Accurate estimation of seismic collapse capacity is essential for performance\-based earthquake engineering and fragility assessment\. Incremental Dynamic Analysis \(IDA\) is widely regarded as a reliable benchmark for collapse capacity evaluation; however, its high computational cost limits its application in large\-scale or rapid assessment studies\. This study evaluates the accuracy and computational efficiency of conventional Cloud Analysis and the Cloud\-to\-IDA procedure compared with IDA for steel moment\-resisting frames subjected to near\-field and far\-field ground motions and investigates the influence of structural height and ground\-motion characteristics on the accuracy of each assessment method\. Three two\-dimensional steel moment\-resisting frames with 3, 5, and 9 stories were modeled in OpenSees and analyzed using 22 far\-field and 28 near\-field ground motion records from the FEMA P695 suite\. Spectral acceleration at the fundamental period, Sa\(T1\), was adopted as the intensity measure, and maximum inter\-story drift ratio was used as the engineering demand parameter\. A drift\-based collapse limit state was defined using HAZUS inter\-story drift thresholds to provide a consistent comparison basis among the three methods\. The results show that conventional Cloud Analysis can produce height\- and record\-dependent bias, with the bias diminishing as frame height increases from 3 to 9 stories, in estimating median collapse capacity\. The largest discrepancy was observed for the 3\-story frame under far\-field records, where Cloud Analysis overestimated the IDA\-based median collapse capacity by approximately 20%\. In contrast, Cloud\-to\-IDA closely reproduced the IDA results across all studied frame heights and ground\-motion categories, with median collapse capacity errors limited to only a few percent\. Dispersion estimates from Cloud\-to\-IDA were also more consistent with IDA than those obtained from conventional Cloud Analysis\. In terms of computational demand, Cloud\-to\-IDA substantially reduced the number of nonlinear dynamic analyzes and achieved approximately 86–91% reduction in computational time relative to IDA, while maintaining close agreement in both median capacity and dispersion\. These findings indicate that Cloud\-to\-IDA can provide an efficient and reliable alternative for drift\-based collapse fragility assessment of steel moment\-resisting frames, provided that its applicability is carefully limited to the structural systems, ground\-motion sets, and collapse criteria considered in the study\.
