# Amelanotic subungual melanoma becoming clinically apparent within 24 h after minor blunt trauma: a case report

**Autores:** Huiru Wang; Peng Wang; Yanyan Suo; Kang Lu; Qingfeng Xue; Huahua Li; Rui Gao; Yunchang Guo; Songjia Qu; Xiaowei Cai

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Amelanotic subungual melanoma \(ASM\) is a rare variant of nail unit melanoma \(NUM\) lacking melanin pigmentation, frequently misdiagnosed as a benign post\-traumatic or infectious lesion\. To our knowledge, ASM becoming clinically apparent within 24 h following minor blunt trauma has not been reported previously\. We report the case of a 66\-year\-old male who presented with a persistent flesh\-colored nodule beneath his right thumbnail that was manifested within 24 h after minor blunt trauma caused by nail clippers\. The lesion was non\-tender, non\-pigmented, and unresponsive to 2 weeks of antibiotic therapy\. Histopathology revealed diffuse subepidermal proliferation of atypical cells with signet\-ring morphology\. Immunohistochemistry \(IHC\) demonstrated positivity for vimentin, HMB45, and Melan\-A, and negativity for cytokeratin 5 \(CK5\) and S\-100\. Ki\-67 was approximately 30%; Breslow thickness was 2\.5 mm\. Final diagnosis: ASM, AJCC stage IIA \(T3aN0M0\)\. Diagnostic excisional biopsy with a 2\-mm margin was performed; sentinel lymph node biopsy of two axillary lymph nodes was negative\. At 1\-year follow\-up, no local recurrence or distant metastasis was observed\. This case demonstrates that ASM can become clinically evident within 24 h after acute trauma, likely through unmasking of a pre\-existing subclinical tumor\. For post\-traumatic nail lesions unresponsive to antibiotics, early biopsy with a comprehensive IHC panel \(including Melan\-A, HMB45, and S\-100\) is essential\.
