# Altered cytokine profiles and elevated lactate levels in children with autism spectrum disorder: indications of a dysregulated immune\-metabolic network?

**Autores:** Nicole Beyer; Theresia Volk; Hella Luksch; Melanie Ring; Nora C\. Vetter; Angela Roesen\-Wolff; Min Ae Lee‐Kirsch; Veit Roessner

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Autism spectrum disorder \(ASD\) is a lifelong neurodevelopmental condition characterized by difficulties in social communication and restricted, repetitive behavior\. The etiology and pathophysiology of ASD remain largely unknown; however, a complex interplay of genetic and environmental factors is widely assumed\. More recently, altered cytokine/chemokine profiles and a potential vulnerability to mitochondrial alterations have been proposed\. Methods We measured blood lactate and pyruvate and quantified a cytokine/chemokine panel including IL\-1β, IL\-2, IL\-6, TNF\-α, IL\-8, IFN\-β, RANTES, MCP\-1/CCL2, MIP\-1α/CCL3, MIP\-1β/CCL4, CCL11/Eotaxin\-1 and CXCL10/IP\-10 in a group of autistic children in comparison to a non\-autistic age\-, gender\- and IQ\-matched control group\. Results We observed elevated lactate levels, lower concentrations of IL\-1β and TNF\-α in autistic\- compared with control children\. In addition, lactate, MIP\-1β/CCL4 and TNF\-α was significantly associated with behavioral measures of autistic behavior\. Pyruvate, the lactate/pyruvate ratio and all other analytes did not differ between groups\. Discussion We conclude that the observed cytokine/chemokine profile may reflect a dysregulated immunological network in autistic children\. Elevated lactate may point to metabolic abnormalities with possible mitochondrial involvement\. However, unchanged pyruvate and lactate/pyruvate ratio do not support overt mitochondrial dysfunction\. These findings require replication in larger studies\.
