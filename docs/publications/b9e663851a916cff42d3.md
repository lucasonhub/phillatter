# The Digital Siege and Its Self\-Backlash: Tech\-Capital Collusion in DeLillo&\#x27;s Cosmopolis

**Autores:** Qing Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Don DeLillo&\#x27;s 2003 novel Cosmopolis, framed as a single\-day narrative set in early 21st\-century Manhattan, delivers a prescient account of the profound collusion between technology and capital in the digital age and its attendant social consequences\. Drawing on Fredric Jameson&\#x27;s seminal theoretical framework The Cultural Logic of Late Capitalism, this paper systematically unpacks the construction mechanism and runaway logic of the &quot;digital siege&quot; depicted in the novel\. By virtue of digitization, algorithmization and automation, technology thoroughly remakes the real world into a tradable, manipulable system of virtual symbols across three dimensions: perceptual mediation, spatial form, and linguistic carrier\. This study argues that DeLillo incisively exposes the inherent contradiction at the heart of the tech\-capital collusion: virtual expansion divorced from the grounding of reality will inevitably culminate in systemic collapse through self\-backlash\. Tracing this analytical trajectory from &quot;construction&quot; to &quot;loss of control&quot;, the paper seeks to systematically articulate DeLillo&\#x27;s literary critique of tech\-capital collusion and its practical cautionary relevance to the contemporary AI era\.
