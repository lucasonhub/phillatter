# Distribution\-Shift\-Aware Conformal Prediction for Meteorite and Asteroid Reflectance Spectroscopy: Finite\-Sample Class Guarantees from the Laboratory to the Telescope

**Autores:** Ali Amir Khairbek

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Machine\-learning classifiers trained on laboratory reflectance spectra of meteorites are increasingly used to interpret asteroid observations, but they are routinely deployed outside the conditions under which they were validated: telescope targets differ from laboratory powders in grain size, viewing geometry, space weathering, class abundances, and sensor range\. We introduce distribution\-shift\-aware conformal prediction to planetary reflectance spectroscopy, converting any spectral classifier into a set\-valued predictor with finite\-sample coverage guarantees, and we measure exactly how those guarantees behave when the deployment assumptions fail\. From 16,291 RELAB Planetary Data System products, we curate 2,949 meteorite spectra into a leakage\-controlled, provenance\-tracked data set spanning 9 classes and 756 physical specimens\. Same\-specimen controlled experiments show that empirical coverage degrades with physical shift severity: grain\-size mismatch removes up to 22 points of coverage, pulsed\-laser space weathering removes 15 points on identical rocks, viewing geometry up to 40–60° phase leaves coverage intact, and a linear domain\-classifier AUC tracks severity while proving necessary but not sufficient for failure\. Class\-conditional calibration is invariant to arbitrary class\-prior changes and rescues the scientifically critical CI class, which marginal calibration silently abandons\. Likelihood\-ratio\-weighted conformal prediction restores nominal coverage under a pure covariate shift but misfires when the shift carries a label component, yielding a practical combined prescription\. Transferred to 694 MITHNEOS near\-Earth\-asteroid spectra, the calibrated sets remain informative and recover the Itokawa and Vesta\-family ground truths, whereas returned\-sample tests on Ryugu and Bennu expose exactly where representation, not calibration, becomes the binding constraint\.
