# RESISTANCE TO CHANGE ORIGINATING IN MANAGEMENT: A MIXED\-METHODS STUDY IN ROAD CONSTRUCTION COMPANIES IN COLOMBIA

**Autores:** Manuel Alfonso Benitez Amaya

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Road construction acknowledges the need to innovate while adopting new processes and technologies slowly\. The literature has located resistance to change in operational staff and has treated management as the agent that manages it, whereas resistance originating in decision\-making levels remains underexamined\. The aim was to characterize the managerial competencies and technological tools of managers in road construction organizations in Colombia, and to identify their challenges and opportunities\. A mixed\-methods, descriptive and exploratory design was adopted with convenience sampling: a questionnaire applied to 84 leaders and ten interviews with road project managers, analyzed through bivariate statistics and content analysis\. All six bivariate cross\-tabulations were significant \(p &lt; 0\.001\), with Cramér&\#x27;s V between 0\.374 and 0\.526 and Spearman correlations between 0\.45 and 0\.72; the strongest association linked receptiveness with the management of innovative projects, and the highest correlation linked data use with commitment to training\. The interviews showed that resistance is not confined to staff: four of the ten managers located it in bosses, executives and owners, and even in the self\-censorship of managers themselves\. It is concluded that resistance to change is a bidirectional phenomenon and that innovation depends less on available technology than on managerial competencies, training and communication\.
