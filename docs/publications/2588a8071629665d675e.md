# Whole\-body volatile organic compound profiling reveals metabolic pathway perturbations in a murine polymicrobial sepsis model: a feasibility study

**Autores:** Wan\-yi Feng; Yu Chen; Xiu\-ya Zhang; Rong Fu; Qin Hu; Fengfeng Mo; Jia\-feng Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Sepsis rewires host metabolism, and these changes may be readable in the volatile organic compounds \(VOCs\) released from the body\. Whether whole\-body VOC profiles can be mapped onto specific disrupted biochemical pathways has not been tested\. In this discovery\-phase, single\-time\-point feasibility study, polymicrobial sepsis was induced by cecal ligation and puncture \(CLP\) in C57BL/6 mice \(14 CLP, 10 sham controls\)\. Whole\-body headspace gases were collected 12 h after surgery and analysed by thermal desorption\-gas chromatography\-mass spectrometry\. After preprocessing, 113 features were tested univariately with Benjamini\-Hochberg correction and by orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis \(OPLS\-DA\)\. Candidate VOCs \(FDR &lt; 0\.1, \|log2FC\| &gt; 0\.26, VIP &gt; 1\.0\) were annotated against PubChem, KEGG COMPOUND, and the Human Metabolome Database\. OPLS\-DA separated the groups \(R2X = 0\.489, R2Y = 0\.981, Q2 = 0\.777; P = 0\.001 by 1,000 permutations\), and leave\-one\-out cross\-validation classified 95\.8% of animals correctly, though n = 24\. Of 54 candidate VOCs, 18 had established endogenous origins; 16 fell and 2 rose in septic mice\. Pathway mapping indicated four perturbed domains: lipid peroxidation \(12 depleted VOCs\), ketone body metabolism \(3 decreased\), cholesterol/isoprenoid metabolism \(isoprene, decreased\), and putative gut microbiota\-associated metabolism \(toluene and carbon disulfide, increased\)\. All 18 endogenous VOCs kept candidate status after environment\-derived VOCs were removed\. Because whole\-body headspace volatiles superimpose tissue metabolism, ventilation, activity, and background, these results suggest the septic volatilome may be mapped onto distinct pathway perturbations; ventilation and activity effects cannot be excluded\. These findings generate testable hypotheses for non\-invasive metabolic phenotyping in sepsis\.
