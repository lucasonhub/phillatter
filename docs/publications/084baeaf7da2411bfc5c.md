# Underused capability and burnout: distributive\-justice appraisal and psychological capital among Chinese public\-sector employees

**Autores:** Zhe Cui; Chenxi Sun; Zhao Xi\-nan

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Occupational burnout may co\-occur with employees’ appraisal that their capability is underused and their contributions insufficiently recognized\. Methods Guided primarily by conservation of resources theory and social exchange theory, and situated within the broader Job Demands–Resources framework, this study analyzed a two\-section survey of 415 employees from 21 Chinese public\-sector organizations\. Psychological capital \(PsyCap\) was assessed in the first section; perceived overqualification \(POQ\), less favorable distributive\-justice appraisal \(LF\-DJA\), and burnout were assessed together in the later section\. A latent\-variable structural equation model examined the core associations, with complementary interaction regressions and sensitivity analyses\. Results POQ showed positive total and LF\-DJA\-related indirect associations with emotional exhaustion and cynicism, but its residual direct associations were not statistically distinguishable from zero\. Low professional efficacy \(LPE\) showed a different exploratory pattern\. PsyCap most consistently conditioned the POQ–LF\-DJA association\. LF\-DJA\-related moderation depended on outcome and specification; conditional indirect associations were not supported in the model containing both outcome interaction terms\. Procedure and occupational\-context adjustment produced similar focal coefficients\. Contribution The findings distinguish capability\-role appraisal, distributive\-justice appraisal, and personal resources in an employee well\-being account\. Limitations The contemporaneous assessment of POQ, LF\-DJA, and burnout precludes temporal or causal conclusions; multiplicity and limited precision qualify weaker findings\.
