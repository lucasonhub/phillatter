# The hidden challenges of return migration: Linguistic and academic re\-adaptation of Syrian students to the Arabic Education System

**Autores:** Sevda Doğan Dolapçıoğlu; Yunus Emre Akbana; İlayda Uzun; Gülten Koşar

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

While extensive research has examined refugee students&\#x27; educational adaptation in host countries, the educational reintegration phase following voluntary return remains underexplored\. This qualitative study investigates the linguistic and academic adaptation experiences of Syrian students transitioning from Turkish\-medium education in Türkiye to Arabic\-medium schooling in Syria after voluntary return\. The study addresses an overarching question: how do returnee students experience the linguistic, academic, social, and psychosocial dimensions of this reverse educational transition? Using an exploratory qualitative design, data were collected through a seven\-item open\-ended written questionnaire from 15 middle\-school students who had received several years of formal schooling in Türkiye\. Qualitative content analysis revealed that return does not constitute a simple linguistic recovery, but rather a reverse academic transition characterized by profound difficulties with academic Arabic, conceptual transfer, written production, and in\-class assessment practices\. The findings indicate that students&\#x27; challenges were shaped not only by uneven Arabic literacy development but also by curriculum misalignment, pedagogical practices that participants perceived as more teacher\-directed, limited peer interaction, and assessment practices that they perceived as unclear\. Furthermore, psychosocial pressures, including re\-migration\-related distress, concentration difficulties, and increased family responsibilities, complicated their academic adaptation\. Ultimately, these struggles reflect systemic institutional and contextual mismatches rather than individual deficits\. By conceptualizing return as a distinct educational phase, this study contributes to the broader discourses on refugee education and bilingualism\. The implications underscore the critical need for academic Arabic bridging programs, background\-sensitive assessment frameworks, psychosocially responsive pedagogies, and school\-based psychosocial support to ensure equitable reintegration into Arabic\-medium schooling\.
