# Long\-term sport participation is associated with a selective auditory\-musical performance profile

**Autores:** Bartłomiej Jacek Swebodziński; Kuba Korta; Jacek Łantowski

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Long\-term sport participation has been associated with selective differences in temporal and perceptual\-cognitive processing, but it remains unclear whether such differences extend to auditory\-musical perception\. We examined overall and component\-level performance on the Montreal Battery of Evaluation of Amusia \(MBEA\) in 83 participants: 31 sport\-trained participants and 52 psychology\-student controls\. Between\-group differences were tested using ordinary least\-squares regression with HC3 robust standard errors, adjusting for age and years of formal musical education; Holm correction was applied across the six MBEA subtests\. Sport\-trained participants obtained higher Total MBEA scores than controls \( B = 15\.34, p = 0\.002\)\. At the component level, they scored higher on the a priori temporal outcomes Rhythm \( B = 2\.87, Holm\-adjusted p = 0\.030\) and Meter \( B = 3\.72, Holm\-adjusted p = 0\.012\)\. An exploratory difference was also observed for Memory \( B = 4\.20, Holm\-adjusted p = 0\.002\), whereas Contour did not remain significant after correction and no significant differences were found for Scale or Interval \. Supplementary d ′ models and their leave\-one\-out sensitivity analyses converged on the same pattern for Rhythm , Meter , and Memory \. Within the sport\-trained group, years of sport participation were not positively associated with Total MBEA performance after adjustment for age \( B = −0\.78, p = 0\.108\)\. These findings suggest that long\-term sport participation is associated with a selective rather than generalized profile of auditory\-musical differences, with the clearest group differences in temporal processing and an exploratory difference in recognition memory\. The cross\-sectional design does not permit causal attribution to sport training, and the observed profile may reflect training\-related adaptations, pre\-existing individual differences, or both\.
