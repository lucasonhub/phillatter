# In situ reduction\-driven formation of sub\-nanometer alternating heterostructures

**Autores:** Yuan Huang; Zhenyu Ren; Yaru Gao; Haoyang Li; Hai Yan Xiao; Qingda Liu; Xun Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Sub\-nanometer alternating heterostructures provide a platform for modulating electronic structure and interfacial reactivity through strong atom\-level coupling, with potential applications in energy conversion\. However, their precise construction remains challenging\. Here we show a strategy for constructing sub\-nanometer alternating heterostructures through confined spinodal decomposition and ligand\-mediated in situ reduction\. Oleylamine ligands adsorbed on NiMoO4 sub\-nanowires act as mild reductants, selectively reducing Ni2\+ to spatially confined metallic Ni domains and forming coherent Ni/NiMoO4 heterointerfaces\. Experimental and theoretical analyses suggest that the Ni/NiMoO4 interfaces modify the local electronic structure and facilitate the adsorption and activation of hydrogen evolution intermediates\. The resulting catalyst requires an overpotential of 14\.4 ± 2\.9 mV to reach 10 mA cm−2 in 1 M KOH and delivers 2 A cm−2 at 1\.62 V in an anion\-exchange membrane electrolyzer at 80 oC, with stable operation over 2200 h at 25 °C\. These findings provide insights into the construction and interfacial regulation of sub\-nanometer heterostructures through in situ reduction\. Sub\-nanometer heterostructures offer opportunities to tune interfacial reactivity, but their precise construction remains challenging\. Here, the authors report alternating Ni/NiMoO₄ heterostructures that promote hydrogen evolution and sustain durable high\-current electrolysis\.
