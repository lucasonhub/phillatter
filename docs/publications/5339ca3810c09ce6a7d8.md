# О регламентации электронного документооборота \- 19 принципов, которых нет в ITILWhat this article is about and what you will gain\. This article is for those who manage critical information systems in large enterprises and the public sector\. It presents 19 principles that cannot be circumvented, a three\-lines\-of\-defense model, practical cases on protecting the security of your systems, their owners, operators, and users, and the role of the incident manager with veto power\. The material is based on 26 years of experience and has been validated in a system with 10,000\+ users and 3,000\+ legal entities\. Who it is for\. IT directors, owners, operators, and technical support operators of critical information systems, EDI product owners, heads of technical support and maintenance services, methodologists, and system ideologists\. Why this matters right now\. Ten to fifteen years ago, electronic document interchange was seen as a convenient add\-on to paper workflows: files, folders, approval routes\. Today the situation has changed fundamentally — and in several directions at once: 1\. Information systems have become incomparably more complex\. A modern EDI system is not a &quot;folder with files\.&quot; It is dozens of integrations: with HR systems, with the Unified State Register of Legal Entities, with electronic signature systems, with external counterparties, with mobile applications, with reporting systems\. Each integration is a point where a technical action can become a legal one\. 2\. Legislation has become more complex and stricter\. Requirements for information protection are constantly growing\. FSTEC orders, requirements for government information systems, personal data systems, and critical information infrastructure are not &quot;recommendations&quot; but mandatory norms\. A regulation written five years ago may no longer comply with any current requirement — and no one will know until an inspection or an incident occurs\. 3\. Supervision over personal data violations has become stricter\. Fines under Federal Law No\. 152\-FZ have risen to RUB 20 million\. Each identified document containing personal data in an unintended system is qualified as a separate violation\. 4\. Electronic signatures have turned document flow into a legally significant process\. Under Federal Law No\. 63\-FZ, an electronically signed document is equated to a paper document with a handwritten signature\. This means that any administrative action with such a document — replacing a file, deleting metadata, adding a scan that differs from the signed version — is not a &quot;correction&quot; but potential falsification of evidence or official forgery \(Articles 292, 303 of the Criminal Code of the Russian Federation\)\. 5\. Artificial intelligence without principles will turn chaos into chaos squared\. AI does not account for legal risks unless it has been told to\. If AI is implemented in a system without described processes, unified approaches, and principles, it does not eliminate chaos — it scales it\. The only way to use AI safely is to describe the principles first, and automate afterwards\. What this means for EDI regulation\. All five factors converge at a single point: a regulation built solely on ITIL practices, GRC requirements, and HRO principles no longer protects\. It describes processes but does not answer the question of what to do if a technical action is a legal act\. It assumes that principles are already established — but does not say where they come from\. It describes reliability — but does not provide a structure\. That is why today we need principles that stand before methodologies and beyond their scope\. Principles that cannot be circumvented by an order, by urgency, or by personal agreements\. Principles that protect the system before it becomes a source of legal risk\.
