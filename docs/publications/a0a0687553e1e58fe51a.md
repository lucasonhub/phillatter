# Distinguishing Process Change, Instrument Fault and Data\-Path Corruption in Streaming Telemetry: A Reference Implementation and Benchmark

**Autores:** Oleksandr Klymenko

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

A reading departs from what a monitoring system expects for one of three reasons: the physical process changed, the instrument is failing, or the value was corrupted between the instrument and the software\. The three call for different responses and look alike in a single sample\. We state the three\-cause problem, prove which pairs of causes one channel cannot separate, and propose a bank of online detectors \- a Kalman tracker with CUSUM and EWMA charts on its innovation, a Hampel median with generalized\-ESD confirmation, a robust noise statistic with ADWIN, exact\-value checks and an optional two\-channel complementary filter \- combined by a deterministic, inspectable attribution rule\. The detectors were merged into a public algorithm library\. On 2,942 injected events of thirteen fault types the method detects 96\.3% \(95% interval 95\.7\-97\.0\) at 0\.22 false alarms per hour and attributes 91\.2% \(90\.6\-91\.8\) of detected events correctly from one channel and 99\.0% \(98\.6\-99\.4\) with a second, rate channel; from one channel only 2 of 207 detected sensor drifts are attributed to the instrument, as the problem statement predicts\. Injected into recorded industrial signals, the same catalogue is detected at 80\.8% and attributed correctly at 84\.5%, at 3\.6 false alarms per hour\. On the Skoltech Anomaly Benchmark the method detects 32 of 33 experiments with a median delay of 15 s, at 46 false alarms per hour against 11 for a rolling z\-score\.
