# Resistance training volume for major depressive disorder: the MoveMind randomized controlled trial protocol

**Autores:** Giovanni Henrique Quizzini; Jessenia Marise Sales Campos; Thaís Aparecida Medeiros Amorim; Marcelo Augusto Alves dos Santos; André Felipe Oliveira da Cruz; Mateus Sanches Jokura; Matheus Augusto Martins Correia; Julia Elis Baraldo; Marina Trombetta\-Lima; Camila Fernandes Nascimento; Andréa Camaz Deslandes; André O\. ## Resumo

This randomized controlled trial aims to investigate the effects of resistance training \(RT\) volume \(high vs\. low\) and non\-exercise group on depressive symptoms, remission rates, adherence and the psychological and neurobiological mechanisms underlying these effects in patients with major depressive disorder \(MDD\)\. A total of 150 adults \(18–59 years\) diagnosed with MDD will be randomized into three groups: high\-volume RT \(HV, n=50; 4 sets of 10–12 repetition maximum \[RM\]\), low\-volume RT \(LV, n=50; 2 sets of 10–12 RM\), and a non\-exercise control group \(CG, n=50\)\. Participants in the intervention groups will train three non\-consecutive days per week for 8 weeks\. The primary outcome, depressive symptoms, will be assessed by a psychiatrist using the Montgomery–Åsberg Depression Rating Scale\. Secondary outcomes will include potential psychological mechanisms \(perceived self\-efficacy, affective responses to exercise, and well\-being\), assessed at baseline, post\-intervention, and at the 3\-month follow\-up, and neurobiological mechanisms assessed by inflammatory and metabolic biomarkers related to MDD pathophysiology, including TNF\-α, IL\-6, IL\-10, IL\-1β, CRP, IGF\-1, BDNF, insulin, glucose, and cortisol, measured before and after the intervention\. Tertiary outcomes will include dietary intake and ultra\-processed food consumption \(24\-hour dietary recall\), body composition \(DXA\), muscular strength, and sleep quality\. Intervention efficacy will be analyzed using intention\-to\-treat linear mixed\-effects models\. The findings may contribute to more effective, personalized, and sustainable RT prescriptions that improve adherence and complement conventional treatments for MDD\. Clinical trials registration https://ensaiosclinicos\.gov\.br/rg/ , identifier RBR\-9tn4ncp\.
