# Artificial intelligence \(AI\)‐enabled RNA therapeutic design: from molecular optimization to system‐level evaluation

**Autores:** Yongtian Wang; Jiaqi Wu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

RNA therapeutics are increasingly moving from empirical screening towards rational, computation\-guided design\. Recent advances in RNA language models, generative models and structure\-aware learning have improved sequence representation, candidate generation and structure\-constrained optimization\. However, favourable molecular properties do not necessarily translate into desirable biological performance, because therapeutic outcomes also depend on modality\-specific mechanisms, delivery and exposure, molecular interactions, cellular state, immune responses and network\-level adaptation\. Here, we review AI\-enabled RNA therapeutic design through the specific lens of the interface between molecular optimization and system\-level evaluation\. We compare the design constraints of siRNAs, antisense oligonucleotides, aptamers, mRNAs and translation\-oriented circRNAs, and examine the capabilities and current limitations of sequence\-, generative\- and structure\-based computational models\. We further discuss the data resources and perturbation representations required to connect molecular candidates with broader cellular\-response models\. In this context, virtual cells are considered as approximate computational frameworks for candidate prioritization and risk\-oriented system\-level assessment rather than complete digital replicas of living cells\. Finally, we outline a conceptual generation\-simulation\-feedback\-optimization framework for integrating molecular design with progressively higher\-level evaluation\. This perspective emphasizes the need for better interfaces among RNA design, pharmacological context and cellular\-response modelling, rather than relying on molecular optimization alone\.
