# Social norms in contemporary urban food sharing contexts: a critical interpretive review

**Autores:** Ana\-Maria Gatejel; Oona Morrow; Lucie Sovová; Jessica Duncan

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In recent decades, collective approaches to food provisioning have garnered increasing attention in agri\-food studies and on the ground\. A wide range of community\-oriented food practices, including community gardens, urban allotments, community kitchens, social supermarkets, surplus redistribution networks and more have been documented and theorized in the academic literature\. Urban food sharing, understood here as collective acts across the food system, describes any activity that involves the sharing of food, spaces and stuff for the purpose of growing produce, eating and cooking, or redistributing surplus\. Furthermore, food sharing encompasses not just organizational routines, but also social expectations, norms, symbolic values, and different ways of knowing and doing\. This critical interpretive review collected and summarized peer reviewed research published between 2000 and 2023 to identify how the existing literature on urban food sharing attends to social norms across geographies\. Our analysis uncovers ongoing tensions between competing normative demands—such as autonomy versus safety, efficiency versus conviviality, or charity versus solidarity—especially in non\-familial, semi\-public settings\. Having identified gaps in the literature, we call for future research into food sharing to challenge normative blind spots by taking into account dynamics of care, the conflictual politics of food sharing initiatives, and changing social norms\.
