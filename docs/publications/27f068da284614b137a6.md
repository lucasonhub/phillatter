# Differential evolution\-based optimization of the 3GPP UMi propagation model for 3\.5 GHz urban microcell environments in Nigeria

**Autores:** Hafiz Bola Mustapha

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The increasing deployment of fifth\-generation \(5G\) wireless communication networks has created a need for accurate radio propagation models capable of predicting signal attenuation under different environmental conditions\. However, standardized propagation models may exhibit prediction errors when applied to urban environments with geographical characteristics that differ from their original calibration conditions\. This study develops a Differential Evolution \(DE\)\-based optimized 3GPP TR 38\.901 Urban Microcell \(UMi\) propagation model for the 3\.5 GHz frequency band using field\-measured data from a Nigerian urban environment\. The study characterizes radio propagation behavior across different transmitter–receiver separation distances and evaluates the prediction performance of the standard 3GPP UMi model\. A comparative optimization framework comprising the standard 3GPP model, Particle Swarm Optimization \(PSO\), and Differential Evolution \(DE\) is adopted to estimate optimized propagation\-model coefficients by minimizing the mean squared error between measured and predicted path\-loss values\. The performance of the models is assessed using Root Mean Square Error \(RMSE\), Mean Absolute Error \(MAE\), coefficient of determination \(R2\), maximum absolute error, mean error, convergence behavior, computational time, and coefficient stability\. The optimized model is validated using an independent test dataset to establish its generalization capability\. The study provides a locally calibrated propagation model that improves path\-loss prediction accuracy and supports more reliable coverage estimation and radio network planning for 5G deployment in Nigerian urban microcell environments\.
