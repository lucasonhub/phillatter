# Integrated AMMI, GGE Biplot, and MTSI analyses reveal yield stability and adaptation patterns in cowpea in Ghana

**Autores:** Theophilus Kwabla Tengey; Patrick Attamah; Peter Quandahor; Ophelia Asirifi Amoako; Mariam Seidu; Memunatu Issahaku; Rashid Seidu; Mathew Bapaak Laar; David Nayindow Kpasah; Josiah Darko Osei\-Wusu

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Genotype × environment interaction \(G×E\) complicates the identification of high\-yielding and stable cowpea genotypes across variable production environments\. This study evaluated 20 advanced cowpea breeding lines for grain yield, stability, and multi\-trait performance across five location\-year environments in Ghana during the 2024 and 2025 cropping seasons\. Methods Trials were conducted using a randomized complete block design with three replications\. Both seasons were drought\-prone, although moisture stress varied among location\-year environments because of erratic rainfall distribution\. Grain yield was analysed using additive main effects and multiplicative interaction \(AMMI\) and genotype plus genotype × environment \(GGE\) biplot analyses, while grain yield, biomass yield, harvest index, hundred\-seed weight, and days to 95% pod maturity were integrated using the Multi\-Trait Stability Index \(MTSI\)\. Results Analysis of variance revealed significant \(p &lt; 0\.001\) effects of environment, genotype, and G×E\. The first two interaction principal components explained 92\.15% of G×E variation, with Wa\_2025 showing the largest IPCA1 score\. Tuzievallenga had the highest mean grain yield \(1149 kg ha −1 \), whereas IT17K\-2030–2 had the lowest AMMI Stability Value \(ASV = 114\.54\)\. Five genotypes combined high yield with relative stability: Tuzievallenga, IT17K\-2030\-2, IT17K\-1095\-2\-2, IT17K\-849\-2\-1, and IT17K\-1802\-1\. MTSI values ranged from 2\.035 to 5\.518, with IT17K\-1095\-2\-2, IT17K\-849\-2\-1, IT17K\-1403\-1\-1, IT17K\-2024\-4, and Tuzievallenga having the lowest values\. IT17K\-1095\-2\-2, IT17K\-849\-2\-1, and Tuzievallenga were common to the top five selections from both approaches\. GGE identified one common sector comprising Damongo\_2024, Manga\_2025, and Nyankpala\_2024, with Tuzievallenga as the winning genotype, while Wa\_2025 and Nyankpala\_2025 formed distinct sectors associated with SARI\-tuya and IT17K\-1266\-2\-1, respectively\. Discussion/Conclusion The findings demonstrate substantial G×E differentiation and identify promising genotypes for farmer\-managed on\-farm evaluation and complementary use in cowpea breeding\.
