# Digital Health Africa \(DHA 2025\) – Conference Report

**Autores:** Tirhani Maluleke; Catriona John Waitt; Veshni Pillay\-Fuentes Lorente; Bernd Rosenkranz; Beate Kampmann; Karin Steinecke; Goonaseelan Colin Pillai

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction The Digital Health Africa 2025 Conference \(DHA\-2025, https://digitalhealthafrica\.org/ \) addressed the integration of digital tools within critical global health priorities, including maternal and child health \(MCH\), antimicrobial resistance artificial intelligence \(AI\), and pharmacometrics\. While the adoption of digital health shows immense potential for improving healthcare access in Africa’s resource\-limited environments, its practical application in the public sector remains sub\-optimal due to policy misalignment and inadequate systemic investments\. Digital health interventions can achieve sustained, large\-scale impact when properly supported, as demonstrated by successful platforms like MomConnect in South Africa and MomCare in Kenya\. However, successfully scaling these systems beyond initial pilots requires a robust foundation built on three pillars: technical capacity \(infrastructure\), legal legitimacy \(regulation\), and effective management \(governance\)\. Furthermore, while advanced tools like AI and pharmacometric modeling are now practical realities in healthcare in developed countries, the most significant challenge remains operationalizing and transitioning these sophisticated tools safely from the laboratory to the clinical bedside in Africa\. Conclusion Scientific advancement in digital health is insufficient on its own; the true test of innovation lies in its safe and practical integration into end\-user healthcare delivery\. To successfully scale digital health solutions in resource\-constrained settings, stakeholders must prioritize cross\-continental collaborations, invest heavily in capacity building, and secure long\-term commitments to infrastructure and policy development\.
