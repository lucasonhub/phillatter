In Real Time: A Reinvention in British Metaphysics 1883\-1928\. Oxford University Press\. 2026Do the past and future exist? This chapter argues that, within the history of English\-language philosophy, this question was first explicitly posed and debated in an 1897 Aristotelian Society symposium, “In What Sense, If Any, Do Past and Future Time Exist?”\. Three participants gave papers: Bernard Bosanquet, Shadworth Hodgson, and G\. E\. Moore\. The symposium came about as a response to work from Hermann Lotze and F\. H\. Bradley, who act as invisible debate participants\. Between these five men, we can distinguish three positions: presentism in Lotze; time anti\-realism in Bradley, Bosanquet, and Moore; and eternalism in Hodgson\. This chapter explores each of the three points on this triangle, enquiring into their assumptions and arguments\.This chapter conflicts with a much\-cited thesis from philosopher of time John Bigelow: that ‘everyone’ held presentism until the nineteenth century\. Along the way, this thesis is discussed and ultimately rejected\.
