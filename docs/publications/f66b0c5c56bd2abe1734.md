# Hepatocellular adenoma associated with recurrent hydrocelom in a Mexican axolotl \(Ambystoma mexicanum\)\- a case report

**Autores:** Alessandro Vetere; Antonella Volta; Martina Fumeo; Federico Armando; Melania Di Pentima; Martina Gavezzoli; Jacopo Casalini; Rosanna Di Lecce; Francesco Di Ianni

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The Mexican axolotl \( Ambystoma mexicanum \) is an increasingly popular companion animal and an important model organism in regenerative medicine\. However, information on neoplastic diseases in this species remains limited\. Hepatocellular adenoma is a benign hepatic neoplasm characterized by well\-differentiated hepatocytes and loss of normal lobular architecture\. This report describes an axolotl with hepatocellular adenoma and concomitant hydrocelom\. A 9\-year\-old, 230\-g captive\-bred female Mexican axolotl was presented with a three\-week history of progressive celomic distension\. Despite marked celomic enlargement, the animal was alert and responsive\. Radiography revealed celomic effusion and a small radiopaque focus in the hepatic region\. Ultrasonography demonstrated free celomic fluid, a small gallbladder stone, and a 1 × 0\.7 cm hepatic mass in the right liver lobe\. Echocardiography and hematologic and biochemical analyses were unremarkable\. Analysis of the aspirated fluid identified a pure transudate and bacterial culture was negative\. Computed tomography confirmed the hepatic mass\. The lesion was surgically removed\. Grossly, the mass was oval, well demarcated, smooth, and lighter than the surrounding hepatic parenchyma\. Histopathology revealed a nodular proliferation of well\-differentiated hepatocytes with mild anisocytosis and anisokaryosis, no mitotic figures, and an absence of portal spaces, supporting the diagnosis of hepatocellular adenoma\. Histochemical stains for potential infectious agents were negative\. Postoperative recovery was uneventful\. Although hydrocelom recurred four months later and required periodic drainage, serial ultrasonography revealed no recurrence of hepatic or other celomic lesions during the subsequent three\-year follow\-up\. This case expands knowledge of neoplastic diseases in axolotls and demonstrates the feasibility of advanced imaging and surgical management of hepatic masses in this species\.
