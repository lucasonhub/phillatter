# GIS\-Based Assessment of Potential Emergency Assembly Areas: A Staged Planning Framework and Population\-Coverage Analysis

**Autores:** Ceren Yağcı

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Emergency assembly\-area planning requires distinct assessments of safety, accessibility, capacity, and operational readiness\. This study combines a literature\-informed planning framework with a GIS\-based analysis of potential population coverage\. The framework is structured around six stages: hazard screening, accessibility assessment, demand–capacity analysis, operational preparedness evaluation, comparison of feasible alternatives, and scenario\-based testing\. The accessibility stage was demonstrated in the Odunpazarı and Tepebaşı districts of Eskişehir, Türkiye, using a 2024 WorldPop gridded population dataset and 444 selected urban parks\. Euclidean buffers of 300 m, 500 m, and 800 m were generated and dissolved at each threshold to avoid double\-counting populations in overlapping service areas\. Estimated population coverage increased from 66\.9% within 300 m to 78\.7% within 500 m and 82\.5% within 800 m\. The relatively small increase beyond 500 m indicates diminishing coverage gains as the assumed spatial reach expands\. These estimates represent potential spatial coverage only; they do not demonstrate pedestrian accessibility or operational suitability\. Thus, the case study illustrates the population\-coverage component of the staged framework, while hazard exposure, usable capacity, operational preparedness, and performance under disrupted network conditions remain to be evaluated\. The study provides a structured basis for identifying the further assessments required before potential locations can be designated as suitable emergency assembly areas\.
