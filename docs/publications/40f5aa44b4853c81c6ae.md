# An elasto\-viscoplastic fast fourier transform framework for predicting spatially resolved fatigue performance in additively manufactured metals

**Autores:** Nathan Aaron Searle; Krishna Prasath Logakannan; Brian R\. Phung; Benjamin S\. Anglin; Ashley D\. Spear

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Accurate prediction of fatigue life in additively manufactured \(AM\) metals remains challenging due to inherent microstructural sensitivity and corresponding scatter in fatigue performance, particularly under high\-cycle fatigue conditions\. In this work, a structure\-property framework based on crystal plasticity fast Fourier transform \(FFT\) methods is extended to simulate microstructure\-sensitive fatigue\-damage evolution; the results of which are used to map location\-specific fatigue performance in AM build domains\. Cyclic plasticity behavior is captured through the incorporation of kinematic backstress hardening, and fatigue indicator parameters \(FIPs\) are computed to resolve local propensity for fatigue damage under cyclic loading\. The framework is demonstrated on direct energy deposition \(DED\) stainless steel \(SS\) 316L microstructures\. A FIP\-to\-life curve is established for the DED SS316L material to map the FIP evolution behavior from simulation to an estimate of lifetime as a function of strain amplitude\. A comprehensive convergence study is carried out to quantify the error in estimated fatigue life as a function of the number of explicitly simulated cycles \(up to 3000\), and the results confirm that reliable fatigue life estimates can be obtained with a limited number of simulated loading cycles under the assumed FIP\-to\-life approach\. Application of the framework across multiple process\-informed AM build domains captures spatial variations in fatigue life and reproduces the expected increase in microstructure\-induced scatter with decreasing strain amplitude\. Furthermore, the computational efficiency of the FFT solver enables high\-throughput simulations to support statistical analysis and uncertainty quantification\. The proposed framework offers a pathway for integrating microstructure\-sensitive fatigue life prediction into fatigue\-critical qualification and design of AM components\.
