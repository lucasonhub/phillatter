# Beyond Consent: Collective Data Rights and the Case for Indigenous AI Sovereignty

**Autores:** Adeel Salman

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Individual consent, while essential to privacy and informational autonomy, is insufficient to govern artificial intelligence systems that generate collective harms, extract collective value, and process culturally sensitive knowledge\. This paper examines the limitations of individual\-centred privacy governance and advances a framework for Indigenous AI sovereignty grounded in collective authority, community protocol, and enforceable control throughout the AI lifecycle\. Drawing on First Nations&\#x27; OCAP principles, the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance, the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples, and relevant Canadian and international AI governance frameworks, the paper examines the relationship between data sovereignty, digital infrastructure, cultural representation, and downstream technological use\. Its central contribution is a proposed Indigenous AI Sovereignty Audit designed to evaluate whether collective rights are translated into operational governance\. The framework addresses community authority, secondary\-use restrictions, data possession, provenance, derivative AI artifacts, benefit\-sharing, cultural safety, and mechanisms for challenge and redress\. The paper argues that meaningful Indigenous AI sovereignty requires more than consent to data collection\. It requires the continuing ability of Indigenous peoples and their legitimate governing institutions to determine how information is used, transformed, commercialized, and represented through artificial intelligence\. Publication note: Independent research working paper\. Research and source review current as of 10 April 2026\. This manuscript has not been represented as peer\-reviewed\.
