# An opinion evolution prediction model based on graph attention and ordinary differential equations

**Autores:** Quan Liu; Liuyang Duan; Meimei Jia; Ya Li

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurately predicting the dynamic evolution of user opinions on social media is important for understanding opinion diffusion mechanisms, identifying patterns of collective behavior, and supporting public decision\-making\. Existing opinion dynamics models provide interpretable descriptions of opinion evolution but rely on idealized assumptions and may not adequately capture the structural complexity of real\-world social networks\. In contrast, purely data\-driven methods have strong approximation capabilities but lack explicit modeling of underlying social interaction processes\. To address these limitations, we propose GA\-ODE, an opinion evolution prediction model that integrates a graph attention mechanism with neural ordinary differential equation \(ODE\) constraints\. GA\-ODE adaptively learns differentiated aggregation weights for neighboring nodes through a graph attention mechanism and employs a gated evolution unit to balance individual opinion persistence and neighborhood assimilation effects\. We further introduce a structure\-aware ODE consistency regularizer based on probabilistic bounded confidence and incorporate the adjacency matrix into the dynamical residual as a topology\-aware mask\. Experimental results show that the topology\-aware mask provides limited benefits on clean graphs but improves prediction performance under structural perturbations and helps maintain dynamical consistency in degraded network conditions\. Evaluations under non\-idealized dynamical settings further show that GA\-ODE maintains prediction performance in the presence of opinion repulsion effects and external event shocks\. Experiments on multiple synthetic datasets and one real\-world dataset validate the effectiveness and robustness of GA\-ODE\. However, the model assumes that social interactions remain static within the observation window and is sensitive to sparsely represented classes\.
