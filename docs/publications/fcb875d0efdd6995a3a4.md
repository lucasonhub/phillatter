## Descrição do feed

\_Informational Qualia Competence Without Experience in Artificial and Human Pattern Recognition\_ 4\. Translated by Silvia Porras\. 2026Public and philosophical debate about large language models \(LLMs\) oscillates between two extremes: the claim that these systems genuinely understand, and the claim that they are mere stochastic parrots\. This essay develops a middle position built around a concept named informational qualia: the capacity of a system to recognize, represent, and effectively deploy the patterns associated with subjective experience without undergoing that experience\. The concept is motivated first by twenty\-seven years of adult literacy teaching with immigrant and refugee learners — sustained, repeated professional observation suggesting that competence precedes comprehension in human language acquisition — and second by a qualified analogy to Frank Jackson&\#x27;s knowledge argument\. The essay is explicit about where that analogy holds and where it does not: LLMs do not possess complete physical facts in Mary&\#x27;s sense, only complete exposure to how humans describe experience, and the difference matters\. Drawing on the philosophical literature on multiple realizability and tacit knowledge, the essay proposes a spectrum model of understanding in place of the binary, answers the objection that informational qualia is behaviorism renamed, and situates the concept within a broader research program on human\-AI relational cognition\. Informational qualia, it concludes, is not a diminished form of understanding\. It is the layer of intelligence that humans and machines demonstrably share — and therefore the honest foundation on which claims about their partnership can be built\. \(direct link \)
