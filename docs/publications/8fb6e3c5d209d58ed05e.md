# Targeting upstream alarmins \(TSLP, IL\-33, IL\-25\): a new frontier in treating type 2 chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps

**Autores:** Shuhua Li

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps \(CRSwNP\) affects 1%\-4% of adults globally and imposes a substantial burden on quality of life\. Despite endoscopic surgery, topical corticosteroids, and systemic glucocorticoids, postoperative recurrence exceeds 54% at five years\. Even with the advent of biologics targeting downstream type 2 effectors, such as dupilumab, mepolizumab, and omalizumab, a meaningful subset of patients, particularly those with mixed or non\-type 2 endotypes, remains inadequately controlled\. The epithelial alarmins thymic stromal lymphopoietin \(TSLP\), interleukin\-33 \(IL\-33\), and interleukin\-25 \(IL\-25\) occupy the apex of the inflammatory cascade, functioning as proximal triggers that bridge innate barrier disruption to adaptive type 2 amplification\. This review examines the shared and distinctive pathobiology of these three molecules in CRSwNP, compares the clinical trajectories of tezepelumab \(Phase 3\), anti\-IL\-33/ST2 agents \(Phase 2/3\), and emerging anti\-IL\-25 biologics \(early\-phase\), and outlines a conceptual framework for future investigation\. We propose that upstream alarmin inhibition offers a broader immunomodulatory scope than downstream cytokine blockade, yet its clinical translation hinges on three unresolved challenges: overcoming functional redundancy among alarmins, defining the long\-term safety profile of sustained epithelial\-immune suppression, and validating clinically accessible predictive biomarkers\. The proposed algorithms and biomarker cut\-offs remain exploratory and require prospective validation in CRSwNP\-specific cohorts\.
