# Cognitive Load and Workload in Desktop\-Based Educational Virtual Worlds: A Computational Machine Learning and Structural Equation Modeling Study with Computer Science Students

**Autores:** Dimitrios Magetos; Sarandis Mitropoulos; Christos Douligeris

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Educational virtual worlds and metaverse environments are increasingly utilized to foster immersive and experiential learning in higher education\. However, their instructional efficacy depends not only on visual fidelity and presence, but fundamentally on how effectively learners’ cognitive resources are managed\. This study investigates intrinsic \(ICL\), extraneous \(ECL\), and germane cognitive load \(GCL\) alongside NASA Task Load Index \(NASA\-TLX\) workload dimensions in a desktop\-based educational virtual world developed in Spatial\.io for an undergraduate Computer Networks course\. A cohort of 120 second\-year Computer Science students \(75 males, 45 females\) participated in a five\-week intervention designed to model the relationships among cognitive load, perceived workload, and meaningful engagement\. A multi\-method analytical framework was implemented, combining psychometric validation and Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling \(PLS\-SEM\) with machine learning techniques \(K\-Means clustering, K\-Nearest Neighbors \(KNN\), Random Forest, Decision Trees\) and Psychometric Network Analysis\. The findings revealed strong internal consistency across the cognitive load scales, moderate overall workload, and significant positive associations between all three cognitive load dimensions and perceived workload, with extraneous cognitive load emerging as the variable most strongly and consistently linked to high\-overload cluster membership, frustration, and reduced engagement across the machine learning and network analyses\. These results suggest that desktop\-based educational virtual worlds may support self\-reported germane cognitive engagement, provided that extraneous interface demands are systematically minimized and navigation is scaffolded\. The study contributes an empirical and computational perspective to educational metaverse research, offering practical guidelines for designing cognitively sustainable immersive learning environments\.
