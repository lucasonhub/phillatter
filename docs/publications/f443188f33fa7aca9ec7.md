# Emergency Medicine Reimagined: Development of the Patient Rights Index for Emergency Medicine

**Autores:** Matthew Ryan

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Emergency medicine has evolved beyond episodic acute care to encompass a growing range of clinical, social, and system\-level responsibilities\. This scholarly perspective proposes the Patient Rights Index in Emergency Medicine \(PRI\-ED\), a conceptual framework designed to organize recurring domains through which emergency departments promote access to care, patient safety, relief of suffering, autonomy, and dignity\. Methods: The PRI\-ED was developed through iterative domain generation informed by clinical observation, professional experience, and targeted review of peer\-reviewed literature, policy analyses, and professional organizational reports\. No primary data collection, systematic review, formal consensus process, or empirical validation was performed\. Results: We identified 12 domains that represent recurring clinical, operational, and system\-level challenges encountered in emergency medicine\. These domains were organized within a standardized framework linking domain\-specific objectives, intervention strategies, measurable indicators, and relevant policy or funding mechanisms\. Conclusion: The PRI\-ED is a proposed conceptual framework for organizing and evaluating domains in which emergency medicine intersects with access, safety, autonomy, relief of suffering, and dignity\. The framework is intended to inform future implementation, evaluation, and policy discussion and requires feasibility testing and outcome validation before effectiveness can be determined\.
