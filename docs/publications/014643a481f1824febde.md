# Validation of AI\-Generated Testing Artifacts in Software Quality Assurance

**Autores:** Tetiana Zhyrova

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This presentation examines the validation of artificial intelligence \(AI\)\-generated testing artifacts in software quality assurance, focusing on the limitations of formal correctness and the need for systematic, evidence\-based acceptance procedures\. The presentation introduces the VASTA \(Validation of AI\-Generated Software Testing Artifacts\) model, a structured framework for evaluating the suitability of AI\-generated testing artifacts for their intended use\. The model distinguishes four categories of artifacts: test documentation, executable tests, test data and components, and analytical materials\. The proposed validation framework comprises six interconnected groups: Provenance and Context – verification of source reliability, version consistency, generation records, and traceability\. Structural Correctness – assessment of artifact structure, syntax, required fields, and dependencies\. Executability and Reproducibility – verification of repeatable execution, environmental consistency, and supporting evidence\. Semantic Correctness – alignment with approved requirements, business rules, and independently verified expected results\. Defect Detection Capability – evaluation of the ability to identify relevant defects through boundary testing, controlled mutations, and other validation techniques\. Engineering Quality and Security – assessment of maintainability, dependencies, data protection, credentials, and integration requirements\. An illustrative financial transaction scenario demonstrates how an AI\-generated test can pass despite an underlying software defect when its expected results are derived from outdated requirements\. The example highlights the importance of independent test oracles, requirements traceability, and version\-specific validation\. The presentation also proposes criteria, indicators, thresholds, and evidence requirements for artifact acceptance, revision, or rejection\. Particular attention is given to integrating validation procedures into continuous integration and continuous delivery \(CI/CD\) workflows while maintaining a clear distinction between artifact acceptance and product release decisions\. The proposed approach emphasizes that automated checks support evidence collection, whereas final acceptance decisions remain the responsibility of designated reviewers or teams\. Further research directions include empirical evaluation of the VASTA model across different artifact categories, comparisons with expert\-review baselines, assessment of validation thresholds, and investigation of revalidation requirements following changes to software systems and their operating environments\.
