# Bogdan, Alex: The Origin and Persistence of Anatomical Pattern

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

An egg has no limbs to repair, yet its descendants will form a specific body and later restore lost parts of it\. Michael Levin’s bioelectric experiments show that the anatomical target guiding such repair is a rewritable memory, and his recent proposal locates the origin of that target in a structured, non\-physical space of patterns\. This paper asks the question that sits between those two claims: how does a memory that can be rewritten come to hold its first entry? I argue that anatomical information changes form as development proceeds\. An inherited organization establishes a capacity to generate pattern; developing tissues acquire commitments that can later guide reconstruction; and some of those commitments are maintained by selective sensitivity to a few informative signals\. Two formal tools make the proposal testable\. W\. Ross Ashby’s law of requisite variety yields a compatibility condition stating exactly when a sparse set of cues suffices for successful correction, and a second result shows that the minimal number of cue values a regulator needs is set by its repertoire of corrective actions, not by the number of states it must handle\. Free Energy Heuristics, an active\-inference account of cue selection under uncertain precision, then specifies when a regulator should settle on few cues rather than many\. Together they convert the question of where patterns come from into questions about what a tissue must distinguish, what it can safely ignore, and what persists when it saves a correction\. I close by stating what this account does not explain, and what observation would separate it from Levin’s\.
