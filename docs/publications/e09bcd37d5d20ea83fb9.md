# A CD68\-expressing tissue\-homing subset in peripheral CD8\+ T cells predicts response to anti\-PD\-1 therapy in lung cancer

**Autores:** Ying Wang; Jing Huang; Zhengyi Sun; Yumei Pei; Jiancheng Xu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background T\-cell function is regulated by immunometabolism, and lysosomes are signaling hubs\. CD68 \(LAMP4\), traditionally regarded as a monocyte/macrophage marker, has been largely neglected in T cell biology\. We aimed to assess the link between CD68 expression in T cells and anti\-PD\-1 response in lung cancer, and its potential as an early dynamic biomarker in peripheral blood CD8 \+ T cells\. Methods We analyzed transcriptomic \(GSE126044, GSE135222, GSE166449\) and single\-cell \(GSE207422\) data from anti\-PD\-1\-treated lung cancer cohorts to identify lysosome related pathways and key genes, validated with peripheral blood CD8 \+ T cell data \(GSE111414\)\. Prospectively, we enrolled lung cancer patients receiving anti\-PD\-1 therapy, collecting peripheral blood at baseline and week 3\. Flow cytometry measured CD68 in CD8 \+ T cells and subsets, assessing association with response, progression\-free survival \(PFS\), and functional markers \(granzyme B, perforin, Ki\-67, CD69, IFN\-γ, IL\-1β\)\. Results Responders showed enrichment of lysosome pathways and upregulation of 8 lysosome related genes including CD68, positively correlated with monocyte and T cell infiltration\. Single\-cell data linked CD68 expression in T cell to favorable response\. After one cycle, the proportion of CD8 \+ CD68 \+ T cells was lower in non\-responders \( P&lt;0\.05 \), with AUC 0\.803 \( P=0\.044 \) and cutoff 1\.015%, stratifying PFS \( P=0\.0225 \)\. CD68 was highest in naïve T cells at baseline, but decreased across subsets after treatment in non\-responders \(all P&lt;0\.01 \)\. Functionally, compared with CD8 \+ CD68 \- T cells, CD8 \+ CD68 \+ T cells exhibited lower expression of granzyme B and perforin but higher expression of Ki\-67, CD69, IFN\-γ, and IL\-1β \(all P&lt;0\.001 \), suggesting a CD8 \+ T cell state characterized by lower cytotoxicity and enhanced proliferative and cytokine producing capacities, accompanied by a tissue residency associated phenotype\. Conclusion CD8 \+ CD68 \+ T cells exhibit lower cytotoxicity but greater proliferative and cytokine producing capacities, together with a tissue residency associated phenotype, suggesting a precursor\-like state\. Low CD68 expression in tumor\-infiltrating T cells and the post\-treatment reduction of CD68 expression in peripheral blood CD8 \+ T cells are associated with resistance to anti\-PD\-1 therapy in lung cancer\. These findings suggest that CD8 \+ CD68 \+ T cells may serve as a potential early dynamic immune biomarker for monitoring and assessing response to anti\-PD\-1 therapy in lung cancer\.
