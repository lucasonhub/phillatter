# An interpretable, physics\-constrained model for woody\-plant evapotranspiration under heat and drought extremes in China

**Autores:** Jianzhe Ma; Qingshan Xu; Minfen YU; Xingna Lin; Shuting Zhang; Fang Zhou; Huajun Wang; Wenwen Yuan

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Evapotranspiration \(ET\) integrates the water, energy and carbon economy of woody\-plant ecosystems and is a primary channel through which climate change acts on forests\. Yet existing ET products diverge markedly over forested terrain, and purely data\-driven models tend to lose skill precisely under the hot, dry and high vapour pressure deficit \(VPD\) conditions that increasingly threaten woody plants\. Here, an interpretable, physics\-constrained adaptive\-weighting framework was developed to estimate daily latent heat flux \(LE\) for woody\-plant ecosystems and was evaluated across a climate gradient from tropical rainforest to boreal larch forest in China\. The framework couples a Penman–Monteith physical branch, in which a machine\-learning model predicts surface resistance, with a data\-driven branch that predicts LE directly, and fuses the two through a sample\-level adaptive weight learned from the data\. The model was trained and validated on 17,515 daily eddy\-covariance observations from twelve woody\-plant flux sites, together with reanalysis meteorology, satellite vegetation indices and a spaceborne canopy\-height product\. Under random cross\-validation, the adaptive model and the data\-driven branch performed comparably \( R 2 = 0\.83\), whereas the physical branch alone was weaker \( R 2 = 0\.40\)\. Under extrapolation to unseen climate extremes, the data\-driven branch explained less variance, particularly under drought \( R 2 = 0\.24\), while the adaptive model retained greater skill in each extreme regime \(heat R 2 = 0\.64; drought R 2 = 0\.61; high\-VPD R 2 = 0\.74; all\-extreme R 2 = 0\.88\)\. Interpretability analysis identified net radiation and canopy greenness as the dominant controls on woody\-plant ET, with soil\-moisture control emerging under water\-limited conditions\. Multi\-year mean forest ET declined from 2\.9 mm day −1 in rubber plantation to 0\.9 mm day −1 in boreal larch\. Two widely used ET products \(GLEAM and GLDAS\) differed by about 16% in their spatial means over China’s forests, and their mean pixel\-wise relative disagreement was 25%\. The framework provides a promising interpretable, physically consistent basis for further development of woody\-plant water\-use monitoring, rather than an operational monitoring tool\.
