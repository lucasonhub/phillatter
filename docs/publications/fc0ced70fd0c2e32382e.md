# Slice\-Group\-Level Brain Tumor Classification Using CRNN\-GRU

**Autores:** Mohammed Z\.M\. Almadani; Maslita Abd Aziz; T\. Zalizam T\. Muda

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Interpreting brain MRI for tumor diagnosis is time\-consuming and varies between raters, and single\-slice decisions can be fragile\. This paper compares two classification pipelines on the Brain Tumor Classification \(MRI\) Dataset from Kaggle \(glioma, meningioma, pituitary, no\-tumor\) of T1\-contrast\-enhanced images\. The first is a per\-slice CNN\. The second is a per\-group CRNN\-GRU that reads an ordered sequence of ten slices with a bidirectional GRU and inter\-slice attention\. Because the dataset provides no patient identifiers, the analysis unit is a surrogate slice group, and the splits are group\-disjoint by construction; all models are compared at the group\-level\. The per\-slice CNN reached 80\.0% slice\-level accuracy\. Aggregated to the group\-level, the same CNN reached 92\.68% by majority voting and 95\.12% by mean\-probability pooling\. The CRNN\-GRU reached 90\.2% in a single pass, and 87\.8% with test\-time augmentation\. No pairwise difference is statistically significant \(exact McNemar, all p &gt;= 0\.25\)\. The results do not show that sequential modelling and inter\-slice attention improve on simple probability aggregation on this dataset, and the simplest configuration was the most accurate\. A content\-level audit found a small number of images duplicated across partitions, so all figures should be read as upper bounds\. These results describe internal performance on one dataset under group\-disjoint evaluation with a content\-level leakage audit and do not establish clinical generalization\.
