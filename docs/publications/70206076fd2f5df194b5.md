# A Cyber Risk Quantification and Governance Architecture for Critical Infrastructure: From Posture Measurement to Executive Reporting

**Autores:** Mayokun Philips Adegbite

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Cybersecurity governance in critical infrastructure organizations requires defensible, decisiongrade quantification of risk that withstands board, investor, and regulatory scrutiny\. Traditional heat map approaches conflate likelihood and impact estimation with subjective judgment in ways that resist comparison across business units, programs, and years\. Cyber risk quantification methods address these limitations by translating control posture, threat intelligence, and asset impact into monetary loss distributions and prioritized investment decisions\. This paper presents a cyber risk quantification and governance architecture tailored to critical infrastructure organizations\. The architecture spans five domains\. The posture measurement domain captures control maturity and operational evidence from information technology, operational technology, and third party sources\. The exposure analysis domain identifies the most material attack paths through asset modeling and adversary informed scenarios\. The loss modeling domain uses methods consistent with the Factor Analysis of Information Risk standard and related approaches to translate exposure into monetary distributions\. The portfolio analysis domain integrates loss estimates across business units, presenting risk concentration, treatment options, and trade offs\. The executive and regulatory reporting domain communicates results to boards, investors, and regulators with appropriate uncertainty quantification\. The architecture aligns with the National Institute of Standards and Technology cybersecurity framework, the National Institute of Standards and Technology Special Publication 800 30 on risk assessment, the International Organization for Standardization 31000 risk management guidelines, and emerging Securities and Exchange Commission disclosure expectations\. The paper illustrates the architecture through three scenarios in energy and water infrastructure and closes with implementation guidance, governance considerations, and an agenda for future research\.
