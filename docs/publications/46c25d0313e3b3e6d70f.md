# Genome\-reduced Aridivitia evolved predation to thrive in Earth’s oligotrophic depths

**Autores:** Xiaonan Wan; Wenlu Feng; Yang Zhang; Tom A\. Williams; Gergely J\. Szöllősi; Hongyu Dang; Xuejun Yan; James M\. Tiedje; Jizhong Z\. Zhou; Xun Qian

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Deep soils represent one of Earth’s largest yet most enigmatic microbial habitats, where survival strategies under extreme oligotrophy remain poorly understood\. Here, we report the Aridivitia group, a globally distributed bacterial lineage enriched in oligotrophic soils and thriving down to 22 m depth across sites in the United States and China\. Despite its genome reduction \(average size \~2\.9 Mb\), Aridivitia exhibits high metabolic flexibility in utilizing diverse carbon and energy sources\. Cellular, genomic, and metatranscriptomic evidence, including small cell and genome sizes, host\-associated metabolism, and the active expression of attachment\- and attack\-associated surface structures such as Tad pili and pore\-forming toxins, indicate a predatory lifestyle in Aridivitia\. Ancestral genome reconstruction reveals that Aridivitia originated as free\-living mixotrophs, but subsequently lost the ability to fix carbon as they evolved a predatory lifestyle\. Collectively, Aridivitia exemplifies a unique evolutionary strategy that reconciles genome reduction with functional versatility, expanding our understanding of microbial adaptation and evolution at the energetic limits of life\.
