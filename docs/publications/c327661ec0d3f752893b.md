# POD\-HDWNO: A hierarchical decoupled wavelet neural operator framework with POD reconstruction for efficient full\-field response prediction of high\-speed railway bridges

**Autores:** Libing Chen; Li Song; Zhiwu Yu; Xin Nie; Lilong Fan; Jian Hou

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The full\-field train\-induced vibration responses of high\-speed railway bridges are characterized by high output dimensionality, strong spatiotemporal coupling, and pronounced spectral differences between displacement and acceleration\. Consequently, conventional vehicle\-track\-bridge coupled dynamic simulations are computationally demanding for large\-scale stochastic analyses\. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a POD\-HDWNO framework that integrates proper orthogonal decomposition \(POD\) with a hierarchical decoupled wavelet neural operator \(HDWNO\)\. POD projects the high\-dimensional displacement and acceleration fields onto low\-dimensional modal spaces\. Shallow feature sharing, deep task\-specific decoupling, and adaptive channel calibration are then employed to capture their shared dynamic mappings and distinct spectral characteristics\. The full\-bridge spatiotemporal response fields are subsequently reconstructed from the predicted POD coefficients\. Based on a coupled vehicle\-track\-bridge dynamic model validated against field monitoring responses, the framework is systematically evaluated in terms of full\-field prediction accuracy, large\-scale stochastic analysis capability, robustness to input noise, out\-of\-distribution generalization, and applicability to different bridge and train configurations\. The results show that POD\-HDWNO substantially reduces the output dimensionality while accurately reconstructing both response fields and capturing their differences in temporal fluctuations, spatial distributions, and statistical dispersion\. Its online computation is approximately 6530 times faster than conventional dynamic simulation, while the end\-to\-end comparison including offline construction also confirms a clear overall computational advantage\. The framework further maintains stable prediction performance under input noise, nearby out\-of\-distribution conditions, and the different bridge and train configurations considered\. This study provides an efficient surrogate modeling approach for large\-sample probabilistic analysis and rapid assessment of the full\-field dynamic responses of high\-speed railway bridges, demonstrating its potential for evaluating bridge dynamic performance under complex operating conditions\.
