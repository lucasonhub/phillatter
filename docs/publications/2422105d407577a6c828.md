# Disentangling Propagation Effects from the Spectro\-Temporal Properties of Fast Radio Bursts

**Autores:** Aishwarya Kumar

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Fast Radio Bursts \(FRBs\) are energetic radio\-frequency transients with no established progenitor model despite nearly two decades of observations\. Their observed spectro\-temporal properties provide insights into the emission mechanism but can be significantly modified by dispersion and scattering\. This thesis investigates how these two propagation effects alter the sub\-burst slope law derived within the Triggered Relativistic Dynamical Model \(TRDM\)\. This law describes an inverse relation between sub\-burst slope and duration, with a proportionality constant A characterizing the emission process\. We present a modified law incorporating the scattering timescale and residual dispersive delay, together with a methodology to recover these quantities alongside the intrinsic parameters\. We test our methodology using simulated FRBs generated within the TRDM using superradiance as the emission mechanism, with a range of scattering strengths and residual dispersion offsets introduced into the bursts\. The modified sub\-burst slope law approaches different limiting regimes depending on the observing frequency and the relative strength of the propagation effects\. At high frequencies, the TRDM slope law is recovered, whereas at low frequencies propagation effects dominate the relation\. Simulations recover the scattering timescale with high precision, while residual dispersion is less reliably recovered because of its degeneracy with A\. Applying our methodology to observations of FRB 20121102A yields a scattering timescale of \~2\.4 ms at 1 GHz when the 400\-800 MHz CHIME observation is included\. Excluding this measurement leaves the scattering timescale unconstrained at its lower bound\. These results demonstrate that broad frequency coverage is essential for separating intrinsic and extrinsic contributions, with high\-frequency observations constraining the intrinsic emission behavior and low\-frequency observations providing strong leverage on propagation effects\.
