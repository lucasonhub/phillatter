# Clustering experimental data for the prediction and classification of mechanical properties of carbon fiber–polysulfone composite materials

**Autores:** Nikitin Yu\. Nikita; Stepashkin A\. Andrey

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate determination of carbon fiber type and analysis of failure causes in carbon fiber–polysulfone composites critically depend on the ability to predict and classify their mechanical properties from experimental data\. However, inherent heterogeneity in such data – arising from different fiber types or varying processing parameters – often degrades the performance of prediction and classification models\. This study aims to select the most effective clustering algorithm for the preliminary grouping of experimental data, while determining the accuracy of predicting stability limits and deformation at high values\. Five algorithms \(K\-Means, CURE, HDBSCAN, DPMM, and Louvain\) are compared on two datasets: a heterogeneous set of 817 specimens with four fiber types, and a homogeneous set of 215 specimens with a single fiber type but varying polysulfone concentration\. Clustering quality is assessed by external metrics \(ARI, AMI, V\-measure, FMI\) and internal metrics \(Silhouette, Calinski–Harabasz, Davies–Bouldin, DBCV, Jaccard\), followed by ranking via principal component analysis \(PCA\)\. For the heterogeneous dataset, HDBSCAN achieves the best quality \(ARI = 0\.988\)\. For the homogeneous dataset, K\-Means yields the best results \(ARI = 0\.756\) and identifies statistically distinct groups corresponding to different polysulfone concentrations\. CURE and DPMM show poor clustering quality on the second dataset \(ARI ≤ 0\.004\)\. Integrating the obtained clusters as features into random forest and convolutional neural network models increases the coefficient of determination R² for tensile strength prediction from 0\.735 to 0\.947 \(first dataset\) and from 0\.977 to 0\.987 \(second dataset\)\. Improvements are also observed for strain at maximum load for the CNN model on the homogeneous dataset \(R² from 0\.899 to 0\.995\)\. The improvement in the quality metric was evaluated against the expert labeling of the dataset\. The proposed approach – preliminary clustering followed by separate modeling on the identified groups – significantly enhances the accuracy of predicting the mechanical properties of composite materials\. For heterogeneous data with multiple fiber types, HDBSCAN is recommended; for homogeneous data with varying processing parameters, K\-Means is recommended\.
