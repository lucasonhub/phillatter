# Retrograde Cystic Duct Milking Before Ligation for Isolated Gallbladder Ascariasis with Acute Acalculous Hydrops: An Observational Analytic Case Study

**Autores:** Muhammad Arisma Dwirian Putra; Ahmad Fakhrozi Helmi; Ali Hanafiah; Alsen Arlan

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Ascaris lumbricoides infects an estimated 700 million to one billion people, but gallbladder involvement accounts for only about 2\.1% of hepatobiliary ascariasis\. A worm impacted in the cystic duct can cause acute acalculous hydrops and may be transected during cholecystectomy\. Objective: To describe the presentation, open surgical management with retrograde cystic duct milking, and six\-month course of isolated gallbladder ascariasis with acute acalculous hydrops\. Methods: This study describes a 57\-year\-old woman with acute\-on\-chronic right upper quadrant pain in Indonesia, assessed clinically, biochemically, and sonographically from admission \(T0\) to month 6 \(T6\)\. Results: At admission, pain was 8/10, leukocytes 15,460/mm³, eosinophils 8\.0% \(1,236/mm³\), C\-reactive protein \(CRP\) 45\.0 mg/L, and alkaline phosphatase 133 U/L, with normal bilirubin \(0\.8 mg/dL\)\. Ultrasonography showed a tense, stone\-free gallbladder \(10\.5 × 4\.5 cm; estimated volume 111\.3 cm³; wall 4\.5 mm\) with the inner tube and stripe signs of a live worm impacted in the cystic duct\. Under rapid sequence induction without nitrous oxide, open cholecystectomy with retrograde cystic duct milking displaced an intact 22\.0\-cm A\. lumbricoides into the gallbladder before ligation\. Albendazole 400 mg was given on postoperative day 1 after flatus; she was discharged on day 3\. At month 6, she was asymptomatic, CRP \(−96\.9%\) and eosinophils \(−88\.9%\) had fallen, and liver enzymes were normal\. Conclusion: In this patient, retrograde cystic duct milking during open cholecystectomy, followed by albendazole after the return of bowel function, removed the impacted worm intact and was followed by full recovery\.
