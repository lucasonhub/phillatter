# The role of professional learning communities in teacher implementation of problem\-based learning in teaching senior high school mathematics

**Autores:** Rose Ampomah; Benjamin Adu Obeng; Francis Ohene Boateng

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Problem\-Based Learning \(PBL\) is increasingly recognised as an effective approach for promoting students&\#x27; critical thinking, problem\-solving, and conceptual understanding in mathematics\. Although PBL is emphasised in the Ghanaian Senior High School mathematics curriculum, its classroom implementation remains limited, and empirical evidence on the role of Professional Learning Communities \(PLCs\) in supporting it is scarce\. This study examined the extent to which PLCs are associated with teachers&\#x27; implementation of PBL\. An explanatory sequential mixed\-methods design was adopted\. Data were collected from 86 mathematics teachers and school administrators in three public Senior High Schools using a structured questionnaire \(83 valid responses retained for quantitative analysis\), followed by semi\-structured interviews with 15 teachers\. Quantitative data were analysed using descriptive statistics and multiple linear regression, and qualitative data were analysed thematically\. Participants reported high levels of PLC participation \(M = 3\.47\) and PBL implementation \(M = 3\.48\)\. The five PLC dimensions jointly predicted PBL implementation, R² = \.424, F\(5, 77\) = 11\.345, p &lt; \.001\. Shared Mission, Vision and Values, Collective Responsibility for Student Learning, and Collective Inquiry were significant individual predictors\. Interviews indicated that PLCs were associated with collaborative lesson planning, professional dialogue, reflective practice, and teachers&\#x27; confidence in implementing PBL, while inadequate time, limited instructional resources, and insufficient institutional support constrained implementation\. Effective PLCs are associated with teachers&\#x27; greater capacity to implement PBL in Senior High School mathematics\. Strengthening PLC activities through continuous professional development, structured collaboration, and adequate resource provision is recommended to support sustained PBL implementation\.
