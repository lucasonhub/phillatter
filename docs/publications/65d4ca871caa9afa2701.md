# The “Triple identity” of generative AI in public governance: dilemmas and adaptive solutions

**Autores:** Sihan Lu; Yan Zhou; Tao Hong

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The profound integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence \(GenAI\) is reshaping the frameworks and operations of public governance\. While academic interest has surged, extant literature frequently examines GenAI through a siloed lens—treating it either as a governance tool, a regulatory object, or a novel decision\-making agent\. Such a fragmented perspective obscures the micro\-level governance paradoxes and systemic risks that emerge when these roles intersect\. To bridge this theoretical gap, this study conceptualizes the public management system as a complex adaptive system, identifying GenAI as an “adaptive agent” within this framework to explore the micro\-paradoxes, macro\-risk emergence, and governance models driven by its triple identity\. The research uses a single\-case study of Singapore’s “Smart Nation” initiative\. Tracking governance strategies and outcomes across various situations yields general insights for GenAI governance\. The findings show a “Governance Impossible Triangle” in GenAI’s triple identity\. This triangle has persistent tensions among maximizing efficiency, safeguarding the legitimacy of decisions, and risk control\. To resolve these tensions, the study proposes a “Contextual Identity Configuration Model” driven by problem complexity, risk severity, value conflicts, and explainability requirements\. By analyzing four prototypical scenarios—routine optimization, professional assistance, high\-stakes discretion, and complex crises—the paper elucidates how management systems dynamically recalibrate algorithmic tool\-intensity and agentic power through continuous feedback loops\. The study concludes that policymakers must move beyond “one\-size\-fits\-all” regulatory approaches toward an agile, context\-based governance paradigm\. By implementing multi\-layered defense systems and joint accountability networks, governments can strive to achieve a profound alignment between technological empowerment and core public values\.
