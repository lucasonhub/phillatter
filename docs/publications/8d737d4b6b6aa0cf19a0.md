# The Impact of Artificial Intelligence on Portfolio Performance: A Comparative Study of Machine Learning and Traditional Portfolio Optimisation Approaches

**Autores:** S Reshma; V Narendra

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial intelligence and machine learning are now being used in investment products, but it remains uncertain whether these tools consistently outperform established portfolio optimisation methods or control risk more effectively\. This study compares three AI/ML\-oriented exchange\-traded funds—AIEQ, QRFT and AMOM—with three funds following minimum\-variance or low\-volatility strategies—USMV, SPLV and ACWV\. The comparison covers 2021–2025 and relies on secondary data for annual total returns, the S&amp;P 500 total\-return benchmark and the three\-month Treasury bill rate\. Performance is assessed using returns, Sharpe and Sortino ratios, and the worst annual loss\. Risk\-management efficiency is represented by a combined measure based on volatility, downside capture, worst\-year loss and beta\. The analysis uses chi\-square tests, one\-way and two\-way ANOVA, multiple regression and mediation testing\. Traditional funds recorded stronger risk\-management efficiency \(F = 67\.9, p = 0\.001\), while the difference in average annual returns between the groups was not statistically significant \(10\.8% for ML\-based funds and 7\.8% for traditional funds; p = 0\.622\)\. Although the regression model was significant overall \(R² = 0\.76\), market conditions were the only significant individual predictor\. Results also indicate that the relative performance of the two approaches changes with the market: ML\-based funds did better in rising periods but experienced substantially larger losses in 2022\. Risk\-management efficiency did not significantly explain returns, and the proposed mediation effect was not supported\. Given the limited sample, these findings should be treated as preliminary rather than conclusive\.
