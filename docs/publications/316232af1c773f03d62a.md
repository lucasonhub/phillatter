# Qin, Han: The Ontological Positioning of Large Language Models

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Large language models are emergent structures produced by a reduction in the discreteness of linguistic representation\. They are neither subjects nor mere tools: they are products of human chiseling whose representation spaces possess powerful emergent organization but lack an intrinsic direction of their own\. The paper distinguishes discreteness—the resolution of differentiation within a given dimension—from dimension—the number of directions in which differentiation can occur\. On this account, current language models reduce discreteness without adding a new dimension\. This explains both their striking capabilities and their limits\. Emergence, hallucination, and alignment are three aspects of the same structural change: reduced discreteness restores associations among meanings, can over\-restore them, and then requires selective reconstruction of boundaries\. The account compares this transition to the expansion from integers to real numbers and argues that eliminating hallucination without sacrificing some emergence is impossible for the same low\-discreteness generative kernel, although external grounding can redistribute the tradeoff at the system level\. It concludes that the next qualitative advances are more likely to arise from architectural change than from scale alone\. \(direct link \)
