\_Zenodod\_ 1\. 2026What does naming add to operations that already produce real effects? This paper examines the conditions under which names become established, acquire referential functions, and alter subsequent possibilities without treating naming as a universal prerequisite for reality or scientific activity\. It distinguishes names, concepts, identifiers, record linkages, evaluations, institutional authority, and agent attribution, examining how these functions interact without necessarily coinciding\. Engaging Frege, Russell, Marcus, Kripke, Sellars, Brandom, Brassier, and research on classification and institutional evaluation, the paper investigates the semantic, causal, modal, and practical dimensions of naming\. It develops an operational\-shape approach to the formation, continuity, division, and dissolution of subjects and agents, including autonomous scientific systems\. Laozian namelessness is examined as a historical and comparative resource rather than a universal explanatory principle\. The proposed Laozi Machine serves as a prospective framework for comparing naming dependencies, intervention conditions, correction delays, synchronization, resource allocation, and operational reachability\. The paper further connects these problems to Xenoscience, in which scientific objects, variables, observational configurations, and agent boundaries may themselves undergo revision\. Its central claim is that naming may enable, constrain, redirect, or leave unchanged real operations, depending on the configurations through which referential and material processes become coupled\. The Laozi Machine remains a theoretical research proposal; no experimental results are claimed\. \(direct link \)
