# HUMAN\-AI COLLABORATIVE DEBATE IN EFL CLASSROOMS: RE\-EXAMINING ARGUMENTATIVE SPEAKING COMPETENCE, ACADEMIC INTEGRITY, AND PEDAGOGICAL AGENCY THROUGH COPILOT AND CHATGPT

**Autores:** Ranta Butarbutar; Rubén González Vallejo; Mohammed Boukezzoula

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Argumentative speaking competence in English as a foreign language is multidimensional and includes linguistic accuracy, rhetorical logic, interactive response, and affective regulation\. However, its development remains challenging, particularly in terms of argument coherence, anxiety management, and academic integrity in the era of learning\-based AI\. This study employs a mixed\-method sequential explanatory design with one group pre\-test and post\-test over eight weeks, involving ChatGPT and Copilot as debate partners and reflections through Google Classroom\. Quantitative analysis showed significant improvement in ASC \(d= 2\.86\), academic integrity \(d=2\.04\), and pedagogical agency \(d= 2\.37\)\. Meanwhile, qualitative data confirm that students perceive AI as idea facilitators that help to find out contra\-argument and increase self\-confidence, but still remain critical to keep integrity\. These findings strengthen cross\-culture communicative competence and AI as a cognitive partnership and are consistent with the UNESCO and OECD principles of AI ethics\. Practically, this suggests that educators should utilize AI as a sparring partner in the reflective pedagogy framework, students should use AI as an idea’s facilitator, not a final answer or argument, and decision makers and EFL curriculum and material developers should organize particular modules about AI\-ethical communicative competence so that integrating technology still aligns with academic values\.
