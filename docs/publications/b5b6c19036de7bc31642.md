# Infection‐Phase Biomolecular Coronas in Inhaled Nanomedicine

**Autores:** Zhenchao Wu; Taikang Yao; Ning Shen

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

An inhaled nanomedicine encounters two successive forms of remodeling: device\-related changes during aerosol generation and biological conditioning after deposition\. Respiratory infection adds a moving interface, as epithelial secretion, surfactant composition, plasma leakage, and neutrophil\-derived material vary across airway compartments and over time\. Pulmonary and disease\-corona studies already establish that host environments influence nanoparticle identity\. The next question is how this knowledge should change formulation selection and dosing for infected lungs\. We propose an infection\-phase framework based on measured, overlapping airway environments\. It connects the post\-aerosol particle to its surface\-associated biomolecules, distinguishes this interface from aggregation and bulk mucus effects, and links analytical measurements to functional delivery\. A staged research program begins with one compartment and two contrasting environments, then adds controlled particle\-transfer experiments, candidate\-component perturbation, and donor\-level validation\. Lipid nanoparticles, polymeric carriers, and nanozymes require different functional endpoints within this shared framework\. The immediate translational output is a biorelevant performance stress test that identifies robust or environment\-selective formulations and guides the next experiment\.
