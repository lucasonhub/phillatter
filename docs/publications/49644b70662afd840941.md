# Clinical outcome and implant accuracy in cervical fractures stabilization using 3D\-printed patient\-specific drill guides for placement of bicortical screws in 16 dogs

**Autores:** Alba Manzanedo‐Delgado; Bill Oxley; Sébastien Behr

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective To describe the clinical outcome of dogs with cervical fractures undergoing spinal stabilization surgery using 3D\-printed patient\-specific drill guides, and to evaluate the accuracy of pedicle screw placement with this technique\. Study design Retrospective descriptive study\. Animals or sample population Sixteen dogs\. Methods Medical records were searched between August 2015 and January 2025 for dogs with traumatic spinal fracture via CT scan causing signs of cervical myelopathy that underwent spinal stabilization surgery using 3D\-printed patient\-specific drill guides followed by a postoperative CT scan\. Neurological status was graded retrospectively with the Modified Frankel Score \(MFS\) on presentation, preoperatory on the day of the surgery, postoperatively on the first day after surgery, at hospital discharge and at the follow\-up examination performed 4–6 weeks after surgery, when available\. A telephone questionnaire was obtained for the long\-term outcome\. Results Sixteen dogs met the inclusion criteria \(3/16 grade 2 MFS, 5/16 grade 3 MFS, 7/16 grade 4 MFS and 1/16 grade 5 MFS\)\. Two dogs showed mild minor transient intraoperative complications\. Optimal pedicle screw placement was achieved in 85/90 \(94\.4%\) of screws\. Twenty\-four hours after surgery, the neurological grade remained unchanged in 10/16 dogs, improved in 5/16, and transiently deteriorated in 1/16 dog, which recovered by discharge\. Postoperative complications occurred in 10/16 dogs and resolved with medical management in 9/10 cases\. Short term follow\-up revealed a static stable \(6/14\) or improved \(8/14\) neurological grade since discharge\. Among the 12 dogs with owner\-reported long\-term follow\-up, all were reported by their owners to be ambulatory and to have improved neurologically\. Long\-term quality\-of\-life \(5 = excellent; 1 = poor\) was scored by the owners 5/5 in 11/12 \(91\.7%\) dogs and 4/5 in 1/12 \(8\.3%\) dogs\. Conclusion Dogs in this study overall had an improved neurological status and favorable long\-term outcomes when follow\-up information was available\. However, postoperative adverse events, including complications, should be considered when interpreting overall clinical outcome\.
