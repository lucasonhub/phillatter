# Lifetime maternal stress, fetal sex, and microRNAs in prenatal extracellular vesicles and placenta in relation to children’s internalizing problems

**Autores:** Rosalind J\. Wright; Rosalind J\. Wright; Hachem Saddiki; Regina Dominguez; Hoatian Wu; Whitney Cowell; Robert O\. Wright; Robert O\. Wright; Andrea Baccarelli; Elena Colicino

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Background Prenatal stress predicts offspring anxiety and depressive disorders and placental microRNA \(miRNA\) signaling in a sex\-specific manner\. Methods We prospectively examined associations of miRNAs measured in placental tissue and in extracellular vesicles \(EVs\) from prenatal maternal blood with preschool Child Behavior Checklist \(CBCL\) outcomes \[Total Problems, Internalizing Problems, Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders\-oriented scales for Anxiety and Affective Problems\]\. We first used Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator regressions embedded in a stability selection procedure to identify miRNAs most consistently associated with CBCL outcomes\. Then multivariable linear regressions examined all selected miRNAs in relation to each outcome within a single model\. Finally, we examined miRNA\-CBCL associations stratified by lifetime maternal stress measured prenatally and fetal sex and fit models including a three\-way interaction term for stress, sex, and miRNA expression\. Results Among miRNAs selected by the machine learning models, miR\-330\-5p in blood EVs and miR\-548aw in placenta emerged as top candidates related to CBCL outcomes\. When jointly considering fetal sex and lifetime maternal stress as modifiers, the association between blood miR\-330\-5p and affective problems was positive in females exposed to low stress \(β: 0\.73, 95%CI: 0\.15, 1\.3\) but negative in males with high stress exposure \(β: \-1\.64, 95%CI: \-3\.01, \-0\.28\), pint=0\.03\. Placental Let\-7b expression was negatively associated with total problems among males with high stress exposure \(β: \-6\.58, 95%CI: \-12\.45, \-0\.71\) but positively associated among males with low stress \(β: 3\.63, 95%CI: 1\.26, 6\.01\), pint=0\.04\. Top KEGG pathways included Hippo, FoxO, and PI3K/mTOR signaling\. Conclusions Prenatal miRNA signaling may play a role in sex\-specific lifetime maternal stress effects on neuropsychological development\.
