# Legal Tactics Versus Communication Strategy: The Ethics of Arbitration Clauses in Crisis Management

**Autores:** Kaspar Beelen; Katherine Ireland; Tim Samples

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Digital platforms increasingly deploy mandatory arbitration clauses in their terms of use \(TOUs\) to channel user disputes into private resolution, shielding conflicts from public view while blocking class actions\. We argue that TOU\-based arbitration raises ethical dilemmas for strategic communication: by privatizing disputes in advance, platforms may prioritize legal risk containment over transparent stakeholder engagement\. Drawing on Situational Crisis Communication Theory \(SCCT\) and the READINESS framework, we show that both frameworks assume an organization retains the capacity to respond when a crisis arrives, and we examine how arbitration constrains that capacity before any crisis emerges\. Arbitration strategies create practical and ethical tensions with accommodative responses such as apology and corrective action, and they occupy an ethical “twilight zone” of legally sanctioned opacity\. Such strategies may even exacerbate the informational conflicts and reputational costs characteristic of “sticky” crises\. Using a trained classifier with an original longitudinal corpus of 861 TOUs from fifty major platforms, we document the proliferation of arbitration clauses in the digital marketplace over time\. Our interdisciplinary analysis identifies arbitration as a pre\-crisis blind spot in crisis communication theory and a question of ethical leadership for communication professionals navigating tensions between legal containment and stakeholder trust\.
