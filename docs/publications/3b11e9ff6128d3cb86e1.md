# Sustainable by All\-Biobased Design: Recyclable Flax Fiber/Silk Matrix Composites with Machine Learning\-Assisted Mechanical Performance Prediction

**Autores:** Fang He; Zhixiang Xu; Yuhang Lin; Mingying Yang; Ming Liu; Zongpu Xu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Fiber\-reinforced polymer composites are indispensable structural materials, yet their sustainability is limited by two key issues: the environmental impact of manufacturing and the difficulty of recycling at the end of life\. Here, we develop fully biobased composites by combining flax fibers with a regenerated silk fibroin matrix to overcome both obstacles\. Owing to the room\-temperature self\-assembly capability of the silk matrix, chemical additives such as crosslinkers, curing agents, and catalysts are completely avoided during fabrication\. The resulting composite achieves a tensile strength and modulus of up to ∼70 MPa and 2\.4 GPa, comparable to conventional petroleum\-based counterparts, while the silk matrix can be dissolved to non\-destructively recover flax fibers for repeated reuse\. To complement this all\-biobased material design, we further construct a machine learning model trained on experimental datasets for predicting the mechanical properties of fiber\-reinforced silk matrix composites\. By narrowing the experimental formulation search space and identifying a promising fiber\-content range for subsequent experimental validation, this data\-driven approach serves as a low\-resource design aid that reduces trial\-and\-error experimentation\. Collectively, the combination of fully biobased composite design with machine learning\-guided formulation offers a promising pathway toward sustainable composite development from the present to the future\.
