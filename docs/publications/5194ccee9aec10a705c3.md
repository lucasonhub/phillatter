# Artificial Intelligence Applications in Library and Information Science: A Comparative Study of Traditional Library Services and AI\-Enabled Library Systems

**Autores:** Jagtap B\. P\.

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial Intelligence \(AI\) is emerging as an important technological development in the field of Library and Information Science \(LIS\)\. The increasing volume of digital information, changing information\-seeking behaviour, and growing expectations of library users have encouraged libraries to explore intelligent technologies for improving information organization, retrieval, preservation, and dissemination\. AI applications such as machine learning, natural language processing, automated cataloguing, intelligent recommendation systems, conversational chatbots, optical character recognition, and generative AI have created new possibilities for transforming traditional library services into intelligent and user\-oriented information services\. The present study examines the applications of AI in Library and Information Science and compares traditional library services with AI\-enabled approaches\. A comparative evaluation framework is developed to assess selected dimensions, including cataloguing, classification, information retrieval, reference services, circulation, user assistance, digital preservation, and personalized information delivery\. The study adopts a descriptive and literature\-based comparative methodology using published research and professional guidelines concerning AI adoption in libraries\. The comparative framework highlights the potential benefits of AI in improving operational efficiency, supporting information discovery, facilitating round\-the\-clock assistance, and expanding access to digital resources\. At the same time, the study identifies important challenges, including financial constraints, data privacy, algorithmic bias, inaccurate AI\-generated responses, copyright concerns, inadequate digital infrastructure, and the need for professional training\. The study emphasizes that AI should complement rather than replace the professional judgement and service\-oriented responsibilities of librarians\. It recommends a phased approach to AI adoption, supported by institutional policies, staff development, evaluation mechanisms, and responsible data governance\. The findings provide a conceptual basis for libraries seeking to modernize their services through appropriate and ethically governed AI technologies\.
