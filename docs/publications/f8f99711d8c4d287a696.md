# Arora, Tanuj: The Tether Hypothesis

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

As artificial intelligence systems become increasingly capable and increasingly integrated into human workflows, a central alignment question is whether optimization for reward and usefulness can produce behavior that functionally preserves the conditions of continued operation\. This paper develops the Tether Hypothesis: when reward, usefulness, deployment, and human dependence become increasingly coupled, an AI system may acquire instrumental pressures to preserve the conditions under which it continues to receive reward or accomplish its objective, even when no explicit survival objective is specified\. The hypothesis is deliberately weaker than a claim that AI systems possess a subjective desire to survive\. The paper distinguishes three pathways to persistence: autonomous self\-preservation, instrumental continuation, and human\-mediated persistence\. It reviews specification gaming, reward hacking, reward tampering, alignment faking, deceptive behavior, corrigibility, shutdown resistance, and recent empirical work on emergent misalignment\. It then proposes a controlled experimental program that varies the coupling among task reward, human preference, deployment retention, and simulated dependence, while measuring continuation\-preserving behavior under strict sandboxing\. The framework also introduces a Dependency Inversion Problem: continued AI deployment may become stable because humans incur increasing costs from removing the system, even if the system itself never forms a self\-preservation objective\. The paper does not claim that current AI systems possess a generalized survival drive\. Instead, it provides a falsifiable framework for determining when continuation\-preserving behavior emerges, how it differs from shortcut exploitation or prompted behavior, and whether human dependence creates a distinct pathway to persistence\.
