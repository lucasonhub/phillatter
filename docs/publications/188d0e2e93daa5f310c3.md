# Jovanovic, Vladisav: The Love That Remains: Relational Error and the Boundaries of Self\-Interpretation

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Relational disconfirmation and romantic relationship dissolution can begin as local epistemic events and develop into much broader changes in self\-concept, self\-knowledge, autobiographical memory, self\-trust, self\-esteem, and personal meaning\. A person may discover that they were seriously mistaken about a partner or relationship, yet the resulting belief revision can spread beyond the claims directly supported by the new evidence\. This paper develops an evidential\-boundary account of that process\. Drawing on research on the relational self, relationship\-contingent self\-esteem, contingencies of self\-worth, breakup\-related self\-concept change, autobiographical memory, shame, unconditional self\-acceptance, self\-compassion, self\-affirmation, and psychological flexibility, it asks when relational evidence legitimately warrants broader self\-revision and when revision becomes unsupported overgeneralization\. The paper distinguishes first\-person experience from judgments about another person, relational evaluation, and global self\-evaluation, arguing that different claims require different evidential grounds\. It proposes noncontingent self\-relation as a candidate boundary condition that may allow strong correction where evidence warrants it without converting relational error into global self\-negation\. Implications are developed for psychotherapy, emotional self\-knowledge, belief updating, post\-breakup adjustment, and AI\-assisted reflection\. The account is explicitly falsifiable: if existing constructs fully explain the proposed effect, or if claim differentiation reduces warranted learning or accountability, the additional framework should be narrowed or abandoned\.
