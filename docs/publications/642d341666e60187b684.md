# Proximity as a factor of space cluster formation in a European peripheral economy: evidence from the Greek Space 4\.0 ecosystem

**Autores:** Hrysi Stathaki; Pantoleon Skayannis; Yeoryios A\. Stamboulis; Apostolos Vetsikas

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Space 4\.0 has transformed predominantly state\-led space activity into heterogeneous ecosystems requiring coordination across organizational and institutional boundaries\. Although proximity research explains knowledge exchange and collaboration, its governance role in emerging space ecosystems remains insufficiently specified\. This study examines how proximity shapes partner selection, collaboration design, and uncertainty management in the Greek Space 4\.0 ecosystem\. Methods This qualitative case\-study draws on 22 semi\-structured interviews with representatives of si\-Cluster firms and research organizations\. Theory\-informed thematic analysis combined deductive coding around five proximity dimensions with inductive pattern identification and constant comparison\. The analysis progressed from individual dimensions to recurring configurations, interaction mechanisms, functional effects, and the proximity profile\. Results Collaboration depended less on co\-location, cluster membership, or national affiliation than on context\-specific proximity configurations\. Cognitive and organizational proximity jointly shaped knowledge integration and project execution, while social and institutional proximity influenced expectations under uncertainty\. Misalignment generated coordination difficulties and constrained collaborative viability\. Geographical proximity played a conditional role and was partly offset by non\-spatial alignment, digital interaction, and temporary co\-presence\. The relative significance of proximity dimensions varied across project stages\. These findings informed the proximity profile, a qualitative framework for identifying relational complementarities, vulnerabilities, and alignment requirements in partnership design\. Discussion The findings position proximity as a dynamic governance mechanism whose effects arise from interactions among multiple dimensions\. Collaborative viability depends on evolving patterns of relational alignment rather than isolated dimensions or geographical concentration\. Instead of proposing an additional proximity dimension, the study advances a configurational interpretation of how established dimensions interact under particular contextual conditions\. The proximity profile translates this logic into a context\-sensitive basis for partnership assessment, while multi\-level governance depends on relational compatibility across EU/ESA, national, ecosystem, and project levels\.
