# Child, William: Self\-consciousness, Self\-knowledge, and the Practice of Using ‘I’

## Descrição do feed

In Christian Kanzian, Volker Munz, Anja Weiberg &amp; Joseph Wang\-Kathrein, Contributions to International Wittgenstein Symposium 2026, 50 Years Wittgenstein Symposium\. Kirchberg am Wechsel: ALWS\. 2026According to the standard reading of Blue Book pp\. 66\-7, Wittgenstein endorses a distinction between two kinds of use of ‘I’: those in which ‘I’ is used ‘as object’, which involve ‘the recognition of a particular person’ and are vulnerable to error through misidentification; and those in which ‘I’ is used ‘as subject’, where there is ‘no question of recognizing a person’, and no possibility of errors of misidentification\. Rachel Wiseman \(2019\) has argued that the standard reading fails to appreciate ‘the complete reorientation of our philosophical thinking about the self and self\-consciousness’ that is implied by Wittgenstein’s work\. In particular, it wrongly conceives of self\-consciousness ‘in terms of a special epistemological capacity’\. The correct, and properly Wittgensteinian, approach to understanding self\-consciousness is instead to ‘describe the form of life of creatures with self\-consciousness’\. But why might it be objectionable to think that self\-consciousness involves the possession of a distinctively first\-person way or ways of knowing about ourselves? Does Wittgenstein give us good reason not to think in that way? And does the project of ‘describing our form of life through a study of \[our\] linguistic practices’ – specifically, the practice of using the word ‘I’ – provide a genuine alternative to the kind of appeal to distinctive, first\-person epistemic capacities involved in the standard reading? The paper explores these questions with particular reference to Wittgenstein’s treatment of statements about our own past intentions, wishes, feelings etc\., and to Anscombe’s work on subjectivity and the first person\. \(direct link \)
