# “Calcare Cavernoso” as a key predisposing factor for sinkhole occurrence in Tuscany \(Italy\): inventory update, remote sensing detection, and GIS–ML susceptibility modelling

**Autores:** Marina Funaro; Stefania Nisio; Sergio Madonna

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The “Calcare Cavernoso” \(cavernous limestone\) formation is a dolomitic carbonate unit characterized by a vacuolar texture and high intrinsic porosity, which favours groundwater\-driven dissolution and cavity development and may predispose to subsidence\. This study investigates the spatial relationship between sinkholes and the distribution of “Calcare Cavernoso” in Tuscany \(Italy\) and updates the regional subset of the ISPRA national sinkhole database\. An integrated approach combined bibliographic information, targeted field surveys, historical cartography, and multi\-source geospatial datasets\. Geological and thematic layers were retrieved from the Tuscany “Geoscopio” portal, while Copernicus Sentinel\-2 imagery was processed using the Normalized Difference Water Index \(NDWI\) to support the detection of flooded sinkholes\. Historical maps were exploited to infer sinkhole persistence and potential evolutionary trends, including partial or complete infilling\. All thematic layers \(geology, faults, hydrography, quarries, mines, topography, and sinkholes\) were harmonized, georeferenced, and rasterized on a 250 × 250 m grid within an ESRI ArcGIS environment\. Susceptibility modelling was implemented using density\-based proxies and an Extreme Gradient Boosting \(XGBoost\) framework, producing probability outputs subsequently grouped into classes to enhance operational usability\. The results substantially expand the regional sinkhole inventory \(from 88 to 1223 mapped features\) and support a strong spatial association between sinkhole occurrence and “Calcare Cavernoso”, further corroborated by targeted field checks\. The most influential susceptibility drivers include “Calcare Cavernoso”, low\-slope settings, DTM\-derived parameters, fault density, and anthropogenic underground activities \(quarries and mines\), while other variables show secondary or more complex contributions\.
