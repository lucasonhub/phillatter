# Enhancing nursing students’ learning of nutrition through podcasting: An active approach for Generation Z students in Morocco

**Autores:** Saïd Bouchefra

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Student\-generated video podcasting may support active learning in nursing education, but evidence from low\- and middle\-income settings remains limited\. Material and Methods This two\-academic\-year, non\-randomized consecutive\-cohort study was conducted at the Taza annex of the Higher Institute of Nursing and Health Techniques, Fes, Morocco\. The 2024\-2025 cohort \(n = 135\) completed a collaborative student\-generated video\-podcasting activity for the therapeutic diets unit, involving group content research, script preparation, recording, presentation, peer viewing, and supervised discussion, whereas the 2025\-2026 cohort \(n = 119\) received traditional instruction\. In both cohorts, the nutrition unit was taught and assessed first, followed by the therapeutic diets unit\. Each unit was evaluated by an end\-of\-unit written examination graded on a 10\-point scale, and the overall module grade was the mean of the two unit scores\. The nutrition score therefore preceded the therapeutic\-diets intervention and was used as a pre\-intervention academic comparator\. The therapeutic\-diets written examinations used different questions across academic years but assessed the same construct and learning objectives; item difficulty indices were used to minimize differences in examination difficulty\. Welch t\-tests and an ANCOVA model adjusted for nutrition score were used\. Results Cohorts were comparable for gender, program, age, and pre\-intervention nutrition score \(6\.16 ± 1\.37 vs\. 6\.18 ± 1\.35; p = 0\.907\)\. The intervention cohort achieved a higher therapeutic\-diets written examination score than the traditional cohort \(7\.20 ± 1\.21 vs\. 6\.24 ± 1\.30; mean difference = 0\.96, 95% CI 0\.65 to 1\.27; p &lt; 0\.001; d = 0\.77\)\. The adjusted mean difference was 0\.97 points \(95% CI 0\.69 to 1\.25; p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Satisfaction was favorable, but internal consistency was below the conventional 0\.70 threshold \(Cronbach’s alpha = 0\.69\), so item\-level interpretation was retained\. Conclusions The implemented video\-based collaborative learning activity was associated with a higher therapeutic\-diets written examination score\. Because the intervention was multicomponent and cohorts were not randomized, the observed association cannot be attributed specifically to video podcasting\. Prospective controlled replication using a common or item\-banked examination is needed\.
