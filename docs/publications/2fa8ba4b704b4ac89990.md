# The role of regional organisations as peacebuilders in climate\-related conflicts: the case of the African Union

**Autores:** Francisco Proença García; Lucie Calléja; Raquel dos Santos Duque

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Due to its size and geographical characteristics, which span the latitudes of the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn and various sub\-regions, the African continent is exposed to a range of extreme weather events, including heatwaves, droughts, floods, and storms\. While Malawi, Zambia and Zimbabwe struggled with severe drought in 2024, Kenya, Tanzania, and Burundi suffered heavy flooding between March and May\. While climate change is not in itself the sole cause for fuelling a conflict, it acts as a threat multiplier that exacerbates instability\. In this context, this article analyses how regional governance can contribute to preventing climate\-related conflicts by focusing on the role of the African Union\. Despite some technological measures implemented by some African countries to improve preventive knowledge on weather forecasts and early warnings to enable quick responses at the national level, coordinated regional approaches may prove key to enhancing information\- sharing and preventive action\. Drawing on the Africa Multi\-Hazard Early Warning and Early Action System \(AMHEWAS\), the article explores how the AU institutionalizes climate\-security linkages and integrates environmental risks into peacebuilding frameworks\. Using a qualitative methodology that combines document analysis with questionnaire responses from AU practicioners and experts, the article offers an exploratory assessment of the AU’s capacity to translate early\-warning into early\-action\. The findings contribute to debates on climate security by demonstrating how regional institutions can function as peacebuilders through anticipatory governance mechanisms, while acknowledging the limitations of the empirical basis and the need for further research\.
