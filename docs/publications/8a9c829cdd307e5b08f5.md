# Hospital and health\-center bed expansion and persistent relative mismatch across geometry\-stable non\-district Chinese counties, 2015–2023

**Autores:** Kai Dai; Ziao Chen; Ling Wang; Zihao Lin; Yiqiang Feng

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Territorial health equity depends not only on expanding health resources but also on aligning local capacity with territorial planning contexts\. We examined whether changes in hospital and health\-center bed allocation were associated more strongly with territorial planning context or local fiscal\-economic capacity\. Methods We analyzed a 2015\-2023 panel of 1,787 geometry\-stable non\-district counties in China\. The analysis combined county and province\-by\-year fixed effects, long\-difference models, block\-level Shapley decomposition, high\-mismatch\-state transitions, spatial diagnostics, discrete\-time exit models, and adjacent\-county pair comparisons\. Results Median beds per 10,000 registered residents increased from 35\.1 in 2015 to 54\.0 in 2023, whereas the distribution of relative planning\-supply mismatch changed little\. Planning\-context and capacity blocks explained similarly small shares of annual variation in total beds\. For bed density, the larger baseline planning\-context share was driven primarily by the negative population coefficient and did not indicate allocation aligned with observed healthcare need\. Higher initial capacity was associated with greater cumulative bed\-density growth, lower relative mismatch, and a higher probability of exiting the high\-mismatch state\. Among county\-years previously classified as high\-mismatch, 84\.0% remained in that state in the following year\. Relative mismatch remained spatially clustered, but prefecture boundaries did not significantly amplify capacity\-related bed\-density gaps\. Discussion Aggregate expansion, relative territorial alignment, and recovery from persistent high mismatch represent distinct health\-system outcomes\. The findings support context\-sensitive allocation, capacity\-sensitive financing, and duration\-sensitive monitoring, while remaining associational rather than causal\.
