# Employing Artificial Intelligence in the Morphological Analysis of the English Language

**Autores:** زينب محمد لفته الباحث

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study evaluates how accurately artificial intelligence \(AI\) analyzes the morphological structure of English words\. Seventeen systems from five paradigms were compared: rule\-based analyzers, unsupervised statistical learning, the subword tokenizers of pretrained large language models \(LLMs\), supervised feature\-based learning and neural networks\. All were applied to the 57,755 English test words of the SIGMORPHON 2022 morpheme segmentation benchmark and scored with the official morpheme\-level F1, exact match, boundary detection and lemmatization accuracy\. Neural canonical segmenters were the most accurate \(90\.5–93\.6 F1; up to 90\.4% exact match\), followed by a conditional random field \(CRF; 74\.0\), a feed\-forward neural network \(71\.9\), a knowledge\-based affix\-stripping analyzer \(64\.1\), Morfessor \(45\.0\) and LLM tokenizers \(18\.1–28\.8\)\. On words without spelling change, the CRF placed boundaries nearly as well as the neural systems \(90\.2 vs 94\.8 boundary F1\)\. The neural advantage came from restoring underlying forms in the 30% of words with orthographic alternation, where dedicated surface segmenters lost 16–32 F1 points and neural systems less than one\. Only 20–33% of the boundaries drawn by LLM tokenizers coincided with morpheme boundaries, and larger vocabularies barely improved alignment\. The knowledge\-based analyzer matched a CRF trained on 5,000–10,000 annotated words, and a linguistic coding of residual errors showed that most concern the depth of analysis and annotation conventions rather than clear mistakes\. AI can thus analyze English word structure at a near\-expert level, provided it models morphophonological alternation, but LLM subword units should not be equated with morphemes\.
