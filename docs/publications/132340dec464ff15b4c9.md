# Interlayer‐Confined LiNO3 Reservoir Enables the Formation of a Li‐Containing Hybrid SEI for Robust Sodium Metal Anodes

**Autores:** Jiaze Lv; Qiman Zhang; Zhen Tang; Jiajun Wan; Yan Peng Cao

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

ABSTRACT Sodium metal batteries hold great promise for energy storage applications, but are constrained by limited charge transfer kinetics and unstable solid electrolyte interphases \(SEI\)\. Here, we construct a montmorillonite‐based sustained‐release separator by confining within interlayer galleries and integrating it onto a glass‐fiber separator\. This interlayer‐confined reservoir provides a sustained local nitrate supply during cycling, continuously regulates the solvation environment, and maintains anion‐participated interfacial reactions\. The sustained‐release process induces the formation of a dense Li/Na hybrid SEI enriched with LiF, , and together with Na‐based inorganic species\. DFT calculations indicate that representative Li‐containing phases exhibit lower diffusion barriers than their Na‐based counterparts, facilitating interfacial ion transport and homogenizing Na deposition\. As a result, the modified separator enables highly reversible cycling for over 4000 h at 0\.25 mA /0\.25 mAh \. In full cells, retains 83\.7% capacity after 2300 cycles at 5 C, while high‐loading cells maintain 91\.5% capacity retention\. This work highlights interlayer‐confined sustained release as an effective separator‐engineering strategy for programming solvation chemistry and constructing Li/Na hybrid SEIs in sodium metal batteries\.
