# Unsupervised clustering of pulsed infrared thermography for automated assessment of a Qing Dynasty brass mirror

**Autores:** Haifeng Tang; Xue Yang; Zhenglin Xie; Guan Wang; Pan Xiao; Qi Liu

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Non\-destructive assessment of complex multi\-layered structures in cultural heritage artifacts, such as Qing Dynasty lacquer\-coated brass mirrors, is critical for conservation and technical study\. Pulsed thermography \(PT\), an active infrared thermographic technique, provides pixel\-wise temporal thermal responses sensitive to subsurface variations, but machine\-learning\-based interpretation is often limited by the scarcity of annotated data\. To address this challenge, a fully unsupervised framework combining Thermographic Signal Reconstruction \(TSR\), a Deep Autoencoder \(DAE\), and a Gaussian Mixture Model \(GMM\) is proposed to cluster thermal responses and extract thermally distinct subsurface features without prior labeling\. The method was validated on a custom\-designed test sample and successfully applied to a Qing Dynasty lacquered brass mirror, revealing spatially distinct thermal\-response patterns associated with the lacquer\-adhesive coating system\. This label\-free approach provides a practical tool for non\-destructive evaluation of cultural heritage objects, offers new insights into historical craftsmanship, and is extendable to other metal\-coating systems, supporting preservation strategies and informed conservation decision\-making\.
