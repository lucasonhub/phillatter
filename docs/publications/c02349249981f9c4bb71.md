# CONCEPTUALIZATION OF MULTIPLICITY IN ENGLISH AND AZERBAIJANI: A COGNITIVE\-DISCURSIVE ANALYSIS

**Autores:** Sevinj Mahmudova

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Multiplicity is commonly associated with the grammatical contrast between singular and plural forms\. However, linguis\- tic expressions of multiplicity extend far beyond morphological number marking\. Speakers may construe a collection as a set of separate individuals, a unified group, an indefinite quantity, a distributive configuration, or a culturally salient social category\. The present article examines how multiplicity is conceptualized and discursively represented in English and Azerbaijani\. The study adopts a qualitative comparative approach combining insights from cognitive linguistics, discourse analysis, and contemporary research on plural semantics\. The analysis focuses on nominal plural markers, collective nouns, numerals, quantifiers, bare nouns, pronouns, agreement patterns, and context\-dependent interpretations\. English generally marks nominal plurality through the suffix \-s/\-es, although irregular forms, zero plurals, collective nouns, and mass\-count shifts contribute to a more complex system\. Azerbaijani mainly employs the suffixes \-lar/\-lər, selected accord\- ing to vowel harmony, yet plural meaning may remain morphologically unmarked after numerals and certain quantitative expressions\. The comparison demonstrates that morphological form alone does not determine plural interpretation\. In both languages, discourse context activates collective, distributive, generic, associative, and approximative readings\. Never\- theless, the two languages differ in how explicitly number is encoded\. English usually requires plural marking after numer\- als and reflects number distinctions in agreement, whereas Azerbaijani generally uses a morphologically singular noun following a cardinal numeral\. Azerbaijani plural morphology also develops pragmatic meanings such as respect, family association, approximation, and social grouping\. The article argues that multiplicity should be treated as a flexible concep\- tual domain constructed through the interaction of grammar, lexical semantics, cognition, and discourse\. This approach provides a more comprehensive account than analyses that restrict plurality to a binary singular–plural opposition\.
