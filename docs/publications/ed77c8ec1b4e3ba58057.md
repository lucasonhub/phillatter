# Heterogeneous effects of climate change on real GDP per capita: evidence from selected MENA countries and Türkiye

**Autores:** Hüseyin Yılmaz

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The Middle East and North Africa \(MENA\) region is highly exposed to climate risks, but the economies considered in this study differ substantially in their resource structures and sectoral composition\. It therefore remains unclear whether a single regional panel average can adequately reflect the relationship between climate change and real gross domestic product \(GDP\) per capita across these countries\. This study examines this relationship using an 11\-country panel covering selected MENA countries and Türkiye over the period 1995–2024, with particular attention to Gulf\-non\-Gulf and country\-level variation\. The climate variables were the temperature anomaly, the squared temperature anomaly, and the Standardized Precipitation\-Evapotranspiration Index \(SPEI\), with real GDP per capita as the dependent variable\. The Cross\-Sectionally Augmented Autoregressive Distributed Lag \(CS\-ARDL\) model was employed as the baseline estimator to account for cross\-sectional dependence and slope heterogeneity, with Common Correlated Effects Mean Group \(CCEMG\) and Driscoll\-Kraay estimates used as complementary checks\. Full\-panel results show no strong and consistent average relationship between the climate variables and real GDP per capita across estimators\. Subsample estimates suggest a possible negative temperature pattern in the non\-Gulf group, but the corresponding marginal effects are not statistically significant at the 5% level and the pattern is not reproduced in additional sensitivity checks\. Formal tests do not indicate a statistically significant difference between Gulf and non\-Gulf temperature responses\. Additional full\-panel robustness specifications do not materially alter this conclusion\. The study contributes by examining the relationship at the full\-panel, subgroup, and country levels\. The results suggest that regional averages may conceal some variation across countries and groups, although the small subgroup sizes limit inference and these patterns require confirmation in larger samples\. Policy assessment in the region should therefore take country\-level conditions into account rather than rely on a single regional average\. The findings are also relevant to the broader objectives of the Sustainable Development Goals \(SDGs\), particularly SDG 8 and SDG 13\.
