# Effects of pressure on the stable carbon isotope composition of gas generated from continental type\-I and type\-III kerogen under semi\-closed conditions

**Autores:** Yanju Li; Hao Yang; Zhenhua Ning; Shengling Jiang; Biyang Feng; Xiulan Zhu; Wanchang Zhang; Xianghe Ma

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Hydrous semi\-closed pyrolysis was performed to clarify how water pressure \(WP, 50–1,200 bar\) and lithostatic pressure \(LP, 125–2000 bar\) control molecular composition and stable carbon isotopes of thermogenic gas from lacustrine Type I and Type III kerogen, and to verify whether pressure triggers carbon isotope reversal\. Immature calcareous shale \(Type\-I, Ro = 0\.40%\) and low\-mature carbonaceous mudstone \(Type\-III, Ro = 0\.68%\) from the Liaohe Shahejie Formation were heated at 450 °C and 480 °C, respectively, with a constant pyrolysis duration of 10 h\. All produced hydrocarbon gases display normal isotope sequences δ 13 C 1 &lt;δ 13 C 2 &lt;δ 13 C 3 ; no isotope reversal was observed under all experimental pressure conditions\. For oil\-prone Type\-I kerogen: moderate water pressure \(50–190 bar\) increases δ 13 C 1 by accelerating oil cracking, while WP &gt; 190 bar restrains secondary oil cracking and generates 12C\-enriched methane, ethane, and propane\. LP exerts dual controls: 125–625 bar LP gradually shifts the reactor toward a closed system and boosts oil cracking to form 13 Crich C 1 –C 2 , whereas 625–2,000 bar LP suppresses oil cracking and lightens all gaseous δ 13 C values\. For gas\-prone Type\-III kerogen, elevated WP facilitates primary kerogen cracking; progressive mixing of early\-formed 13 C\-enriched initial gas and late\-stage 12 C\-depleted cracking gas causes continuous depletion in 13 C 1 \. LP of 125–625 bar weakens hydrocarbon expulsion and hinders kerogen cracking, yielding heavier methane isotopes, while LP above 625 bar imposes negligible impacts on kerogen pyrolysis\. Distinct pressure responses between the two kerogen types originate from their divergent hydrocarbon generation pathways \(secondary oil cracking vs\. primary kerogen breakdown\)\. Our work demonstrates that pressure must be incorporated into natural gas maturity assessments using carbon isotope proxies, and confirms the favorable deep hydrocarbon resource potential of continental lacustrine source rocks in eastern China\.
