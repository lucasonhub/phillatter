# Examining West African Regional Integration on the Scale of Anglophone\-Francophone Linguistic Strain

**Autores:** Anita Tolulope Olugbemi

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The hope of the eventual transformation of the Economic Community of West African States into a cohesive regional union, accentuated with the dynamism and transformation of the institution from 1975 to the present, reflects evolving exigencies\. However, five decades down the line, the dream of a fully integrated West African community remains a pipe dream\. The nascent nationalism manifesting as a military uprising in the sub\-region further obfuscates attempts at characterising the milieu within globalisation and regionalism contexts\. There is palpable relapse in the auspicious ECOWAS of Peoples initiative, with deep\-seated colonial legacies, specifically the persistent Anglophone/Francophone linguistic blocs\. The animosity it produces has not received adequate attention\. This study examines how the persistent linguistic strain continues to inhibit regional integration\. The study adopts a Descriptive Method in analyzing data from dual sources\. This study argues that the linguistic divide is deeply entrenched and will endlessly weigh down integration, because while multilingualism serves a centripetal purpose in the European Union for example, it is the reverse in the ECOWAS\. Linguistic Relativity also known as the ‘Sapir\-Whorf Hypothesis,’ was used to explain the linguistic phenomenon\. It recommends a more robust socio\-cultural and multilingual approach which emphasises globalism epitomized in democracy despite the essential economic and political asymmetries\.
