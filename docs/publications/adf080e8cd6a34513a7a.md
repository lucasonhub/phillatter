# Full\-Path Classification and Phrase\-Level Faithfulness Validation for Short Railway Fault Texts

**Autores:** Guo Rongchang; Feilong Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Short, terminology\-dense railway fault records with long\-tailed class distributions require accurate full\-path classification and inspectable prediction evidence\. We formulate full\-path single\-label multiclass classification of 1,228 texts spanning eight first\-level equipment categories and 58 mutually exclusive equipment\-category–fault\-mode classes\. A dual\-head RoBERTa\-wwm\-ext–BiGRU classifier with class\-balanced cross\-entropy \(CB\) for the primary full\-path objective serves as the evaluated neural implementation\. Evaluation covers classical and neural baselines, five seed\-labelled runs, parallel loss objectives, auxiliary\-head ablation, repeated holdouts and paired uncertainty analyses\. On the fixed split, TF\-IDF\+SVM led full\-path accuracy \(0\.851 ± 0\.000\) and TF\-IDF\+LR led Observed53 Macro\-F1 \(0\.785 ± 0\.000\); Final CB reached 0\.833 ± 0\.019 and 0\.729 ± 0\.028, respectively \(mean ± sample standard deviation\)\. Among the four loss objectives, CB achieved the highest mean Tail Macro\-F1; model rankings varied with the evaluation metric and data partition\. Full and No\-label lexicon rationales were compared with matched Random, Integrated Gradients \(IG\) and LIME under MASK and label\-neutral replacement\. Comprehensiveness, sufficiency and flip rates quantified rationale effects\. Both lexicons produced stronger faithfulness effects than matched Random, with consistent ordering across operators\. Removing label\-, taxonomy\- and rule\-derived entries retained the faithfulness signal but reduced predicted\-path coverage from 106/134 to 40/134\. IG and LIME generally achieved stronger comprehensiveness than the lexicon\. The contributions are full\-path task formulation, robustness\-oriented evaluation and controlled phrase\-level faithfulness validation\.
