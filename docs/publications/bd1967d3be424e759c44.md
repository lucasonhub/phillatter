# The impact of fatigue on lower limb neuromuscular control in female college soccer players

**Autores:** Tao Xiang; Tingzheng Zhu; Huiru Ma; Ziwen Mu; Zhiqiang Han; Kazuhiro Imai; Shaoshuai Shen; Zhiguang Guo; Xiao Zhou

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Fatigue is a key risk factor for non\-contact lower\-limb injuries in female soccer players\. This study aimed to identify the impact of fatigue on lower limb neuromuscular control in female college soccer players\. Methods Twenty female collegiate soccer players performed the Yo\-Yo Intermittent Endurance Level 2 Test for fatigue induction\. Before and after fatigue, single\-leg landing \(SLL\), double\-leg landing \(DLL\), and zigzag running were assessed using surface electromyography \(sEMG\) and force plates\. Normalized sEMG amplitude \(%PEAK\), peak vertical ground reaction force \(vGRF, BW\), stabilization time, and center of pressure \(COP\) displacement area were analyzed\. Twenty participants performed the Yo\-Yo Intermittent Endurance Level 2 Test for fatigue induction\. Before and after the test, single\-leg landing \(SLL\), double\-leg landing \(DLL\), zigzag running, surface electromyography, force plates, and center of pressure analysis were used to assess neuromuscular and kinetic responses\. Fatigue altered sEMG amplitude in a task\-specific manner\. Results In DLL, normalized sEMG amplitude decreased significantly in the right vastus lateralis \( P = 0\.002; FDR\-adjusted P = 0\.024\), left vastus lateralis \( P = 0\.006; FDR\-adjusted P = 0\.036\), and right vastus medialis \( P = 0\.010; FDR\-adjusted P = 0\.040\)\. In zigzag running, a pre\- fatigue bilateral rectus femoris asymmetry \( P = 0\.037; exploratory, FDR\- adjusted P &gt; 0\.05\) was no longer significant post\-fatigue \( P = 0\.112\)\. In SLL, peak vGRF \(BW\) decreased bilaterally \(left: 2\.52 ± 0\.31–2\.34 ± 0\.27 BW, P = 0\.015; FDR\-adjusted P = 0\.045; right: 2\.69 ± 0\.36\-2\.40 ± 0\.30 BW, P &lt; 0\.001; FDR\-adjusted P &lt; 0\.003\)\. Discussion These findings provide insights into fatigue\-induced neuromuscular changes in female collegiate soccer players, with the DLL vastus muscles&\#x27; responses surviving FDR correction, while other findings remain exploratory and require confirmation\. The results offer preliminary theoretical support for targeted training and injury prevention strategies, though the fixed testing sequence and potential recovery effects limit strong causal conclusions\.
