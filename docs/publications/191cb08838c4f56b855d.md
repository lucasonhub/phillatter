# Growth, Methodological Evolution, and Clinical Maturity of Artificial Intelligence Research in Major Medical Journals, 2018–2025: A Longitudinal Bibliometric Study

**Autores:** Abdulraoof Alsaeedi; Norah AlQarni; Ibrahim Rozi; Mohamed Ibrahim

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objectives: To quantify changes in the volume, methods, and clinical maturity of artificial intelligence \(AI\) and machine learning \(ML\) research in major medical journals from 2018 through 2025\.Methods: We conducted a longitudinal bibliometric study of PubMed\-indexed records from 99 leading clinical and general medical journals\. The search was conducted on July 2026\. Records were deduplicated and classified as empirical AI/ML research, evidence synthesis, AI discourse, or unclassified\. Empirical studies were categorized by model family and clinical maturity\. Journal\-year Poisson generalized estimating equations used total substantive publications as an offset and clustered observations by journal\.Results: Among 6,891 unique publications, 4,205 \(61%\) were empirical AI/ML studies\. Empirical articles increased from 213 in 2018 to 903 in 2025\. The adjusted publication rate rose by 20\.5% annually \(incidence rate ratio, 1\.205; 95% confidence interval, 1\.166–1\.245; p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Blinded validation yielded 92\.7% agreement \(Cohen&\#x27;s kappa, 0\.854\)\. Traditional ML remained dominant, while transformer, large language model, and generative AI studies rose sharply after 2022\. Overall, 81\.9% of empirical studies were retrospective development studies, and 17\.4% reached external validation, prospective evaluation, or real\-world deployment\.Conclusion: The AI/ML research became a larger component of major\-journal output, but clinical translation lagged behind publication growth\. Progress should be assessed through external validation, prospective evaluation, real\-world effectiveness, equity, and transparent reporting\.
