# Evaluating procollagen type III N\-terminal propeptide \(P3NP\) as a biomarker of sarcopenia in women with type 2 diabetes: a cross\-sectional study\.

**Autores:** Thyago Bruno de Lira Dos Santos; Giselle Rabelo Maciel; Francisco Alfredo Farias Bandeira

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OBJECTIVE: To determine whether circulating procollagen type III N\-terminal propeptide \(P3NP\) improves the diagnosis of dual\-energy X\-ray absorptiometry \(DXA\)\-defined sarcopenia in women with type 2 diabetes mellitus \(T2DM\)\. SUBJECTS AND METHODS: This cross\-sectional study enrolled 99 women with T2DM who underwent standardized clinical assessment, handgrip strength testing, and whole\-body DXA\. Sarcopenia was defined using a DXA\-derived fat\-free mass index\. Multivariable logistic regression models included age, body mass index, glycated hemoglobin \(HbA1c\), and estimated glomerular filtration rate, with and without log\-transformed, z\-standardized P3NP\. Discriminatory performance was quantified by the area under the curve \(AUC\) with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals; sensitivity analyses excluded participants with an estimated glomerular filtration rate below 60\. RESULTS: P3NP showed weak bivariate correlations with age \(p = \-0\.210\) and the Short Physical Performance Battery \(p = 0\.225\)\. When incorporated into the clinical model, P3NP did not yield a clinically significant improvement \(ΔAUC = 0\.007\)\. As a sole predictor, P3NP had poor discrimination \(AUC 0\.53\) and was not independently associated with DXA\-defined sarcopenia \(odds ratio per 1\-standard deviation, p &gt; 0\.05\)\. Results were consistent after excluding participants with reduced kidney function and across interaction analyses; model calibration was adequate\. P3NP absolute values were elevated, with 80\.8% above the high threshold \(&gt;31 μg/L\), and clinical/functional profiles were broadly similar P3NP strata\. CONCLUSION: In women with T2DM, circulating P3NP was not an independent discriminator of DXA\-defined sarcopenia and provided no clinically meaningful advantage over standard clinical predictors\. Routine screening should continue to rely on assessments of muscle strength, function, and DXA\-based body composition, while P3NP may be considered an adjunct marker of extracellular matrix remodeling\.
