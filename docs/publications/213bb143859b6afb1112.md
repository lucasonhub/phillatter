# Toponymic knowledge infrastructures in five\-state Middle Asia: linguistic transformation, manuscript cartography, and historical\-geographical reconstruction

**Autores:** Bibaisha Iliasovna Nurdauletova; Sholpan Saparbaikyzy; Ж\.О\. Артыкбаев; Tursynkhan Zakonuly Kairken; Saltanat Spatayeva; Elizabeth Shilunga

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Toponymic records embedded in Arabic, Persian, and Chinese manuscript traditions provide evidence for reconstructing the historical geographies of Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan\. This review uses a structured narrative and conceptual\-synthesis methodology to compare place\-name transmission across languages, scripts, manuscript genres, and political regimes\. A diachronic framework separates pre\-Islamic external attestations, medieval Arabic\-Persian documentation, early\-modern cartography, imperial and Soviet standardisation, and post\-Soviet renaming rather than treating linguistic layers as synchronous\. Direct evidence from the five\-state core is distinguished from supporting evidence from neighbouring transmission corridors and comparative regions\. Micro\-cases of Koneurgenish/Gurganj, Dargan/Dargan\-Ata, hydronymic compounds, and political renaming are combined with explicit cross\-script harmonisation rules and spatial\-chronological triangulation\. Toponymy is operationalised as a sociopolitical knowledge infrastructure when names function as repeatable identifiers linking routes, jurisdictions, administrative records, gazetteers, and maps\. Digital approaches extend this framework through georeferenced historical gazetteers, variant\-name alignment, route modelling, and confidence\-ranked reconstruction\. Reliable interpretation depends on preserving source provenance, separating transliteration from phonological reconstruction, and distinguishing linguistic change from administrative replacement\.
