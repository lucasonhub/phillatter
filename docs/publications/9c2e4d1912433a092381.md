# Infectious burden and cellular immune impairment in higher\-risk myelodysplastic syndromes with biallelic TP53 alterations

**Autores:** Binbin Lai; Xiao Yan; Lieguang Chen; Yi Wang; Lixia Sheng; Guifang Ouyang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Higher\-risk myelodysplastic syndromes \(MDS\) are frequently complicated by severe infections, yet the impact of molecular subgroups on infection susceptibility remains poorly defined\. Biallelic TP53 \(bi\- TP53 \) alterations identify a high\-risk molecular subset linked to adverse outcomes, but their contribution to infection incidence and infection\-related mortality has not been systematically studied\. Methods We retrospectively analyzed 97 hospitalized higher\-risk MDS patients \(IPSS\-R ≥4\) identified through hospitalizations between June 2023 and April 2026\. Patients were grouped by TP53 allelic status, and infection\-related outcomes were compared\. Clinically significant infections were defined as suspected or documented events requiring inpatient management and/or intravenous anti\-infective therapy\. Outcomes included infection incidence, recurrent infection, anti\-infective escalation, baseline lymphocyte subsets, overall survival, and composite event\-free survival \(cEFS; infection\-related death or AML transformation\)\. Cox regression evaluated cEFS, and Firth penalized logistic regression assessed infection\-related death\. IPSS\-M risk category was also analyzed\. Results Among 97 patients, 31 had bi\- TP53 alterations\. During a median follow\-up of 0\.8 years, 59 patients \(60\.8%\) developed clinically significant infections and 27 \(27\.8%\) died from infection\. Compared with non\-bi\- TP53 patients, bi\- TP53 patients had higher rates of infection \(87\.1% vs\. 48\.5%\), recurrent infection \(54\.8% vs\. 30\.3%\), infection within 100 days after diagnosis \(77\.4% vs\. 28\.8%\), and infection within 1 year after diagnosis \(83\.9% vs\. 39\.4%; all P&lt;0\.05\)\. Among infected patients, bi\- TP53 alterations were associated with more severe infection, greater anti\-infective treatment escalation \(85\.2% vs\. 31\.3%\), more frequent antifungal therapy use \(74\.1% vs\. 15\.6%\), and higher infection\-related mortality \(85\.2% vs\. 12\.5%; all P&lt;0\.001\)\. Baseline neutrophil, hemoglobin, and platelet counts were comparable between groups, whereas CD3\+ T\-cell, CD4\+ T\-cell, CD8\+ T\-cell, and NK\-cell counts were significantly lower in bi\- TP53 patients\. In multivariable analysis, bi\- TP53 alterations remained associated with shorter cEFS \(HR = 7\.56, 95% CI: 2\.79–20\.46\) and infection\-related death among infected patients \(OR = 7\.52, 95% CI: 1\.15–49\.37\)\. Conclusions Bi\- TP53 alterations were associated with greater infectious burden, lower circulating T\-cell and NK\-cell counts, and higher infection\-related mortality in hospitalized patients with higher\-risk MDS\. These observational findings do not establish causality or independence from neutropenia\. Prospective multicenter studies should assess their value for infection risk stratification and supportive\-care planning\.
