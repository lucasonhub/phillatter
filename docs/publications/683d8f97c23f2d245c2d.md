# TA\-TMA as a clinical model for precision complement therapeutics in stem cell transplantation

**Autores:** Sonata Jodele

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Transplant\-associated thrombotic microangiopathy \(TA\-TMA\) has evolved from a poorly understood and frequently fatal complication of hematopoietic stem cell transplantation \(HSCT\) into the leading clinical model for precision complement therapeutics\. Studies of TA\-TMA have established endothelial injury and complement dysregulation as clinically actionable mechanisms and demonstrated that successful complement inhibition requires more than selecting an effective therapeutic agent\. Longitudinal pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic \(PK/PD\)\-guided dosing, durable terminal complement blockade, and integration of biomarkers reflecting complement activation, endothelial injury, inflammation, and organ dysfunction have transformed eculizumab into the first example of individualized complement\-directed therapy in transplantation\. Beyond improving outcomes in patients with TA\-TMA, these advances have fundamentally changed our understanding of post\-transplant endothelial injury, revealing shared biological pathways across multiple transplant\-associated complications and supporting a transition from syndrome\-based to mechanism\-based clinical management\. The expanding availability of eculizumab biosimilars and complement pathway\-specific inhibitors, including lectin pathway inhibition, further broadens opportunities for personalized therapeutic strategies while emphasizing the need for harmonized diagnostic criteria, standardized response assessment, and biomarker\-guided patient selection\. Together, the lessons learned from TA\-TMA provide a framework for future precision complement therapeutics in transplantation and illustrate how mechanistic understanding of disease biology can transform clinical practice\.
