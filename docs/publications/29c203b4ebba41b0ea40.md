# Low\-performing students and growing educational inequalities in Finland

**Autores:** Jonna Pulkkinen; Heli Kauppinen; Arto Kalevi Ahonen

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Finland is known worldwide for its good learning outcomes and success in reducing educational inequalities\. In this study, we examined trends in educational equality from the perspective of low\-performing students in Finland using the Programme for International Student Assessment \(PISA\) data from 2003 to 2022\. We studied changes in the share of low\-performing students in mathematics by gender, socioeconomic status \(SES\), immigrant background, and the community location of schools using descriptive statistics\. In addition, we used logistic regression analysis to study the factors that are associated with low performance in 2012 and 2022\. The results showed that the share of low\-performing students increased across all groups\. Since 2012, the share of low\-performing students between girls and boys has begun to diverge\. The results also showed a strong difference in performance according to SES, with a marked decline in performance among students with low SES\. However, the gap between native and immigrant students narrowed from 2012 to 2022, which is explained by the fact that the share of low\-performing native students increased\. In 2022, for the first time, there was also a difference between schools located in cities and schools located in rural areas\. Overall, the examination of trends in the share of low\-performing students in different groups indicates growing educational inequalities in Finland\. The increase in the share of low\-performing students also shows that the compulsory education system is no longer able to level off the performance gap between students, as before\.
