# Vessel\-associated microglia contribute to blood\-brain barrier disruption via a Notch\-IL\-1β axis in the diabetic brain

**Autores:** Da\-Hye Lee; Min\-Joo Jung; Han\-Seok Jeong; Seung\-Hoon Kim; Gwanghun Kim; Hyun Mu Shin; Chung‐Hyun Cho

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Diabetes mellitus \(DM\) is associated with central nervous system \(CNS\) complications, including cognitive impairment and an increased risk of neurodegenerative diseases\. However, the cellular mechanisms underlying blood\-brain barrier \(BBB\) disruption in the diabetic brain remain poorly understood\. In this study, we investigated the contribution of microglia to diabetes\-induced BBB dysfunction\. Using a streptozotocin \(STZ\)\-induced mouse model of type 1 diabetes, we observed increased BBB permeability and an accumulation of vessel\-associated microglia\. Transcriptomic analysis of isolated microglia revealed upregulation of genes associated with migration, angiogenesis, and vascular permeability, alongside activation of Notch signaling\. We found that endothelial cell \(EC\)\-derived CCL20, upregulated by TNF\-α, promotes microglial migration via AKT signaling\. Furthermore, direct interaction with ECs activates Notch signaling in microglia, inducing IL\-1β expression and disrupting EC tight junctions\. Treatment with minocycline attenuated microglial activation, restored endothelial Tjp1 expression, and reduced vascular leakage\. These findings identify a novel microglia\-EC signaling axis that contributes to BBB impairment in diabetes and suggest that targeting microglial activation may offer therapeutic potential for protecting neurovascular integrity in diabetic neuropathology\.
