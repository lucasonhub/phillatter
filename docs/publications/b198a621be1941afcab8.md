# From bodily signals to safe action: an interoceptive self\-regulation loop for whole\-person health

**Autores:** Haojie Dai; Min Wang; Xiaohong Gu

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Health systems increasingly measure physiological and behavioral outputs, yet the transition from a bodily signal to a safe, feasible decision remains difficult to specify\. We propose the Interoceptive Self\-Regulation Loop \(ISRL\), a provisional framework linking context and exposure, bodily state, interoceptive sampling, appraisal, action, and feedback\. Four measurement strata distinguish contextual inputs, functional changes, subjective signals, and clinical indicators\. A safety gate operates throughout the loop through condition\-specific rules, named clinical responsibility, and documented escalation and follow\-up\. Its purpose is to support decisions without transferring diagnosis or risk assessment to individuals\. The proposed contribution is an explicit, testable account of cross\-stratum discordance and evidence\-responsive appraisal, rather than the invention of interoception, triage, or behavior\-change support\. We specify independent candidate measures of appraisal flexibility and action proportionality, illustrate the safety gate using recent\-onset chest pain, and compare ISRL with a simpler combination of the common\-sense model, COM\-B, and identical triage guidance\. Six propositions distinguish supporting predictions from the more discriminating tests of discordance, flexibility, and timely care\. A bounded initial research program concerns adults in primary care, daily observations, and episode\-level decisions, with prospective validation before interventional use\. Repeated failure to improve prediction or decisions over the active comparator, despite adequate measurement and precision, would count against retaining ISRL as a distinct framework in that setting\. Clinical safety, monitoring burden, cultural adaptation, and unequal access are explicit evaluation outcomes\. ISRL remains a theory for investigation, not a validated diagnostic tool or treatment\.
