# Fujie, Naoto: Is a Truth That Is Too Slow Still Operationally True? Revision Latency, Real Effects, and the Limits of Wolfendalean Rationality in a High\-Speed Credit\-Scoring Society

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper asks whether a belief, model, or institutional judgment that is eventually corrected should still count as operationally adequate when the correction arrives only after its effects have already reorganized the world\. The central problem is revision latency: the temporal gap between the generation of an epistemic error and the successful revision of the commitments, classifications, or inferential structures that produced it\. Drawing on Wolfendalean rationality as a demanding model of revisable commitment and reason\-responsive self\-correction, the paper asks whether rationality remains operationally sufficient when environmental, institutional, or algorithmic reconfiguration proceeds faster than the system’s capacity for correction\. High\-speed credit\-scoring systems provide the principal case\. A score may later be shown to be inaccurate or unjustified while its earlier deployment has already altered access to credit, housing, employment, insurance, reputation, or subsequent data\. The paper therefore distinguishes eventual epistemic correction from timely operational correction and analyzes how false or weakly justified classifications can produce real effects that feed back into later scoring conditions\. The broader claim is that truth\-tracking and rational revisability must be evaluated temporally: a system whose corrections systematically arrive after its classifications have irreversibly changed downstream possibilities may remain epistemically revisable while becoming operationally inadequate\. \(direct link \)
