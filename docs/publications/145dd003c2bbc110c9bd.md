# Thermal\-inertia Aware Load Balancing with Energy\-memory in Distributed Cloud Systems

**Autores:** Zryan Najat Rashid

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Distributed cloud systems now are used to run data\-intensive, IoT, and real\-time applications, yet the rising computational load presents significant energy and thermal\-management challenges\.It is important to note that existing load\-balancing techniques are based on the instantaneous utilization or power measured and ignore recent history of energy consumption, and delayed behavior of heating and cooling,\.In this work, a lightweight online scheduling framework, Energy\-Memory and Thermal\-Inertia Aware Load Balancing \(EMTI\-LB\), is proposed to include energy use history, first\-order thermal modeling, and energy estimation of tasks, while also accounting for thermal risk and scheduling delay in a single function\.For every incoming task, EMTI\-LB computes the cost of all candidate nodes and selects the node with the lowest predicted cost to which to assign the task\.The O\(N\) scheduling complexity per task is for the method where N is the number of candidate nodes\.The evaluation of EMTI\-LB has been done in CloudSim 5\.0 with 10 different heterogeneous VMs and 500 dynamically\-arrived cloudlets\.Each experiment was run 30 times with EMTI\-LB compared to Random, Round\-Robin and conventional Energy\-Aware scheduling\.EMTI\-LB consumed 125 kWh, achieving energy reductions of approximately 7\.4% compared to Energy\-Aware scheduling \(135 kWh\), 13\.8% compared to Round\-Robin \(145 kWh\), and 21\.9% compared to Random scheduling \(160 kWh\)\.It also lowered thermal violations from 4\.5 \(Energy\-Aware\), 6\.0 \(Round\-Robin\), and 8\.0 \(Random\) to 2\.0 per 100 tasks \(reductions of 55\.6%, 66\.7%, and 75\.0%, respectively\), and reduced SLA violations from 10\.1% \(Random\), 7\.5% \(Round\-Robin\), and 5\.0% \(Energy\-Aware\) to 3\.1%, representing reductions of 69\.3%, 58\.7%, and 38\.0%, respectively\.The results of the ablation showed that the energy needs were higher for both with the removal of thermal inertia \(132 kWh\) and energy memory \(137 kWh\)\.The results show that using energy memory with thermal inertia can enhance energy efficiency, maintain thermal stability and ensure the quality of the service, without requiring the usual training and computationally intensive optimization\.
