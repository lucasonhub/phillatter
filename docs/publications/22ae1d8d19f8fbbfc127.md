# Predictive Modeling of Urban Heat Stress Using Machine Learning: A 2050 UTCI\-Based Assessment

**Autores:** Ninitha Arumugakani; Madhumathi Anbu; Hourakhsh Ahmad Nia; Ranjitharamasamy Sudhakara Pandian

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Urban heat islands and increasingly severe thermal stress pose critical challenges to sustainable urban development, yet physics\-based simulation tools remain computationally expensive and ill\-suited to early\-stage climate\-responsive design\. This study applies machine learning to predict outdoor air and surface temperature using 20 years \(2005\-2025\) of daily maximum temperature data ranging from 15°C to 40°C\. Four algorithms were evaluated: Linear Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting and Extreme Gradient Boosting \(XG Boost\)\. Gradient Boosting achieved the best performance with RMSE = 1\.869, MAE = 1\.405, and R² = 0\.708, indicating robust predictive ability across diurnal temperature and seasonal patterns\. 2050 projections, based on direct extrapolation of the trained model&\#x27;s seasonal temperature pattern, show a shift toward moderate\-to\-strong Universal Thermal Climate Index \(UTCI\) heat stress conditions across the majority of the year, underscoring the need for climate\-responsive urban design even under conservative, non\-scenario\-scaled projections\. The results strengthen the acceptance of data\-driven ML frameworks for the application in urban planning and climate\-responsive architectural design practice\.
