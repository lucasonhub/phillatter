# Stepwise multimodal management of acquired post\-intubation subglottic stenosis: a proposed treatment algorithm

**Autores:** Kul Bhushan Bali; Deepthy Das; Nidhi Mohan; Vishwanatha B; Sindhuja Mudiyala

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Acquired post\-intubation subglottic stenosis \(SGS\) requires tailored interventions\.Aim and Objectives: To evaluate clinical presentations, management strategies, and outcomes of an individualized, stepwise multimodal surgical approach while proposing a practical treatment algorithm for affected patients\.Materials and Methods: This retrospective single\-centre case series included 10 consecutive patients with acquired post\-intubation SGS treated between January 2022\-December 2025, with last follow\-up till June 2026\. Clinical, endoscopic, radiological and outcome data were analysed descriptively\. Management was individualized according to stenosis severity, length, scar characteristics, cartilage involvement, airway stability and previous interventions\. Endoscopic treatment included coblation\-assisted scar excision, neodymium\-doped yttrium aluminium garnet \(Nd:YAG\) laser as an adjunct, balloon dilatation, selected intralesional corticosteroid and mitomycin\-C application, and temporary stenting\. Open reconstruction was reserved for advanced or recurrent disease\.Results: The mean age was 30\.3 years \(range 2 years 7 months–45 years\), and 6 patients were male\. Grade III stenosis accounted for 60% of cases\. Endoscopic management with different modalities alone was sufficient in 7 patients \(70%\), while 3 \(30%\) required open reconstruction with anterior cricoid split and cartilage grafting\. Restenosis occurred in 3 patients \(30%\), and successful decannulation was achieved in 9 of 10 patients \(90%\)\. No major postoperative complications or perioperative deaths occurred during a mean follow\-up of 12 months \(range 6–18 months\)\.Conclusion: An individualized stepwise multimodal strategy combining complementary endoscopic techniques with timely open reconstruction was associated with favorable airway outcomes in this series\. Treatment should be guided by airway anatomy, stenosis characteristics, previous interventions, and response to treatment rather than stenosis grade alone\. The proposed algorithm provides an experience\-based framework requiring validation in larger prospective multicenter studies\.
