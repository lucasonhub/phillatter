# Digital educational games in veterinary education: a scoping review with comparison to human medical education

**Autores:** Ingrid Toftaker; Karin Berggren; Lena‐Mari Tamminen

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Inroduction Game\-based learning, including approaches such as serious games and gamification, is used to enhance engagement and learning in education\. While its use in medical education has been reviewed, its application in veterinary education remains underexplored\. This scoping review summarizes the use of digital game\-based learning in veterinary medicine\. Methodology Searches of Web of Science, Medline, and Scopus were conducted, following the Population/Concept/Context framework and PRISMA\-ScR guidelines\. Thirteen veterinary education publications were identified and compared to 177 human medical education articles\. Results In both fields, games were mainly used in clinical sciences, with common types including simulations and case\-based learning covering diverse topics\. Ten veterinary studies included evaluations, with six assessing learning outcomes, and eight examining affective outcomes such as motivation and confidence\. Cognitive findings were mixed, while some studies reported positive effects on learning outcomes, others found no significant differences, and one study reported lower scores in part of the assessment for the intervention group\. Control groups that did not interact with the game were included in only two of the veterinary studies and only one used a systematic allocation procedure\. Access to games was often poorly documented\. Discussion Overall, the included studies suggest that game\-based learning is generally well\-received and may enhance learning, although one study reported low uptake of the game activity\. However, further research is needed to determine the conditions that optimize its effectiveness\. Systematic review registration https://osf\.io/cnpk5/overview \.
