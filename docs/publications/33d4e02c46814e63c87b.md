# Hypervolume rankings decouple from unbiased predictive performance in multi\-objective wavelength selection for NIR spectroscopy of Wolffia globosa

**Autores:** Nonpravit Chianrungsaeng; Somboon Sukpancharoen

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Multi\-objective metaheuristics are widely used to select informative wavelengths in near\-infrared \(NIR\) spectroscopy, and their quality is conventionally assessed with Pareto\-based indicators such as hypervolume\. Such indicators assume deterministic objectives, yet one objective here, the cross\-validated prediction error, is an estimator carrying non\-negligible variance, and the consequences of treating it as fixed remain largely unquantified\. We benchmark four binary multi\-objective metaheuristics \(NSGA\-II, MO\-BPSO, MO\-BDE, MO\-bGWO\) and the CARS selector on a 50\-sample, 474\-wavelength NIR protein calibration problem in Wolffia globosa, a small\-sample regime where bias is most severe, under matched budgets, a shared selection operator and an identical fitness function, with the cardinality prior controlled separately\. Under conventional flat cross\-validation the algorithms separate decisively \(Friedman chi\-square = 52\.75, p = 2\.1e\-11\) and hypervolume ranks NSGA\-II first\. Under nested cross\-validation the same comparison is not significant \(chi\-square = 5\.85, p = 0\.119\) and the rank order changes\. Three controlled experiments identify the mechanism\. A budget sweep shows optimistic bias rising monotonically with search effort \(21\.9% to 40\.7% from 270 to 3030 evaluations\) while honest error does not improve\. A label\-permutation null shows the strongest searcher recovering apparent signal from destroyed labels \(RMSECV 7\.96 against a no\-skill value of 8\.69\), whereas weaker searchers do not\. A landscape analysis finds 386 solutions within 10% of the optimum with median pairwise Jaccard 0\.056, including disjoint pairs of equivalent fitness\. This degeneracy is an engineering asset rather than a defect, since many channel sets reach the same accuracy and the filter set of a low\-cost instrument can be chosen on cost or manufacturability grounds\. Hypervolume computed on estimated objectives therefore separates algorithms along a dimension uncorrelated with unbiased performance, search intensity converts into selection bias, and the low selection stability routinely reported reflects structural non\-identifiability rather than algorithmic deficiency\. A minimum reporting protocol is proposed\.
