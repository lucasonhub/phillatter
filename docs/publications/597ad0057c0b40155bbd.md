# Detection of old\-growth forests using airborne laser scanning and other auxiliary data combined with National Forest Inventory field plot data

**Autores:** Janne Toivonen; Parvez Rana

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate detection and mapping of old\-growth forests \(OGFs\) are essential for biodiversity conservation and forest management, yet remain challenging due to their structural complexity, rarity and varying definitions\. In this study, we developed a framework for classifying OGFs in boreal forests by integrating airborne laser scanning \(ALS\) data, and multispectral Sentinel\-2 imagery and aerial imagery with different modelling approaches\. OGF classes were defined using Finnish National Forest Inventory data \(26,411 plots\) based on structural naturalness, deadwood continuity and human impact, resulting in three alternative definitions of increasing strictness\. We evaluated the performance of gradient boosting \(XGBoost\), random forest and logistic regression models, and assessed the effects of class imbalance using SMOTE\-based oversampling and class weighting\. Model performance varied across OGF definitions, with the highest accuracy \(F1\-score of 0\.65\) achieved for the least restrictive definition and decreasing performance for stricter definitions\. XGBoost consistently outperformed other modelling approaches, while class balancing or class weighting did not improve classification performance\. Stand age emerged as the most important variable, complemented by structural metrics from ALS and red\-edge \-based spectral indices from Sentinel\-2 data\. Spatial patterns indicated higher detection success in northern regions of Finland, reflecting underlying gradients in forest structure and management history\. Our results demonstrate that multi\-source remote sensing combined with machine learning provides a robust approach for large\-scale mapping of structurally mature forests\. However, the detection of more strictly defined OGFs remains limited by the ability of remote sensing data to capture ecological attributes related to disturbance history and continuity\. This is the first large\-scale study combining ALS, Sentinel\-2, aerial image, other auxiliary variables and NFI field data to model OGFs\. These findings highlight the importance of aligning OGF definitions with observable forest characteristics and integrating complementary data sources to improve the mapping of ecologically complex forests\.
