# Yari, Masoud: Romantic Love as an Evolutionary Bug in Human Consciousness

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-19 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper argues that the modern category of romantic love characteristically attenuates affective self\-authorship, making a subject’s affective life appear to originate elsewhere—in the beloved, in fate, or in love itself\. The underlying states are real; culture organizes their classification and the attribution of their authorship\. Building on James Averill’s syndrome account of emotion, I develop a feature his framework identifies but does not adequately explain: romantic love’s distinctive non\-agentive construal\. I distinguish unchosen onset, limited control, and external elicitation from the further claim that an affective state is authored beyond the subject\. Drawing on receptive self\-description, practical helplessness, asymmetries in explanatory demand, and the selective social uptake of non\-agentive claims, I use Wegner’s exclusivity condition as a candidate mechanism to argue that the romantic paradigm does more than acknowledge involuntariness\. It makes alternative loci of authorship culturally available and discounts the subject’s own contribution\. I describe this pattern as an “evolutionary bug in human consciousness”: not a defect in evolved systems of attraction, attachment, and reward, but a failure of self\-understanding in which the subject’s involvement in their affective life becomes obscured\. This framing may help sustain the attachment it renders intelligible, creating a possible feedback loop between cultural interpretation and lived experience\. External elicitation and limited control do not, by themselves, warrant displaced authorship\. The paper concludes with testable predictions concerning authorship attribution, explanatory demand, and normative mitigation\.
