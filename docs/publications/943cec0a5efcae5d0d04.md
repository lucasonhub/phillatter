# Prognostic prediction model construction and validation based on regional cerebral oxygen saturation and bispectral index monitoring in pediatric traumatic brain injury

**Autores:** Baowen Li; Ruhai Ma; Zhuyun Tian; Youcang Wan; Xiaoli Zhong; Wenhai Wang; Weixiao Yan; Shengkui Zhao

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Continuous multimodal cerebral monitoring may improve detection and management of secondary injury after pediatric moderate\-to\-severe traumatic brain injury \(msTBI\)\. We developed and validated a prognostic model for 6\-month functional outcome integrating regional cerebral oxygen saturation \(rScO₂\), bispectral index \(BIS\), clinical and biomarker data\. Methods Prospective cohort of 93 children with msTBI monitored with rScO₂ and BIS \(monitoring group\) and a retrospective control cohort of 66 children without such monitoring\. Primary outcome was Glasgow Outcome Scale \(GOS\) at 6 months\. Multivariate logistic regression identified independent predictors; a nomogram was constructed and internally validated using receiver operating characteristic \(ROC\), calibration curves, and decision curve analysis \(DCA\)\. Results Total N = 159\. Monitoring\-group patients had shorter hospital and pediatric intensive care unit \(PICU\) lengths of stay and shorter invasive ventilation duration than controls \( p &lt; 0\.05\)\. Multivariate analysis identified admission Glasgow Coma Scale \(GCS\) \[odds ratio \(OR\) 1\.939, 95% confidence interval \(CI\): 1\.261 \~ 2\.984, p = 0\.003\], mean rScO₂ \(OR 1\.121, 95%CI: 1\.027 \~ 1\.225, p = 0\.011\), mean BIS \(OR 1\.115, 95%CI: 1\.041 \~ 1\.193, p = 0\.002\) and neuron\-specific enolase \(NSE\) \(OR 0\.843, 95%CI: 0\.769 \~ 0\.924, p &lt; 0\.001\) as independent outcome\-associated variables\. The nomogram achieved AUC 0\.91 \(95%CI: 0\.84–0\.97\), with favorable calibration and net clinical benefit on DCA\. Conclusion A prognostic model combining admission GCS, mean rScO₂, mean BIS and NSE provides accurate individual risk estimation for pediatric msTBI and may assist bedside risk stratification and management decisions\.
