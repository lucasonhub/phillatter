# Novel assay for accelerated anaerobic phenotypic susceptibility testing for Clostridioides difficile

**Autores:** Meriem Boukili; Omar Zmerli; Sara Bellali; Saidi Daoud; Rita Abou Abdallah; Florence Fenollar; Jacques  Yaacoub Bou Khalil

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Clostridioides difficile infection \(CDI\) remains one of the leading healthcare\-associated infections\. CDI management is increasingly complicated due to rising antimicrobial resistance, variability in treatment response, and the lack of standardized antimicrobial susceptibility testing \(AST\) in routine practice\. The current reference method, agar dilution, is laborious and time\-consuming, leaving therapy largely empirical\. Methods We present the development and proof\-of\-concept for a novel accelerated AST assay for C\. difficile using tabletop scanning electron microscopy \(SEM\)\. Following confirmation of C\. difficile growth in liquid medium, a broth microdilution\-based AST protocol was established and evaluated using SEM imaging at early time points\. Susceptibility to fidaxomicin and vancomycin was assessed for eight isolates with different toxigenic profiles\. Results Analysis of SEM images enabled the detection and quantification of antibiotic effects, including bacterial growth inhibition, reduced bacterial viability, and pronounced morphological alteration as early as three hours following exposure\. This facilitated the determination of SEM\-based minimum inhibitory concentration \(SEM\-MIC\) ranges, which concorded with MICs determined using standard agar dilution\. Conclusion The SEM\-AST assay achieved a substantial reduction in turnaround time from 48 hours to approximately 7 hours, without compromising accuracy\. These results demonstrate that combining optimized culture conditions with SEM imaging at early time points allows for rapid and reliable MIC estimation, establishing the first SEM\-based AST approach for strict anaerobes and providing promising potential for future CDI diagnostics\.
