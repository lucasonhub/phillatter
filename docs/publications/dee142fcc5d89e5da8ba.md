# Use of NSAIDs in orthopedic pain management: a Delphi consensus promoted by the Italian Society of Orthopedics and Traumatology \(SIOT\)

**Autores:** Paolo Tranquilli Leali; Alberto Corrado Di Martino; Michele Mercurio; Maria Silvia Spinelli; Flaminia Coluzzi; Francesca Gargano; Lisa Berti; Fabrizio Rivera; Paolo Grossi; Giuseppe Solarino; Alessandro Urbani; Francesco Scaglione; Pietro Simone Randelli

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Non\-steroidal anti\-inflammatory drugs \(NSAIDs\) are a cornerstone of pain management in orthopedics, yet significant variability persists in prescribing practices and adherence to guidelines\. Methods A two\-round modified Delphi consensus was conducted under the coordination of a methodologist, involving a scientific board of seven members and a broader expert panel of orthopedic surgeons, anesthesiologists, and pharmacologists affiliated with the Italian Society of Orthopedics and Traumatology \(SIOT\)\. Twenty statements spanning four domains\-NSAID mechanisms of action, perioperative pain management in elective surgery, traumatic pain, and non\-oncological/oncological pain\-were submitted for evaluation using a six\-point Likert scale; consensus was defined as ≥75% of ratings concentrating within a single tertile\. Results Of the 20 statements, 12 reached consensus in the first round; six revised statements reached consensus in the second round; and two were eliminated because of persistent opinion dispersion\. Final recommendations endorse short\-cycle NSAID therapy \(7–10 days\) within multimodal analgesia, support the use of symptomatic slow\-acting drugs for osteoarthritis to reduce NSAID consumption in osteoarthritis, recommend NSAIDs perioperatively in frail fracture patients to limit opioid use and delirium, identify ketoprofen lysine salt as having a favorable risk\-benefit profile for postdischarge pain, and emphasize individualized NSAID selection based on cardiovascular, renal, and antiplatelet risk factors\. Discussion This SIOT consensus provides evidence\-informed, clinically actionable recommendations for NSAID use across the principal orthopedic pain scenarios, aiming to improve prescribing appropriateness and patient safety in routine practice\.
