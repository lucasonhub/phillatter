# Detection and Analysis of Extended Defects in III\-Nitride Quantum Wells

**Autores:** Mahdi Khalili Hezarjaribi

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

The present research addresses the efficiency limitations in green\-emitting GaInN/GaN quantum well structures, which remain a critical bottleneck for III\-nitride\-based light emitters, known as the “green gap”\. This work combines advanced machine learning techniques with materials characterization to investigate the role of extended defects in nonradiative recombination processes\. Conventional analysis of extended defects in scanning electron microscopy \(SEM\) images has relied on manual counting or threshold\-based software like ImageJ, requiring days of work per sample and often producing inaccurate results\. This research introduces an object detection framework, trained on synthetically generated data, that automates the quantification of V\-pits in SEM images and enables systematic distinction between large regular V\-pits and small V\-pits based on detected size\. The resulting framework provides the statistical foundation for correlating specific defect populations with optical performance across large sample series\. Beyond methodological advancement, this work investigates whether the extended defects associated with small V\-pits contribute to nonradiative recombination in quantum well structures\. While large V\-pits enhance quantum efficiency by screening threading dislocations, the role of small V\-pits has remained an open question\. This work employs time\-resolved photoluminescence data to correlate detected defect densities with nonradiative carrier lifetimes across a systematic sample series grown on both sapphire and bulk GaN substrates, and uses transmission electron microscopy to identify the crystallographic structure of the underlying extended defects, revealing prismatic stacking faults as a previously unreported origin of small V\-pits\. Correlation analysis across both substrate types demonstrates that small V\-pit density and nonradiative lifetime are independent consequences of the same underlying point defect population rather than causally related, showing that small V\-pits are passive structural indicators of point defect concentration rather than active contributors to nonradiative recombination\.
