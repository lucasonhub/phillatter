# GREEN TECH PARADOX An Analysis of AI in Environment Sustainability

**Autores:** Ms\. Kirandeep Kaur; Mrs\. Harpreet Kaur

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract: The integration of artificial intelligence into environmental sustainability strategies presents a critical dilemma known as the &quot;Green Tech Paradox\.&quot; While AI offers unprecedented capabilities to optimize resource allocation, predict climate patterns, and enhance energy efficiency across industrial sectors, its deployment carries substantial, often overlooked, ecological costs\. This research provides a critical analysis of the dual role AI plays as both a catalyst for eco\-innovations and a significant contributor to environmental degradation\. On the positive frontier, AI\-driven applications accelerate sustainability by enabling smart power grids, improving carbon capture systems, facilitating precision agriculture, and modeling complex environmental crises\. Machine learning algorithms enhance operational efficiency, reducing waste and carbon footprints in manufacturing and logistics\. However, these beneficial outcomes are counterbalanced by the escalating physical footprint of AI infrastructure\. The training and continuous execution of complex neural networks demand massive amounts of electricity, heavily relying on fossil\-fuel\-dominant energy grids\. Furthermore, data centers consume millions of liters of freshwater daily for cooling mechanisms, accelerating localized water scarcity, while the rapid obsolescence of specialized hardware generates growing volumes of hazardous electronic waste\. This paper evaluates this structural tension through a multidimensional synthesis of secondary data, life\-cycle assessments, and resource consumption metrics across global data centers\. The analysis reveals that without stringent regulatory frameworks and sustainable design interventions, the environmental degradation caused by AI&\#x27;s hardware lifecycle risks surpassing its ecological benefits\. Ultimately, the study advocates for a paradigm shift toward &quot;Green AI&quot;—prioritizing algorithmic efficiency, renewable energy integration for computing infrastructure, sustainable hardware lifecycle management, and policy\-driven eco\-design standards\. By establishing transparent auditing protocols, researchers and industry leaders can mitigate the hidden ecological liabilities of artificial intelligence, ensuring that modern technological advancement aligns genuinely with global net\-zero targets and long\-term planetary boundaries\. Keywords: Green AI, Climate change mitigation, Sustainable development goals \(SDGs\), Environmental governance, Lifecycle assessment of AI\.
