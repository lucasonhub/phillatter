# Monolithic zero\-bias memristive vision device with in\-materio spectral kernels

**Autores:** Mohit Kumar; Hyunmin Dang; Hyungtak Seo

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial vision hardware can detect rich optical information, but color and pattern recognition still relies on pixel arrays, spectral filters, and external machine learning\-based computation, which limits miniaturization, increases power consumption, and prevents feature extraction directly at the sensor\. Here, we show that this bottleneck can be addressed with a monolithic memristive vision device with zero\-bias \(0 V\) photocurrent readout in which controlled electrical stressing of unipolar metal oxide memristors creates ten distinct photo\-responsive states within a single element, consistent with a proposed Ag\-assisted ionic or electrochemical modification of the oxide whose microscopic origin is not established here\. These electronically written states function as programmable spectral kernels that convert incident light into a ten\-dimensional photocurrent fingerprint, enabling multidimensional optical feature encoding and in\-materio preprocessing at the point of detection\. Using direct template matching against a calibration library recorded for each measurement configuration, the device identifies colors, resolves fruit colors, and discriminates projected optical patterns, and held\-out numeral scans are assigned correctly in 120 of 120 cases within one mask placement\. The system operates with an end\-to\-end response time of \~1 s, set by the sequential ten\-probe scan at 100 ms per channel, while the sensing transduction operates at zero bias\. Beyond optical recognition, the device also provides proof\-of\-concept detection of a pulse\-modulated transmitted\-light signal and time\-resolved reception of color\-sequenced digital, multilevel, and analogue optical packets\. These results identify electrical state writing in a memristive junction as a proof\-of\-concept mechanism for compact artificial vision hardware in which sensing and preprocessing are colocalized within one electronically programmable device\.
