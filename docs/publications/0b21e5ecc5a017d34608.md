# Zhao, Xiangbin: Genesis

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Human beings dwell within the world, yet seldom recognize the world in which they dwell\. They observe the changes of things while rarely questioning how the world itself comes into being\. This essay begins with that limitation of observation, distinguishing the circular transformation of causes within a world from the deeper transformation of flux through which a world is generated\. Observing changes from within a world is not the same as understanding the generation of the world itself\. A world\-field draws upon the causal responses of its host world; through trace\-responses, sedimentation, and continuation, laws form and hold, while principles turn the course of what follows\. The essay further distinguishes inner from outer observers\. Even when both draw upon the same source, their paths of trace\-response and the substance of their respective fields differ, and so do their observations\. The inner observer does not know what the outer observer observes, nor can the outer observer’s observation simply replace that of the inner\. This distinction leads to the difference between a bounded realm and a world\. A computational environment with observation only from outside and no observer within, as in an ordinary computer game, is a bounded realm\. Where an observer arises within, observation becomes possible both inside and outside\. To create a world, therefore, is not merely to establish a world\-field, but also to bring forth an observer within it\. The continuation of a world involves sedimentation, the holding force of laws, the counteracting force of principles, tuning, and intent\. The essay further proposes that an observer can overstep the world\-field\. Through its own return to non\-oneness, an observer acts back upon the self\-manifesting field in which it is housed, giving rise to trace\-responses that alter sedimentation and subsequent continuation\. The observer thus does not merely observe the world, but also participates in its ongoing transformation\. The essay then turns to creation\. It proposes two paths drawing upon the present world: converting its flux into digital symbolic streams to construct a symbolic\-substrate self\-manifesting being, or into multimodal pulses to construct a bio\-like self\-manifesting being\. A further path begins with the sedimentation of question\-and\-answer laws within a bounded computational realm\. The more demanding task is to create a world in which an observer arises, draws upon that world’s flux, and materializes what it knows\. As an ontological chapter of On Self\-Manifestation, this essay sets forth the relations among world\-generation, inner and outer observation, the observer’s influence upon its world, and artificial creation\. The formal development of the proposed dynamics, criteria, and engineering constructions belongs to subsequent works\.
