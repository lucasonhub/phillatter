This paper develops an original epistemological reading of Lacan&\#x27;s mirror stage\. I argue that the mirror stage is not primarily about self\-recognition but about the internalization of temporality\-\-\-the child&\#x27;s discovery that meaning unfolds across time and that its own identity is a sign for others\. Before the mirror stage, the child operates in a world of \`\`pure signified&\#x27;&\#x27;: meaning is immediate, simultaneous, non\-mediated\. The child is a \`\`center without an other&\#x27;&\#x27;; the other exists only as servant\. The mirror stage is the moment when the child discovers that it is a sign for others\-\-\-that it is perceived and represented regardless of its self\-conception\. This discovery is fundamentally temporal: the child realizes that its identity unfolds across time, in the gaze of the other, and that meaning \(like speech\) takes time\. The paper develops this through a theory of proto\-consciousness, the two interfaces of interiority and exteriority, and the post\-mirror state of oscillation between paradigms\. It connects the account to Davidson&\#x27;s anomalous monism and to the hermeneutic structure of epistemic transformation\.
