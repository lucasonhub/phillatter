# THE TURKISH ADAPTATION OF THE GENDER DISCRIMINATION INVENTORY \(GDI\): RELIABILITY, VALIDITY, AND MEASUREMENT EQUIVALENCE

**Autores:** Ayşe Hazal Dündar; Hacer Harlak

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study aims to translate the Gender Discrimination Inventory \(GDI\), which consists of two scales, originally developed by Kira et al\. \(2015\), into Turkish and assess its psychometric properties in terms of reliability and validity in a Turkish sample\. The inventory consists of the Gender Discrimination Scale \(GDS\) and the Gender Discrimination Coping Scale \(GDCS\)\. Data were collected from 673 adult women aged 20\-64 years\. The Brislin back\-translation method was used to translate the scale\. The psychometric properties of the GDI were then assessed in terms of construct validity \(exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses\), criterion\-related validity \(correlation analyses\), measurement invariance \(multigroup confirmatory factor analysis\), and reliability measures \(McDonald’s omega, Cronbach’s alpha internal consistency, and test\-retest correlation coefficients\)\. The results of exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses confirmed that the original three\-factor structure of both measurement instruments was preserved in the sample of adult Turkish women without any item omissions\. Criteria\-related validity tests demonstrated that an increase in the level of perceived gender discrimination was significantly associated with a decrease in self\-esteem; in the context of coping strategies, the internalization and conflict subscales showed a negative correlation with self\-esteem, while the resistance subscale exhibited a positive correlation\. Additionally, the results of the multi\-group confirmatory factor analysis indicated that structural and metric invariance were established for both scales with respect to age and marital status\. Concerning employment status, the analysis revealed structural, metric, scalar, and strict invariance for the GDS, and structural, metric, and scalar invariance for the GDCS\. Reliability analyses generally demonstrated that the total scale scores and the majority of the subscales exhibited satisfactory internal consistency and test\-retest reliability\. The results of the statistical analyses provided satisfactory evidence regarding the psychometric properties of the Turkish versions of the scales in the present sample of adult women\.
