# Comprehensive Analytical Study on Spatial Reasoning and Adaptation of Human Brain Versus Artificial Intelligence \- A Review Report

**Autores:** Sahasra V\.; Yukaciny S\.; Nithiyasri V\.; Santhanalakshmi V\.; Logeshwari \.; D\. P\. Sivasakti Balan; R\. J\. Thayumanaswamy

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Recent advances in computer vision, spatial computing, robotics, and embodied artificial intelligence \(AI\) have substantially improved machine capabilities in visual perception, navigation, spatial reasoning, and object manipulation\. However, performance on structured benchmarks and controlled environments does not necessarily demonstrate equivalence to human spatial intelligence in complex real\-world situations\. This review examines whether current AI systems can reproduce the breadth and flexibility of human spatial and embodied intelligence, with particular emphasis on their implications for the potential replacement of humans by AI\. The review synthesizes theoretical and empirical literature on human spatial cognition, embodied AI, vision\-language\-action models, robotics, and relevant AI capability benchmarks\. The evidence is organised around four dimensions: \(1\) biological sensorimotor coupling and proprioception, \(2\) zero\-shot spatial problem\-solving and dynamic affordance recognition, \(3\) physical common\-sense reasoning and adaptation, and \(4\) energy\-efficient and context\-sensitive spatial processing\. Existing research indicates that AI systems have achieved considerable progress in perception, navigation, planning, and manipulation, but continue to encounter challenges involving unfamiliar environments, occlusion, uncertainty, dynamic obstacles, changing object configurations, physical interaction, long\-horizon adaptation, and generalisation beyond training distributions\. Human spatial intelligence, in contrast, integrates perception, proprioception, embodied experience, contextual knowledge, physical intuition, memory, and flexible real\-time decision\-making as an interconnected system\. The reviewed evidence therefore suggests that current AI can augment and extend human spatial capabilities but cannot yet reproduce the breadth, adaptability, embodied experience, and contextual flexibility of human spatial intelligence\. Spatial ability consequently represents an important dimension of human irreplaceability and supports a future paradigm of human–AI complementarity rather than complete human substitution\.
