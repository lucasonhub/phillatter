# From deficit to possibility: Indigenous knowledge, asset\-based approaches, and the advancement of SDG 4 in rural Africa

**Autores:** Kemi Olajumoke Adu

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Efforts to advance Sustainable Development Goal 4 \(SDG 4\) in rural Africa are habitually framed through a deficit lens that foregrounds shortages of infrastructure, qualified teachers, learning materials, and finance\. These shortages are real and severe, yet the framing that organises them conceals the knowledge, relationships, cultural practices, local institutions, and social networks already present in rural communities\. This theoretical article argues for a shift from deficit\-oriented educational development towards an asset\-based perspective that treats Indigenous knowledge and community resources as productive foundations for inclusive, equitable, and relevant quality education\. Bringing Asset\-Based Community Development into dialogue with Indigenous Knowledge Systems, and using the Community Capitals Framework as an operational tier, the article theorises how rural African communities can be repositioned from sites of lack to active knowledge and resource partners\. Three documented African cases, drawn from the Eastern Cape, KwaZulu\-Natal, and Uganda, serve as analytical illustrations rather than as sources of new empirical evidence\. The article develops the Indigenous Asset\-Based Model for SDG 4, a four\-layer construct comprising epistemic recognition, asset mapping and mobilisation, relational connection and co\-production, and structural support and accountability\. The model retains community agency while refusing the romanticism that would license state retreat from rural schooling\.
