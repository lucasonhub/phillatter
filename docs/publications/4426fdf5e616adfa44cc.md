# Jarosek, Stephen: Association as downward causation

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper proposes association—defined in Peircean semiotic terms as the triadic process linking signs, objects, and interpretants—as a fundamental principle that unifies upward and downward causation, with its strongest empirical grounding in biological systems\. Physicalist frameworks struggle to reconcile entropy with the emergence of organised complexity and meaning, while purely computational models often rest on mechanistic assumptions\. In contrast, association offers a non\-reducible foundation for coherence, adaptation, and agency in living systems\. Evidence from neural and cellular plasticity demonstrates that associative processes drive biological adaptation beyond genetic mechanisms alone\. Studies of single\-celled organisms further reveal associative learning as a basic biological capacity\. Quantum contextuality and Rovelli’s Relational Quantum Mechanics provide suggestive relational analogies at the physical level, though full semiotic interpretation remains tied to living interpretants\. Square\-cube scaling serves as a phenomenological heuristic to reframe subatomic behaviour as context\-dependent rather than object\-like\. Grounded in dual\-aspect monism, this framework positions association as the connective tissue of meaning and causation in biological organisation, with broader philosophical implications for understanding complexity and emergence\.
