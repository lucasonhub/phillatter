# Advanced detection of carcinogenic heterocyclic amines in meats: an overview of analytical and nanotechnology methods

**Autores:** Farrukh Samavia; Ume Roobab; Farrukh Aqsa; Zia Ud Din Momand; Zahra Batool; Gholamreza Abdi

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Despite extensive research on the formation and health risks of heterocyclic amines \(HAs\) in cooked meats, there is a gap in comparative analysis of advanced detection methods, particularly juxtaposing classical analytical techniques against emerging nanotechnology\-based methods\. This review aims to bridge this gap by comparing classical analytical techniques and nanoparticle\-based methods for detecting carcinogenic HAs in cooked meats, focusing on their efficacy, sensitivity, and specificity\. The findings indicate that traditional methods such as ultrahigh\-performance liquid chromatography\-tandem mass spectrometry \(UHPLC\-MS/MS\) offer high sensitivity and specificity\. However, nanoparticle\-based methods, including those involving magnetic nanoparticles and nanoemulsions, have shown superior advancements in detection limits and efficiency\. The integration of enabling technologies such as magnetic solid\-phase extraction using functionalized magnetic nanoparticles has simplified separation without filtration/centrifugation and reduce solvent use; UHPLC\-MS/MS has significantly lowered detection limits and improved efficiency; nanoemulsion\-based systems increase interfacial area and mass transfer; and surface\-enhanced Raman scattering is known for rapid, portable, highly sensitive &\#x27;fingerprint&\#x27; detection in complex matrices\. Unlike previous studies, this review provides a systematic comparison of conventional analytical techniques and emerging nanotechnology\-based detection methods, emphasizing recent advances and trends in nanotechnology\-based approaches for HA detection, thereby contributing to improved food safety standards\. © 2026 Society of Chemical Industry\.
