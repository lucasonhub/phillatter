# BRUTE FORCE LOGIN DETECTION: A COMPARATIVE STUDY OF IPBASED, ACCOUNT\-BASED, MACHINE LEARNING\-BASED, AND HYBRID APPROACHES

**Autores:** 

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Brute force login attacks remain a persistent threat, and detection research has split into IP\-based, account\-based, machine learning\-based, and hybrid approaches that are rarely compared on common terms\.This paper reviews 18 papers published between 2006 and 2026, classifies them by the component that makes the detection decision, and compares them on reported accuracy, resilience to IP rotation and low\-rate attacks, computational overhead, real\-time suitability, dataset realism, and deployment complexity\.No experiments are run, and all numerical results are as reported by the authors of the cited works\.Accuracy figures are plentiful but not directly comparable: nine studies report values from 94\.3% to 99\.995%, mostly on public benchmarks, and only four papers use production data\.Evasion is tested directly in three papers, overhead is measured in four, and SSH dominates protocol coverage\.Account\-based methods have the thinnest evidence, while hybrid methods carry the strongest evidence on distributed and low\-rate attacks\.The paper closes with criteria\-based deployment recommendations and a call for a shared benchmark that includes evasion scenarios\.
