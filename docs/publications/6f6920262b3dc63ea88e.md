# What Factors Influence University Students’ Well\-Being and Satisfaction When Engaging in Gig or Platform\-Based Work?

**Autores:** Tiankai Wu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Despite rapid gig economy growth among university students, research on their well\-being is limited\. Using the JD\-R model, this study examines how job demands \(insecurity, income instability\) and resources \(social support\) affect subjective well\-being and job satisfaction in Chinese student gig workers, with work stress and engagement as mediators, and academic stress and algorithmic transparency as moderators\. A cross\-sectional survey of 155 Chinese student gig workers was analysed via SPSS and PROCESS\. Results show insecurity negatively predicts well\-being, income instability reduces satisfaction, and social support has the strongest positive effects\. Work stress fully mediates the insecurity–well\-being link, while engagement partially mediates social support effects\. No moderation effects were found\. Findings extend the JD\-R model to this under\-studied population, reveal distinct psychological pathways, and offer practical implications for platforms, universities, and policymakers\. Limitations and future directions are discussed\.
