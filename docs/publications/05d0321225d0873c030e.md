# Improving deep neural network prediction of hydraulic fracturing initiation pressure in hydrate\-bearing sediments through feature engineering and architecture search

**Autores:** Thi Huynh Ho; Thuat Thanh Trinh

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Accurate prediction of hydraulic fracturing initiation pressure \( $$P\_b$$ \) in natural gas hydrate\-bearing sediments provides screening\-level support for laboratory\-scale hydraulic fracturing design in NGH\-bearing sediments\. This study demonstrates that systematic feature engineering and neural architecture search yield substantial improvements for predicting $$P\_b$$ on a 114\-sample published dataset\. The framework first expands the feature space from 12 to 208 variables via polynomial transformations, interaction terms, and domain\-specific features, followed by deduplication\. Multi\-method feature selection identifies a 14\-feature subset, and extended evaluation identifies a verified 12 $$\\times $$ 32 model as the best\-performing saved configuration\. The reproducible\-by\-retraining reference is R $$^2$$ $$\\approx $$ 0\.87; the verified 12 $$\\times $$ 32 saved model achieves R $$^2$$ = 0\.92 on its original held\-out split and is reported as an upper bound on the dataset\. The best of 60 retraining runs reaches 0\.85—none reproduces 0\.92, confirming that 0\.92 is a saved\-artifact result, not a reproducible target\. The verified 12 $$\\times $$ 32 model reduces MSE from 19\.34 to 8\.22 MPa $$^2$$ and MAE from 3\.16 to 2\.14 MPa compared to the best prior reported result \(R $$^2$$ = 0\.81\)\. Feature importance and SHAP analysis show that the model assigns highest importance to stress\-composition interactions and stress\-derived statistical terms, consistent with known geomechanical controls on fracture initiation\. Generalization is modest \(CV mean R $$^2 \\approx $$ 0\.50\), and simpler models \(RandomForest, SVR\) are recommended as production\-ready baselines; the 12 $$\\times $$ 32 DNN is retained as the best verified saved artifact\. These findings confirm that the dataset contains untapped predictive capacity, and that methodological refinement through domain\-informed feature engineering combined with systematic architecture search provides an effective pathway for improving prediction fidelity in small\-sample geomechanical problems\.
