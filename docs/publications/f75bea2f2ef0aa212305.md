# WELDING WIRE COMPOSITION DESIGN FOR A514 HIGH\-STRENGTH STEEL WELDED JOINTS BASED ON AN INTERPRETABLE RANDOM FOREST MODEL

**Autores:** Dinglu Wu; Yutong Zhao; Feiyang Tao; Jiasong Zhang; Bowen Song; Zheng Yan; Wenxian Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

To address the challenge of designing welding wire compositions for high\-strength steel, this study constructs a random forest model based on 119 sets of MAG welding solid wire data\. Nine elements, including C and Mn, were used as input features, and four mechanical properties, including tensile strength, were used as output targets\. Following five\-fold cross\-validation to optimise the model hyperparameters, high prediction accuracy was achieved for both the training and test sets\. For example, the tensile\-strength model achieved a training\-set R2 of 0\.905 and a test\-set MAPE of 5\.782 %\. Considering the inherent physical scatter in impact energy at –40 °C, a high\-reliability Random Forest classification model \(Threshold=100 J\) was innovatively developed to ensure design safety, achieving a test accuracy of 79\.17 % and an AUC of 0\.844\. SHAP and partial dependence plot analyses showed that Mo, Ni, and Mn are positively correlated with tensile strength and yield strength, while Cr, Si, P, etc\., are negatively correlated with elongation after fracture and significantly decrease the probability of achieving high impact toughness at low temperatures\. To eliminate subjective bias, a high\-throughput virtual screening framework combining Latin Hypercube Sampling \(LHS\) and Mahalanobis distance applicability domain \(AD\) evaluation was established\. Based on rigid multi\-objective engineering constraints, the optimal welding wire composition range was quantitatively determined\. This data\-driven method can effectively shorten the R&amp;D cycle and provides a scientific and quantitative basis for wire composition design\.
