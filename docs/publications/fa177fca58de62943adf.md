# Physics\-informed machine learning\-based dose calculation for patients undergoing fluoroscopically guided interventional procedures

**Autores:** Haegin Han; Keith Griffin; Monjoy Saha; A Kyle Jones; Steve D\. Mann; Kevin A\. Wunderle; Donald L\. Miller; Nina Petoussi\-Henss; D\. G\. Sutton; Choonsik Lee

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OBJECTIVE: To develop and evaluate a physics\-informed machine\-learning framework for rapid organ\-dose estimation in fluoroscopically guided interventional procedures without requiring patient\-specific imaging\. Approach: We used 385 adult radiation dose structured reports from three procedure types\-bilateral carotid angiography, percutaneous biliary drainage, and liver chemoembolization\-comprising more than 50,000 irradiation events\. A body\-size\-matched computational phantom was selected using patient sex, height, and weight\. RDSR\-derived beam parameters and engineered descriptors of beam\-organ geometry and attenuation were used to train organ\-specific XGBoost models for localized organs and a multi\-output multilayer perceptron model for four distributed tissues\. Monte Carlo\-derived dose coefficients from NCIRF3 were used as target values\. Main results: In the held\-out test set of 12,683 irradiation events, 49\.4% and 84\.8% of XGBoost\-based localized\-organ estimates were within ±5% and ±20% of the Monte Carlo\-derived values, respectively\. The corresponding proportions were 88\.2% and 98\.0% for the distributed\-tissue estimates and 87\.6% and 98\.3% for detriment\-weighted dose\. The median relative prediction error across organs and tissues was 0\.37% \(interquartile range, \-3\.68% to 4\.99%\)\. A reliability score was developed to support uncertainty\-aware interpretation\. The framework required approximately 0\.3 s per irradiation event, corresponding to an approximately 290\-fold acceleration relative to conventional Monte Carlo simulation under the benchmark conditions\. Significance: The proposed image\-free framework enables rapid organ\-dose estimation when patient imaging is unavailable and may support large retrospective epidemiological studies and uncertainty\-aware dose monitoring\. Because development and validation were limited to three procedure types and relied on reconstructed phantom\-beam geometry, external validation is required before application to other procedures or high\-precision individual clinical decisions\.
