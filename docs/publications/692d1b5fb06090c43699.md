# Familiopathic and thymopathic borderline: Proposal of an etiological typology of borderline personality disorder

**Autores:** Giovanna Azevedo Rodrigues; Marcelo Eduardo Caixeta

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Borderline personality disorder \(BPD\) is clinically heterogeneous, which helps explain the uneven response of patients to treatment\. This article aims to propose an etiological typology that sorts patients by the relative weight of two sources of instability, the family and mood\. This theoretical essay is supported by a directed narrative review of 30 references, mostly register\-based, genetic and meta\-analytic studies and guidelines from 2021 to 2026\. In familiopathic borderline, instability is maintained by a pathogenic family environment still active; medication acts only on symptoms, and the decisive intervention targets the family and the protection of the patient\. In thymopathic borderline, instability expresses a constitutional affective dysregulation, with a hypothesized delayed response to lithium\. The mixed form combines both and is argued to predominate in Brazilian practice\. Recent findings support both axes and the rarity of the pure familial form: heritability of 46%, a high genetic correlation with depression and a moderate one with bipolar disorder, and a minimal contribution of the environment shared by siblings\. The axes interact, since part of the inherited liability appears to be a greater sensitivity to adversity, and family load is distinguished as active or past\. Maltreatment by a non\-parent maternal partner is proposed as the most destructive family factor\. The pharmacological component exceeds current evidence and is presented as a hypothesis\. A five\-step classification algorithm, an evidence map, implications for early identification and four testable predictions are provided\. The typology offers the clinician a simple question and a research agenda, pending empirical validation\.
