# Circ\_0024390 promotes renal tubular EMT through the miR\-6890\-3p/CHD4 axis: implications for renal interstitial fibrosis

**Autores:** Xue Yang; Lujie Zhang; Yan Song; Gang Liu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Renal interstitial fibrosis \(RIF\) is a central pathological event in the progression of various chronic kidney diseases \(CKD\) to end\-stage renal disease, for which effective therapeutic strategies remain lacking\. Epithelial\-mesenchymal transition \(EMT\) of renal tubular epithelial cells serves as a pivotal mechanism driving RIF\. While circular RNAs \(circRNAs\) have been implicated in various pathological processes, their specific roles and underlying mechanisms in the progression of RIF remain incompletely elucidated\. Methods A circRNA microarray was utilized to screen differentially expressed circRNAs in a TGF\-β1\-induced HK\-2 cell model of renal fibrosis\. Loss\-of\-function experiments were performed to explore the biological effects of circ\_0024390 on renal tubular EMT, as well as the proliferation and migration of HK\-2 cells\. Mechanistic assays were further conducted to verify the competing endogenous RNA \(ceRNA\) regulatory mechanism of circ\_0024390\. Results Circ\_0024390 was significantly upregulated in the in vitro fibrosis model, specifically in TGF\-β1\-induced HK\-2 cells\. Functional studies demonstrated that knockdown of circ\_0024390 effectively attenuated renal tubular EMT and inhibited key cellular processes, including proliferation and migration\. Mechanistic investigations further revealed that circ\_0024390 functioned as a ceRNA by sponging miR\-6890\-3p\. This sponging relieved the repressive effect of miR\-6890\-3p on its downstream target, chromodomain helicase DNA\-binding protein 4 \(CHD4\), leading to the upregulation of CHD4 expression\. Conclusion These findings delineate a novel circ\_0024390/miR\-6890\-3p/CHD4 axis that promotes EMT in renal tubular epithelial cells and contributes to the progression of RIF\. This study provides new insights into the pathogenesis of RIF and highlights circ\_0024390 as a potential promising therapeutic target for CKD\.
