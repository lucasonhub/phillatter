# Bioactive small\-molecule gas delivery for enhanced bone regeneration: modulation of cellular signaling and remodeling of the regenerative microenvironment

**Autores:** Zhou Zhong; Biao Cao; Xin Duan

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Complex bone defects are characterized not only by structural loss but also by persistent inflammation, insufficient vascularization, oxidative stress, impaired cell recruitment, and disrupted bone remodeling, which collectively limit endogenous repair\. Bioactive small\-molecule gases have emerged as versatile regulators of bone regeneration because of their rapid diffusion, low\-dose activity, and ability to modulate multiple cellular and microenvironmental processes\. This mini\-review summarizes the biological roles and biomaterial\-based delivery strategies of nitric oxide \(NO\), carbon monoxide \(CO\), hydrogen sulfide \(H 2 S\), oxygen \(O 2 \), and hydrogen \(H 2 \) in bone repair\. NO primarily promotes angiogenesis and osteogenic–angiogenic coupling, whereas CO contributes mainly to anti\-inflammatory, antioxidant, and cytoprotective regulation\. H 2 S exhibits broader effects by coordinating redox homeostasis, macrophage polarization, vascularization, mitochondrial function, and osteogenic differentiation\. O 2 and H 2 provide complementary benefits by alleviating hypoxia, reducing oxidative damage, and remodeling senescence\-associated microenvironments\. To overcome the rapid diffusion, short half\-life, limited local retention, and narrow therapeutic windows of free gases, diverse delivery platforms have been developed, including nanoscale gas\-generating systems, stimuli\-responsive materials, injectable hydrogels, biomimetic scaffolds, and living gas\-producing biomaterials\. These systems enable localized, sustained, or on\-demand gas release and can integrate immunomodulation, reactive oxygen species scavenging, angiogenic stimulation, bioelectric cues, and mechanical support\. Representative studies demonstrate promising outcomes in critical\-sized, osteoporotic, diabetic, inflammatory, and aging\-related bone defects\. Nevertheless, clinical translation remains constrained by challenges in dose control, release kinetics, material degradation, biosafety, long\-term metabolism, and stage\-specific therapeutic adaptation\. Future research should focus on precisely matching gas release profiles to the temporal phases of bone healing and on developing multifunctional platforms that combine gaseous signaling with other regenerative cues\.
