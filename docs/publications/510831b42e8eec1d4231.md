# Interpolation of unitaries with time\-dependent Hamiltonians via Deep Learning

**Autores:** Antonio Guerra; Daniel Uzcátegui Contreras; A\. Delgado; Esteban S\. Gómez

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Quantum systems governed by time\-dependent Hamiltonians pose significant challenges for the accurate computation of unitary time\-evolution operators, which are essential for predicting quantum state dynamics\. In this work, we introduce a physics\-informed deep learning approach based on Physics\-Informed Neural Networks to estimate these operators over the full time domain\. By incorporating physical constraints such as unitarity and leveraging the second\-order Magnus expansion or Trotterization on the evolution operator, the proposed framework enables the estimation of unitary matrices at different time intervals\. The model is trained using simulated unitary operators and evaluated on quantum systems ranging from 2 to 6 qubits, governed by a fully non\-zero Pauli Hamiltonian\. For larger many\-body systems, specifically those with 7 and 8 qubits, the same methodology is employed to reconstruct an effective time\-dependent Hamiltonian, restricted to nearest\-neighbor Ising and XYZ Heisenberg models, from which the corresponding time\-evolution operator is computed over the entire temporal domain\. Across all system sizes, the framework achieves high gate fidelities using a limited number of unitary samples, with a statistical analysis over independent Hamiltonian realizations confirming mean fidelities close to 0\.99 and low variability\. Furthermore, once trained, the model interpolates the evolution operator with inference times substantially lower than those of a numerical interpolation baseline, indicating a potential reduction in measurement and data acquisition costs\. These results highlight the effectiveness of the approach for data\-driven simulation and identification of quantum dynamical systems, with direct relevance to quantum computing and quantum simulation applications\.
