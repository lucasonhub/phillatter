# Do small hydrogen\-containing gas molecules support low deuterium in mitochondrial water?

**Autores:** Stephanie Seneff

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Deuterium is a natural heavy isotope of hydrogen, containing a neutron as well as a proton, making it twice as heavy\. Mitochondrial ATP synthase nanomotors are highly sensitive to deuterium, because it impedes the flow of protons through the nanomotors and causes the γ C\-terminal helix to unwind\. It appears that metabolic processes have evolved to devise ways to reduce the deuterium load in the mitochondria, primarily, we argue, by exploiting several small hydrogen\-containing molecular gases\. We propose that, when a gas is produced, deuterium, due to its extra weight, tends to stay behind in the aqueous medium, so the gas becomes deuterium\-depleted\. Furthermore, many of the enzymes that synthesize and/or metabolize these small gas molecules fractionate out deuterium by exploiting proton tunneling\. We hypothesize that the gut microbes play a crucial role in producing deuterium\-depleted gas molecules, such as hydrogen gas \(H 2 \), methane \(CH 4 \), ammonia \(NH 3 \), hydrogen sulfide \(H 2 S\), trimethylamine \(TMA\), formaldehyde \(HCHO\), and methanethiol \(CH 3 SH\)\. Gut microbial metabolism of TMA likely provides a low\-deuterium source of one\-carbon \(1C\) units to fuel the methylation pathway in the host\. The glutamate\-glutamine exchange that takes place between astrocytes and neurons plausibly capitalizes on NH 3 to preferentially deliver 1 H \(protons\) to neuronal mitochondria\. Red blood cells actively import NH 3 while the cation NH 4 \+ is excreted in the urine, possibly as a strategy to fractionate out deuterium\. In this paper, we investigate how small molecular hydrogen\-containing gases are handled in metabolism, from a perspective that considers that these gases may serve a useful role in reducing the deuterium content in the mitochondria\. Our hypothesis awaits validation by expert researchers who can perform appropriate experiments to test the hypothesis\.
