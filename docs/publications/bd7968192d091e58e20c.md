# A comparison between polyacrylamide and arcillite granules as substrates for space agriculture

**Autores:** Nicholas M\. Miklave; Karl H\. Hasenstein

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Plant cultivation in space requires substrates that provide sufficient water retention, root system support, and nutrients\. Arcillite – a calcined montmorillonite clay product – is currently used in growth chambers on the International Space Station despite significant variability in mineral composition and occasional detrimental effects on seed germination and plant growth\. In contrast, hydrogels – which are used as soil amendments in agriculture – can be produced with desired structural and chemical properties\. We compared two batches of arcillite and polyacrylamide \(PAAM\) hydrogel granules by growing and analyzing radishes \( Raphanus sativus \) and observed significant differences in leaf and bulb biomass, evapotranspiration, and photosystem II efficiency between the two batches of arcillite, with soluble calcium, magnesium, selenium, and sulfur being more abundant in the poorly performing arcillite\. There was no difference in leaf and bulb biomass or photosystem II efficiency between PAAM and the arcillite batch without excessive calcium, but radishes grown in PAAM required significantly less water\. Our results indicate that PAAM can outperform arcillite in water\-limited environments because of reduced evapotranspiration rates, greater uniformity, and comparable yields\. Therefore, hydrogels such as PAAM may be beneficial for space plant cultivation\.
