## Resumo

From steel to glass, to catalysts and batteries, to medicines, inorganic chemistry illuminates the materials around us\. Basics of Inorganic Chemistry is a study guide and textbook of inorganic chemistry in simple language for undergraduate students \(first year B\. Sc\. Chemistry\), which can also be used for revision of exams\. It introduces all the fundamental principles of inorganic chemistry in a logical sequence, with building chapter on chapter\. It will be a reliable reference for teachers, all undergraduate and postgraduate students and for self\-learners preparing for competitive exams\. The first chapter on atomic structure and periodic properties is the starting point for the book\. Traces the development of Dalton&\#x27;s, Rutherford&\#x27;s and Bohr&\#x27;s models of the atom to the quantum mechanical model – quantum numbers, electronic configurations, and the Aufbau principle\. The periodic table, atomic radius, ionisation energy, electron affinity, electronegativity scales and the diagonal relationship are then introduced to show how the trends in the periodic table can be used to predict the behaviour of the chemicals\. Chemical bonding follows\. Valence bond theory and hybridization are related to molecular geometry, VSEPR theory is used to predict the molecular shaping and molecular orbital theory is applied, using MO diagrams and bond order\. Next, Coordination chemistry\. It includes coordination compounds, ligands, nomenclature, isomerism, 18\-electron rule, metal carbonyls, crystal field theory, crystal field splitting energy and spectrochemical series, colour and magnetism of transition metal compounds\. The trends in the p\-block elements are also used as a link between metals and nonmetals and its chemistry is handled in terms of the group trends, anomalous behaviour of second period elements and inert pair effect\. Inorganic qualitative analysis, cation and anion identification, group reagents, solubility product, the common ion effect and the Lewis, Lux\-Flood and Pearson \(hard and soft acids and bases\) concepts are then combined with qualitative analysis and acid\-base chemistry\. The final section of the book is the study of chemistry of transition elements, d\-bloc trends, variable oxidation states, coloured compounds and preparation and reactions of potassium dichromate and potassium permanganate\. The theory that is being taught is not treated as something to be taught all by itself\. Every chapter connects the fundamentals to recent research for example on the synthesis of superheavy elements or density functional theory, as well as computational chemistry and machine learning to predict molecular properties\. Additional connections include approaches to capture CO₂ using metal\-organic frameworks, single\-atom catalysts, catalysis using earth\-abundant metals, materials for batteries, green analytical chemistry, paper\-based sensors, and critical minerals for the energy transition\. In this way, students will be introduced to the fact that inorganic chemistry is a living science\. The book is organised for effective learning and self\-study\. Every chapter begins with an abstract and keywords, worked examples and numerical problems are provided where appropriate and each chapter concludes with summary tables and review questions\. Explanations are kept succinct and explanations are illustrated with simple examples that are familiar to students\. I hope this book gives students a sound conceptual foundation in inorganic chemistry and the confidence to move on to advanced inorganic chemistry, organometallic chemistry, bioinorganic chemistry and materials science\. Care has been taken in the preparation of the book, and improvements and suggestions from readers and teachers will be gladly received and used in future editions\.
