# Corpi inclusi in movimento: effetti di un percorso curricolare di Baskin tra status sociometrico, autoefficacia e piacere motorio\.

**Autores:** Lorenzo Cioni; Antonio Ferraro; Angela Magnanını

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Within the theoretical framework of Embodied Education, Baskin represents a context of embodied inclusion that promotes the active participation of students with diverse functioning profiles\. The present study investigated the effects of a curricular Baskin program on physical activity enjoyment and social self\-efficacy among students with Special Educational Needs \(SEN\)\. It also explored the relationships between these variables and changes in sociometric status, previously documented within the same sample\. A quasi\-experimental design was adopted involving 107 students with SEN attending lower secondary school, divided into an experimental group \(n = 62\) and a control group \(n = 45\)\. The Physical Activity Enjoyment Scale \(PACES\) and the Perceived Social Self\-Efficacy Scale \(SASPe\) were administered before and after the intervention\. Repeated\-measures ANOVAs revealed significant increases in physical activity enjoyment and social self\-efficacy in the experimental group, whereas no such improvements were observed in the control group\. Correlational analyses further showed that a reduction in sociometric rejection was associated with increases in both perceived enjoyment and social self\-efficacy\. These findings suggest that Baskin may represent a concrete experience of embodied inclusion, fostering students’ well\-being, participation, and social recognition simultaneously\.
