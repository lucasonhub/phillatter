# A clinical\-deep learning model for prolonged length of stay following endovascular aortic repair

**Autores:** Changfu Hu; Tao Bai; Xiao Qin; Kangsui Tang; Tao Peng; Qingbo Yang; Minkang Jiang; Lin Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Prolonged length of stay \(PLOS\) after endovascular aortic repair \(EVAR\) for abdominal aortic aneurysm \(AAA\) is associated with increased complications, resource burden, and patient distress\. Early identification of high\-risk patients remains challenging\. This study aimed to develop and externally validate a predictive model integrating clinical variables and three\-dimensional deep learning \(3D\-DL\) features from preoperative computed tomography angiography \(CTA\) to predict PLOS after EVAR\. Methods A total of 788 patients with AAA who underwent elective EVAR were enrolled from two hospitals\. The internal training cohort consisted of 556 patients, and the external validation cohort included 232 patients\. PLOS was defined as a LOS above the 75th percentile of the distribution\. A clinical nomogram was developed using LASSO regression followed by multivariate logistic regression\. Deep learning \(DL\) features were extracted from manually segmented computed tomography angiography \(CTA\) images using a 3D ResNet200 network, and LASSO regression was applied to select the most informative features\. An ensemble model integrating the clinical predictors and the selected DL features was constructed\. Model performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic curves, calibration plots, and decision curve analysis\. Results LASSO combined with multivariate logistic regression identified five independent clinical predictors: age, hemoglobin, albumin, maximum aneurysm diameter, and diabetes\. The clinical model achieved an area under the curve \(AUC\) of 0\.917 in the internal cohort and 0\.944 in the external cohort\. From the 3D ResNet200 network, 32 DL features were extracted\. LASSO regression further selected two of these features\. The ensemble predictive model, which integrated the clinical predictors and DL features, demonstrated improved discrimination, with an AUC of 0\.971 in the internal cohort and 0\.958 in the external cohort\. Internal validation with 1,000 bootstrap resamples yielded optimism\-corrected AUCs of 0\.911 for the clinical model and 0\.966 for the ensemble model\. Subgroup analyses demonstrated consistent model performance across age and diabetes strata, while performance in the female subgroup warrants further evaluation in larger cohorts\. Conclusion This study developed an ensemble predictive model to predict PLOS following EVAR\. The model demonstrated excellent discrimination and provided significant prognostic value beyond clinical variables alone\. This nomogram may serve as a preoperative tool for risk stratification and resource planning in this population\. Future studies should focus on prospective validation and clinical integration\.
