# Investigation of First\-Year Students&\#x27; and Lecturers&\#x27; Expectations of University Education: A Case Study of a Public Provincial University

**Autores:** Sokha Thy; Vattey Roeurt; Vichet Un; Thida Khean; Buntith Mak; Sokun Kong; Bopha Kong; Rany Sam

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study investigated \(1\) the expectations that first\-year students bring to universities and \(2\) lecturers&\#x27; expectations of first\-year students at the National University of Battambang \(NUBB\), Cambodia\. A cross\-sectional survey design was used\. The participants included 200 first\-year undergraduates \(130 in social sciences and 70 in applied sciences; 156 female and 44 male; modal age 19 years\) and 24 lecturers \(13 female and 11 male\)\. The student questionnaire, adapted from Hassel and Ridout \(2018\), comprises 29 items in six subscales: reasons for attending university \(ambition, drive and motivation; RM\), lack of other opportunities \(LO\), social factors \(SO\), perceived status and expectations \(PE\), anticipated academic aptitude struggles \(AS\) and other struggles \(OS\)\. The lecturer questionnaire comprised 15 items covering lecturers as facilitators \(EF\), lectures being dictated \(ED\), and universities being easy or similar to high school \(EH\)\. Data were analyzed with descriptive statistics, Cronbach&\#x27;s α, one\-sample t tests against the scale midpoint \(3\) with Cohen&\#x27;s d, and Welch’s t tests for sex and field\-of\-study differences\. Students enrolled mainly for ambition and career reasons \(M = 3\.93 on a 5\-point scale\) and perceived status \(M = 3\.71\) rather than for lack of other opportunities \(M = 2\.72\) or social factors \(M = 2\.65\), and they anticipated moderate academic difficulty \(M = 3\.25\), especially with self\-directed learning, study skills and workload; 61\.5% also expected financial difficulties\. Expectations did not differ significantly by sex or field of study\. Lecturers believe that incoming students expect to manage their own notes and engage in independent learning, group work and research but also expect lecturers to dictate or supply notes\. This points to a gap between school\-based and university learning\. The findings suggest that a structured orientation, explicit communication of academic norms and early academic skills support could help NUBB narrow this gap in expectations\.
