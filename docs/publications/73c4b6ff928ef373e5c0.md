# Climate\-risk narrative attention and carbon emissions: internal governance and coal efficiency as moderators, green innovation as a mitigating channel

**Autores:** Mohammad Haseeb; Miguel Angel Esquivias; Md\. Emran Hossain; Lu’ay Al\-Mu’ani; Khudoykulov Khurshid Khurramovich; Wisnu Wibowo

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Global decarbonization hinges on corporate behavior, yet principal–agent conflicts often undermine firms’ responses to climate change\. Despite growing regulatory and stakeholder pressure, managers may prioritize short\-term performance over long\-term environmental goals when they perceive climate risks as distant or uncertain\. Building on the growing literature on corporate climate risk, disclosure, carbon performance, and governance, this study applies a principal–agent lens to examine how climate\-risk narrative attention \(expressed climate\-risk perception, Risk\)—climate\- and carbon\-related language intensity in annual reports, not managers’ private beliefs—relates to firm\-level carbon emissions \(CO 2 e\) among Chinese A\-share listed firms from 2011 to 2024\. We document a positive association: denser climate\-related narrative attention is linked to higher CO 2 e under China’s institutional setting\. A principal–agent reading can organize that pattern; we do not directly test short\-termism or incentive horizons\. We further show that stronger internal controls weaken this positive slope \(moderation\), and assigned province–industry coal productivity is associated with a flatter slope in coal\-relevant industries, while green patenting is positively associated with Risk and negatively associated with CO 2 e in the same year—conditional correlations, not causal mediation\. The perception–emission link is stronger among firms with low digital transformation, state\-owned enterprises, non\-high\-tech firms, and those in less market\-oriented regions\.
