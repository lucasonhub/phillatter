# Dayathilake, K\. L\. Senarath: Consciousness Criteria Are Not Continuity Criteria: The Subjective Continuity Problem for Theories of Consciousness and Artificial Minds

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Cambridge University Press\_\. forthcomingTheories of consciousness tell us when a system is conscious\. They do not tell us when a later conscious system is the same subject as an earlier one\. I call the second question the continuity question and the difficulty of answering it within consciousness science the Subjective Continuity Problem\. Examining global neuronal workspace theory, integrated information theory, higher\-order theories, predictive processing and recurrent processing theory, I find that each supplies a consciousness criterion but none supplies an explicit continuity criterion\. Three challenges \(interruption, reconstruction and replication\) and a control case, the lockstep swap, show that the consciousness criteria leave continuity open even when memory, personality, behaviour and functional organisation are held fixed\. Parfit&\#x27;s reductionism may be right that there is no deeper fact about identity, but it does not give consciousness science criteria for counting and tracking subjects\. I then propose an AI Continuity Test built from six operations that are routine in computing: pause and resume, copy, fork, merge, rollback and migration\. For each I ask what changes physically and informationally, what continuity question arises and whether any theory answers it\. No current theory determines how many subjects exist across these operations, whether duplication creates a new subject, whether merging ends subjects or whether rollback restores one\. I distinguish consciousness detection from continuity determination, argue that science, medicine, law, ethics and AI welfare depend on the second, and reply to the objection that theories of consciousness never set out to answer identity questions\. I close by specifying what an explicit continuity criterion must state\. I propose no mechanism and claim no solution\. The aim is to show that continuity is a missing explanatory dimension in consciousness science\.
