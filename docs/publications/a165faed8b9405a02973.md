# Stance, Legitimation, and Digital Authority on LinkedIn: A Corpus\-Informed Critical Discourse Analysis of Bill Gates&\#x27; Posts

**Autores:** Abdulhameed Abdulhadi Alhuwaydi

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper studies how Bill Gates’ LinkedIn posts enact digital authority and legitimate global development intervention through stance, quantification, progress\-talk, and solution\-oriented agency\. The corpus includes 14 fact\-checked English\-language LinkedIn posts published by Bill Gates between 23 September 2025 and 7 June 2026\. Corpus\-informed critical discourse analysis employed reproducible Python\-generated token counts alongside post\-level thematic and legitimation coding\. The total corpus size was 1,222 words \(M = 87\.3 words/post; range = 24\-199\)\. Posts related to global health or child survival were most frequent at eight posts \(57\.1%\)\. First\-person singular self\-mentions and collective references each appeared in nine posts\. Predictive appeared 18 times across the corpus Quantification/evidentialization appeared in nine posts, solution/intervention language appeared in 11 posts, and agency assigned to institutions or experts appeared in 10 posts\. Close reading revealed a common pattern of legitimation through stances which move from problem definition, to evi\-dence and social alliances, towards solution\-carrying organizations, programs/companies, researchers, or technologies\. This pattern diffuses moral responsibility while centralizing the capacity for action\. Results suggest executive discourse on LinkedIn blends personal judgments, quantified endorsements of urgency, collective solidarity, and futurological expertise to frame institutions and their solutions as natural\.
