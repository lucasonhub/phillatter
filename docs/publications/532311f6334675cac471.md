# Environmental DNA metabarcoding performance for biodiversity monitoring at livestock–wildlife interfaces

**Autores:** Alberto Perelló; Filipa M\. S\. Martins; Marta Perez Sancho; David Relimpio; Gloria Herrero‐García; Alberto Díez‐Guerrier; Ángela Marín\-Rojo; Paulo C\. Alves; Christian Gortázar; João Queirós

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Open\-air farming systems represent critical landscapes for integrated wildlife monitoring, enhancing biosecurity and reducing the risk of pathogen transmission at the wildlife\-livestock interface\. Therefore, there is a need for comparative studies to improve non\-invasive methodologies for monitoring wild animal populations in these livestock\-dominated environments\. This study aims to conduct comparative monitoring of the biodiversity and detectability of wild species using methods based on morphological identification and environmental DNA \(eDNA\) sampling\. Research was conducted on 13 farms distributed across the Iberian Peninsula, where 27 to 30 camera traps were deployed, and water samples were collected by filtration at one lentic water body per study site\. Our results showed that camera traps detected higher taxa richness than eDNA under the sampling design used in this study, and ecological characteristics of the species, including their classification as gregarious or non\-gregarious, were associated with differences in eDNA detection rates\. Nevertheless, the complementary use of both approaches is recommended for wildlife population assessments\. Livestock density was associated with variation in some molecular parameters of water samples under the conditions evaluated in this study\. This study provides preliminary insights into the most suitable non\-invasive approaches for assessing wildlife populations in livestock\-dominated landscapes, within an integrated wildlife monitoring framework\.
