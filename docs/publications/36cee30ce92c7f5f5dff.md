# Jones, Jeremy C\.: Against Intelligence\-First — Intelligence as Plastic Feedback and Constraint Navigation \(Not Mystical Agency\)

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper clears a single, widely used term: intelligence\. It corrects a recurring misread — treating &quot;intelligence&quot; as a primitive essence, a synonym for complexity or optimization, or a private, human\-only possession — and replaces it with an operational claim: a system is intelligent when it can update its own constraints, using memory and feedback, so that it improves prediction, control, and transfer across contexts\. On this view intelligence is not &quot;more complexity&quot; and not consciousness; it is the capacity to turn feedback into lasting, reusable change — adaptive constraint navigation\. The paper draws the line carefully: intelligence requires an endogenous update loop that modifies behavior in response to evaluative feedback and yields gains that persist beyond the training context, which is what separates genuine learning from mere regulation \(a thermostat corrects but does not learn\)\. Crucially, it turns intelligence\-claims into something testable: a minimal litmus test, a feedback ladder that distinguishes regulation from learning, and a compact reporting measure that makes claims comparable against a stated non\-learning baseline and transfer suite\. The paper does not claim that an intelligent system is conscious, understands its task, or has inner experience, personhood, or rich agency; nor that large state spaces, emergent patterns, or optimization\-shaped behavior are themselves evidence of intelligence; nor that intelligence is uniquely human\. Its claim is narrower and checkable: intelligence is an endogenous, feedback\-driven update loop that improves performance across contexts — present or absent by structural criteria, measurable against a baseline, and possible without consciousness or understanding\. The aim is conceptual hygiene, not new theory: coherence is constraint made visible; intelligence is constraint made learnable\. This is the fourth of five short &quot;Prime&quot; papers — a conceptual\-hygiene layer that, within Universal Collapse Theory, sets how the framework is read\. But the Primes are written to stand on their own and ask no commitment to that framework: each clears a term the reader already holds and can judge on its own ground\. This one is for anyone — in artificial intelligence, cognitive science, biology, animal cognition, or philosophy of mind — who makes or weighs claims about intelligence and wants a criterion that separates genuine learning from complexity, optimization, or anthropomorphic projection, without smuggling in consciousness\. \(direct link \)
