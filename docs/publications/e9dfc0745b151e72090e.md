# Clinical Lexicon\-Guided Error Correction as an AI Safety Layer for Handwritten Prescriptions: Reducing Word Error and Flagging High\-Risk Transcriptions in South Sudan

**Autores:** Aguet Elizabeth Deng

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background:The same constraint, however, can create a dangerous failure mode if out\-of\-lexicon terms are forced toward plausible but incorrect medication names\. Objective: This study designed and validated a lexicon\-based post\-processing module for a Residual U\-Net handwritten\-text\-recognition system, with the pre\-specified requirement that post\-processing reduce final Word Error Rate \(WER\) by at least two percentage points\. Methods: Handwritten prescriptions remain a patient\-safety concern where electronic prescribing is limited and clinical staff must interpret variable, abbreviated and sometimes illegible scripts\. Results: Raw predictions were compared with a curated clinical lexicon containing drug names, dosage expressions, units, frequency abbreviations, dosage forms and common instructions\. The reported WER fell from 8\.5% before correction to 3\.2% after correction, an absolute reduction of 5\.3 percentage points and a derived relative reduction of 62\.4%\. These results show that domain\-constrained post\-processing can materially improve transcription fidelity while providing a basis for risk\-sensitive human review\. Conclusion: Low\-confidence tokens were evaluated with Levenshtein distance and sufficiently close candidates were mapped to the nearest valid clinical term\.
