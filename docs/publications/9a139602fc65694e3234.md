# Using the artificial intelligence tool Rayyan to conduct and report systematic reviews and meta\-analyses: a didactic review

**Autores:** I Alkassoum Salifou; Abdel Nacer Amoukou Issaka; Hassane Moussa Djongolé; Amadou Bouba Traoré Hassane; Zeinabou Tondi Moussa; Abdou Atté Bachir; Djibrilla Baoua Bonkano; Roukayatou Karimou Issaka

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Background\. Systematic reviews and meta\-analyses are time\-consuming, particularly at the study selection stage\. Rayyan, a web and mobile AI\-assisted screening platform, is widely used, including in francophone Africa, often without prior methodological training\. Objective\. To provide a didactic review of the use of Rayyan in conducting and reporting a systematic review with meta\-analysis, in line with international recommendations on responsible AI use\. Methods\. Narrative synthesis of Rayyan&\#x27;s official documentation \(accessed September 2026\), studies describing and evaluating the tool, reporting guidelines \(PRISMA 2020, PRISMA\-S\) and the 2025 joint position of Cochrane, the Campbell Collaboration, JBI and the Collaboration for Environmental Evidence; illustration with an entirely fictitious worked example\. Key messages\. Rayyan supports reference import, deduplication, blinded multi\-reviewer screening and conflict resolution and, depending on the plan, data extraction, risk\-of\-bias assessment and PRISMA flow diagrams\. Its relevance ranking requires at least 50 decisions, including 5 inclusions and 5 exclusions\. In one evaluation, sensitivity reached 97\-99% at the most cautious threshold, with a specificity of only 19\-58%\. AI should therefore prioritise, not replace, human decisions, and its use must be reported\. Conclusion\. Used appropriately, Rayyan saves time and improves traceability; responsibility remains entirely human\. Keywords: systematic review; meta\-analysis; Rayyan; artificial intelligence; study selection; PRISMA 2020; sub\-Saharan Africa\.
