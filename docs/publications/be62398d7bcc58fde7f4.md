# A Self\-supervised Temporal Deep Learning Framework with Diffusion\-based Rebalancing and Causal Explainability for Student Performance Prediction in Learning Analytics

**Autores:** Shweta Tiwari; Ruchika Gupta; Deepika Sharma

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Predicting student academic performance from Virtual Learning Environment \(VLE\) traces is a central task in learning analytics, yet current deep\-learning pipelines suffer from four shortcomings: they rely on fully supervised training despite the abundance of unlabelled interaction logs, they model long clickstreams with recurrent or selfattention backbones that do not scale, they use feature\-space oversampling methods such as SMOTE that introduce unrealistic synthetic samples, and they provide only correlational explanations\.This paper presents a unified architecture that closes these gaps with four tightly coupled components: \(i\) EduCLR, a self\-supervised contrastive pretraining module that learns transferable representations from unlabelled daily clickstream sequences using temporal augmentations; \(ii\) EduMamba, a selective state\-space temporal backbone that captures long\-range dependencies in linear time and exposes time\-varying selection weights as importance signals; \(iii\) DiffEdu, a conditional latent diffusion rebalancer; and \(iv\) CausalEdu, a causal\-counterfactual explanation layer\.The framework is evaluated on the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset \(OULAD\) as the main large\-scale temporal benchmark and on the xAPI\-Edu\-Data dataset as a cross\-domain generalisation benchmark\.The proposed framework achieves 94\.2% accuracy, 93\.5% macro\-F1, and 96\.1% macro\-AUC on four imbalanced outcome categories, outperforming the best recent baseline by 3\.8 percentage points in accuracy and 4\.4 points in macro\-F1\.It reaches 92\.6% accuracy on xAPI\-Edu\-Data with a consistent improvement across the High, Medium, and Low performance bands\.Ablation analysis shows that every module yields measurable benefits, and the causal explanation layer provides counterfactual interventions that educators can apply directly\.All results, for the proposed framework and for every re\-implemented baseline, are averaged over the same five random seeds and reported as mean ± standard deviation; the standard deviations of the proposed framework remain below 0\.6 percentage points, confirming statistical reliability\.The results demonstrate that combining self\-supervised representation learning, efficient temporal modelling, generative rebalancing, and causal interpretability yields a principled and deployable predictive\-analytics pipeline for virtual learning environments\.
