# Emerging Biosensing Technologies for Circulating Biomarkers in Precision Cancer Diagnostics

**Autores:** Ashvin Dudhrejiya, Krishna Tandel\*, Amit Vyas, Harshil Jadav, Abhishek Pandey

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Cancer is a worldwide problem\. Improved quality of life, increased survival, and a cheaper cost of cancer treatments\-\-which are higher at later stages of detection—all depend on early detection\. This evaluation focuses on the strengths of the current market market\. Despite their effectiveness and widespread clinical use, traditional techniques like imaging and biopsy are frequently hindered by their invasiveness, high cost, and lack of sensitivity for early\-stage cancer\. As a result, biosensors have become potential instruments that can discover biomarkers connected to cancer, such as circulating tumor DNA and tumor\-associated proteins\. These devices are fast, highly sensitive, non\-intrusive, and capable of real\-time monitoring\. This study links engineering principles with clinical readiness by covering basic concepts, material developments, and data supporting clinical application across electrochemical, optical, and piezoelectric biosensors\. We talk about wearable devices, amplification, wearable biosensor devices\. Problems with sensitivity, nonspecific binding and signal drift, standardization, manufacturing scalability, production costs, regulatory validation, and real\-world repeatability restrict the broad clinical applicability of current biosensors\. This research also discusses the integration of biosensors, AI, and liquid biopsy procedures that could result in point\-of\-care diagnostics and personalized cancer treatment\. In the battle for early cancer diagnosis, early detection and treatment are essential\. The detection of ctDNA has lately become a viable approach for early cancer screening\. The detection of ctDNA in fluid samples has drawn a lot of attention in the diagnosis and treatment of cancers\. Both are actively researching this scientific scientific community as well as the industry\.
