# Bridging predictive processing and illusionism

**Autores:** Sergio Cermeño\-Aínsa

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Theories of consciousness are commonly divided into two broad categories: functional and metaphysical\. Functional theories seek to explain how cognitive processes generate conscious experience, whereas metaphysical theories attempt to explain why there is something it is like to be a conscious system\. These two traditions have largely developed in parallel, rarely intersecting\. In this paper, I argue that they can be systematically connected\. Specifically, I argue that if Predictive Processing \(PP\) provides the correct computational architecture of cognition, then it supports an illusionist interpretation of phenomenal consciousness\. The argument proceeds through a series of conditionals linking predictive perception, predictive introspection, and phenomenal consciousness\. I first argue that perceptual experience is necessarily mediated by inferential, model\-dependent representations and therefore involves structural misrepresentation\. I then argue that the predictive architecture extends recursively to introspective awareness\. Finally, I argue that phenomenal consciousness is generated by introspective awareness and therefore inherits its inferential and model\-dependent character, thereby providing a systematic argument that Predictive Processing is most coherently interpreted within an illusionist framework\. The paper thus illustrates how a functional theory of cognition can entail substantive metaphysical consequences for our understanding of consciousness\.
