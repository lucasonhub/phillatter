# Beyond aggregate metrics: diagnosing the long\-tailed failure of oracle bone character detection and recognition

**Autores:** Dan Sui; Yuhang Xing; Penglan Liu; Bang Li

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Oracle bone inscriptions, the earliest mature Chinese writing system, are a primary source for studying ancient Chinese civilization, yet their interpretation remains labor\-intensive\. Automatically detecting and recognizing visible characters on rubbing images is therefore an entrance task for AI\-assisted interpretation\. The Oracle Bone Inscription Multi\-modal Dataset exposes the problem of extreme long\-tailed class distribution: aggregate evaluation conceals severe performance defects on rare classes—the detector can reach an overall AP 50 of 0\.63, while the few\-shot class AP 50 is only 0\.06\. We conduct a systematic diagnosis with a frequency\-aware stratified evaluation protocol\. Controlled comparisons show that localization is less limiting than rare\-glyph recognition, and that in\-head and crop\-level remedies trade aggregate accuracy against tail accuracy\. We thus propose FAR\-YOLO, a minimal decoupled baseline that separates localization and recognition training, optimizing the recognition branch only with frequency\-aware sampling and morphology\-preserving augmentation\. While maintaining nearly unchanged overall AP 50 , FAR\-YOLO improves the few\-shot AP 50 by 10\.5 percentage points\. It ranks second on all four frequency\-stratified metrics among representative long\-tailed methods, with consistent tail gains across seven recognition backbones\.
