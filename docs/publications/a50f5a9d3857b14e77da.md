# A Hybrid Physics\-Data Decision\-Support Framework for Real\-Time Prediction and Season\-Adaptive Optimization of a Natural Evaporative Water Cooler

**Autores:** Milan Trivedi; Diptesh Patel

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract This study presents a hybrid physics–data decision\-support framework for a natural evaporative water cooler that unifies real\-time temperature\-drop prediction with season\-adaptive operational optimization and delivers both as a deployable tool\. A one\-dimensional resistance\-network heat\-transfer model of the cooler forms the physics layer, and machine\-learning surrogates trained on 81 balanced experimental runs spanning summer, monsoon and winter \(dry\-bulb temperature 19\.3–42 °C, relative humidity 24\.6–80\.5 %, temperature drop 1\.8–9\.8 °C\) form the data layer\. Under ten\-fold cross\-validation a Gaussian\-process regressor is the most accurate model \(R2 = 0\.973\), while a tuned artificial neural network \(R2 = 0\.941\) is selected as the deployed surrogate because it compiles to a compact, dependency\-free model of 241 parameters suited to low\-cost field hardware\. The neural network is coupled with a differential\-evolution search that, for any ambient dry\-bulb temperature and relative humidity, returns the operational settings \(fan speed, sprinkled\-water flow rate and coil\-water velocity\) that maximize the temperature drop at a fixed, design\-selected viscose\-fibre pad, generalizing three discrete seasonal optima to a continuous envelope\. The global optimality of the search was verified against an exhaustive grid in 1500 independent runs, and the best of five runs came within 0\.01 °C of the global optimum in 97 % of cases\. On twelve independent experimental runs the framework predicts the temperature drop with R2 = 0\.985 and a mean absolute percentage error of 3\.6 %\. The framework is delivered as a self\-contained, browser\-based console that returns recommended settings and predicted performance from the ambient conditions and the inlet water temperature, offering an accessible route to energy\-efficient, refrigerant\-free water cooling for hot regions\.
