# Scalp Topography of Event\-Related Potentials Reveals Differences in Audiovisual Speech Processing in Autistic and Non\-Autistic Children

**Autores:** Kacie Dunham\-Carr; Jacob I\. Feldman; David M\. Simon; Mark T\. Wallace; Micah M\. Murray; Tiffany G\. Woynaroski

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

PURPOSE: In our prior work, conventional event\-related potential \(ERP\) responses to audiovisual versus auditory\-only speech were found to explain variance in language abilities in autistic and non\-autistic children but failed to differentiate autistic children from non\-autistic children\. This study extends previous ERP analyses of responses to audiovisual versus auditory\-only speech in autistic and non\-autistic school\-aged children to investigate group and condition differences in the strength and topography of the ERP based on data\-driven, reference\-independent metrics that overcome limitations of conventional ERP analyses\. METHODS: Extant ERP data from 50 5\-12\-year\-old children \(25 per group, matched at the group level on age and sex assigned at birth\) recorded during a passive task of listening and watching videos of auditory\-only and audiovisual speech syllables were analyzed for differences in ERP strength and topography, quantified via global field power \(GFP\) and global map dissimilarity \(GMD\), respectively\. RESULTS: GFP was significantly reduced for audiovisual compared to auditory\-only speech at 245\-275 ms post\-stimulus\. Groups differed in GMD of the ERPs reflecting the difference in response to audiovisual versus auditory\-only speech at 139\-170 ms post\-stimulus, wherein microstates suggested predominantly differential occipital electrode responses\. CONCLUSIONS: The benefits of audiovisual integration manifest as a facilitation effect \(reduced signal strength of the ERP response to audiovisual versus auditory\-only speech\) across autistic and non\-autistic children\. Autistic children also display different patterns of activation underlying ERPs in response to audiovisual versus auditory\-only speech relative to non\-autistic children\. Topographic ERP analyses show promise for advancing our understanding of audiovisual speech processing in autism\.
