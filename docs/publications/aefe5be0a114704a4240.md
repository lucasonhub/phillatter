# Time\-series analysis of the impact of short\-term exposure to atmospheric pollutants on stroke risk among residents in Guiyang City, 2016–2021

**Autores:** Ling Zhang; Minmin Su; Shuangfei Zhai; Meiqin Jiang; Xuanhao Chen; Ruifang Liu; Gang Chen; 张建华

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Stroke is one of the leading causes of death among Chinese residents, and air pollution is a significant environmental risk factor\. Currently, research on the association between air pollution and stroke incidence remains limited in low\-pollution karst cities in Southwest China\. This study aims to evaluate the short\-term effects of major air pollutants on stroke incidence in Guiyang City\. Methods Daily data on stroke incidence, air pollutants, and meteorological conditions for permanent residents of Guiyang from 2016 to 2021 were collected\. A time\-series analysis was conducted using a generalized additive model \(GAM\) based on a quasi\-Poisson distribution\. Long\-term trends, meteorological factors, day\-of\-week effects, and holiday effects were controlled\. The relative risk \( RR \) and 95% CI for stroke incidence per 10 μg/m 3 increase in pollutant concentration \(CO: 1 mg/m 3 \) were assessed\. Single\-day and cumulative lag models were used to examine lag effects, and analyses were stratified by gender and age\. Results A total of 11,517 stroke cases were reported, with a daily average of 5\.25 cases\. Short\-term exposure to PM₂\.₅, PM₁₀, SO₂, NO₂, and O₃ \(per 10 μg/m 3 increase\) and CO \(per 1 mg/m 3 increase\) was positively associated with stroke risk\. The RRs \(95% CI \) at maximum lag were: PM₂\.₅ 1\.026 \(1\.005–1\.048\), PM₁₀ 1\.015 \(1\.001–1\.030\), SO₂ 1\.075 \(1\.016–1\.137\), NO₂ 1\.057 \(1\.017–1\.098\), O₃ 1\.013 \(1\.003–1\.023\), and CO 1\.346 \(1\.072–1\.691\)\. Women were more sensitive to all six pollutants, especially CO \(lag0 RR = 1\.548, 95% CI : 1\.116–2\.147\)\. Older adults \(≥65 years\) showed increased risk across all pollutants, while only NO₂ and PM₁₀ had statistically significant effects on those &lt;65 years\. Conclusion Even under low pollution levels, short\-term exposure to air pollutants is significantly associated with increased stroke risk with a lag effect, particularly among women and older adults\. Strengthening air quality management and protection for high\-risk populations is recommended to potentially reduce the burden of stroke\.
