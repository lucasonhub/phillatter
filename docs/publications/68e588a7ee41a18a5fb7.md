# Migration Characteristics and Mechanism of Active Components From Jieyu Anshen Granule in Different Insomnia Mouse Models

**Autores:** Shiqi Yan; Mengting Lu; Ping Sun; Run Wan; Yong Ge; Jia Yan; Yuhang Zhang; Mo Sun; Xiang Xu; Yaoqun Wu; Chenning Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

RATIONALE: Insomnia is a highly prevalent disorder with substantial health and socioeconomic impacts, for which traditional Chinese medicine is an important treatment option\. Jieyu Anshen Granule \(JYAS\-G\) shows excellent clinical efficacy in insomnia treatment, but its active components, in vivo migration characteristics and underlying mechanisms remain unclear\. METHODS: Two mouse insomnia models induced by p\-chlorophenylalanine \(PCPA, chemical method\) and sleep deprivation machine \(SDM, physical method\) were established respectively, and mice were administered with low and high doses of JYAS\-G, with open field test used to evaluate the pharmacodynamic effects\. UHPLC\-Q\-Orbitrap\-MS combined with GNPS was applied to systematically identify the in vitro components of JYAS\-G and its migration profiles in mouse plasma, liver, heart, prefrontal cortex, hippocampus, and hypothalamus\. RESULTS: The SDM model induced insomnia with manic\-like behaviors, which were significantly ameliorated by JYAS\-G, whereas the PCPA model produced no significant insomnia\-like phenotypes under the current conditions\. In total, 89 in vitro components and 43 blood\-absorbed components of JYAS\-G were identified, showing marked tissue\-specific distribution\. Network pharmacology and molecular docking identified core components, including furanogermenone and curcumenol, predicted to exert anti\-insomnia effects by targeting ADORA2A, GABRB3, and SLC6A4 and regulating the neuroactive ligand\-receptor interaction pathway\. CONCLUSIONS: This study characterizes the in vivo migration characteristics of JYAS\-G and provides insights into its multi\-target anti\-insomnia mechanism, while comparing the modeling differences between two common insomnia animal models\. These findings provide scientific evidence for the rational clinical application and further development of JYAS\-G\.
