# From health trackers to compasses: can wearables and apps deliver on their health prevention promise?

**Autores:** S\. M\. Goodday; Anna Goldenberg; Finale Doshi‐Velez; Stephen Henry Friend

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Despite long\-standing knowledge of the significant contribution of lifestyle factors in the development of chronic conditions, lifestyle preventive strategies have failed to lower chronic disease rates globally \[1,2\]\. Personal digital health technologies \(P\-DHTs\) that can be worn on the person \(wearables, smart devices and mobile phones\) can be used for self\-monitoring directly by the individual increasing health agency and access, with promise for transformation in health prevention\. While these tools offer granular, continuous data on behaviours, emotions, physiology and stress, their passive information often fails to translate into actionable insights due to noisy signals and lack of individual level context\. The P\-DHT field has scaled P\-DHT tools enormously over the past decade, but without scaling understanding\. This perspective highlights how P\-DHTs are currently shaping the health and wellness field as health trackers, and frames the opportunities and challenges in shifting these tools to the prevention field as ‘compasses’ where they are capable of making tangible changes in health outcomes\.
