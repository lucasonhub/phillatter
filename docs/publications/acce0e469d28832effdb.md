# Multi\-material 3D printing by laser powder bed fusion: a review

**Autores:** Bharat Kalia; Rupinder Singh; B\. Singh Pabla

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose This study aims to review multi\-material three\-dimensional \(3D\) printing by laser powder bed fusion \(LPBF\) process\. Advancements in the LPBF process for multi\-material 3D printing of different metallic alloys, focusing on material pre\-alloying and chamber refilling approaches to achieve functionally graded and multi\-layered compositions, have been presented\. Design/methodology/approach The methodology involved an in\-depth analysis of prominent research and peer\-reviewed publications related to multi\-material 3D printing using LPBF\. A database of the Web of Science from the previous 20 years was examined using VOSviewer software for bibliometric analysis to identify research gaps\. A networking diagram highlighting the research gap was generated using the study’s obtained relevance score\. A case study was conducted for a single\-hopper\-based LPBF process for multi\-material 3D printing of metals, exploring two novel approaches: material pre\-alloying and chamber refilling to control material composition and layering with a single hopper\. Findings A comprehensive review of multi\-material 3D printing by LPBF, followed by a case study, highlights the challenges and advances in single\-hopper\-based LPBF for multi\-material 3D printing of metallic components, focusing on innovative methods \(material pre\-alloying and chamber refill\) to enable compositional gradients and layered structures\. A functionally graded material \(FGM\) comprising 17–4 precipitate\-hardened \(PH\) stainless steel \(SS\) and Ni625 was successfully 3D printed using chamber refill and material pre\-alloying in a single\-hopper LPBF system\. However, a few limitations apply to multi\-material 3D printing using LPBF, including managing phase evolution, mechanical characteristics and material compatibility\. Integrating machine learning and AI\-driven process optimization can enhance predictive capabilities and facilitate compelling exploration of process parameters to achieve desired material performance and structural integrity\. Originality/value This study provided a comprehensive review of multi\-material 3D printing using the LPBF technique and discussed a novel single\-hopper multi\-material 3D printing approach as a case study\. This review offers insights into the maintenance and operation of single\-hopper LPBF machines as an operational resource for industry/ academia for multi\-material printing\.
