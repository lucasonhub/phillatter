# Presenteeism, health\-related productivity loss, burnout, quality of nursing care, and retention intention among nurses: a structural equation modeling study

**Autores:** Mengyun Peng; Jiqin Ma; Wenyu Shi; Suyan Chen; Z\. Zhang; Xiaoling Zhu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background The interrelationships between presenteeism, health\-related productivity loss, burnout, quality of nursing care, and retention intention remain insufficiently understood in nursing populations\. Objective This study examined the associations between presenteeism, health\-related productivity loss, burnout, quality of nursing care, and retention intention\. Methods A cross\-sectional survey was conducted involving 511 nurses\. Composite scores from the Work Productivity and Activity Impairment \(WPAI\) questionnaire, the Maslach Burnout Inventory \- Human Services Survey \(MBI\-HSS\), the Quality of Nursing Care Tool, and the Nurse Retention Intention Scale were analyzed using an observed\-composite path structural equation modeling \(SEM\) approach\. Indirect effects were estimated using 5,000 bootstrap resamples with percentile\-based 95% confidence intervals\. Results Presenteeism was positively associated with burnout \(β = 0\.262, p &lt; 0\.001\) and negatively associated with the quality of nursing care \(β = −0\.250, p &lt; 0\.001\) and retention intention \(β = −0\.334, p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Burnout was negatively associated with the quality of nursing care \(β = −0\.177, p &lt; 0\.001\) and retention intention \(β = −0\.179, p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Bootstrap analyses indicated indirect associations between presenteeism and productivity loss and both outcomes through burnout\. The model showed an acceptable but imperfect fit: χ 2 \(4\) = 13\.151, CFI = 0\.959, TLI = 0\.877, RMSEA = 0\.067, and SRMR = 0\.043\. Conclusion Higher presenteeism was statistically associated with lower retention intention and poorer nursing care quality, both directly and indirectly, through burnout\. Health\-related productivity loss showed indirect associations with both outcomes through burnout\.
