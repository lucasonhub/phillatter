# Understanding Smart Workplace Engagement among Women Returning from Maternity Leave: Technology Adoption as a Mediator and Work\-Life Balance as a Moderator

**Autores:** Shilpa Kulkarni; M\.M\. Munshi

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study looks at the factors influencing women IT employees who choose to work from the office after maternity leave in the context of Smart Workplace Engagement \(SWE\)\. Analysis of the influence of ATT, TA, SE, PBC, SN, and WLB is undertaken\. Based on the Theory of Planned Behavior, the study examined direct, indirect, and moderating effects of work\-life balance\. Three hundred and fifty women working in IT had their data analyzed using SmartPLS to check for validity, reliability, and structural connection\. Results reveal that subjective norms and environmental self\-efficacy are the most significant variables driving technology adoption, with selfefficacy partially mediating the link between SWE and technology adoption\. While perceived behavioral control does not predict engagement, attitude and work\-life balance do\. The need of balancing work and personal responsibilities is supported by moderation analysis, which also confirms that work\-life balance improves the attitude\-engagement connection\. The Importance–Performance Map Analysis suggests that increasing social support, self\-efficacy, and work\-life balance can optimize engagement outcomes\. These results provide IT organizations with practical implications for developing interventions that promote technology adoption, employee confidence, and the integration of work and life, finally leading to increased productivity, innovation, and employee satisfaction in smart workplace settings\.
