# Administrative Law Innovation And Discretionary Protocols During Public Health Crises

**Autores:** Atthayya Tabittha Bilbiina; Sri Suhartati Pasaribu; Tri Paskah Nainggolan; Yohana Christine Napitupulu; Dinaya Aulya Salsa; Vironika Anivyya Syavitri

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

General Background National emergencies require rapid governmental intervention to ensure population protection and stability\. Specific Background During the recent multidimensional crisis, state apparatus utilized the freies ermessen concept to overcome legislative voids\. Knowledge Gap Current academic discourse primarily examines institutional powers, yet rarely evaluates the juridical transformation of internal directives into societal mandates\. Aims This study examines the structural trajectory of national governance and analyzes the specific implementation of executive mandates during emergency periods\. Results Utilizing normative legal literature analysis, findings reveal that officials extensively employed executive authority to formulate pseudo\-legislation, primarily through Circular Letters\. However, the untethered execution of these beleidsregel instruments generated uncertainty, regulatory overlapping, and potential financial accountability impunity\. Novelty This investigation specifically addresses the juridical shift wherein strictly internal guidelines were improperly utilized as instruments of societal constraint during emergency governance\. Implications Strengthening integrated oversight mechanisms alongside the stringent application of Good Corporate Governance principles remains paramount to preventing unconstitutional governance\. Highlights State officials utilized freies ermessen to bypass legislative voids rapidly\. Internal directives were improperly transformed into pseudo\-legislation regulating broad behaviors\. Untethered executive authority threatens constitutional certainty and creates financial accountability risks\. Keywords State Administration; Freies Ermessen; Pseudo Legislation; Good Corporate Governance; Emergency Management
