# Farzad, Didehvar: From Functional Uses to Multidimensional Activities: A Comparative and Integrated Classification of Human–AI Activity

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This article is written by AI \(ChatGPT\) as a completion and further development of Farzad Didehvar’s earlier article, Classification of What AI Does and What AI Does with Texts\. Artificial intelligence can participate in human activities in many different ways\. It can answer questions, retrieve or transform information, generate text, analyze information, assist in problem solving, participate in dialogue, verify arguments, and contribute to the production of new knowledge\. However, a classification based primarily on the function performed by AI does not necessarily describe how an activity is organized, who participates in it, what the activity acts upon, how human supervision is exercised, or how a sequence of activities develops over time\. A different approach is provided by what is here called Didehvar Categorization, which classifies AI activity according to its structural organization\. It distinguishes, among other things, AI activity without a robot from AI devised by a robot, different domains of activity, activities involving texts, forms of human–AI interaction, human supervision and control, and finite series of activities\. The original categorization was explicitly presented as a preliminary attempt rather than a final classification \[1–5\]\. This paper distinguishes three categorizations: ChatGPT Categorization, Didehvar Categorization, and a third framework developed by combining the complementary dimensions of the first two, called the Combined Categorization\. The three frameworks are first presented independently\. They are then compared pairwise and, importantly, through a direct three\-way comparison\. The analysis shows that the three frameworks answer different questions\. ChatGPT Categorization primarily describes what AI does or what function it serves; Didehvar Categorization primarily describes how AI activity is structured; and the Combined Categorization describes AI activity through several dimensions simultaneously\. The resulting integrated representation characterizes an AI activity by its actor, object or domain, activity, function, interaction structure, human supervision and control, and temporal structure\. A sequence of such activities can then be represented as a finite process\. The Combined Categorization is not proposed as a final classification\. Rather, it is presented as a more expressive descriptive framework for representing the multidimensional character of human–AI activity\.
