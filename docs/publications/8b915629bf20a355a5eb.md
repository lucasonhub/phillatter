# Data\-Driven Strength Prediction and Techno\-Economic Optimization of SCBA–RHA Concrete Using Explainable AI

**Autores:** Rakhee Rangari; Dr\. Swati Agrawal; R\. R\. L\. Birali

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The decarbonization of concrete requires supplementary cementitious materials that simultaneously deliver mechanical performance, durability, resource efficiency and economic value\. This study upgrades an experimental sugarcane bagasse ash \(SCBA\)–rice husk ash \(RHA\) concrete investigation into an explainable artificial intelligence framework by combining Artificial Neural Network \(ANN\), extreme Gradient Boosting \(XGBoost\), and Shapley Additive explanations \(SHAP\)\. The experimental database contains 24 compressive\-strength observations obtained from six mixtures with 0–25% combined SCBA–RHA replacement and curing ages of 7, 14, 28 and 56 days\. The experimental results show that SR\-15, containing 15% combined SCBA–RHA replacement, achieved the best overall balance, with 56\-day compressive strength of 56\.3 MPa, flexural strength of 6\.21 MPa and split tensile strength of 4\.84 MPa\. Water absorption, chloride penetration and RCPT charge were reduced to 3\.71%, 11\.3 mm and 1268 C, respectively\. For the AI extension, leave\-one\-out cross\-validation was used because of the small experimental sample\. ANN achieved R²=0\.940, RMSE=2\.086 MPa and MAE=1\.522 MPa, whereas XGBoost achieved R²=0\.920, RMSE=2\.411 MPa and MAE=1\.867 MPa\. Exploratory SHAP analysis identified curing age as the dominant predictor, followed by replacement level\. An exploratory paired comparison between SR\-15 and the control across the four reported curing ages produced t=7\.44 and p=0\.005; however, this is not interpreted as specimen\-level inferential evidence because replicate\-level observations were unavailable\. The proposed framework therefore provides a transparent route from experimental characterization to prediction, explanation and sustainable mix\-design decision support\.
