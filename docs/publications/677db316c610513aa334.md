# Ornithonyssus bacoti \(tropical rat mite\): a critical review of biology, vector potential, and integrated management

**Autores:** Peng‐Wu Yin; Xian\-Guo Guo

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The tropical rat mite, Ornithonyssus bacoti \(Hirst, 1913\), is an obligate hematophagous gamasid mite of global veterinary, animal, and public health importance\. While traditionally recognized as a cause of rodent mite dermatitis in humans, its potential involvement in zoonotic pathogen transmission requires rigorous critical evaluation\. This paper provides a comprehensive review of research progress on O\. bacoti , synthesizing evidence retrieved from major databases regarding its taxonomy, morphological diagnostics, life history, ecology, pathogen associations, genetic and physiological features, and integrated management\. Morphologically, O\. bacoti is distinguished from sibling species \( O\. sylviarum and O\. bursa \) by its long central dorsal setae, narrow tapering dorsal shield, and specific sternal plate setation\. Under optimal laboratory conditions \(28 °C, 75% relative humidity\), its life cycle completes in 7–14 days, exhibiting arrhenotokous parthenogenesis\. Epidemiologically, O\. bacoti is a proven vector of Rickettsia akari \(the causative agent of rickettsialpox\) and serves as an intermediate host for the rodent filarial nematode Litomosoides sigmodontis \. However, its role in transmitting Hantaviruses, Bartonella species, and other pathogens remains largely restricted to molecular detection or laboratory vector competence models, lacking definitive proof of natural transmission to humans\. Furthermore, genomic insights remain preliminary, reliance on extrapolation from related poultry mites \( Dermanyssus gallinae \) must be carefully contextualized, and field resistance to pyrethroids warrants systematic monitoring\. Effective mitigation requires a One Health strategy integrating host reduction, environmental sanitation, targeted acaricide use, and active vector surveillance\.
