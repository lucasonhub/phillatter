# Ancient Continental Signatures Preserved in Eocene Intraplate Volcanism at the Conrad Rise, Indian Ocean

**Autores:** Hiroshi Sato; Kenji Horie; Mami Takehara; Yoshifumi Nogi

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

ABSTRACT Oceanic rises in the Indian Ocean are generally interpreted as products of mantle plume magmatism that developed on the oceanic lithosphere; however, evidence suggests the presence of inherited continental materials beneath select oceanic plateaus and rises\. We report zircon and titanite petrochronological data from Eocene intraplate basalt dredged from the Conrad Rise in the southern Indian Ocean\. Zircon cores yielded concordant Mesoproterozoic U–Pb ages, with a weighted mean 206 Pb/ 238 U age of 1052 ± 26 Ma, and exhibited oscillatory zoning, heavy rare earth element \(HREE\)‐enriched REE patterns, and high Th/U ratios \(&gt; 0\.1\), whereas the thin zircon rims yielded younger ages of ca\. 550 Ma and low Th/U ratios \(&lt; 0\.1\)\. Titanite yielded concordant Eocene ages \(39–44 Ma\) overlapping the previously reported 40 Ar– 39 Ar age of the host volcanic rock\. These data suggest the preservation of continental crustal materials beneath or within the Conrad Rise during Eocene intraplate volcanism\.
