# Durable partial response with low\-dose, intermittent nivolumab in combination with gemcitabine for high PD\-L1 metastatic triple\-negative breast cancer: a case report

**Autores:** Po Lin Ooi; Jamin Munchar Elias; Francis Yew Fu Tieng

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Immune checkpoint inhibitors \(ICIs\) have improved outcomes in selected patients with metastatic triple\-negative breast cancer \(mTNBC\), particularly those with high programed death\-ligand 1 \(PD\-L1\) expression\. However, optimal dosing strategies balancing efficacy, toxicity, and cost remain unclear\. We report the case of a 34\-year\-old woman with recurrent mTNBC and high PD\-L1 expression \(combined positive score, CPS ≥ 60; immunohistochemistry 22C3 pharmDx assay\)\. She achieved durable disease control over 22 months \(September 2024 to June 2026\) using a combination of gemcitabine maintenance chemotherapy and low\-dose intermittent nivolumab \(40 mg fixed dose, administered 3\-weekly\)\. This de\-escalated regimen was adopted due to early treatment toxicity and severe financial constraints\. The patient achieved a confirmed partial response by RECIST v1\.1 criteria \(48% reduction in target lesion size\), accompanied by manageable CTCAE Grade 2 hypothyroidism requiring oral levothyroxine replacement\. This case illustrates that in biomarker\-selected, PD\-L1\-high mTNBC, combination chemotherapy with low\-intensity PD\-1 blockade can maintain prolonged antitumor activity\. These observations support the working hypothesis of a minimum effective immune dose, warranting prospective evaluation in resource\-constrained clinical settings\.
