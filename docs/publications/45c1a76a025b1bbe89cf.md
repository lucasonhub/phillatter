# The Non\-Technical User as an LLM Threat Vector: Linguistic Strategies for Bypassing AI Safeguards and Implications for AI Security

**Autores:** Aleksandr V\. Sokolov; Gleb Sokolov

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The threat that ordinary non\-technical users pose to large language models \(LLMs\) still sits at the margins of AI\-security research\. These are people who work with AI assistants every day — at work, in study, in everyday life — yet hold no specialised background in information technology or machine learning\. A post\-laboratory survey of first\-year humanities undergraduates \(n=70\) returned an unexpectedly high figure: 95\.7% cleared all main protection levels of the Gandalf platform \(Lakera AI\) without stepping outside purely linguistic techniques\. These included sign\-form decomposition and formatting \(M=2\.00\), cross\-language code\-switching \(M=1\.29\), indirect semantic extraction \(20\.3% of open\-ended responses\), and pragmatic pressure\. We read these micro\-level data against the macro picture of the Gandalf\-RCT crowdsourcing experiment \(279,675 prompts\) and against academic work on filter evasion in image\-generation systems \(SurrogatePrompt, PC², DALL\-E 3, Shedevrum/Yandex\)\. The pattern runs through all of them\. From text LLMs to multimodal generators, the filter yields to the same moves, none of which require a line of code: a proper name is swapped for a physical description, direct reference for allegory or an adjacent associative term, the prompt language is switched, the grapheme is distorted\. A three\-tier analysis of LLM break\-resistance grounds the paper’s central claim — that rising everyday AI literacy produces a structural security deficit left uncovered by current surface\-level filtering\. Several implications for AI safety system design follow\.
