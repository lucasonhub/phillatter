## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper asks what remains after the heterogeneous phenomena grouped under the term “consciousness” are operationally reduced under matched\-control and learned benchmarking conditions\. Rather than treating consciousness as a single pre\-given property, the study decomposes candidate phenomena into operationally distinguishable functions and tests which differences remain non\-redundant after stronger controls are introduced\. The analysis combines matched\-control benchmarking with learned PyTorch benchmarks to compare whether putatively consciousness\-specific effects can be reproduced, substituted, or eliminated by alternative models under equivalent observational and task conditions\. Operational reduction is defined as successful only when a candidate distinction can be removed without measurable loss in prediction, discrimination, intervention, robustness, or downstream behavioral organization\. Residual ignition regions are then identified as configuration\-dependent domains in which previously reduced differences become non\-substitutable under changes in scale, memory, temporal integration, task structure, environmental coupling, or model capacity\. The paper therefore distinguishes phenomenological grouping from operational necessity and rejects both premature eliminationism and premature reification of consciousness\. The broader claim is that consciousness research should identify which residual differences survive increasingly strong empirical controls, rather than assuming in advance that all phenomena labeled “consciousness” share a single mechanism, essence, or irreducible property\. \(direct link \)
