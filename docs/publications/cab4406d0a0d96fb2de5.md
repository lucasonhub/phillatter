# Preliminary in vitro antibacterial activity of Globularia trichosantha root extract and associated changes in tet\(A\) and floR transcript abundance in a single Vibrio anguillarum isolate

**Autores:** Teoman Özgür Sökmen

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Antibiotic resistance among aquaculture pathogens has stimulated interest in plant\-derived antibacterial agents\. This preliminary in vitro study examined the antibacterial activity of a single\-batch methanolic root extract of Globularia trichosantha against a single Vibrio anguillarum isolate from diseased rainbow trout and described the relative transcript abundance of tet\(A\) and floR after extract exposure\. The derivatized extract was profiled by gas chromatography–mass spectrometry \(GC\-MS\); peak assignments are tentative because authentic reference standards were not used\. Disk diffusion \(three replicate plates per treatment\) used extract\-loaded blank disks \(2 mg extract per disk\), florfenicol \(30 µg per disk\) and enrofloxacin \(5 µg per disk\); in combined treatments the extract disk and the antibiotic disk were separate disks on the same plate\. Mean zone diameters \(including the 6 mm disk\) were 17\.0 mm for the extract and 12\.5 mm for enrofloxacin, whereas florfenicol alone and both combined treatments showed no measurable inhibition beyond the 6 mm antibiotic disk\. These results are presented descriptively\. Minimum inhibitory and bactericidal concentrations \(MIC/MBC\), checkerboard/fractional inhibitory concentration index \(FICI\) and time–kill assays were not performed, so the combined\-disk observations are not interpreted as evidence of an interaction between the extract and the antibiotics\. Reverse\-transcription quantitative PCR \(RT\-qPCR; 2 −ΔΔCt method \(Ct, threshold cycle\), 16S rRNA as the only reference gene, primer efficiencies not determined\) was performed on three parallel replicate cultures per group derived from the single isolate\. Relative to the vehicle control, tet\(A\) and floR showed markedly lower relative transcript abundance at ≈1,000 µg mL \- ¹ extract \(0\.24 and 0\.19\), intermediate values at ≈500 µg mL \- ¹ \(0\.58 and 0\.71\) and values close to the control at ≈250 µg mL \- ¹ \(1\.11 and 1\.06\)\. No inferential test was applied, and because viability was not measured concurrently, lower transcript abundance cannot be distinguished from reduced viability, growth or RNA yield\. The findings are limited to one isolate, one extract batch and the conditions tested; MIC/MBC, interaction testing, viability\-controlled expression analysis and multi\-isolate validation are required before any application in aquaculture can be considered\.
