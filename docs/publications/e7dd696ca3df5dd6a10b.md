# Investigation of algorithmic facilitation vs\. self\-led learning in complex skill development for operational training

**Autores:** Esther J\. Putman; Kylie Haynie; Joi Harvey; Chad C\. Tossell; Torin K\. Clark; Allison Paige Anderson

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Adaptive\-difficulty algorithms can automatically modulate task difficulty to improve skill acquisition of complex tasks\. Using virtual reality \(VR\) enables training in an immersive way without requiring an in\-person training facilitator or complex training facilities\. However, it is not known whether an adaptive\-difficulty algorithmic facilitation can outperform highly\-motivated individuals facilitating their own training by self\-selecting the difficulty level based on their own preferences\. This study enrolled individuals \(n = 45\) with specialized aviation education to train using VR and then perform a spacecraft entry, descent, and landing task\. Subjects were randomly assigned into one of three training groups: algorithm\-facilitated condition, self\-led condition, and static difficulty condition\. Subjects trained for 30 trials \(i\.e\., landings\) in immersive VR, followed by 5 evaluation trials\. Task performance, difficulty level, and crashes, as well as subjective reports of flow and motivation were collected\. Despite training at a lower difficulty level, on average, than the self\-led group \(p = 0\.0006\) the algorithm\-facilitated group had improved task performance compared to the static group \(p = 0\.004\) and marginally significance compared to the self\-led group \(p = 0\.097\)\. Further, the self\-led group reported lower confidence, as a subscale within flow \(p = 0\.048\), and decreased flow late in training \(p = 0\.02\) compared to the algorithm\-facilitated group\. The results of this study inform the utility and efficacy of algorithmic facilitation in learning complex tasks\.
