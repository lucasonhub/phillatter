# A temporal graph dissimilarity measure capturing epidemic spreading behavior

**Autores:** Salvatore Freni; Lorenzo Dall&\#x27;Amico; Francesca Pistilli; Alain Barrat; Tatiana Tommasi; Ciro Cattuto

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Comparing temporal networks is a challenging problem in network analysis\. Commonly adopted methods compare structural properties of temporal graphs and can be highly sensitive to the pre\-selected features\. However, temporal networks are the support of dynamical processes, and a process may take place in statistically indistinguishable ways on structurally different temporal graphs\. In particular, a dissimilarity measure that compares temporal graphs based on how spreading processes unfold on them is highly desirable\. In this work, we first define the Time\-Shift DTW, a dissimilarity between epidemic curves that accounts for both differences in their shape and temporal shifts\. We then introduce Epidemic\-Aware Temporal Graph Dissimilarity \(EATGD\), a deterministic and differentiable dissimilarity between temporal graphs that combines an embedding\-based temporal graph distance with a set of topological and temporal features\. Its parameters are calibrated using stochastic SIR simulations to maximize the correlation with the Time\-Shift DTW, after which graph comparisons require no further epidemic simulations\. Using empirical human proximity networks from the SocioPatterns collaboration and synthetic temporal graphs, we show that EATGD captures epidemic\-based graph dissimilarity and generalizes across heterogeneous network settings\. Its differentiability and computational efficiency make EATGD suitable for integration into machine\-learning pipelines and provide a potential loss function for training generative models to reproduce epidemic dynamics without requiring an exact reconstruction of fine\-grained network structure\.
