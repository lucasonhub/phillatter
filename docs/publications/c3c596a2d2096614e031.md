# Communication as a catalyst: strengthening literacy through teacher–parent partnerships

**Autores:** Mologadi Lucia Maisela

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In rural inclusive education, communication between educators and parents transcends logistical necessity; it serves as a transformative catalyst for learners’ literacy advancement\. In Foundation Phase classrooms, where learners rely significantly on both home and school contexts, successful communication serves as a conduit for continuous learning support\. This article examines the crucial role of communication in promoting literacy among learners with diverse abilities through effective partnerships between educators and parents in inclusive rural schools\. Drawing on Epstein&\#x27;s theory, it highlights the importance of trust and mutual respect in fostering collaborative relationships\. The study employed a qualitative methodology, including focus group interviews with fifteen Foundation Phase educators and nine parents, to examine the challenges and strategies for effective communication\. Findings indicate that consistent, culturally responsive communication has a significant impact on parental involvement and literacy outcomes\. The article concludes with recommendations for policy and practice to strengthen teacher−parent partnerships in rural inclusive education\. The study enhances the conversation on inclusive education by providing policy and practice recommendations to promote teacher training in culturally responsive communication, adaptable engagement tactics, and collaborative school−community collaborations to enhance literacy in the foundation phase\. The study redefines communication as a relational, context\-dependent practice, offering a paradigm to enhance literacy through inclusive, collaborative methods in rural education\.
