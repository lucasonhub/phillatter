# Rethinking immunotherapy sequencing in multiple myeloma: a conceptual adaptive immunotherapy sequencing model

**Autores:** Aliaa Khalili

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Chimeric antigen receptor T\-cell \(CAR\-T\) therapy and bispecific antibodies \(BsAbs\) have transformed treatment for relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma \(RRMM\)\. Contemporary guidelines increasingly incorporate prior antigen\-directed exposure, disease kinetics, patient fitness, toxicity, access, and patient preference\. However, a prospectively validated framework that jointly operationalizes immune fitness, tumor antigen status, and treatment feasibility for sequencing T\-cell–redirecting therapies is not available\. Main body This Perspective introduces the Adaptive Immunotherapy Sequencing Model \(AISM\), an author\-proposed framework for research and multidisciplinary discussion\. AISM proposes that sequencing should be studied in relation to two evolving biological domains: the capacity of the host T\-cell compartment to support therapy and the probability that the relevant tumor antigen remains therapeutically actionable\. The model uses provisional 0–10 research constructs for T\-cell fitness and antigen\-escape risk to make these domains explicit\. AISM hypothesizes that, for eligible patients with early RRMM and feasible access, referral for BCMA\-directed CAR\-T before prolonged exposure to T\-cell–redirecting BsAbs may preserve a potentially favorable treatment option\. It also recognizes that off\-the\-shelf BsAbs may be necessary when disease tempo, frailty, access, or patient preference make CAR\-T impracticable\. After prior BCMA\-directed therapy, an antigen\-switching approach is biologically plausible, but antigen loss is not universal and other mechanisms of resistance must be considered\. Conclusions AISM provides a testable framework for prospective comparative studies\. Such studies should evaluate whether biologically informed sequencing improves progression\-free survival \(PFS\), while also assessing overall survival, toxicity, infections, treatment\-free time, feasibility, and patient\-reported outcomes\.
