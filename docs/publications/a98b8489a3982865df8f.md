## Descrição do feed

\_AI and Society\_:1\-16\. forthcomingThe diffusion of generative AI \(GenAI\) and large language models \(LLMs\) is reshaping knowledge production, labor organization, institutional decision\-making, and public governance\. Existing research has identified ethical principles and governance challenges, but often treats functional redistribution, human–AI cooperation, alignment, adaptation, and accountability as separate concerns\. This article introduces the 3$$\\times$$2A Framework as a profile\-based interpretive approach for coordinating these concerns in situated AI\-mediated institutional configurations\. It examines six interdependent axes organized into three diagnostic dyads: automation–augmentation, alliance–alignment, and adaptation–accountability\. Rather than reducing AI governance to static compliance or technical optimization, the framework treats human–AI interaction as dynamic, context\-sensitive, and institutionally situated\. Its primary output is the six\-axis 3$$\\times$$2A profile, which reveals functional, relational, adaptive, and accountability\-related tensions\. The article clarifies the epistemic status of the framework’s outputs\. Qualitative profile bands are ordered, evidence\-informed interpretive categories rather than numerical or cardinal scores\. Numerical scoring, where methodologically justified, requires explicit rubrics, evidence standards, scoring rationales, and interpretive limits\. The 3$$\\times$$2A quotient may therefore be used only as a secondary and conditional synthesis of an established profile, subject to explicit activity–axis judgments, evidential bases, weighting assumptions, aggregation rules, and interpretive limitations\. A worked benchmark re\-analysis of the publicly documented Province of Fryslân environmental\-monitoring case illustrates procedural use without claiming empirical validation\. The case is represented through an ordinal qualitative six\-axis profile, without numerical scoring or a composite quotient\. The framework thus offers a profile\-first, evidence\-sensitive, and epistemically modest approach to reflective and accountable human–AI governance\. \(direct link \)
