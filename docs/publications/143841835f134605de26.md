# A multi\-domain Shannon\-entropy descriptor vector for integers up to 106

**Autores:** Naveen Joy; Aparna Rajan; Lijin Varghese; Aswathi Balakrishnan; Akhila Sheela; Ivin Shajan; Muhammed Shahul; Rahul Singh; Sonet Daniel Thomas; Rajesh Raju

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Classical number theory has developed many descriptors for integers, including predicates, arithmetic functions, and structural invariants\. The majority of them capture a specific aspect of an integer rather than providing a unified representation across multiple mathematical domains\. We introduce the Universal Entropy Fingerprint Framework \(UEFT\), a computational framework for representing integers through multiple Shannon entropy coordinates derived from selected mathematical transformations across five complementary domains: algebraic \(prime\-exponent structure\), geometric \(divisor\-lattice rank distribution\), arithmetic \(σ\-weighted divisor magnitude\), positional \(multi\-base digit distribution\), and dynamical \(Collatz trajectory parity\)\. Every coordinate is computed using a single Shannon entropy definition, avoiding the ad hoc mixing of incompatible information measures that has limited previous integer\-invariant proposals\. We compute the full fingerprint for all integers up to N = 1,000,000 using sieve\-based factorization and closed\-form combinatorial identities, eliminating the need to explicitly construct divisor graphs or partition objects\. The resulting 19\-coordinate fingerprint space requires 17 of 19 standardized coordinates to reach 95% cumulative variance under Principal Component Analysis \(PCA\); this is partly driven by the 15 near\-collinear positional coordinates\. Exemplar integers spanning primes, prime powers, highly composite, and perfect numbers exhibit distinct fingerprint profiles, illustrating how classical arithmetic categories are represented within a continuous information\-geometric space\.
