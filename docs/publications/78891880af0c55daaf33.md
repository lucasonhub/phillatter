# Divergent Failure Modes of Two Intrusion Detection Benchmarks Under A Common Audit

**Autores:** Arya K S; Selvavinayaki

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Two public corpora carry much of the empirical load in machine learning research on intrusion detection for wireless sensor networks and for industrial internet of things deployments\. Accuracy on both is routinely reported near the ceiling\. We audit both with one procedure, holding features, models, metrics and seeds fixed, and they fail in different ways\. On Edge\-IIoTset the binary task is settled before any model is fitted\. Four string valued fields record how the capture was serialised rather than how the traffic behaved, because the absent value was written as one token on benign rows and a different token on attack rows, and a depth one decision stump on any of them reaches 1\.0000 where the majority baseline is 0\.8417\. All seven learners in the bank reach that ceiling\. Removing the four fields separates them again\. On WSN\-DS the same audit returns almost nothing, and that is the second result\. Discarding 8,873 duplicate records moves the bank average equal error rate by 0\.0000, and a node disjoint split that shares no sensor with training moves it by 0\.0005, against a spread of 0\.0158 across the models themselves\. A third check finds what neither corpus level audit does\. With one attack family withheld from training, detection of the withheld behaviour runs from 0\.007 to 0\.886\. These checks are cheap, and which of them bites is not predictable in advance\.
