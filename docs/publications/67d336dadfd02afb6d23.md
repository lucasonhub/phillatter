# Groundwater potential zoning in the Grande River Basin: A semi\-arid region of Northeast Brazil

**Autores:** Thyago Anthony Soares Lima; Paulo de Tarso Amorim Castro

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Seasonal water scarcity in western Bahia, Brazil, has led to increased dependence on groundwater resources; however, basin\-scale hydrogeological measurements remain insufficient for preliminary assessments\. This study presents a geographic information system\-based groundwater potential map for the Grande River Hydrographic Basin \(GRHB\), where groundwater potential is defined as a relative index reflecting conditions favorable for recharge and subsurface storage, rather than a direct measure of aquifer productivity\. Ten hydrogeological and terrain proxy layers, derived from existing datasets and remote sensing, were standardized and integrated using an analytic hierarchy process weighting scheme and a weighted linear combination\. The resulting index was classified into five groundwater potential categories\. The very high\- and high\-potential classes account for 48\.08 and 25\.41% of the basin, respectively, while the moderate class accounts for 19\.90%, and the low\- and very low\-potential classes together comprise 6\.59%\. Spatial association with available well locations was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic analysis and the area under the curve, which yielded a value of 0\.821, indicating that wells are more frequently located in areas with higher index values\. Additionally, a groundwater abstraction\-pressure proxy, derived from wells with reported daily abstraction volumes, was used to identify potential stress areas where high favorability coincides with concentrated abstraction activities\. These outputs serve as a first\-order screening tool to guide field verification, monitoring, and targeted data acquisition across the basin\.
