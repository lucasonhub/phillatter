# Evaluating social cognitive remediation for labour participation in severe mental illness: insights from a small\-scale randomised controlled trial

**Autores:** Guillaume Barbalat; Julien Plasse; Marie‐Cécile Bralet; Sophie Cervello; Nicolas Franck B

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Employment rates remain critically low among individuals with severe mental illness\. Although social cognition explains a significant portion of the variance in functional outcomes, research into social cognitive interventions has remained scarce\. Aims To evaluate the impact of a social cognitive remediation \(SCR\) programme on functional and psychological outcomes over a 6\-month post\-intervention period\. Method This randomised controlled trial was conducted across three sheltered employment centres in France \(2020–2024; ClinicalTrials\.gov protocol record: 2019\-A00124\-53\)\. We evaluated the efficacy of a 12\-week SCR programme versus an active control with respect to the ratio of hours effectively worked versus those expected at 6 months post treatment \(primary outcome\)\. Secondary outcomes included patient\-reported measures of self\-esteem, internalised stigma, recovery and anxiety\. Intention\-to\-treat and per\-protocol effects were estimated using targeted minimum loss\-based estimation, with attrition accounted for through inverse probability of censoring weights\. Results Of the 105 randomised participants \(SCR: n = 53; control: n = 52; mean age: 35\.6 \[s\.d\. = 8\.8\] years; 71% male\), 39 dropped out, yielding a per\-protocol sample of 33 participants per arm\. Attrition was 28% immediately after the intervention and 37% at 6\-month follow\-up\. SCR was associated with a 6\.5% increase in the ratio of hours worked versus those expected in the intention\-to\-treat analysis and a 3\.6% increase in the per\-protocol analysis \(both P &lt; 0\.05\)\. SCR also reduced internalised stigma at 6 months post treatment \( P &lt; 0\.03\)\. Conclusions Our findings suggest that a 3\-month SCR programme improves employment maintenance and reduces internalised stigma in individuals with severe mental illness\. Future research should use larger samples and improve retention to confirm these findings\.
