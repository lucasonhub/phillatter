# A Benchmark of Unsupervised Anomaly Detectors for Credit Card Fraud under Two Training Protocols

**Autores:** Ruyu Guo

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Credit card fraud detection is a critical problem in financial security, yet real\-world deployment scenarios often lack labeled fraud samples at initial stages, making unsupervised anomaly detection an appealing approach\. However, the comparative performance of different unsupervised detectors under varying training data compositions—whether trained exclusively on normal transactions or on mixed data containing unlabeled frauds—remains insufficiently studied\. This paper presents a head\-to\-head benchmark of five unsupervised anomaly detectors—Mahalanobis distance, Isolation Forest, Local Outlier Factor \(LOF\), One\-Class SVM, and Elliptic Envelope—on the publicly available ULB Credit Card Fraud Detection dataset\. Each detector is evaluated under two training protocols: novelty detection \(trained solely on normal transactions\) and outlier detection \(trained on the full dataset containing both normal and fraudulent samples\)\. Performance is measured using PR\-AUC, Precision@k, and recall at low false positive rates\. Results show that Mahalanobis distance and LOF achieve strong performance under the novelty detection protocol \(PR\-AUC &gt; 0\.73\) but suffer catastrophic degradation when trained on mixed data, with PR\-AUC dropping by 0\.57 and 0\.72 respectively\. Isolation Forest gives solid and reliable results on both two tested protocols, and its PR\-AUC score increases slightly from 0\.3953 to 0\.4432\. After parameter adjustment \(with parameters set as support\_fraction=0\.9, contamination=0\.01\), Elliptic Envelope gets moderate performance \(PR\-AUC≈0\.37\) and is almost insensible to protocol changes\. The conclusions obtained in this part can provide actual instruction for choosing unsupervised detectors to be used in real fraud detection systems working under various conditions of label availability and data quality\.
