# Reconsidering pathways to global shipping decarbonization: idealism vs\. realism

**Autores:** Wenwen Li; Yizhou Sun; 胡正良 Hu Zhengliang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction The 2023 IMO Strategy on Reduction of GHG Emissions from Ships and its 2025 Draft Net\-Zero Framework exhibit evident features of idealism\. These instruments have not fully considered vessels’ long\-term asset cycles, technological bottlenecks for zero\-carbon marine fuels, cross\-country disparities in shipping development, and safety constraints related to green maritime infrastructure\. Methods This study integrates legal realism, resource dependence theory, and securitization theory into a climate\-realism framework\. It uses doctrinal legal analysis, qualitative policy evaluation, and a Norway–China comparative case study\. Results The limitations of the IMO Draft Net\-Zero Framework are dissected across four dimensions: transition costs, technological maturity, carbon\-pricing fairness, and infrastructure security\. The Norway–China comparison shows that a one\-size\-fits\-all aggressive pathway cannot reconcile industry survival with climate governance goals, whereas a gradual, inclusive, and technology\-neutral route is more sustainable\. Discussion To reconcile idealism with reality, global shipping decarbonization governance should establish a dynamic adjustment mechanism matched to technological iteration, implement differentiated carbon pricing, and build a transnational risk\-sharing system for green infrastructure\. This would balance development rights and maritime economic security while steadily promoting shipping decarbonization\.
