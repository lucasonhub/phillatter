# T cell–driven venetoclax resistance in chronic lymphocytic leukemia: in vivo preclinical activity of opaganib

**Autores:** Valeria Judith Sarapura Martínez; Paula S\. Pérez; Anita Ng; Yu\-Ju Chen; Kelly Wong; Chiara Cassarino; Rosario Custidiano; Carolina Valeria Mahuad; Miguel Arturo Pavlovsky; Raimundo Fernando Bezares; Martin Bertini; Mercedes Borge; Monica Elba Vermeulen; Pablo Elías Morande; Mirta Giordano; Matías Ostrowski; Nicholas Chiorazzi; Shih‐Shih Chen; Romina Gamberale

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Venetoclax has transformed the treatment of chronic lymphocytic leukemia \(CLL\), yet therapeutic resistance remains a major clinical challenge\. In addition to leukemia cell\-intrinsic mechanisms, interactions with the immune microenvironment can profoundly reduce venetoclax sensitivity\. We previously identified sphingosine kinase 2 \(SPHK2\) as a therapeutic vulnerability underlying T\-cell\-supported venetoclax resistance\. Here, we investigated the contribution of CD4 \+ and CD8 \+ T cell subsets and their extracellular vesicles \(EVs\) to this resistant phenotype and evaluated the therapeutic activity of the SPHK2 inhibitor opaganib in vivo \. Methods We evaluated the role of activated CD4 \+ and CD8 \+ T cells and their EVs in the development of the venetoclax\-resistant phenotype in primary CLL cells\. The in vivo activity of opaganib was assessed in a xenograft model generated by co\-engraftment of primary CLL cells with autologous activated T cells\. Mice were treated with venetoclax, opaganib, or their combination, and leukemic burden and the T cell compartment were evaluated\. Anti\-apoptotic BCL\-2 family proteins were also assessed in residual leukemic cells\. Results Activated CD4 \+ , but not CD8 \+ , T cells induced leukemic cell activation and venetoclax resistance, and CD4 \+ T cell\-derived EVs were sufficient to reproduce this phenotype\. In vivo , venetoclax monotherapy failed to reduce leukemic burden, confirming that adaptive interactions with the immune microenvironment are sufficient to sustain therapeutic resistance\. Although opaganib alone showed minimal antileukemic activity, its combination with venetoclax reduced splenic leukemic burden by approximately 50% and remodeled the tumor\-associated T\-cell compartment, without consistent modulation of anti\-apoptotic BCL\-2 family proteins in residual leukemic cells\. Discussion These findings are consistent with the notion that the therapeutic effects of opaganib extend beyond leukemic\-cell\-intrinsic mechanisms and may include modulation of the immune microenvironment\. Our study shows that opaganib reduces microenvironment\-driven venetoclax resistance in a preclinical CLL model in vivo , supporting its clinical evaluation in combination with BCL\-2 inhibition\.
