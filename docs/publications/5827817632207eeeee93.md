# Radiation\-induced xerostomia in nasopharyngeal carcinoma survivors: psychosocial burden, survivorship implications, and the potential role of malic acid

**Autores:** Cong Wang; Jiajie Xu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Radiation\-induced xerostomia is a common late effect of radiotherapy for nasopharyngeal carcinoma \(NPC\)\. It can impair oral comfort, eating, swallowing, speech, and sleep, with adverse consequences for oral health\-related quality of life \(OHRQoL\)\. Xerostomia, the subjective sensation of oral dryness, should be distinguished from objectively measured salivary gland hypofunction, although the two frequently coexist\. This structured narrative review examines the clinical and psychosocial burden, pathophysiology, assessment, and management of radiation\-induced xerostomia in NPC survivors\. It also evaluates malic acid as a candidate topical sialogogue\. The literature indicates that assessment should combine patient\-reported measures, clinical examination, and unstimulated and stimulated salivary flow measurements\. Management requires a multidisciplinary framework that distinguishes prevention or preservation of gland function from treatment of established hypofunction and symptomatic relief\. Short\-term studies of low\-concentration malic acid formulations in non\-cancer xerostomia populations have reported improvements in perceived dryness or salivary flow, but their relevance to irradiated NPC survivors remains uncertain and long\-term dental safety is insufficiently characterised\. NPC\-specific trials should define eligibility, formulation, dose, and treatment duration and prespecify patient\-centred outcomes and oral\-safety monitoring\.
