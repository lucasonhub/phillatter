# Safe\-by\-Design and Circular\-by\-Design Antimicrobial Bionanocomposites: Bridging Laboratory Innovation and Industrial Adoption

**Autores:** Sylvere Ndikumana

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose This project synthesises and quantifies the evidence on antimicrobial bionanocomposites for food packaging, framed by two design principles: Safe\-by\-Design \(SbD\) and Circular\-by\-Design \(CbD\)\. Its purpose is to determine whether laboratory performance reported for these materials can translate into safe, circular, industrially viable packaging, and to specify the conditions under which that translation is achievable\. Design and scope PRISMA 2020 systematic review\. Four databases \(Web of Science, Scopus, PubMed, Google Scholar\), January 2018 – June 2026\. Three\-block Boolean strategy \(packaging/matrix; active agents and green synthesis; circularity and safety\)\. Dual independent screening \(Cohen&\#x27;s κ = 0\.87 title/abstract; 0\.91 full text\)\. 3,248 records identified; 1,187 duplicates removed; 2,061 screened; 527 full texts assessed; 279 excluded; 248 studies retained for qualitative synthesis; 112 studies \(360 datasets\) entered the weighted meta\-analysis\. All 248 retained DOIs manually verified via doi\.org and CrossRef\. All 248 records carry a complete quality appraisal across eight materials\-specific criteria \(i–viii\)\. Evidence base 248 retained records: original research with extractable quantitative data prioritised, complemented by Q1/Q2 peer\-reviewed reviews\. 360 datasets pooled across five metrics: WVTR \(94\), OTR \(75\), tensile strength \(85\), Zn migration \(59\), Ag migration \(47\)\. 18 high\-reliability core sources \(Supplementary Table S3\), of which 7 are sugarcane\-bagasse\-specific originals, 3 are migration/safety/SbD critical, 4 are TEA/LCA/circularity\. Safety and regulatory mapping across four jurisdictions \(EU, US, China, Japan\)\. Quantitative findings Three\-level random\-effects models \(REML; Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman; metafor ≥4\.8\-0\), with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction and Egger/trim\-and\-fill bias assessment\. WVTR: 45\.2 g·m⁻²·day⁻¹ \(95% CI 35\.1–55\.3\); I² = 76% OTR: 25\.1 cm³·m⁻²·day⁻¹ \(18\.2–32\.0\); I² = 72% Tensile strength: 40\.3 MPa \(32\.1–48\.5\); I² = 82% Zn migration: 1\.21 mg·kg⁻¹ \(0\.82–1\.60\) — below the EU SML of 5 mg·kg⁻¹ Ag migration: 0\.48 mg·kg⁻¹ \(0\.21–0\.75\); trim\-and\-fill 0\.38 \(0\.15–0\.61\) — above the EFSA guidance value of 0\.05 mg·kg⁻¹ Nanocellulose reinforcement is the strongest positive moderator of tensile strength \(β = \+22\.1, p &lt; 0\.001\); nanoparticle type, matrix and green synthesis are significant WVTR moderators\. Sensitivity analyses excluding high\-risk studies shift point estimates by less than 10%\. Safety and regulatory findings ZnO/Zn²⁺ migration is predominantly ionic and generally compliant, though high\-loading formulations approach the SML\. Ag migration is the critical risk: pooled means are \~7\.6–9\.6× the EFSA guidance value; every Ag subgroup exceeds it, with loading as the clearest driver\. EU, US, Chinese and Japanese frameworks diverge on nano\-specific authorisation; no specific migration limits exist for organic nanomaterials \(nanocellulose, nanochitin\)\. Chronic toxicity, ecotoxicity and end\-of\-life nanoparticle fate data remain incomplete\. Circularity and techno\-economics Advanced bionanocomposites cost 6–20 USD·kg⁻¹ versus 2–6 USD·kg⁻¹ for conventional controlled\-release films, at TRL 3–5\. Nanocellulose minimum selling price is 14\.6–16\.5 USD·kg⁻¹ at pilot scale, falling to 3\.4–7\.5 USD·kg⁻¹ at 1,000–2,000 t·yr⁻¹, with a 2030 target of ≤2\.8–4\.7 USD·kg⁻¹\. Payback depends on food\-waste savings exceeding the 2–4 USD·kg⁻¹ premium\. Agricultural\-residue feedstocks \(notably sugarcane bagasse\) provide the strongest circularity potential, but composting behaviour and nanoparticle fate are poorly characterised\. Expected outcomes A quantified, bias\-corrected evidence base linking matrix, agent, architecture and processing to barrier, mechanical, antimicrobial and migration performance\. A regulatory\-grade minimum data package for nano\-enabled antimicrobial films: worst\-case migration in real foods and simulants, released\-form speciation \(ion, particle, aggregate\), chronic and ecotoxicity data, and end\-of\-life fate\. An integrated SbD–CbD decision framework coupling hazard/exposure/validation loops with feedstock, end\-of\-life, LCA and business\-model decisions, using &quot;days of safe shelf life&quot; as functional unit\. A four\-pillar 2026–2035 roadmap \(material innovation, process scale\-up, regulatory alignment, circular design and business models\) with quantitative milestones: ≤2\.8 USD·kg⁻¹ nanocellulose and TRL 6–7 by 2030; ≤8 USD·kg⁻¹ finished films, ≥30% shelf\-life extension and net GHG reduction by 2035\. A FAIR structure–property–performance dataset to support machine\-learning\-assisted design\. Limitations High heterogeneity \(I² 65–82%\) and non\-standardised test protocols; English\-language restriction; provisional single\-pass quality appraisal, with dual independent full\-text re\-appraisal in progress; meta\-analysis N discrepancy \(112 in the manuscript versus 116 S2 flags; 4 borderline method/TEA records noted\); one retained EFSA technical report \(No\. 145, Usmani et al\. 2024\) held under D–HOLD justification\. Alignment SDG 2 \(zero hunger\), SDG 12 \(responsible consumption and production\), SDG 13 \(climate action\)\.
