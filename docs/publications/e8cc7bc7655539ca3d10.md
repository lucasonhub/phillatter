# A Text Book of Machine Learning

**Autores:** Dinesh Bhagwan Hanchate; Varsha Kiran Bhosale; Pooja Mahadev Pondkule

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** book

## Resumo

Machine learning has gone from being a niche field to being a vital part of AI and data science and has also found application in fields like health care and business\. A Text Book of Machine Learning is designed for undergraduate and postgraduate students, and for teachers and early career practitioners, for those who wish to have a clear, rigorous and practical introduction to machine learning algorithms and applications\. The language is kept simple to ensure readers from varied backgrounds can follow the treatment, while the theory is treated rigorously and faithfully to ensure that machine learning can be trusted as a good scientific discipline\. The book is organised into five units\. The foundations are established in unit 1: basic definitions, supervised learning, unsupervised learning and reinforcement learning, space of hypotheses and inductive bias, evaluation of the model and cross validation, linear regression, decision trees, overfitting, learning based on instances: the method of the customers k\-nearest neighbors, reduction of dimensions: the principal component analysis, systems of recommendation: collaborative filtering\. Unit 2 builds probabilistic ML: in addition to various probabilistic tools such as Bayes, MLE, naive Bayes, BN, logistic regression, regularization, SVM, and kernel methods with kernel trick\. The unit also traces the evolution of the perceptrons to deep learning, along with multilayer networks, activation functions, back\-propagation, gradient descent, convolutional networks, recurrent networks, attention, transformer, foundation models and LLMs that have been setting the tone for the field\. Unit 4 introduces you to the computational learning theory such as PAC learning, sample complexity and VC dimension, along with ensemble learning, bagging, random forests, boosting and AdaBoost\. Unit 5 moves on to cluster analysis, including k\-means clustering, hierarchical clustering, Gaussian mixture models, and the expectation\-maximization algorithm; anomaly detection and learned embeddings also are discussed\. Each unit features three themes\. The first topic is generalization: bias \- variance, overfitting and model selection are discussed repeatedly as they appear: the only real measure of a model is how it is performing on data that has not been yet seen\. Second, credible and accountable AI: explainable machine learning \(XAI\), fairness, private, robust, federated learning and uncertainty quantification \(including conformal prediction\) are presented alongside the classical algorithms, instead of as a footnote\. Third, link between classic and current practice: At the end of each chapter, full sections provide future trends and directions, illustrating ways by which the classic and current pattern recognition and data mining practices continue to inspire big data analytics and deep learning efforts\. To facilitate active learning, there is a chapter opening abstract, relevant learning objectives \(where applicable\), and a chapter ending conclusion, review questions and practice activities\. Worked illustrations, comparison tables and figures elucidate the behavior of algorithms and explain when they go awry and how to know which to use for real\-world data sets\. Readers are encouraged to try out different methods, to repeat them on the same data and to challenge each result on which they rely\. I hope this book will become a reliable machine learning textbook for both courses and self\-study or setting up research programs\. Any student starting out in machine learning for the first time will find a soft entry into the field, and any teacher trying to structure the material into a semester\-length course will find that it is easy to get right\. Readers&\#x27; comments and suggestions are being eagerly invited and will be used in future editions to enhance them\.
