# Biology\-aware association for identity\-consistent leaf tracking in top\-down plant phenotyping

**Autores:** Dongze Zhou; Cong Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Reliable leaf\-level trait trajectories require identity\-consistent tracking across growth, overlap, and appearance change\. We introduce a biology\-aware association module combining one appearance cue and three biological/geometric cues: spatial continuity, area\-growth plausibility, and lifecycle plausibility\. On CanolaTrack, adaptive weighting reduces ID switches from 228 to 126 ± 5 \(44\.7%; per\-plant paired p = 1\.6 × 10 −5 , n = 37\) and improves HOTA from 88\.17 to 89\.69 ± 0\.10\. Difficulty\-stratified analysis shows IDSW reductions across all 12 CanolaTrack strata, averaging 40\.0% versus Appearance\-only and 45\.0% versus LeafTrackNet\. External association\-only Arabidopsis and KOMATSUNA tests identify clean low\-displacement GT\-box regimes where centroid matching or Fixed bio\-constraints are more reliable than zero\-shot adaptive\-weight transfer\. These results provide an operating\-envelope recommendation: adaptive weighting is most useful in dense CanolaTrack\-like sequences, whereas Fixed bio\-constraints or centroid matching are more reliable in clean low\-displacement external regimes—where nearest\-centroid assignment already resolves most associations, so that even a predictor retrained on the target domain does not surpass it\. The limiting factor is thus primarily the association difficulty of the regime rather than dataset size\.
