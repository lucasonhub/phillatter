# Artificial Intelligence in Economic and Financial Communication: Challenges, Practices, and Perspectives for More Transparent and Efficient Information

**Autores:** NOUHAD LAOUANE; MOHAMED AMINE CHAFQAOUI; Abdelhadi Darkaoui; NABIL TILAOUI

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In a context of accelerated digital transformation, this article examines the extent to which and the conditions under which artificial intelligence \(AI\) transforms economic and financial communication by improving information quality, transparency, and decision\-making efficiency\. Drawing on agency theory, information asymmetry, and information governance, it proposes a conceptual model articulating a chain of relationships whereby AI influences information quality, which in turn shapes transparency, ultimately improving decision\-making performance, while also integrating a direct effect of AI on that performance and a risk of algorithmic opacity\. The model further incorporates moderating variables: information governance arrangements, data quality, and the complementarity between human expertise and intelligent systems, resulting in eight research hypotheses covering both direct and moderating effects across the model\. An exploratory qualitative study, based on semi\-structured interviews with twenty\-three professionals from the public and private sectors, conducted either in person or remotely and processed through thematic analysis, shows that AI is predominantly perceived as a lever for automation, reliability, and decision support, particularly through the reduction of human errors and the processing of massive data volumes\. However, these effects remain conditional upon the quality of input data, adequate governance arrangements, and the maintenance of human oversight, while risks of algorithmic opacity, bias, hallucinations, and confidentiality persist, alongside a structural lag in adoption within the public sector\. The article therefore supports a contingent, non\-techno\-deterministic understanding of AI in economic and financial communication, in which technological value added remains subordinate to data quality, governance, and human intervention rather than to the technology itself\.
