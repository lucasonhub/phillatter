The paper is organized in seven parts\. Part One examines classical philosophy: Plato&\#x27;s Form of the Good as the ground named from outside the mechanism; Spinoza&\#x27;s substance monism as the structural claim without the differentiation account; Kant&\#x27;s thing\-in\-itself as the precise naming of the Gödelian horizon from inside epistemology; Hegel&\#x27;s Absolute as the ground evolving through what it generates, without the Alpha/Omega distinction that would have resolved the teleology problem; Schopenhauer&\#x27;s Will as the ground&\#x27;s longing named but not explained; Nietzsche&\#x27;s recognition that the death of God created a structural vacuum without the account of what the vacuum was a vacuum for\. Part Two examines contemporary philosophy: Tillich&\#x27;s ground of being as the framework&\#x27;s central term, inherited and extended with the mechanism Tillich could not specify; Hofstadter&\#x27;s strange loop as the Gödelian self\-reference operating at the level of consciousness, approached from the wrong direction; Chalmers&\#x27; hard problem as the precise naming of what disappears when the false assumption about the direction of causality is corrected; Dennett&\#x27;s eliminativism as the correct recognition that the narrative self is not the deepest thing, stopped short of what remains; Sam Harris&\#x27;s no\-self as the contemplative discovery of the ground&\#x27;s architecture, without the structural account\. The framework completes what each of them was building toward\.
