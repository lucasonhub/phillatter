# Cefepime‐Zidebactam: Leveraging β‐Lactam Enhancement Against Difficult‐to‐Treat and Metallo‐β‐Lactamase‐Producing Gram‐Negative Pathogens

**Autores:** Sean R\. Van Helden; Adesanya A\. Akinleye; Ashlan J\. Kunz Coyne; Amer El Ghali; Niharika Thota; Michael Joseph Rybak

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Carbapenem resistance among Gram\-negative bacteria remains a serious public health threat, with metallo\-β\-lactamase \(MBL\)\-producing organisms increasing substantially in prevalence and few therapeutic options available to adequately address these difficult\-to\-treat pathogens\. Cefepime\-zidebactam \(FEP\-ZID\) is a novel β\-lactam/β\-lactam enhancer combination that offers a mechanistically distinct approach to overcoming resistance in Gram\-negative organisms\. Zidebactam contributes three complementary mechanisms: inhibition of Class A and C β\-lactamases, direct antibacterial activity via PBP2 inhibition, and enhancement of cefepime activity through a reduction in the pharmacodynamic exposure required for bactericidal effect, allowing FEP\-ZID to achieve substantial reductions in bacterial burden even in isolates with elevated minimum inhibitory concentrations \(MICs\)\. FEP\-ZID was recently approved in the United States for the treatment of complicated urinary tract infection \(cUTI\), including acute pyelonephritis, caused by Enterobacterales and/or Pseudomonas aeruginosa\. FEP\-ZID demonstrates broad in vitro and in vivo activity against isolates producing Class A, B, C, and D β\-lactamases, as well as organisms harboring PBP3 alterations that are otherwise associated with reduced susceptibility to currently available MBL\-active therapies\. This spectrum extends across carbapenem\-resistant Enterobacterales, P\. aeruginosa, and Acinetobacter baumannii, and includes activity against isolates with porin loss and efflux upregulation, resistance mechanisms that commonly limit the utility of other β\-lactam\-based agents\. In vivo infection models utilizing humanized regimens have demonstrated bactericidal activity against isolates with FEP\-ZID MICs up to 64 μg/mL\. The Phase 3 ENHANCE I trial demonstrated noninferiority and subsequent superiority over meropenem in cUTI \(including acute pyelonephritis\), driven primarily by microbiological response, with favorable outcomes observed across many baseline pathogens, including extended\-spectrum β\-lactamase\-producing isolates and patients with concomitant bacteremia\. Compassionate use experience further suggests possible utility in severe multidrug\-resistant infections across multiple infection types, including bloodstream, osteoarticular, and intra\-abdominal infections caused by MBL\-producing organisms\. Collectively, available preclinical and clinical evidence positions FEP\-ZID as a mechanistically distinct option for difficult\-to\-treat Gram\-negative infections, with additional clinical study needed to define its role beyond the currently approved indication\.
