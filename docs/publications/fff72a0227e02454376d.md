# Neoadjuvant therapy for hepatocellular carcinoma: Evolving multimodal strategies and unresolved clinical challenges

**Autores:** Yi Zhou; Yan Guan; Zhi\-Yong Huang; Zun\-Yi Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Hepatocellular carcinoma \(HCC\) is associated with a high risk of postoperative recurrence despite advances in surgical and locoregional treatment\.Neoadjuvant therapy \(NAT\) has recently gained increasing interest as a strategy to improve resectability, reduce micrometastatic disease, and improve long\-term oncological outcomes\.This review summarizes current evidence for NAT in HCC, including transarterial chemoembolization, hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy, with a focus on multimodal strategies combining locoregional and systemic approaches\.Encouraging pathological response and conversion\-toresection rates have been reported, but these observations derive largely from selected patients within early\-phase, single\-arm, or retrospective studies, and apparent improvements in recurrence control should be interpreted in that context\.Key clinical challenges remain, including optimal treatment duration, surgical timing, response assessment, and patient selection\.Randomized perioperative evidence has only recently begun to emerge and remains limited to a small number of regimens and selected patient populations; in some cases it is available only as meeting abstracts\.A survival benefit therefore remains unestablished for most neoadjuvant strategies, and reported improvements in resection, pathological response, and recurrence control should be regarded as regimen\-and population\-dependent rather than as evidence of a generalizable benefit\.Standardized treatment protocols, validated response endpoints, and adequately powered randomized trials are required\.
