# Actual Infinity, God, and the Limits of Formal Verification: A Gödelian Critique of Mathematical Reductionism

**Autores:** Sameer Al\-Khawaja

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Debates over actual infinity sometimes compare its acceptance with belief in God on the grounds of finite verifiability and observability\. Taking Doron Zeilberger’s ultrafinitist position as a point of departure, this article distinguishes a local methodological restriction on what mathematics should admit from a global verificationist claim about what may count as real, meaningful, or worthy of rational inquiry\. It argues that the second claim does not follow from the first and should not be attributed to Zeilberger without independent evidence\. Gödel’s incompleteness theorems are examined under their specific technical conditions to clarify the limits of provability within consistent, effectively axiomatizable systems sufficiently strong to express arithmetic\. These results neither refute finitism nor establish the existence of actual infinity or God; rather, they show why mathematical truth in the standard interpretation cannot be identified with provability within any fixed system of the relevant kind\. The discussion then draws on scientific realism, theoretical indispensability, and interventionist entity realism to show that direct observability is not a universal prerequisite for justified ontological commitment in science, without transferring scientific standards uncritically to metaphysics\. The article concludes that the infinity–God analogy is not inherently a category mistake\. A category mistake arises more specifically when rules governing admissibility within a mathematical framework are treated as though they determined the meaning, truth, or epistemic legitimacy of metaphysical claims\. Thus, finitist restrictions may remain coherent within mathematics while leaving substantive questions about God open to philosophical examination\.
