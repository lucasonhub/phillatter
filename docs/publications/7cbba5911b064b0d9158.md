# Case Report: staged segment\-specific open and endovascular treatment of chronic post\-dissection aortic disease with descending thoracic and giant infrarenal aneurysmal components

**Autores:** Azat Chinaliyev; Mukhammejan Orazbay; Gulinur Chinaliyeva; Didar Khassenov; Tokan Sultanaliyev; Irlan Sagandykov; Aktolkyn Daumen

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Contemporary management of chronic post\-dissection aortic disease includes open repair, thoracic endovascular aortic repair \(TEVAR\) with or without adjunctive false\-lumen occlusion, fenestrated/branched endovascular repair, and staged open\-endovascular strategies\. Selection depends on anatomy, rupture risk, malperfusion, physiological reserve, and device availability\. Case presentation A 56\-year\-old woman with body mass index 47\.2 kg/m 2 and major cardiometabolic comorbidity presented with progressive abdominal pulsation\. CTA demonstrated chronic Stanford type B/DeBakey III dissection with descending thoracic aneurysmal degeneration to 52\.7 mm, a dominant symptomatic infrarenal aneurysmal component measuring approximately 11–12 cm, bilateral common iliac involvement, and residual paravisceral dissection without treatment\-triggering enlargement or malperfusion\. No Crawford extent was assigned because complete longitudinal centerline imaging was unavailable\. Stage I comprised transperitoneal open aorto\-bi\-iliac reconstruction\. After 118 days, elective morphology\-driven zone 2 TEVAR was performed to cover the reported proximal entry region and reduce antegrade false\-lumen inflow\. Intentional left subclavian artery \(LSA\) coverage without prophylactic revascularization was followed by symptomatic subclavian steal requiring unplanned carotid\-subclavian bypass\. Results No perioperative death, stroke, spinal cord ischemia, bowel ischemia, myocardial infarction, acute kidney injury, graft thrombosis, prosthetic infection, or access complication occurred\. At latest follow\-up, the patient was asymptomatic and all reconstructions were patent\. Conclusion This case illustrates competing priorities in segment\-specific management of chronic post\-dissection aortic disease\. The unequivocal indication for urgent infrarenal repair contrasted with discretionary pre\-emptive thoracic intervention below the conventional diameter threshold\. The LSA\-related complication emphasizes prophylactic LSA preservation/revascularization, spinal collateral planning, and lifelong surveillance of the untreated paravisceral segment after previous abdominal aortic reconstruction\.
