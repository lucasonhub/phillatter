# Randomized Momentum Transport with Cheap Predictors

**Autores:** El Mahdi Chayti; Martin Jaggi

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Momentum methods such as STORM and second\-order momentum track the gradient by adding a transport correction to momentum, at the cost of an extra oracle call per iteration\. We introduce randomized momentum transport, which evaluates the correction with probability $q$ and otherwise uses a cheap prediction, with importance weighting that preserves the conditional mean and adds $\(q^\{\-1\}\-1\)$ times the prediction loss to the variance\. Under transport\-bias, second\-moment, and residual\-control assumptions, linear minimization oracle \(LMO\) updates, including normalized, sign, and exact spectral \(Muon\) directions, retain an $O\(T^\{\-1/3\}\)$ stationarity bound for each fixed $q&gt;0$ with a single\-gradient initialization\. For unnormalized STORM, some $q&lt;1$ lowers the leading coefficient of our sufficient gradient budget if and only if the gradient\-difference noise modulus exceeds the smoothness constant, by at most about $10\\%$\. An online diagonal predictor admits a regret\-to\-convergence guarantee\. At approximately equal oracle cost, querying a quarter of the corrections lowers the final training loss by $18$ to $43\\%$ on MNIST1D and raises the test accuracy of Hessian\-vector transport on CIFAR\-10 by $1\.5$ points\.
