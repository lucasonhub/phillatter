# Configuration\-dependent predictors of methane variation in forage systems

**Autores:** Ibrahim Osman; 侯向阳 HOU Xiangyang

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Predicting enteric methane \(CH₄\) emissions from forage\-based ruminant systems is challenging because nutrient\-methane relationships may vary across forage and experimental contexts\. This study evaluated whether forage nutritional characteristics provide transferable predictive information across heterogeneous multi\-study datasets\. We analyzed 244 observations from 22 independent studies using eight nutritional predictors: dry matter digestibility \(DMD\), acid detergent lignin \(ADL\), acid detergent fibre \(ADF\), crude protein \(CP\), neutral detergent fibre \(NDF\), CP: NDF ratio, lignification index, and fibre: protein ratio\. Six machine\-learning approaches were evaluated using leave\-one\-study\-out cross\-validation\. Feature importance, pure\-versus\-mixture comparisons, nutrient\-methane correlations, and sensitivity analyses were used to examine configuration\-dependent relationships\. A Proportion\-Weighted Study Ensemble \(PWSE\) analysis was additionally conducted as a secondary evaluation of configuration\-aware representation\. Cross\-study prediction remained challenging across all models\. Random Forest provided the strongest performance \(R² = \-0\.375; RMSE = 29\.88 mL/g DM; MAE = 23\.34 mL/g DM\), followed by Gradient Boosting \(R² = \-0\.389; RMSE = 30\.02 mL/g DM\)\. DMD was the dominant predictive feature, with a mean importance of 0\.626 across the tree\-based models\. However, its relationship with methane was strongly configuration\-dependent: DMD was positively correlated with methane in pure forage systems \( r = 0\.684, p &lt; 0\.001\) but negatively correlated in mixture systems \( r = \-0\.809, p &lt; 0\.001\), with the difference between correlations statistically distinguishable by Fisher’s r\-to\-z analysis \( p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Mixture systems had higher crude protein and lower ADL than pure systems, and methane emissions were also higher in mixture systems \(51\.39 vs\. 43\.61 mL/g DM; p = 0\.033\)\. PWSE showed weak predictive performance and substantial sensitivity to study concentration\. Methane variation is better characterized by configuration\-dependent nutritional relationships than by isolated nutrient effects\. The contrasting DMD\-CH 4 relationships demonstrate that the predictive meaning of a nutritional trait can change across forage contexts\. These findings support context\-aware machine learning, study\-level validation, and improved evidence representation in future methane modelling\.
