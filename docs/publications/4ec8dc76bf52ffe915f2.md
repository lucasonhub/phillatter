# Djatah, Stenly: The Ethical Value of Students’ Use of Artificial Intelligence Through the Lens of Jeremy Bentham’s Hedonic Calculus

## Descrição do feed

\_Seminar Nasional Sanata Dharma Berbagi 2026\_:40\. 2026This research examines how students produce ethical values through the use of Artificial Intelligence \(AI\) in academic assignments, using Jeremy Bentham’s seven Hedonic Calculus indicators: Intensity, Duration, Certainty, Propinquity, Fecundity, Purity, and Extent\. The research asks how students evaluate the ethical consequences of AI use in academic work\. A descriptive\-analytical method was employed through a survey of 81 active students\. The findings show that 72\.5% of respondents perceived AI use as beneficial in terms of Intensity, while 62\.5% expressed confidence in its benefits regarding Certainty\. For Fecundity, 55% believed AI use could generate further benefits\. Only 35% perceived long\-term benefits \(Duration\), whereas 65\.1% perceived immediate benefits \(Propinquity\)\. Regarding Purity, only 38\.7% believed that AI use would not produce negative consequences\. For Extent, 68\.7% recognized that AI use could affect other parties\. Regarding Ethical Judgment, 46\.3% considered AI use in academic assignments ethically justifiable, while 35% remained uncertain\. The findings indicate that students’ ethical evaluations tend toward consequentialist vaues, emphasizing perceived benefits while remaining uncertain about long\-term consequences and potential negative effects\. Keywords: Hedonic Calculus, Artificial Intelligence, Ethics, Bentham \(direct link \)
