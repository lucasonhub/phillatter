# Pharmaceutical Sales and Marketing: A review

**Autores:** Appasaheb Kuhile Darshan Gaikwad\*

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Healthcare has historically relied on population\-based, generalized treatment protocols, often leading to trial\-and\-error prescribing, delayed diagnoses, and variable treatment outcomes across individuals\. A digital twin in healthcare is defined as a dynamic, continuously evolving virtual representation of an individual patient, organ, physiological system, or disease process\. It is constructed through the integration of multi\-modal biomedical data, including genomic and multi\-omics profiles, electronic health records \(HER\), medical imaging \(MRI, CT, ultrasound\), real\-time physiological signals from wearable and IoT sensors, and behavioral/lifestyle data\. Methodology/Approach: This study/review synthesizes current literature and technological frameworks underlying healthcare digital twins, examining the convergence of enabling technologies \- artificial intelligence, machine learning, cloud and edge computing, IoT\-based sensing, and mechanistic physiological modeling \- that make real\-time, patient\-specific simulation computationally feasible\. Significance: Digital twins represent a fundamental shift from reactive, population\-averaged medicine toward predictive, preventive, and individualized care \- operationalizing the core principle of precision medicine: the right treatment, for the right patient, at the right time\. Challenges: Despite this potential, several barriers constrain widespread clinical adoption, including fragmented and non\-interoperable health data infrastructure, difficulties in model validation and clinical\-grade accuracy, data privacy and cybersecurity risks, unresolved questions of data ownership and informed consent, algorithmic bias affecting model fairness across diverse populations, and the substantial computational resources required for high\-fidelity\.
