# Digital marketing and social network effects in new energy vehicle diffusion: evolutionary dynamics in a dual\-layer heterogeneous network

**Autores:** Wanting Zhao; De Xia; Yundong Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In the context of the carbon peaking and carbon neutrality strategy and the booming digital economy, the diffusion of new energy vehicles \(NEVs\) has become an important pathway for industrial green transformation\. This study develops a dual\-layer heterogeneous network evolutionary game model that integrates supply\-side firms and demand\-side consumers\. Based on this model, we examine the effects of multiple government policies, digital marketing mechanisms, social network effects, underlying physical constraints, and network structure, as well as selected interactions among these mechanisms, on NEV diffusion\. The findings reveal several critical insights\. First, NEV diffusion exhibits a pronounced bimodal steady\-state pattern and strong dependence on initial adoption conditions\. It shows an S\-shaped relationship with the initial consumer adoption base\. Second, government policy transmission is asymmetric across the dual\-layer network\. Under standardized proportional policy changes, consumer purchase subsidies have a stronger effect on diffusion than technology transition subsidies and carbon prices\. Third, digital marketing and social network effects are conditional on the prevailing market structure\. When internal combustion engine vehicles initially hold an advantage, stronger marketing utility conversion, local bandwagon effects, and hub node influence can reinforce the incumbent position, whereas information overload weakens this exposure advantage\. Fourth, charging anxiety and perceived technological risk constrain sustained NEV diffusion, while purchase subsidies can only partly compensate for severe charging constraints\. Selected joint analyses and network robustness tests further show that diffusion outcomes depend on interactions among policy, behavioral, and physical mechanisms, while consumer\-network connectivity and topology affect quantitative diffusion outcomes without overturning the main directional relationships\. These findings highlight the importance of initial market conditions, cross\-layer feedback, and network structure in shaping NEV diffusion and provide implications for policy design and firm strategy\.
