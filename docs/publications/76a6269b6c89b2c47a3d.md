# Building proportion of stadium: an approach based on physiologically equivalent temperature in the context of hot and humid climate

**Autores:** Beta Paramita; Hamidie Ronald Daniel Ray; Pipit Pitriani; Andreas Matzarakis

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Designing stadiums with partially covered roofs presents a challenge for achieving outdoor thermal comfort for football players\. This study aims to identify preferred stadium building proportions for outdoor thermal comfort based on the physiologically equivalent temperature \(PET\), considering local climatic conditions and football players&\#x27; physiological characteristics\. The study was conducted for a proposed stadium in Bandung, Indonesia, using annual climatic data including air temperature \(Ta\), relative humidity \(RH\), wind velocity \(v\), and global radiation\. Three football match schedules, 4 p\.m\., 7 p\.m\., and 10 p\.m\., were examined using RayMan Pro\. Eight stadium building proportions were then simulated using Autodesk Flow Design by varying the building width, length, height, and roof\-opening configuration\. The results indicate that building height and roof\-opening configuration influence wind conditions within the stadium and, consequently, affect PET\. The analysis also shows that the thermal comfort conditions differ between afternoon and evening match schedules due to variations in solar radiation and wind conditions\. Based on a combined assessment of PET and wind velocity, several building proportions were identified as preferable under the simulated climatic and physiological conditions in Bandung\. These findings provide a climate\-responsive design approach for considering stadium building proportions and outdoor thermal comfort for football players in comparable conditions\.
