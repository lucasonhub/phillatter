# Academic stress and its associations with the perceived academic outcomes of pharmacy students: a cross\-sectional study

**Autores:** Eman S\. Allahyani; Mohammad Y\. Dilyh; Abdullah A\. Flimban; Abdulmajeed A\. Alsulimani; Moayad A\. Naqshabandi; Abdullah S\. Alamri; Mohammed A\. Almasri; Anmar M\. Khafaji; Mohab M\. Mathkor; Rafal Z\. Felemban; Alaa Shahbar; Saad M\. Wali

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Academic stress may affect pharmacy students&\#x27; academic performance and confidence\. This study examined academic stress, coping behaviours, and perceived academic outcomes among pharmacy students at Umm Al\-Qura University \(UQU\)\. Methods A cross\-sectional study included 200 pharmacy students at UQU\. A self\-administered questionnaire assessed sociodemographic characteristics, academic stress, coping strategies, and perceived academic outcomes\. Multivariable regression analyses examined adjusted associations with stress, self\-perceived academic performance, and confidence\. Results Senior students \(Years 5–6\) reported significantly higher stress \( β = 4\.29, p = 0\.001\), while higher GPA \(3\.50–4\.00\) was associated with lower stress \( β = −2\.74, p = 0\.022\)\. Common stressors included exam anxiety \(66\.0%\), future career concerns \(56\.0%\), sleep deprivation \(53\.0%\), and heavy workload \(50\.0%\)\. Physical exercise and seeking social support were most common \(41\.0% and 35\.5%, respectively\)\. Higher average hours of study per week \(20 h/week or more\) were positively associated with better academic performance \(OR=5\.52, p = 0\.015\) and greater academic confidence \(OR=2\.04, p = 0\.037\), whereas other sociodemographic characteristics and stress scores were not significantly associated with performance or confidence\. Conclusion Academic stress was particularly evident among senior pharmacy students\. Greater weekly study time was associated with favourable self\-perceived academic performance, while studying 5–10 h/week was associated with greater academic confidence\. Academic stress was not significantly associated with either perceived outcome after adjustment\. Prospective studies using validated and objective academic measures are warranted\.
