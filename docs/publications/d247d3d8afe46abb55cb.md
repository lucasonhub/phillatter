# Solid Lipid Nanoparticles and Nanostructured Lipid Carriers in Drug Delivery: A Review

**Autores:** Sandip Kumar; Dr\. Bipul Nath; Bahunshisha Kharsahnoh; Meker Shyrmang; Ruwa O\. Padit

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Many pharmacological compounds suffer from poor aqueous solubility, variable bioavailability, fast degradation, unfavorable biodistribution and dose\-related side effects, which restrict their therapeutic efficacy\. Conventional dosage forms may not achieve the appropriate drug concentrations within the therapeutic window, necessitating the development of improved delivery platforms\. Lipid\-based nanocarriers, especially solid lipid nanoparticles \(SLNs\) and nanostructured lipid carriers \(NLCs\), provide intriguing methods owing to their biocompatibility, biodegradability and formulation diversity\. SLNs are composed of a largely solid lipid matrix supported by surfactants, while NLCs are a mixture of solid and liquid lipids that form a less ordered matrix with more drug accommodation and less drug ejection during storage\. This review critically discusses recent advances, with emphasis on literature published during 2021–2026, on the evolution, classification, structural characteristics, formulation components, preparation and characterization, drug\-loading and release mechanisms, biological fate, surface engineering, administration routes, and therapeutic applications of SLNs and NLCs\. Special attention is paid to safety, regulatory issues, scale\-up, commercialization and emerging technologies including artificial intelligence\-assisted optimization and microfluidic production\. \[There has been\] considerable preclinical progress but little clinical translation\. Long term stability, reproducible large scale manufacturing, regulatory uniformity, and rigorous clinical validation are significant difficulties that need to be systematically investigated\.
