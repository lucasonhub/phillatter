# Berlin, Gary L\.: Toward a Unified Mind Theory: Reflexive Self\-Modeling as a Criterion of Mind

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

The sciences of mind study memory, perception, inference, and learning without an agreed account of what makes a system a mind at all\. The usual appeal to consciousness ties that question to the harder phenomenal one\. This paper separates them and proposes a criterion at the level of running processes rather than mental states: a mind is an occurrent process capable of reflexive self\-modeling, wherein the assessment and regulation of its own self\-modeling are part of the very operation they condition\. Separable monitoring supplies an output that the relevant operation consumes; constitutive self\-modeling is among the activities that operation consists in\. Only the latter meets the criterion\. This is a difference in kind, not in degree, and the resulting organization is what the paper calls the fold\. Nothing is faintly minded: a system either instantiates the fold or does not\. The criterion specifies no necessary substrate, body, survival stake, or persistence beyond the running\. It also individuates: one fold is one mind, and reciprocal constitutive closure excludes nested minds without excluding nested mechanisms\. Whether a system instantiates the fold is open to third\-person investigation\. A further claim, that consciousness is the same process from its own standpoint, is presented separately as a wager on which the criterion does not depend\.
