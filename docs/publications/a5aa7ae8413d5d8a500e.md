# Needs and current status of generative artificial intelligence use among college students: a cross\-sectional survey based in China

**Autores:** Rong Zeng; 雷春涛; Qian Jiang; Xueqin Gan; Xiaoguo Zhang; Yu Feng; Qin Xu; Yan Huang; Yang Qian

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective This study aimed to investigate the current status of Generative Artificial Intelligence \(GAI\) usage needs among vocational college students from China, explore their relationships with learning engagement and academic resilience, and identify variables associated with GAI usage demand\. Methods Using convenience sampling, 3,876 students from five vocational colleges in Sichuan Province were enrolled\. Surveys were conducted using the Generative Artificial Intelligence Application Status and Usage Demand Scale, the Learning Engagement Scale, and the Academic Resilience Scale\. Results The average GAI needs score among vocational college students was 58\.23 ± 12\.77\. Correlation analysis revealed positive relationships between GAI needs and both learning engagement and academic resilience \( p &lt; 0\.01\)\. Multiple linear regression analysis revealed that gender, academic year, availability of GAI learning devices at home, level of GAI understanding, enrollment in GAI\-related courses, frequency of active GAI use, student\-reported frequency of teachers’ use of GAI in teaching, learning engagement, and academic resilience were independently associated with GAI usage demand \(all p &lt; 0\.05\)\. The model explained 24\.4% of the variance in GAI usage demand \[ R 2 = 0\.244, adjusted R 2 = 0\.241, F \(14, 3,861\) = 88\.932, p &lt; 0\.001\]\. Conclusion Vocational college students’ GAI usage demand was slightly above the theoretical midpoint of the scale\. Greater GAI usage demand was independently associated with male gender, earlier academic year, better availability of GAI learning devices at home, a higher level of GAI understanding, enrollment in GAI\-related courses, more frequent active GAI use, more frequent student\-reported teachers’ use of GAI in teaching, higher learning engagement, and greater academic resilience\. Because of the cross\-sectional design, these findings should be interpreted as associations rather than causal relationships\.
