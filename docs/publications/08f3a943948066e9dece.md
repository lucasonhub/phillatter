# Appetite Loss, Inflammation, and Incident Functional Loss Among Community‐Dwelling Older Adults

**Autores:** Germine Soliman; Xiaomin Lu; Emil Coman; Zeyu Hu; Berno Diniz; Anne B\. Newman; Eleanor Marie Simonsick; Shanshan Yao; George A\. Kuchel; Lisa C\. Barry

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: Aging is associated with both reduced appetite and increased inflammation\. Whether and how these factors contribute to or exacerbate functional decline in older adults is unclear\. METHODS: Data from 3073 men and women aged 70\-79 years participating in the Health, Aging, and Body Composition \(Health ABC\) with reported appetite \(good or poor\) and inflammatory markers \(Interleukin \(IL\)\-6, tumor necrosis factor \(TNF\)\-α and C\-reactive protein \(CRP\)\) at baseline were followed for up to 6 years to determine if they developed Persistent Lower Extremity Limitation \(PLL\), defined as two consecutive reports of difficulty walking a quarter mile and/or climbing 10 steps without resting 6 months apart, using Fine\-Gray Proportional hazards models adjusted for demographics, chronic diseases, anxiety, depression, medications, and sensory factors\. RESULTS: A total of 388 \(12\.6%\) participants reported impaired appetite\. These individuals were mainly female \(59%\), Black \(54%\), with a mean age of 73\.9 \(±3\) years\. Those with impaired appetite vs\. good appetite were more likely to develop PLL \(64% vs\. 47%; p &lt; 0\.001\) and had higher IL\-6, TNF\-α, and CRP levels \(p &lt; 0\.001 for each\)\. Cumulative incidence curves showed that 40% and 20% of those with impaired and good appetite, respectively, developed PLL by 18 months\. Subjects with impaired appetite experienced a higher risk of developing PLL \(hazard ratio, adjusted = 1\.44; 95% CI = 1\.2, 1\.7\), as did those with elevated inflammatory markers\. Cumulative incidence curves indicated that individuals with impaired appetite and elevated CRP, IL\-6, and TNF\-α experienced the fastest onset of functional loss, reaching 50% at around 12 months, and nearly all had mobility limitations by 32 months\. CONCLUSION: Impaired appetite and increased inflammatory biomarkers are independently linked to mobility loss\. Appetite impairment might signal early nutritional and supportive care needs, but inflammation\-targeted interventions require further study\.
