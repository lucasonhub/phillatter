# Assessing digital technology use for clinical learning in undergraduate health professional education in Ethiopia

**Autores:** Marema Jebessa Kumsa; Berhanu Abera Kibret; Tefera Tadesse

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background\. The use of digital technologies in clinical placement training is becoming increasingly significant in global health education\. However, limited research has been conducted on the use of digital technology in clinical placement training in low\-income countries\. Objective\. To assess the use of digital technology in the workplace by undergraduate health professional students throughout their clinical placements\. Methods\. A multi\-centre, cross\-sectional survey study was carried out from May to September 2025 at three randomly selected universities in Ethiopia\. Data were collected from 335 undergraduate students in the fields of medicine, nursing and radiological technology using a structured questionnaire on demographic information, frequency of use of digital technologies in the workplace, the challenges encountered in using them, and their experiences with their use\. Descriptive statistics, independent sample t\-tests, and one\-way analysis of variance \(ANOVA\) were used for analysis\. Results\. Undergraduate health professional students used digital technology extensively in their clinical placement studies\. In clinical settings, 86\.3% of students reported that they use digital technology to learn, and 67\.8% said they use it frequently\. These students use digital technology mainly to access educational material for their clinical practice\. Despite this high use, students face several barriers, including lack of internet access and uncertainty about how supervisors, patients and caregivers will react to their use of digital technologies\. The use of digital technology during clinical placement raises questions about the possibility of learning impairment because of overdependence and distraction\. Conclusion\. Undergraduate students in the health professions make extensive use of digital technologies, mainly for accessing educational resources\. While this shows the great usefulness of digital tools and their acceptance by students, there are many practical barriers to their successful use in clinical placements, such as unreliable internet access\. In addition, the use of these technologies raises questions about how they are perceived by supervisors, patients and caregivers, as well as potential concerns about learning loss owing to distraction and extreme reliance on digital resources\. Addressing infrastructure bottlenecks and providing clear guidance on how to use digital technologies optimally in clinical education is crucial\.
