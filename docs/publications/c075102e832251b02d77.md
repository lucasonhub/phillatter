# FROM COMEDY OF MANNERS TO COMEDY OF MENACE: A COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS OF THE COUNTRY WIFE AND THE HOMECOMING

**Autores:** Şermin Sezer Toraman

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper positions William Wycherley’s The Country Wife \(1675\) as a comedy of manners and Harold Pinter’s The Homecoming \(1965\) as a comedy of menace to foreground a structural shift from the mediated hypocrisy of the Restoration period to the unmediated fragmentation of the twentieth century\. To delineate this shift, this study examines three recurring comedic motifs: the role of the intruder, generational conflicts leading to the triumph of the young over the old, and the restoration of the social order through marriage in the end\. Since Western drama flourishes in family and marriage contexts, the discussion centres on three marriages in The Country Wife by comparing them with equivalent relationships in The Homecoming\. Particularly, the intruder motif is analysed in the marriage of Lady Fidget and Sir Jasper Fidget; the theme of the young overpowering the old is exemplified in the marriage of Margery and Pinchwife; and the restoration of social order is reflected in the eventual union of Alethea and Harcourt\. Against the background of Michel Foucault’s theories on discourse, this study argues that the transition from comedy of manners to comedy of menace reflects a transformation in the discursive function of these motifs rather than their disappearance\. By creating a bridge between two generic traditions, this study claims that Pinter’s The Homecoming preserves the structural logic of The Country Wife while simultaneously exposing the weakened capacity of familial and social institutions to mediate conflict and preserve authority\. Thus, it offers a Foucauldian account of how the comedy of menace refunctions the comedic conventions of the comedy of manners to present the crisis of social intelligibility in the post\-war period\.
