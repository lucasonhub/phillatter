# Epistemic Possibility and Textual Source Voice in AI Technology Journalism: A Corpus\-Assisted Case Study of May, Might, Could, and Likely, 2022–2026

**Autores:** Asjad Balla

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

AI journalism often couples statements about current technical capability with predictions whose realization is uncertain\. This exploratory, corpus\-assisted case study investigates may, might, could and likely in 20 criterion\-based TechCrunch AI articles, chosen as two eligible articles from two fixed 14\-day windows each year, 2022–2026\. On October 4, 2026 a script was executed to audit the captured canonical pages, raw html, cleaned editorial text and verification logs\. Case\-insensitive, whole\-word searches in headlines and editorial bodies yielded 40 target tokens\. Was was the most frequent form \(n = 16, 40\.0%\) and occurred in nine articles, but only 7 of the 16 occurrences \(43\.8%\) were epistemic; the other nine were capacity or affordance\. Epistemicity was found in 10 of the 12 may tokens \(83\.3%\) and all the might and likely tokens\. Of these, 29 \(72\.5 %\) were epistemic\. One watermarking article in 2023 accounted for 8 of the 40 tokens, so removing it dropped the proportion of all target tokens in 2023 from 22\.5% to 3\.1%\. Two blinded machine\-assisted coding passes agreed on 35 of 40 semantic codes \(87\.5%; κ = \.679\)\. This is an algorithmic audit diagnostic, not human inter\-rater reliability\. The findings are descriptive, entirely auditable, and limited to this small, purposively bounded corpus Keywords: epistemic modality, uncertainty, artificial intelligence, technology journalism, corpus\-assisted discourse studies,modal verbs, source attribution
