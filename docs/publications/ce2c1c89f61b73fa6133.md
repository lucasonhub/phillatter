# Prescription Patterns in Terminal Cancer Patients: A Prospective Study in a Low\- and Middle\-Income Country Setting

**Autores:** Divya Sai Vanumu; Praneeth Suvvari; Praveen Kumar Kodisharapu; Basanth Kumar Rayani

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objectives: The objective of the study is to evaluate prescription patterns from admission \(Ta\) to discharge/death \(Td\), assess associations between symptom burden and medication use and identify significant co\-prescription patterns contributing to polypharmacy\. Materials and Methods: A prospective observational cohort study was conducted in an acute palliative care unit of a tertiary cancer hospital \(n = 219\)\. Medications were categorised using the World Health Organization anatomical therapeutic chemical codes\. Symptom burden was assessed at Ta using the Symptom Assessment Scale\. McNemar and Chi\-square tests examined changes in prescribing; Pearson correlation assessed relationships between symptom burden and medication use\. Results: Polypharmacy was common \(mean \~7\.5 medications\) throughout admission\. Opioids \(73%\), antacids \(68%\), laxatives \(48\.9%\) and corticosteroids \(48\.9%\) were most frequent at Td\. Significant co\-prescription patterns included prophylactic combinations \(opioid\-laxative, corticosteroid\-antiulcer, non steroidal anti\-inflammatory drugs–antiulcer; p &lt; 0\.05\), symptom cluster management \(opioid\-anxiolytic, opioid\-propulsives; p &lt; 0\.001\) and potential medication\-induced prescribing patterns \(gabapentinoid\-diuretic; p = 0\.035\)\. Individual symptoms predicted the use of relevant medication classes, but overall symptom burden did not predict total medication load at Td \(r = 0\.056, p = 0\.408\), indicating cascade\-driven rather than symptom\-driven polypharmacy\. Only 63\.6% of medications aligned with the International Association of Hospice and Palliative Care Essential Medicines List\. Conclusion: Persistent polypharmacy and identifiable preventive co\-prescribing patterns suggest opportunities for targeted interventions, including structured deprescribing, pharmacist\-led reviews and development of LMIC\-specific essential medicines lists\.
