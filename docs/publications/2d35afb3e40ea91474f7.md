# A novel approach for testing water safety using deep learning inference of microscopic images of unincubated water samples

**Autores:** Sanjay Srinivasan

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Fecal\-contaminated water causes diseases and even death\. Current microbial water safety tests require pathogen incubation, taking 24–72 h and costing $20\-$50 per test\. This paper presents a solution \(DeepScope\) that classifies microscopic images of unincubated water samples by a composite water\-safety determination, meeting UNICEF’s ideal Target Product Profile numerical performance thresholds for presence/absence testing, with an estimated per\-test cost of $0\.44\. By eliminating the need for pathogen incubation, DeepScope reduces testing time by over 98%\. In DeepScope, a dataset of microscope images of bacteria and water samples was assembled from the public DIBaS bacterial image collection and water samples from multiple source types, with labels cross\-validated using color\-change coliform tests\. A novel tile\-swap augmentation technique was developed to expand dataset diversity and mitigate overfitting, swapping random tile pairs to preserve bacterial morphology while producing a large space of augmented images\. Four convolutional neural network models \(ResNet\-18 and ResNet\-50 backbones, with and without dropout regularization\) were developed using transfer learning, then evaluated on a field\-test dataset comprising 100,000 microscope images of unseen, real\-world water samples collected from fourteen different water sources across Sammamish, WA\. On the 160 original microscope acquisitions underlying this dataset, the DeepScope model \(ResNet\-50 with dropout\) achieved 96\.88% accuracy, 98\.61% recall, and 94\.67% precision, corresponding to a 1\.39% false\-negative rate and a 4\.55% false\-positive rate\. A larger field test was then carried out on the 100,000 augmented images derived from these acquisitions, with the operating threshold set to fix precision at 90%\. Under these conditions the model achieved recall of 94\.48% ±0\.11%, accuracy of 92\.79% ±0\.08%, and F1 of 92\.19% ±0\.09%—a 5\.52% false\-negative rate, well below UNICEF’s 10% ceiling and competitive with membrane filtration and color\-change coliform tests without requiring pathogen incubation\. The DeepScope model was tested in a web server environment using mobile applications for Android and iOS\. The testing validated Internet\-based or smartphone\-based water safety testing, with results obtained in seconds\.
