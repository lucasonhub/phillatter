# Perceived Effects of Climate Risks on Food Security and Pathways to Sustainable Development in Turkana County, Kenya

**Autores:** LJ Murkomen; Alice Atieno Oluoko\-Odingo; P Shah; JN Mutemi

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Climate variability has emerged as one of the most dangerous threats to food security and sustainable development, especially in areas where livelihoods are heavily dependent on climate\-sensitive resources\. Turkana County, located in Kenya’s arid and semi\-arid lands \(ASALs\), is characterised by frequent droughts, flooding, unpredictable rainfall, and extreme temperatures that are combined with structural vulnerability factors such as poverty, lack of livelihood options, poor infrastructure, and others, resulting in household food insecurity\. The study sought to explore the connection between climate\-related risks and food security among Turkana youth and sustainable development paths\. In the research process, Martha Fineman&\#x27;s Vulnerability Theory, an institutional approach to structural vulnerability and resilience building, guided the research\. A mixed\-method research design was adopted\. The quantitative data were obtained using structured questionnaires from 258 youths aged 18\-34 years in Turkana Central and Turkana West sub\-counties, while qualitative data were generated through 40 key informant interviews and eight focus group discussions\. The quantitative data analysis was conducted using descriptive statistics, cross\-tabulation, and the chi\-square test in SPSS Version 25, while qualitative data analysis was conducted using thematic content analysis with Taguette software\. From the findings, it is evident that drought, flooding, rainfall variability, and heat stress had impacts that were recurring and widespread in livelihoods, livestock, crop production, water resources, housing, health, education, employment, and migration\. The impact of drought was prominent, contributing to the recurring loss of livestock and crops and also increasing fears over water scarcity\. Flooding has caused loss of livelihoods and properties, injuries, disruptions in education, and decreased access to safe drinking water\. In addition, food insecurity is prevalent in the area, with 43% of the youth not eating any food for a whole day, 37% eating less than they should, while 19% were feeling hungry but were not able to eat any food\. From the Chi\-square test, there was rejection of the null hypothesis, therefore a significant relationship between the impacts of climate risks and food insecurity amongst the youth in Turkana County\. In conclusion, it is clear that climate risks have increased vulnerabilities and slowed down sustainable development efforts\. Some of the recommendations include enhancing climate\-resilient livelihoods, improving water and agriculture infrastructure, establishing climate information and early warning systems, livelihood diversification, participation of youth, coordination of institutions, and continued investments in adaptation initiatives\. Key words: Climate risks, food security, youth, vulnerability, sustainable development, Turkana County
