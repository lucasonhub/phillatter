# Post‐Ejaculatory Prolactin Elevation Resolves Within 2 Hours: Implications for Blood Sampling in Male Infertility Evaluation

**Autores:** Shuai Li; Junjie Tian; Hong Wen; Yaoting Xu; Bo Zhu; Lejun Li

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: The acute post\-ejaculatory prolactin \(PRL\) elevation is well recognized, but its magnitude and recovery kinetics remain poorly defined\. OBJECTIVES: To delineate the post\-ejaculatory PRL kinetics, define its recovery window, and assess the clinical impact of improper sampling timing on hyperprolactinemia misclassification\. MATERIALS AND METHODS: This single\-center retrospective cohort study included 22,590 infertile men \(aged 20\-45 years\)\. Participants were classified into pre\-ejaculatory \(n = 8804\) and post\-ejaculatory \(n = 13,786\) groups, with the latter subdivided into 17 subgroups by sequential post\-ejaculatory time intervals\. Serum PRL, testosterone, luteinizing hormone \(LH\), follicle\-stimulating hormone \(FSH\), and estradiol were measured\. The proportion of participants with PRL exceeding the local upper reference limit \(17 ng/mL\) was calculated\. Interaction effects of ejaculation, sampling time of day, and age were evaluated using three\-way ANOVA\. RESULTS: Ejaculation induced a rapid and pronounced elevation in serum PRL, peaking within the ≤ 0\.1 h interval \(median 17\.9 ng/mL, 58\.4% increase from baseline, p &lt; 0\.01\) and returning to baseline by the ≤ 2 h interval\. Other sex hormones showed only minimal changes\. PRL exhibited significant diurnal variation and age\-related decline, but no significant interactions were observed between ejaculation and sampling time of day \(p = 0\.111\) or age \(p = 0\.330\)\. Notably, the percentage of participants with PRL &gt;17 ng/mL increased from 15\.8% at baseline to &gt; 50% within the first 0\.2 h, and remained significantly elevated through the ≤ 1\.5 h interval\. At the ≤ 2 h interval, the proportion \(17\.4%\) was no longer significantly different from baseline\. CONCLUSIONS: Post\-ejaculatory prolactin elevation resolves within 2 h\. This effect is independent of diurnal variation and age\. Blood for PRL measurement should be collected either before ejaculation or at least 2 h after ejaculation to avoid misdiagnosis\.
