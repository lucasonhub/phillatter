# Precision risk stratification for perioperative safety of minimally invasive versus open TLIF in elderly patients with lumbar degenerative disease

**Autores:** Hongyun Wu; Huichao Fu; Junda Qian; Congyu Yang; Xiuli Wang; Gongping Xu

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Lumbar degenerative disease is highly prevalent in elderly patients, and transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion \(TLIF\) is a standard surgical treatment\. However, the comparative safety of minimally invasive \(MIS\-TLIF\) versus open TLIF in this vulnerable population remains incompletely defined, particularly regarding effect modifiers and underlying mechanisms\. This retrospective cohort study included 728 patients aged ≥ 60 years who underwent primary TLIF, with 1:1 propensity score matching \(PSM\) performed to balance baseline characteristics\. Perioperative outcomes were compared, and 30\-day complications were analyzed using the Fine\-Gray competing risk model\. Subgroup analysis, mediation analysis, and a gradient boosting machine learning predictive model were conducted to explore effect heterogeneity, mechanisms, and risk stratification\. After PSM, all baseline variables were well balanced \(all standardized mean differences &lt; 0\.05\)\. The MIS\-TLIF group exhibited significantly shorter operative time, less intraoperative blood loss, lower transfusion rates, faster postoperative recovery, and reduced 30\-day and 90\-day unplanned readmission rates \(all P &lt; 0\.001\)\. After adjusting for competing risks, MIS\-TLIF was associated with a 52% lower risk of any 30\-day complication \(adjusted subdistribution hazard ratio \[SHR\] = 0\.48, 95% CI 0\.34–0\.68, P &lt; 0\.001\) and a 61% lower risk of major complications \(adjusted SHR = 0\.39, 95% CI 0\.21–0\.72, P = 0\.003\)\. Subgroup analysis revealed significant interactions between treatment effect and age \(P for interaction = 0\.007\) and frailty index \(P for interaction &lt; 0\.001\), with exponentially greater benefits observed in patients aged ≥ 72 years and frail patients\. Intraoperative blood loss and operative time mediated 41\.3% and 18\.9% of the total protective effect, respectively, with a total mediating effect of 60\.2%\. The predictive model achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0\.83 in the validation set, and the relative risk reduction of complications with MIS\-TLIF reached 78% in the very high\-risk group\. MIS\-TLIF significantly improves perioperative safety in elderly patients with lumbar degenerative disease, with the greatest benefit in high\-risk subgroups\. The developed risk prediction model can facilitate personalized surgical decision\-making for this population\.
