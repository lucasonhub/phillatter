# Kelly, Brian: Copernicus did not have Einstein&\#x27;s knowledge

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Copernicus did not have Einstein&\#x27;s knowledge AI’s place is being found in the world\. It seems most of us will not get a say in how we and our children will live\. What will stay human? A Harvard panel has spoken of the Copernican moment of the AI age\. AI reshaping human meaning\. I see them as a trusted voice in this debate\. Let us unravel the original Copernican moment, with the wisdom of hindsight\. Around AD 150: Ptolemy placed Earth at the centre of everything, as he understood it\. 1543: Copernicus placed the Sun at the centre, with Earth and the other planets moving around it, and the faraway stars fixed and set beyond them\. 1610: Galileo observed moons circling Jupiter, a smaller system within the bigger one\. Through his telescope, the Milky Way revealed countless stars previously unseen\. 1610: Galileo again\. Venus showed a new face, its phases, providing evidence for Copernicus’s model\. What we could see had changed, and so did our understanding\. 1838: Stellar parallax\. Bessel reliably measured a nearby star’s apparent shift against more distant stars as Earth goes around the Sun\. We could now measure the distance to a star beyond our Sun\. 1918: Shapley showed that our Sun was away from the centre of the Milky Way\. 1923 to 1924: Hubble \(not the telescope, the man\) established that Andromeda lay beyond our galaxy\. Our galaxy was one of many\. Earth within a solar system, our solar system within a galaxy, our galaxy among galaxies\. Each discovery showed how much more there was to understand\. In the decades that followed, with mightier measuring machines, other peculiar, unexplained gaps emerged\. Dark matter became the name for unseen matter inferred from its gravitational effects\. What it is remains unknown\. The unknown, we still live with knowingly and build upward honestly\. I said before, we look for the unknown in the stars, the wonder of the universe, while so much more wonder may lie here\. We still learn more of the stars every year\. And we respect their unknowns\. As there will one day be light\. But with conscience, we are also at our own Copernican moment, looking up and building our future, though we may be building from the first floor, without knowing our ground\. Conscience is not mentioned in the article reporting the Harvard panel\. It may find its place in the panels still to come\. Physics makes room for what is \(un\)observed but not fully understood\. Could we make room for what many of us recognise as conscience, in this unknowing? The finest computational training models will never convince many of us normals that we do not have one\. Not without the knowing\. AI is the future, a good one, applied with care\. My concern is that we build AI without this gap, as if we know it all, leaving no space for the light\. For me, that is where the knowledge still needs the wisdom\.
