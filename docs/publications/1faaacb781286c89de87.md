# Estrategias neuroeducativas para favorecer la autorregulaciónen la primera infancia: revisión sistemática en contextos latinoamericanos

**Autores:** Paula Vanessa Benavides\-Garzón; Karem Vásquez\-Rendón; Juan Felipe Torres\-Garzón; Steve Fernando Pedraza Vargas

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Self\-regulation is a fundamental process for cognitive and socio\-emotional development during early childhood\. Educational neuroscience proposes pedagogical strategies grounded in knowledge of brain functioning to foster self\-regulation; however, evidence from Latin America remains fragmented\. Methodology: A systematic review was conducted following the PRISMA guidelines and the SPIDER framework\. Six databases were searched, and studies published between 2019 and 2024 addressing neuroeducational strategies to promote self\-regulation in preschool children from Latin America were selected\. Methodological quality was assessed using the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool \(MMAT\)\. Results: Of the 951 records identified, 14 studies met the inclusion criteria\. Five categories of neuroeducational strategies were identified: play\-based activities, neuromotor stimulation, emotional and metacognitive education, multisensory approaches, and teacher training with technology integration\. Overall, the studies reported improvements in executive functions, attention, inhibitory control, and emotional regulation\. Discussion: The evidence supports the use of neuroeducational strategies to promote self\-regulation; however, descriptive and quasi\-experimental designs with methodological limitations predominate\. Conclusions: Neuroeducational strategies show promising results for strengthening self\-regulation in early childhood\.
