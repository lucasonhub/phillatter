# Leakage\-Aware Explainable AI for Credit Risk Assessment of Perishable Food Supply\-Chain SMEs Using Food Shelf\-Life, Postharvest Loss and Digital Banking Data in Nigeria

**Autores:** KOSISOCHUKWU HENRY UKPABI; Abdullahi Mohammed Ibrahim; Zainab Abdulsalam Jamilu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Small and medium\-sized enterprises \(SMEs\) operating in perishable food supply chains face intertwined operational and financial risks: inventory can deteriorate before sale, postharvest losses reduce cash available for repayment, and many firms have thin or irregular formal credit histories\. This study proposes a leakage\-aware, explainable artificial intelligence \(AI\) framework that integrates food shelf\-life, postharvest\-loss, commodity\-price, and digital banking features to assess credit risk for Nigerian perishable\-food SMEs\. Because linked Nigerian lender\-level repayment data and firm\-level shelf\-life measurements were not available for this research, the empirical demonstration uses a clearly labelled dataset rather than claiming results from real borrowers\. A reproducible simulation of 5,000 SME\-loan observations was specified, with 70%/15%/15% chronological train/validation/test partitions, a financial\-only baseline, and an integrated model using LightGBM\-style gradient boosting\. Logistic regression and random forest were included as benchmarks; SHAP\-style feature attribution was used for interpretability\. The simulated results illustrate the intended evaluation, not observed Nigerian banking performance: the financial\-only baseline achieved a test ROC\-AUC of 0\.742 and PR\-AUC of 0\.356, while the integrated model achieved ROC\-AUC 0\.801 and PR\-AUC 0\.421\. At a threshold selected on validation data, recall increased from 0\.61 to 0\.70, while Brier score improved from 0\.176 to 0\.158\. The largest simulated contributors were recent repayment behaviour, cash\-flow volatility, estimated remaining shelf life, postharvest\-loss rate, and debt\-service coverage\. A random split produced more optimistic estimates than chronological evaluation, demonstrating why time\-aware validation is essential\. The main contributions are \(i\) a domain\-specific framework linking food deterioration to credit risk, \(ii\) a leakage\-control protocol for temporal financial data, \(iii\) an explainability and calibration evaluation plan, and \(iv\) a transparent synthetic benchmark that can be replaced by real data\. These results are illustrative only; empirical conclusions require validation using consented, anonymised lending outcomes and measured food\-quality data\.
