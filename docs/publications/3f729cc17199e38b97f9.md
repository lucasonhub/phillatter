# An electric\-potential\-augmented benchmark dataset for electrical capacitance tomography: Electric\-potential supervision for future physics\-guided learning

**Autores:** Xinqi Zhang; Qiming Ma; Lihui Peng

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract While deep learning has significantly advanced image reconstruction of electrical capacitance tomography \(ECT\), most public datasets retain only capacitance vectors and permittivity distributions and do not expose the excitation\-wise electric potential field, a physical intermediate in the forward mapping\. To address this, we present an electric\-potential\-augmented ECT benchmark dataset generated via a COMSOL\-\-MATLAB pipeline for a fixed eight\-electrode sensor\. The dataset comprises 20,000 randomized samples across four typical flow\-pattern families\. Alongside conventional capacitance vectors and permittivity images, each sample preserves eight spatially registered full\-field potential maps\. Governing equations and field\-perturbation visualizations characterize the relation between potential and dielectric structure\. As an initial use of this physical information, we introduce potential\-field intermediate supervision to guide representation learning in simple forward and inverse models\. Under sample\-disjoint independent and identically distributed \(IID\) and fully held\-out two\-bar out\-of\-distribution \(OOD\) evaluation, potential supervision reduces forward capacitance\-prediction mean squared error \(MSE\) by 56\.8\\% and 74\.6\\%, respectively, relative to a structure\- and capacity\-aligned control\. In an exploratory forward experiment, a low\-weight partial differential equation \(PDE\) residual yields 5\.3\-\-8\.3\\% lower mean potential\-prediction MSE\. For inverse reconstruction, the simple model reduces mean MSE by 14\.3\\% and 3\.0\\% on IID and OOD data, respectively, relative to the same control\. The more modest inverse gains are consistent with the difficulty of recovering dense potential maps from limited capacitance measurements\. These results provide initial controlled evidence for the value of potential\-field supervision\. The dataset and reference experiments together offer a foundation and methodological insights for developing more advanced field\-supervised, physics\-informed, and data\-consistent ECT methods\.
