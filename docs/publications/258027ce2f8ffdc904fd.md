# Knowledge, Preparedness, and Information‐Seeking Behavior for Traumatic Dental Injury Management Among Undergraduate Dental Students

**Autores:** Ezgi Cansu Fırıncıoğulları; Aleyna Ateş; Ece Eden

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND AND AIM: Traumatic dental injury management requires both appropriate knowledge and the ability to seek reliable information when uncertainty arises\. This study examined the relationships among objectively measured knowledge, self\-rated knowledge, perceived preparedness, and information\-seeking behavior in undergraduate dental students\. MATERIALS AND METHODS: This anonymous repeated cross\-sectional study analyzed 252 questionnaire responses from the same undergraduate cohort surveyed at two consecutive stages of the curriculum: 137 in the fourth year and 115 in the fifth year\. The present analysis included objective knowledge, self\-rated knowledge, perceived preparedness, perceived source reliability, first\-choice information sources, and perceived barriers\. Associations among readiness dimensions were examined using Spearman correlations and multivariable regression\. Case\-scenario selection of evidence\-based resources and generative artificial intelligence was evaluated using Firth\-penalized logistic regression\. RESULTS: Perceived preparedness was more strongly associated with self\-rated knowledge \(ρ = 0\.45\) than with objectively measured knowledge \(ρ = 0\.23\)\. Objective knowledge remained independently associated with preparedness \(B = 0\.083, 95% CI: 0\.014\-0\.151\), although its contribution was modest relative to self\-rated knowledge\. No statistically significant differences in objective knowledge or preparedness were observed between survey waves after correction for multiple testing\. In the case scenario, evidence\-based source selection was more frequent among fifth\-year\-wave respondents than among fourth\-year\-wave respondents \(87\.0% vs\. 69\.3%\), whereas generative AI selection was less frequent \(7\.0% vs\. 26\.3%\)\. Information\-source selection was associated primarily with trust in the respective resources rather than with objectively measured knowledge\. CONCLUSIONS: Objective knowledge, perceived preparedness, and information\-seeking behavior represent related but distinct domains relevant to traumatic dental injury management\. Undergraduate trauma education should combine knowledge assessment with confidence calibration and guideline\-based information\-seeking training\.
