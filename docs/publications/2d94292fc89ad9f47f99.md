# Optimizing multi\-timescale market participation for distributed renewable energy: A Bi\-level model predictive control and improved reinforcement learning framework

**Autores:** Yuyuan Zhang; Ziren Wang; Wei Li

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

With the rapid development of distributed renewable energy, supply–demand imbalances and stochastic fluctuations across multiple timescales in power systems have become increasingly prominent\. This paper proposes a framework for market participation by distributed renewable energy aggregators \(DREAs\) based on nested bi\-level model predictive control \(MPC\) and improved reinforcement learning\. In the upper layer, weather\-state and unit operating\-state labels are incorporated into a Wasserstein generative adversarial network with gradient penalty to generate joint wind–PV output data\. These data are then used to support rolling coordinated optimization across the medium\- and long\-term, day\-ahead, and tradable green certificate markets, while real\-time deviation information fed back from the lower layer is incorporated to dynamically adjust subsequent trading plans\. In the lower layer, real\-time deviations are corrected through real\-time market participation, self\-consumption load adjustment, and energy storage management, while real\-time operating states and deviations are fed back to the upper layer, forming a prediction–optimization–execution–feedback loop\. The artificial intelligence contribution is the proposed Soft Actor\-Critic with Adversarial Reinforcement Learning \(SAC\-ARL\) algorithm, which introduces state\-perturbation training and policy\-smoothing regularization to improve policy stability under uncertain conditions such as forecast deviations and cyberattacks\. From an engineering perspective, system\-wide coordinated trading strategies are developed for DREAs participating across multiple market types and timescales\. Simulation results show that, compared with the benchmark scheme, the proposed scheme reduces real\-time deviations by 37\.3% and real\-time market regulation costs by 69\.75%, while increasing net profit by 4\.37%, verifying its effectiveness and engineering applicability\.
