# Fujie, Naoto: Critical Points of Science and Nonscience Scientific Operational Shapes, Ignition, Transformation, Extinction, Re\-Ignition, Stellar Operationalization, Philosophically Informed AI Experimentation, Ziran System, and Xenoscience

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper investigates the critical intervals through which scientific operations emerge, transform, cease, and re\-emerge across heterogeneous material, computational, and institutional configurations\. It develops an operational\-realist account of the relations between scientific and nonscientific activity without presupposing a universal or immutable demarcation criterion\. The central argument distinguishes four potentially asynchronous developments: the continuation or cessation of real operational relations, changes in evidential warrant, transformations in scientific classification, and effects on subsequent reality\. Their configurations, temporal scales, and causal interdependencies determine how particular scientific relations become operative, lose nonredundancy, or regain operational significance\. Building upon the theory of scientific operational shapes, the paper examines configured ignition, Scientific Operational Extinction Points, historical re\-entry, correction latency, reality penetration, and the stellar operationalization of science\. It investigates how scientific operations may continue and revise their conditions without requiring human conceptual mediation at every stage\. The philosophical analysis engages scientific realism, inferentialism, object\-oriented ontology, structural mapping, agential realism, and contemporary autonomous science\. It connects these discussions to Xenoscience and the Ziran System, an experimental architecture for heterogeneous observation, intervention, external memory, and configuration revision\. A series of synthetic PyTorch experiments conducted through the Xeno–Ziran Simulator provides limited computational comparisons of observation, intervention, historical information, and downstream reachability\. These experiments do not establish a universal boundary between science and nonscience, demonstrate the independent efficacy of History\-Reentry Generative Intervals, or validate the philosophical frameworks under consideration\. The paper argues that critical transformations of scientific operations can be investigated through locally specified conditions of ignition, persistence, interruption, substitution, and re\-ignition\. Its contribution lies in the philosophical reconstruction of these operational relations and their consequences for the continuing transformation of scientific inquiry\. \(direct link \)
