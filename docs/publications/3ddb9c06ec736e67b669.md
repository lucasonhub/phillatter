# Linking Drift Prediction to Search Planning in Maritime Search and Rescue: A Review of Cross\-Stage Evaluation

**Autores:** Seung\-Yeol Hong; Yong\-Hyuk Kim

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Maritime search and rescue requires probabilistic decision\-making under uncertainty about a drifting target’s location and constraints on search resources\. Classical search theory provides a basis for allocating search effort through the probability of containment, probability of detection, and probability of success\. Advances in numerical modeling and artificial intelligence have expanded the capabilities of drift prediction and search planning, but improvements in prediction accuracy do not necessarily translate into better search outcomes\. This structured narrative review examines the development of these research streams and their coupling through probability representations, including probability\-of\-containment maps\. An illustrative evidence map of ten selected cases distinguishes implemented coupling from evidence that isolates the downstream effects of prediction changes\. The cases provide limited evidence tracing these effects through probability representation, planning, and operational outcomes under common evaluation conditions\. The synthesis organizes evaluation into four stages: Prediction Gain, Information Gain, Decision Gain, and Operational Gain\. Decision Gain denotes improved plan quality under a common evaluation criterion, rather than a change in the plan alone\. The review outlines common evaluation conditions and a factorial comparison for separating prediction\-side, planning\-side, and combined effects\. These recommendations support evaluation of whether artificial\-intelligence\-based prediction improvements lead to better search decisions, detection outcomes, and resource use\.
