# Does etiology matter only in HFmrEF? Data from the HEart failuRe ObsErvational Study \(HEROES\)

**Autores:** Michał Tarnowski; Karolina Molenda; Robert Morawiec; Agata Anna Galas; Przemysław Leszek; Paweł Maeser; Agata Tymińska; Adrian Stefański; Jarosław Dróżdż; Iwona Gorczyca−Michta

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Heart failure \(HF\) with mildly reduced ejection fraction \(HFmrEF\) is a heterogeneous phenotype, and whether the prognostic weight of ischemic versus non\-ischemic etiology differs across left ventricular ejection fraction \(LVEF\) categories remains uncertain\. We examined whether the association between etiology and long\-term all\-cause mortality is modified by HF phenotype\. Material and methods Within the prospective multicenter HEROES registry, 1,410 patients with HF were classified as having ischemic or non\-ischemic etiology and categorized by LVEF\. All\-cause mortality was obtained through national administrative linkage with complete follow\-up\. Entropy balancing \(13 covariates\) and weighted Cox models were applied within each phenotype, with formal testing for etiology\-by\-phenotype interaction\. Robustness was evaluated using restricted mean survival time \(RMST\), the E\-value, and sensitivity analyses\. Results Over a median follow\-up of 14\.8 months, 219 \(15\.5%\) deaths occurred\. Crude mortality was identical between etiologies \(15\.5% vs\. 15\.5%; p = 0\.93\)\. After balancing, no difference was observed in HFrEF \(adjusted hazard ratio \[aHR\] 1\.03, 95% CI: 0\.68–1\.58\) or HFpEF \(aHR = 0\.84, 95% CI: 0\.37–1\.90\), whereas non\-ischemic etiology was associated with higher mortality in HFmrEF \(aHR = 3\.99, 95% CI: 1\.51–10\.51; p = 0\.005\)\. The etiology\-by\-phenotype interaction was significant \(p = 0\.032\)\. In HFmrEF, the RMST difference at 23 months was −2\.38 months \(95% CI: −3\.69 to −1\.00\), with an E\-value of 4\.55 \(lower confidence limit 1\.99\); findings were consistent across sensitivity analyses\. Conclusions The prognostic impact of HF etiology appears phenotype\-dependent, concentrated within HFmrEF, where non\-ischemic etiology substantially worse survival\. Given the limited number of events, this phenotype\-specific signal is hypothesis\-generating and warrants confirmation\.
