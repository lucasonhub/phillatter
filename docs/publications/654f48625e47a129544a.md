# Interaction of sex and insulin resistance with cognitive impairment in community\-dwelling middle\-aged and older adults

**Autores:** Jung Sook Kim; Byung Chul Chun; Joong\-Yeon Lim

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Cognitive impairment is a major global health challenge\. However, whether sex and age interactively varies the association between insulin resistance and cognitive impairment remains unclear\. This cross\-sectional study aimed to evaluate the association between insulin resistance and cognitive impairment and identify sex\- and age\-specific interactions in community\-dwelling adults\. Methods The study involved 3,898 participants \(aged 54–86 years\) from the Ansan\-Ansung cohort of the Korean Genome and Epidemiology Study \(2017–2018\)\. Insulin resistance was assessed via Homeostatic Model Assessment for Insulin Resistance \(HOMA\-IR\), with participants dichotomized by the median \(1\.97\)\. Cognitive impairment was defined as a score of ≤23 on the Korean Mini\-Mental State Examination score\. Multivariable logistic regression was applied to examine the interaction and stratified effects of sex and age \(≥65 vs\. &lt;65 years\) on the insulin resistance–cognitive impairment association\. Results Among 3,898 participants \(mean age 66\.6 years; 55\.5% women\), 635 \(16\.3%\) had cognitive impairment\. A significant interaction was observed between sex and HOMA\-IR \( p = 0\.04\), whereas the interaction for age was not significant \( p = 0\.17\)\. In stratified analyses, higher HOMA\-IR was significantly associated with cognitive impairment exclusively in women aged ≥65 years \(OR 1\.37; 95% CI 1\.02–1\.84; p = 0\.04\)\. Conclusion Higher insulin resistance is a significant modifiable factor for cognitive impairment, specifically in women aged ≥65 years\. These exploratory findings suggest potential sex\-related differences in the metabolic\-cognitive association, underscoring the importance of sex\-specific metabolic management starting from midlife to help preserve cognitive health in later life\.
