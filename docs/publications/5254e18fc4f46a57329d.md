# Quadratic Morphing Morlet Transform and Multi\-Scale Frequency\-Attentive Temporal Network for sEMG Gesture Recognition

**Autores:** Hazret Tekin

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate recognition of surface electromyography \(sEMG\) signals is essential for intuitive human–machine interaction and myoelectric prosthetic control\. However, the inherently transient, nonstationary, and low\-amplitude nature of sEMG signals limits the effectiveness of conventional time–frequency representations and deep learning models, which often struggle to capture short\-duration neuromuscular events and the multi\-scale spectral–temporal characteristics of muscle activity\. To address these challenges, this study introduces the quadratic morphing morlet \(QMM\) wavelet, a newly formulated and fully admissible wavelet designed to enhance transient resolution and spectral selectivity\. Building upon this improved representation, a hybrid deep\-learning architecture, the multi\-scale frequency\-attentive temporal network \(MFATNet\), is proposed as a novel hybrid deep learning architecture\. MFATNet integrates tri\-branch multi\-scale spectral encoding, frequency\-wise attention, and dilated temporal modeling to jointly capture spectral diversity, frequency adaptivity, and gesture dynamics within a unified framework\. The proposed QMM–MFATNet pipeline is evaluated on the 52\-class hand\-gesture corpus of the NinaPro DB1 dataset, comprising sEMG recordings from 27 subjects, wherein QMM\-based continuous wavelet transform \(CWT\) representations are used to generate high\-resolution scalograms for subsequent model training\. Experimental results indicate that QMM provides sharper and more discriminative scalograms than classical Morlet, Gaussian, and Mexican Hat wavelets\. Additionally, MFATNet outperforms standard CNN, ResNet, and MobileNet models, achieving the highest accuracy \(93\.13%\) and F1\-score \(93\.15%\)\. Comparisons of different wavelet types further demonstrate the superiority of QMM, which yields a mean accuracy of 0\.9313 ± 0\.0194, surpassing all traditional kernels\.
