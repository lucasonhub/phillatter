# Quantifying the accuracy of estimating harmful algal bloom cell concentrations using OLCI imagery in the tributaries of lower Chesapeake Bay

**Autores:** Richard Wayne Litaker; Michelle C\. Tomlinson; William Holland; Kimberly S\. Reece; Todd A\. Egerton; Travis O\. Briggs; Gail P\. Scott; Hamish J\. Small; Sylvain Gaillard; Savannah A\. Mapes

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Harmful algal blooms of Alexandrium monilatum and Margalefidinium polykrikoides are a common occurrence in the tributaries of lower Chesapeake Bay, United States of America\. This study evaluated whether daily processed imagery from the Ocean and Land Colour Instrument \(OLCI\) can accurately measure chlorophyll a \(chl a \) concentrations in these turbid, bloom\-prone tributaries\. Since HAB cell concentrations provide a more direct measure of risk than chl a , a second objective was to quantify the accuracy of bloom cell concentrations derived from satellite chl a \. Methods Field collected chl a concentrations and processed OLCI data were used to evaluate the accuracy of satellite\-derived chl a concentrations in the York and James Rivers\. Field, laboratory, and literature data were used to quantify the precision of satellite\-based cell estimates\. Results Satellite\-derived chl a estimates correlated well with laboratory extracted chl a from the field and were stable over time for the entire OLCI satellite image dataset\. The results of laboratory experiments and field studies showed that variations in the pg chl a cell \-1 used to convert chl a to cell estimates, often varied five\- to hundred\-fold, depending on prevailing environmental growth conditions and the chl a contributed by co\-occurring phytoplankton\. Discussion The satellite\-derived chl a concentrations proved accurate over a wide range of chl a concentrations, confirming their usefulness in tracking high biomass blooms and guiding sampling efforts required to evaluate potential adverse impacts, and as a basis for potentially estimating relative HAB cell concentrations\. However, field, literature, and laboratory data showed that chl a cell \-1 estimates at a given chl a concentration can vary by up to 100\-fold\. Consequently, satellite\-based cell concentrations will be highly inaccurate\. More accurate HAB cell estimates might be possible if chl a cell \-1 is measured periodically during a bloom and used to estimate cell concentration from satellite chl a in real\-time\.
