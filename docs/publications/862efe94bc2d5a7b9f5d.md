# WRN helicase addiction in MSI\-H colorectal cancer: biomarker\-guided pharmacology, resistance evolution, and implications for other gastrointestinal cancers

**Autores:** Peng Zhang; Jiangsu Gan; Hongying Wang; Yali Xiong

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Microsatellite instability\-high \(MSI\-H\) and mismatch repair\-deficient \(dMMR\) cancers are enriched for dependence on Werner syndrome RecQ helicase \(WRN\), a synthetic\-lethal vulnerability mechanistically linked to expanded TA\-dinucleotide repeats, non\-B DNA structures, and replication\-associated genome instability\. However, WRN dependence is not universal among dMMR/MSI\-H tumors, indicating that MSI status is an enrichment biomarker rather than a complete predictor of biological dependency\. In this review, we synthesize recent advances in WRN structural and molecular pharmacology, with particular emphasis on colorectal cancer, where the strongest functional and pharmacological evidence currently exists\. We distinguish four translational biomarker layers: pretreatment predictors of WRN dependency, target\-engagement and DNA\-damage pharmacodynamic markers, early treatment\-response biomarkers, and acquired\-resistance markers\. We further examine reversible and covalent WRN inhibitors, mechanisms of primary and therapy\-emergent resistance, immune and replication\-stress\-based combinations, and the expanding early\-phase clinical landscape\. Rather than proposing quantitative WRN dependency as a new biological concept, we present an evidence\-graded pharmacological framework that links established substrate biology with drug exposure, target engagement, downstream DNA damage, treatment response, and resistance evolution\. This framework may guide prospective biomarker development in MSI\-H/dMMR colorectal cancer while identifying the evidence still required for broader application across other gastrointestinal malignancies\.
