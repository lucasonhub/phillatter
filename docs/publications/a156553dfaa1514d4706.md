# Nanotechnology Applied to Anesthesia: The Potential of Local Analgesics and Opioids in Liposomal Systems

**Autores:** Karen Priscilla Oliveira CASTRO; Amanda Muller Baptista Nunes; Gabriela Tais Stiehl; Ruan Fernandes da Rosa; Felipe Ferreira Gomes; Mônica Santos Primeira; Laura Böttcher Lins; Jhonatan De Campos Rosa; Mateus Udino Sari; Caroline dos Santos Nader; Lucas de Campos Spindola; Nicole Ries Girardi

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Nanotechnology has emerged as a promising strategy for the development of controlled\-release drug delivery systems, with potential applications in anesthesiology and the treatment of perioperative pain\.Among these technologies, liposomal systems stand out for their ability to encapsulate both hydrophilic and lipophilic substances, modify their pharmacokinetics, and promote gradual release at the site of action\.The use of local anesthetics and opioids incorporated into liposomal systems aims to prolong analgesia, reduce plasma peaks, decrease the frequency of administration, and potentially reduce opioid consumption and its adverse events\.Liposomal bupivacaine is currently one of the main clinical applications of this strategy, while liposomal formulations of morphine and other opioids remain relevant in the context of research and specific applications\.Experimental studies demonstrate that the encapsulation of opioids can modify systemic exposure and prolong the analgesic effect\.However, the clinical results of liposomal bupivacaine are heterogeneous, with trials and meta\-analyses showing modest benefits or a lack of superiority compared to conventional anesthetics\.Recent studies also indicate that its benefits may depend on the surgical procedure, the regional technique used, and the multimodal analgesia protocol\.Thus, liposomal systems represent an important nanotechnological platform for anesthesia, but their use must be individualized and based on clinical, pharmacoeconomic, and safety evidence\.Future development should focus on optimizing formulations, achieving specific tissue targeting, controlling drug release, and identifying patients who stand to benefit the most\.
