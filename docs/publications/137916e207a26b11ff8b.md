# Long\-term outcomes of daridorexant in those with comorbid insomnia

**Autores:** Soumya Saladi; Hatem Jouda; Teck Yong; Kirstie N\. Anderson

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

AIMS AND METHOD: Daridorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist recently licensed for chronic insomnia disorder\. Guidelines recommend it as a second\-line treatment for insomnia, after cognitive\-behavioural therapy\. Initial trials excluded common comorbidities despite comorbid insomnia being the norm, not the exception\. The long\-term benefit from daridorexant remains unclear, with few long\-term trials\. We aimed to assess long\-term outcomes in those with comorbid insomnia\.This was a retrospective analysis of 38 patients with insomnia who commenced daridorexant\. Mental health disorders were common in those referred\. Adverse events and a range of insomnia outcomes were assessed\. RESULTS: The mean follow\-up period was 10\.5 months\. Of the 38 patients in the study, 95% had comorbid insomnia; 55% experienced benefit from the daridorexant, but 24% discontinued because of side\-effects, including vivid dreams, fatigue and worsening anxiety\. Three patients experienced worsening mood, one of which included suicidality\. CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS: Daridorexant can be effective for those with comorbid insomnia, but side\-effects may be more common than previously reported\.
