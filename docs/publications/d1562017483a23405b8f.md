# Faster but Not Truer, Part 1: A Systematic Scoping Review of Artificial Intelligence in Health Economics and Outcomes Research

**Autores:** Onur Başer

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Artificial intelligence \(AI\) is being introduced into every stage of the health economics and outcomes research \(HEOR\) production line, from literature screening to causal estimation\. The field lacks a defensible count of what has been done, and bibliometric claims about AI adoption circulate without measured error rates\. Methods: Following PRISMA\-ScR, we searched PubMed for AI terms co\-occurring with HEOR terms for publications from 2010 through 2025, retrieved 2642 records, and included 2476 after screening\. Records were classified into 8 nonexclusive application domains and 1 of 6 method categories using a published term dictionary\. To quantify classification error, an independent large language model classifier, blinded to the dictionary labels and given only title and abstract, reclassified a random sample of 300 included records\. We report per label counts, agreement, Cohen kappa, prevalence\-adjusted bias\-adjusted kappa, precision, recall, F1, and a precision\-adjusted corpus size with exact binomial intervals\. We separately assembled the primary performance evidence for each HEOR workflow, requiring a quantitative metric from a primary study\. Results: Included publications rose from 29 in 2010 to 852 in 2025, a factor of 29\.4, with the inflection at 2018 to 2019 rather than at the release of ChatGPT\. AI reached 6\.0% of all indexed HEOR output in 2025 while HEOR fell to 0\.86% of all indexed medical AI output\. Economic evaluation was the most frequently assigned domain \(1559/2476, 63\.0%\), followed by real\-world data and causal inference \(496, 20\.0%\) and governance, reporting, and validity \(494, 20\.0%\)\. Generative AI appeared in no record before 2021 and in 103 in 2025\. Only 154 of 300 sampled records \(51\.3%; 95% confidence interval \[CI\], 45\.7\-56\.9\) were judged genuinely HEOR relevant, implying a precision\-adjusted corpus of 1271 studies \(95% CI 1131\-1410\)\. Agreement was substantial for real\-world data and causal inference \(kappa, 0\.729\) but negligible for economic evaluation \(kappa, 0\.144\), where dictionary precision was 29\.6%\. Of 2476 records, 38 \(1\.5%\) were randomized trials and 17 \(0\.7%\) validation studies\. The replication package is publicly available\. Conclusions: The literature is growing faster than its own measurement infrastructure\. Measured gains concentrate in tasks with observable ground truth, and predictive performance repeatedly fails to predict evidentiary performance\.
