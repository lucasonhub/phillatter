# Using AI\-powered modelling for drought forecasting in the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea

**Autores:** Yong‐Sik Ham; Hyon\-Su Ri; Sang\-Il Jong; Kum‐Chol Om; Kyong\-Bok Sonu; Un\-Sim Paek

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Context The Democratic People’s Republic of Korea \(DPRK\) is vulnerable to drought due to monsoon\-driven variable precipitation that affects agricultural production and water security\. Reliable multi\-timescale drought forecasting aids risk mitigation; however, nonlinear non\-stationary drought signals are hard to capture indata\-scarce regions\. Aims This study developed and validated a Complete Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition with Adaptive Noise\-Long Short\-Term Memory \(CEEMDAN\-LSTM\) hybrid Standardised Precipitation Evapotranspiration Index \(SPEI\) drought forecasting model\. Its performance is systematically compared against a standalone LSTM for multiple SPEI timescales under DPRK climatic conditions\. Methods Monthly meteorological records from 10 stations collected over a period of 60 years were used to derive SPEI time series\. CEEMDAN decomposed SPEI into intrinsic mode functions for noise reduction and trend extraction as LSTM inputs; hyperparameters were optimised by Bayesian optimisation\. Model performance was assessed with Nash\-Sutcliffe Efficiency \(NSE\), Kling\-Gupta Efficiency \(KGE\), Root Mean Square Error \(RMSE\), and Mean Absolute Error \(MAE\)\. Key results CEEMDAN\-LSTM generally outperformed standalone LSTM with timescale\-dependent skill\. Short\-term SPEI predictions had inherent accuracy limits \(NSE &lt; 0\.5\) yet reduced relative errors\. Medium\- to long\-term SPEI yielded large accuracy gains \(max NSE = 0\.95 for SPEI\-12\), with residual\-distribution analysis showing effective suppression of extreme prediction errors\. Spatial heterogeneity existed; complex terrain at stations such as Chongjin limited decomposition\-driven improvements\. Conclusions CEEMDAN\-LSTM improves forecast skill by processing non\-stationary drought signals\. Without complex external predictors, this hybrid method enables reliable medium\-long\-term drought monitoring and extreme\-event detection\. Implications This single\-variable time\-series method enables data\-efficient drought early warning for data\-scarce regions, supporting drought response and adaptive water management without large\-scale remote\-sensing or global datasets\.
