# Efficiency of the Pooled Logistic Regression Approach as an Alternative to the Cox Proportional Hazard Model

**Autores:** Precious O\. Ibeakuzie

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The Cox proportional hazards \(CPH\) model and the exponential Cox model are foundational tools in survival analysis, yet they impose structural assumptions; proportional hazards and constant baseline hazard, respectively that frequently fail in practice\. This paper proposes and evaluates pooled logistic regression \(PLR\) applied to survival\-piled \(stacked\) data as an alternative discrete\-time survival estimator\. Right\-censored survival data are restructured into a long\-format person\-time binary classification dataset, and three models are compared: the exponential Cox model, the classical semi\-parametric CPH model, and PLR\. Monte Carlo simulations with 1,000 replications at each of six sample sizes \(n = 25, 50, 100, 150, 200, 250\) are conducted to assess stability, discrimination, and goodness\-of\-fit\. Performance is measured by Harrell&\#x27;s concordance index \(C\-index\) and the Akaike information criterion \(AIC\)\. Simulation results show that PLR maintains consistently high and stable C\-index values \(0\.742–0\.784\) across all sample sizes, whereas the CPH model exhibits high variability \(0\.575–0\.884\) and the exponential Cox model underperforms throughout \(0\.532–0\.671\)\. AIC comparisons further demonstrate PLR&\#x27;s competitive goodness\-of\-fit at moderate and large sample sizes\. Real\-data validation using the NCCTG lung cancer dataset corroborates these findings\. PLR&\#x27;s theoretical connection to the Cox partial likelihood is established formally, and its compatibility with machine\-learning classification pipelines is discussed\. The findings support PLR as a robust, flexible, and interpretable alternative for discrete\-time survival analysis\.
