# Grant, David Gray ; Behrends, Jeff &amp; Basl, John: Agential Consideration and the Wrongs of Deference to AI Systems

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Philosophical Issues\_\. forthcomingLarge language models increasingly make it possible to delegate virtually any task to an AI system, and to have some hope that it will be done competently \(if not better than you could have done it yourself\!\)\. As these systems become better at reasoning tasks, it will be ever more tempting to allow them to make difficult decisions for us\. In 2023, for example, administrators at Vanderbilt University issued a brief public statement responding to the mass shooting at Michigan State University\. The message included a note indicating it had been generated using ChatGPT\. In response, a recent Vanderbilt alumna told the school’s paper that “\[t\]here is a sick and twisted irony to making a computer write your message about community and togetherness because you can’t be bothered to reflect on it yourself\.” It was wrong for Vanderbilt’s administrators to delegate the task of crafting a suitable message to an AI system\. But why was it wrong, exactly? Grant, Behrends, and Basl \(2025\) suggest that delegating important tasks to AI systems is wrong in some cases because it fails to show agential consideration that is owed to particular stakeholders\. We exercise agential consideration towards an individual just in case we decide how to treat them by exercising our capacities for moral and practical reasoning in a way that is appropriately sensitive to their interests and claims\. Since we are obligated to make some kinds of decisions by exercising agential consideration, and since making a decision by exercising agential consideration is inconsistent with delegating it to an AI system, we often have duties of agential consideration that prohibit delegating to AI systems \(Grant, Behrends, and Basl 2025\)\. This view, while prima facie plausible, faces two seemingly potent skeptical challenges\. One concerns the scope of duties of agential consideration: if they apply in only a narrowly circumscribed range of cases, then they may not frequently matter\. Another concerns their strength: since the duties are in principle overridable, it may be that they are typically overridden, and so of little practical or theoretical concern\. In this paper, we develop an account of agential consideration on which duties of agential consideration are not just pervasive, but often weighty enough to make delegating important tasks to AI systems all\-things\-considered impermissible\. An important upshot of our account is that deference to “moral experts” is morally wrong in a much broader range of cases, and for a broader range of reasons, than has previously been appreciated\.
