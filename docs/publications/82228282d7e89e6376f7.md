# Study on how Al can reduce financial fraud among women

**Autores:** Iqra Khan

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract This study examines the role of Artificial Intelligence \(AI\) in mitigating online financial fraud among women, analyzing survey data from 75 respondents\. The demographic profile is heavily skewed toward younger, digital\-native generations, with 92% of respondents aged 18–35 and nearly half \(46\.7%\) identifying as students\. This sample exhibits high digital integration, with 73\.3% utilizing digital payment applications daily\. Concurrently, risk exposure is pervasive: 89\.3% are aware of online cybercrimes, and 74\.7% have direct or secondary experience with financial scams—predominantly fake loan apps, UPI fraud, and OTP scams\. Consequently, 69\.3% express deep anxiety regarding transaction safety\. To counteract these threats, respondents strongly endorse technological intervention\. General AI literacy is high at 70\.7%, and 68% recognize AI’s current application in banking security\. Optimism regarding its efficacy is robust: 70\.7% believe AI can significantly reduce financial fraud targeting women, primarily because 74\.7% trust it to detect threats faster than human operators\. To establish a sense of security, users demand proactive, real\-time defenses rather than passive analysis, heavily prioritizing instant fraud alerts \(37\.3%\), background transaction monitoring, and automated transaction blocking\. Ultimately, 72% of respondents demand that banking institutions escalate AI security integration\. However, the data highlights that technology alone is insufficient; an overwhelming 78\.7% emphasize that robust AI deployment must be paired with comprehensive financial literacy and grassroots cybersecurity awareness campaigns\. This dual strategy of advanced technological defense and target user education is deemed essential to safely empower women within the digital economy\.
