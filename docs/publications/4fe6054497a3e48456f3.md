# Morphometric typology of Ming Chinese export and domestic porcelain bowls in trade and production contexts

**Autores:** Shengying Feng; Wenkang Huang; Miao Zhao; Yingying Wang; Jinghui Ao; Gangwei Cai; Wenwen Shi

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Chinese export porcelain bowls are heritage materials associated with Ming maritime trade, yet their morphology remains insufficiently quantified\. This study develops a morphometric typological framework to characterize concentration, size variation, and atypical forms, comparing 37 export references with 25 domestic comparators\. Profile extraction, typological coding, AutoCAD scaling, and Rhinoceros\-assisted measurement were applied to 62 bowls\. Round feet and curved bodies comprised 88\.7% \(55/62\) and 69\.4% \(43/62\), while everted and open rims together comprised 66\.1% \(41/62\)\. Rim diameter ranged from 7\.6 to 41\.2 cm, including 20 miniature, 36 regular\-sized, and 6 large bowls\. Among 60 specimens retained for angular analysis, HFJA was concentrated \(mean 59\.0°, median 58\.0°, SD 8\.3°\), whereas LFJA was dispersed \(mean/median 47\.0°, SD 10\.2°\)\. Export specimens showed broader size, rim\-type, and angular variation within a shared morphological framework\. The workflow provides reproducible evidence while treating production, trade, consumption, and function as contextual associations rather than causal mechanisms\.
