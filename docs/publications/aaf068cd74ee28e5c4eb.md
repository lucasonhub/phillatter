# Mattos, José Caetano de: WHERE THE TRADITION DID NOT LOOK: The Rendering Asymmetry in the Catalogue of the Virtues, and the Occupation Hypothesis

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Abstract Virtues can be enacted, asserted, or made perceptible\. Courage is done; honesty is said; grace is seen\. When the rendering is made a coordinate of a virtue classification and measured, it proves inert on how a virtue is captured and strongly predictive of something else: whether a cell of the classification is occupied at all\. Roughly three\-quarters of assertible cells and two\-thirds of perceptible cells stand empty, against fewer than one in five enacted cells\. A catalogue drawn from the whole recorded moral tradition is dense in one mode and nearly barren in the other two\. This article treats that asymmetry as a finding in need of explanation and sets three accounts against each other\. It may be an artefact — of who compiled the catalogue, of who coded it, of what languages happen to lexicalise, of there being fewer such virtues to find, or, most dangerously, of arithmetic, since a classification whose entries are predominantly enacted will leave the other columns&\#x27; cells sparse for no reason at all\. It may be a trace of occupation: where a counterfeit conserves the form and the name well enough to pass undetected, it takes the lexical slot before the virtue is named, and the cell reads empty because something else already answers to the name\. Or it may be an immune response: clean assertion is the unmarked default and goes unnamed, while a community proliferates terms for the varieties of deception as a collective radar\. Evolutionary signalling theory grounds the mechanism — acts are expensive to fake, assertions and appearances are cheap talk — and supplies the third account, which predicts everything the second does\. Two narrower predictions separate them, both decidable from dictionaries by parties with no stake in the framework\. If counterfeits took the names, assertible and perceptible virtue terms should show elevated semantic pejoration: specious once meant beautiful, plausible once meant deserving applause, while courage has not moved\. And detection cost depends on the horizon of exposure as well as on the mode, so the same gradient should appear within the enacted column, where the disputed coordinate is held fixed\. The article establishes none of this\. Its base coefficients are one person&\#x27;s coding of his own catalogue, an audit of that catalogue corrected two of the author&\#x27;s claims and exposed an inconsistency in its indices, and the arithmetic check that could dispose of the whole finding has not been run\. What is offered is a question stated precisely enough to be settled by someone else\. Relevance Every virtue catalogue in the tradition, from Aristotle’s to the most recent psychological classifications, is compiled by asking what a good person does\. If the asymmetry reported here is real, that method has a systematic blind spot, and the blind spot falls precisely where the corresponding counterfeits are hardest to detect\. The proposal is testable by lexicographic means at low cost, and it would bear on any project that classifies moral qualities — philosophical, psychological or institutional\. \(direct link \)
