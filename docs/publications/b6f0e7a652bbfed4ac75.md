# Clinical validation of a morphometric artificial intelligence system for detecting osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures on plain radiographs

**Autores:** Hyun\-Ho Shin; Sung Kyu Kim

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures \(OVCFs\) are a major cause of morbidity and mortality in aging populations, and plain radiography—the most available modality for initial assessment—is operator\-dependent\. This study evaluated the diagnostic performance and clinical utility of a deep learning\-based morphometric artificial intelligence \(AI\) model for detecting OVCFs and quantifying vertebral compression rates on plain radiographs\. A total of 100 patients \(780 vertebral bodies; 685 normal, 95 fractured\) with suspected vertebral compression fractures were retrospectively analyzed\. All patients underwent spine radiographs followed by computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging within 5 weeks and bone mineral density assessment within 1 year\. The AI model calculated vertebral compression rates and classified fractures using a 20% compression threshold\. AI performance was compared with blinded assessments by two orthopedic surgeons, one orthopedic resident, and one general physician\. Metrics were reported with cluster\-adjusted 95% confidence intervals \(patient\-level bootstrap\) and compared using McNemar’s test; balanced accuracy, F1 score, a patient\-level analysis, and compression\-rate agreement \(intraclass correlation\) were also assessed\. The AI model demonstrated high specificity \(96\.6%\) and negative predictive value \(95\.4%\)\. Its sensitivity, however, was lower than that of physicians \(66\.3% vs\. 75\.8%–86\.3%\), with lower balanced accuracy \(81\.5% vs\. 86\.5%–92\.3%\) reflecting the low fracture prevalence \(12\.2%\); on McNemar testing, AI accuracy and sensitivity were significantly lower than the surgeons’ and resident’s but not the general physician’s, whereas specificity did not differ\. Patient\-level sensitivity was 84\.3% \(negative predictive value 70\.3%\)\. AI sensitivity rose with severity, from approximately 48% for sub\-threshold \(&lt; 20%\) fractures to 100% for Genant grade 3\. AI\-derived compression rates correlated with physicians’ measurements \( r = 0\.594–0\.873; intraclass correlation 0\.534–0\.850; all p &lt; 0\.01\), and the area under the ROC curve was 0\.8095 for the AI model versus 0\.8651–0\.9228 for physicians\. The morphometric AI model showed high specificity and meaningful correlations with physician\-derived measurements but did not outperform physician evaluators, and its sensitivity \(66\.3%\) was insufficient for stand\-alone use\. It is therefore not suitable for independent diagnostic, rule\-out, or triage use; rather, it may act as an adjunctive aid that supports physician interpretation, chiefly by providing objective, reproducible quantification of vertebral compression\.
