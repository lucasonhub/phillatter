# The Relatıonshıp Between Leader–Member Exchange and Organızatıonal Cıtızenshıp Behavıors Among Nurses

**Autores:** Kübra Özkara; Yılda Arzu Aba

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Nurses’ organizational citizenship behaviors \(OCB\) are critical to the effectiveness of healthcare services and the quality\-of\-care delivery\. The quality of leader–member exchange \(LMX\) established between nurses and their managers plays a decisive role in fostering these voluntary and discretionary behaviors\. This study aimed to examine the relationship between leader–member exchange and organizational citizenship behaviors among nurses\. Methods: This cross\-sectional study included 322 nurses\. Data were collected using professional characteristics, Leader–Member Exchange, and Organizational Citizenship Behavior scales\. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, Shapiro–Wilk, independent t\-test or Mann–Whitney U test, and linear mixed\-effects models\. Statistical significance was set at p &lt; 0\.05\. Results: The mean total score of the Leader–Member Exchange Scale indicated a moderate level of LMX \(3\.74 ± 1\.00\), whereas the mean total score of the Organizational Citizenship Behavior Scale reflected a high level of OCB \(4\.45 ± 0\.62\)\. Multiple regression analyses showed that the contribution dimension significantly and positively predicted altruism \(β = 0\.347, p &lt; 0\.001\), conscientiousness \(β = 0\.283, p &lt; 0\.001\), sportsmanship \(β = 0\.215, p = 0\.008\), and civic virtue \(β = 0\.318, p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Affect and loyalty positively predicted altruism, whereas professional respect negatively predicted altruism; none of the LMX subdimensions significantly predicted courtesy\. Conclusions: Contribution\-based dimension of leader–member exchange plays a key role in enhancing organizational citizenship behaviors among nurses\. Strengthening leadership practices that promote mutual trust, responsibility, and contribution may increase nurses’ voluntary organizational behaviors, thereby supporting care quality and organizational effectiveness\.
