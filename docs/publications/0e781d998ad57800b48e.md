# The Serotonergic and Cholinergic Signaling Pathways Drive Gastric Precancerous Lesions and Its Potential Mechanism

**Autores:** Shengli Gao; Xufei Feng; Nana Zhang; Jia Su; Yuyan Wang; Linghan Kong; Jianing Zhou; Hui Dong; Feifei Guo

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Gastric cancer \(GC\) is a leading lethal malignancy globally\. Gastric precancerous lesion \(GPL\) represents a critical step in GC, but its neural regulatory mechanisms remain largely unclear\. We combined bioinformatics, patch\-clamp recording, immunofluorescence, and chemogenetics to investigate serotonergic and cholinergic signaling in GPL\. An N\-methyl\-N\-nitrosourea \(MNU\) \-induced mouse model was used for in vivo validation\. High 5\-HTR1D expression correlated with poor survival in gastric adenocarcinoma patients and was upregulated in AGS and MKN45 cells, as well as in gastric tissues of GPL mice\. 5\-HT evoked non\-selective cation currents involving 5\-HTR1D and TRPV4 in gastric epithelial and cancer cells, as supported by both pharmacological inhibition and siRNA\-mediated TRPV4 knockdown\. Blockade of 5\-HTR1D or TRPV4 attenuated intestinal metaplasia and dysplasia, and this improvement was accompanied by reduced AKT/mTOR activation in GPL mice\. Increased gastric M3R/5\-HT co\-localization was associated with elevated acetylcholine and overactivated cholinergic neurons in the dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus \(DMV\)\. Chemogenetic inhibition of DMV cholinergic neurons decreased ChAT and 5\-HT levels and alleviated gastric pathology in GPL mice\. These findings suggest that 5\-HT/5\-HTR1D signaling may promote TRPV4\-involved cation currents, contributing to mucosal pathology and intestinal metaplasia in GPL model mice, whereas DMV cholinergic neurons projecting to the stomach may facilitate gastric 5\-HT secretion and thereby potentially contribute to GPL progression\. This work establishes a novel neuro\-epithelial network linking vagal cholinergic output to serotonergic signaling that may be involved in GPL progression, providing potential targets for prevention and intervention\.
