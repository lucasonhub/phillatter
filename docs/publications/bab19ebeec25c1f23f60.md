# Rising demand, robust triage? Service evaluation of the triage process in a young\-onset dementia service

**Autores:** Amna Khalil; Timona Chetty; Elizabeta Mukateova‐Ladinska; Matthew Critchfield

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

AIMS AND METHOD: To evaluate referrals to our local Young Onset Dementia Assessment Service \(YODAS\), analyse referral trends and re\-referral patterns between 2020 and 2024, and review reasons for non\-acceptance of referrals at triage\. A retrospective review of electronic referral records was undertaken using descriptive analysis\. RESULTS: Referrals to YODAS almost doubled over the study period, while non\-acceptance rates remained relatively stable, rising from 52\.6% in 2020 to 57\.3% in 2024\. Among 760 patients whose referrals were not accepted, the most common reasons for non\-acceptance were: absence of features suggestive of neurodegeneration, cognitive symptoms better explained by mental health conditions, and insufficient referral information\. Over one\-quarter were re\-referred, with two\-thirds subsequently accepted\. Only 6% of individuals whose referrals were initially not accepted later received a dementia diagnosis, indicating effective triage\. CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS: A rise in the demand for dementia assessment from a younger patient population requires a careful triage to ensure the individuals with possible features of neurodegeneration are appropriately assessed, and cases where alternative explanations are more likely are directed to more appropriate pathways\.
