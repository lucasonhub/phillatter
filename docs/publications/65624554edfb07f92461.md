# Multimodal Particle Size Distribution Modeling of Soilless Substrate Using Parametric and Machine Learning Approaches

**Autores:** Md Ehsanur Rashid; Meen Sung Koo; RAGAB E\. ABOUZEID; Jeb S\. Fields; James E\. Altland; Brian E\. Jackson; Qinglin Wu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Soilless substrates are essential for controlled environment agriculture, yet their particle size distribution \(PSD\) remains difficult to characterize across materials with contrasting morphology\. A multimodal framework integrating parametric distribution modeling with interpretable machine learning was developed to advance PSD analysis in engineered horticultural substrates\. Cumulative PSD curves from Douglas fir bark blends, laboratory\-derived wood substrates, and commercial peat\-based retail substrates were fitted using negative exponential and Rosin–Rammler \(Weibull type\) models\. The Weibull model provided improved fits, particularly for substrates with greater variability, while effectively capturing the narrower distributions observed in wood\-derived materials\. Model parameters aligned closely with conventional particle size and mass\-based descriptors\. Image\-derived morphological features were used to predict sieve\-assigned particle size classes using supervised machine learning\. Nonlinear ensemble models outperformed a linear baseline, with random forest selected for interpretation due to its robustness and interpretability\. Cross\-validated performance was strong \( R 2 ≈ 0\.93, mean absolute error ≈ 0\.25\), and feature importance, SHapley Additive exPlanations \(SHAP\), and partial dependence analyses identified particle width as the dominant predictor\. Thermal modification induced modest shifts toward finer particles, likely associated with increased brittleness due to hemicellulose degradation\. Overall, the proposed framework provides a robust and interpretable approach for PSD characterization and highlights the potential of wood\-derived substrates as controllable, sustainable alternatives for soilless cultivation\.
