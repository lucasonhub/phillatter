# Comparison of food carbon footprints using 20\- and 100\-year timeframes

**Autores:** Katie Osborn; Silas Crocker; Kira Presley; Alexis Shenkiryk; Maggie Spire; Jennifer Molidor; Mansha Kapur; Jennifer Ayla Jay

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Global Warming Potential \(GWP\) is a common metric used in life cycle analyses and environmental calculations to quantify the warming of Greenhouse Gases \(GHGs\) relative to CO 2 over a set timeframe\. The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change \(IPCC\) recommends use of both 20\- and 100\-year timeframes to account for impact of short\-lived gases such as methane\. However, while GWP over a 100\-year period \(GWP100\) is commonly used in food emission databases, food system data using GWP over a 20\-year period \(GWP20\) that could be essential in political and institutional food policy shifts are not readily available\. To address this, we \(1\) calculate GWP20\-derived CO 2 \-equivalent emission values for 16 broad food categories and 222 specific food items in a comprehensive database alongside GWP100\-derived values, and \(2\) contextualize GWP differences by comparing emission impacts of different meals at a university dining hall under the two scenarios\. We demonstrate that the difference in CO 2 \-equivalent emission between generally low and high impact foods widens considerably under GWP20, exemplifying the limitations of a one metric system, and the importance of incorporating the impact of short\-lived gases within a shorter timeframe in food emission analyses used for food policy and institutional change\.
