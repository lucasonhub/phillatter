# An Integrated XAI\-RL Architecture for Ethical Decision\-Making in Autonomous Robots

**Autores:** Elshaimaa Nada; Ahmed Ahmed

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Can a robot&\#x27;s ethical reasoning be transparent without slowing it down? That is the question we set out to answer\. Autonomous robots now make decisions that affect people&\#x27;s lives, and if we cannot understand how they decide, trust becomes a serious problem\. We built a unified architecture that couples Explainable AI \(XAI\) with Reinforcement Learning \(RL\) through three connected components: a Perception Layer for situational awareness, a Decision Reasoning Layer that breaks agent utility down into measurable ethical sub\-rewards \(safety, fairness, and well\-being\), and a contrastive Explainability Engine grounded in a real\-time Ethical Ledger\. We tested it against Black\-Box RL and two post\-hoc XRL baselines \(LIME and SHAP\) across two simulation environments and a physical robot pilot on a TurtleBot3 platform\. Our system reached 92±3% ethical alignment in simulation and 89±4% on the physical platform, beating every baseline \(p &lt; 0\.01 throughout\)\. In a controlled, IRB\-approved user study \(N=128\), participants rated it higher on trust \(4\.5±0\.4\), understandability \(4\.6±0\.3\), and perceived safety \(4\.4±0\.5\) than the alternatives\. Explanations were also fast: 4\.2±0\.6 ms on average, versus 18\.3±2\.1 ms for SHAP and 12\.7±1\.8 ms for LIME on the same hardware—well within real\-time limits\. An ablation study and sensitivity analysis back up these architectural choices\.
