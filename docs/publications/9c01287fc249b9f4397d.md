# Photo\-Activatable RNA Switches for Optical Control of Cell\-Free Protein Synthesis

**Autores:** Natalie E\. Connell; Mahla Lashkari; Emmett Hanson; Ian M\. McClain; Maya Dellocono\-Forstner; Maksim Royzen; Mehmet Veysel Yigit

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Programmable RNA switches enable target\-dependent regulation of cell\-free protein synthesis\. Here, we report a photo\-activatable RNA switch using photocleavable Lock DNA \(pcl\-Lock\) to enable optical control of in vitro translation\. Synthesized by solid\-phase methods, pcl\-Lock incorporates a coumarin\-based photocleavable linker that maintains the RNA switch in an inactive state\. Irradiation with 456 nm light cleaves the linker, releasing inhibition and activating translation\. Light\-triggered activation produced β\-galactosidase, yielding approximately 4\-fold and 30\-fold increases in fluorescence and glucose signals, respectively\. Native polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis confirms efficient photocleavage of the pcl\-Lock strand, while control experiments verify that light\-dependent activation does not occur with the Lock strand lacking the photocleavable linker\. This strategy enables optical regulation of cell\-free protein synthesis and establishes a strategy for integrating photochemical control with programmable gene regulation\.
