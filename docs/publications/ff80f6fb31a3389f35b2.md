# Stacked gated recurrent unit\-based prediction of impact energy response in cross\-ply composite laminates under low\-velocity impact

**Autores:** Oluwapelumi O\. Adejumo; Johnson Sunday Alabi; Oluwafemi Moses Ogunmisi; Olumide Abiodun Suberu; Daniel Oluwadamilare Olasupo; Gbenga Daniel Ilori; Temitope Olugbenga Osifalujo

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Composite materials offer a high strength\-to\-weight ratio, stiffness, and corrosion resistance\. However, their layered structure makes them vulnerable to matrix cracking, delamination, and fiber damage under impact loading\. Low\-velocity impact is a major damage concern in fiber\-reinforced composite laminates because it can generate internal defects that are difficult to detect by surface inspection\. Accurate prediction of the impact\-energy response is therefore important for evaluating impact resistance and improving composite structural design\. In this study, a machine learning framework was developed to predict the impact\-energy response of cross\-ply IM7/Cycom 5320\-1 carbon/epoxy laminates under low\-velocity impact loading\. A finite element model of the low\-velocity impact setup was developed in Abaqus and validated against an existing experimental drop\-weight impact result\. After validation, the finite element model was used to generate impact\-energy histories at 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25 J\. These simulated responses were then used to train a stacked gated recurrent unit \(GRU\) model\. The trained model was subsequently used to predict the impact\-energy response at an unseen 30 J impact condition, and the prediction was validated against the corresponding Abaqus 30 J simulation result\. The model achieved good agreement with the Abaqus 30 J simulation result, with a mean absolute error of 0\.79 J, root mean square error of 0\.89 J, coefficient of determination of 0\.99, and mean absolute percentage error of 6\.42%\. These findings show that the stacked GRU model was able to predict the impact\-energy response at an unseen impact condition with good agreement with the finite element result\.
