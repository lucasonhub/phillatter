# Six\-month supervised telerehabilitation combining pilates\-based therapeutic exercise and self\-myofascial release in adults with fibromyalgia: a randomized controlled trial

**Autores:** Thomai Marantidou; Evangelia Germanou; Βασιλική Μάνου; Nikolaos Koutlianos; Evangelos Sykaras; Ioannis P\. Terzidis

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OBJECTIVE: To evaluate the effects of a 6\-month supervised multicomponent telerehabilitation programme combining Pilates\-based therapeutic exercise and self\-myofascial release on fibromyalgia impact, sleep quality, and psychological outcomes\. DESIGN: Two\-arm, parallel\-group randomized controlled trial\. SUBJECTS/PATIENTS: 68 adults with fibromyalgia confirmed according to the 2016 ACR criteria\. METHODS: Participants were randomized to a 6\-month supervised home\-based telerehabilitation programme \(n = 35\) or usual care \(n = 33\)\. The intervention comprised 3 live online sessions weekly combining Pilates\-based therapeutic exercise, self\-myofascial release, core stabilization, low\-load resistance exercise, stretching, and relaxation\. Outcomes were assessed at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months using validated patient\-reported measures\. RESULTS: Significant group\-by\-time interactions were observed for fibromyalgia impact \(p &lt; 0\.001, partial η2 = 0\.132\), sleep quality \(p &lt; 0\.001, partial η2 = 0\.129\), depressive symptoms \(p = 0\.002, partial η2 = 0\.129\), and perceived stress \(p = 0\.028, partial η2 = 0\.065\), but not for anxiety \(p = 0\.701\)\. Mean intervention adherence was 87\.5%, with no adverse or serious adverse events reported in either group\. CONCLUSION: 6 months of supervised multicomponent telerehabilitation produced favourable longitudinal changes in fibromyalgia impact, sleep quality, depressive symptoms, and perceived stress compared with usual care\.
