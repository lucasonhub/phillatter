# Impact of different surgical approaches on prognosis in patients with pancreatic neck cancer: A retrospective cohort study

**Autores:** Ming\-Jian Ma; He Cheng; Yusheng Chen; Xian\-Jun Yu; Chen Liu

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUNDPancreatic neck cancer occurs in a unique anatomic location\.The optimal radical surgical approach among distal pancreatectomy \(DP\), pancreaticoduodenectomy \(PD\), and total pancreatectomy \(TP\) remains controversial, especially in patients without gastroduodenal artery \(GDA\) invasion\. AIMTo evaluate how different surgical procedures affect overall survival \(OS\) and disease\-free survival \(DFS\) in pancreatic neck cancer patients without preoperative radiographic evidence of GDA invasion\. METHODSThis single\-center retrospective cohort study included 174 consecutive patients with pancreatic neck cancer who underwent radical resection with curative intent at Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center between January 2017 and January 2022\.None of the patients showed preoperative radiographic signs of GDA invasion\.Patients were stratified into three groups based on surgical procedure: DP, PD, or TP\.Survival curves were generated using the Kaplan\-Meier method and compared with the log\-rank test\.Cox proportional hazards regression models were employed for univariate and multivariate analyses\. RESULTSThe cohort comprised 107 patients \(61\.5%\) in the DP group, 41 \(23\.6%\) in the PD group, and 26 \(14\.9%\) in the TP group\.The median OS for the entire cohort was 27\.3 months, and median DFS was 17\.5 months\.On univariate analysis, TP was associated with significantly worse survival compared to PD and DP \(OS: 17\.7 vs\. 35\.1 vs\. 32\.2months, 4 / 41 P = 0\.014; DFS: 11\.9 months vs 19\.2 months vs 20\.3 months, P = 0\.017\)\.Cox multivariate analysis identified T stage \[hazard ratio \(HR\) = 1\.840, 95%CI: 1\.166\-2\.905\],tumor differentiation grade \(HR = 0\.311, 95%CI: 0\.109\-0\.888\),and R1 resection \(HR = 2\.226, 95%CI: 1\.258\-3\.938\)as independent prognostic factors for OS\.After adjusting for these confounders, the type of surgical procedure was not significantly associated with OS or DFS \(P &gt; 0\.05\)\. CONCLUSIONSurgical approach is not an independent prognostic determinant in pancreatic neck cancer patients\.When R0 clearance can be achieved by DP, extending resection to PD or TP does not appear to confer a survival advantage\.
