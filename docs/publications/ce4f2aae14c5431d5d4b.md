# Robust Short\-Term Wind Power Forecasting Using Variational Mode Decomposition and Weighted Machine Learning Models

**Autores:** Mohammed Altayeb Awad Gendeel; Yuxian Zhang; Xiaoyi Qian; Shaik Javeed Hussain

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Wind power forecasting is essential for maintaining grid stability due to the inherent intermittency and nonlinear dynamics of wind energy\. This study proposes a robust two\-stage hybrid framework for short\-term wind power prediction integrating variational mode decomposition \(VMD\), artificial neural networks \(ANNs\), and weighted least\-squares support vector machine \(W\-LSSVM\)\. In the first stage, VMD decomposes wind speed time series into band\-limited intrinsic mode components to reduce non\-stationarity and noise\. Each component is predicted using a nonlinear ANN model\. In the second stage, a residual\-based adaptive weighted LSSVM aggregates the predicted components to estimate SCADA\-measured wind power, mitigating the influence of outliers and noisy samples\. A chronological rolling\-origin evaluation protocol is employed to preserve temporal ordering and assess generalization within the evaluated temporal windows\. Performance is evaluated over multiple non\-overlapping test windows and reported as mean ± standard deviation\. Comparative experiments against classical baselines, persistence forecasting, and LSTM models demonstrate competitive short\-horizon accuracy and improved robustness under the evaluated degraded\-data conditions\. Empirical prediction intervals additionally provide an assessment of forecast uncertainty\. The results indicate that the proposed framework provides competitive short\-term forecasting performance and improved robustness under the evaluated degraded\-data conditions\. However, validation using longer\-term and multi\-site datasets is required to establish broader generalizability\.
