# Discrete Wavelet Transform and Multi\-scale Parallel Convolutional Network for gas recognition

**Autores:** Jingxiao Li; Lingwei Yu; Hai Yang; Zhe Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Electronic nose \(e\-nose\) systems based on metal oxide sensor arrays are widely deployed in food safety, environmental monitoring, and medical diagnosis\. However, accurate gas identification remains challenging due to two factors: \(1\) most deep learning methods insufficiently exploit the transient dynamics of sensor response signals, discarding critical time\-frequency information; \(2\) the limited receptive fields of standard convolutional neural networks \(CNNs\) hinder the modelling of long\-range dependencies across sensor channels and temporal evolution\. To address these issues, we propose a Discrete Wavelet Transform and Multi\-scale Parallel Convolutional Network \(DWT\-MPCN\) that formulates gas recognition as a time\-frequency image classification problem\. Specifically, a fixed five\-level db5 discrete wavelet transform \(DWT\) module converts multi\-channel raw time\-domain signals into two\-dimensional wavelet coefficient maps without manual feature engineering, preserving both rapid transient details and slow recovery trends\. A Multi\-scale Parallel Convolution \(MPC\) module then employs parallel convolutional branches with different kernel sizes to simultaneously capture local transient features and broader contextual patterns while maintaining a lightweight architecture \(only 1\.75M parameters\)\. Finally, a convolutional Transformer encoder adaptively weights cross\-sensor and cross\-scale features to produce discriminative representations\. Evaluated on three public e\-nose datasets with distinct sensor configurations and gas types, DWT\-MPCN achieves competitive performance, including 99\.72% accuracy on the OPEN SAMPLING dataset, 99\.22% on the TWIN dataset, and 100% on the large\-scale array sensor dataset\. Component ablations, capacity\-aware comparisons, hyperparameter analyses, and wavelet\-parameter sensitivity studies validate the contribution of each component and clarify the design choices\. The proposed method provides a compact and interpretable framework for edge\-intelligent olfaction systems\.
