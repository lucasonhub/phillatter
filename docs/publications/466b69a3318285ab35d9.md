# Appraising the causal role of physical activity and sedentary behavior in female reproductive health: A Mendelian randomization study

**Autores:** Youzhu Li

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Physical inactivity and sedentary behavior have been associated with adverse female reproductive outcomes, but whether these associations are causal remains uncertain\. Methods: We used genome\-wide association study summary data to evaluate four self\-reported activity\-related traits—leisure screen time \(LST\), moderate\-to\-vigorous physical activity \(MVPA\), sedentary behavior at work \(SDW\), and sedentary commuting behavior \(SDC\)—in relation to 25 female reproductive conditions\. Linkage disequilibrium score regression was used to assess genetic correlations\. Univariable and multivariable Mendelian randomization \(MR\) analyses were performed, with MR\-Egger, weighted median, weighted mode, MR\-RAPS, and Causal Analysis Using Summary Effect Estimates \(CAUSE\) used for sensitivity analyses\. Results: Longer LST was causally associated with higher odds of adenomyosis, endometriosis, excessive, frequent, and irregular menstruation, ectopic pregnancy, and abortion\. MVPA was causally associated with lower odds of adenomyosis, endometriosis, and excessive, frequent, and irregular menstruation\. Multivariable MR further supported independent associations of LST with abortion and postpartum depression\. SDW showed limited associations, whereas no significant causal association was identified for SDC\. Conclusions: Genetic evidence supports causal associations of physical activity and sedentary behavior with several female reproductive outcomes\. Reducing leisure screen time and increasing moderate\-to\-vigorous physical activity may represent modifiable strategies to support female reproductive health\.
