# Early Manifold Shaping: A Relational Account of Infant Pre Conceptual Experience

**Autores:** Denis Bailey

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Early Manifold Shaping presents a relational account of infant pre‑conceptual experience, reframing early development as a process of directional and tonal structuring rather than cognition or object perception\. Before infants can recognize caregivers, perceive objects, or form expectations, they inhabit a continuous multimodal manifold composed of skin, visual, auditory, proprioceptive, vestibular, visceral, and endocrine signals\. Repeated directional shifts within this manifold generate patterned contours that are felt rather than recognized\. These contours carry warm \(settling\) and cool \(alerting\) tonalities that precede emotion and valence\. Through repetition, tonalities stabilize into strekions—recurring relational shapes that orient the infant without memory or representation\. As strekions cluster, the infant encounters proto‑objects, not as entities but as persistent relational contours that “return” within experience\. Orientation emerges as the infant leans toward familiar warm contours and away from familiar cool ones, establishing the earliest sense of “where” before space, objecthood, or self\. This relational model dissolves the assumption that infants begin with cognition and instead shows how meaning, valence, and proto‑objecthood arise from directional structure\. The framework provides a generative foundation for later perception, attachment, and agency, and offers a developmental instantiation of Relational Structuralism \(RS\)\.
