# Trans\-anethole attenuates streptozotocin\-induced cognitive dysfunction by modulating oxidative stress and cholinergic activity

**Autores:** Shruti S\. Mole; Prarambh S\. R\. Dwivedi; Wilona Loren Lobo; C\. S\. Shastry; Pukar Khanal

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Cognitive impairment is a neurological disorder defined as an inability to learn, think, judge, remember, and make decisions\. The primary cause of chronic, age\- related cognitive decline is Alzheimer’s disease \(AD\), which is frequently studied as a representative model\. The current therapies for AD provide only symptomatic relief, which necessitates the need for safer, multi\-target treatments\. Trans\- anethole, the primary constituent of Illicium verum , Pimpinella anisum , and Foeniculum vulgare , is an aromatic molecule that possesses several pharmacological activities, including anti\-inflammatory and antioxidant effects, which may have neuroprotective benefits\. Methods The current study integrated in silico , in vitro , and in vivo approaches to examine the neuroprotective potential of trans\- anethole\. In silico studies were carried out to find out the potential molecular targets and pathways associated with the effects of trans\-anethole against AD\- related cognitive dysfunction\. Cytotoxicity, antioxidant activity, and cholinesterase inhibition were assessed in vitro on BV2 cells\. Intracerebroventricular \(ICV\) administration of streptozotocin \(STZ: 3 mg/kg\) in rats induces an insulin\-resistant brain state leading to progressive cognitive and memory impairment, a condition that closely relates to sporadic Alzheimer’s disease\. ICV\-STZ\-induced Wistar rats were used to assess the effect of trans\- anethole on AD\-related cognitive dysfunction through behavioral, biochemical, and histopathological assessments\. Results A total of 174 common targets were identified, enriching pathways including apoptosis, AGE\-RAGE, TNF, and PI3K\- Akt signaling\. Stable interactions were observed in molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations\. Trans\-anethole demonstrates moderate cholinesterase inhibition and antioxidant activity in BV2 cells\. Comparing STZ\- treated rats to disease control, trans\-anethole at doses of 100 mg/kg and 400 mg/kg significantly reduced lipid peroxidation, restored antioxidant enzyme levels, and attenuated cholinergic dysfunction\. Histopathological findings showed qualitative preservation of hippocampal neuronal morphology\. Discussion Overall, these findings suggest that trans\-anethole may represent a potential neuroprotective preclinical candidate for further investigation in Alzheimer’s disease\-related cognitive dysfunction\.
