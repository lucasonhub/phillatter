# Reinforcement Learning\-driven Diffusion Models: Language, Vision, and Beyond

**Autores:** Zhiyuan Ma; Jiabao Wei; Tianyu Wang; Hanqing Wang; Jiadong Pan; Haowen Xu; Liangliang Zhao; Ruixun Liu; Yuhan Wang; Yuwei Zhang; Liang Feng; Junhao Xiao; Kaiyan Zhang; Xuekai Zhu; Daizong Liu; Biqing Qi; Yihao Liu; Ganqu Cui; Ning Ding; Linfeng Zhang; Jianjun Li; Kun He; Xiang Bai; Bowen Zhou

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Diffusion models \(DMs\) have steadily demonstrated outstanding performance in high\-fidelity, controllable generation across diverse scenarios, following the &quot;pretraining\-finetuning&quot; paradigm established in LLMs\. Despite these advances, conventional DMs still face critical limitations in sophisticated reasoning, autonomous exploration of generation pathways, and adaptability to complex, human\-aligned objectives\. Inspired by RLHF&\#x27;s success in LLMs, RL has recently emerged as a pivotal post\-training mechanism to iteratively refine DMs through reward\-driven trajectory optimization\. This paper presents the first comprehensive review of RL\-driven DMs and an empirical analysis of how RL reshapes the diffusion process towards experience\-centric, goal\-directed generation\. We propose a novel technical panorama elucidating RL&\#x27;s multifaceted roles in diffusion\-based language, multimodal, robotic, and scientific models, with a granular focus on diffusion trajectory optimization and reward engineering\. Through extensive experiments on multiple task\-specific benchmarks, we systematically evaluate representative methods to intuitively highlight the advantages of different RL post\-training strategies\. Furthermore, we reveal and dissect three crucial bottlenecks that currently hinder the real\-world deployment of RL\-driven DMs: reward design fragility, exploration\-exploitation trade\-off in high\-dimensional generation spaces, and training instability under sparse rewards\. Based on these perspectives, we provide a principled roadmap for the integration of RL into DMs and outline promising future research directions for robust, interpretable RL\-diffusion systems\. We also construct a public repository to continuously track the latest developments in this rapidly evolving field: https://github\.com/MAIR\-Lab\-HUST/Awesome\-RL\-for\-Diffusion\-Models\.
