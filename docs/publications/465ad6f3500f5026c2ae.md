# Introduction to Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel&\#x27;s Phenomenology of Spirit

**Autores:** Martin Sjardijn

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Foreword To gain a better understanding of Hegel’s Phenomenology of Spirit and to grasp the significance of his thought, I used AI as a modern form of the Socratic midwife: not as an authority that supplies ready\-made answers, but as a conversation partner that stimulates my own thinking through questions, counterquestions, and the examination of contradictions\. This maieutic approach enabled me to explore Hegel’s complex concepts from different perspectives and to relate their meaning to the technological and philosophical questions of our time\. From this dialogue emerged the question of how Hegel’s concept of Absolute Knowing relates to contemporary human beings, who in the age of Large Language Models \(LLMs\) have gained access to an unprecedented quantity of knowledge and intellectual connections\. Does this technological development represent a new shape of the human spirit, or does it instead bring into sharper focus the difference between having knowledge available and actually living through that knowledge? The article below is the result of this exploration\. It does not claim to offer a definitive explanation of Hegel, but seeks to put his thought back into question in light of the current relationship between human beings, machines, and consciousness\. I regard AI not only as a tool for accessing philosophical knowledge, but also as an occasion to examine critically what thinking, understanding, and being human mean in our time\. In this way, the use of AI itself becomes part of the philosophical question: can a machine help us understand Absolute Knowing without itself being the embodiment of it?
