# Antony, Michael Verne: Consciousness, Content, and Cognitive Architecture

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This thesis consists of three essays in the philosophy of mind\. Essay 1 contains an argument against functionalist theories of consciousness\. The argument exploits an intuition to the effect that parts of an individual&\#x27;s brain that are not in use at a time t, can have no bearing whatever on whether that individual is conscious at t\. After presenting the argument, I defend it against two possible objections, and then distinguish it from two arguments which appear, on the surface, to be similar to the argument of this essay\. ;Essay 2 takes up Tyler Burge&\#x27;s thesis, based on his thought experiments in &\#x27;Individualism and the Mental&\#x27;, that propositional attitudes are properties individuals have in virtue of social relations they bear to other language users\. An alternative interpretation of Burge&\#x27;s thought experiments is offered on which the intuitions Burge evokes can be accepted while his conclusions about the social character of thought are denied\. The alternative interpretation given, I go on to argue that it is preferable to Burge&\#x27;s\. ;Essay 3 concerns Fodor and Pylyshyn&\#x27;s argument against connectionism as a theory of the cognitive architecture\. That argument contains two premises: first, that the Language of Thought is true at the level of cognitive architecture, and second, that connectionism and the Language of Thought are incompatible\. I argue that Fodor and Pylyshyn&\#x27;s defenses of both premises fail, and I provide positive reasons for supposing the second premise is false\. \(direct link \)
