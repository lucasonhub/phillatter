# Sheep pulmonary image recognition \(SPIRO\): An explainable deep learning framework for macroscopic assessment of ovine pulmonary lesions

**Autores:** Muhammad Furqan Arshad; Tesfalem Mehari Berhe; Giovanni Lai; Ludovica Mura; Antonio Varcasia; Maria Luisa Pinna Parpaglia; Elisabetta Antuofermo; Marta Polinas; Marina Antonella Sanna; Maria Giovanna Cancedda; Md Sabbir Ahmed; Delaram Ghadir; Setayesh Ghadir; Stefano Giordano; Ciriaco Ligios; Davide Adami; Giovanni Pietro Burrai

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate detection and classification of pulmonary diseases in sheep are critical for ensuring animal welfare, food safety, and effective disease surveillance in abattoir settings\. In this study, we propose a two\-stage, explainable, histopathology\-informed deep learning framework for the automated classification of ovine lung lesions\. The dataset comprises 1052 sheep lung images, annotated through a dual\-level diagnostic process involving macroscopic inspection and histopathological confirmation, and classified as healthy, inflammatory, or neoplastic\. Four convolutional neural network architectures\-VGG16, VGG19, ResNet50, and EfficientNetB0\-were benchmarked under identical conditions\. EfficientNetB0 achieved the highest observed standalone performance and was integrated into a two\-stage pipeline, in which YOLOv11 first localized lung regions and EfficientNetB0 subsequently classified the cropped images\. Model interpretability was evaluated using Gradient\-weighted Class Activation Mapping \(Grad\-CAM\)\. The YOLOv11\-EfficientNetB0 pipeline achieved an overall test accuracy of 90% and a weighted F1\-score of 0\.88\. Neoplastic lesions were classified with the highest performance, reaching precision, recall, and F1\-score values of 0\.98, 0\.99, and 0\.99, respectively, while healthy and inflammatory lungs achieved F1\-scores of 0\.83 and 0\.82, respectively\. Grad\-CAM visualizations indicated that predictions were mainly driven by diagnostically relevant pulmonary regions\. These findings demonstrate the feasibility of explainable artificial intelligence for objective, scalable, and biologically grounded post\-mortem assessment of sheep lungs, with potential applications in abattoir surveillance, veterinary pathology, and flock health monitoring\.
