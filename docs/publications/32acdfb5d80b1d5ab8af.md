# From Geographic Immersion to Digital Immersion: Rethinking English Language Learning Without Study Abroad

**Autores:** Khalid Al\-Seghayer

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Study abroad has traditionally been regarded as one of the most effective contexts for second\-language development, providing learners with extensive target\-language exposure, sustained interaction, and contact with culturally situated communicative practices\. However, the expansion of digital communication challenges the assumption that meaningful language immersion requires geographical mobility\. Learners who remain in their home countries can participate in international communities, engage in virtual exchange, access authentic audiovisual and written materials, and use English for academic, professional, and social purposes\. Drawing on research in second\-language acquisition, study abroad, computer\-mediated communication, telecollaboration, virtual exchange, and learner autonomy, this article argues that immersion is more productively conceptualized as sustained and meaningful engagement with a target language than as physical presence in a particular geographical location\. To operationalize this concept, the article proposes a seven\-dimensional model of digital immersion encompassing input, interaction, output, feedback, intercultural engagement, identity, and autonomy\. The model provides a framework for examining the extent to which digital environments create conditions conducive to sustained language development\. At the same time, digital immersion cannot fully reproduce the embodied interaction, spontaneous encounters, and physically situated cultural experiences associated with study abroad\. Nevertheless, when deliberately structured, digital environments can provide substantial opportunities for language input, interaction, output, intercultural learning, and learner agency\. The article concludes by discussing implications for curriculum design, digital literacy, authentic assessment, and educational accessibility, and considers how digital immersion may reshape the future of English language learning\.
