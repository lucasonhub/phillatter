# Empowering Urban Design: Leveraging Deep Learning and Crowdsourced Data for Spatial Justice — a Mixed\-Methods Case Study Of Constantine, Algeria

**Autores:** Badr\-Eddine BOUMALI; Nedjwa Elafri; Abdallah Lalmi

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Rapid urbanisation in North Africa frequently neglects the diverse needs of citizens, resulting in spatial injustice\. This research investigates the disconnect between top\-down planning and actual space utilisation in Constantine, Algeria, by testing an integrated framework that combines citizen\-generated data with deep learning techniques\. Based on critical realism, a convergent parallel mixed\-methods design was employed over a six\-month period \(January–June 2024\), involving the recruitment of 300 residents \(100 per district\) through stratified quota sampling across the central, intermediate, and peripheral zones\. Participants utilised a trilingual \(Arabic/French/Berber\) mobile application to submit geotagged observations of public space usage; a total of 8, 742 observations were collected\. Concurrently, a ResNet\-50 convolutional neural network was fine\-tuned on Sentinel\-2 imagery \(10\-meter resolution; eight land\-use classes\) using stratified 5\-fold cross\-validation, a class\-weighted cross\-entropy loss function, and Bayesian hyper parameter optimisation\. Civic engagement was assessed through a validated 20\-item composite index at baseline and conclusion; paired\-sample t\-tests with Cohen&\#x27;s d and bootstrapped 95% confidence intervals quantified changes\. The composite civic behaviour score increased from 2\. 14/10 to 7\. 79/10, representing a 264% relative rise \(95% CI: 241–287%; d = 2\. 87; t \(272\) = 18\. 42, p &lt; \.001; n = 273 completers\), which should be interpreted as an association rather than causation given the single\-group pre\-post study design\.
