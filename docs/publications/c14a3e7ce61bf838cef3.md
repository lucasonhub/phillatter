## Resumo

Background and Aim: Faced with rising antimicrobial resistance, environmental concerns, and growing consumer demand for clean\-label food products, applied food biotechnology plays a pivotal role in establishing sustainable, resilient, and health\-oriented food networks\. This manuscript synthesizes recent advancements across three interconnected domains: natural antimicrobials, industrial fermentation, and probiotic delivery systems\. Material and Methods: Critical analysis of the manuscript publiched in last years from Applied Food Biotechnology was performed\. Results and Conclusion: In biopreservation, natural antimicrobials, including plant essential oils, polyphenols, lytic bacteriophages, and microbial bacteriocins, offer non\-toxic alternatives to synthetic chemical additives\. These bioactive compounds disrupt cell membranes and suppress quorum sensing, effectively controlling persistent pathogens such as Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli O157:H7, and Listeria monocytogenes without promoting cross\-resistance\. These delivery systems safeguard microbial viability during thermal processing and gastrointestinal transit, facilitating targeted delivery to support gut homeostasis, immune modulation, and metabolic regulation\. Together, these multidisciplinary strategies bridge fundamental microbiology with industrial applications to build safer, sustainable food systems\. INTRODUCTION Knowledge is rarely built in isolation; it thrives at the intersection of diverse disciplines\. In multidisciplinary fields like applied food biotechnology, progress depends on bridging microbiology, biochemistry, food engineering, and green technology\. Applied Food Biotechnology is a journal that welcomes a wide variety of topics acting as a vibrant ecosystem of ideas\. By bringing together research on natural biopreservatives, precision fermentation, strain engineering, and bio\-nanotechnology, it reflects a core truth: variety is the foundation of modern understanding\. Embracing a spectrum of perspectives breaks rigid scientific silos, encouraging cross\-pollination where a discovery in one domain sparks innovation in another\. Ultimately, celebrating thematic breadth equips us to navigate complex global food challenges with agility, creativity, and lasting resilience\. The modern landscape of food science, human health, and agricultural sustainability faces unprecedented challenges\. The global rise of antimicrobial resistance \(AMR\), driven by the overuse of conventional antibiotics, poses a major threat to public health and clinical medicine\. Concurrently, changing climate patterns, expanding international supply chains, and evolving consumer demands are forcing the food industry to transition away from synthetic chemical additives, artificial preservatives, and environmentally detrimental manufacturing work\-flows\. In response to these pressing global issues, the integration of natural antimicrobials, food microbiology, and functional probiotic delivery systems has emerged as a crucial paradigm for the next generation of safe, resilient, and health\-oriented food systems\. At the center of this paradigm shift is the exploitation of natural antimicrobial compounds derived from plants, animals, and beneficial microorganisms\. Bioactive molecules, such as plant essential oils, polyphenols, microbial bacteriocins, and lytic bacteriophages, offer potent, non\-toxic alternatives to traditional chemical pesticides and synthetic food preservatives\. Unlike single\-target synthetic drugs, natural antimicrobials frequently operate via complex, multi\-target mechanisms, such as bacterial cell membrane disruption, cell wall lysis, or the suppression of quorum sensing gene expression, which drastically lowers the likelihood of target pathogens developing cross\-resistance\. Integrating these natural biopreservatives into food matrices effectively inhibits persistent foodborne pathogens \(including Staph\. aureus, Escherichia coli O157:H7, Listeria monocytogenes, and Clostridium species\) while extending product shelf\-life and ensuring consumer safety\. Finally, the convergence of food microbiology and probiotic research continues to expand the therapeutic landscape of functional nutrition\. Beyond traditional fermented dairy products, innovative plant\-based matrices, postbiotic supernatants, and advanced microencapsulation technologies \(e\.g\., electrospun nanofibers, bio\-nanocomposites, and chitosan complexes\) are critical for preserving microbial viability against thermal processing and gastrointestinal stress\. These delivery vehicles enable targeted intestinal release, supporting host gut homeostasis, immune modulation, and metabolic regulation, such as dyslipidemia and blood glucose control \[1\]\. Collectively, recent advancements in applied food biotechnology bridge fundamental biological research with practical industrial applications\. By uniting bio\-preservation, metabolic strain engineering, bioprocess optimization, and targeted functional delivery, scientific innovation continues to build sustainable, safe, and health\-promoting global food networks for the future\.
