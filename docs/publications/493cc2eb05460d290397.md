# The Impact of Social Media on the Realization of the Right to a Fair Trial in the Republic of Kosovo

**Autores:** Valeri Qatani

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Social media may create conditions that influence public perceptions and potentially exert pressure on judicial actors\. Using statistics obtained directly from citizens of the Republic of Kosovo, this paper examines how social media has influenced the realization of judicial impartiality\. However, this paper particularly emphasises the negative role of social media in the realization of judicial impartiality\. Another challenge is that public opinion is shaped by how information is distributed through social media and how convictions are formed in society\. The impact of social media is inevitable, as we are witnessing enough cases in practice; the prejudices transmitted on social media are reaching their peak and are being perceived by society\. Method: This study employed a quantitative survey\-based research design complemented by an analysis of selected judicial cases\. The study collected empirical data through a structured questionnaire administered to 492 respondents in the Republic of Kosovo\. The questionnaire included five\-point Likert scale items designed to test three research hypotheses concerning public opinion formation, the presumption of innocence, and perceived pressure on judicial actors\. The data were analysed using descriptive statistics and interpreted alongside selected high\-profile criminal cases and the applicable legal framework\. Results and Conclusions: The results indicate that social media influences public opinion about judicial processes in the Republic of Kosovo, clearly shaping how the public perceives them\. Furthermore, exposing judicial cases on social media jeopardises the presumption of innocence, as public information and commentary can create bias before the judicial process concludes\. At the same time, social media can compromise judicial impartiality through public pressure and the influence of social perceptions on judicial decision\-making\.
