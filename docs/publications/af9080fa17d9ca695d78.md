# Strategies for widening participation in the biomedical science at London South Bank University—a case study

**Autores:** Ruth Gyereh Dogwul; Bernadine Idowu; Eiman A\. Aleem

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction and context Black, Asian and Minority Ethnic \(BAME\) professionals make essential contributions to UK healthcare, biomedical science research, academia, and the life sciences industry, yet remain underrepresented in senior roles\. Three barriers recur: limited access to the discipline, a weak sense of belonging, and few visible role models\. This study presents targeted strategies implemented within London South Bank University&\#x27;s Biomedical Science Programme \(BMS\) to address these barriers at key stages of students&\#x27; career development journey\. Aims This case study had two linked Aims: \(1\) to describe strategies addressing these barriers, and \(2\) to examine, through a survey of BMS alumni and students, whether barriers were experienced, whether strategies reached them, and what remained unmet\. Methods Initiatives included the Black in Biomed conference, providing role models and networking; STEM workshops for school pupils; and BMS Day outreach, offering hands\-on engagement and diverse representation\. An anonymous Qualtrics survey examined the same three areas: role models and mentors, when students first learned about biomedical science careers, support and inclusion and how the course prepared them for their careers\. Results The Black in Biomed conference was valued for early career exposure, mentorship, and representation\. Among survey respondents, 76% were BAME, 94% rated the course positively, and 78% reported access to BAME role models or mentors\. Forty percent had attended a widening participation event and reported positive influence\. However, 63% first learned about biomedical science at sixth form or college, while 33% reported financial constraints\. Conclusion Programme strategies address barriers to access, belonging, and representation, while the survey identifies where these persist\. Inclusive, career\-focused initiatives embedded within the curriculum may support equity and representation\. Priorities include earlier career guidance, relatable role models, hands\-on experience, and financial support\. The initiatives presented in this work could be easily introduced to other programmes\.
