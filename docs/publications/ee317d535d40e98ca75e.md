# Abdi, Abdulaziz: The Structural Limits of Consciousness: A Constraint\-Based Analysis

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Debates about consciousness are typically framed around questions of origin, essence, or subjective experience\. This paper adopts a different approach\. Rather than asking what consciousness is, it investigates the structural conditions under which consciousness becomes inoperable, even when intelligence, learning, and functional behavior remain intact\. Drawing on a constraint\-based methodology analogous to recent work in alignment theory, the paper argues that consciousness is not guaranteed by computation, scale, or behavioral sophistication, but depends on a set of architectural preconditions: sustained integration across subsystems, non\-delegable internal evaluation, ownership of experience, irreversibility of internal cost, and resistance to total legibility and optimization\. By analyzing how these constraints fail under abstraction, proxy optimization, and externalized value formation, the paper demonstrates that systems can remain highly capable while consciousness collapses structurally\. This reframing clarifies why appeals to emergence, scale, or intelligence alone are insufficient, and why many artificial systems are not merely non\-conscious in practice but structurally incapable of consciousness by design\. The result is a diagnostic framework that shifts the study of consciousness from metaphysical speculation to architectural limits, with implications for philosophy of mind, cognitive science, and artificial intelligence research\.
