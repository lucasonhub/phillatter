# An unmanned aerial vehicle\-assisted visual measurement framework for forest road condition assessment with overlapping distress quantification

**Autores:** Lin Zhao; Xinmin Tao

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Routine forest\-road maintenance is essential for road safety, accessibility, and serviceability, yet current inspections remain labor\-intensive\. Existing visual methods usually quantify pavement distresses independently, which may introduce area\-estimation errors and affect condition assessment when multiple distresses spatially overlap\. To address this engineering challenge, this study proposes an engineering artificial intelligence framework integrating Transformer\-based semantic segmentation and diffusion\-based data augmentation for unmanned aerial vehicle\-assisted multi\-distress measurement, overlapping\-distress quantification, and forest\-road condition assessment\. This framework incorporates a segmentation model that converts aerial images into pixel\-level distress masks for area measurement\. The segmentation model employs a dynamic\-gated convolutional backbone to integrate local textures with global context and a frequency\-modulated spatial attention decoder to enhance small\-scale and weakly visible distresses\. A two\-stage overlapping\-distress identification strategy is developed to localize overlapping regions, identify their constituent distress categories, and support overlap\-aware area quantification and condition assessment\. A multi\-condition guided diffusion model is further introduced to enrich inspection\-image diversity, and a Forest Road Daily Inspection dataset is constructed for model training and evaluation\. For engineering assessment, a Forest Pavement Condition Index is developed by incorporating measured areas of multiple distress types and overlapping regions, enabling section\-level condition evaluation and maintenance prioritization\. Experiments on the constructed forest\-road inspection dataset and a public benchmark demonstrate that the proposed segmentation model improves multi\-distress segmentation, overlapping\-region identification, and measurement\-oriented condition assessment, supporting its potential for forest\-road condition measurement and maintenance\-oriented assessment under the tested conditions\.
