## Descrição do feed

Abstract Singer is stronger wherever the question is one of measurement and scope — who counts, how much, compared with what\. The Philosophy of Virtues is stronger wherever the question is one of constitution and protection\. The table summarizes the comparison, with the relevant explanations set out below\. Peter Singer’s utilitarianism and the Philosophy of Virtues are frequently described as returning opposite verdicts on the same cases, and the description is usually illustrated by compelled redistribution\. This paper argues that the illustration is wrong and that the underlying disagreement is both deeper and more tractable than the illustration suggests\. Singer’s flagship argument is addressed to agents and establishes a duty; it does not establish an enforcement, and the Inversion Theorem applies only where freedom is actively replaced by compulsion\. The two frameworks therefore do not meet where they are usually said to meet\. They meet at a different point: whether the reason for which an act is performed contributes to the act’s moral identity\. Eight structural dimensions are specified — locus of value, bearer, the role of the agent’s reason, aggregation, time, foundation, coercion, and self\-presentation — with a test fixed in advance for each, chosen so that either framework can lose\. The adjudication returns three decisive advantages to the Philosophy of Virtues and one to Singer, two further advantages to the programme and one to Singer at reparable cost, and one draw\. The indirect consequentialist reply — Hare’s two\-level model, Railton’s sophisticated consequentialism, Adams’s motive utilitarianism — is engaged directly, and it moves one verdict’s scope\. Four formal results are established: an asymmetric extensibility theorem showing that the locus dispute is a matter of unequal cost rather than of equal residues; a debunking symmetry result showing that no genealogical exemption criterion is available for the impartiality axiom; an esoteric inversion theorem showing that a theory licensing its own concealment reproduces internally the structure it is meant to evaluate; and a capacity\-production gap showing that a framework treating agents as fixed converters cannot compute its own long\-run optimum\. Three amendments are then imposed on the Philosophy of Virtues, one of which — the absence of any doctrine of moral patiency — is recorded as an unrepaired defeat\. Relevance Virtue\-theoretic programmes are habitually assessed by referees trained inside consequentialism, and the assessment usually turns on whether the programme can state its disagreement in terms the referee recognises\. This paper supplies that statement\. It also supplies the reverse service: it identifies the two dimensions on which the strongest living consequentialist holds a structural advantage that the present programme cannot currently answer, and records them as losses rather than as open questions\. \(direct link \)
