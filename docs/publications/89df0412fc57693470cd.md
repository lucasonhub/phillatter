# Minamikata, Juza: A Non\-Inheritance Principle for Consciousness Research: Evidence, Classification, and Common Structure

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Consciousness research routinely moves from evidence to classifications, and from classifications to claims about common underlying structure\. This paper argues that such transitions require independent justification\. It develops a Non\-Inheritance Principle: warrant established for one analytical target, grain, inferential role, or validated scope does not by itself warrant a different target, grain, role, or broader scope\. The principle is operationalized through a Source–Target–Warrant–Scope audit for identifying where evidential support is preserved, where an additional bridge is required, and where a transfer exceeds its validated range\. The framework is applied to multidimensional accounts of consciousness, no\-report paradigms, natural\-kind approaches, color perception, pain, skilled action, dreaming, cognitive motor dissociation, unity, boundary, and conscious content\. A worked analysis of cognitive motor dissociation shows how evidence may strongly support task\-specific internal processing while leaving a further consciousness classification conditional on an additional bridge\. The paper also distinguishes classificatory, evidential, and state commonality, and asks what would be required to establish experience as a genuine common variable rather than merely naming a remainder after decomposition\. The view is non\-eliminativist: common consciousness structure may exist, but it cannot be inherited from shared terminology, convergent evidence, or established classifications alone\. \(direct link \)
