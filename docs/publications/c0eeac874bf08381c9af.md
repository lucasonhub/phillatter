# Genomic Epidemiology and Antimicrobial Resistance of Salmonella enterica serovars Typhimurium and Enteritidis

**Autores:** Samitha Bandaranayake Mudiyanselage

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Salmonella enterica serovars Typhimurium \(STm\) and Enteritidis \(SEn\) are leading causes of foodborne illnesses globally with poultry acting as a major reservoir\. The increasing availability of whole genome sequencing data has enabled high resolution investigation of epidemiology, transmission, and antimicrobial resistance \(AMR\)\. The scale and complexity of genomic datasets require robust analytical processes to generate actionable public health insights\. This thesis integrates multilevel genome typing \(MGT\) and machine learning \(ML\) approaches to investigate the transmission dynamics, AMR patterns and predictive epidemiology of STm and SEn\. Analysis of STm isolates from the Australian meat chicken production system revealed substantial genomic diversity, widespread sharing of MGT5 sequence types \(STs\) across multiple production stages and across processing plants\. At MGT9 \(highest resolution level\) 30 STs exhibited 18 transmission patterns with hatcheries and breeder farms acting as key transmission points\. No identical MGT8 or MGT9 STs were observed in both poultry and human isolates\. However, 15 MGT5 STs from chicken isolates matched human infections in Australia and globally\. AMR in meat chicken STm was rare, with only sporadic detection of the blaTEM\-1 gene, suggesting limited resistance within these populations\. Global analysis of over 60,000 publicly available SEn genomes demonstrated dynamic AMR trends, including an increase in ciprofloxacin intermediate resistance over time\. Overall, resistance was relatively low with &lt;6% of the isolates resistant to one or two antibiotics, and &lt;4% multidrug\-resistant \(MDR, ≥ 3 antibiotics\)\. The proportion of SEn isolates predicted to have intermediate resistance to ciprofloxacin increased from 26% in 2015\-2019 to 37\.5% in 2020\-2024 \(27% to 34% in the UK, 8% to 49% in the USA\), primarily driven by mutations in the gyrA gene\. Distinct AMR associated MGT STs were identified: 68 MGT STs resistant to one or two antibiotics and 26 to MDR\. ML models were developed using core genome \(MGT9\) allele profiles and other genomic features to predict epidemiological trends\. Random Forest models were found to achieve high predictive performance for STm source attribution, SEn continent origin, and SEn lineage prevalence\. Overall, this thesis demonstrates the utility of MGT for understanding STm transmission in the meat chicken production chain and tracking AMR\-associated SEn globally, supporting genomic surveillance for realtime monitoring and targeted interventions\.
