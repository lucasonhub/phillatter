# Digital Health Literacy and Health\-Seeking Behaviour Among Hospital Outpatients: Evidence from Traditional, Professional and Online Search Patterns

**Autores:** Polat Tunçer; Müberra Dinçer

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction\. Digital health literacy may shape how patients seek health information in increasingly digitalized environments where patients use computers, smartphones, tablets and AI\-supported search tools to access health information\. This study examined the association between digital health literacy competencies and traditional, professional and online health\-seeking behaviour among hospital outpatients\. Methods\. A cross\-sectional descriptive\-correlational survey was conducted with 400 volunteer outpatients aged 18 years and older at a training and research hospital in Türkiye\. Participants were recruited equally from pulmonology, internal medicine, obstetrics and gynaecology, ophthalmology and cardiology clinics using quota\-based convenience sampling\. Data were collected using demographic questions, the Health\-Seeking Behaviour Scale and the Digital Health Literacy Competency Scale for Citizens\. Nonparametric group comparisons and Spearman correlation analyses were performed using SPSS 26\. Results\. Traditional health\-seeking behaviour had the highest mean score, followed by professional and online health\-seeking behaviour\. Online health\-seeking behaviour was positively correlated with health information literacy \(r\_s=\.446, p&lt;\.001\) and digital competence \(r\_s=\.493, p&lt;\.001\)\. Online health\-seeking behaviour, health information literacy and digital competence decreased with age and increased with education\. Women reported higher online health\-seeking behaviour and digital competence than men\. Conclusion\. Digital health literacy competencies were most clearly associated with online health\-seeking behaviour, while traditional and professional sources remained important\. Outpatient clinics may be useful settings for practical, age\-sensitive and education\-sensitive digital health literacy support\.
