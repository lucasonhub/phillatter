# The Well\-Being of British South Asian Musicians in the UK

**Autores:** Chelsea Kielash Persad

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Currently, British South Asian musicians are significantly underrepresented in academic research on well\-being in the UK creative industries\. Literature on musicians have typically focused on white Western experiences as well as have frequently adopted clinical frameworks which often overlook social, structural and cultural realities experienced by minoritised artists\. This thesis addresses this gap in the research by exploring the ways in which British South Asian musicians experience, understand and navigate the subject of well\-being\. This study employs in\-depth qualitative interviews and focuses on the lived experiences of musicians who work across a wide range of genres\. This research also utilises a broader definition of what it means to be a musician\. As defined in this thesis, a musician must satisfy the following criteria: a predisposition towards music, self\-identification as a musician and at least six years of expertise and skill\. This allows for a more inclusive and culturally sensitive exploration of well\- being which goes beyond clinical or productivity\-focused models often found in formal music education or in the medical field\. The findings of this thesis revealed that the well\-being of British South Asian musicians was shaped by a complex interplay of cultural expectations, economic precarity, underrepresentation as well as industry\-related challenges such as self\-marketing, emotional labour and knowledge gaps due to a limited access to insider networks\. Additionally, interviewees frequently experienced tensions between fulfilling their creative ambitions and supporting themselves financially\. They also found difficulty with navigating multiple identities and assessing their value as creatives within predominantly white spaces\. Mental health stigmas within South Asian communities as well as a limited knowledge of support structures available to them proved to be barriers to their well\-being\. Music, as expected, emerged as a necessary coping mechanism and avenue for self\-expression\. The process of creating music oNered interviewees a sense of purpose as well as provided opportunities for emotional regulation and for creating community and connection\. Most musicians relied heavily on their art for its therapeutic benefits and interviews indicated a lesser emphasis on clinical approaches such as therapy or medication\. Physical exercise was also emphasised as a crucial component for their well\-being\. By centring the voices of fifteen British South Asian musicians, this thesis provides an interdisciplinary exploration of well\-being which spans several fields such as musicology, cultural studies, creative industries and public health\. As a result, this work provides the foundations for future research towards the creation of a well\-being framework for musicians which is both culturally sensitive and structurally informed\.
