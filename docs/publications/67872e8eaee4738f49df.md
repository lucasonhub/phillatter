# Dietary frequency, body satisfaction, anxiety symptoms, and depressive symptoms among offspring of healthcare workers in China

**Autores:** Tingke Xu; Rongxiu Ding; Chao Lin; Qiuhua Zhang; Pan Ding

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Dietary behaviors may be associated with body image and emotional health in young people, but evidence integrating these outcomes in Chinese populations remains limited\. This study examined associations of dietary frequency with body satisfaction, anxiety symptoms, and depressive symptoms\. Methods This cross\-sectional study included 584 offspring of healthcare workers aged 10–24 years in Zhejiang, China\. A 10\-item food\-frequency questionnaire generated a frequency\-based score reflecting more frequent consumption of meat, fish, vegetables, fruit, eggs, soy products, and dairy products and less frequent consumption of pickled, sugary, and fried foods\. Multivariable logistic regression and restricted cubic splines were used to assess associations and dose–response patterns\. Counterfactual mediation analysis estimated indirect associations through body satisfaction\. Results The proportions screening positive for anxiety and depressive symptoms were 23\.1 and 33\.6%, respectively\. In fully adjusted models, higher dietary frequency scores were associated with higher odds of body satisfaction \(OR = 1\.21, 95% CI: 1\.02–1\.44\) and lower odds of anxiety \(OR = 0\.65, 95% CI: 0\.52–0\.79\) and depressive symptoms \(OR = 0\.84, 95% CI: 0\.70–0\.99\)\. Restricted cubic spline analyses indicated approximately linear associations, with no evidence of nonlinearity\. For each 1\-SD increase in the dietary frequency score, indirect associations through higher body satisfaction were observed for anxiety \(OR = 0\.97, 95% CI: 0\.93–0\.99; 6\.8%\) and depressive symptoms \(OR = 0\.96, 95% CI: 0\.92–0\.99; 17\.3%\)\. Conclusion Higher dietary frequency scores were associated with greater body satisfaction and lower odds of anxiety and depressive symptoms among healthcare workers’ offspring\. These findings cannot be generalized to other young people in China\.
