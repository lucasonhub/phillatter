# Accelerating Ghana’s sustainable transport transition: the role of electric vehicles in achieving SDG 7 and SDG 13

**Autores:** Ebenezer Arthur Duncan; Samuel Affran

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Urban transport in Ghana relies heavily on imported fuel and contributes significantly to emissions\. Electric vehicles have been proposed as an alternative, but we still know little about what shapes drivers’ willingness to adopt them in major city corridors\. Methods We used Social Cognitive Theory to assess how two factors \- tax incentives and environmental awareness \- relate to three self\-reported outcomes: intention to adopt EVs, sustainable energy consumption, and beliefs about greenhouse gas emissions\. We did not measure actual emissions\. The survey covered 800 commercial drivers and private car owners recruited from transport union hubs in Accra, Tema, Kumasi, and Takoradi, and we analyzed the data with PLS\-SEM, including tests for moderation\. Results Drivers who reported higher tax incentives and greater environmental awareness also reported stronger EV adoption intentions and more sustainable energy use, and lower perceived emissions\. Environmental awareness moderated these links: it amplified the link between tax incentives and adoption intentions but dampened the links between tax incentives and both energy consumption and perceived emissions\. Discussion Among unionized operators in these four urban corridors, tax incentives and environmental awareness are linked to intentions and perceptions, not to verified behavior or emission cuts\. Because the sample is urban and union\-based, the results cannot be extended to all of Ghana, and the model does not show whether intending to adopt an EV actually lowers emissions\.
