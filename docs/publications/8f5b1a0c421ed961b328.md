# Association between infant crawling characteristics and fundamental movement skill development in preschool children

**Autores:** Yuehui Zhou; Huizi Yin; Yingxiang Fang; Bingao Chen; Juhua Wu; Yang Yuan

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examined the associations between infant crawling experience and crawling characteristics and fundamental movement skill \(FMS\) development in preschool children to characterize population\-level associations and generate hypotheses for prospective research\. A retrospective cross\-sectional design was used, including 557 children aged 3–6 years from kindergartens in Qufu, Shandong Province, and the surrounding areas\. Infant crawling experience and crawling characteristics were collected retrospectively through guardian questionnaires, and FMS was assessed using the Test of Gross Motor Development–Third Edition \(TGMD\-3\)\. Children with a total FMS score below the 25th percentile were classified as having relatively insufficient FMS development, and a multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to examine these associations\. Compared with children with relatively sufficient crawling experience, those with insufficient crawling experience had lower locomotor skill and ball skill scores, as well as lower total FMS scores\. These findings suggest that insufficient crawling experience and selected crawling characteristics, particularly daily crawling duration and crawling\-posture diversity, are statistically associated with relatively insufficient FMS development in preschool children; however, their significance requires further confirmation in prospective studies\.
