# Rectal colonization with multidrug\-resistant organisms in adult acute leukemia patients undergoing allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation: a retrospective, longitudinal, single\-center study

**Autores:** Edoardo Tamellini; Chiara Bonetto; Annarita Mazzariol; Fulvia Mazzaferri; Erica Diani; Francesca Tolio; Sara Pagnin; Alessandra Vatteroni; Angela Crema; Alberto Schena; Cristina Tecchio

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Rectal colonization with multidrug\-resistant organisms \(MDROs\) represents a frequent issue in patients undergoing allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation \(allo\-HSCT\) for acute leukemia\. However, the prognostic significance of MDRO\-colonization in these patients remains debated\. Methods This retrospective, longitudinal, single\-center study included 100 patients with acute myeloid or lymphoblastic leukemia treated at Verona University Hospital from diagnosis to allo\-HSCT\. MDRO\-colonization was monitored by rectal swab at admission and weekly during hospitalization\. Colonized patients underwent targeted antibiotic therapy when clinically required\. Aim of the study To assess epidemiology, timing, and impact of rectal MDRO\-colonization on infection occurrence, infection\-related mortality \(IRM\) within day \+100, overall survival \(OS\), and transplant\-related mortality \(TRM\)\. Results Rectal colonization with 44 MDRO strains was detected in 35 \(35%\) patients undergoing allo\-HSCT, either at hospital admission \(45\.7%\) or during hospitalization \(54\.3%\)\. Enterobacterales accounted for 72\.7% of MDROs, with 68\.5% of colonizing strains being detected during previous hospital stays for induction or consolidation therapies\. MDRO\-related bloodstream infections \(BSI\) occurred in 9 \(9%\) patients, 7 \(77\.8%\) of whom being rectal carriers\. IRM within day \+100 was null\. OS was significantly lower in MDRO\-carriers as compared to non\-carriers only at univariate analysis\. TRM did not differ significantly between the two groups in both univariate and multivariate analyses\. Conclusion Despite the limitations of a single\-center retrospective study, our data show that careful monitoring of rectal colonization and prompt use of targeted antibiotics may temper the negative impact of MDRO\-related infections in adult acute leukemia patients undergoing allo\-HSCT, even in the setting of a higher rate of BSIs\.
