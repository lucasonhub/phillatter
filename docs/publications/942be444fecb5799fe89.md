# Numerical Simulation Engines and Deep Learning Architectures for Inverse Design of Multilayer Metamaterials

**Autores:** Federico Rodas; Laura Malache; Osmani Mujica Pérez

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The inverse design of multilayer metamaterials —artificial structures with engineered electromagnetic, acoustic, thermal, or mechanical properties— represents a high\-dimensional optimisation challenge that has driven the integration of deep learning into computational photonics and materials science\. This paper presents a systematic literature review conducted under the PRISMA 2020 protocol, aimed at mapping the numerical simulation methods employed as forward engines or training data generators within deep learning\-assisted inverse design pipelines\. Starting from 392 records retrieved from Scopus and Web of Science over the 2010–2025 period, a multi\-stage screening process based on a heuristic prioritisation index termed CARS \(Citation\-Adjusted Relevance Score\) identified 44 core articles\. Eleven distinct numerical methods were identified and organised into two conceptual layers: classical simulation engines \(finite element method, topology optimisation, guided\-mode expansion, adjoint method, and genetic algorithms\) and deep learning architectures \(tandem CNN, transformer, GAN, PINN, and diffractive neural networks\)\. Results show a strong dominance of the electromagnetic\-photonic domain \(72%\), accelerated growth of deep learning\-based publications from 2022 onward, and a clear trend toward hybrid physics, data and learning pipelines\. The review concludes by proposing a prioritised experimental roadmap for future research\.
