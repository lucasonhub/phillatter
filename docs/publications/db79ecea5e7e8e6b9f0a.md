# Towards a Digital Twin for the Quantitative Risk Assessment of Domino Effects in Fuel Storage Facilities

**Autores:** Yanelys Cuba Arana; Felipe San Luis Gutiérrez; Deivis Ávila; Jesús Luis Orozco; Ramón Quiza

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Fuel storage facilities concentrate large inventories of flammable substances, so that a single primary accident may escalate, through thermal radiation, into a cascading sequence involving several tanks \(domino effect\)\. This work presents the probabilistic core of a digital twin for such facilities, understood as a probabilistic replica of the facility with a fast query interface \(live\-data assimilation is not yet implemented\): a permanently available, interactively queryable model that characterises, in probabilistic terms, the thermal radiation field generated by accident scenarios in a storage facility and derives from it an integrated assessment of its safety and economic risk\. The framework combines \(i\) a Monte Carlo simulation of 108 facility\-years, in which primary accidents and their fire\-driven escalation are sampled using validated models of pool fires, thermal radiation and equipment failure; \(ii\) a point\-wise condensation of the resulting radiation intensity into Gamma distributions fitted by maximum likelihood, whose parameter maps embody the frequency of every fire configuration; and \(iii\) a machine\-learning surrogate, based on Generalized Regression Neural Networks, that renders the probabilistic field continuous over the terrain and evaluable in a fraction of a millisecond\. On this backbone, the model delivers mutually consistent, receptor\-level indicators: annual exceedance frequencies of harm thresholds \(contour maps\), the expected number of fatalities, distinguishing sheltered and outdoor population, and the Expected Annual Loss of every asset\. In a demonstration on a five\-tank facility storing light naphtha, jet fuel and diesel within a mixed industrial–residential complex, 16\.5% of the accidents escalated to multiple tanks, the individual risk at the most exposed third\-party location lay more than one order of magnitude below the 10−6 per year land\-use criterion, and 97\.5% of the expected losses were borne by the facility itself\. The scope is limited to pool fires in fixed\-roof atmospheric tanks, in wind\-free conditions and without credited safety barriers\.
