# The Effect of Sensory Motor Training on Pain Outcomes in PostMenopausal Women with Non\-Specific Low Back Pain

**Autores:** 

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: The study aimed to examine the impact of sensory\-motor training \(SMT\) on pain outcomes, including pain intensity and pressure pain threshold, in postmenopausal women with nonspecific low back pain \(NSLBP\)\.Subjects: Fifty postmenopausal women, who complained of NSLBP for at least 3 months, participated in this study\.They were between 50 and 65 years old, they were at least 3 years post\-menopausal, and their BMI was &lt; 30 kg/m²\.Design: It was a randomized controlled trial\.Methods: Participants were equally and randomly allocated into two groups: Group A \(control group\) \(n = 25\) performed strengthening exercises for eight weeks, and Group B \(study group\) \(n = 25\) performed strengthening exercises in addition to SMT\.Both groups received their program three times weekly for eight weeks\.Assessment: The pressure algometry was utilized to measure pain pressure threshold \(PPT\); the Visual Analogue Scale \(VAS\) was utilized to measure pain severity for all patients in the two groups before and following the treatment\.Results: within groups, there was a statistically significant increase in mean values of PPT of the iliopsoas, quadratus lumborum, gluteus medius, and gluteus maximus muscles following the intervention compared with the pre\-intervention\.Additionally, there was a statistically significant decrease in pain intensity, as measured by the VAS, following the intervention compared with the pre\-intervention\.Between groups, before treatment, no statistically significant difference was noted among groups in any of the outcome measures\.However, following treatment, group B, the study group, exhibited a statistically significant variation in pain intensity, additionally; no statistically significant variation was indicated in the mean PPT values across both groups\.Conclusion: Adding sensorimotor training to the rehabilitation program for patients with NSLBP as been shown to reduce pain and improve functional performance\.
