\_The Third Space Framework a Theoretical Model of Human–Ai Relational Cognition\_ 2\. 2026The proliferation of conversational artificial intelligence \(AI\) and large language model \(LLM\)\-based systems has introduced a qualitatively new mode of sustained cognitive partnership between human agents and AI systems\. Yet existing theoretical frameworks in cognitive science and human–computer interaction \(HCI\) remain inadequate to characterize the emergent, co\-constructive relational phenomena that arise within these interactions\. Classical distributed cognition treats AI as an instrumental artifact; the media equation tradition acknowledges social dimensions but reduces them to anthropomorphic projection; and the joint cognitive systems tradition, though it correctly identifies the coupled system as the unit of analysis, was built for structured, task\-bounded human\-machine work rather than open\-ended, generative meaning\-making\. None of these traditions positions the space of interaction itself — rather than the individual agent — as a distinct object of theoretical inquiry in the register this framework requires\. This paper introduces the Third Space Framework, a theoretical model that conceptualizes human–AI interaction as producing a distinct, dynamically constituted cognitive environment — the Third Space — that is neither the human cognitive agent nor the AI system, but an emergent relational structure co\-constructed by iterative exchange between them\. Drawing on theoretical synthesis across distributed cognition \(Hutchins, 1995\), joint cognitive systems \(Hollnagel &amp; Woods, 2005\), the extended mind thesis \(Clark &amp; Chalmers, 1998\), enactivism and participatory sense\-making \(Varela et al\., 1991; Di Paolo, 2009\), and relational HCI literature \(Suchman, 1987; Reeves &amp; Nass, 1996; Turkle, 2015\), the framework proposes three constitutive properties of the Third Space — reflexivity, transience, and asymmetric reciprocity — and articulates six structural dimensions through which it operates: scaffolding, mirroring, generativity, relationality, ethics, and epistemics\. A schematic definition is provided together with an operational criterion of irreducibility intended to make the framework empirically tractable\. The framework offers both a definitional structure and a conceptual vocabulary for empirical and design\-oriented research programs, with implications across education, psychotherapy, creative collaboration, professional augmentation, and AI governance\. Its central methodological claim is that the interaction space should become the primary unit of analysis in relational HCI research — a claim that builds on, and specifies for the open\-ended and generative case, the older insight of cognitive systems engineering that the coupled system is where competence actually resides\. \(direct link \)
