# The role of artificial intelligence in change management: opportunities and challenges

**Autores:** David; id\_orcid 0000\-0002\-6878\-8135 Edgar; Roberto Biloslavo; Matteo Rizzo

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose This paper examines the accelerating integration of artificial intelligence \(AI\) into organizational change management \(OCM\)\. It explores AI’s dual role as both a powerful facilitator of change and a continuous disruptor itself, focusing specifically on the ethical and cultural challenges it introduces to traditional operational and decision\-making frameworks\. Design/methodology/approach Employing a narrative literature review approach, this study synthesizes 47 peer\-reviewed journal articles, conference proceedings, relevant practitioner papers, and book chapters published since 2010\. The review systematically analyzes the literature on the integration, impact, and long\-term consequences of applying AI in OCM contexts\. Findings The review underscores AI’s transformative capacity to reshape OCM from a traditional, linear process into a dynamic, continuous “meta\-change” formalized here as an integrative process linking AI’s technological properties to three generative mechanisms, two governing elements, and OCM outcomes expressed as four theoretical propositions\. While AI enhances change efficiency, it also introduces significant cultural and ethical hurdles, including algorithmic bias, identity threat among management, and the unintended reshaping of workplace culture and norms\. The findings indicate that successfully navigating this change landscape requires a strategic shift toward staged implementation, organizational agility, cultural alignment, effective ethical policies and, most importantly, human\-AI symbiosis, underpinned by Explainable AI \(XAI\) to foster workforce trust\. Originality/Value This paper bridges the critical gap between theoretical discourse on AI\-driven OCM and practical, ethical implications and implementation\. While AI’s potential to optimize change is widely recognized, existing research remains fragmented regarding the ethical governance and cultural frictions associated with its adoption\. By identifying emergent themes from the narrative literature review, this study offers a theoretically grounded agenda for “meta\-change,” reconciling technological agility with human\-centric governance in AI\-enabled organizational change\.
