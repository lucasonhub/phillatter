# Patient\-specific hemodynamic evaluation of fenestrated physician\-modified endografts in complex abdominal aortic aneurysms

**Autores:** Xiaolong Du; Shichen Jiang; Hongyue Zhang; Huiying Yu; Li Zhang; Kai Ren; Feng Ran

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose Physician\-modified endografts \(PMEGs\) are increasingly used for complex abdominal aortic aneurysms \(AAA\), but the patient\-specific flow consequences of the geometric reconstruction are not well quantified\. This study evaluated PMEG\-associated changes in hemodynamic and wall\-mechanical surrogate markers\. Materials and Methods Eight patients were retrospectively analyzed\. Pre\- and post\-PMEG geometries were reconstructed from computed tomography angiography and simulated with pulsatile computational fluid dynamics under matched inlet and outlet conditions\. Time\-averaged wall shear stress relative residence time and endothelial cell activation potential were quantified; one representative case underwent fluid\-structure interaction analysis\. Results Mean velocity increased from approximately 0\.8–1\.5 m/s \( P = 0\.0021\), pressure drop decreased \( P = 0\.0183\), low\-shear regions became less extensive, and RRT and ECAP decreased significantly \(both P &lt; 0\.05\)\. FSI showed lower computed wall deformation after repair\. At 1 year, all branch stents remained patent, with no endoleak or reported adverse event\. Conclusion Together with prior paired PMEG simulations, these findings support a favorable hemodynamic and wall\-mechanical effect of PMEG in complex anatomy unsuitable for conventional infrarenal EVAR and suggest a less stasis\-prone postoperative environment\. Larger controlled cohorts remain necessary to determine whether these surrogate changes translate into fewer clinical thrombotic or rupture events\. The observed changes arise from PMEG\-induced geometric remodeling, which modifies local flow acceleration, separation, near\-wall shear, and pressure\-loss characteristics\.
