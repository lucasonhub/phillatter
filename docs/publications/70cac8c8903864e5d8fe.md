# A Causal\-Comparative Study of Self\-Efficacy in Certificate and Degree Programs at the Community College Level Upon Graduation

**Autores:** Stephen Wakefield Phelps

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This quantitative causal\-comparative study investigated whether student\-perceived occupational self\-efficacy differs between graduates of community college certificate programs and those completing Associate of Applied Science \(AAS\) degrees\. The research also addresses a gap in the literature regarding how award type influences occupational self\-efficacy among students in automotive, diesel, manufacturing, and welding programs\. The study employed a causal\-comparative research design, examining the relationship between the categorical independent variable of award type \(certificate or AAS degree\) and the quantitative dependent variable of occupational self\-efficacy\. Participants \(N=209\) were students in their graduating term from community colleges in a Northwestern state who were completing one of the target programs\. Data was collected using the Occupational Self\-Efficacy Scale \(OSES\)\. Descriptive statistics summarize demographic and study variables, including means, standard deviations, frequencies, and percentages\. A two\-way ANOVA tested for statistically significant differences and found no significant interaction or main effects between the award types or the disciplines on occupational self\-efficacy\. The findings of this study expand the literature on occupational self\-efficacy at the community college level with technical graduates and offer insights that can support program development and leadership decision\-making\. This research serves as a foundation for future studies exploring the nuances of student self\-efficacy post\-graduation\. Future research should assess data through longitudinal study methodology and incorporate a mixed\-method approach among a different population compared with a different instrument\.
