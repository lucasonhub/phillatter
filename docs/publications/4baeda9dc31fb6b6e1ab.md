# Interpretable Hybrid Machine Learning for Predicting Microbial Growth Curves: Comparing Direct Data\-Driven, Two\-Step Hybrid, and One\-Step Hybrid Approaches

**Autores:** Fatih Tarlak; Özgün Yücel

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Predictive microbiology relies on a two\-step workflow in which a primary model is fitted to each growth curve and secondary models relate the kinetic parameters to environmental conditions, which can propagate primary\-fit uncertainty\. This study compared three strategies for predicting Escherichia coli growth from temperature, pH, and water activity: \(i\) direct machine learning \(ML\) prediction of log counts; \(ii\) a two\-step hybrid approach, in which modified Gompertz parameters estimated for each curve were mapped to environmental conditions by a neural network; and \(iii\) a one\-step hybrid approach, in which the network predicted Gompertz parameters and was trained end\-to\-end on the growth\-curve misfit\. The 396 ComBase growth curves \(4082 observations\) were evaluated by repeated five\-fold cross\-validation grouped by environmental condition\. Direct ML achieved an RMSE of 0\.690 ± 0\.085 log CFU/mL but yielded no kinetic parameters\. The one\-step approach was statistically equivalent to direct ML \(RMSE = 0\.712 ± 0\.075; margin ±0\.1 log CFU/mL\), whereas the two\-step approach was slightly less accurate \(RMSE = 0\.745 ± 0\.101\)\. The one\-step model’s lag times and maximum growth rates agreed with independent primary\-fit estimates \(Spearman ρ = 0\.89 and 0\.93\)\. Within the studied conditions, it offered the most favourable balance between accuracy and interpretability\.
