# Evidence boundaries and integrated disease management of microbial disease risks in protected Cymbidium faberi production: a translational framework and validation agenda

**Autores:** Yufeng Deng; Dongfeng Wei; Shichao Gu; Min Yu; Jinzhi Yao; Meicheng Zhao; Lei Qin; 寇太记

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Cymbidium faberi \( C\. faberi \) is an economically and culturally important orchid in East Asia, yet direct species\-specific evidence for microbial diseases under protected cultivation remains limited\. This critical narrative review synthesizes evidence on orchid diseases, protected\-crop disease ecology, plant pathogen detection and diagnosis, biological control, and plant microbiome interactions\. Direct C\. faberi studies comprise one localized anthracnose\-oriented report, baseline studies of root\- and rhizosphere\-associated microorganisms, and candidate antagonistic endophytes\. However, they do not establish a contemporary pathogen inventory, species\-resolved epidemiology, disease\-associated microbiome dynamics, or validated management efficacy\. Evidence from related Cymbidium species, other orchids, protected crops, and general plant\-pathology systems is therefore interpreted according to host proximity, pathogen biology, production environment, and management relevance\. Within this synthesis, disease risk is organized provisionally in a disease\-complex framework in which environmental filtering, pathogen pressure, host status, and microbiome resilience are considered jointly; the framework does not assume that stable multi\-pathogen synergistic pathogenesis has been demonstrated\. A node\-based translational Integrated Disease Management framework links mother\-plant accession, clonal propagation, potting, vegetative growth, flowering, abnormal\-batch disposition, and release decisions\. The principal research gaps are pathogen isolation and causal confirmation, species\-resolved pathogen surveillance, quantitative microclimate and inoculum thresholds, microbiome\-function testing, and prospective validation of diagnostic and prediction tools\. The framework is intended as a provisional decision\-support and research\-prioritization structure rather than a validated C\. faberi \-specific management standard\.
