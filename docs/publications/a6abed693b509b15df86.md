## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper asks why operations resembling libido, unconscious motivation, archetypal recurrence, or collective unconscious organization can appear across different historical and cultural configurations before the concepts later associated with Freud or Jung are explicitly formulated\. Rather than treating such similarities as evidence that later psychoanalytic concepts were already present in earlier societies, the paper distinguishes conceptual identity from operational recurrence\. It analyzes how recurrent patterns of desire, affective weighting, symbolic condensation, intergenerational transmission, ritual organization, memory externalization, and socially stabilized interpretation can emerge under heterogeneous configurations without requiring a shared doctrine or universal psychological substrate\. Civilizational cumulative intervals designate periods in which operational differences are preserved, recombined, and transmitted across institutions, media, practices, and external memory, while Operational\-Shape Penetration Dynamics tracks how locally generated patterns propagate into later bodily, symbolic, institutional, and interpretive configurations\. Freud’s libido and Jung’s collective unconscious are therefore treated as historically specific conceptual compressions of broader operational patterns rather than timeless categories projected backward onto earlier cultures\. Xenoscience provides the comparative framework for identifying when similar operations are genuinely non\-redundant across configurations and when apparent similarity is produced by retrospective conceptual projection\. The broader claim is that recurrence across civilizations should be explained through transmission, constraint, reactivation, and configuration\-dependent re\-formation rather than by assuming either conceptual universality or independent rediscovery of identical psychological entities\. \(direct link \)
