# Deep learning for biodegradable metallic biomaterials: from degradation modeling to performance prediction with a practical case study

**Autores:** Tongfei Shen; Xiaohui Wang; Huaicheng Li; 潘中泽; Zhaoliang Gao

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Biodegradable metallic implants are transitioning from laboratory concept to clinical deployment, yet predicting their corrosion rate and residual strength in the physiological environment remains a substantial scientific challenge\. The interplay of electrochemical, mechanical, and biological factors creates a high\-dimensional problem that conventional modelling cannot easily capture, while experimental datasets are often too sparse and fragmented for traditional statistical approaches\. Deep learning architectures, including graph, recurrent, and transformer networks, combined with physics\-informed loss functions, now offer accurate forecasts of degradation rates, yield strength, and biological responses\. Interpretation tools such as SHAP \(SHapley Additive exPlanations\) and attention mapping further reveal which alloying elements and processing parameters dominate performance, tightening the prediction\-experiment loop\. This work also demonstrates MAGIC \(Multi\-task Alloy Gated\-network with Integrated Constraints\), a multi\-task learner that fuses composition and processing modalities through learned uncertainty weighting\. On a magnesium alloy dataset of 600 records it delivers cross\-validated R 2 values of 0\.864 for ultimate tensile strength and 0\.907 for yield strength, with root\-mean\-square errors of 28\.0 and 26\.8 MPa for ultimate tensile strength \(UTS\) and yield strength \(YS\), outperforming seven standard baselines and providing a reproducible template\. The boundary of this contribution is explicitly emphasised: MAGIC predicts the pristine \(as\-processed\) ultimate tensilestrength and yield strength of undegraded material; it does not predict corrosion rates or post\-implantation residual strength, and the coupling between corrosion damage and mechanical degradation is identified as a key open direction rather than a solved capability\. Substantial barriers persist, notably the gap between in vitro and in vivo environments, scarce openly shared data, and the rigorous demands of clinical validation\. Nevertheless, deep learning is progressively integrating into biodegradable metal research, and the central question is evolving from feasibility to whether the community can establish the required data infrastructure and interdisciplinary trust\.
