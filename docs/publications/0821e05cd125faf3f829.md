# AI\-Enabled Decision Intelligence for Managing Material Shortages in Engineer\-to\-Order Supply Chains: A Reproducible Simulation Study

**Autores:** Daniel Filip; Violeta Firescu; Livia Filip; Ioan Alexandru Popan; Ionuţ Adrian Chis

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Engineer\-to\-order \(ETO\) manufacturing faces material shortages arising from changing requirements and uncertain supply\. This study develops Artificial Intelligence–Supply Chain Decision Intelligence \(AI\-SCDI\), linking shortage prediction to feasible interventions under cost and capacity constraints\. Methods: Six specialists assessed framework content and parameter plausibility\. A locked experiment used 12 synthetic cohorts and 6000 paired Monte Carlo replications per policy, supplemented by model\-recovery diagnostics, matched policy ablations, calibration assessment, and joint sensitivity analysis\. Results: Histogram gradient boosting achieved a mean precision–recall area under the curve of 0\.7587, compared with 0\.5399 for logistic regression and 0\.7415 for random forest\. Under baseline assumptions, AI\-SCDI reduced disruption time by 30\.0% and synthetic operating cost by 19\.1% relative to no proactive intervention\. At baseline capacity, estimated\-net\-benefit selection and abstention contributed more to cost reduction than impact weighting alone\. Learner rankings varied across generating mechanisms, horizon\-specific calibration showed risk overestimation, and mean net benefit was negative in 19 of 64 informed\-parameter scenarios\. Conclusions: AI\-SCDI provides a reproducible, capacity\-constrained decision heuristic\. Its benefits remain conditional on synthetic assumptions; external validation and empirical cost calibration are required before industrial effectiveness can be claimed\.
