# Cardiovascular complications as a consequence of coronavirus infection in pregnant women managed in ambulatory settings

**Autores:** Ilia Davarashvili; Ariel Israel; Shlomo Vinker; Hila Hochler

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background SARS\-CoV\-2 has been linked to cardiovascular complications in the general population\. However, data regarding cardiovascular outcomes among pregnant women remain limited, particularly in community\-based ambulatory populations, as most previous studies focused primarily on hospitalized or critically ill patients\. In this retrospective population\-based matched cohort study, we evaluated whether COVID\-19 infection during pregnancy is associated with an increased incidence of cardiovascular complications among pregnant women managed predominantly in outpatient settings\. Methods We conducted a retrospective population\-based matched cohort study using electronic health record data from pregnant women between 2020 and 2025\. Women with COVID\-19 during pregnancy were matched 1:1 to women without documented COVID\-19 during pregnancy according to demographic, obstetric, and clinical characteristics\. The final analytical cohort included 4,068 women in each group\. Cardiovascular outcomes during pregnancy and within a year since last menstrual period were assessed\. Baseline demographic and obstetric characteristics were comparable between the COVID\-19 and control groups\. No significant differences were observed between the groups in the incidence of major cardiovascular complications, either when analyzed individually or as a composite endpoint \(0\.15% vs\. 0\.15%; OR 1\.00, 95% CI 0\.27–3\.74\)\. Specifically, there were no significant differences in the occurrence of atrial fibrillation/flutter, congestive heart failure, cardiomyopathy, myocarditis, pulmonary embolism, or deep venous thrombosis\. In contrast, cough and backache were significantly more frequent among women with COVID\-19 than among matched controls \(6\.88% vs\. 4\.87%; OR 1\.45, 95% CI 1\.19–1\.75; 13\.8% vs\. 11\.8%; OR 1\.20, 95% CI 1\.05–1\.37; p = 0\.007 respectively\)\. Conclusions Our findings suggest that among pregnant women in ambulatory community settings, COVID\-19 infection is not associated with an increased risk of major cardiovascular complications\.
