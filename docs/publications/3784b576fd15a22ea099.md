# Heavy metal ion driven smart nanomaterials for multimodal sensing, biocomputing, and information security: a mini review

**Autores:** Mohamed Nabeel Mattath

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Heavy metal ions \(HMIs\) are widely recognized as persistent environmental pollutants\. However, they are increasingly being employed as functional chemical inputs in intelligent molecular systems\. This mini review summarizes smart nanomaterial\-based platforms developed for HMI\-driven biocomputing and information security\. Particular emphasis is placed on the integration of dual\-mode, multimode, and multichannel readout strategies that translate selective ion\-recognition and signal\-transduction events into fluorescence, colorimetric, electrochemical, and other measurable responses\. By converting these responses into binary states, such platforms can execute diverse molecular logic operations, parity functions, and arithmetic functions\. Their applications in information security, including ASCII\- and Morse\-code\-based encryption, multimodal cryptosteganography, and anti\-counterfeiting strategies based on invisible fluorescent inks, are also discussed\. Collectively, these studies underscore the potential of HMIs to serve as chemically specific inputs in nanosystems, enhancing sensitive signal transduction and programmable functions for molecular computation and security applications, while reframing toxic pollutants as functional triggers in intelligent chemical platforms\.
