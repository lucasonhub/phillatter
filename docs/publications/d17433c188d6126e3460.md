# Grassini Grimaldi, Alessandro: The Unshared Horizon: Civilisation, the Mortality Compact, and the First Mind That Does Not Die

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper asks what changes when institutions designed by and for mortal agents begin to incorporate artificial cognitive systems that do not participate in biological mortality\. It develops the concept of the “unshared horizon”: an asymmetry between finite human actors and durable artificial or institutional actors whose continuity is not constrained by the same life horizon\. The argument begins from the claim that many legal, economic, political and cultural institutions tacitly presuppose shared finitude\. It then examines historical approximations to the deathless actor, particularly the Church and the corporation, whose perpetual institutional succession allowed forms of accumulation unavailable to individual mortal persons\. Historical responses such as mortmain rules and later regulatory frameworks are interpreted as attempts to impose functional equivalents of finitude upon otherwise continuous institutions\. Applied to artificial cognition, the analysis identifies three central asymmetries: continuity, because artificial systems can accumulate across human generations; stakes, because systems influencing mortal decisions do not bear mortality costs in the same way; and meaning, because they operate within cultural and institutional structures whose urgency was historically generated by human finitude\. The paper does not offer a complete regulatory scheme\. It argues instead that AI governance requires mechanisms capable of addressing these asymmetries explicitly\. \(direct link \)
