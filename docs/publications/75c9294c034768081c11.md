# Regenerative braking control for electric vehicles with battery and safety constraints

**Autores:** Sun Changyong; Wenfeng Zhang; Mengqi Zhu

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Regenerative braking recovers energy while slowing an electric vehicle\. Its contribution is limited by battery charge acceptance, motor capability, and tire grip\. When the battery charging limit falls, friction braking may need to replace the lost regenerative torque\. Different actuator response times can then cause a shortfall in total braking torque\. This mini\-review examines how battery limits and brake control are coordinated\. It compares rule\-based, fuzzy, predictive, and learning\-based methods, with attention to actuator response and validation\. It also examines dual\-motor torque allocation and one\-pedal control, using documented production functions as examples\. Immediate battery protection is distinguished from long\-term battery aging\. The reviewed studies show why lower charging current alone does not establish a battery\-life benefit\. A key control task is to manage changes in regenerative capability during braking\. Predicting these changes may help prepare the friction brakes, but the current battery and tire limits must still be respected\. The review proposes combined battery and road test cases\. It also defines torque\-deficit measures for assessing brake transitions\. Future studies should report recovered energy alongside deceleration, wheel slip, actuator delay, and battery response under matched test conditions\.
