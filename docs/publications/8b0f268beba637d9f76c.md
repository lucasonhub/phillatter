# Precision Cardio\-Oncology: A Multimodal Approach to Cancer Therapy\-Related Cardiovascular Toxicity Prediction and Assessment

**Autores:** Carl Simela; Ishikaa Mukherjee; Abhinav Kandala; Aruni Ghose; Turja Chakraborty; Navin Mathiyalagan; Amitava Banerjee; Arjun Kumar Ghosh

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Cancer therapy\-related cardiovascular toxicity \(CTR\-CVT\) is associated with morbidity, mortality, and interruptions to cancer treatment\. Current cardio\-oncology clinical assessment tools generally rely on a limited range of clinical data; however, in the era of precision medicine, it may be possible to leverage multimodal data, including imaging and multi\-omic inputs, to personalise CTR\-CVT prediction and assessment\. In this review, we examine the genotypic and phenotypic predictors of CTR\-CVT and the evidence supporting their incorporation into multimodal risk models\. Most research focuses on cancer therapy\-related cardiac dysfunction \(CTRCD\)\. We identify a strong evidence base for multimodal imaging\-guided prediction, particularly cardiovascular magnetic resonance \(CMR\) and transthoracic echocardiography \(TTE\)\-derived global longitudinal strain measurements\. Emerging evidence also supports the potential value of novel imaging parameters, including computed tomography \(CT\) and TTE\-derived radiomic features\. From the genomic and proteomic perspective, truncating titin variants and microRNA\-126 may be informative predictors of cancer therapy\-related cardiac dysfunction \(CTRCD\)\. At the same time, the literature also highlights potential epigenomic markers of CTR\-CVT\. Finally, we consider how these diverse data can be integrated into multimodal risk models and how digital innovation and advanced machine learning methods may further improve model predictive performance\. However, a large implementation gap remains, with challenges such as data privacy and a lack of external validation studies preventing routine clinical implementation\.
