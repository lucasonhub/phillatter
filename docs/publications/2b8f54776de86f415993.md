# Ecological and socioeconomic dimensions of oceanic shark governance

**Autores:** José Carlos Báez; Andrés Domingo; Rui Coelho

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Since 2010, reported global shark fisheries have been dominated by blue shark, which represents the largest share of species\-specific reported catches, although many shark catches remain unidentified at the species level\. Blue shark is a relatively productive oceanic species supporting stable, well\-regulated fisheries under tuna Regional Fisheries Management Organizations, whereas many other sharks remain conservation priorities\. Oceanic shark fisheries also provide affordable animal protein and contribute to food security in many developing countries\. This study highlights the need for species\-specific conservation and management of oceanic sharks, recognizing differences in biological productivity, conservation status, ecological roles, and socioeconomic importance\. We argue that generalized conservation narratives and broad trade or catch restrictions may undermine science\-based management and propose integrated approaches for sustainability\.
