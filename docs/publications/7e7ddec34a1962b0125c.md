# Fenfluramine treatment and seizure‐free days in pediatric and adult patients with Lennox–Gastaut syndrome: A post hoc analysis

**Autores:** Stéphane Auvin; Ingrid Eileen Scheffer; António Gil‐Nagel; Patrick Joseph Healy; Amélie Lothe; Mélanie Langlois; Jayne Abraham; Lieven G\. Lagae; Kelly G\. Knupp

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The most common primary endpoint for assessing treatment efficacy in patients with Lennox\-Gastaut syndrome \(LGS\) is change in frequency of seizures associated with a fall\. Increased number of seizure\-free days \(SFDs\) is more strongly associated with improved quality of life than seizure frequency reduction\. Fenfluramine is approved for treatment of seizures associated with LGS in patients ≥2 years old\. Data from a fenfluramine randomized controlled trial \(RCT\) in patients with LGS, and its open\-label extension \(OLE\), were used to evaluate \(1\) mean change in percentage of SFDs \(seizures associated with a fall, generalized tonic\-clonic seizures, all countable \[motor\] seizures\) from baseline overall and by baseline seizure quartile; and \(2\) relationships between percentage of SFDs and change in seizure frequency, and Clinical Global Impression\-Improvement \(CGI\-I\) scale scores\. Fenfluramine treatment was associated with a greater increase in percentage of SFDs versus placebo \(RCT; all seizure categories, p &lt; \.0001\), and higher doses corresponded to greater percentages \(OLE\) consistently across baseline seizure quartiles\. SFD correlations with seizure frequency were strong \(ρ = \-\.776 to \-\.974\); SFD correlations with CGI\-I score were weak/modest \(ρ = \-\.2494 to \-\.3203\)\. Assessment of SFDs enhances the evaluation of treatment efficacy and may be clinically meaningful for patients with LGS and their families\.
