# Impact\-based flood\-damage assessment in Japan under longitudinal typhoon\-track perturbations and levee\-height variations during Hagibis \(2019\)

**Autores:** Xiaoyang Li; Kei Yoshimura; Hironori Fudeyasu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Impact\-based flood\-damage assessment integrates hydrometeorological information with vulnerability and exposure data to generate comprehensive evaluation of flood damage\. This study uses the Integrated Land Simulator to assemble an impact\-based flood\-damage assessment framework for Typhoon Hagibis \(2019\), one of the most powerful typhoons to strike Japan in recent years\. To systematically evaluate how typhoon\-track position and levee height influence flood damage, we conduct longitudinal typhoon\-track perturbation experiments and levee\-height variation scenario analyses\. Here we show that the flood damage is highly sensitive to small typhoon\-track shifts, with a 0\.2° longitudinal shift causing an average damage difference of 83\.3 billion JPY\. A 1\.0° eastward shift causes the highest damage in Kanto and across Japan, mainly around Tokyo, while 1\.6° and 0\.4° westward shifts produce the greatest damage in Kinki and Chubu, mainly around Osaka and Nagoya, respectively\. The levee\-height sensitivity experiments show an average flood\-damage reduction of approximately 9\.1 billion JPY for each 10% increase in levee height\.
