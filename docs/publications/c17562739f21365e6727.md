# Bulgaria, Jamel: The Structural\-Constructivist Architecture of Musical Affect: Grounding the Emotional Engine within the Core Emotion Framework \(CEF\)

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper develops a structural constructivist account of musical affect by grounding emotional experience in the ten functional operators of the Core Emotion Framework \(CEF\)\. Rather than treating emotions as discrete biological kinds or culturally constructed narratives, the CEF models affect as irreducible functional movements within a 3×3\+1 architecture of the Human Operating System\. Applying this ontology to music, the paper argues that musical structures operate as mechanical instructions that modulate relational aperture, cognitive signal processing, and somatic fluidity\. Rhythmic, harmonic, and melodic features are shown to interact with specific operators—such as Constricting, Expanding, Calculating, Achieving, Arranging, and Appreciating—thereby explaining how music induces, sustains, or resolves affective states without invoking representational or appraisal based theories\. The account is further supported by contemporary affective computing models that map musical features and listener states into continuous affective embeddings, which can be reinterpreted as coordinates within the CEF’s ten dimensional operator space\. Cross cultural findings demonstrate stable structural patterns in musical experience, reinforcing the universality of operator level dynamics\. Clinical applications are explored through operator level analyses of anxiety, depression, and the Good Person Anxiety Pattern, illustrating how music can function as an external emotional engine that restores operator agility\. By integrating philosophical analysis, affective science, and computational modeling, the paper offers a unified framework for understanding musical emotion as a form of structural modulation within the human affective architecture\. Keywords: Core Emotion Framework; Musical Affect; Structural Constructivism; Philosophy of Music; Emotion Regulation; Affective Computation; Cognitive Architecture; Operator Theory; Affective Neuroscience; Human Operating System\. Philosophical Significance This paper advances a structural\-constructivist account of musical affect by grounding emotional experience in the ten functional operators of the Core Emotion Framework \(CEF\)\. In contrast to traditional emotion theories that treat emotions as either innate biological categories or culturally constructed narratives, the CEF reframes affect as a set of irreducible functional movements within a 3×3\+1 architecture of the Human Operating System\. By applying this ontology to music, the paper demonstrates that musical structures operate as mechanical instructions that modulate relational aperture, cognitive processing, and motoric momentum\. This provides a philosophically novel explanation of how music influences emotion without invoking folk\-psychological categories or metaphorical descriptions\. The result is a unified account that links phenomenology, computational modeling, and affective regulation within a single structural framework\. Relation to Existing Theories The account developed here diverges from Basic Emotion Theory by rejecting the idea that emotions are discrete, biologically hardwired modules\. It also departs from psychological constructionism \(e\.g\., Barrett\) by positing that the underlying structure of affect is not culturally assembled but functionally universal, consisting of ten operators that govern perception, relational stance, and motoric action\. Unlike enactivist or embodied cognition approaches, which emphasize dynamic coupling with the environment, the CEF provides a formal internal architecture that specifies how affective transitions occur\. In the philosophy of music, the paper challenges expressivist accounts that treat music as representing or conveying emotions, arguing instead that music directly modulates the listener’s internal operators\. This positions the CEF as a structural alternative to representational, narrative, and appraisal\-based theories of musical emotion\.
