# Auditing Future\-Memory Use in Point\-in\-Time Language Agents

**Autores:** Rongtian Ye

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Point\-in\-time \(PIT\) agents can recall post\-cutoff facts even when their external evidence respects a historical cutoff\. We examine whether this access predicts evidence gathering in 48 historical company\-comparison tasks\. Independent closed\-book revenue probes identify the better\-remembered company for each model on tasks with unequal recall, fixing the predicted direction before agent runs\. We then compare paired runs with real or anonymized company identities while keeping external evidence PIT\-valid\. Both Qwen and DeepSeek recalled future facts, but neither supported the prespecified shift toward the better\-remembered company\. Qwen&\#x27;s four allocation estimates on 28 unequal\-recall tasks were negative, with descriptive intervals spanning zero\. A prospective DeepSeek replication showed an inverse pattern across all four metrics on 20 such tasks, with intervals below zero\. These estimates remained negative after each company, pair, and future\-fact omission, and all four post\-hoc max\-T\-adjusted sign\-flip p\-values were below \.05\. In a prospectively specified follow\-up, explicit future facts produced small estimated shifts away from the supplied\-fact company, with intervals spanning zero, while final choices shifted toward it\. The mechanism behind the natural inverse pattern remains unresolved\. These findings support separate assessments of memory accessibility, search allocation, and final outputs alongside cutoff checks on external evidence\.
