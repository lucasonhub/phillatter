# Electrochemical Nitrogen Reduction to Ammonia with Mesoporous Molybdenum\-Carbon Electrocatalysts in a Gas Diffusion Flow Cell

**Autores:** Chaoyun Tang; Dipankar Saha; Jiacheng Wang; Arvind Chandrasekar; Negar Sabouhanian; Connor L\. Witt; Yulei Wang; Aicheng Chen; Wei Fan; Chung‐Li Dong; Zhu Chen; James J\. Watkins; Nianqiang Wu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Commercialization of electrochemical nitrogen reduction \(eNRR\) is hindered by limited catalyst performance, the lack of scalable electrochemical cell architectures, and an incomplete understanding of performance\-limiting processes\. This work aims to address these challenges through a systematic investigation of catalyst composition and microstructure, device configuration, and reaction mechanisms\. Molybdenum \(Mo\) metal single atoms and clusters supported on mesoporous nitrogen\-doped carbon were synthesized with tunable pore sizes via a five\-second photothermal pyrolysis of a self\-assembled bottlebrush block copolymer \(BBCP\) template\. For eNRR, improved Mo dispersion enhanced electrochemical accessibility, while the coexistence of atomically dispersed Mo species and Mo clusters produced a structurally heterogeneous catalytic environment that benefits eNRR\. A gas\-diffusion electrode \(GDE\) flow cell was constructed for continuous operation testing; the catalyst–device system achieved a Faradaic efficiency of 21\.6% and an ammonia yield rate of 34\.7 μg h–1 mg–1\. Intermediate pore sizes and balanced electrode wettability promoted effective triple\-phase boundary formation and active\-site utilization\. Electrochemical characterization combined with machine\-learning analysis of flow\-cell parameters indicated that activation polarization is the dominant loss mechanism, governed primarily by intrinsic active\-site kinetics and electrochemically accessible surface area\. In contrast, pore size, metal dispersion, and electrode wettability primarily regulate site accessibility rather than mass\-transport limitations\. Together, these results provide a framework linking catalyst structure, electrode architecture, and kinetic limitations in eNRR flow\-cell systems and offer practical design guidelines for advancing scalable eNRR technology\.
