# BUILDING TRUST IN DIGITAL PAYMENTS: THE ROLE OF CYBERSECURITY ASSURANCE IN OVERCOMING CONSUMER SKEPTICISM

**Autores:** Javeria Iqbal; Muhammad Hafeez; Ahras Rashid; Zainab Tayyab

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The growing use of digital payment technologies has significantly changed the way consumers carry out financial transactions\. This shift is particularly evident among Generation Z, who are generally more comfortable with digital technologies and increasingly prefer cashless payment methods\. However, despite the rapid expansion of digital payment services, concerns regarding security, privacy, fraud, and trust remain important factors that may discourage users from fully adopting these technologies\. Much of the existing research on digital payment adoption has focused on factors such as perceived usefulness, ease of use, convenience, and technology acceptance\. Comparatively less attention has been given to how cybersecurity assurance and Institutional Trust can reduce users’ concerns and strengthen their confidence in digital payment systems\. This issue is particularly relevant in Pakistan, where the digital payment ecosystem is expanding rapidly, yet users may still have concerns about the security of their financial information, the reliability of digital platforms, and the institutions responsible for providing these services\. Therefore, this paper aims to assess the Cybersecurity Assurance and Institutional Trust on Digital Payment Usage among Generation Z consumers in Pakistan\. Understanding these relationships can provide valuable insights into how security measures and trust in financial institutions shape consumers’ willingness to use digital payment services\. The findings may help fintech companies, banks, and other financial institutions better understand the factors that influence digital payment behavior and develop strategies that improve users’ confidence in their services\. In particular, stronger cybersecurity practices, transparent security mechanisms, and greater institutional credibility may help address users’ concerns and encourage more frequent use of digital payment platforms\. By examining these factors within the Pakistani context, the study addresses an area that has received relatively limited attention in the existing literature and contributes to a broader understanding of digital payment adoption among Generation Z\. The findings may also provide useful recommendations for policymakers and regulatory authorities seeking to strengthen cybersecurity standards, promote institutional trust, and develop a safer, more reliable, and inclusive digital payment ecosystem in Pakistan\.
