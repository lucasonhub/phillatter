# Heterogeneous Computing Systems: A Review of Architectural Evolution and Design Methodologies

**Autores:** Zhengyuan Xia

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Heterogeneous computing has emerged as an important architectural response to the slowdown of conventional semiconductor scaling, particularly in the post\-Moore era\. This review follows the shift from homogeneous multicore processors toward integrated platforms that place CPUs, GPUs, FPGAs, and domain\-specific accelerators on the same die\. The transition is closely related to two long\-standing technology constraints, namely the slowing of Moore&\#x27;s Law and the breakdown of Dennard scaling, while Amdahl&\#x27;s Law still limits the speedup that can be obtained from parallel acceleration\. From the system perspective, the review focuses on three recurring obstacles: the memory wall, cache coherence in shared\-memory heterogeneous architectures, and on\-chip communication supported by Network\-on\-Chip \(NoC\) designs\. I also reviewed low\-power VLSI approaches, especially clock gating and low\-swing flip\-flop designs, together with design methodologies such as SystemC\-based high\-level synthesis \(HLS\), the Catapult HLS flow, and latency\-insensitive design\. Based on these 24 research contributions, I organize the discussion into a multi\-level technical framework for heterogeneous SoC design in AI and IoT applications\.
