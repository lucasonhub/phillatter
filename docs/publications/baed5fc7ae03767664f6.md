# From minds to machines

**Autores:** Hang Li

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Recent progress in generative and multimodal machine learning has produced models capable of synthesizing high\-quality text, images, and code\. While these systems often exhibit behaviors that resemble human understanding, their impressive surface\-level fluency does not reliably indicate the presence of stable, grounded, or systematically reusable internal representations\. Empirical evidence suggests that this &quot;illusion of understanding&quot; reflects a critical tension: models that achieve strong benchmark performance can still suffer from brittle failures, semantic drift, and a heavy reliance on shortcut correlations\. This dissertation investigates the structural conditions required to bridge the gap between statistical mimicry and robust semantic reasoning, moving from empirical audits of current systems to the design of cognitively inspired architectures\. We approach machine understanding not as a binary property, but as a set of structural conditions that can be empirically evaluated\. Taking the concept as the unit of analysis, we introduce three diagnostic dimensions to characterize semantic integrity: availability, invariance, and robustness\. Availability examines whether high\-level concepts are explicitly encoded within a model&\#x27;s internal geometry in an interpretable form\. Invariance evaluates whether these representations preserve their identity across modalities and architectures\. Robustness tests whether representations support compositional reasoning and resist degradation under counterfactual or relational perturbations\. The first stage of this research presents a series of empirical audits and interventions based on these dimensions\. We demonstrate that diffusion models encode interpretable semantic structure within internal bottleneck representations, or $h$\-space\. We propose a self\-supervised optimization framework for discovering and manipulating these semantic directions to improve model fairness and safety without requiring manual annotations\. We then analyze semantic consistency at the vision\-\-language interface, showing that meaning often degrades during cross\-modal translation\. To address this, we introduce a cycle\-consistent fine\-tuning framework that enforces bidirectional semantic preservation between image\-to\-text and text\-to\-image models\. Finally, we develop a generative hard\-negative mining pipeline that synthesizes fine\-grained counterfactuals, compelling models to move beyond global statistical shortcuts and yielding substantial improvements in compositional reasoning\. These empirical findings underscore a fundamental limitation: while post\-hoc interventions can improve model behavior, the &quot;semantic fragility&quot; of current systems is often rooted in unstructured, monolithic design\. This observation motivates the second stage of the research, which shifts from auditing machines to drawing inspiration from the functional organization of the human mind\. We explore architectural inductive biases as a necessary complement to large\-scale learning\. We introduce the Tensor Brain framework, which decouples symbolic indexing from subsymbolic representation and helps resolve the conflation of logic and probability in general\-purpose models\. Furthermore, we demonstrate a biologically inspired neural path\-finding mechanism that achieves constant\-time relational reasoning, providing evidence that structural constraints derived from cognitive systems can significantly improve the efficiency and systematicity of machine cognition\. In summary, this dissertation advances the analysis of semantic representations by providing both a mechanistic understanding of existing models and a roadmap for their architectural evolution\. By systematically evaluating the availability, invariance, and robustness of internal concepts, we identify the specific failure modes of modern generative AI and provide targeted methods for repair\. Beyond these interventions, we demonstrate that incorporating structural principles from cognitive science, specifically the separation of symbolic indexing and relational navigation, offers a path toward more stable and reliable systems\. These contributions provide a technical foundation for building a new generation of artificial intelligence that is not only high\-performing but also semantically grounded, systematically robust, and human\-aligned\.
