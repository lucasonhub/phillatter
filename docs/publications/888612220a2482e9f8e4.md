# A balanced set of standardized affective environmental images for climate change research

**Autores:** Erkin Asutay

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Evocative visual imagery is central to research on affective pathways of environmental engagement, yet existing stimulus sets often overrepresent negative imagery and make it difficult to compare positive and negative affective responses under comparable conditions\. We developed a balanced database of affective environmental images designed to support controlled comparisons of positive and negative environmental imagery\. In total, 587 participants rated 203 images depicting consequences of unmitigated climate change and context\-matched positive alternatives on pleasantness and activation\. Participants also reported belief in human caused climate change, perceived risk of climate change, and environmental values\. The image set covered a broad range of pleasantness while positive and negative images showed comparable activation levels\. Across stimuli, pleasantness and activation were quadratically related, consistent with affective stimulus sets spanning over positive and negative valence\. Trial\-level analyses showed that stronger biospheric values and higher risk perception predicted more negative and more activating responses to negative images\. For positive images, stronger biospheric values predicted more pleasant and more activating responses, whereas higher risk perception predicted lower activation\. This balanced, context\-matched image database provides a standardized resource for studying how affective responses to environmental imagery may shape risk perception and environmental engagement\.
