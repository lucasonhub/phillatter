# Benchmarking time\-series foundation models for multitask predictive maintenance in digital twins

**Autores:** Sizhe Ma; Katherine A\. Flanigan; M\. Berges

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Digital twins provide a comprehensive virtual representation of physical assets, distinguishing themselves from traditional control systems through long\-term diagnostic and prognostic capabilities\. However, current digital twin architectures face a critical scalability barrier: they rely on dedicated, single\-purpose models for every distinct query, task, physical scale, and operating context, resulting in unsustainable engineering overhead\. To address this limitation, there is an urgent need for flexible, general\-purpose analytical backbones capable of interpreting diverse time\-series data without constant reconfiguration\. While time\-series foundation models \(TSFMs\) offer a potential solution through large\-scale pre\-training, their capacity to generalize beyond forecasting to the complex, multi\-objective downstream requirements remains largely unverified\. Addressing this challenge, this study utilizes the Commercial Modular Aero\-Propulsion System Simulation \(C\-MAPSS\) dataset to benchmark three representative multi\-task TSFMs \(MOMENT, UniTS, and FFTS\) across four downstream tasks \(imputation, anomaly detection, classification, and regression\) within a predictive maintenance context\. We frame this evaluation as a rigorous test of generalization, assessing whether architecturally distinct TSFMs can adapt to distinct downstream tasks such as regression for remaining useful life estimation\. Our benchmark reveals that although TSFMs generalize across all evaluated tasks, they do not yet match specialized baselines trained only on the C\-MAPSS dataset\. We further find that the three backbones separate most clearly when adaptation is restricted to a small set of parameters, where the model combining channel\-mixing with explicit task tokens adapts best to multi\-objective use, including regression, a task absent from all three pre\-training objectives; in the zero\-shot regime no single backbone leads across tasks\. Because the models differ simultaneously in architecture, capacity, pre\-training corpus, and patch size, we report these as patterns observed under one shared benchmarking framework rather than as isolated causes\. The evaluation is conducted on raw sensor series alone, without the semantic or physics\-based components of a mature digital twin, so these findings provide an empirical baseline for the analytical layer such a system would host rather than an evaluation of digital twin integration itself\.
