# Machine learning predictors of cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy in type 2 diabetes: insights from a prospectively collected single\-center registry

**Autores:** Katalin Farkas; Viktor Vedelek; Máté Vámos; Eva Csajbok; István Baczkó; János Zádori; Andrea Szentesi; P ter Hegyi; Tamás T\. Várkonyi; Csaba Lengyel; Anna Vágvölgyi

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Cardiac autonomic neuropathy \(CAN\) is a severe complication of type 2 diabetes \(T2DM\) associated with increased cardiovascular morbidity and mortality\. Machine learning \(ML\) can integrate multidimensional clinical data and identify features associated with CAN\. We aimed to characterize predictors of CAN categories using ML\. Methods Data were obtained from a prospective single\-center observational registry of patients with T2DM undergoing standardized cardiovascular autonomic reflex tests \(CARTs\) based on Ewing’s protocol\. Demographic, anthropometric, clinical, laboratory, and medication data were analyzed\. Patients were classified as CAN0 \(no or borderline CAN\), CAN1 \(early CAN\), or CAN2 \(definite or severe CAN\)\. Three extreme gradient boosting models were evaluated: a CART\-inclusive model, a CART\-excluded model restricted to patients with T2DM, and a CART\-excluded model supplemented with non\-diabetic controls without abnormal CART results\. Model performance was assessed using area under the receiver operating characteristic curve \(AUC\), and feature contributions were interpreted using SHapley Additive exPlanations values\. Results Among 693 patients with T2DM, 19 \(2\.7%\) had completely normal autonomic function, whereas 674 \(97\.3%\) exhibited at least one borderline or abnormal CART result\. Greater CAN severity was associated with older age, longer diabetes duration, higher systolic blood pressure, and poorer renal function\. The CART\-inclusive model achieved perfect discrimination in the best iteration \(weighted AUC, 1\.00; accuracy, 100%\), with all five CARTs ranking among the leading predictors\. After CART exclusion, performance declined markedly \(weighted AUC, 0\.64\), with class\-specific AUCs of 0\.59 for CAN0, 0\.59 for CAN1, and 0\.68 for CAN2\. Inclusion of non\-diabetic controls improved the weighted AUC to 0\.78, with the greatest improvement for CAN0 \(AUC, 0\.85\), followed by CAN2 \(0\.78\) and CAN1 \(0\.70\)\. Age, diabetes duration, and renal function markers were recurrent non\-CART predictors, while anthropometric, hematological, metabolic, and treatment\-related features showed category\- and model\-specific contributions\. Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection demonstrated substantial overlap among CAN categories, particularly for CAN1\. Conclusions CARTs remained essential for accurate CAN classification and staging\. Routinely available clinical variables provided complementary phenotypic information but were insufficient to robustly distinguish CAN categories without CART data, particularly CAN0 and CAN1\. ML may support multidimensional phenotyping and hypothesis generation but cannot replace standardized autonomic testing\.
