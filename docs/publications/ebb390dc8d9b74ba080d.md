# Diagnostic Preservation After ECG Digitization: From Risk Stratification to Reliability Assurance

**Autores:** Thai Nho Nguyen; Đoàn Văn Thắng

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

As Electrocardiogram \(ECG\) digitization becomes increasingly accurate, waveform similarity alone does not establish preservation of diagnostically relevant information\. This study investigates whether post\-digitization diagnostic instability can be identified without access to the native digital ECG and whether such reference\-free observability is sufficient to support reliability assurance\. PTB\-XL waveforms were rendered as 3×4 ECG pages at 25 mm/s, 10 mm/mV, and 4 pixels/mm and re\-digitized with a fixed image\-based trace\-recovery pipeline\. Analyses on PTB\-XL showed that unobserved lead\-time regions contributed only limited incremental diagnostic information under the tested setting, indicating that missing temporal support alone could not explain the observed preservation loss\. A reference\-free instability score derived solely from digitized ECGs retained above\-chance record\-level discrimination on a patient\-disjoint cohort \(AUROC 0\.614, unitless, for distinguishing ECGs with versus without at least one probe\-defined new diagnostic error\), but calibration deteriorated after population transfer\. Selective acceptance reduced the occurrence of probe\-defined new diagnostic errors without satisfying the multimetric preservation requirements, while class\-wise analysis revealed strongly directional and diagnosis\-dependent failures\. Application to 99 real\-world ECGs from Tam Duc Hospital further showed that the representation remained technically applicable despite structured feature\-domain shifts\. These findings establish a distinction between risk observability and preservation assurance: diagnostic instability can be partially stratified without an oracle reference, but successful ranking alone is insufficient to guarantee stable diagnostic decisions or calibrated reliability
