# Evaluation of Physics\-Based Pose Prediction and the Role of Receptor Flexibility in a Community Antiviral Blind Challenge

**Autores:** Asma Feriel Khoualdi; Finlay Clark; Josh T\. Horton; Daniel J\. Cole

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Prediction of protein–ligand binding modes is a key step in computer\-aided drug design, since incorrect pose prediction inevitably limits the achievable accuracy of downstream physics\-based affinity predictions\. The ASAP\-Polaris\-OpenADMET antiviral competition was launched in 2025 as a means to prospectively test computational methods across a range of measurements that guide early prioritization during a drug discovery campaign including pose prediction\. Here, we describe our two open\-source, physics\-based entries to the pose prediction challenge, which are based on templated molecular growth with hybrid machine learning/molecular mechanics interatomic potentials\. We show that our approach was among the top\-performing physics\-based methods, and with a retrospective change to the workflow to make greater use of the training data as templates, we obtain a success rate of 74% across SARS\-CoV\-2 and MERS\-CoV main protease target protein structures\. Finally, we investigate the importance of receptor side\-chain flexibility on retrospective performance\. We find that building poses against an ensemble of feasible side\-chain conformers is useful in generating improved binding poses but that the tested scoring functions are unable to successfully pick out native structures from the generated ensemble\.
