# Identification of Novel PFAS Transformation Products in AFFF\-Impacted Surface and Groundwater

**Autores:** Emmanuel C\. Tolefe; Gavyn C\. Lastoria; Jaanus Liigand; Lindsay S\. Cahill; Karl J\. Jobst

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract The widespread use of Aqueous Film\-Forming Foams \(AFFF\) has left thousands of contaminated “hotspots” across North America\. In Canada, only 25 per\-/polyfluoroalkyl substances \(PFAS\) are regulated in drinking water at 30 ng/L\. In the present study, cyclic ion mobility–tandem mass spectrometry \(cIMS\-MS2\) was used to identify 17 regulated and 52 unregulated PFAS in surface waters near a former firefighting training facility in Atlantic Canada, including 6 new PFAS that have not been previously reported in environmental waters: hydrido\-ketone\-5:2\-fluorotelomer sulfonate \(FTS\), ketone\-8:2\-FTS, unsaturated 8:2\-FTS, N\-methyl\-perfluorohexane sulfonamido sulfinate, N\-methyl\-perfluorooctane sulfonamido sulfinate, and perfluorohexane sulfonamido propionate\. Semi\-quantitation of unregulated PFAS was enabled by a machine\-learning model validated using a set of 21 targeted PFAS \(R2 &gt; 0\.95\)\. The total concentration of PFAS declined from 2500 ng/L to 364 ng/L as the surface water entered the North Atlantic approximately 5 km away from the hotspot\. Seven of forty\-two drinking water wells located along a 5 km transect from the source were characterized by exceedances between 30 and 191 ng/L\. Twenty\-seven PFAS were identified in the wells, including 13 that are unregulated and 5 that result from \(bio\)transformation of AFFF\. While the fraction of unregulated PFAS was low \(&lt;18%\), these transformation products may inform forensic studies aimed at establishing links between the source of pollution and its receptors\.
