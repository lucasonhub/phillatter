# Comparación de modelos estadísticos y aprendizaje automático para predecir rendimiento académico universitario con datos abiertos

**Autores:** Johnny Gerardo Barriga\-Arizabala; Rosman José Paucar Córdova; Nelson Marcelo Aldaz Herrera

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Predicting academic performance using statistical models and machine learning algorithms may provide useful information for the early identification of university performance patterns\. However, meaningful comparisons require homogeneous validation protocols and control of information leakage\. This study aimed to compare the performance of one statistical model and three machine learning algorithms for predicting university academic performance before the final examination\. A quantitative, observational, analytical, comparative, and predictive study was conducted using 560 open academic records\. The target variable was Categories, comprising five performance levels\. Seven predictors available before the final examination were used: Quiz, Midterm, Assignment\_1, Assignment\_2, Assignment\_3, Project, and Presentation\. Multinomial logistic regression, decision tree, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting were compared using an 80/20 stratified split and five\-fold stratified cross\-validation\. Macro\-F1 was the primary metric\. Random Forest showed the best overall profile, with a Macro\-F1 of 0\.7360 in cross\-validation and 0\.7633 on the test set, followed by Gradient Boosting with 0\.7099 and 0\.7477, respectively\. Random Forest achieved an Accuracy of 0\.7768 and an F1 score of 0\.8571 for Fail\. Midterm showed the highest permutation\-based predictive importance\. Ensemble\-based models showed a predictive advantage over the statistical reference model, although the findings are limited to the analyzed dataset and require external validation\.
