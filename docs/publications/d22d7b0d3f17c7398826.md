# Catastrophe Theory\-Informed Hybrid Modeling for Chip Splitting State and Specific Cutting Force Prediction in Thread Turning

**Autores:** Liangshan Xiong; Peng Wu; 徐明显; Shaonan Zhang; Baoyi Zhu

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Chip\-splitting can reduce cutting resistance, but its abrupt transition makes quantitative prediction difficult\. This study proposes a catastrophe theory\-informed framework for predicting the chip state and specific cutting force in the thread turning of 6061\-T6 aluminum alloy\. The framework combines a random forest classifier with a fully connected neural network and uses domain\-adversarial training to introduce the fold geometry of a cusp catastrophe equilibrium surface as a regularizer\. The experimental dataset comprises 1252 densely sampled equilibrium points from seven cutting force curves\. Classification used a stratified 1001/251 train/test split, whereas regression held out Experiments 2, 4, and 6 \(539 samples\) and used the remaining 713 samples for training and validation\. The random forest achieved 100\.00% accuracy on the stratified test set\. Across three initializations, the full regression model achieved MAPE = 1\.74% ± 0\.36%, R2 = 0\.9815 ± 0\.0020, maximum absolute percentage error = 4\.47%, and normalized RMSE = 0\.1315, which outperformed transfer learning, support vector regression, and the unconstrained FCN\. The proposed framework shows that combining catastrophe theory geometry with machine learning can improve the prediction of chip\-splitting states and specific cutting force in thread turning\.
