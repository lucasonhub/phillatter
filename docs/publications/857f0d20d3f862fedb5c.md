# Reconnections: Art, Education, and Inclusive Futures

**Autores:** Adrienne Boulton

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

researches et enjeux en éducation artistique, our Editorial Board collaborated on a publication call to invite arts education researchers to reflect on art education&\#x27;s priorities, purposes, and possibilities for the present and the future and the response was overwhelming\.In this issue, we hear from 26 authors in 10 articles and Salon Section visual essay to examine how arts education holds a unique space to call people in to cultivate inclusive, creative, and critical spaces for dialogue, reflection, and action \(CRAE Call for Submissions: Special Issue 2025\)\.LeBlanc, Reid, Weibe, Moalem, &amp; Qian approaches this call through a profound discussion of an artists&\#x27; residency program which activated anti\-racist and anti\-oppressive modes of engagement for students and gallery attendees\.Drawing on contemporary art practices, this work explores play\-, will\-, and care\-infused practices for their potential to stimulate empathetic responses as sites for transformative learning\.Trabelsi &amp; Moldoveanu&\#x27;s work examined the arts for its potential to cultivate inclusive, creative teaching practices in the visual arts to foster the well\-being of students with special educational needs\.Drawing on educational and public programs that were developed for Place Where the Waters Crossed, a solo exhibition by Diné artist Raven Chacon, Krishnan &amp; Dumoulin\-Bush problematize the framing of reconciliation and introduce the concept of conciliation to establish a framework for the non\-colonial museum programs\.The authors contend that conciliation must become a continuous institutional practice, not a temporary project, and that meaningful collaboration is where that work begins\.Beaudry, Brehm, Hammoun, Carignan, &amp; Sénéchal found that, in a study of 80 grade 11 high school students, a slam poetry program made poetry and writing feel more accessible, engaging, and relevant, particularly for students who were less interested in school\-based cultural activities\.Through creative workshops and performance, students gained confidence, participated more actively, and developed stronger connections with their classmates, themselves, and the arts\.The findings suggest that school\-based arts programs can create meaningful opportunities for student expression and inclusion, while also underscoring the importance of supportive artists, flexible participation, and continued funding for such initiatives\.Author and featured artist Lo&\#x27;s Silk Cocoon examines the uses of silk and collaborative art\-making to recover fragmented family histories while exploring the complexities of Chinese\-Canadian identity and belonging\.Drawing on the history and symbolism of silk, Lo transforms a lost legacy of craftsmanship into a contemporary meditation on migration, cultural hybridity, memory, and the possibility of reimagining inherited narratives\.The project ultimately argues that art education can use material engagement as a powerful form of knowledge\-making, connecting personal histories to broader cultural histories while creating space for empathy and more hopeful futures\.
