# AI and Legal Language Education: A Review of Pedagogical Applications, Writing Development, and Emerging Challenges

**Autores:** N\. Polraj alias Goutham; K\. Gurusamy; A\. Pradheepa

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Generative AI now writes passable legal prose, and law schools are still deciding what that means for the teaching of legal English\. This systematic review maps how artificial intelligence has been used in legal language education between 2020 and 2026, with attention to pedagogical applications, writing development and the problems that recur across studies\. The review draws on Scopus, Web of Science, ERIC, SpringerLink and ScienceDirect\. Studies were coded for context, design, AI tool, pedagogical use, writing\-related outcomes and reported limitations\. The evidence base is small, recent and uneven\. Most studies appeared after the release of ChatGPT in late 2022, and they concentrate on exam performance, legal vocabulary and instructor perceptions\. Controlled evidence on legal writing development is scarce, and the few experiments available suggest that benefits depend on prior skill and on whether students receive structured guidance\. Reported challenges include fabricated legal authority, shallow issue\-spotting, over\-reliance, academic integrity and low instructor preparedness\. Studies from India and from learners using English as an additional language are almost absent\. The review closes with a research agenda centred on longitudinal designs, validated writing rubrics, and tools built on verified legal corpora\.
