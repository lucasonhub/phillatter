# Explainable machine learning using pretreatment complete blood count parameters and demographic characteristics for gastric cancer risk stratification: a temporal validation study

**Autores:** Yongfeng Qiao; Qian Long; Jin Tang; Xin Wang; 王守力

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Gastric cancer \(GC\) is often diagnosed at an advanced stage, while endoscopy is invasive and conventional serum tumour markers have limited sensitivity\. A low\-cost, widely available tool that can identify patients at higher risk and prioritise them for endoscopic evaluation could therefore strengthen early\-detection pathways\. The complete blood count \(CBC\) is routinely available and well suited to such first\-contact risk stratification; however, existing CBC\-based machine\-learning models are often developed in cohorts that mix prevalent, postoperative, on\-treatment and surveillance records, potentially confounding the haematological signal\. We therefore developed and temporally validated an explainable CBC\-based model to stratify the risk of newly diagnosed GC versus benign gastric disease in a rigorously verified, treatment\-naïve cohort\. Methods In this retrospective single\-centre study, 2,044 patients \(477 pathologically confirmed, newly diagnosed, treatment\-naïve GC and 1,567 benign gastric\-disease controls\) were assembled through case\-by\-case chart review, patient\-level identity de\-duplication and application of the same prespecified comorbidity\-screening criteria\. The baseline CBC was obtained at admission before treatment\. A parsimonious model incorporated six routinely available variables: age, sex, and neutrophil, lymphocyte, monocyte and platelet counts\. Eight algorithms were trained using five\-fold cross\-validation in the training cohort \(n = 1,309\) and evaluated in a temporally separated validation cohort \(n = 735\)\. Random forest was designated as the reference explainable model and interpreted using SHAP\. Model behaviour in tumour\-marker\-negative GC and robustness in sensitivity analyses were also assessed\. Results Discrimination was similar across algorithms\. In temporal validation, random forest achieved an AUC of 0\.831 \(95% CI 0\.798–0\.864\), which was numerically similar to that of the highest\-AUC model, XGBoost \(AUC 0\.832\), with substantially overlapping 95% confidence intervals\. Random forest had a low Brier score \(0\.133\) and showed positive net benefit across the evaluated threshold\-probability range\. SHAP identified lymphocyte count, male sex and age as the leading predictors, with an identical feature ranking under XGBoost \(Spearman ρ = 1\.00\)\. Adding derived inflammatory ratios did not improve discrimination \(ΔAUC −0\.010, p = 0\.139\)\. Among temporal\-validation patients with triple\-negative GC, defined as CEA\-, CA19\-9\- and CA72\-4\-negative disease, the model classified 62\.7% \(95% CI 50\.0–73\.9\) as high risk\. Conclusions A six\-variable model combining pretreatment CBC parameters with age and sex discriminated newly diagnosed GC from benign gastric disease in a temporally separated validation cohort and showed broadly acceptable graphical calibration and a low Brier score\. The model classified a substantial proportion of tumour\-marker\-negative GC cases as high risk, supporting further evaluation as a low\-cost pre\-endoscopic triage aid\. External multicentre validation is required\.
