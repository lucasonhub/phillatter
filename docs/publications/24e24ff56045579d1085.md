\_Communication and Cognition: An Interdisciplinary Quarterly Journal\_ 59 \(3\-4\):155\-176\. 2026Thinking is typically conceived of as applying general rules to specific situations\. This is believed to hold even in cases where somebody \(e\.g\. Newton\) is in the process of constructing a new such set of general rules; for it is assumed that in doing this, he is following pre\-existing methodological precepts\. This &quot;First Principles&quot; approach to reasoning is fundamentally false\. It does not model how AI works, and it does not model what we know about how the mind works from the way AI works\. In the present work, it is shown that connectionism accommodates many of the successes of cognitive science \(e\.g\. Universal Grammar\) that have traditionally been seen as versions of computationalism\. This eviscerates the Searle\-Wittgenstein\-style dismissals of cognitive science on the grounds that they involve a &quot;homunculus fallacy&quot;: this line of attack only has merit relative to the absurdly crude view that to think is to read off of an internal teleprompter\. Having made a case for a connectionist theory of mind, the present work then tries to answer a new question: Is there anything in existing AI that is functionally comparable to consciousness? The answer is no\. It is argued that consciousness is fundamentally integrative in nature—that, indeed, its very purpose is to &quot;incorporate&quot; what would otherwise be disparate cognitive currents\. It is then argued that if AI were created that had to protect itself from environmental threats, there would have to be a system within it that looked for information on the basis of which to protect itself\. This system would thereby have some of the functional properties of consciousness, in particular: \(i\) its tendency to bring together otherwise mutually isolated thoughts and streams of experience; \(ii\) its tendency to assign degrees of relevance to a given situation to said thoughts, thereby organizing them hierarchically; and, what follows, \(iii\) its ability to turn gelatinous heaps of mentation into a goal\-oriented, action\-oriented self\.
