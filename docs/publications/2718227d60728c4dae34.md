# Chronic Kidney Disease Progression Prediction Using Clinical Data

**Autores:** Shravani Khond

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Chronic Kidney Disease \(CKD\) is a long\-term condition in which kidney function declines gradually, and early identification of high\-risk patients supports timely monitoring and treatment\. Machine learning can combine many clinical and laboratory variables, such as age, blood pressure, serum creatinine, eGFR, urine albumin, blood glucose, diabetes and hypertension, to estimate a patient’s kidney health category\. This paper describes a prototype that classifies patients into five categories \(Healthy Kidney, Mild CKD Stage 1–2, Moderate CKD Stage 3, Severe CKD Stage 4, and Kidney Failure Stage 5\) from a tabular dataset of 21,000 records and 36 columns, with an 80:20 split \(16,800 training and 4,200 test records\) and a Streamlit interface for entering patient values\. The prototype’s initial test run reported 100% accuracy\. This paper treats that figure as a warning sign rather than a finding: a perfect score on a tabular clinical dataset often indicates target leakage, for example when eGFR, which is used to define CKD stage, is supplied as an input\. The paper also points out that a single cross\-sectional snapshot supports classifying current CKD stage, not predicting progression, which needs repeated measurements over time\. Rather than presenting unsupported performance figures, it reviews the relevant literature and proposes an evaluation framework with leakage checks, per\-class metrics, confusion matrices, calibration and external validation\. The system is intended for research and education only and is not a substitute for professional medical diagnosis\.
