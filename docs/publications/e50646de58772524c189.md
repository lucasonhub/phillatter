# Sequential phage therapy against Pseudomonas aeruginosa colonising a chronic venous skin ulcer

**Autores:** Matteo Passerini; Novella Cesta; Caterina Ferretti; Francesco Petri; Michela Pellegrini; Rita Carminati; Simona Landonio; Ahmed El Goutbi; GINEVRA MORELLI; Sara Giordana Rimoldi; Sara Bolognini; Maria Vittoria Cossu; Irene Mileto; Gherard Batisti Biffignandi; Greta Petazzoni; Benjamin K\. Chan; Andrea Gori; Mariagrazia Di Luca

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Chronic venous ulcers are often complicated by persistent bacterial colonisation and biofilm formation, impairing healing and limiting definitive surgical closure\. We report the compassionate\-use treatment of a chronic venous leg ulcer, presumed to be adversely affected by persistent Pseudomonas aeruginosa colonisation using sequential topical administration of two lytic bacteriophages, TIVP\-27M and LPS\-26\. Phage therapy was well tolerated and associated with improvement in clinically suspected biofilm\-related indicators\. Surgical ulcer coverage was subsequently performed\. Longitudinal microbiological and genomic analyses showed persistence of a clonal P\. aeruginosa lineage, with transient emergence of a phage\-resistant isolate on day 16\. This isolate exhibited impaired growth and reduced swimming, swarming, and twitching motility, alongside genomic alterations affecting surface\-associated and motility\-related functions, including pilR , flgE , galU , and hmgA \. Later isolates remained susceptible to both phages\. These findings support personalised topical phage therapy as a potential adjunctive strategy for selected chronic wounds while highlighting complex evolutionary dynamics during treatment\.
