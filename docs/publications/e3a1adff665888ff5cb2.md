# Reading the Genetic Clues: How Bioinformatics and One Health Can Help India Prevent the Next Zoonotic Outbreak

**Autores:** Akash Balasaheb Mote1, Vibha Singh1, Aaliya Fayaz2, Sanjumon E S1, Anuradha Panwar3, Aakir Ahmad Kutay4, Amitava Paul5, Haris Ayoub1\*

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Emerging infectious diseases increasingly highlight the interconnectedness of human, animal and environmental health\. Climate change, habitat disturbance and changing patterns of contact between species can influence the emergence and spread of zoonotic pathogens\. The One Health approach addresses these challenges through collaboration among medical, veterinary and environmental sectors\. Bioinformatics strengthens this approach by enabling genomic sequencing, phylogenetic analysis, metagenomic surveillance and the investigation of antimicrobial resistance across human, animal and environmental samples\. Evidence from outbreaks such as Nipah virus in Kerala demonstrates how genomic comparisons can help identify relationships between pathogens detected in different hosts, although epidemiological and field investigations remain essential to confirm transmission pathways\. Wastewater surveillance and machine\-learning approaches also offer opportunities to identify unexpected pathogens and prioritize surveillance activities\. However, fragmented surveillance systems, unequal laboratory capacity, data\-sharing barriers and ethical concerns can limit effective implementation\. Strengthening genomic infrastructure, developing skilled personnel and improving coordination among relevant departments are important steps towards a more integrated surveillance system\. Combining bioinformatics with One Health can support earlier threat detection, evidence\-based public\-health decisions and more effective prevention and control of zoonotic diseases in India\.
