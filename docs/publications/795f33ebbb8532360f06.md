# Limits of Statistical Assurance for Embodied and Agentic AI under Adaptive Adversaries

**Autores:** Inderjeet Singh

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Finite safety assessments cannot exclude an adversary that preserves unattacked behavior until certification and activates afterward\. We characterize this limitation for embodied and tool\-use AI\. An adversary that matches the complete observation law until observable, irrevocable certification and retains intervention access afterward attains $v\\mathbb\{E\}\[\(n\-S\_0\)\_\+\]$ expected post\-certification events over $n$ trials, where $v$ is the active event probability and $S\_0$ the unattacked certification time\. The result covers adaptive and privately randomized evaluators\. Switching at certification is exactly optimal for a fixed likelihood\-ratio test under memoryless, event\-increasing interventions without a binding intervention budget\. A positive clean\-certification requirement forces linear worst\-case expected excess when intervention raises event rates over an authorized deployment horizon, even if certification is deferred to the deadline\. In simulated RoCo collaboration, switching yields commanded\-separation violations in 34 of 36 post\-certification episodes\. Preregistered randomized AgentDojo trials estimate 1\.98 additional security violations per four\-task continuation \(95% interval \[1\.67, 2\.29\]\)\. An adversarial language\-model controller infers hidden certification from noisy evaluation receipts and times intervention accordingly\. With permanent revocation, a mixture likelihood\-ratio monitor bounds cumulative excess above a prespecified reference rate at square\-root order, up to logarithmic factors, on every sample path\. A single fixed alternative can permit linear expected excess under stationary attacks\. Against a controller observing the monitor&\#x27;s rule and statistic, live mixture monitoring reduces violations from 12\.00 to 3\.81 per 24 assigned task opportunities\. Revocation also reduces tasks executed\.
