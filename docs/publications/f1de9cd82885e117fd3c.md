# Hyoscine N\-butylbromide administration before colostrum feeding improves passive transfer in dairy calves

**Autores:** Durmuş Fatih BAŞER; Abdurrahman Kürşat ÖZSOY

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study evaluated whether hyoscine N\-butylbromide administration before colostrum feeding could influence colostral immunoglobulin G \(IgG\) absorption and passive transfer in neonatal Jersey calves\. Eighteen clinically healthy calves were allocated to a control group \( n = 6\) or a hyoscine N\-butylbromide\-treated group \( n = 12\)\. Treated calves received hyoscine N\-butylbromide at 0\.4 mg/kg intramuscularly 15–30 min before the first colostrum feeding\. All calves received high\-quality maternal colostrum \(Brix ≥25%\) within 6 h after birth\. Serum Brix was measured at 48 h, and the same serum samples were analyzed for IgG, total protein, gamma\-glutamyl transferase \(GGT\), and glucose\. Treated calves had higher serum Brix than controls at 48 h \(10\.96 ± 0\.81 vs\. 9\.53 ± 0\.7%; p = 0\.002\)\. Serum IgG was also higher in treated calves at 48 h \(3,243 ± 794 vs\. 2,175 ± 1,150 mg/dL; p = 0\.034\), whereas total protein, GGT, and glucose did not differ between groups \( p ≥ 0\.217\)\. These preliminary findings suggest that hyoscine N\-butylbromide administration before colostrum feeding may be associated with improved passive transfer indicators in neonatal calves; however, confirmation in larger, adequately powered studies is required\.
