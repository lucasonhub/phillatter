# Designing intergroup interaction in social virtual reality: avatar identity\-cue visibility and environmental plausibility

**Autores:** Timor Schwartz Miler; Nili Steinfeld

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Social Virtual Reality \(SVR\) enables designers to configure the conditions under which users meet and interact\. This qualitative study examines two design dimensions in intergroup encounters: the visibility of identity cues in avatars, which governs which social group categories are immediately available to participants, and the plausibility of the environment, which governs which everyday interactional scripts are available for acting on them\. 166 Israeli participants from the Haredi and Ethiopian Israeli minorities, and the Jewish non\-Haredi majority population, took part in structured encounters across three theoretically selected designed configurations: a plausible environment with avatars displaying visible identity cues, the same environment with identity\-neutral avatars, and a less plausible environment with visible identity cues\. Session recordings, observational notes, open\-ended questionnaires and semi\-structured interviews were analyzed through inductive thematic analysis and compared across configurations descriptively\. Where identity cues were visible, participants referred more often to appearance and group affiliation, formed group\-based connections, and adapted their communication to others’ perceived backgrounds\. Where avatars were identity\-neutral, recognition was available through names, accents and interactional style\. The less plausible environment was associated with more extended and exploratory dialogue, the plausible setting with rapid, task\-focused agreement\. Proximity\-based affiliation, humor, embodied communication and interpersonal familiarization appeared across all configurations\. Technological proficiency, unevenly distributed along the same social lines the encounter was meant to bridge, was translated into initiative and leadership, somewhat eroding the equal status condition intergroup contact emphasizes\. The study positions SVR as a designed social environment and draws design implications for intergroup encounters\.
