# Machine learning\-based modeling of soil respiration components across four forest types in a subtropical mountainous forest with implications for modeling strategies and hydrothermal controls

**Autores:** Nan Deng; Yaqin Xiao; Fengfeng Ma; Yuxin Tian

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Soil respiration \(Rs\) is a key component of the terrestrial carbon cycle, yet its accurate prediction remains challenging due to complex interactions among environmental drivers and the distinct mechanisms governing its components\. In this study, we conducted monthly 24\-h measurement campaigns at hourly intervals of total soil respiration \(Rs\) and its components, autotrophic respiration \(Ra\) and heterotrophic respiration \(Rh\), across four typical forest types in the Wuling Mountains, a representative subtropical mountainous region in central–southern China\. Multiple machine learning \(ML\) models were developed under different modeling strategies, feature engineering schemes, and validation protocols\. Component\-based modeling markedly outperformed unified and forest\-type\-based approaches, suggesting that under the current experimental design, differences among respiration components exerted a stronger influence on predictive performance than differences among forest types\. Under random validation, ensemble tree models achieved high accuracy for Rs and Rh \( R 2 &gt; 0\.90\), but their advantage narrowed substantially under leave\-one\-month\-out blocked validation\. Notably, CatBoost trained without temporal variables showed the most consistent temporal generalization and was therefore selected for ecological interpretation\. SHAP analysis revealed that deepest monitored soil hydrothermal coupling and vertical thermal structure were the dominant controls on Rs and Rh, particularly the interaction between the deepest monitored soil temperature and moisture; depth\-ablation analysis further confirmed the independent predictive contribution of deep\-layer measurements\. In contrast, Ra was primarily related to deep\-layer daytime temperature and moisture balance but remained the least predictable component, reflecting unmeasured plant physiological controls\. Our results highlight the importance of integrating appropriate modeling strategies, validation protocols, and machine learning techniques to improve prediction of soil respiration components\. The findings also emphasize the role of hydrothermal coupling and vertical heterogeneity in regulating soil carbon fluxes in subtropical forest ecosystems\.
