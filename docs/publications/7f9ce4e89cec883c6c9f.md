# Early serum n‐6 and n‐3 fatty acid profile and preschool‐age neurodevelopment

**Autores:** Sara Berggren; Josefine Roswall; Maria Blomqvist; Ola Andersson

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OBJECTIVES: Evidence suggests that adequate supplies of omega\-6 \(n\-6\) and omega\-3 \(n\-3\) fatty acids \(FAs\) are beneficial for brain development, but serum data are scarce\. We explored correlations between early serum proportions of n\-6 and n\-3 FAs and neurodevelopment at 4 years of age\. METHODS: Serum proportions for docosahexaenoic acid \(DHA\), total n\-3 and the n\-6/n\-3 ratio were analysed from umbilical\-cord, at 4 and 12 months for 119 children born vaginally at full\-term at the Halland Hospital Halmstad, Sweden, in 2008\-2009\. Neurodevelopmental testing at 4 years of age included the complete Wechsler Preschool and Primary Scale of Intelligence \(WPPSI\-III\) and the Ages and Stages Questionnaire \(ASQ\-3\)\. Pearson correlation analysis was conducted for WPPSI\-III and Spearman&\#x27;s rho for ASQ\-3 followed by forward\-selection regression models for significant correlations\. RESULTS: Higher proportions of DHA, n\-3 FAs and low n\-6/n\-3 ratio at birth correlated with higher gross motor scores at age 4 \(Spearman&\#x27;s rho, 0\.278, 0\.292 and \-0\.261, p &lt; 0\.05\)\. Low n\-6/n\-3 ratio at 12 months correlated with higher full\-scale intelligence quotient \(IQ\) and higher verbal IQ \(\-0\.279 and \-0\.269, p &lt; 0\.05\), at age 4\. CONCLUSIONS: In this exploratory study, elevated serum n\-3 FAs and low n\-6/n\-3 ratio during the brain growth spurt show statistical correlations with higher neurodevelopmental scores with respect to motor control, verbal abilities, and overall cognition at 4 years of age, with small\-to moderate effect sizes\.
