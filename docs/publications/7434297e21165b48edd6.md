# Post\-lesson supervisory dialogue and instructional adaptation in physical education: a qualitative multiple\-case study

**Autores:** Haifa Abdullah Jamhour

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction This qualitative cross\-case comparison examined three physical education supervisor–teacher dyads in Palestinian government schools to explore how post\-lesson supervisory dialogue was associated with opportunities for reflection and how discussed ideas were subsequently taken up in short\-term practice\. Methods Data comprised three audio\-recorded dialogues, three follow\-up teacher interviews, three follow\-up supervisor interviews, and one classroom observation per teacher\. Reflexive thematic analysis, complemented by a focused sequential dialogue analysis of contiguous conversational turns, was used to analyze the data\. Results Four themes were generated: affirmation as a relational entry point; variation in reflective participation associated with question design; movement from stated intention to reported and observed adaptation; and power dynamics expressed through evaluative framing, interactionally ambiguous agreement, and the delayed visibility of teacher agency\. Across the three cases, open and context\-sensitive questions were followed by more elaborated teacher explanations, whereas presuppositional questions and direct recommendations were more often followed by brief agreement\. Follow\-up interviews and observations showed direct adoption, adapted implementation, and selective non\-adoption\. Discussion These case\-specific patterns document short\-term instructional adaptation and agreement\-implementation divergence, but they do not establish causal effects, sustained change, or measurable effects on student learning\.
