# Stone Characteristics and Surgical Treatment Patterns Among Patients With Urolithiasis at a Brazilian Referral Hospital: A Retrospective Observational Study

**Autores:** Arthur B Bernardes; Lucas S Pontes; Ruan P Melotti; Alexander Hatsumura Casini

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Urolithiasis is a common and recurrent disorder that can cause obstruction, infection, renal impairment, repeated emergency care, and the need for surgical intervention\.Real\-world treatment patterns may help referral centers understand case mix and plan endourological resources\.Objective: To characterize the institutional surgical case mix of patients undergoing operative treatment for urolithiasis at a Brazilian nonprofit teaching hospital\.Secondary exploratory analyses assessed the associations of stone location and stone size with the primary surgical procedure performed\.Methods: This retrospective observational study included 624 unique patients aged 16 years or older who underwent rigid ureteroscopy, flexible ureterorenoscopy, or percutaneous nephrolithotomy between June 1 and December 31, 2025\.Demographic, stone\-related, operative, anesthetic, and ureteral stent variables were extracted from institutional records\.Three secondary exploratory contingency analyses evaluated procedure versus stone location, procedure versus stone size, and stone location versus stone size using Pearson&\#x27;s chisquare tests; Cramer&\#x27;s V quantified effect size\.Analyses involving missing stone size or location used complete cases\.Results: Men accounted for 361 patients \(57\.9%\), and mean age was 46\.6 ± 13\.8 years\.Flexible ureterorenoscopy was the most frequent primary procedure \(436; 69\.9%\), followed by rigid ureteroscopy \(147; 23\.6%\) and percutaneous nephrolithotomy \(41; 6\.6%\)\.Spinal anesthesia, alone or with sedation, was used in 559 patients \(89\.6%\), and a double\-J stent was used in 448 \(71\.8%\)\.Procedure distribution varied markedly according to stone location \(χ 2 \(4\) = 1108\.77,p &lt; 0\.001; Cramer&\#x27;s V = 0\.946; n = 619\) and stone size \(χ 2 \(6\) = 301\.23,p &lt; 0\.001; Cramer&\#x27;s V = 0\.505; n = 591\)\.Stone size and location were also associated \(χ 2 \(6\) = 323\.63,p &lt; 0\.001; Cramer&\#x27;s V = 0\.525; n = 588\)\.Conclusions: Surgical care was dominated by minimally invasive endourological procedures\.Procedure distribution varied markedly according to stone location and size\.These findings describe the operative case mix of a Brazilian referral hospital and may support service planning and future prospective quality studies\.
