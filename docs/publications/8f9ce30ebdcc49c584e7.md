# Single\-Cell Whole\-Transcriptome Detection Expands the Observable RNA Space for AI Virtual\-Cell Models

**Autores:** Xudong Liu; Bo Zhang; Kefei Zou; Ruikai Wan; Qiangyuan Zhu

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Single\-cell RNA sequencing has supplied cell atlases and artificial intelligence \(AI\)\-enabled virtual\-cell models with scalable, gene\-level descriptions of cellular state\. Yet prevalent poly\(A\)\-based counting assays capture only part of the RNA landscape\. Here, “single\-cell whole\-transcriptome detection” denotes sequencing\-based efforts to detect a broader range of RNA biotypes than conventional poly\(A\)\-based gene counting; no assay detects or quantifies every cellular RNA species without bias\. We compare RNA\-capture scope, transcript\-structure resolution, temporal labeling, spatial context, and additional modalities as distinct measurement dimensions\. From a biosensing perspective, analytical sensitivity, transcript coverage, throughput, and assay bias determine which signals are available to computational models\. We then examine cell\-state representations, regulatory networks, dynamics, perturbation prediction, and foundation models\. Total\-RNA assays reveal additional non\-coding RNAs, long\-read methods inform transcript structure, and temporal assays constrain RNA kinetics\. Whether these signals improve prediction beyond conventional gene\-count inputs requires matched, task\-specific model comparisons\. We distinguish molecular observability, statistical association, predictive performance, and causal validation, which requires appropriate interventions\. We propose benchmarks controlling sequencing depth, cost, model architecture, and held\-out samples while testing feature ablations, out\-of\-distribution performance, uncertainty calibration, and experimental outcomes\. Together, high\-throughput poly\(A\)\-based profiling and broader RNA measurements provide complementary cellular breadth and regulatory depth\.
