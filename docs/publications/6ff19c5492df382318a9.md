# Clinical Predictors of Relapse in Pediatric Trichotillomania: A Retrospective Study

**Autores:** Yüksel Sümeyra Naralan; Uğur Tekeoğlu

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: This study examined sociodemographic and clinical characteristics associated with relapse presentation in pediatric trichotillomania \(TTM\)\.Methods: Medical records of 72 children and adolescents diagnosed with TTM between January 2015 and January 2025 were reviewed retrospectively\. Cases were classified as first presentation or relapse presentation according to the presence of a documented prior remission period followed by re\-emergence of hair\-pulling symptoms leading to clinical re\-presentation\. The primary outcome was relapse presentation at the index clinical assessment\. Firth penalized logistic regression was used as an exploratory small\-sample model\.Results: Relapse presentation was observed in 36\.1% of cases\. In group comparisons, relapse presentations were more frequent among adolescents aged 12–18 years and showed higher frequencies of multisite involvement, eyelash involvement, and several other behavioral and site\-related characteristics\. In the exploratory Firth penalized model coded with relapse presentation as the event, adolescent age group \(odds ratio \[OR\], 31\.56; 95% confidence interval \[CI\], 2\.99–333\.02; P = \.004\), eyelash involvement \(OR, 15\.22; 95% CI, 2\.07–112\.19; P = \.008\), and multisite involvement \(OR, 10\.63; 95% CI, 1\.82–62\.01; P = \.009\) were associated with higher odds of relapse presentation\. The distribution of psychiatric comorbidity burden showed a borderline between\-group difference \(P = \.050\)\.Conclusion: In this retrospective clinical sample, relapse presentation was more frequently observed among adolescents and among patients with multisite or eyelash involvement\. These findings should be interpreted as relapse\-associated clinical characteristics rather than validated predictors or causal risk factors\. Prospective studies with systematic follow\-up are needed to confirm whether these features independently predict relapse\.
