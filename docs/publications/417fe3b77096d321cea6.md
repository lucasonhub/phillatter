# Lightweight anomaly detection via knowledge distillation from ensemble teachers

**Autores:** Xia Cui; Tong Li

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper proposes a knowledge distillation\-based anomaly detection method that employs a heterogeneous Isolation Forest ensemble as the teacher and a lightweight neural network as the student\. The student learns the teacher’s soft labels by minimizing the Kullback–Leibler \(KL\) divergence\. Evaluated on three categories from the MVTec 3D\-AD dataset \(bagel, carrot, and cookie\), the teacher achieves a macro\-average area under the receiver operating characteristic curve \(AUROC\) of 0\.6406, whereas the student, with 2\.010 million parameters in the multilayer perceptron \(MLP\) head and an approximately 0\.051 ms detector\-head inference time per image \(both excluding the ResNet\-50 backbone\), achieves a macro\-average AUROC of 0\.5937 and delivers an approximately 11\.6\-fold detector\-head inference speedup over the teacher ensemble\. Compared with representative deep learning baselines \(RD4AD and PatchCore\), the proposed method achieves moderate accuracy with lower detector\-head latency and a smaller detector\-head model footprint \(excluding the backbone\)\. These results suggest that knowledge distillation can serve as an effective compression technique, transferring ensemble knowledge to a lightweight student with a measurable accuracy trade\-off\. The benefit of distillation is category\-dependent: the student outperforms the teacher on one category but exhibits performance degradation on the others\. Moreover, the No\-KD ablation \(student trained without KD\) achieves a higher macro\-average AUROC \(0\.6057 vs\. 0\.5937\), indicating that soft\-label distillation is a viable compression strategy rather than a universal performance enhancer\. The conditions under which distillation improves or degrades performance are discussed\.
