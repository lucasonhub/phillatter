# Rethinking the Lecturer in the Digital Age: A Critical Conceptual Analysis of Role Transformation in Higher Education

**Autores:** Elizabeth Karoya; Patrick Bwowe

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose: This paper critically examines how digital transformation is reshaping the lecturer’s professional role in universities\. Rather than simply describing technology adoption, it asks how digital transformation reshapes academic identity, pedagogical agency, and institutional power, and argues that role transformation is contested and non\-linear rather than a smooth technological progression\. Design/Methodology/Approach: The paper employs a critical conceptual analysis methodology\. Literature was identified through targeted searches of major education\-technology and higher\-education journals, prioritising studies published since 2012 that directly addressed lecturer roles, digital pedagogy, or academic identity, and was synthesised thematically around three theoretical traditions: Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge \(TPACK\), Engeström’s Activity Theory, and Selwyn’s critical technology studies\. These traditions are combined to construct an original analytical framework for understanding lecturer role transformation\. Findings: The analysis reveals three core tensions structuring the transformation of lecturer roles: \(i\) the tension between pedagogical agency and institutional control embedded in digital platforms; \(ii\) the tension between professional identity stability and the demand for perpetual digital re\-skilling; and \(iii\) the tension between the rhetoric of student‑centred learning and the managerial logics that increasingly govern digital higher education\. The paper argues that these tensions are systemic rather than incidental, and that dominant theoretical models, particularly TPACK, under\-theorise the structural and political dimensions of role transformation\. Practical Implications: The findings call for a reorientation of institutional digital transformation strategies away from technocentric implementation toward critically informed professional development models that recognise the structural conditions shaping lecturer agency\. Originality/Value: The paper’s main contribution is the Critical Ecological Framework of Lecturer Role Transformation \(CEFLRT\), which integrates structural, agentic, and identity dimensions that existing models treat separately\. It repositions the lecturer not as a passive recipient of technological change but as a reflexive professional navigating contested institutional terrain\.
