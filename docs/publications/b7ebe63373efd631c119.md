# Target selection of native yeasts isolated from the industrial process: a strategy to optimize bioethanol production

**Autores:** Cláudia Steckelberg

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The intensification of climate change demands a transition toward renewable energy matrices, positioning bioethanol as a sustainable alternative capable of reducing greenhouse gas \(GHG\) emissions by up to 90%\. Although Brazil’s bioethanol sector is highly consolidated, optimizing alcoholic fermentation remains a challenge, particularly regarding the selection of robust yeast strains\. Industrial fermentation typically begins with commercial Saccharomyces cerevisiae inocula, which are frequently outcompeted and replaced by wild, autochthonous strains throughout the harvest season\. While some native strains compromise production efficiency, others exhibit superior adaptation to industrial stress\. This study presents an industrial case study based on long\-term microbiological monitoring \(2018–2024\) of an operational unit that integrated the native S\. cerevisiae strain DBP\-701–isolated in 2017 due to its superior fermentative performance–into a starter blend with commercial strains \(PE\-2, Angel, and CAT\-1\)\. Population dynamics revealed that DBP\-701 consistently exhibited high prevalence within the microbial consortium, maintaining strong population persistence during the 2021 and 2022 cycles from the beginning to the end of the harvests\. This high prevalence was associated with a reduction in the establishment of invasive wild strains under routine factory conditions\. However, the 2024 harvest diverged from historical patterns; we hypothesize that extreme weather events, including intense heatwaves and sugarcane fires, contributed to increased metabolic stress and altered raw material quality, coinciding with a disruption in strain persistence\. Overall, deploying selected native yeasts like DBP\-701 represents a valuable strategy for enhancing population stability under standard operational conditions, while highlighting potential vulnerabilities under severe environmental perturbations\.
