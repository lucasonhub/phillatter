# Neighborhood transformation in a shrinking city: using multimodal large language models to evaluate housing redevelopment spillover effects in Buffalo, New York

**Autores:** Li Yin; Robert Mark Silverman

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Across the American Rust Belt, post\-industrial “shrinking” cities are characterized by sustained population decline and weakened housing markets in the aftermath of deindustrialization\. These conditions have produced deteriorating housing stock and constrained private reinvestment, underscoring the need for alternative planning approaches centered on stabilization rather than growth\. This study examines the potential of public\-private\-nonprofit partnerships \(PPNPs\) in public housing redevelopment to generate neighborhood\-level spillover effects in underdeveloped areas of Buffalo, New York\. Understanding these dynamics is critical for informing equitable revitalization strategies in legacy cities where traditional market mechanisms have stalled\. This study employs a novel methodological framework using street\-level imagery and multimodal large language models\. Image data collected at two time points, approximately a decade apart, are analyzed to assess parcel\- and street\-level changes in residential property conditions surrounding the Perry Homes redevelopment site\. This approach enables scalable, temporally comparative analysis while mitigating limitations associated with conventional survey\-based methods\. The findings indicate that nearly 10 times more properties are categorized as “Improved” than as “Declined,” reflecting a strong asymmetry associated with an overall positive shift in neighborhood conditions between 2014 and 2025\. This improvement spans a spectrum from minor rehabilitation to substantial upgrades\. These patterns suggest that anchor\-led PPNP investments can contribute to localized housing stabilization, albeit unevenly\. The results demonstrate the utility of generative AI for evaluating urban redevelopment impacts and provide empirical evidence that strategically structured PPNPs can foster incremental neighborhood recovery\. This research contributes methodological innovation and policy\-relevant insights for stabilizing shrinking cities without reliance on population growth\.
