# Ethical Artificial Intelligence Leadership for Multilingual and Intercultural Language Education in Developing Countries

**Autores:** Abdul Walid QADIRI; Abdul Wali Sirat; Musawer Hakimi

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial intelligence \(AI\) is entering university governance, teaching, and language education across developing countries, yet ethical\-leadership scholarship remains concentrated on well\-resourced, digitally mature institutions\. This paper reports a PRISMA\-informed, PICO\-guided literature review examining how ethical AI leadership can be theorized for multilingual and intercultural language education in developing\-country higher education, with a conflict\-affected higher\-education setting in South Asia as an illustrative case\. Fifty\-six sources met inclusion criteria after a staged identification, screening, and eligibility process conducted by a single reviewer using open academic search engines and publisher platforms rather than subscription\-indexed databases\. Thematic synthesis identified four recurring barrier domains infrastructural access, institutional governance, socio\-cultural capacity, and linguistic/data equity and three leadership theories \(transformational, distributed, and ethical leadership\) that jointly inform a proposed six\-dimension conceptual framework for ethical AI leadership\. The review finds that AI tools trained predominantly on high\-resource languages risk marginalizing low\-resource languages such as Dari and Pashto, and that governance models assuming dedicated oversight offices do not transfer to capacity\-constrained institutions\. The paper contributes a context\-sensitive, evidence\-informed framework and a transparent, reproducible search protocol for future empirical validation in developing\-country and conflict\-affected higher\-education settings\.
