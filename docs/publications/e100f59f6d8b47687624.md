# การจำแนกรปแบบอปสงคการเปรยบเทยบวธการพยากรณยอดขายระยะสนสำหรบสนคาทนมลคาสง กรณศกษาผจดจำหนายเครองตรวจสอบและวเคราะหโลหะมคา

**Autores:** Wanussuda Kamput

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This research aimed to 1\) classify the demand patterns of precious\-metal analyzers by model using the Average Demand Interval \(ADI\) and the squared Coefficient of Variation \(CV²\) criteria, 2\) compare the accuracy of several short\-term monthly sales forecasting methods through Rolling\-Origin evaluation, and 3\) propose a decision framework for selecting a forecasting method suited to each analyzer model&\#x27;s demand pattern\. This research was quantitative case\-study research, examining data from a single precious\-metal analyzer distributor and using actual sales transaction data of three analyzer models — namely XC\-1, XC\-2, and XC\-1M — over a period of 15 months\. The data comprised 15 monthly periods \(March 2025 – May 2026\), with the first seven periods used as the initial training window and eight forecast origins used for out\-of\-sample evaluation\. The instrument used for data collection was a sales transaction record form\. The statistics used for data analysis were the Average Demand Interval \(ADI\), the squared Coefficient of Variation \(CV²\), the Mean Absolute Deviation \(MAD\), the Root Mean Squared Error \(RMSE\), and the Mean Absolute Scaled Error \(MASE\), with forecasting accuracy evaluated through Rolling\-Origin evaluation together with the Friedman test\. The research results were as follows: All three models were continuously sold products, with model XC\-1 exhibiting smooth demand, while models XC\-2 and XC\-1M exhibited erratic demand\. The Simple Moving Average \(SMA\) and Holt&\#x27;s methods were more accurate than the Naïve method for every model, with Holt&\#x27;s method being the most accurate for model XC\-2 and the Simple Moving Average being the most accurate for models XC\-1 and XC\-1M; however, the differences in accuracy among the smoothing methods were not statistically significant \(χ2 = 1\.911, p = \.591\)\. The most accurate forecasting method differed according to each model&\#x27;s demand pattern, leading to the proposal of a decision framework \(the CASE model\) that enables distributors to select a forecasting method systematically, whereby simple forecasting methods proved robust and adequate for supply planning\.
