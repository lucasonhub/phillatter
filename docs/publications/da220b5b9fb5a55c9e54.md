# Mishel theory\-based perioperative nursing after trabeculectomy for primary glaucoma: effects on illness uncertainty, self\-management, and quality of life

**Autores:** Qi\-Ying Ding; Chun Li; Xue\-Man Yin

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective To evaluate the effects of a perioperative nursing intervention based on Mishel’s Uncertainty in Illness Theory in patients with primary glaucoma undergoing trabeculectomy\. Methods This single\-center, two\-arm, parallel\-group randomized controlled study enrolled 198 patients undergoing first trabeculectomy at The First Affiliated Hospital of Suzhou University between February 2024 and December 2025\. Participants were randomly allocated 1:1 to routine perioperative ophthalmic nursing or routine care plus a structured Mishel theory\-based nursing intervention\. Assessments were conducted 1 day before surgery \(T0\), at discharge \(T1\), and at 1 month \(T2\) and 3 months \(T3\) after surgery\. The primary outcome was illness uncertainty measured using the Mishel Uncertainty in Illness Scale\. Secondary outcomes included psychological status, discharge readiness, self\-management, medication adherence, sleep quality, vision\-related quality of life, satisfaction with nursing care, postoperative ophthalmic outcomes, and complications\. All randomized participants completed follow\-up and were analyzed according to their assigned groups\. Results After Holm adjustment, the intervention group had lower illness\-uncertainty scores at T1, T2, and T3; lower anxiety scores at T1 and T3 and lower depression scores at T2 and T3; higher discharge\-readiness and satisfaction\-with\-nursing\-care at T1; higher self\-management and medication\-adherence scores and lower sleep\-quality scores at T2 and T3; and lower GQL\-15 scores at T3\. At T3, the model\-estimated between\-group differences were \-8\.13 points for illness uncertainty \(95% CI, \-10\.74 to \-5\.53\), 5\.64 points for self\-management \(95% CI, 4\.08\-7\.19\), 6\.05 points for medication adherence \(95% CI, 2\.98\-9\.12\), and \-3\.71 points for GQL\-15 \(95% CI, \-5\.41 to \-2\.00\)\. At discharge, the between\-group difference in discharge readiness was 1\.23 points \(95% CI, 0\.99\-1\.47\)\. No statistically significant between\-group differences were detected in postoperative intraocular\-pressure trajectories, visual\-acuity recovery, medication\-free status, postoperative procedures, or complications within 3 months\. Conclusion In this single\-center randomized study, Mishel theory\-based perioperative nursing was associated with greater improvements in illness uncertainty and several psychological, behavioral, and recovery\-related patient\-reported outcomes after trabeculectomy\. No statistically significant differences were detected in short\-term objective ophthalmic outcomes or postoperative complications\.
