# Comparison of Smartphone Images to Perform Telediagnosis of Occlusal Caries Lesions

**Autores:** Ali R\. Suleyman; Gül Yıldız Telatar

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: To investigate the efficacy of direct clinical assessment and digital photographs obtained with smartphones, intraoral cameras, and macro cameras in the diagnosis of occlusal caries ex vivo and in vivo according to the International Caries Detection and Assessment System \(ICDAS\) II criteria\. Methods: Ex vivo \(n = 46\) and in vivo \(n = 268\) permanent molars were evaluated both visually and using bite\-wing radiographs by 2 dentists according to the ICDAS II criteria, and the consensus was accepted as the gold standard\. The occlusal surfaces were photographed by a different clinician using 4 different camera types \(Samsung Galaxy A71 phone, Samsung Galaxy A70 phone, Super Cam CF689 intraoral camera, and Canon EOS 400D macro camera\)\. Two examiners evaluated the photographs on the computer screen\. Sensitivity, specificity, and interexaminer agreement were calculated\. Results: Inter\-examiner agreement was assessed using weighted Cohen’s kappa statistics ex vivo 0\.63 to 0\.79; in vivo 0\.70 to 0\.87\. Regarding agreement with the reference standard, the highest weighted kappa value in the ex vivo assessment was obtained with the Samsung Galaxy A70 \(0\.72\), whereas in the in vivo assessment, the Samsung Galaxy A71 showed the highest weighted kappa value \(0\.63\)\. The intraoral camera demonstrated lower agreement than the other devices, while the macro camera showed agreement values comparable to those obtained with the smartphones\. Diagnostic accuracy was higher for initial and moderate caries lesions compared to severe caries across all imaging modalities Conclusion: The Samsung Galaxy A70 and A71 smartphones demonstrated agreement levels comparable to those of the macro camera and generally higher than those of the intraoral camera for the detection of occlusal caries\. Overall, photographic assessment showed higher accuracy for the diagnosis of initial and moderate occlusal caries lesions than for severe lesions according to the ICDAS II criteria\.
