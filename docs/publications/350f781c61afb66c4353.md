# Cross\-omics mapping of the human HIV restrictome across clinical states, immune\-cell compartments, and protein responses

**Autores:** Reuben S\. Maghembe

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Host restriction factors can block HIV at multiple stages, but they are often studied as individual genes or within a single molecular layer\. I performed an evidence\-structured computational reanalysis of three independent public datasets to determine how the human HIV restrictome varies across clinical states, immune\-cell compartments, and protein responses\. The analysis used bulk monocyte RNA sequencing \(GSE157198\), PBMC single\-cell/CITE\-seq data \(GSE256089\), and primary CD4 T\-cell proteomics \(PXD012263\) within a prespecified restrictome\-immunometabolism framework\. In GSE157198, ART\-naive monocytes had higher core\-restrictome activity than healthy controls \(difference = 1\.154, p = 0\.000746\), and core scores were strongly correlated with interferon/innate\-sensing scores \(Spearman r = 0\.800, p = 7\.47 × 10^\-11\)\. Core\-lipid/cholesterol correlation was weaker and was not independent of interferon after conditional analysis\. In GSE256089, participant\-paired core\-restrictome scores were enriched in NK cells relative to B cells, monocytes, and T cells, and matched random\-gene\-set analyses supported enrichment beyond background expression structure\. FCGR3A monocytes also had higher participant\-paired core scores than CD14 monocytes\. Exploratory sensing/death\-gene analysis identified cell\-type\-specific expression patterns but did not establish pathway activation, pyroptosis, or apoptosis\. In PXD012263, the historical q &lt; 0\.05 proteomic selection contained 19 curated genes and retained mixed response polarity; sensitivity thresholds q &lt; 0\.01 and q &lt; 0\.10 selected 10 and 25 genes, respectively\. Together, the results show that HIV restrictome activity varies across clinical and immune\-cell contexts and is embedded within broader interferon\-linked and immunometabolic states\. Because the datasets are independent and not subject matched, the findings are observational and hypothesis\-generating rather than causal\.
