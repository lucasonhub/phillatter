# Corris, Amanda: Cognition: Embodied, Embedded, Extended, Enactive, and Affective

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-19 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

In Introducing Philosophy of Mind, Today\. Routledge\. 2027Where should we look to find the mind? A common response might be to look to the inner\-workings of the brain\. After all, cognition happens in the brain, right? Not entirely, say the proponents of “4EA cognition”, a collection of views that expand the limits of the mind beyond the brain\. 4EA cognition emphasizes several unconventional aspects of mentality\. The mind is extended beyond the boundaries of the brain, out into the world to include things like notebooks and calculators\. The mind is embodied, part of a living body that moves, perceives, acts, and interacts\. The mind is embedded in an environment, an environment it engages with in its everyday activity\. The mind is enactive, bringing forth—or enacting—a world rich with significance and meaning\. Some philosophers have recently argued for a fifth letter: this time, an A\. They say the mind is also affective; it is shaped by our emotional, felt states of being\. Taken together, we have 4EA cognition\. While there are discrepancies between specific views within 4EA cognition, what unites them is an emphasis on how richly interconnected the mind is with the body and the rest of the world\. The aspects of cognition represented by each of the letters \(the 4 Es, and the A\) situate the mind in the world\. What this means is that the mind is not merely ensconced within the brain, and the brain is not the only thing that matters for how we understand mentality\. So, we cannot look only to what is going on inside of our heads to make sense of our mental lives\. We must also look to our bodies, our environments, and even other people\.
