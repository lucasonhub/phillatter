# Association of Structured Self\-Talk Intervention Assignment with Short\-Term Tuberculosis Medication Adherence: A Sequential Quantitative Study

**Autores:** Tiwik Eriskawati; Chatarina Umbul Wahyuni; Joko Prasetyo; Agustin Widyowati

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Tuberculosis \(TB\) treatment requires sustained medication use, but adherence can be influenced by cognitive, psychosocial, and healthcare\-related factors\. Evidence on structured self\-talk for supporting TB medication adherence remains limited\. This study examined factors associated with adherence and whether assignment to the structured self\-talk intervention was associated with short\-term improvement in self\-reported adherence\. Methods: A sequential quantitative design was used\. Phase I included 117 patients with pulmonary TB in a cross\-sectional study assessing TB knowledge, access to health facilities, self\-efficacy, and family support\. Phase II used a quasi\-experimental pretest–posttest control\-group design with 68 patients\. Participants were allocated according to clinic attendance schedules\. The structured self\-talk intervention involved twice\-weekly practice for 4 weeks\. Phase I data were analysed using Spearman&\#x27;s rank correlation and binary logistic regression\. Between\-group changes in the study\-specific modified 11\-item medication adherence scores in Phase II were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test; ANCOVA was used for the adjusted analysis\. Results: In Phase I, TB knowledge, access to health facilities, self\-efficacy, and family support were positively associated with medication adherence in bivariate analyses \(all p &lt; 0\.001\)\. After adjustment, only self\-efficacy \(OR = 2\.34; 95% CI: 1\.41–3\.89\) and family support \(OR = 3\.97; 95% CI: 2\.16–7\.31\) remained significantly associated with good adherence\. In Phase II, the primary non\-parametric between\-group analysis showed a Hodges–Lehmann location shift of 3\.00 points \(95% CI: 2\.00–4\.00; p &lt; 0\.001\)\. ANCOVA yielded an adjusted between\-group coefficient of 3\.08 points for the post\-intervention study\-specific modified 11\-item medication adherence score \(95% CI: 1\.85–4\.31; p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Conclusion: Cognitive, psychosocial, and contextual factors were associated with TB medication adherence\. Assignment to the structured self\-talk intervention was associated with greater short\-term improvement in self\-reported adherence\. However, interpretation should consider the non\-randomised design, self\-reported outcome measurement, and short follow\-up period\. Randomised studies with longer follow\-up and objective adherence measures are warranted\.
