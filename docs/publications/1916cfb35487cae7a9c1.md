# Fuzzy\-Based Cross\-Cultural Validation of a Serious Game Model: 34 Nations

**Autores:** Mei Kurniawan; M Suyanto; Ema Utami; Kusrini Kusrini

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose: This study examines whether the DEGREE \(Digital Educational Game Review and Evaluation Engine\) model demonstrates stable conceptual suitability and global applicability across diverse cultural contexts\. It responds to the need for scalable validation methods for Serious Games evaluation models beyond local psychometric testing\. Methodology: A cross\-sectional single\-click expert\-validation design was combined with fuzzy set theory\. A total of 385 screened expert responses from 34 countries were analysed\. Likert\-scale judgments were converted into triangular fuzzy numbers and defuzzified using the centroid method to derive regional and global conceptual\-suitability scores\. Findings: The model achieved a high global Fuzzy Mean of 0\.84\. Regional results showed Very High suitability in Asia\-Pacific \(0\.91\) and High suitability in Europe \(0\.82\), North America \(0\.79\), and South America and Others \(0\.75\), indicating broad agreement with moderate regional variation\. Originality: The study advances prior DEGREE research by shifting from local instrument validation to global expert\-based conceptual validation\. It introduces a scalable, language\-independent validation paradigm that combines minimal\-interaction expert judgment with fuzzy modelling\. Impact: The findings support the use of DEGREE as a globally adaptable conceptual framework for Serious Games evaluation and provide a rapid validation strategy that can inform intelligent educational technologies, adaptive learning systems, and future user\-based empirical studies\.
