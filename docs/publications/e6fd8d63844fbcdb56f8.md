# Identification of Landslide\-Prone Areas and Mitigation Measures along the Proposed Chaumala–Garbha Road, Kailali, Nepal

**Autores:** Nabin Adhikari; Khet Raj Dahal; Sagar Sedhain

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study identifies landslide\-prone sections and appropriate mitigation measures along the proposed Chaumala–Garbha Road, a 22\.317 km alignment \(Chainage 0\+000–22\+317\) in Kailali and Doti districts, Sudurpashchim Province, Nepal\. Primary data \(field observation, GPS survey, landslide inventory and photographic documentation\) and secondary data \(DEM, geology, land use/land cover and rainfall\) were compiled at 500 m chainage intervals \(46 stations\) within a 1 km road\-corridor buffer\. A GIS\-based Analytical Hierarchy Process \(AHP\) integrating eight conditioning factors – slope, geology, rainfall, land use/land cover, drainage, road proximity, aspect and elevation – was used to derive factor weights \(slope 31\.2%, geology 20\.1%, rainfall 14\.6%, land use/land cover and road proximity 10\.4% each, drainage 6\.8%, elevation 3\.9%, aspect 2\.6%\) with a consistency ratio of 1\.3%, well within Saaty&\#x27;s acceptable limit\. The resulting Landslide Susceptibility Index classified 11\.9 km \(53\.6%\) of the corridor as Moderate, 9\.7 km \(43\.4%\) as High and 0\.7 km \(3\.0%\) as Low susceptibility, with no, very low or very high sections\. The longest continuous high\-risk stretches occur at chainage 4\+550–10\+700, 11\+450–13\+400 and 17\+250–18\+600\. Receiver Operating Characteristic \(ROC\) validation returned an Area Under the Curve of 0\.81, indicating good predictive accuracy\. Field investigation attributed recurring failures mainly to inadequate drainage, haphazard road excavation, over\-steepened cut slopes \(70–80° against the 34–45° recommended for sandy soil\), absence of stabilization structures and deforestation, corroborated by a focus group discussion and an in\-depth interview with local stakeholders\. Structural measures \(retaining walls, breast walls, gabion walls and rock bolting\) are recommended for high\-susceptibility sections, and non\-structural measures \(surface drains, slope trimming and bioengineering\) for moderate\- and low\-susceptibility sections\. The study demonstrates that a GIS\-based AHP framework provides a validated, corridor\-level basis for prioritizing landslide mitigation investment on rural mountain roads\.
