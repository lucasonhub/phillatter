# Crime, Conflict, and Community Security in the Hill Tracts of Bangladesh: An Empirical Investigation

**Autores:** Mohammad Qasim Khan; Marufa Islam

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examined the intersection of crime, conflict, and community security in Bangladesh’s Chittagong Hill Tracts \(CHT\)\. The signing of the Chittagong Hill Tracts Peace Accord in 1997 did not solve the insecurity problems caused by crime, land disputes, ethnic tensions, political rivalry, and poor confidence in security institutions in the region\. The study focuses on Rangamati, Khagrachhari and Bandarban districts and evaluates the effect of these elements on the safety perception of the people\. This study used a quantitative cross\-sectional design with primary data collected from 400 respondents using a structured questionnaire\. Sample size was calculated by Cochran’s formula and participants were picked by multistage sampling procedure\. The questionnaire covered criminal victimization, fear of crime, exposure to war, land insecurity, security institutions’ trust and perceived community security\. Data were examined with descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, multiple regression, independent\-sample t\-tests and one\-way ANOVA in SPSS\. The study hypothesizes that criminal victimization, conflict exposure and land insecurity diminish perceived community security but trust in police, local administration and community\-based institutions increase perceived community security\. The study offers quantitative evidence to the literature on peacebuilding, public safety, local governance, and conflict prevention in the CHT by comparing the three hill districts\. The findings are meant to help policy makers and local stakeholders in building more inclusive and context sensitive methods to enhance community security\.
