# Dentofacial and Maxillofacial Aspects of Obstructive Sleep Apnea: A Review

**Autores:** Satoru Tsuiki

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This review article outlines the pathogenesis of obstructive sleep apnea \(OSA\) from dentofacial and maxillofacial perspectives to increase the understanding of the link between its pathogenesis and treatment\.Current reports suggest that both anatomic and nonanatomic factors are involved in the pathogenesis and development of OSA\.Among these endotypes, dentofacial and craniofacial anatomic impairments in OSA are visible, indicating that dentists can detect high risk of OSA by understanding the background dentofacial characteristics of OSA as follows\.Individuals with excessive soft tissue inside the oral cavity \( large tongue\) do not necessarily develop OSA if the jaw size is large relative to the amount of soft tissue\.Conversely, a patient is highly likely to exhibit OSA when the jaw size is not sufficiently large relative to the tongue size\.Accordingly, anatomical balance, expressed as soft\-tissue volume relative to dentofacial size \(the degree of &quot;oropharyngeal crowding&quot;\), determines upper airway obstruction during sleep and the development of OSA: the anatomical balance theory\.Oral appliances for the treatment of snoring and OSA are intended to protrude and stabilize the mandible to maintain a patent airway during sleep\.Based on the concept of anatomical balance, oral appliances can be interpreted to normalize oropharyngeal crowding by mandibular advancement as mandibular advancement reduces the relative amount of soft tissue to the jaw\.In this review, the anatomical balance theory is highlighted to account for the underlying mechanisms of both oropharyngeal crowding and oral appliances for the treatment of OSA\.
