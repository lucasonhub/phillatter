# A data\-driven online fault detection framework for industrial batch fermentation

**Autores:** Oliver Pennington; Alexander W\. Rogers; Adam Wilson; Carolina Cruz; Dongda Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Industrial batch fermentation processes are susceptible to deviations that can compromise productivity and product quality, motivating reliable and early fault detection\. This study presents a systematic framework for evaluating and selecting statistical machine learning techniques for online monitoring of batch fermentations\. Principal Component Analysis \(PCA\), Dynamic PCA \(DPCA\), moving\-window Functional PCA \(MWFPCA\), and autoencoders are compared to assess the benefits of incorporating time\-derivative feature representations, historical measurements, and continuous trajectory representations, while also evaluating robustness to realistic measurement noise\. When applied to an industrial bioethanol fermentation process, incorporating historical measurements through lagged observations substantially improves the earliness and reliability of fault detection, with orthonormal polynomial MWFPCA providing the strongest overall performance\. MWFPCA identifies faulty batches more than 40% earlier than PCA and more than 60% earlier than directly observing online concentration measurements, while improving the distinction between nominal and faulty batches by more than six\-fold\. Finally, tuning the number of lags included in each monitoring observation reveals that faulty batches can be detected a further 12% earlier for MWFPCA relative to the initially selected configuration\. Overall, this study provides a real\-world demonstration for selecting, developing, and optimising data\-driven techniques for industrial bioprocesses monitoring and fault detection, facilitating early fault detection for improved bioprocess productivity\.
