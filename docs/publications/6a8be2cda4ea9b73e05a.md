# Nonlinear vegetation dynamics driven by socio\-ecological coupling in the alpine canyon region of northwestern Yunnan, China

**Autores:** Tingyu Xiao; Yuewei Ma; Yanmei Chen; Yali Gui; Changyan Zhou; Xiaoli Yi

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Northwestern Yunnan, located in the core of the Hengduan Mountains, is both part of a global biodiversity hotspot and a vulnerable socio\-ecological system\. In this region, complex topography and human activities jointly shape vegetation change, which exhibits pronounced nonlinear dynamics\. Using long\-term remote\-sensing data from 2000 to 2023, this study integrates the BFAST01 model with the optimal parameters\-based geographical detector \(OPGD\) to characterise the coupled gradual\-abrupt dynamics of fractional vegetation cover \(FVC\) and identify their associated driving mechanisms\. The results show that: \(1\) regional vegetation conditions have generally improved but remain strongly constrained by vertical zonation; the 1500–3500 m belt functions as the core ecologically active zone, whereas areas above 4500 m exhibit an abrupt decline in FVC under extreme environmental stress\. \(2\) Nonlinear analysis reveals an apparent vegetation recovery accompanied by substantial instability: \~80% of areas identified as significantly increasing display highly unstable growth trajectories, corresponding to a potential degradation\-risk estimate of 73\.3%\. \(3\) Temperature and net primary productivity \(NPP\) form the fundamental natural baseline regulating vegetation dynamics, while the explanatory power of human activities—proxied by night\-time light intensity—increased by 87\.9% over the past two decades\. Through nonlinear interactions with this baseline, human activities appear to exert a disproportionately strong restructuring influence on local ecological patterns\. These findings reveal a mismatch between vegetation evolution and environmental pressures in alpine valley systems and provide cross\-disciplinary evidence supporting differentiated governance strategies that reconcile energy development with long\-term ecosystem resilience\.
