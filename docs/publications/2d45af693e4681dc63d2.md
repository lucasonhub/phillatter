# Rethinking cultural awareness in think\-aloud research

**Autores:** Jingjing Hu; Xuesong Andy Gao

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Think\-aloud protocols have long been valued as a window into cognitive processes\. However, insufficient attention has been paid to how culture shapes what participants articulate, how they do so, and what remains unspoken during think\-aloud tasks\. Purpose This commentary re\-examines think\-aloud methodology on the basis of established theoretical, philosophical, and micro\-linguistic foundations\. Building on what has been achieved in this collection of studies, it aims to bolster cultural awareness in think\-aloud research by proposing a reflective framework that foregrounds culture as integral to cognition rather than viewing it as methodological noise\. Results The developed reflective framework consists of five core dimensions of cultural awareness: \(1\) participant and researcher positionality; \(2\) the situatedness of tasks and materials; \(3\) cultural considerations in protocols and instructions; \(4\) cultural sensitivity in data analysis; and \(5\) the generalizability of research claims\. Drawing on the studies in this special issue, each of these dimensions is detailed with an array of concrete, actionable aspects that operationalize cultural awareness across study design, data collection, analysis, and conclusion framing\. The empirical practices featured throughout this special issue illuminate transformative horizons for future research, offering operational guidance to help researchers move beyond assumptions of procedural neutrality and treat verbal reports as culturally situated performances\. Conclusions Future think\-aloud research should critically interrogate its methodological assumptions, foreground cultural dimensions throughout the analysis process, and ensure that theoretical claims are ecologically grounded in the data from which they are derived\.
