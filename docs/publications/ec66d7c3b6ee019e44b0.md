# Machine learning frameworks for quantitative proteomics: applications to Parkinson&\#x27;s disease biomarker discovery

**Autores:** Duc Tung Vu

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Large\-scale proteomics has become indispensable for advancing our understanding of complex diseases, and this involves integrating highly complex computational algorithms with translational biomarker discovery\. This thesis advances the field by developing novel quantitative and statistical methods for mass spectrometric data analysis while applying them to uncover molecular mechanisms in Parkinson&\#x27;s disease \(PD\), with a focus on LRRK2\-driven pathology and sporadic disease variants\. The first part of this work aims at improving the quantitative capabilities of MS\-based proteomics\. I developed QuantSelect, a deep learning\-driven framework for fragment ion selection that substantially improves quantitative accuracy and precision\. QuantSelect uses a self\-supervised approach with a novel weighted variance loss function, to learn a representation based on quality features that surpass conventional filtering methods\. It demonstrates superior performance as exemplified by using a single\-cell proteomics dataset and differential expression analysis\. I also contributed to MultiCova, a statistical framework for differential expression analysis that accounts for multiple covariates in clinical datasets while also providing interpretable effect sizes\. This replaces conventional fold changes and permutation\-based false discovery rate estimation\. Together, these computational advances enable more robust and reliable quantification in large\-scale clinical proteomics studies\. The second part of my thesis applies high\-throughput proteomics to elucidate molecular mechanisms in Parkinson&\#x27;s disease using clinically accessible biofluids and cellular and in\-vivo models\. In my main project, I investigated urinary proteomes from over 1,200 individuals across three independent cohorts to identify robust signatures of LRRK2 kinase activity in PD\. Leveraging machine learning, I identified a 30\-biomarker panel that distinguishes LRRK2 G2019S carriers with exceptional performance \(ROC AUC up to 0\.96\) and, critically, stratifies patients by LRRK2\-like phenotypes independent of mutation status\. I validated these findings in a transgenic rat experiment that showed a conserved proteomic signature across species in pathogenic Lrrk2 mutations\. LRRK2 inhibition showed negative correlation with the human biomarker panel, establishing its potential as a pharmacodynamic marker\. We extended this work to LRRK2 variants associated with inflammatory disease\. Here I contributed proteomics analysis demonstrating that the Crohn&\#x27;s disease\-associated N2081D mutation activates LRRK2 through distinct mechanisms compared to the PD\-associated G2019S variant, resulting in differential patterns of Rab substrate phosphorylation\. Beyond LRRK2\-driven pathology, I revealed mechanisms in sporadic PD using patient\- derived cellular models\. Through multi\-omics integration of transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics in hiPSC\-derived neural cells, I helped demonstrate that sporadic PD exhibits hypometabolism centered on α\-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase complex dysfunction and sonic hedgehog pathway dysregulation\. Longitudinal analysis revealed that sporadic PD accelerates the aging process, with transcriptional changes preceding proteomic alterations and progressive hypermethylation indicating loss of cellular plasticity during disease development\. Finally, the thesis contains a number of ubiquitin – related studies, which was a secondary focus of my work\. Together, this thesis addresses challenges in computational method development and our mechanistic understanding of PD, advancing both the technical capabilities of quantitative proteomics and our biological knowledge of PD\. By combining machine learning\-based data analysis methods with biomarker discovery and mechanistic validation across species and model systems, this work provides invaluable insights into PD pathogenesis\.
