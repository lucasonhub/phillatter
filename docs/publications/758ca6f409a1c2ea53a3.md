# Fan, Yao: Large Language Models, Algorithmic Monoculture, and Group Decision Making: An Epistemic Braess Paradox

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper studies the social epistemic consequences of algorithmic monoculture of large language models \(LLMs\) in group decision making and introduces a new epistemic Braess paradox, where individual epistemic improvement leads to collective epistemic deterioration\. A simple binary framework is used to keep the analysis accessible\. Yet, the phenomenon is more general and not specific to the model of choice\. The analysis distinguishes two effects of LLM use that pull in opposite directions: a competence gain and an independence loss\. Their net effect depends on empirical factors including LLM reliability, the degree of monoculture among others\. Two representative remedies are considered\. At the individual level, agents can strategically report judgments based only on their independent evidence\. This can mitigate the Braess effect but places unrealistic epistemic assumptions and coordination burdens on agents\. At the group level, pivoting uses peer prediction to discount shared evidence\. The paper’s distinctive contribution is to identify a further source\-provenance problem: because LLM interactions appear private and personalized, they can obscure the extent to which agents’ evidence derives from overlapping sources and thereby engender an illusion of independence\. This problem limits pivoting and other remedies whose success depends on distinguishing shared sources of evidence from independent ones\.
