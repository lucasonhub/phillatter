# IMU\-Based Bilateral Kinematic Waveform Analysis Associated with Shoulder Pain History in Competitive Swimmers

**Autores:** Ben Gronbach; Kyle Capili; Fatemeh Khatami

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Shoulder injuries are one of the most common overuse problems in competitive swimming, yet objective, field\-deployable methods for quantifying pain\-related movement alterations remain limited\. This study investigates whether bilateral IMU sensors placed on the upper humerus can identify kinematic bilateral waveform similarities tied to a history of shoulder pain in swimmers\. In this study, thirty\-four NCAA Division 1 collegiate swimmers and water polo players completed a standardized swimming test and were classified into moderate/severe pain \(n = 22\) and none/mild pain \(n = 12\) groups based on self\-reported shoulder pain scores\. Kinematic features were extracted and evaluated using statistical tests and machine learning classification\. The none/mild pain group showed a higher peak left\-right shoulder cross\-correlation across all axes \(0\.85 vs\. 0\.78, p = 0\.002\)\. The best\-performing model Classification accuracy for distinguishing between moderate/severe pain and none/mild pain conditions achieved a balanced accuracy of 70\.6% \(24/34 participants\), with a balanced accuracy of 69\.7% \(95% CI \[52\.7–85\.2%\]\) and an ROC AUC of 0\.731 \(95% CI \[0\.557–0\.890\]\), suggesting that personalized machine learning approaches may be necessary to improve individual\-level classification performance\. Further investigation of intra\-subject associations between shoulder pain history and kinematic bilateral waveform similarities is warranted to better understand their relationship and determine potential causal mechanisms\.
