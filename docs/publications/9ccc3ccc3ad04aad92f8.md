# Strategic Organizational Adaptation and Performance of Audit Firms in Nairobi City County, Kenya\.

**Autores:** Erick Ombati Nyatangi; Susan Ingasia Naikuru; Peter Ngatia Maku

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Firm performance, reflected in profitability, sustainability, client retention, referrals and market position, is a critical measure of the competitiveness and survival of audit firms in Kenya\. Despite growing regulatory demands, rapid digitalization of audit work and rising client expectations for advisory services, about 75% of the 993 audit firms licensed by the Institute of Certified Public Accountants of Kenya \(ICPAK\) continue to operate under traditional compliance\-based models, and many small and mid\-tier firms face client churn and an erosion of market share\. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to establ ish the influence of strategic organizational adaptation on the performance of audit firms in Nairobi City County, Kenya\. It specifically examined the influence of regulatory compliance, technological integration, structural flexibility and client\-centric service on firm performance\. The study was anchored on Institutional Theory, the Technology Acceptance Model, Dynamic Capabilities Theory and Service Quality \(SERVQUAL\) Theory\. An explanatory cross\-sectional survey design was adopted, targeting 745 ICPAK\-licensed audit firms domiciled in Nairobi City County, from which a sampling frame of 186 eligible firms was identified\. A sample of 127 firms was determined using the Yamane formula and selected through disproportionate stratified random sampling\. Primary data were collected from firm owners and senior managers using a structured Likert\-scale questionnaire with open\-ended items, and were analysed using descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation and multiple regression analysis, with results presented in tables and discussed\. The findings revealed that the four adaptation dimensions jointly explained 79\.3% of the variation in firm performance \(F = 99\.843, p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Structural flexibility had the strongest positive and significant influence on performance, followed by regulatory compliance, while client\-centric service and technological integration had positive and significant but comparatively smaller influences\. The study, therefore, recommends that audit firms should institutionalize formal strategic planning and ambidextrous service portfolios, embed proactive compliance with ICPAK, UDIN, KRA eTIMS and IESBA requirements, adopt structured advisory and client feedback models, and invest in audit technologies supported by staff training, while ICPAK should establish subsidized digital infrastructure and continuing professional development programmes for small and mid\-tier firms\.
