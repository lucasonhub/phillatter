# For Patients With Sequelae From Bell&\#x27;s Palsy, Electrical Stimulation May Cause Increased Synkinesis When Aggressively Applied in the Acute Phase of Recovery

**Autores:** Soyeon Jung; Ji Hae Lee; Jun Sung Sur; Min Young Kwak; Jae‐Joong Kim

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OBJECTIVES: To investigate the phase\-specific impact of cumulative nonselective electrical stimulation \(ES\) on long\-term functional sequelae and subsequent botulinum toxin \(BoNT\) therapeutic durability in patients with severe Bell&\#x27;s palsy\. METHODS: In this retrospective cohort study, 317 patients with severe unilateral Bell&\#x27;s palsy \(predominantly ≥ 90% ENoG degeneration, all ≥ 80%\) who developed clinically confirmed facial synkinesis were analyzed\. Patients were stratified based on ES onset timing \(acute &lt; 1 month, subacute 1\-6 months, and chronic &gt; 6 months\) and cumulative duration\. Outcomes were assessed using the Sunnybrook Facial Grading System, Synkinesis Assessment Questionnaire \(SAQ\), and BoNT durability \(duration of effect\)\. Age\-adjusted analysis \(ANCOVA\) and machine\-learning\-based decision tree regression were employed to identify prognostic thresholds\. RESULTS: ES exposure was significantly associated with increased synkinesis \(9\.32 vs\. 8\.27, p = 0\.007\) and reduced BoNT durability \(3\.15 vs\. 4\.51 months, p &lt; 0\.001\) compared to the non\-ES group\. A critical safety threshold for cumulative ES was identified at 4\.64 h, beyond which synkinesis scores deteriorated by 35\.7% and BoNT durability decreased by 23\.8%\. Notably, ES showed a strict phase\-dependent dichotomy: acute\-phase ES correlated with the highest synkinesis \(p &lt; 0\.001\), whereas chronic\-phase ES was associated with significantly lower synkinesis \(6\.11 ± 2\.05\) and superior functional recovery compared to both acute\-phase and non\-ES cohorts \(p = 0\.015\)\. CONCLUSIONS: The impact of ES on facial nerve recovery is highly phase\-specific\. While chronic\-phase application offers therapeutic potential, excessive acute\-phase ES correlates with exacerbated synkinesis and reduced BoNT durability\. Our data suggest a cumulative safety threshold of 4\.64 h, emphasizing the need for a judicious, time\-dependent approach to ES during the acute phase of nerve regeneration\.
