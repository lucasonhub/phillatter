# Modelo MNAR: Mediación Neurodidáctica del Saber Pedagógico en Actividades Rectoras de Transición Rural

**Autores:** Sully Johanna Calixto Pedraza

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In rural Colombian early childhood education there is a persistent gap between the pedagogical knowledge teachers build through practice and their access to neurodidactic principles that could guide the design of core activities: play, art, literature and exploration of the environment\. This article presents the MNAR Model \(Neurodidactic Mediation of pedagogical knowledge in core activities of rural kindergarten\), formulated through a qualitative study grounded in the socio\-critical paradigm and an action research design, conducted with kindergarten teachers from three rural schools in Oicatá, Soracá and Sotaquirá \(Boyacá, Colombia\)\. Data were produced through semi\-structured interviews and participant observation recorded in field journals during an eight\-meeting professional development process \(January–June 2025\), and were analysed through open and axial coding in Atlas\.ti and triangulation\. The diagnosis identified 79 codes grouped into seven subcategories: teachers displayed reflective pedagogical knowledge oriented towards innovation and socio\-emotional development, together with limited conceptual knowledge of neurodidactics and adverse conditions \(multigrade classrooms, lack of materials, weak family support\)\. During the intervention, changes were recorded in teachers&\#x27; discourse and planning: they moved from describing their practices as intuitive to grounding them, and they designed sequences and materials with neurodidactic intent\. On this basis, the MNAR Model was formulated, articulating four guidelines that correspond to the study categories and two emergent cross\-cutting guidelines: the pedagogy of love and teachers&\#x27; socio\-emotional competence\. The model is a situated conceptual proposal; its effect on classroom practice and children&\#x27;s learning was not evaluated and requires further research\.
