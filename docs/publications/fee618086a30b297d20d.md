# A Comparative Multivariable Study of Institutional and Workforce Factors Associated with Health Information Management Practices in South\-South Nigeria

**Autores:** Ebimene Japheth Teddy Kurokeyi; PhD Pro\. Sancho N\. Chukwu; Pro\. Anthony i\. Wegbom; Dr\. Oduak Peter

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Health Information Management \(HIM\) involves the systematic collection, organisation, analysis and use of health data for clinical care, administration, research and decision making\. However, little evidence has shown whether any difference between practices is mainly due to institutional context or workforce factors in Nigeria\. This research evaluated institutional and workforce factors associated with HIM practices in South\-South Nigeria applying a comparative multivariable analysis\. A cross\-sectional survey involving 124 HIM professionals was conducted at the University of Benin Teaching Hospital \(UBTH\), University of Calabar Teaching Hospital \(UCTH\) and University of Port Harcourt Teaching Hospital \(UPTH\)\. Data were obtained using a structured questionnaire on workforce characteristics and four domains of HIM practices; electronic data entry, disease coding and indexing, clinical data analysis and clinical research\. Descriptive statistics, chi\-square, analysis of variance effect size measures and Hierarchical multiple regression using HC3 robust standard error were used\. Age \(p=\.013\), work experience \(p&lt;\.001\), literacy level \(p=\.008\) differed significantly between institutions\. However, general HIM practices did not significantly differ by institutional setting \(F=\.354, p=\.702, η2=\.006\) and practice domains also varied insignificantly between institutions except one domain\. Institution explained 0\.6% of the variance in the observed practices\. Workforce factors contributed to an increase of 17\.6% explained variance to 60\.7%, and the full model explained 94\.4%\. Experience, literacy skills, professional title, and certification were still significantly and independently related to HIM practices\. The results revealed greater correlation between workforce characteristics and HIM practices rather than between institutional affiliation and HIM practices\. emphasis therefore should be placed upon competency\-based professional growth, a competent career march, and continual workforce competency evaluations\.
