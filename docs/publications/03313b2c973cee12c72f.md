# The Enlightenment Reborn

**Autores:** The Honorable Xiąże John McClellan Marshall; Roger F\. Malina

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In this article the authors recognize that the rapid adoption of artificial intelligence and virtual reality has created an epistemic crisis\. This requires a renewal of the Enlightenment commitment to empirical inquiry, critical reason and human responsibility\. An historical conceptual analysis, supported by illustrative cases from law and medicine, demonstrates how technological efficiency is increasingly confused with intelligence, judgment and wisdom\. In common parlance, the terms such as “artificial intelligence” and “virtual reality” obscure the dependence of these technologies on human\-designed mathematical systems, selected data, embedded biases, and potentially vulnerable infrastructures\. The central problem, however, lies not solely in the machine but in the man–machine interface, i\.e\. users may abdicate judgment without adequately examining the origins, limitations or accuracy, and possible hallucination, of the information produced\. While computational systems can search extensive bodies of data with remarkable speed, they lack the embodied human experience, empathy, moral intuition, and practical wisdom required for reliable consequential decisions\. Only a human can make the final decisions, and it is in the retention of the human factor in this man\-machine relationship that the Enlightenment concept of the application of human intellect to the analytical process comes to the fore\. The beneficial future of artificial intelligence will therefore depend upon preserving human critical agency and treating machines as instruments of inquiry rather than substitutes for judgment\.
