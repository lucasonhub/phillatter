# 2D Transition Metal Dichalcogenide \(TMD\) Memristive In\-Sensor Computing and Neuromorphic Vision Chips: Atomically Thin Photodetectors, Sub\-Nanosecond Optoelectronic Modulation, and Edge Visual Processing

**Autores:** Ayush Gupta, Harshit Jain, Dr\. Rajeev Kumar Verma, Dr\. Vishal Shrivastava, Dr\. Akhil Pandey,   Er\.Ram Babu Buri

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Autonomous vision systems, edge artificial intelligence, and real\-time robotics are fundamentally constrained by the energy\-prohibitive data transfer overhead between separated image sensors \(CMOS cameras\) and digital processing units \(GPUs, TPUs\)\. In\-Sensor Computing establishes a transformative paradigm shift that fuses optical sensing, non\-volatile memory, and neural computation directly into a single unified atomically thin hardware layer\. TwoDimensional Transition Metal Dichalcogenides \(2D TMDs, such as MoS2, WSe2, and MoSe2\) provide an exceptional material platform for optoelectronic in\-sensor computing, featuring direct bandgaps, high optical absorption coefficients, pronounced excitonic effects, and memristive conductance switching via trap\-assisted charge\-trapping\. By integrating 2D TMD photodetectors with memristive crossbar arrays, artificial vision chips can capture visual inputs, store synaptic weights, and execute parallel matrix\-vector multiplications for feature extraction and pattern recognition natively at the optical focal plane with sub\-nanosecond latency and ultra\-low power dissipation\. However, translating atomically thin TMD optoelectronic devices from laboratory flakes into wafer\-scale in\-sensor vision chips introduces profound physical and manufacturing bottlenecks: high contact resistance at metal\-TMD interfaces, spatial variations in defect density causing conductance drift, sub\-bandgap trap relaxation kinetics limiting frame rates, and large\-scale 2D material growth uniformity\. This paper delivers an exhaustive, mathematically formal technical investigation and empirical benchmark of 2D TMD memristive in\-sensor computing and neuromorphic vision chips\. We formalize optoelectronic photoconductive gain via trap\-assisted recombination models, derive crossbar in\-sensor dot\-product transfer functions, and model spatiotemporal spiking vision processing\. Furthermore, we construct an enterprise multi\-physics co\-simulation and physical wafer\-scale testbed evaluating 64x64 MoS2 optoelectronic memristive crossbar arrays across real\-time edge image classification \(MNIST, CIFAR\-10\) and optical pattern recognition\. Our empirical findings demonstrate that our 2D MoS2 in\-sensor vision chip achieves an energy efficiency of 342\.4 Tera\-Operations per Second per Watt \(TOPS/W\)—a 35x improvement over conventional CMOS camera\-GPU pipelines—while sustaining a sub\-nanosecond sensing\-computation latency and 97\.8% image classification accuracy\. Finally, we formulate open challenges in wafer\-scale 2D epitaxy, multispectral color filtering, and all\-optical reset mechanisms\.
