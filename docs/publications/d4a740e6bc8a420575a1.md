# &quot;Role of Digital Education Innovation in Building a Technologically Literate Society: A Conceptual Study with Reference to Generation Z Students&quot;

**Autores:** Prof\. Sangeeta

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In the twenty\-first century, digital technology brought about extensive changes in the nature, process, and objectives of education\. Moving beyond traditional teacher\-centered and classroom\-centered teaching, education is now advancing towards online learning, blended learning, smart classrooms, mobile learning, digital teaching resources, and artificial intelligence \(AI\)\-based teaching\. In this context, Generation Z students are particularly important because their social and educational environment has developed alongside digital technologies\. Technological media has made the teaching\-learning process more flexible, participatory, and student\-centered\. Generation Z is the generation that has grown up with digital technologies\. Thus, digital education innovation is not merely a means of modernizing the teaching process; it is also an important means of developing technological literacy among students\. The objective of this conceptual research paper is to clarify the conceptual relationship between digital education innovation and technological literacy with reference to Generation Z students\. In this study, digital education innovation is viewed as an educational process in which digital devices, online teaching, AI\-based technologies, digital resources, and innovative teaching methods are effectively used\. Technological literacy refers to students’ ability to understand, use, evaluate, and to employ technological devices and digital resources safely and responsibly\. This paper argues that effective digital education innovation can provide a strong foundation for students’ technological competence, digital problem\-solving ability, information evaluation ability, and digital citizenship\. At the same time, it is necessary to pay attention to challenges such as the digital divide, technological inequality, digital safety, teacher training, availability of technological resources, misinformation, privacy, cybersecurity, and uncontrolled use of artificial intelligence\.
