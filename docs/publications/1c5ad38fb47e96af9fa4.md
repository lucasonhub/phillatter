# Schooling Without Learning: Foundational Skills, Human Capital and Nigeria&\#x27;s Unfulfilled Demographic Dividend

**Autores:** Olufemi Jonathan Babalola

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Nigeria&\#x27;s youthful population offers substantial potential for economic development, but realizing a demographic dividend depends on the human capital and productive capabilities acquired by young people\. School participation alone cannot generate these capabilities when children progress through education without acquiring foundational literacy and numeracy\. This paper examines Nigeria&\#x27;s education challenge from a learning rather than schooling perspective and develops a Foundational Learning\-to\-Productivity framework linking foundational learning with subsequent skill accumulation, labour productivity, and structural transformation\. Drawing on existing research and national and international education evidence, the analysis highlights substantial inequalities in learning across geographical areas, household socioeconomic groups, and school contexts\. The paper argues that foundational learning should be treated as an upstream component of economic capability while recognizing that learning improvements require complementary productive opportunities and effective institutions\. It proposes a nationally consistent Foundational Learning Guarantee centred on common learning goals, structured pedagogy, continuous teacher support, targeted instruction, accelerated learning pathways, equity\-sensitive financing, and stronger education data systems\. The framework provides testable propositions for future empirical research and a policy basis for connecting foundational education reform with Nigeria&\#x27;s broader human\-capital and development strategy\.
