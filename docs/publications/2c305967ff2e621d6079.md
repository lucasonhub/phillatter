# Fractal\-Inspired Biorefineries for Sustainable Polymer Production: A Conceptual Framework Integrating Artificial Intelligence and Industry 5\.0

**Autores:** Young Chan Ko; Soon Wan Kweon; Yong Ju Lee; Byoung Geun Moon; Hyoung Jin Kim

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The transition from petroleum\-derived polymers to renewable materials requires biorefineries capable of managing the compositional variability and multiscale structure of lignocellulosic biomass\. This conceptual narrative review examines fractal\-inspired principles—including hierarchical organization, branching, modularity, and finite\-range statistical self\-similarity—as a design perspective for integrated biorefineries\. Deterministic mathematical fractals are distinguished from statistical fractals and hierarchical engineered systems, and methods for characterizing scale\-dependent surfaces, porous catalysts, and transport networks are reviewed\. The coordinated valorization of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin is then considered within an integrated biomass\-to\-chemicals framework for producing platform chemicals, functional materials, and polymer precursors\. Petroleum crude\-to\-chemicals processing, including the S\-OIL Shaheen Project, is discussed as a limited industrial benchmark for integrated fractionation, residue upgrading, and product diversification rather than as direct evidence of fractal engineering\. An integrated framework is proposed in which fractal\-inspired design provides the physical and organizational architecture, artificial intelligence supports characterization, prediction, optimization, and control, and Industry 5\.0 provides the objectives of sustainability, resilience, and human\-centered manufacturing\. The framework remains conceptual\. Its potential benefits must be evaluated through transport modeling, mass and energy balances, process simulation, experimental comparison, techno\-economic analysis, and life\-cycle assessment\.
