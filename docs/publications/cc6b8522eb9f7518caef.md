# Medication Adherence and Associated Factors Among Geriatric Population with Chronic Diseases in Rural Northern Kerala: A Cross\-sectional Study

**Autores:** Narayanan Namboothiri G; Arya Ravindran; Rinsha Rasheed V P; Rinshiya Fathima C; Rushdha Thanikatt; Safa K; Sahana Benzey; Saina N; Salma A N; Sameeha

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Medication adherence among elderly patients with chronic diseases is crucial for effective disease control but often remains inadequate\. Limited Indian data and under\-assessment justified studying its determinants and impact\. Objectives: This study aimed to assess medication adherence and associated factors among the geriatric population using the Morisky Medication Adherence Scale \(MMAS\-8\)\. Methodology: A community\-based cross\-sectional study was conducted among 292 individuals aged ≥60 years with chronic diseases in a rural field practice area of a tertiary care centre in Northern Kerala, in 2025\. Participants were selected using simple random sampling\. Data were collected through face\-to\-face interviews using a structured questionnaire, and adherence was assessed using the MMAS\-8 scale\. Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26, and chi\-square test was applied to identify associated factors\. Results: Medication adherence among geriatrics showed that 62\.3% had low adherence and 37\.7% had high adherence\. Higher adherence was significantly associated with younger elderly age, female gender, higher education, higher socio\-economic status, and living with family, as well as shorter duration of illness and having a single chronic condition\. Additionally, factors such as motivation to take medications, regular follow\-up, absence of cost\-related barriers, shorter waiting times, receipt of counseling, medication availability, and satisfaction with healthcare services were significantly associated with better adherence \(p&lt;0\.05\)\. Conclusion: Medication adherence among the geriatric population with chronic diseases was suboptimal and influenced by multiple socio\-demographic, clinical, behavioral, and healthcare system factors\. Improving adherence requires comprehensive, patient\-centered strategies focusing on education, accessibility, and strengthening healthcare support systems\.
