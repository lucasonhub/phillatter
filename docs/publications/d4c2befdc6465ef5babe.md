# Data\-Driven Spatial Analysis and Temporal Prediction of Piracy and Armed Robbery Against Ships

**Autores:** Onur Uca; Taha Talip Türkistanlı; Nur Jale Ece; Okan Demir; Güler Alkan; Ayşenur Karakas

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Piracy and armed robbery against ships remain critical threats to global supply chains, evolving in response to economic pressures and enforcement measures\.This study applies a multi\-method quantitative design, combining spatial density analysis, global Moran&\#x27;s I, bias\-corrected Cramér&\#x27;s V, and autoregressive integrated moving average \(ARIMA\) forecasting, using International Maritime Bureau data\.The analysis used International Maritime Bureau reports for 2015\-2025, while the annual forecasting series covered 1993\-2025\.Among 1,677 geocoded incidents with usable positions, 1,319 \(78\.7%\) occurred within 20 km of the physical shoreline, and global Moran&\#x27;s I indicated positive spatial autocorrelation \(I=0\.1226;permutation p=0\.001\)\.In the incident\-level records, Boarded incidents were the dominant incident category, and tankers were the most frequently recorded vessel category\.The hold\-out validation selected ARIMA\(0,2,3\) \(root mean square error=21\.18\),but its forecast of 103\.5 incidents for 2025 underestimated the realised total of 137 incidents by 24\.5%\.The 2026\-2030 forecasts have wide intervals and are interpreted as an exploratory baseline\.Overall, the findings show geographically concentrated reporting patterns and substantial uncertainty in medium\-term forecasts\.
