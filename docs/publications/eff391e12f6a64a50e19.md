# A longitudinal evaluation of a university\-wide faculty development grant program: pedagogical innovation, student priorities, and programmatic evolution

**Autores:** Saud Alkathiri; Ahmad Abdul\-Wahhab Shahba; Elham Alsadoon; Abeer A\. Alharbi; Reem Alrusaiyes; Ghaniah Ain; Khalid A\. Alnowibet

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Faculty development grant programs are strategic levers for improving teaching quality, curriculum design, and student learning, yet longitudinal evaluations remain scarce\. In this study, we assess King Saud University&\#x27;s Program for Excellence in Learning and Teaching \(PELT\)— an early university\-wide faculty teaching\-grant initiative —examining its implementation, faculty uptake, and programmatic evolution over a decade\. Methods Using a longitudinal, multi\-source program\-evaluation design, we analyzed eight PELT cycles through 157 completed grants, faculty \( n = 108\) and student \( n = 413\) surveys, and descriptive benchmarking against 11 international peers\. Survey data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, and selected faculty ratings were compared using Mann–Whitney U \-tests\. Results PELT evolved from a high\-volume inaugural phase \(71 grants\) to a more selective model \(6–11 grants annually\) with a 95\.7% completion rate\. Participation remained broadly balanced across Humanities \(34\.4%\), Science \(30\.6%\), and Health \(28\.0%\), supporting cross\-disciplinary engagement\. Teaching and assessment strategies \(51% of funded projects\) consistently dominated, with targeted emphases on job\-market skills and e\-learning in specific cycles\. Among student respondents, 71\.9% prioritized professional skills for employability, 52% favored problem\-based learning, and 57% preferred project\-based assessment over traditional exams\. Successful grantees reported high overall satisfaction with the program \(mean = 4\.5/5\), whereas non\-applicants highlighted awareness gaps \(54\.5%\) and workload constraints \(33\.3%\) as key barriers\. A statistically significant perceptual divide emerged between accepted and rejected applicants \( p ≤ 0\.001\), and international benchmarking positioned PELT in alignment with corresponding teaching\-grant schemes\. Conclusions PELT offers a replicable model for university teaching\-grant schemes that combines faculty development, student\-centered priorities, and longitudinal evolution\. The findings support strategies such as targeted outreach, formalized resubmission pathways, and tiered funding to advance pedagogical innovation and strengthen educational development\.
