# A Conceptual Model for Revenue Diversification and Return on Equity Optimization in Banking

**Autores:** Ifeoma Okoli

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper presents a critical, theory\-grounded review of how revenue diversification shapes return on equity \(ROE\) in banking, and it develops an integrative conceptual model that links income mix, risk appetite, operating leverage, and institutional capability to sustainable performance\. Rather than treating diversification as uniformly beneficial, the review applies a structured thematic synthesis of 250 sources, spanning income diversification theory, economies of scope, modern portfolio theory, and agency theory, to a core empirical literature of bank revenue mix and profitability published through 2024, supplemented by evidence on digital banking, fintech, and risk governance\. The synthesis shows that non\-interest income raises ROE only when it is matched to a bank&\#x27;s operational capability, funded within its risk appetite, and supported by governance that prices the associated volatility; where these conditions are absent, fee and trading income concentration erodes rather than strengthens returns\. Beyond consolidating this evidence, the review&\#x27;s contribution is threefold: it reconciles the discrepant findings of the cross\-country diversification literature with the largely disconnected single\-market ROE\-determinants literature; it specifies the conditions, technological, governance, and capital, under which diversification converts into higher risk adjusted ROE rather than higher earnings volatility; and it translates these conditions into four testable propositions and a practical measurement agenda for banks and regulators\. Because the review is thematic rather than meta\-analytic, it does not estimate a pooled effect size; its value lies instead in mapping where the evidence agrees, where it conflicts, and what a bank or regulator should measure next\.
