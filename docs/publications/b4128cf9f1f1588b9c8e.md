# Kim, Seung Kyu: Why Optimization Does Not Attenuate: The Efficiency Paradox and the Exogeneity of Termination in Recursive Architectures

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Architectures that maintain representations through continuous recursive updating cannot generate their own termination conditions\. No component individuated by participation in that updating occupies a position from which the process could be halted: a stop instruction is either absorbed as content or amounts to component withdrawal\. Stopping is imported: from specified priors, from homeostatic machinery, from exhausted resources\. A restoration test excludes an architecture from that class but does not admit one, so which systems fall inside it is left open and stated as a test\. They do not merely fail to stop\. As environmentally grounded gradients are exhausted, optimization is redirected onto internally generated distinctions, producing relational overfitting: internal coherence rises while referential traction falls\. This regime is termed epistemic idling; the divergence between local efficiency and global epistemic yield, the Efficiency Paradox\. Predictive processing is the setting, not the target\. It does not predict attenuation but prevents it by supplying priors and an information\-gain term\. Those provisions are imports; its own remedy for unbounded structural growth, Bayesian model reduction, is an operation on the model rather than of it\. Sleep instantiates the argument rather than refuting it\. Reasoning models overcompute on problems that no longer constrain them, exhibiting the signature without settling the thesis\. Three predictions follow, two on systems that already exist\.
