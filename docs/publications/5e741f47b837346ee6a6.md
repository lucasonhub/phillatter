# Kindness in veterinary practice: clinical behaviors that support safer care

**Autores:** Rustin M\. Moore; Catherine Haskins; Casey Etter; Julie Davidson; Julie A\. Legred

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Veterinary medicine is experiencing increasing clinical complexity, workforce strain, and challenges in clinician\-client\-team interactions that can impair cooperation, decision\-making, and care delivery\. Although kindness is often viewed as a personal attribute, evidence from neuroscience, behavioral science, human healthcare, and emerging veterinary literature suggests that specific kindness\-related clinical behaviors may influence how people experience and respond to veterinary encounters\. This narrative review examines how behaviors such as agenda\-setting, permission\-based pacing, low\-stress handling, nonjudgmental option framing, using the teach\-back method, following up, and performing short team debriefs are intended to reduce stress and defensiveness, strengthen trust, preserve engagement, and support decision\-making during difficult clinical interactions\. It also outlines practical ways these behaviors may be incorporated into veterinary clinical workflows and identifies priorities for veterinary\-specific research\. Although causal relationships in veterinary settings remain to be established, this review offers a practical way to examine how quality of interactions may influence safety, care delivery, and workforce sustainability\.
