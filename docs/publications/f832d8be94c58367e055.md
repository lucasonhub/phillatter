# Organizational capacity supporting STEAM implementation in early childhood education: a mixed\-methods study

**Autores:** Jovita Ponomariovienė; Daiva Žitkevičienė

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

STEAM education in early childhood settings requires not only teaching and learning resources but also organizational conditions that support their use in educational practice\. This study examined resource provision and the broader organizational context associated with STEAM implementation in Lithuanian early childhood education institutions using a sequential mixed\-methods design\. The quantitative component drew on national survey data from 831 institutions and examined teaching and learning resource provision\. The subsequent qualitative component involved open\-ended responses from 38 educational leaders from institutions included in the quantitative dataset and explored their perspectives on resources, professional learning, collaboration, leadership practices, and barriers associated with STEAM implementation\. The quantitative findings revealed variation in resource provision levels across curriculum areas and resource types\. Within “Exploring and Understanding the Environment,” several resources showed provision levels above 70%, whereas the provision level for specialized STEAM laboratory kits was 30\.01%\. Qualitative findings showed that material resources and digital technologies were the most frequently identified resource subcategory \( n = 27\), followed by teachers’ STEAM competencies \( n = 23\)\. Leaders also identified learning environments, professional learning, collaboration, leadership practices, partnerships, and organizational conditions\. Material barriers were prominent, particularly limited specialized STEAM resources and digital technologies \( n = 17\) and lack of dedicated STEAM learning spaces and laboratory equipment \( n = 15\), alongside pedagogical, organizational, and financial barriers\. Integration of the findings indicates that material provision is an important but not isolated dimension of STEAM implementation\. The qualitative findings situate national patterns of resource provision within a broader organizational context involving professional capacities, leadership practices, collaboration, learning environments, and institutional conditions\. A data\-informed conceptual framework synthesizes these dimensions without implying causal or hierarchical relationships\. The findings suggest that efforts to support STEAM implementation may benefit from addressing material provision and broader organizational conditions in combination\.
