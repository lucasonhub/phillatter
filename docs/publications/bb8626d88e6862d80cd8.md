# Central rapidity net baryon density and baryon number carriers in high\-energy collisions

**Autores:** Jiayu Chen

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This article focuses on the experimental progress of net baryon density measurements in the central rapidity region, spanning from the AGS and SPS to the RHIC and LHC energy regimes\. It comparatively analyzes the interpretation capabilities of three theoretical frameworks for net baryon density distributions across different energy regions: the valence quark transport model, the hadron cascade and hydrodynamics model, and the gluon junction model\. It explicitly points out that the magnitude of net baryon density in the central rapidity region is essentially determined by three interrelated factors: the center\-of\-mass energy of the collision system, the initial overlap degree of the colliding nuclei, and the transport properties of baryon number carriers\. In the low center\-of\-mass energy regime \( s N N &lt; 10 GeV\), the net baryon density in the central rapidity region remains at a relatively high level, where baryon number carriers are dominated by valence quarks, and the gluon junction effect plays a secondary role\. In the intermediate energy regime \( s N N = 10 ∼ 200 GeV\), the net baryon density in the central rapidity region exhibits an evolutionary trend of decreasing rapidly first and then flattening out as the collision energy increases, with the contribution of gluon junctions gradually becoming dominant and acting as the core carrier for baryon number transport\. In the high energy regime \( s N N &gt; 200 GeV\), the net baryon density in the central rapidity region stays at a low plateau level, and the baryon number carriers are dominated by sea quark pairs produced in initial collisions and partially thermalized residual structures of gluon junctions\.
