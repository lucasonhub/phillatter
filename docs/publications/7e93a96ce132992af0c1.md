# The digital and social affordances for knowledge co\-creation in online collaboration

**Autores:** Tuuli Toivikko

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

This dissertation investigates digital and social affordances for online collaboration, focusing specifically on how those working on knowledge co\-creation through digital technology perceive its action potential for solving complex issues\. The need to study topics related to knowledge co\-creation in digital collaborative settings arises from approaches to conducting collaborative efforts remotely, which has become common in recent years\. The theoretical frame used here builds on affordance theory and the concept of digital knowledge co\-creation\. The particular collaboration and knowledge co\-creation setting focused on in this study is that of large\-scale collaboration \(LSC\)\. The ability of LSC to address and solve complex problems has hitherto chiefly been explored from multiple perspectives at the individuals’ level of affordance and technology adoption\. However, our understanding of that which affords action for large groups in digital collaboration remains meagre, particularly in light of the continuously and rapidly changing effect of artificial intelligence \(AI\) on collaboration\. Hence, one of this dissertation’s core research objectives revolves around how AI‑enabled tools shape actions in LSC\. The affordance approach frames the methodology and choice of methods used here, which include the qualitative analytical methods of thematic analysis, the application of the Gioia method, literature review, and narrative analysis\. The data informing this dissertation consist of various types of data sets, from digital platforms’ traces to focus group interviews\. Four articles constitute the basis of this dissertation\. The first publication studies and presents online collaboration in large virtual expert groups, exemplified through the fast expert teams \(FET\) model\. The second publication discusses how AI affects trust in team\-level collaboration\. The third publication explores the adoption of technology by leaders in a fast\-organising knowledge co\-creation process taking place on a collaboration platform\. The fourth publication addresses the perceived action potential, i\.e\., affordances based on AI\-enabled tools, in a collaboration that employs the FET model, which presents a novel way of working that enables the combining of dispersed and diverse expert knowledge\. This dissertation’s findings show that studying technology‑mediated LSC through an affordance lens allows us to understand online collaborations’ practical preconditions, their digital environment, and what participants afford for it\. The perceptions of these preconditions direct digital knowledge co‑creation participants’ actions; and, alongside these perceptions and participants’ own potential for knowledge co\-creation, this dissertation investigates those digital affordances and asks how human–AI collaboration and team processes are shaped by individuals’ perceptions of AI’s trustworthiness\. In conclusion, this dissertation proffers a comprehensive view of those action potentials that originate in the digital collaboration tools used by participants in LSC\. The four publications contained here have been conducted by recognising which digital and social affordances enable action, how trust in human–AI interaction emerges through cross‑level dynamics, and how AI‑enabled tools support essential knowledge processes such as organising and synthesising information\. Understanding affordances, trust dynamics, and the AI\-enabled potential or hindrances that technology sets for online collaboration provides implications for practice, and these are presented here as a synthesis of the social and digital affordances shaping technology\-mediated knowledge processes\.
