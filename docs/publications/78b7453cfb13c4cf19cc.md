# A multi\-stage engineering framework for feasible scheduling of large\-scale energy batch transport systems

**Autores:** Renfu Tu; Qi Liao; Hao Zhang; Zhengbing Li; Rui Qiu; Guangtao Fu; Enrico Zio; Yongtu Liang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The energy transition is driving batch transport systems toward multi\-product, high\-frequency operations, with more batches and smaller batch volumes\. However, strong couplings among material flows, operational decisions and temporal constraints make large\-scale scheduling increasingly difficult, often rendering direct optimization computationally infeasible within practical engineering timeframes\. This challenge is particularly pronounced in large\-scale multi\-product pipeline systems\. To address this, this study proposes a multi\-stage engineering framework that enables feasible and scalable scheduling of large\-scale, batch\-centric transport systems\. The framework integrates two complementary mechanisms: a coarse\-to\-fine feasibility identification strategy that progressively narrows the admissible operational space, and a divide\-and\-conquer temporal decomposition strategy that partitions long scheduling horizons into tractable sub\-horizons\. The proposed framework is validated using two distinct industrial\-scale pipeline systems\. In a six\-product benchmark, the framework achieves zero or negligible optimality gaps\. For a complex branching network, it obtains feasible, high\-quality schedules within the engineering time limit, whereas direct optimization fails to achieve a gap below 3%\. Temporal decomposition reduces active binary variables and improves solution quality by over 50%\. The framework provides a generalizable approach for large\-scale batch transport and emerging multi\-carrier energy infrastructures\. A multi\-stage framework enables feasible, high\-quality scheduling of large\-scale energy transport systems that challenge conventional optimization, supporting more efficient operation of emerging multi\-carrier energy infrastructure\.
