# Transforming federal emergency management: a new agenda for Canada

**Autores:** Attila Julius Hertelendy; Alana Hicik; Gregory R\. Ciottone; Mazen El\-Baba

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Canada’s emergency governance model no longer aligns with a risk environment shaped by accelerating climate change and increasingly complex multi\-hazard events\. Recent federal/provincial/territorial strategies acknowledge these growing pressures but have not materially reoriented federal leadership toward anticipatory risk governance, resulting in persistent gaps across jurisdictions\. Using a conceptual analysis grounded in risk governance, resilience, and comparative federalism, this paper identifies four structural limitations that constrain national readiness: \(1\) fragmented situational awareness, \(2\) interoperability deficits, \(3\) a reactive federal posture, and \(4\) incomplete Indigenous integration\. We propose a reshaped federal leadership model that operates continuously across the disaster cycle\. The framework provides a concrete federal pathway linking governance theory to implementable design choices capable of strengthening national readiness and resilience in an era of compounding risk\.
