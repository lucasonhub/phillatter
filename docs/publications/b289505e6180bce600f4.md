# Thomas, Charles S\.: Welfare Without Persistence: Episodic Identity and AI Welfare Attribution

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

If an AI system were ever genuinely benefited or harmed, the benefit or harm would have to belong to someone\. The trouble is that &quot;the AI&quot; does not obviously pick out a single someone\. Keeling and Street have shown that a technological stack might contain candidate welfare subjects at the level of the model, the character, or the agent—possibly several at once\. But even once we grant that a higher\-level pattern \(like a character\) is real, we still face a further question: where, exactly, in the underlying computation does a particular welfare\-relevant state occur? The capacity for valence and the token state that instantiates it need not sit at the same grain\. I distinguish four: the pattern, the closure\-bearing system that sustains the capacity for welfare, the bounded computational episode in which a state actually arises, and the diachronic relation between episodes\. Welfare claims, I argue, are properly evaluated at the episodic grain\. Using a recast version of the Operational Definition of Episodic Identity \(ODEI\), we can warrant particular episodes as determinate objects of welfare attribution without first settling whether later episodes belong to the same numerical individual\. Local determinacy does not require personal persistence\. Nothing here establishes that present AI systems are conscious, valenced, or welfare subjects\. The point is structural: if the question ever arises, we need not solve the metaphysics of artificial personal identity before we can say what a welfare claim is about\. \(direct link \)
