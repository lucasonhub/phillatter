# The impact of microbiota and interferons on the regulation of dendritic cells across age

**Autores:** Ramin Shakiba

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Conventional dendritic cells \(cDC\) are key immune sentinels, orchestrating context specificimmune responses\. They exist as type 1 and type 2 cDC \(cDC1, cDC2\) with specialized roles in immunity\. Previous work in our lab identified differences in the capacity to support Th17 and Treg polarization between splenic cDC2 from neonatal and adult mice, which correlated with shifts in cytokine signaling across age\. However, it is unclear when cDC2 function transitions from a neonatal to an adult phenotype and how this process is regulated\. During the period spanning from birth to weaning the organism establishes life\-long immune homeostasis\. The transition to solid foods is accompanied by an exponential increase in intestinal microbial diversity\. Commensal derived signals also reach the spleen where they induce interferon \(IFN\) signaling, which promotes cDC function\. As we found functional differences of cDC2 in early life to disappear around weaning, we hypothesised that changes in microbial colonization at this age mediate this transition\. Longitudinal single\-cell RNA sequencing \(scRNA\-seq\) confirmed an enrichment of IFN stimulated genes \(ISG\) in cDC2 around weaning and quantitative real\-time PCR \(qRT\-PCR\) identified a corresponding increase of splenic IFNg expression with age\. However, functional analyses of cDC2 from germ\-free \(GF\) mice or mice with a DC\-specific loss of STAT1 \(CD11cCreStat1flox\) which mediates intracellular signaling downstream of IFN receptors found that neither IFN signaling nor microbial colonization are responsible for changes in support of Th17 polarization by cDC2 across age\. cDC1 are potent activators of CD8\+ T cells\. As with cDC2, longitudinal scRNA\-seq of splenic cDC1 revealed decreased ISG in 1\-week\-old mice, compared to mice at weaning age or older\. While microbial colonization did not appear to regulate cDC1 function, cDC\-specific loss of STAT1 \(Clec9aCreStat1flox\), but not type I IFN receptor, lead to reduced production of IL\-12p40 and CXCL9, which are cytokines driving CD8\+ T cell responses\. Concurrently, Clec9acreStat1flox mice exhibited a specific reduction of CD49dhi CD8\+ T cells with high cytotoxic capacity, which phenotypically resemble effector memory T cells\. scRNA\-seq revealed a loss of CXCL9\-expressing cDC1 with an activated phenotype in Clec9acreStat1flox mice compared to littermate controls\. In contrast, CCR7\+ cDC1 arising from homeostatic maturation were unaffected\. Cell\-cell interaction analysis confirmed impaired interaction between CXCL9 producing cDC1 and cytotoxic T cells via the CXCL9/CXCR3 axis\. Taken together our data indicate an increase of splenic IFNg signaling around weaning that induces a distinct activation state of cDC1 which drives the homeostasis of cytotoxic CD8\+ T cells\.
