# A versatile Bacillus esterase reveals a carboxylesterase blueprint for PET intermediate degradation

**Autores:** Hadjira Bounabi; Bilal Yahiaoui; Meriem Gasmi; Meroua Safa Mechouche; Nico Bäse; Marno Gurschke; Rebecka Molitor; Pablo Pérez\-García; Wolfgang R\. Streit

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract The biodegradation of polyethylene terephthalate \(PET\), a major commodity polymer, has become a core focus of multidisciplinary research\. To date, most of the 300 known PETases cluster into only a few evolutionary lineages\. While the well\-known Ideonella sakaiensis PETase \( Is PETase\) is highly specific for the polymer, and leaf\-compost cutinase \(LCC\) types sequentially cleave the polymer and its subsequent oligomers, most known PET\-active enzymes actually prefer monomeric intermediates; thus, they have not evolved as true PETases sensu strictu \. Here, we describe a novel PET\-active enzyme derived from Bacillus halotolerans \(BhEstB\)\. BhEstB is a highly promiscuous para \-nitrobenzyl ester hydrolase that is structurally related to BsEstB from B\. subtilis but divergent from typical PETases, as it lacks the characteristic Trp\-Met\-Tyr/Phe motif known to facilitate PET binding\. While its overall activity on the bulk polymer was only 21% of wild\-type LCC \(LCC wt \), BhEstB significantly outperformed the engineered variant LCC ICCG and the recently published archaeal feruloyl esterase PET46 in the turnover of bis\- and mono\(2\-hydroxyethyl\) terephthalate \(BHET and MHET\)\. BhEstB exhibits remarkably high activity toward BHET and MHET, degrading up to 50 mM of BHET and 38 mM of MHET at 30°C within 1 hour and 24 hours, respectively, with a K cat of 15\.5 \+/\-1\.25 s \-1 for BHET\. These results establish BhEstB as one of the most efficient BHET\-hydrolyzing enzymes characterized to date\. From a phylogenetic viewpoint, BhEstB serves as a blueprint for an enzyme transitioning from monomeric intermediate processing toward true polymer activity, offering high potential for synergistic formulation in thermophilic plastic upcycling\.
