# From downstream demand to upstream innovation: how a digital platform coordinates agri\-food value chain upgrading—the case of Taotian Digital Agriculture

**Autores:** Fan Cao

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Digital platforms are becoming increasingly involved in upstream agricultural production, yet existing research offers limited explanation of why consumer\-facing platforms move beyond transaction intermediation or how they coordinate innovation across fragmented agri\-food value chains\. This study addresses this question through an in\-depth case study of Taotian Group’s ready\-to\-eat kiwifruit project in China, drawing on interviews, field observations, internal corporate documents, government materials, and public sources\. The case evidence indicates that upstream involvement followed the platform’s recognition that unstable fruit quality, inadequate postharvest technologies, and weak market rewards for quality improvement were constraining downstream value creation\. Taotian Digital Agriculture responded by translating consumer demand into a clearly defined product target and coordinating the technological and organizational adjustments required across production, processing, storage, logistics, and sales\. Demand forecasting, product grading, and differentiated market channels further reduced uncertainty over innovation returns and reinforced upstream investment\. The study identifies a demand\-to\-innovation mechanism through which downstream market capabilities are converted into upstream innovation and coordination capabilities\. The case further indicates that the durability and inclusiveness of platform\-led upgrading may depend on stable economic returns, accessible local services, manageable compliance costs, risk\-sharing arrangements, and platform governance\. The study therefore clarifies how a consumer\-facing platform can link demand interpretation, upstream innovation coordination, and market\-value realization, while identifying economic and social conditions that warrant further empirical examination\.
