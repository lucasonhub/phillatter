# Neural Network\-Based Surrogate Modelling for Accelerated and Accurate Thermal History Prediction in Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing \(WAAM\) of Ti\-6Al\-4V alloy

**Autores:** Andi Ismail; Omar Fadili; Rijal Surya Rahmany

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Thermal history during wire arc additive manufacturing \(WAAM\) governs microstructural evolution and mechanical properties, but FEM simulations used to predict it are too computationally expensive for iterative process optimisation\. Prior surrogate models have also rarely reported which input features drive their predictions, leaving physical interpretability largely unaddressed\. We developed an interpretable feed\-forward neural network surrogate \(FFNN\) for fast thermal\-cycle prediction in WAAM of Ti\-6Al\-4V, built in PyTorch and trained on \~65 million spatiotemporal points from FEM simulation\. The model achieved a training R² of 0\.998 and RMSE of 8\.7 °C across \~25\-2000 °C\. On unseen current levels, it generalized well \(R² = 0\.9968–0\.9971, RMSE = 10\.9\-13\.4 °C\), with per\-current RMSE ranging from 7\.7 °C to 23\.9 °C across all four monitoring points\. Feature analysis, re\-verified on the full test set, identified cumulative deposition time, torch position, and arc source distance as the dominant predictors; current ranked lowest under both permutation and SHAP importance, though partial\-dependence analysis confirmed this reflects smaller effect size not physical unimportance with current exerting a real, monotonic influence that interacts with build height\. The FFNN\-surrogate cuts computation time substantially, offering a practical tool for rapid process analysis and optimisation in Ti\-6Al\-4V WAAM\.
