# Capacity strengthening for community health workers in trachoma prevention and referrals: a mixed\-methods study in Al Sabra district, Ibb governorate, Yemen

**Autores:** Leen Daoud; Magid Al Gunaid; Aïssatou Diawara; Mahanna Elsheik; Alison Krentel; Sami A Alhaidari

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Trachoma remains a preventable cause of blindness in fragile and conflict\-affected settings, where community\-level prevention is often weak\. This study examined short\-term changes in community health worker capacity for trachoma prevention and referral practices, and community\-reported behaviors following a capacity\-strengthening intervention in Al Sabra district, Ibb, Yemen\. A mixed\-methods pre–post implementation design was used around an intervention delivered from 7 May to 9 July 2025\. Quantitative data included CHW knowledge surveys and routine facility records of reported eye infections as a proxy indicator\. Qualitative data were collected through in\-depth interviews, community dialogues, and focus group discussions\. Fifteen CHWs participated alongside supervisors, health staff, and community members\. At endline, CHWs demonstrated high knowledge of trachoma transmission, prevention, and referral, alongside high self\-reported confidence in case identification, referral, and community education\. Because the baseline survey included CHWs and supervisors, whereas the endline survey included CHWs only, these findings were interpreted descriptively rather than as a direct paired comparison\. Qualitative findings suggested greater awareness, less reported reliance on traditional remedies, and wider use of facial cleanliness and hygiene practices, particularly among mothers and children, and described CHWs as trusted agents of change\. Routine facility data did not show a clear or sustained change in reported eye infection cases during the short follow\-up period, reflecting limitations related to proxy measurement and diagnostic capacity\. These exploratory findings suggest that targeted CHW capacity strengthening may support prevention behaviors and community engagement relevant to the facial cleanliness and environmental components of the SAFE strategy\. The results highlight the importance of investing in community\-based systems alongside biomedical interventions in fragile settings\.
