# Imatinib\-associated fluid retention with persistent NT\-proBNP elevation during treatment of gastrointestinal stromal tumor: a case report

**Autores:** Bozhidar Krastev; Vera Megdanova\-Chipeva; Assen Goudev

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background : Imatinib is an established first\-line treatment for unresectable or metastatic gastrointestinal stromal tumors and is generally regarded as having an acceptable cardiovascular safety profile\. Fluid retention, however, is a well\-recognized adverse effect, and uncommon cardiovascular complications have also been described during treatment\. Results : A 49\-year\-old woman with gastrointestinal stromal tumor of the small intestine started imatinib at a dose of 800 mg/day after histological and immunohistochemical confirmation of the diagnosis\. Two months after treatment initiation, she developed dyspnea, facial and lower\-limb edema, and elevated arterial blood pressure\. Echocardiography showed preserved left ventricular ejection fraction and preserved global longitudinal strain\. A small, hemodynamically insignificant pericardial effusion adjacent to the right atrium was detected during follow\-up after initiation of imatinib therapy\. NT\-proBNP levels became elevated during follow\-up and fluctuated over time, whereas troponin values remained low\. After treatment with torasemide and candesartan, the patient reported clinical improvement, and the pericardial effusion resolved, although NT\-proBNP levels continued to fluctuate over time\. Conclusion : The case points to a possible imatinib\-associated presentation in which fluid retention, elevated arterial blood pressure, NT\-proBNP elevation with subsequent fluctuations, and a small pericardial effusion occurred despite preserved systolic function\. It also underlines the value of early cardiological assessment, echocardiographic follow\-up with strain imaging, and cardiac biomarkers in patients who develop cardiovascular symptoms during imatinib therapy\.
