# Does ESG protect profitability in a crisis? Evidence from US industrial firms during COVID\-19

**Autores:** Zhongbin Tong; Norkhairul Hafiz Bajuri

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Using the COVID\-19 pandemic as a common macroeconomic shock to which firms were differentially exposed, we investigate whether pre\-crisis environmental, social, and governance \(ESG\) performance was associated with corporate financial resilience, measured through return on assets \(ROA\)\. Two competing directional hypotheses are evaluated: the insurance\-value hypothesis \(H1a\), under which accumulated stakeholder trust protects profitability in crises, and the short\-run cost hypothesis \(H1b\), under which relatively rigid ESG\-related commitments depress accounting returns when demand contracts\. Using a panel of 70 US industrial firms over 2012–2021 \(690 firm\-year observations\), a two\-way fixed\-effects \(TWFE\) difference\-in\-differences \(DiD\) framework compares changes in ROA across firms with stronger versus weaker pre\-crisis ESG performance\. The results are consistent with the short\-run cost hypothesis: a one\-standard\-deviation higher pre\-crisis ESG score is associated with an approximately 1\.06 percentage\-point larger relative decline in crisis\-period ROA \(continuous specification, p = 0\.006\), and high\-ESG firms experienced a 1\.97 percentage\-point relative decline compared to low\-ESG counterparts \(p = 0\.019; 95% confidence interval \(CI\) = −3\.60 to −0\.33; N = 70 firms\), approximately 24% of mean ROA\. Dynamic event\-study estimates show no differential pre\-trends and significant high\-ESG differentials in both 2020 and 2021; the penalty is unchanged by controls for research\-and\-development \(R&amp;D\) and advertising intensity, is imprecisely larger in the most pandemic\-exposed industries in a triple\-difference specification, and is attenuated within industry\-by\-year cells\. Pillar\-level estimates are precision\-limited nulls\. Where included, financial leverage is the strongest single predictor of crisis\-period profitability\. The findings are interpreted through a sustainability trade\-off mechanism in which environmental and social commitments behave like fixed capacity investments that build relational value over the long run but increase operating leverage in the short run; they identify a robust profitability pattern consistent with this mechanism, rather than a direct evaluation of it, and caution against treating ESG ratings as guarantees of short\-run accounting resilience\.
