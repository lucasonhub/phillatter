# Occupational Stress Among Healthcare Workers and the Feasibility of a Machine Learning\-Based Risk Screening Approach

**Autores:** Juan A Sanchez\-Martinez; Vision Bagonza; Anna Sanchez\-Martinez; Wei\-Cheng Hung; Agata Dąbrowska; Marek Dąbrowski; Ovinuchi Ejiohuo

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Occupational stress among healthcare workers is a major determinant of burnout, reduced quality of care, and adverse mental health outcomes\. In Poland, evidence remains fragmented across professions, limiting system\-wide understanding\. This study aimed to identify sociodemographic and occupational predictors of stress among healthcare workers and to evaluate the feasibility of a machine learning \(ML\)\-based proof\-of\-concept screening model for detecting high\-risk individuals\. Materials and methods: A cross\-sectional survey of healthcare professionals in Poland \(N = 178\) was conducted between November 2025 and January 2026 using the Perceived Stress Questionnaire \(PSQ\-30\)\. Participants were categorized into a high stress \(PSQ ≥ 0\.60\) and a low–moderate stress group\. Statistical analyses included reliability testing, non\-parametric comparisons, MANOVA, and multivariable logistic regression\. A Random Forest classifier with repeated cross\-validation was developed to predict high levels of stress using demographic, occupational, and psychosocial variables\. Results: The PSQ\-30 demonstrated excellent reliability \(Cronbach’s α ≈ 0\.96\)\. High stress prevalence was 14\.6%\. Night\-shift work emerged as the strongest predictor of high stress \(OR ≈ 3\.45–12\.4\), while greater work experience was associated with lower levels of stress\. The profession was not an independent predictor after adjustment, although nurses showed a higher proportion of stress\. Significant differences across professions were observed only for the Joy subscale \(p = 0\.026\), with paramedics reporting lower scores\. Department differences were not significant after accounting for sample size variability\. The ML model achieved an ROC AUC value of 0\.728, with 70% sensitivity and a high negative predictive value \(97%\), supporting its utility as a screening tool\. The key predictors included age, pediatric department, and workplace conflict\. Conclusions: Occupational stress in Polish healthcare workers is driven primarily by work patterns, particularly night shifts, rather than the profession alone\. ML\-based screening models seem promising for early identification of high\-risk individuals, enabling timely preventive interventions\.
