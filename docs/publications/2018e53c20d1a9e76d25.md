# Fujie, Naoto: What Can the Concept of “Concept” Lose and Still Remain a Concept? An Experimental Search for Minimal Non\-Redundant Constituents

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper asks a subtractive question: what can the concept of “concept” lose before concept\-like operation ceases to remain non\-redundant? Rather than defining concepts by a universal essence, six candidate constituents—configuration, temporal window, scale, ignition, retention, and re\-entry—are treated as experimentally removable search coordinates\. The framework permits a universal minimal set, multiple minimal realizations, or failure of “concept” to form a single operational kind\. A synthetic computational proof\-of\-protocol tests ablation, minimal\-set transition, retention, transformed reuse, dissolution, and re\-entry across controlled configurations\. The experiments shift the target from an invariant constituent inventory toward a configuration\- and criterion\-dependent transition structure\. Retention is not uniformly beneficial; replay is not equivalent to useful re\-entry; supplied transformation can improve cross\-interval reuse; and repeated attempts at autonomous transformation discovery and joint unit–transformation search fail to outperform fresh learning\. These negative results constrain rather than confirm the framework\. The paper therefore proposes not a new essence of concepts, but an experimentally vulnerable method for determining when distinctions, constituents, and even the concept of “concept” itself remain operationally non\-redundant\. \(direct link \)
