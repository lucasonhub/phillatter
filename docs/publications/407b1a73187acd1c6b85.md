# Dodd\-Frank After 2022: How Financial Regulation Adapted After the Banking Disruptions of 2023

**Autores:** W\. Terry Dancer

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The Dodd\-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act became law on July 21, 2010, following the 2007–2008 financial crisis\. It represented one of the largest expansions of federal financial regulation since the Great Depression and aimed to strengthen oversight and consumer protection\. Critics argued that Dodd\-Frank increased compliance costs and placed disproportionate burdens on community banks\. The Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act of 2018 made the largest legislative modification to Dodd\-Frank since its enactment\. Although it provided targeted regulatory relief, it preserved the core post\-crisis framework\. Previous research by the authors found that community bankers expected substantial operational benefits, but the actual effects were more limited\. This paper examines the evolution of Dodd\-Frank from 2022 to the present amid rising interest rates, inflation, and several regional bank failures\. The findings indicate that Dodd\-Frank has evolved rather than disappeared\. Regulatory requirements have become more tailored to institutional size, complexity, and risk, while its central objectives continue to shape U\.S\. financial regulation\.
