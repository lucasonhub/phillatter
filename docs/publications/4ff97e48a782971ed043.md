# Hard Way, The Only Way: Provable Merits of Training on Hardest Examples

**Autores:** Elvis Dohmatob

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Despite a surge of empirical interest in the \`\`unreasonable effectiveness&\#x27;&\#x27; of drastic data pruning for AI systems, a unified theoretical understanding of this phenomenon remains elusive\. In this work, we provide a precise high\-dimensional theory of learning under data pruning via weighted empirical risk minimization \(ERM\)\. As in previous work, we analyze a setting where pruning is performed along a one\-dimensional subspace of the input space\. Our analysis precisely characterizes the limiting test error curves in the regime where the input dimension N and sample size P tend to infinity with a fixed ratio N/P\. The resulting theory recovers several recent results in the literature as special cases and provides a general lens to evaluate how pruning strategies interact with the loss function and label noise\. Crucially, we characterize the optimal pruning strategy in the high\-sample regime where P greatly exceeds N\. We demonstrate that strategic pruning can accelerate the decay of classification test error from the classical sqrt\(N/P\) rate, typical of vanilla ERM with loss functions without exponential tail \(e\.g\., quadratic loss\), to the much faster Bayes\-optimal scaling of order N/P\. In this setup, we prove that, surprisingly, this optimal performance is achieved by a drastic pruning strategy: discarding almost all training data except for a small proportion, consisting strictly of the 2N hardest training examples\.
