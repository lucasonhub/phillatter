# Can AI\-enabled smart cities advance the constitutional right to life? A rights\-based index, governance\-opacity audit, and robustness analysis for sustainable urban India

**Autores:** Kavali Munisekhar; Bangalore Morarji

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial intelligence \(AI\) increasingly forms the operating logic of Indian urban governance, embedded in command\-and\-control centres, adaptive traffic systems, surveillance networks, and algorithmically mediated services\. Yet smart\-city scholarship evaluates such systems against managerial efficiency, leaving unexamined whether AI\-enabled governance advances or erodes the constitutional guarantees underpinning public authority\. This paper develops the Human\-Centred Constitutional Urban AI Governance Framework and operationalises it as the Constitutional Smart\-City Governance Index \(CSGI\), scoring urban AI against Article 21 of the Indian Constitution, human\-centred AI principles, and Sustainable Development Goals 11 and 16\. Six pillars constitutional rights, human\-centred AI, sustainable development, service delivery, participation, and accountability comprise thirty\-eight indicators with operational definitions, official secondary\-data sources, a transparent normalisation\-and\-weighting model, and a validation strategy\. The CSGI is applied to four leading smart cities Bengaluru, Pune, Surat, and Indore through a governance\-opacity audit, an index\-divergence analysis, and a Monte Carlo robustness test\. The cities are 86%–93% opaque; none publishes an algorithm register, data\-protection impact assessment, independent algorithmic audit, or municipal AI policy\. The CSGI correlates strongly negatively with the Ease of Living Index \(Spearman ρ = −0\.80\) yet positively with municipal\-performance and sanitation indices \( ρ = \+0\.80 to \+ 0\.95\), revealing a constitutional blind spot in liveability metrics steering national policy\. Across 20,000 sampled weight vectors, the lowest\-ranked city is weight\-invariant\. Because comparable cross\-city data remain sparse, the application is illustrative rather than validated itself a finding\. The paper contributes a transferable, reproducible instrument, repositions constitutional law as urban\-governance infrastructure, and offers tiered recommendations for national, state, and local authorities\.
