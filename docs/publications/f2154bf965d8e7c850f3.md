# C\-Reactive Protein\-to\-Albumin Ratio \(CAR\) as a Prognostic Biomarker in Newly Diagnosed Acute Myeloid Leukemia Treated with Intensive Induction Therapy: A Single\-Center Retrospective Cohort Study

**Autores:** Esen Savaş; Aytek Peker; Selin Küçükyurt; Lale Aydın Kaynar; Jale Yıldız; Hacer Berna Afacan Öztürk; Merih Kızıl Çakar; Murat Albayrak; Şahika Zeynep Akı; Ahmet Kürşad Güneş

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: Inflammation plays an important role in acute myeloid leukemia \(AML\) biology and may influence clinical outcomes\. The C\-reactive protein\-to\-albumin ratio \(CAR\) is a simple marker of systemic inflammation\. We evaluated the prognostic value of baseline CAR in patients with AML treated with intensive therapy\.Materials and Methods: We retrospectively analyzed 114 adults with newly diagnosed AML who were eligible for intensive induction therapy and received it \(7\+3 idarubicin and cytarabine regimen\) at Ankara Etlik City Hospital between November 2022 and May 2025\. CAR was calculated at diagnosis as CRP \(mg/L\) divided by serum albumin \(g/L\)\. Patients were stratified into low\- and high\-CAR groups according to the cohort median\. Overall survival \(OS\) and progression\-free survival \(PFS\) were estimated by the Kaplan–Meier method and compared with the log\-rank test\. Cox regression analysis was performed for OS\.Results: The median age was 49 years, and 62 patients \(54\.4%\) were male\. OS was significantly longer in the low\-CAR group than in the high\-CAR group, with estimated 2\-year OS rates of 78\.0% and 55\.6%, respectively \(p=0\.008\)\. Similarly, PFS was significantly longer in the low\-CAR group than in the high\-CAR group, with estimated 2\-year PFS rates of 65\.9% and 44\.1%, respectively \(p=0\.016\)\. In ELN intermediate\-risk patients, 2\-year OS was significantly higher in the low\-CAR group than in the high\-CAR group \(88\.2% vs\. 50\.0%; p=0\.021\)\. Among non\-transplanted patients, high CAR was associated with inferior OS \(p=0\.042\)\. In multivariable analysis of ELN non\-favorable\-risk patients, high CAR \[HR 4\.63 \(1\.61–13\.30\); p=0\.004\] and absence of allo\-HSCT \[HR 6\.39 \(2\.19–18\.67\); p=0\.001\] were independently associated with inferior OS\.Conclusion: In newly diagnosed AML patients, CAR is a simple and accessible biomarker that may help predict survival\. Although genetic risk stratification remains the cornerstone of prognostic assessment, CAR may provide additional prognostic information, particularly in patients with intermediate\-risk AML\.
