# Neural Wasserstein Splines: Representation\-Invariant Second\-Order Priors for Trajectory Inference

**Autores:** Amartya Banerjee; Harlin Lee; Caroline Moosmüller

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Population dynamics are often observed only as unpaired snapshots, which leave the evolution between measurements underdetermined\. Second\-order regularization can constrain it, but a penalty on the accelerations of sampled particles is a prior on joint particle paths, and different particle representations of the same marginal curve can have different acceleration costs\. We therefore introduce Neural Wasserstein Splines \(NWSP\), a sample\-based method that learns a continuous population curve under a Wasserstein smoothness prior\. A neural network maps latent variables to the control points of a cubic spline in time, so samples at any time come from the spline basis without numerical integration\. We project the generator&\#x27;s velocity onto gradients of a finite dictionary of scalar potentials and differentiate this measure\-dependent projection in time, obtaining a finite\-dimensional surrogate for the intrinsic Wasserstein acceleration, trained jointly with distributional fit losses\. For a fixed dictionary and ridge parameter, we prove that the population penalty depends only on the marginal curve and bound its sensitivity to moment\-estimation errors\. Learned potentials \(NWSP\-N\) sharpen acceleration estimates\. On various synthetic and single\-cell benchmarks, NWSP is highly competitive with transport and flow\-based methods, ranking in the top two in 11 of 16 settings\. The penalty improves on an unregularized spline in 13 of 16 settings and, while competitive with a particle\-acceleration penalty, adds provable representation invariance\.
