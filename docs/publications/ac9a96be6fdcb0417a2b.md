# Towards optimizing repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation protocols for chronic subjective tinnitus

**Autores:** Dibyendu Das; Koyel Das; Henry Benson Nongrum; Indranil Chatterjee; Dinesh Bhatia

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Chronic subjective tinnitus is associated with maladaptive activity across auditory and non\-auditory networks\. Therapeutic effects of repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation \(rTMS\) to modulate brain activity are uneven due to diversity in stimulation protocols, patient characteristics, outcome assessments, and control conditions\. Objective To systematically review rTMS clinical data for persistent subjective tinnitus and examine how stimulation frequency, cortical target, dose, localization, comparator design, and participant characteristics influence treatment response\. Methods PRISMA 2020 was used for the systematic review using PICOTS eligibility criteria\. We searched PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, the Cochrane Library, and Web of Science using tinnitus and rTMS\-related controlled vocabulary and free\-text keywords\. A multi\-author sequential selection process examined titles, abstracts, and full texts\. Participant data, trial design, stimulation target and parameters, localization, comparison, follow\-up, tinnitus outcomes, responder criteria, adverse events, and primary results were extracted\. Risk\-of\-Bias was assessed using validated design\-specific tools, including RoB 2 for randomized trials and ROBINS\-I for non\-randomized comparative studies\. Because of substantial clinical and procedural heterogeneity, findings were synthesized qualitatively rather than statistically pooled\. Results Twenty\-eight studies were included in the primary qualitative synthesis; low\-frequency 1\-Hz auditory or temporoparietal rTMS was most common\. Tinnitus burden decreased in small and early investigations, but several larger sham\-controlled trials did not demonstrate consistent superiority over sham\. High\-frequency temporal stimulation was associated with transient reductions in tinnitus perception rather than durable therapeutic improvement\. Combined auditory–prefrontal or multisite protocols showed more sustained improvement in selected studies, although findings were inconsistent\. THI, TQ, and perceptual ratings were analyzed separately because they represent distinct tinnitus outcome domains\. Stimulation dose, target location, sham credibility, follow\-up, hearing condition, tinnitus phenotype, and responder criteria varied greatly\. In safety trials, rTMS was generally feasible and well\-tolerated; however, transient worsening of tinnitus and treatment\-related withdrawal occurred in selected studies, and safety reporting was inconsistent\. Conclusion No specific rTMS protocol is currently supported for routine clinical treatment of chronic subjective tinnitus\. Combined auditory\-prefrontal and multisite procedures are helpful in certain trials, especially for reducing both perceptual and tinnitus\-related strain but have not consistently surpassed single\-site stimulation\.
