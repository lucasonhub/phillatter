# Orgasmic Meditation, Sleep, and Next\-Day Somatic Distress: A 30\-Day Observational Study

**Autores:** Caroline Griggs

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Orgasmic Meditation \(OM\) is a structured attention\-training practice conducted between two people who follow a predefined set of detailed instructions\.One person, the stroker, gently strokes the clitoris of the other person, the strokee, for 15 minutes while both place their attention on the point of contact and notice what they feel\.Climax is not the goal\.This study examined whether OM practice was associated with app\-assessed sleep and next\-morning Tumescence Survey responses\.Methods: In this 30\-day observational study, 41 experienced OM practitioners enrolled, and 36 contributed analyzable data, yielding 1,026 participant\-nights\.The Sleep Cycle Premium application assessed sleep quality, duration, regularity, and time to sleep onset\.Each morning, participants reported whether they had practiced OM the previous day and completed the 15\-item Tumescence Survey\.Linear mixed\-effects models accounted for repeated nights from the same participants and separated within\-person from between\-person associations\.Results: OM practice was not significantly associated with sleep quality \(b = \-1\.87index points; 95% CI: \-3\.96 to 0\.21; p = \.079\),sleep duration \(\-10\.11minutes; 95% CI: \-22\.37 to 2\.15; p = \.106\),time to sleep onset \(\+2\.01 minutes; p = \.225\),or sleep regularity \(\+0\.05; p = \.948\)\.On mornings after OM, participants reported less dissociation from their bodies and less agitation\.These two item\-level associations remained significant after correction for 15 comparisons\.Craving and irritability scores were slightly lower after OM, but the differences were not statistically significant\.Factor 2, which combined craving, physical discomfort, agitation, and dissociation, was lower after OM in the primary model \(b = \-0\.77;95% CI: \-1\.17 to \-0\.37; p &lt; \.001\)\.The estimate weakened when the OM association was allowed to vary among participants \(b = \-0\.67;95% CI: \-1\.36 to 0\.02; p = \.056\)\.Sleep did not mediate or modify this association\.With OM practice and sleep duration in the same model, a day of OM was associated with the same reduction in Factor 2 as 3\.8 additional hours of sleep \(95% CI: 1\.8 to 9\.5\), and with the same reduction in the total survey score as 1\.7 additional hours \(95% CI: 0\.2 to 3\.9\)\.Climax was reported on 19\.4% of OM days and was not associated with sleep outcomes within OM days\.Conclusions: OM practice was not associated with app\-assessed sleep in this sample\.On mornings after OM, participants reported less dissociation from their bodies and less agitation, and these differences were not explained by sleep duration\.The difference associated with an OM day was comparable in size to the difference associated with 3\.8 additional hours of sleep for Factor 2 and 1\.7 additional hours for the total survey score\.The estimates weakened when the OM association was allowed to vary among participants\.This comparison describes the relative size of observational associations and does not show that OM reduces sleep need\.The sample was small and highly selected, and neither the sleep application nor the Tumescence Survey has been validated for this use\.Controlled studies using validated measures are needed\.
