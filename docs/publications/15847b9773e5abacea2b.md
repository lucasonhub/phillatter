# A hybrid CNN–BiLSTM network with additive attention for automated four\-stage classification of Alzheimer&\#x27;s disease from brain MRI

**Autores:** T\. Deenadayalan; S\. P\. Shantharajah; Swarna Priya Ramu

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

AD is a progressive and irreversible neurodegenerative disorder\. Because the structural changes that accompany the earliest stages of the disease are subtle and easily overlooked, manual interpretation of MRI is time\-consuming and prone to inter\-observer disagreement\. This study presents an end\-to\-end deep learning framework that fuses spatial and sequential reasoning to classify brain MRI into four clinically meaningful stages: Person with no dementia, Person with very mild dementia, Person with mild dementia, and Person with moderate dementia\. An additive attention mechanism subsequently weights the most informative positions before a compact dense head produces the final decision\. Class imbalance is addressed by capped oversampling of real minority slices combined with residual cost\-sensitive weighting, applied to the training fold only; pixel\-space SMOTE is deliberately avoided because interpolating between two unregistered MRI slices produces images without an anatomical referent\. Exact\-duplicate screening is applied prior to partitioning, and a quantitative near\-duplicate audit is reported for the fixed folds\. Trained on 6,400 grayscale MRI slices under a stratified 70/15/15 protocol, the proposed CNN–BiLSTM–Attention model attains a test accuracy of 95\.94%, a balanced accuracy of 96\.08%, an MCC of 0\.9334, a Cohen&\#x27;s kappa of 0\.9325 and a macro\-averaged one\-vs\.\-rest AUC of 0\.9954, on a slice\-level held\-out test fold\. Per\-class analysis shows strong sensitivity and specificity for the three better\-represented stages; the extremely rare Person with moderate dementia class \(nine test images\) shows a favorable point estimate but is too small to support a claim of robust detection on its own\. Attention maps and Grad\-CAM overlays localize the evidence the network uses, and an error\-structure analysis shows that no misclassification spans more than two stages\. Because the corpus carries no subject identifiers, patient\-grouped partitioning is not possible and the reported accuracies should be interpreted as an upper bound attainable under a slice\-level split; this constraint is quantified explicitly and motivates the external\-validation study outlined in the manuscript\. Overall, the results highlight the methodological value of coupling convolutional feature extraction with bidirectional sequence modeling and additive attention under a leakage\-aware, imbalance\-aware evaluation protocol for multi\-stage AD staging\.
