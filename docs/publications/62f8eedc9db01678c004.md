# Machine Unlearning: Techniques, Challenges, Evaluation, Applications, and Future Directions

**Autores:** Akshat Kumawat; Balraj; Vishal Shrivastava; Akhil Pandey

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract—The widespread deployment of deep neural networks and foundation models has introduced acute legal, operational, and ethical challenges regarding data privacy and algorithmic accountability\. Regulatory mandates, notably the European Union General Data Protection Regulation \(GDPR Article 17 &quot;Right to be Forgotten&quot;\) and the California Consumer Privacy Act \(CCPA\), legally obligate data controllers to purge personal records upon individual request\. However, physical data deletion from storage disks fails to sanitize trained models: gradient optimization permanently embeds training data signatures into overparameterized weights\. While complete model retraining from scratch on retained data provides exact statistical revocation, its prohibitive computational, financial, and environmental costs render it intractable for modern architectures and streaming deletion requests\. To overcome this bottleneck, Machine Unlearning has emerged as a fundamental discipline in trustworthy artificial intelligence, aiming to excise the influence of designated training subsets without compromising general model utility on retained data\. This paper presents an academically rigorous, comprehensive survey of machine unlearning\. We mathematically formalize the unlearning problem under Empirical Risk Minimization \(ERM\), classify paradigms into exact, approximate, and certified regimes, and establish an exhaustive taxonomy spanning data\-partitioning \(SISA\), gradient inversion, influence functions, knowledge distillation, parameter\-saliency pruning, certified differential perturbations, federated unlearning, and autoregressive Large Language Models \(LLMs\)\. We examine multi\-dimensional evaluation suites—including membership inference attacks \(MIA\), loss distribution divergence, and relearning susceptibility protocols—while systematically contrasting unlearning with differential privacy and model editing\. Finally, we analyze critical vulnerabilities and outline thirteen structured future research directions for verifiable unlearning systems\.
