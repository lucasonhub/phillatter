# External validation of the D\-PACE score for predicting late high\-grade atrioventricular block after transcatheter aortic valve replacement in a Colombian cohort

**Autores:** Juan David Zuluaga\-Monroy; Hugo Oswaldo Alvarado Mora; Diana Fernanda Bohorquez Gonzalez; J\.J\. Sierra; Edgar Fernando Hurtado Ordoñez

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Transcatheter aortic valve replacement \(TAVR\) carries a clinically significant risk of late high\-grade atrioventricular block \(AVB\), yet no validated tool exists to predict this complication in Latin\-American populations\. The D\-PACE score was developed in European cohorts for this purpose\. Methods In a retrospective cohort of 175 consecutive TAVR procedures at a Colombian center \(2012–2025\), 141 patients met inclusion criteria for D\-PACE validation\. The primary outcome was late high\-grade AVB \(≥Mobitz type II\) occurring between 24 h and 30 days post\-TAVR\. Discrimination, logistic\-regression calibration, and decision curve analysis were performed\. Results Late AVB occurred in 31 patients \(22\.0%\), with 80\.6% of events within 72 h\. The D\-PACE score demonstrated good discrimination \(AUC 0\.82; 95% CI 0\.71–0\.92; sensitivity 77\.4%, specificity 86\.4% at a cutoff of 5\.5 points; NPV 93\.1%\)\. Late AVB rates rose progressively across strata: 5\.9% \(low\-risk, score 0–3\), 8\.8% \(intermediate, 4–5\), and 61\.5% \(high\-risk, ≥6\), with the high\-risk group concentrating 77\.4% of all events\. Decision curve analysis confirmed net clinical benefit across threshold probabilities from 8% to 60%\. All four low\-risk events occurred among valve\-in\-valve patients assigned a score of 0 per the original algorithm; excluding this subgroup \( N = 114\), the AUC rose to 0\.91 \(95% CI 0\.85–0\.96\) with zero low\-risk events\. In an exploratory analysis applying the full seven\-variable scoring uniformly, including valve\-in\-valve cases, the AUC was 0\.90 \(95% CI 0\.85–0\.95; NPV 96\.7%\), and 0\.91 \(95% CI 0\.78–1\.00\) within the valve\-in\-valve subgroup \( N = 27, 4 events\)\. Conclusions Applied as originally specified, the D\-PACE score showed good discrimination for late high\-grade AVB in this cohort \(AUC 0\.82; 95% CI 0\.71–0\.92\)\. The algorithm&\#x27;s automatic low\-risk classification of valve\-in\-valve patients was not supported \(14\.8% AVB rate\)\. Discrimination was higher both in the sensitivity analysis excluding valve\-in\-valve cases and in the exploratory analysis applying the seven\-variable scoring uniformly, indicating that the scoring variables transfer well while the valve\-in\-valve exemption warrants reconsideration\. These findings provide the first external validation of D\-PACE in Latin America\.
