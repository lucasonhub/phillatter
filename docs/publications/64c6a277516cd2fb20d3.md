# Dietary Nutrient Intake and Self\-Reported Dermatitis/Eczema in U\.S\. Adults: A Survey\-Weighted Cross\-Sectional Analysis of National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey \(NHANES\) 1999\-2004

**Autores:** Wed A Alawfi; Hadeel Aldhowayan; Rafif M Tahiri; Ghaday A Alrehaili; Hibah A Basehi; Nada A Algarni; Zainab W Kondarji; Raghad J Almuhaishi; Maryam J Alabdi

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Dermatitis and eczema are major chronic inflammatory skin conditions that may substantially affect quality of life\.Data regarding the association between dietary intake and dermatitis/eczema remain limited and inconsistent\.Thus, our aim is to characterize dietary differences between U\.S\. adults with and without self\-reported dermatitis/eczema\.Methods: This is a survey\-weighted cross\-sectional analysis of pooled National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey \(NHANES\) 1999\-2004 data among adults aged 20\-59 years with valid Day 1 24\-hour dietary recall data\.Nutrient intake was compared by dermatitis/eczema status, and survey\-weighted univariable and multivariable logistic regression models assessed associations between individual dietary nutrients and dermatitis/eczema\.We used a random forest classifier with temporal validation \(development on 1999\-2002 data, testing on 2003\-2004 data\) and SHapley Additive exPlanations \(SHAP\)\-based interpretation to evaluate the discriminative capacity of dietary variables\.Results: Among 8,702 participants, 1,076 \(12\.36%\) reported dermatitis/eczema within the previous 12 months\.Those affected tended to be older, more commonly non\-Hispanic White, more educated, and more likely to be obese or former smokers\.Overall dietary intake was similar across groups, with one exception: riboflavin was the only nutrient significantly linked to dermatitis/eczema, both in descriptive comparisons \(2\.29 ± 0\.05 mg vs\. 2\.19 ± 0\.03 mg, p=0\.049\) and in adjusted regression analysis \(adjusted odds ratio \(aOR\) 1\.075, 95% CI 1\.001\-1\.154,p=0\.046\)\.A random forest model based solely on dietary variables showed only modest predictive ability \(area under the receiver operating characteristic curve \(AUROC\) 0\.531\), performing worse than a model using clinical and demographic variables alone \(AUROC 0\.585\)\.Combining both dietary and clinical/ demographic data did not improve predictive performance\.Conclusion: Except for a slight, borderline correlation with riboflavin, dietary nutrient consumption in U\.S\. adults exhibited little independent connection with self\-reported dermatitis/eczema\.For differentiating illness status, sociodemographic and clinical factors performed better than nutritional variables, indicating that diet has a minor impact compared with other factors\.
