# Aoun, Patrick David: Phenomenological Absolutism, Mutual Exclusivity, and the Dissolution of the Free Will Problem

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

The free will problem has persisted for centuries because virtually all positions in the debate—hard determinism, libertarianism, and compatibilism—share a common and untenable presupposition: the existence of a persisting self that navigates a continuous causal timeline ontologically distinct from the physical world\. In this paper, we demonstrate that this presupposition dissolves once we follow the mandatory commitments of austere relativistic quantum field theory to their logical endpoint\. Building upon our framework of Mutual Exclusivity, with Phenomenological Absolutism as its core principle \(Aoun 2025, 2026a, 2026b\), we argue that the sole ontic reality consists of localized, mutually exclusive actualized field configurations—each constituting an absolute “is\-ness” that is simultaneously the complete phenomenal experience of that moment\. There is no persisting manifold, no trans\-realization causal substrate, and no ontological gap between “self” and “physics\.” Both determinism and the felt sense of freedom emerge as internal phenomenological configurations within the absolute is\-ness itself\. We introduce the emptiness of all phenomena \(their full appearance as experienced without intrinsic essence or independent existence\) and illustrate the point through a simple phenomenological example: an encounter in a dream that remains fully real as experience regardless of subsequent waking labels\. Because each is\-ness is exclusive and empty—even of itself—attention retains genuine configurational openness\. One can choose to interpret, attach to, or detach from any experience, including the interpretations “I am determined” or “I am free\.” This configurational openness is the lived phenomenology of agency and free will\. The resulting account preserves scientific realism, the predictive success of physics, moral responsibility \(reframed as present\-moment stewardship\), and the full texture of deliberative experience while eliminating the metaphysical burdens that have long plagued the debate\. The free will problem does not require solution; it requires recognition that its foundational arena was never ontologically real\. Freedom is the native, acknowledgeable openness of the absolute is\-ness—here, now, and always available for reconfiguration\.
