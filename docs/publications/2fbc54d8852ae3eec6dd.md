# Lysosomal exoglycosidases as biomarkers of systemic diseases – what’s new?

**Autores:** Iwona Ptaszyńska\-Sarosiek; Weronika Wiktoria Sarosiek; Gabriela Iga Sarosiek; Anna Charuta; Mateusz Maciejczyk

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Numerous studies suggest that lysosomal exoglycosidases can be utilised in the diagnosis and monitoring of various diseases\. This paper summarises recent reports on the clinical use of N\-acetyl\-β\-D\-hexosaminidase \(HEX\), β\-glucuronidase \(GLU\), β\-galactosidase \(GAL\), α\-mannosidase \(MAN\) and α\-L\-fucosidase \(FUC\) in systemic diseases\. The clinical relevance of exoglycosidases has been demonstrated in liver disease, alcohol use disorder \(AUD\) in adults, nicotine addiction, kidney disease, cardiovascular disease, diabetes, cancer, nasopharyngeal disease and musculoskeletal disorders\. Exoglycosidase activity correlates with disease severity and is characterised by high sensitivity, though low specificity\. However, lysosomal exoglycosidases may have a particular application in addiction medicine\. Compared to routine biomarkers, they are more sensitive and specific to alcohol\-induced damage\. Importantly, lysosomal exoglycosidases \(especially HEX and its isoenzymes\) show a greater correlation with acute ethanol exposure and are less dependent on individual factors \(age, gender, comorbidities\)\.
