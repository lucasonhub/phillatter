# Defect and stress\-aware operating window analysis of a leadfree Cs 2 CuBiBr 6 solar cell using SCAPS\-1D

**Autores:** Joshna Palepu; Bridjesh Pappula; Moyya Sundeep; Seshibe Stanford Makgato

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract This study uses SCAPS\-1D to quantify thickness selection and performance losses associated with interface defects, parasitic resistance, and temperature in a Ni/CuSCN/Cs2CuBiBr6/STO/FTO/Al solar cell\. The device was simulated under AM1\.5G illumination at 100 mW cm\-2 and 300 K\. Absorber thickness, CuSCN and STO thicknesses, absorber bulk defect density, coupled interface defect density, series resistance, shunt resistance, temperature, generation, recombination, and capacitance\-voltage responses were systematically evaluated\. The proposed solar cell delivered open circuit voltage of 0\.7169 V, short circuit current density of 35\.3576 mA cm\-2, fill factor of 77\.9267%, and power conversion efficiency \(PCE\) of 19\.7531%, with an optimum Cs2CuBiBr6 absorber thickness of approximately 0\.55 μm\. Increasing absorber bulk defect density from 1015 to 1018 cm\-3 reduced the simulated PCE from 19\.75% to 1\.24%, demonstrating strong sensitivity to absorber quality within the adopted model\. In contrast, coupled interface defects produced only a minor PCE reduction, indicating that the device is primarily limited by bulk recombination under the adopted parameter set\. Resistance and temperature analyses also demonstrated significant sensitivity to low shunt resistance and elevated temperatures, with PCE dropping to 6\.93% at 475 K\. The results of generation, recombination and capacitance\-voltage analyses were consistent with photogeneration centered within absorber and Shockley Read Hall recombination\. The results give a realistic design hierarchy of Cs2CuBiBr6 photovoltaics, prioritizing the suppression of absorber defects, control of leakage, low resistance contacts and thermal stability over secondary transport layer thickness refinement\.
