# Reliable Scale\-Free Bandits via Empirical Likelihood

**Autores:** Pratham Gupta; Alhad Sethi; Shubhada Agrawal

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

We study stochastic bandits without knowledge of reward scales or tail parameters\. Almost\-sure regret guarantees describe eventual learning but do not quantify expected performance or the risk of large regret in a finite run\. We introduce $\\texttt\{EL\-F\}$, an anytime empirical\-likelihood \(EL\) extension of the $g$\-Forcing policy of Cowan and Katehakis \(2020\), and characterize its performance at three levels\. First, for integrable rewards and non\-decreasing, diverging exploration schedules $g$ with vanishing increments, $\\texttt\{EL\-F\}$ preserves the $g$\-Forcing asymptotics for pseudo\-regret $R\(T\)$: $R\(T\)/g\(T\)\\to\\sum\_\{i:Δ\_i&gt;0\}Δ\_i$ almost surely as $T\\to\\infty$, where $Δ\_i$ is the suboptimality gap of arm $i$\. Second, on every sub\-exponential instance, for non\-decreasing $g\(t\)=ω\(\\log t\)$ satisfying a mild feasibility condition, expected pseudo\-regret is at most $g\(T\)\\sum\_\{i:Δ\_i&gt;0\}Δ\_i\+C\_\{\\underline\{P\}\}$, where $C\_\{\\underline\{P\}\}$ is an instance\-dependent constant independent of $T$\. Moreover, we also establish finite\-time tail bounds with super\-polynomial decay in regret beyond the forced\-exploration budget\. For slowly varying $g$, we determine the exact exponent of linear regret deviations on two\-armed Gaussian instances with common variance and establish a matching lower bound among policies satisfying the same expected\-regret budget on every such Gaussian instance\. These quantitative guarantees also cover the greedy variant obtained at zero EL radius\. A fixed positive radius introduces local optimism while preserving the leading asymptotic regret\. We establish coupled pathwise dominance over the greedy variant in two\-armed bandits with a deterministic suboptimal arm\. Experiments demonstrate reductions in prolonged suboptimal allocation compared with $g$\-Forcing on challenging instances\.
