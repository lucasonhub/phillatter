# MANIFESTO OF CONSCIOUSNESS: Where It Begins, How It Evolves, and What It May Become

**Autores:** Mykola Iabluchanskyi; Andriy Yabluchanskiy

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** book

## Resumo

abluchanskyi M\., Yabluchanskiy A\., MANIFESTO OF CONSCIOUSNESS: Where It Begins, How It Evolves, and What It May Become, 2026, 15,095 words\. Consciousness remains the biggest unresolved scientific problem of the twenty‑first century not because we lack data, but because we hide different phenomena under a single word\. This book offers an ontological definition of consciousness as the union of subjectivity \(“what it is like”\) and reflection \(self‑monitoring of one’s own states\), and systematically draws demarcation lines between consciousness, sentience, irritability, and complex cognitive behavior\. At the intersection of philosophy, neuroscience, and clinical medicine, the authors reconstruct the evolutionary threshold of consciousness, examine the octopus as a living demarcation line, reconsider the Cambridge, New York, and Vienna declarations, and show why embodiment is a necessary condition of any conscious experience\. A separate part of the book turns to future forms of consciousness—from embodied robots to hybrid bio‑silicon systems—and formulates the principles of graded moral status and the presumption of consciousness as the basis for a new ethics in a world where human consciousness is not a final authority but only one stage in an open evolutionary process\.
