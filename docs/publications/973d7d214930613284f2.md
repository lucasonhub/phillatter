# O\., T\.: Dissecting the Leiden Declaration: The Missing Bridge from Mathematical Validity to Human Authorship

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_\. 2026The Leiden Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics, published on 2 June 2026 and endorsed by the International Mathematical Union, responds to the challenges that artificial intelligence poses to mathematical research\. It grounds its recommendations in what it calls the characteristic values of mathematical research: the certainty and understanding conferred by proof, the attribution of results to specific authors, the transparency and independent verifiability of arguments, proper evaluation against shared standards, and the understanding and judgment of autonomously guided communities\. This paper neither rejects the Declaration nor evaluates the risks of artificial intelligence\. It examines the internal structure of the Declaration, and asks whether the epistemic values that concern mathematical knowledge are distinguished from the institutional values that concern mathematical practice\. We establish three claims of non\-entailment: the epistemic values do not entail human authorship, under any formalization that speaks only of results and their arguments; the requirement that a responsible subject exist does not entail that the generator of a result be human; and the path of legitimacy the Declaration describes, results attributed to named authors and published in peer\-reviewed venues, is neither a necessary nor a sufficient condition of validity\. Each claim of non\-entailment is established by an explicit model construction, and its finite instances are verified by exhaustive enumeration\. The central thesis is that the Declaration may legitimately defend both kinds of values, but it does not thereby establish that the latter are conditions of the former\. The Declaration’s reasonable protections — disclosure, responsibility, verification, vigilance against plausible but false arguments — are retained, and are shown to become stronger once their epistemic and institutional components are separated\. The dissection is itself conducted under the Declaration’s own epistemic values: every step rests on the published text of the Declaration, every inference is open to independent verification, the use of automated tools is disclosed, and responsibility for the content rests with the author\. The paper is therefore not an external critique of the Declaration but an application of its epistemic standards to the Declaration itself\. Its validity, if it has any, does not depend on the identity of its author\. \(direct link \)
