# Naoto, Fujie: How Far Can David Roden Remove the Human? Ignition Conditions for Unbounded Posthumanism, the Hyperagent, Xenopoetics, Necroconceptuality, and Weird Aesthetics

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper asks how far David Roden’s posthumanism can remove the human as a necessary measure of agency, cognition, normativity, and aesthetic production\. Beginning from Roden’s distinction between anthropologically bounded and anthropologically unbounded posthumanism, it reconstructs the latter not as a prediction about enhanced humans but as a refusal to impose a priori human constraints on the space of possible posthuman agents\. The paper then examines the ignition conditions under which agency, interpretation, norm\-sensitive behavior, and self\-transformation could become operationally non\-redundant without preserving human embodiment, phenomenology, intentional architecture, or inherited social forms\. Roden’s Hyperagent provides a further test case: a maximally mutable technological formation destabilizes assumptions that collective agency must remain organized around human\-scale subjects, fixed purposes, or stable material substrates\. Xenopoetics, necroconceptuality, and weird aesthetics are analyzed as attempts to register forms of conceptual and aesthetic production whose organization may no longer be recoverable through familiar human\-centered interpretive constraints\. The paper nevertheless distinguishes removal of anthropological invariants from successful demonstration of human\-independent operational continuity\. Its central question is therefore not simply whether the human can be conceptually deleted, but which operations remain identifiable, re\-ignitable, and consequential after human\-specific conditions are withdrawn\. The broader claim is that unbounded posthumanism becomes strongest where the limits of the human are experimentally or operationally revisable rather than merely negated\. \(direct link \)
