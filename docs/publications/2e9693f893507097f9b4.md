# Impact of Hybrid Work on Employee Well\-being and Organizational Commitment

**Autores:** Shweta Chauhan; Neeraj Kumar Singh; Sneha Gupta

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Hybrid work has emerged as a significant post\-pandemic work arrangement, reshaping employee experiences, organizationalpractices, and workplace relationships\. This study examines the impact of hybrid work on employee well\-being and organizational commitment through a systematic literature review of Scopus\-indexed studies\. The review focuses on three major dimensions: the influence of hybrid work on employee well\-being, its effect on affective, continuance, and normative commitment, and the role of organizational support, leadership communication, and technology\-enabled work practices in shaping employee outcomes\. Initially, 110 studies were identified from the Scopus database using keywords related to hybrid work, remote work, employee well\-being, organizational commitment, burnout, engagement, organizational support, leadership communication, and technology\-enabled work\. After screening the records on the basis of title, abstract, keywords, relevance, and bibliographic completeness, 44 studies were selected for detailed review and thematic synthesis\. The findings reveal that hybrid work enhances flexibility, autonomy, work\-life balance, and productivity; however, it may also contribute to burnout, digital fatigue, social isolation, boundary blurring, and weakened affective commitment if not supported by effective organizational systems\. The study highlights that successful hybrid work requires strong HR policies, supportive leadership, perceived organizational support, psychological safety, responsible technology use, and deliberate efforts to maintain organizational culture and employee belonging\.
