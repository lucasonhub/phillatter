# Feature\-level fusion of Sentinel\-1 SAR and Sentinel\-2 optical imagery for crop\-type mapping in humid tropical mountainous terrain

**Autores:** Indarto Indarto; Gunawan Gunawan; Mahrus Irsyam; Fahmi Arif Kurnanto

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate crop\-type mapping in humid tropical mountains is challenged by persistent cloud cover, rugged topography, and fragmented smallholder parcels\. This study evaluates a feature\-level fusion workflow that integrates Sentinel\-2 optical indices and Sentinel\-1 SAR backscatter for crop\-type mapping in Candipuro District, Lumajang, East Java, Indonesia\. Sentinel\-2 surface reflectance and Sentinel\-1 IW data acquired in June–July 2023 were harmonised in Google Earth Engine to a common 10 m pixel grid, so that all comparison maps were evaluated on the same spatial support\. The optical component comprised NDVI, NDBI, and NDWI, whereas the radar component used the VH/VV polarisation ratio\. A DEM was used only to characterise the mountainous setting and to assist terrain interpretation, and was not included as a predictor in the classifier\. The stacked feature image was classified using a Random Forest model trained with 101 field\-survey ground control points representing eight land\-cover/crop\-related classes\. Accuracy was assessed using 393 stratified random validation points\. The fused\-image classification achieved an overall accuracy of 93% and a kappa coefficient of 0\.90\. The fusion workflow yielded richer thematic differentiation than the optical\-only comparison scenarios, particularly for water bodies, sugarcane, and shrubland\. However, class\-level results reveal substantial uncertainty for corn fields and moderate confusion in built\-up and water classes\. Therefore, the present workflow should be interpreted as a site\-specific, single\-date proof of concept rather than a fully transferable operational solution\. The results indicate that Sentinel\-1/Sentinel\-2 feature fusion is promising for crop\-type mapping in cloud\-prone mountainous landscapes, while future work should incorporate multi\-temporal phenology, richer vegetation indices, SAR texture features, uncertainty mapping, and broader field sampling\.
