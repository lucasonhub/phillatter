# Shear behavior of cement mortar after exposure to elevated temperatures under different cooling conditions

**Autores:** Yanfu Zhao; Hongtu Xiu; Haoran Shi; Ding Ding; Song Yang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In this study, the effects of elevated temperature and cooling method on the direct shear behavior of cement mortar were experimentally investigated\. Mortar specimens were exposed to temperatures ranging from 25 °C to 500 °C and subsequently subjected to natural air cooling or water cooling\. Direct shear tests were performed under a constant normal stress of 1 MPa, while acoustic emission \(AE\), post\-failure morphology, and scanning electron microscopy \(SEM\) were used to characterize damage evolution\. The results show that the peak shear stress and shear modulus of air\-cooled specimens exhibit a non\-monotonic temperature dependence, with local maxima near 300 °C, whereas water cooling causes more pronounced mechanical deterioration\. Reduced pre\-peak AE activity and partial closure of pre\-existing interfacial microcracks observed at moderate temperatures suggest a temporary reduction in mechanically active defects, although the underlying physicochemical mechanism cannot be uniquely determined from the present evidence\. At higher temperatures, extensive thermal microcracking develops in the cement paste and paste–sand interfacial regions\. Water cooling further intensifies thermal damage because of the rapid temperature gradient and associated thermal stresses\. The final shear fracture propagates predominantly through the cement paste matrix and paste–sand interfaces rather than by extensive transgranular fracture of sand particles\.
