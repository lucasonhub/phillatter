# Biodiversity, trophic position, and nutritional ecology of euphausiid species in the Benguela upwelling system

**Autores:** Tine Jordan; Anna Schukat; Tim Dudeck; Sabrina Dorschner; Holger Auel

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Lantern shrimp \(krill, Euphausiacea\) play a key ecological role in the Benguela Current coastal upwelling system \(BUS\) off Namibia and South Africa due to their high biomass, their physiological plasticity, and their position as a link between primary production and higher trophic levels\. In total, 15 krill species were identified\. Euphausiid species composition was variable at spatial scales between nearshore and offshore areas and between the northern and southern Benguela upwelling subsystems \(nBUS and sBUS\)\. Euphausia lucens was the most dominant species in both subsystems in terms of biomass and distribution range\. In the nBUS, Hansarsia megalops and Euphausia hanseni were predominantly found\. Euphausia similis occurred in the sBUS\. Offshore stations were dominated in both subsystems by Euphausia recurva \. Biodiversity increased from coastal to oceanic waters\. Krill species in the BUS occupy multiple trophic levels because of species\-specific feeding behaviors that range from herbivory via omnivory to exclusive carnivory\. Stable isotope and fatty acid biomarker analysis revealed trophic positions \(TP\) between 2 and 3\.7 and carnivory indices \(CI\) between 1\.7 and 19\.5 for krill species in the BUS\. Furcilia larvae and small juveniles \(&lt;10 mm\) were predominantly herbivorous, specified by low TPs of 1\.9\-2\.2 and low mean CI of 2\.3\. E\. recurva and E\. lucens were predominantly herbivorous to partially omnivorous indicated by elevated proportions of the diatom marker fatty acid 18:1\(n\-7\) \(2\.7–4% of total fatty acids \(TFA\)\)\. A high proportion of the carnivory marker fatty acid 18:1\(n\-9\) supported an omnivorous feeding behavior with a tendency toward carnivory for E\. hanseni \(10\.4–12\.5% TFA\)\. CI and TP of H\. megalops and E\. similis suggested trophic plasticity and a tendency to a carnivorous life style \(mean CI of 3\.5/3\.2 and TP of 2\.1\-3\.4\)\. Species of the genus Stylocheiron were exclusively carnivorous \(TP of 2\.7\-3\.8 and mean CI of 15\.6\)\. Only H\. megalops had fatty alcohols indicating the production of wax esters as storage lipids\. This study shows that the ecological role of krill is more complex than just linking primary production to higher trophic levels\. For realistic food\-web models, the different functional groups within the krill taxon should be considered\.
