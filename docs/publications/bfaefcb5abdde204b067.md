# Wind energy in european balancing markets: policy challenges, market design, and a multi\-criteria feasibility index

**Autores:** Bassey Okon Samuel; María Paz COMECH; Julio J\. Melero

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The transformation of the European electricity market under the Clean Energy Package \(CEP\) has positioned wind power as a cornerstone of the EU’s renewable energy transition\. Balancing markets have consequently become strategically important, requiring wind power to assume a more active and technically capable role\. Despite ongoing reforms, technical, regulatory, and economic barriers still affect its effective participation\. This study reviews the European electricity market, focusing on the role of wind power in ancillary services, specifically frequency containment reserves \(FCR\), automatic frequency restoration reserves \(aFRR\), and manual frequency restoration reserves \(mFRR\), across the major synchronous areas\. Based on ENTSO\-E guidelines, national grid codes, and market frameworks, it identifies key requirements, enabling factors, and remaining barriers\. The paper introduces the Wind Participation Feasibility Index \(WPFI\), a composite metric that evaluates participation feasibility by integrating technical compatibility, regulatory accessibility, market design, operational burden, and economic viability into a normalized 0–100 score\. Results show clear regional differences\. Continental Europe reaches 76% for all three services; the Nordic region scores 76% for FCR and 72% for aFRR/mFRR; Great Britain reaches 64%; and Ireland and Northern Ireland score 56% for FCR/aFRR and 60% for mFRR\. Sensitivity analysis confirms the robustness of the regional ranking under alternative weighting schemes\. The WPFI provides a practical framework for benchmarking balancing markets and supporting their adaptation to increasing renewable penetration\.
