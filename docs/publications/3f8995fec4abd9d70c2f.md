# A hybrid FEM\-ML approach for predicting the thermal conductivity of UO₂\-Mo composite nuclear fuels

**Autores:** Zhu Wang; Yuxiang Zheng; Yule Yan; Quanming Bi; Jiaxi Leng; Rui Yuan; Yuru Ma; Yutong Li

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

ABSTRACT Predicting the effective thermal conductivity of particle reinforced composites remains computationally demanding when high fidelity finite element method \(FEM\) simulations are performed across large microstructural parameter spaces\. This study introduces a computationally efficient surrogate framework that integrates a pixel grid FEM meshing strategy with machine learning regression to accelerate thermal property evaluation\. Representative volume elements of UO 2 \-Mo composites are generated with systematically varied Mo volume fractions \(0 to 27\.5 percent\) and UO 2 microsphere radii \(10 to 200 μm\) and UO₂\-matrix burnup \(0 to 12\.5 at\.%\)\. A pixel based discretization is employed to mitigate mesh distortion at phase interfaces, reducing single case computation time by approximately 59 percent compared to conventional unstructured meshing\. Pearson correlation analysis and gradient boosting decision trees are sequentially applied to identify dominant microstructural descriptors, with Mo volume fraction emerging as the primary predictor\. Four regression models, namely least squares support vector machine \(LSSVM\), least squares boosting \(LSBoost\), random forest \(RF\) and eXtreme gradient boosting \(XGBoost\), are benchmarked, with LSBoost achieving a mean test R² of 0\.9138 against held\-out FEM labels and, against the independent experimental dataset of the literature, maximum absolute errors of 0\.51 W·m⁻¹·K⁻¹ for the Mo volume\-fraction series and 0\.72 W·m⁻¹·K⁻¹ for the UO 2 cell\-size series\. An inverse design routine is further established to retrieve feasible microstructural intervals for prescribed thermal conductivity targets\. The proposed workflow provides a robust numerical tool for microstructure property exploration in multiphase composites, demonstrated here on a UO 2 \-Mo cermet system\.
