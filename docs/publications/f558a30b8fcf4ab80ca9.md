# Fish, Nicholas: Φ as a System’s Capacity for Consciousness: An Illusionist Integrated Information Theory Approach

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-19 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Integrated Information Theory \(IIT\) attributes subjective experience to integrated information, which can be measured quantitatively as a value, Φ, and it has shown real empirical success through tools like the perturbational complexity index \(PCI\)\. However, IIT is not widely accepted, and I’d like to make the case that though Φ is heavily correlated with what we describe as consciousness, it actually measures an alternative state of our neural system, and that our assertions of consciousness being necessarily related to this correlation are false\. I take Illusionist Integrated Information Theory \(iIIT\), first introduced by Kelvin McQueen, as the groundwork for this claim\. Illusionism holds that phenomenal consciousness, as we conceive of it, is not at face value real\. McQueen argues that an illusionist framework makes IIT more coherent and less metaphysically demanding, in part because it avoids the logic gate objection\. From there, I argue that IIT has not always had a consistent definition of consciousness, most visibly in its treatment of dreamless sleep\. IIT’s axioms force us to “bite the bullet” and grant consciousness to a state with no phenomenal experience, no memory, and no unified self\. Rather than defend this, I propose that Φ is better understood as the ground on which consciousness is experienced\. In other words, Φ indicates a system’s capacity to produce the memory, conceptions of self, and higher\-order cognitive behaviors that give rise to our belief in consciousness, and not consciousness itself\. By reframing Φ this way, it loses some potency as the singular indication of consciousness, but it becomes a more tangible theory that can be explored with greater perspective\.
