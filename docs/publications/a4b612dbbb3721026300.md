# Zhang, Sulin: Beyond Propositional Truth: Toward a Theory of Lived Philosophy

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Philosophy is commonly represented through articulated arguments, explicit concepts, systematic theories, and standards of propositional justification\. Yet human beings also formulate, inherit, revise, and employ philosophically significant understandings within ordinary life that do not normally appear as formally articulated theories\. People develop principles concerning trust, dignity, dependence, maturity, reciprocity, responsibility, loss, intimacy,, and selfhood through accumulated experience and emotionally significant relationships\. Such understandings may be incomplete, context\-sensitive, internally heterogeneous, and only partially expressible in technical language, while nevertheless possessing conceptual organization and normative force in practical life\. This article develops a systematic theory of lived philosophy\. Lived philosophy is defined as an experientially organized, normatively oriented, relationally situated, and action\-guiding configuration of ordinary concepts, judgments, affective evaluations, memories, narratives, and practical commitments through which an agent interprets and navigates recurrent situations of life\. The theory distinguishes lived philosophy from both systematic philosophy and mere opinion, and argues that its structure is neither chaotic nor reducible to formal logical deduction\. Its characteristic architecture is experiential connectivity: concepts become connected through recurrent episodes, affective salience, narrative interpretation, social transmission, and practical consequences rather than primarily through explicit theoretical inference\. The article introduces several further distinctions: theoretical validity versus lived viability; global consistency versus local coherence; technical concepts versus experiential concepts; individual formation versus relational transmission; and propositional justification versus practical warrant\. Lived philosophy is therefore not conceived as a domain in which truth becomes irrelevant\. Rather, it instantiates a plural normative regime in which truth, coherence, authenticity, experiential adequacy, practical reliability, relational intelligibility, and revisability can all contribute to philosophical validity\. Drawing on Wittgensteinian accounts of forms of life and ordinary language, phenomenological analyses of the lifeworld, pragmatism, theories of tacit knowledge and habit, practice theory, hermeneutics, narrative identity, practical wisdom, and theories of social imaginaries, the article argues that lived philosophy constitutes a neglected but conceptually identifiable mode of philosophical cognition\. The proposed theory does not claim that every piece of life advice is philosophical, nor that lived beliefs are immune to criticism\. Its stronger claim is that philosophicality cannot be exhaustively identified with systematicity\. Philosophy may occur not only when experience is transformed into an explicit theory, but also when experience becomes sufficiently conceptually organized to orient a life\.
