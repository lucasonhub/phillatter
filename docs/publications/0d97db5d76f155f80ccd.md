# Physical Fitness and Psychological Stress Among University Students: A Cross\-Sectional Observational Study

**Autores:** P Reddy Haritha; K Madhavi

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Sedentary study routines and academic pressure may reduce physical fitness and increase psychological stress in university students\. Physiotherapy\-based exercise is a proposed non\-pharmacological approach, but the current fitness and stress profile of students, and how the two relate, is not well described\. Objective: To describe physical fitness \(BMI, flexibility, muscular endurance\) and psychological stress in students aged 17\-25 years, to compare stress across domains and by sex, and to examine fitness\-stress associations\. Methods: In this cross\-sectional observational study, fitness data from 52 Bachelor of Physiotherapy students \(41 females, 11 males\) and responses to the 40\-item Student Stress Inventory \(SSI\) from 51 students were analysed\. Stress domains were compared by repeated\-measures ANOVA with Bonferroni\-adjusted paired t\-tests; sex differences were tested with Welch&\#x27;s t\-test and the Mann\-Whitney U test; associations were tested with Pearson and Spearman correlations, with Holm adjustment \(p &lt; 0\.05\)\. Results: Mean age was 20\.46 ± 2\.23 years and mean BMI 22\.72 ± 4\.64 kg/m2; 42\.3% of students were outside the normal BMI range \(19\.2% underweight, 23\.1% overweight or obese\)\. Mean total stress was 85\.2 ± 19\.6 out of 160 \(Cronbach&\#x27;s α = 0\.91\)\. Domain scores differed \(F\(3,150\) = 15\.51, p &lt; 0\.001, partial η2 = 0\.24\): environmental stress was highest and remained significantly higher than every other domain after Bonferroni correction\. Stress did not differ by sex \(all p ? 0\.29\)\. Males had higher weight \(63\.96 ± 12\.20 vs 55\.41 ± 10\.62 kg; Mann\-Whitney p = 0\.027\) and BMI \(25\.43 ± 5\.50 vs 22\.00 ± 4\.17 kg/m2; p = 0\.043\), although Welch&\#x27;s t\-test was borderline \(p = 0\.052 and 0\.076\)\. Flexibility correlated with environmental stress \(r = 0\.31, p = 0\.028\), which was not confirmed by Spearman correlation \(p = 0\.092\) or after Holm adjustment \(p = 0\.42\)\. Conclusion: Students showed moderate stress, mainly environmental, and a substantial proportion had abnormal BMI, with no robust fitness\-stress associations\. These findings support screening students for weight status and stress and provide reference data for future studies, including intervention trials of physiotherapy\.
