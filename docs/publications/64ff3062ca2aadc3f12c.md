# Distributed organizational intelligence for physics\-consistent inverse problem solving in dynamic propagation environments

**Autores:** Mahmoud Rokaya

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Dynamic propagation environments require artificial intelligence methods capable of inverse reconstruction from sparse and noisy observations while maintaining physical consistency and computational efficiency\. This study introduces a distributed organizational intelligence framework in which Shallow Adaptive Cooperative Units \(SACUs\) perform localized estimation, specialization, and coordination\. Physical constraints are incorporated through local residual\-driven adaptation\. The framework was evaluated on controlled synthetic wave\-propagation fields with known governing physics and paired ground\-truth states\. Comparisons with centralized and distributed reference architectures were conducted under both the original nominal evaluation and a common controlled protocol\. No Global Positioning System \(GPS\), ultra\-wideband, acoustic\-localization, or other real\-world positioning data were used\. In the original nominal evaluation, SACU achieved a mean absolute error of 0\.0331 and a root mean squared error of 0\.0567, while residual magnitude decreased from 0\.002525 to 0\.000420\. Under the common controlled protocol, the evaluated architectures exhibited distinct trade\-offs among reconstruction accuracy, full\-field PDE consistency, boundary consistency, and computational cost, with no architecture uniformly superior across all four dimensions\. Ablation analysis showed that removing physics\-aware integration increased residual magnitude from 0\.000274 to 0\.013969\. Stable behavior was also observed under noise, sensor dropout, and temporal drift, although substantial computational cost was measured for the current SACU implementation\. These results demonstrate the potential of distributed organizational intelligence for physics\-consistent inverse reconstruction while identifying multidimensional performance trade\-offs\. Real\-world positioning effectiveness and application\-specific real\-time suitability require separate validation\.
