# Motivation And Performance In Project\-Based Organizations: Evidence From Abia State, Nigeria

**Autores:** Ogike Adamma; Okorocha Kevin Aku; Isiawuike Karachi Chikodi

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examined the relationship between motivation and performance in project\-based organizations in Abia State, Nigeria\.Specifically, it investigated the influence of intrinsic motivation on individual performance; the effect of extrinsic motivational factors on team performance; the mediating roles of knowledge sharing and collaborative behaviors in the motivation\-innovation performance relationship; and the moderating role of corporate culture in the motivation\-project performance relationship\.The study adopted a pragmatist philosophical position and a convergent parallel mixed\-methods design\.Quantitative data were obtained through a structured questionnaire from 402 valid respondents out of 420 distributed questionnaires, representing a 95\.7% response rate, while qualitative evidence was obtained from 22 semi\-structured interviews\.Data were analysed using descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, regression, Hayes PROCESS mediation and moderation procedures, and thematic analysis\.Results showed that intrinsic motivation strongly predicted individual performance \(β = 0\.634, R² = 0\.402, p &lt; 0\.001\), while extrinsic motivation significantly predicted team performance \(β = 0\.492, R² = 0\.242, p &lt; 0\.001\)\.Knowledge sharing and collaborative behaviors significantly mediated motivation and innovation performance, with indirect effects of 0\.152 and 0\.127, respectively\.Corporate culture significantly moderated the motivation\-project performance relationship \(interaction B = 0\.192, p &lt; 0\.001\), with the model explaining 42\.0% of variance\.The findings indicate that project\-based organizations should combine intrinsic and extrinsic motivational practices with supportive cultural conditions and structured knowledge\-sharing mechanisms\.
