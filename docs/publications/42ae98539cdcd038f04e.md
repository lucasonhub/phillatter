# Jagarlapudi, Shastri S\.: On the Generative Dynamics of Experiential Systems

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

For centuries, philosophy has asked what consciousness is\. This paper contends that this inquiry ultimately presupposes a more fundamental question: What makes consciousness possible? To address this question, it introduces Omnijectivism, a foundational philosophical framework according to which consciousness, cognition, meaning, and reality emerge from a unified generative architecture rather than from independent physical or mental processes\. Rejecting the ontological primacy traditionally accorded to objects, Omnijectivism advances a triadic conception of reality grounded in subject, object, and their intervening interface, and develops a methodology for investigating the structural conditions that make experience, intelligibility, and cognition possible\. From this perspective, the Hard Problem of Consciousness, the mind–brain relation, and the limitations of contemporary artificial intelligence are understood not as isolated theoretical puzzles, but as symptoms of a deeper epistemic limitation inherent in object\-oriented modes of inquiry\. The paper develops the conceptual foundations of Omnijectivism and argues that meaningful progress on these questions may ultimately require a re\-examination of the conceptual foundations underlying science, mathematics, and computation\. In doing so, it offers a unifying conceptual framework for interdisciplinary inquiry into the generative conditions of experience and intelligence\. Beyond advancing a philosophical perspective, Omnijectivism is presented as an operational framework that expands the conceptual vocabulary through which scientific and technological inquiry may proceed\. Rather than offering another explanatory theory of consciousness, it introduces distinctions that render previously inadmissible questions scientifically intelligible\. Central among these is intrinsic autonomy – not autonomy as increasingly sophisticated optimization or behavioral independence, but as the capacity of an agency to originate and evaluate its own purposes\. Within this framework, consciousness is characterized not as a localized property of the brain or an emergent feature of computation, but as the enabling condition of a unified generative process from which mental states, neural activity, and subjective experience arise in structured correspondence\. By making intrinsic autonomy an admissible object of inquiry, Omnijectivism provides a conceptual basis for investigating human\-like artificial intelligence while broadening the scope of inquiry across philosophy, cognitive science, mathematics, and computation\.
