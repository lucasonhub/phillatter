# Twelve Prompts for Literature Review : A Methodological Framework for Artificial IntelligenceAssisted Evidence Synthesis

**Autores:** Zen Revista

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Systematic literature reviews are among the most consequential and expensive instruments of modern scholarship: they condense vast bodies of primary research into the evidence on which policy, clinical practice, and further scientific inquiry depend\. At the same time, producing a rigorous review remains a slow, labour\-intensive, and error\-prone process, with median timelines measured in months or years and screening workloads that grow alongside the exponential expansion of the published record\. Large language models \(LLMs\) now demonstrate capabilities—including fluent multilingual reading, structured summarisation, code generation, and instruction following—that map directly onto the tasks dominating review production\. A rapidly growing empirical literature shows that these models can accelerate screening, translation, data extraction, and analysis; the same literature, however, documents hallucinated citations, silent omissions, unstable outputs, and legal and ethical constraints that make unsupervised delegation dangerous\. What remains missing is a methodological architecture capable of capturing these efficiency gains while containing the associated risks\. This thesis addresses that gap by developing, specifying, and illustrating a twelve\-prompt framework for artificial intelligence\-assisted literature review, organised as a four\-layer pyramid: foundation \(mapping previous reviews and defining concepts\); planning \(selecting the review type, structuring the search strategy, and generating Boolean expressions\); execution \(screening studies, translating titles and abstracts, evaluating methodological quality, and summarising articles\); and synthesis \(extracting data, drafting meta\-analysis code, and deepening theoretical ideas through Socratic questioning\)\. For each prompt, the thesis specifies its objective, a parameterised prompt template, the expected output format, characteristic failure modes, and the verification protocol that the reviewer of record must complete before incorporating the output into the review\. The framework is grounded in the reporting standards that discipline contemporary evidence synthesis—PRISMA 2020, PRISMA\-S, and the critical appraisal and evidence synthesis traditions of the Cochrane and GRADE ecosystems\. It also draws on empirical studies of LLM\-assisted screening published between 2023 and 2025 to establish realistic boundaries for the technology’s capabilities and reliability\.
