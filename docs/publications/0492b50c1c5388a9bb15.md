# Definitive radiotherapy versus surgery after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy for locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma: a propensity score\-matched study of efficacy and safety

**Autores:** Qiuying An; Yage Jia; Menglu Liu; Liyuan Fu; Jingxin Bai; Zihan Zhang; Jin Yan; Yajing Wang; Hongshan Ji; Yuhao Su; Ping Zhang; Zhiguo Zhou

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background The optimal local treatment strategy for locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma \(LA\-ESCC\) following neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy \(NICT\) remains controversial\. This study aimed to compare the survival outcomes and recurrence patterns of radical surgery and definitive radiotherapy \(RT\) following NICT in patients with LA\-ESCC\. Methods Patients with LA\-ESCC who underwent NICT \(2019–2023\) were enrolled and categorized into surgery or RT groups\. Propensity score matching \(PSM\) and Kaplan\-Meier methods were used to estimate progression\-free survival \(PFS\) and overall survival \(OS\)\. The differences in recurrence patterns between the two groups were compared\. Results A total of 278 patients were included, comprising 218 \(78\.4%\) and 60 \(21\.6%\) patients in the surgery and RT groups, respectively\. After PSM, the surgery group \(n=117\) maintained a significant OS benefit over the RT group \(n=60\) \(3\-year OS rates: 72\.1% vs\. 59\.5%; P = 0\.028\), whereas the PFS rates were comparable between the two groups \(3\-year PFS rates: 59\.4% vs\. 46\.9%; P = 0\.12\)\. For patients with upper esophageal cancer, RT showed a trend toward improved survival \(3\-year PFS rates: 76\.9% vs\. 55\.5%, P = 0\.34; 3\-year OS rates: 74\.0% vs\. 61\.8%, P = 0\.50\)\. Among the responders after NICT \(near\-cCR/cPR\), PFS and OS were similar between the surgery and RT groups \(3\-year PFS rates: 68\.9% vs\. 66\.9%, P = 0\.64; 3\-year OS rates: 80\.2% vs\. 70\.7%, P = 0\.20\)\. Regional lymph node recurrence predominated in the surgery group \(50\.0%\), whereas locoregional esophageal recurrence was most frequent in the RT group \(37\.0%\)\. The treatment\-related mortality rates were 0\.8% \(1/117\) in the surgery group and 1\.7% \(1/60\) in the RT group, indicating the overall safety of the treatments\. Conclusions Radical surgery remains the standard treatment for LA\-ESCC after NICT\. Definitive RT may be an organ\-preserving option in selected patients, especially those with upper esophageal cancer or a favorable response to NICT\.
