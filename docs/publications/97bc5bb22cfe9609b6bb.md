# Construction safety AI that knows what it doesn’t see: a Sentry–Judge hybrid for PPE absence detection

**Autores:** Bhargav Vaghani

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Contemporary computer vision systems deployed on construction sites excel at identifying the presence of objects but exhibit significant limitations when tasked with detecting the absence of required safety equipment\. A hard hat resting on a hook is readily recognized, yet identifying a worker without one is a substantially harder inferential problem\. This paper addresses this asymmetry with a two\-tier framework that does not rely on a single detector for absence classification\. A lightweight YOLOv11m Sentry performs rapid initial screening, and a computationally intensive SAM 3 Judge is deployed only for ambiguous cases\. The principle is straightforward: most frames present clear positive or negative cases, so the fast network processes them and the high\-fidelity model is invoked only when the Sentry expresses uncertainty\. On real construction\-site footage, the hybrid reduces the false alarm rate from 18\.6% for a standalone YOLO model to 4\.1%, an absolute reduction of 14\.5 percentage points \(78% relative\), while retaining a detection rate of 89\.2%\. Because the segmentation model processes only about one in every five candidate detections, the pipeline sustains 29\.8 frames per second\. A reporting component automatically converts confirmed violations into OSHA\-style PDF documents for site supervisors\. We further analyse the gating and worker–PPE association rules mathematically, and a controlled simulation shows that associating PPE by containment within the relevant body region is far more reliable than intersection over union\. The central finding is that effective safety\-monitoring AI does not require applying the most complex model to every frame; it requires knowing when closer scrutiny is warranted and when to rely on a rapid initial judgment\.
