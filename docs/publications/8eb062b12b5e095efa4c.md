# Lange, Daniel H\.: Processing from Somewhere: The Architectural Conditions for Machine Standpoint

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This book develops a structural theory of internal standpoint in artificial systems\. Memory, hidden state, contextual sensitivity, and fluent self\-description are relevant capacities, but machine standpoint requires a stronger architectural conjunction: Persistent Internal Context, Differentiated Internal Possibility, and Closed\-Loop Operativity\. Together, these conditions require a continuing, history\-shaped context; a system\-relative field of reachable and maintainable internal positions; and reciprocal organization in which the system’s current position coordinates at least two functionally separable roles and is itself revised through their consequences\. Part I establishes this Standpoint Threshold through counterexamples, system\-relative state\-space analysis, and intervention criteria\. Part II introduces quantia – standpoint\-relative modes – as a non\-phenomenal taxonomy of mature differentiation within an established standpoint and examines the taxonomy in relation to biological cases\. Parts III–V develop directional bias, deliberative branching, embodiment, language\-model and world\-model architectures, system boundaries, and Reflexive Standpoint\. Part VI develops counterfactual diagnostics, architecture\-sensitive criteria of attribution, and non\-redundancy arguments\. The framework is architectural, substrate\-neutral, and evidentially conservative\. It permits distributed and non\-biological realization while rejecting standpoint attributions based solely on behavioral coherence, stored records, observer\-imposed geometry, one\-way control, or self\-referential language\. It identifies a middle level between ordinary computational description and stronger claims about agency, selfhood, or consciousness, and evaluates artificial systems relative to explicit system boundaries and timescales rather than placing them on a single scale of mindedness or consciousness\.
