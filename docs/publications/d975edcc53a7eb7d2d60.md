# Embodied Faith and Athletic Cognition: Interoception, Religious Meaning\-Making, and Self\-Regulation in Sport Başlığı kopyala

**Autores:** Karadağ Erdal

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Athletic cognition is shaped not only by explicit goals and strategic decisions but also by bodily sensations, expectations, attention, and culturally grounded interpretations of experience\. This critical integrative review considers whether religious and spiritual meaning\-making may alter how athletes interpret effort, arousal, fatigue, uncertainty, and setbacks, without presuming that religious commitment improves objective performance\. It connects research on interoception, embodied cognition, attentional regulation, stress appraisal, religious coping, and sport resilience\. The synthesis distinguishes sensory detection from appraisal, deliberate contemplative practice from automatic motor skill, and personal faith narratives from experimentally supported mechanisms\. A conditional embodied\-meaning framework is developed in which bodily signals and contextual expectations interact with identity and coping strategies to influence interpretation, behavioral choices, and participation\. Potentially beneficial processes include flexible attentional reorientation, acceptance of transient discomfort, and value\-consistent persistence; adverse pathways include hypervigilance, shame, ritual dependence, and misinterpretation of medically significant symptoms\. No new participants, original quantitative data, or experimental effects are reported\. The article proposes longitudinal and mixed\-method studies comparing religious and secular meaning frameworks, using preregistered outcomes, matched interventions, measurement\-invariance testing, and ethical safeguards\. Existing literature establishes the relevance of bodily perception and meaning\-making as separate domains, but their specific interaction in athletic performance remains insufficiently tested\. The framework is intended to generate falsifiable questions, not a new diagnostic or performance assessment tool\. embodied cognition; interoception; religion and spirituality; sport psychology; cognitive appraisal; self\-regulation
