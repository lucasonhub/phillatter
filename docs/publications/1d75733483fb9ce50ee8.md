# Perspectives on tissue\-selective insulin derivatives

**Autores:** Maxmilián Hejl; Jiřı́ Jiráček

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Insulin is a central regulator of metabolic homoeostasis with diverse, tissue\-specific actions across multiple organs\. Despite major advances in insulin formulations, current therapies rely on systemic delivery and do not reproduce the physiological distribution of endogenous insulin, resulting in non\-physiological tissue exposure and associated metabolic limitations\. In this perspective, we discuss emerging strategies aimed at achieving tissue\-selective insulin action, including molecular engineering of insulin analogues, modulation of insulin receptor isoform specificity and targeted delivery approaches\. Attention is given to hepato\-preferential, muscle\-directed, brain\-targeted and adipose\-modulating strategies, highlighting their mechanistic basis, therapeutic potential and inherent limitations\. While proof of principle has been established for several approaches, challenges remain in balancing tissue selectivity with whole\-body metabolic control\. Advances in protein design and delivery technologies may ultimately enable the development of next\-generation insulin therapies that better mimic physiological insulin action and improve clinical outcomes in metabolic and neurodegenerative diseases\.
