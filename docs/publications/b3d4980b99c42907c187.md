# A cumulative biochemical profile of low uric acid and high total bilirubin is associated with hemorrhagic transformation after intravenous thrombolysis in acute ischemic stroke: a retrospective cohort study

**Autores:** Huan Liang; Baiyu Zeng; Jingquan Wang; Lizhen Yi; Linxing Huang; Ziyan Lin; Cai Zhou; Zenghui Yue

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background and objective Uric acid and bilirubin are endogenous redox\-related metabolites, but their joint association with hemorrhagic transformation \(HT\) after intravenous thrombolysis \(IVT\) remains uncertain\. We examined whether relatively low uric acid and high total bilirubin define a cumulative biochemical profile associated with hemorrhagic risk\. Methods In this single\-center retrospective cohort, we analyzed 733 patients with verifiable pretreatment uric acid and total bilirubin measurements from an IVT registry\. Low uric acid \(≤278 μmol/L\) and high total bilirubin \(≥12\.67 μmol/L\) contributed one point each to a 0–2 adverse\-feature score\. Any HT was the primary outcome; parenchymal hematoma \(PH\) and database\-defined symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage \(sICH\) were secondary outcomes\. Analyses used logistic regression, Firth bias\-reduced regression, multiple imputation and restricted cubic splines\. Results HT occurred in 79/733 patients \(10\.8%\)\. Observed HT rates were 7\.0%, 13\.6% and 26\.5% across zero, one and two adverse features\. Each additional feature was associated with higher odds of HT after covariate adjustment \(OR 1\.76, 95% CI 1\.19–2\.60; P = 0\.004\)\. PH occurred in 30/733 patients \(4\.1%\), and database\-defined sICH occurred in 33/664 patients with observed status \(5\.0%\)\. Continuous spline analyses in 731 patients showed evidence of an overall association for uric acid \( P overall=0\.044\) and total bilirubin \( P overall=0\.040\), without strong evidence of nonlinearity \( P nonlinear=0\.193 and P nonlinear=0\.704, respectively\)\. No multiplicative interaction between the continuous biomarker terms was detected \(OR 0\.99, 95% CI 0\.77–1\.28; P = 0\.938\)\. Conclusions Relatively low uric acid and high total bilirubin defined a cumulative biochemical profile associated with greater odds of HT after IVT\. The association requires external validation and should not be interpreted as evidence of causality or as a clinical treatment threshold\.
