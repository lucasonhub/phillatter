# Soft tissue sarcoma unmasked: An atypical presentation of primary cutaneous B\-cell lymphoma

**Autores:** Paridhi Agarwal; Frances Jenkins Bennett; Carlos López; Christine G\. Roth; Meredith C\. Mason

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Primary cutaneous follicle center lymphoma \(PCFCL\) is the most common subtype of primary cutaneous B\-cell lymphoma and is typically an indolent malignancy with an excellent prognosis\. However, atypical presentations may mimic soft tissue sarcoma or high\-grade lymphoma, creating significant diagnostic challenges\. Case Report: In this report, we describe a unique case of a young male with a history of systemic lupus erythematosus \(SLE\) who presented with a slowly enlarging, ulcerated soft\-tissue mass of the lower back with associated drainage, pain, fever, and weight prompting treatment with dose\-adjusted R\-EPOCH \(etoposide, prednisone, vincristine, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, and rituximab\) loss\. Upon admission, cross\-sectional imaging and fluorine\-18 fluorodeoxyglucose \(18F\-FDG\) positron emission tomography–computed tomography \(PET\-CT\) revealed a large \(15 cm\) right dorsal soft tissue mass with intense FDG uptake\. Core needle biopsy initially demonstrated a high\-grade appearing B\-cell lymphoma with a concern for high grade lymphoma\. Following additional immunostaining and multidisciplinary hematopathology review, the diagnosis was revised to PCFCL with a diffuse growth pattern\. The patient completed four cycles of inpatient chemotherapy with a reduction in mass size after each cycle\. Conclusion: Here we present a case of PCFCL with a diffuse growth pattern that mimicked both soft\-tissue sarcoma and high\-grade B\-cell lymphoma, highlighting the importance of prompt tissue diagnosis and comprehensive pathologic evaluation\.
