# Learning from degrowth: strengthening circular economy approaches through case studies of plastic packaging and textiles

**Autores:** Frieder Rubik; Pauline Deutz; Ulrich Petschow; Anne Kildunne; Florian Hofmann

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

To date, the circular economy \(CE\) and degrowth literatures have had a substantially separate existence\. Whilst a CE is increasingly regarded as a central element of sustainable development, its meaning is far from uniform, with diverse interpretations in both literature and practice\. This heterogenous discourse mirrors the tension between green growth, degrowth and post\-growth approaches in the wider sustainability debate\. Moreover, whilst the idea of a CE has taken a firm hold with policymakers, there are questions regarding its adequacy for the challenges faced, especially in terms of achieving absolute reductions in environmental impacts and positive social outcomes\. By contrast, the concept of degrowth calls for significant societal changes to achieve a functioning but smaller and more equitable economy\. It is less politically and economically palatable than CE\. Degrowth strategies, however, overlap significantly with those favored by some of the CE literature\. The potential for these overlaps to support a CE capable of delivering a more sustainable future has received little attention\. This paper offers both theoretical and empirical insights by exploring how degrowth and circular economy principles intersect in case studies focused on textiles and plastic packaging\. Using integrative literature reviews and empirical data, the case studies assess the extent to which different stakeholders approach the CE, drawing on semi\-structured interviews with textile manufacturers in Germany and stakeholders of plastic packaging on the east coast of England\. Our analysis finds that the far\-reaching socio\-ecological transformation towards short\-loop options likely necessary for a CE to confront urgent sustainability crises is seldom addressed in the literature and lies beyond the current scope of stakeholders to action or comprehend\. We argue that there remains a significant implementation gap between CE and \(de\)growth policies with an urgent need for analysis of how a transformation of specific sectors could be achieved linking company, networking and policy approaches\.
