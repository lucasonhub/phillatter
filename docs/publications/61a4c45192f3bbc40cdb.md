# EEG\-biometric authentication system using multi\-paradigm neurophysiological measurements and artificial intelligence

**Autores:** Chetan Rakshe; P Sriram Kumar; Christy Bobby Thomas; Mohanavelu Kalathe; Vanteemar Sathyanarayana Sreeraj; Ganesan Venkatasubramanian; John Thomas; Yuvaraj Rajamanickam; Deepesh Kumar; Jac Fredo Agastinose Ronickom

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Electroencephalography \(EEG\) provides a promising physiological modality for biometric authentication due to its inherent neural uniqueness and resistance to spoofing attacks\. However, reliable EEG\-based authentication remains challenging because brain signals are highly non\-stationary and vary across cognitive states\. To address this limitation, this study proposes a multi\-paradigm neurophysiological measurement protocol designed to capture stable subject\-specific EEG signatures across diverse brain conditions\. EEG signals were recorded from participants during six complementary paradigms, including eyes\-open and eyes\-closed resting states, left and right\-hand motor tasks, working\-memory encoding, steady\-state visual evoked potentials \(SSVEP\), and steady\-state auditory evoked potentials \(SSAEP\)\. The recorded signals were preprocessed and segmented into 2\-s epochs with 50% overlap\. We extracted 28 quantitative descriptors per channel from time, frequency, time\-frequency, and nonlinear domains\. These descriptors were used to evaluate several decision models, including Mahalanobis distance\-based template matching, logistic regression \(LR\), support vector machine, random forest, and a one\-dimensional convolutional neural network\. The performance of the proposed measurement system was analyzed under both single\-paradigm and multi\-paradigm configurations using cross\-session validation\. Experimental results show that the multi\-paradigm measurement protocol reduces the equal error rate \(EER\) by 2% compared with single\-paradigm systems\. The LR decision model achieved the best performance with an EER of 1\.6% \(95% confidence interval: 1\.48−1\.73\), with false rejection rates of 2\.06% and 9\.01% at false acceptance rates of 1% and 0\.1%, respectively\. Feature analysis indicated that Hjorth mobility and Mel\-frequency cepstral coefficients derived from fronto\-central cortical channels provide the most reliable neural identity descriptors\. These results demonstrate that multi\-paradigm EEG acquisition improves the stability of neural measurements across cognitive states and enables reliable biometric authentication in dynamic conditions\.
