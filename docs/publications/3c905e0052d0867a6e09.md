# Internal Quality Assurance Mechanisms for Teaching and Learning, Research, and Community Engagement in Higher Education: A Critical Examination

**Autores:** Nedejje Halima; Isabirye Mohammed

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper critically examines the internal quality assurance \(IQA\) mechanisms that higher education institutions use to govern teaching and learning, research, and community engagement\. Drawing on a documentary analysis of peerreviewed scholarly literature, it identifies and evaluates key IQA instruments: student evaluation of teaching, peer observation of teaching, programme and departmental reviews, performance appraisals, internal quality assurance cells, research ethics committees, research output assessments, and community partnership frameworks\. The analysis reveals a persistent tension between the developmental and accountability\-oriented functions of IQA\. Teaching\-focused instruments are frequently co\-opted for administrative surveillance rather than pedagogical improvement; research quality assurance remains heavily output\-driven, with limited attention to process integrity; and community engagement, though increasingly recognised as a core mission, lacks robust internal metrics for assessing impact and reciprocity\. The paper argues for an integrated, culture\-based approach to IQA that aligns quality mechanisms with institutional mission, fosters professional trust, and positions internal quality assurance as a catalyst for transformation rather than mere compliance\. Recommendations include strengthening formative feedback loops, decoupling evaluative metrics from punitive administrative decisions, and developing context\-sensitive indicators for community engagement\.
