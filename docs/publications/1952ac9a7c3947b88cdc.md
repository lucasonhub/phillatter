# Post\-fumigation soil vapor extraction of dimethyl disulfide for fumigant capture, residue reduction, and early replanting

**Autores:** 毛克修; Yang Jun; 舒敏艳; Mian Hou; Jinjing Xiao; Qiuxia Wang; Xiujun Tang

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Soil fumigation controls soilborne pests but can release volatile fumigants into the atmosphere and leave phytotoxic residues that delay replanting\. We evaluated soil vapor extraction \(SVE\) as a post\-fumigation technique to capture dimethyl disulfide \(DMDS\), accelerate residue dissipation, and shorten the replanting interval\. Laboratory experiments compared three soils \(Shunyi, Suihua, and Wenshan\) at an airflow rate of 1\.0 L min −1 and three airflow rates \(0\.5, 1\.0, and 1\.5 L min −1 \) in Shunyi soil\. DMDS extraction rate was highest immediately after SVE initiation and decreased subsequently\. Early extraction was consistent with advection and volatilization and positively correlated with the 0\.5–2 mm particle fraction\. Wenshan soil, which contained the largest proportion of these particles, exhibited the fastest early extraction and highest total capture\. Later extraction was consistent with desorption and intra\-aggregate diffusion limitations and negatively correlated with specific surface area and clay content\. Increasing the airflow rate enhanced early extraction but did not consistently improve the final capture\. At 1\.0 L min −1 , 24 h of SVE reduced DMDS residues by 97\.55%–99\.51% across three soils to 0\.30–0\.93 μg g −1 \. These residues did not inhibit cucumber seedling growth\. Activated carbon captured 79\.33%–89\.35% of the applied DMDS, indicating substantial fumigant removal and reduced potential for atmospheric release\. These findings support SVE for post\-fumigation DMDS management, suggesting its potential to accelerate residue dissipation and shorten the replanting interval\. Before commercial implementation, field\-scale studies should quantify emission fluxes, energy requirements, treatment uniformity, pest control efficacy, and the purity and reusability of captured DMDS\.
