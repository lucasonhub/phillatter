# Guarino, Brian: A Base Dimension for Sensory Intensity: Homogeneity, Scale Type, and the Non\-Identifiability of the Unit

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Psychophysics assigns sensory intensity a ratio scale but no dimensional home, and has never fully separated the scale of the attribute from the function through which observers report it\. This paper treats sensory intensity as carrying a base dimension, written Ξ, adjoined to the physical base set, and relates it to stimulus intensity by a two\-stage homogeneous bridge: a dimensionless drive formed from the excess of the stimulus over a modality reference, and a coefficient κΞ that supplies the whole of the dimension\. Three results follow\. First, the coefficient is not a constant of nature but a unit: it cancels from every ratio the bridge delivers to measurement, and the ex\- tension is conservative over every purely physical dimensional analysis\. Second, the coefficient and the observer’s numeral map enter magnitude estimation only through their composite, and the transduction exponent is recovered free of that map only by cross\-modal matching; this yields a derivation of the pattern the axiomatic record actually shows, in which commutativity of magnitude productions is satisfied for every observer while multiplicativity divides them, and a previously untested prediction that an observer’s violation of multiplicativity and that observer’s departure from power\-function magnitude estimation are one phenomenon and must covary\. The division is not an anomaly the account absorbs but the pattern it predicts, since commutativity holds for any strictly increasing numeral map and multiplicativity only for a power map, and the published cross\-modal data show the sample dividing evenly on the production side, which is one of the two arms the test requires; the other, magnitude estimation from the same observers, was not collected, so the prediction awaits a study running both tasks on one panel\. Third, because the drive is formed from an excess rather than a ratio, the power law is the supra\-threshold limit of the bridge rather than its exact form\. Cross\-modal composition and transitivity follow as necessities rather than regularities, and the conditions under which the account would be given up are stated\.
