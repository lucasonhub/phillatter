# Pre\-clinical evaluation of a smartphone\-assisted navigation system for surgical pin positioning in total knee arthroplasty

**Autores:** Dr\. David Bull; Jiaxin Zhang; Shuqiao Xie; Pierluigi Cuomo; Gareth G\. Jones; Yuqi Chen; Anthony M\. J\. Bull

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Total knee arthroplasty \(TKA\) success is highly dependent on accurate implant alignment, however, 80% of procedures globally are performed manually\. Manual procedures have higher alignment errors compared to robotic platforms and patient\-specific instrumentation, yet these have limitations of high cost and intraoperative inflexibility, respectively\. This study aimed to evaluate the intraoperative pin positioning accuracy and flexibility of a smartphone\-assisted navigation system using CT\-derived surgical plans\. Three participants used the navigation system to perform 108 guide pin insertions \(54 femoral, 54 tibial\) on surrogate bone models based on CT\-derived surgical plans\. Testing included both default mechanical axis targets \(0°\) and user\-selected navigated targets ranging from \-3° to 3°\. Accuracy was assessed by comparing achieved pin trajectories with the preoperative surgical plan\. Median absolute angular deviations achieved ranged from 0\.9° to 1\.6° across all component\-plane combinations, with an overall median deviation of 1\.3° \(IQR 0\.6\-2\.1°\)\. The proportion of insertions within a predefined ±3° clinical accuracy threshold ranged from 83\.3% to 100%, and all component\-plane combinations demonstrated deviations significantly within this threshold \(p &lt; 0\.00001\)\. These findings support the technical feasibility of the smartphone\-assisted navigation system for surgical pin positioning under controlled pre\-clinical conditions\. Further studies will evaluate the navigation system in cadaveric models with experienced orthopaedic surgeon participants\.
