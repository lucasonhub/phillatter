# Cannabidiol preserves astrocyte viability and attenuates injury\-induced glial stress responses across zebra finch song control nuclei

**Autores:** Dylan A\. Marshall; Karen Litwa; Ken Soderstrom

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Chronic neuroinflammation following traumatic brain injury \(TBI\) increases the risk of affective disorders and early\-onset dementias\. Existing anti\-inflammatory therapies are limited by systemic side effects with chronic use and by poor brain penetration of newer biologics\. Cannabidiol \(CBD\), a non\-euphorigenic phytocannabinoid with a favorable safety profile and multi\-target neuroprotective actions, is a promising alternative\. Using a songbird microlesion model of learned vocal recovery after targeted damage to the premotor nucleus HVC, we previously showed that systemic CBD \(10 mg/kg/day\) reduces vocal disruption, inflammatory cytokines, oxidative stress, and synaptic loss\. Here we tested the hypothesis that these benefits involve astrocytes\. CBD \(10 mg/kg/day IM for 7 days\) preserved astrocyte viability, attenuated injury\-induced lysosomal stress, suppressed reactivity markers \(C3, S100A10, aromatase\), and upregulated key metabolic and antioxidant transcripts \(glutamine synthetase and GCLM\) across the interconnected song\-control nuclei HVC, RA, and Area X\. These changes were accompanied by reduced neuronal apoptosis in lesioned HVC\. The results identify astrocyte\-associated responses as a candidate cellular substrate of CBD’s circuit\-wide protective effects and support its translational potential to mitigate secondary injury and communication deficits after TBI\.
