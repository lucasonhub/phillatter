# The impact of AI service quality on researchers’ work wellbeing: a dual\-path moderated mediation model

**Autores:** Shuyang Liu; Tongyao Liu

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

As AI tools permeate scientific research, a critical question emerges: does better AI actually make researchers happier? Grounded in the Job Demands\-Resources \(JD\-R\) model, this study proposes and tests a dual\-path moderated mediation framework in which perceived AI service quality influences researcher work well\-being through two parallel psychological routes\. The motivational gain pathway posits that higher quality is positively related to research self\-efficacy, while the loss\-alleviation pathway posits that higher quality is negatively related to cognitive verification load\. AI trust is introduced as a moderator of both first\-stage paths\. Using data from 439 researchers and PROCESS macro models 4 and 7, we found that AI service quality did not directly affect well\-being \( β = 0\.07, p = 0\.260\), but exerted a fully mediated total effect\. The loss pathway via cognitive verification load reduction was the sole significant mediating mechanism \(indirect effect = 0\.08, 95% CI \[0\.028, 0\.135\]\)\. AI trust significantly moderated only the loss pathway \( β = −0\.21, p &lt; 0\.001\): the cognitive\-relief effect became significant only at medium and high trust levels, remaining inoperative for low\-trust researchers\. These findings reframe the value of AI quality in research settings as cognitive offloading rather than competence enhancement, and position trust as a threshold condition rather than a continuous amplifier of AI benefits\.
