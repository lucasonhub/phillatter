# The psychology of digital credit: the association of financial anxiety and impulsive traits with “buy now, pay later” delinquency

**Autores:** Chenchen Ma; Mei Ge; Mingqiu Gu; Ziran Ding

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Digital credit structures, exemplified by the “Buy Now, Pay Later” \(BNPL\) model, combine frictionless transactional accessibility with the underlying psychological vulnerabilities of users\. This cross\-sectional investigation maps the statistical covariation between perceived financial anxiety and distinct digital credit usage dimensions\. Methods Utilizing a dataset of 890 respondents \(708 of whom are active BNPL users\), regression and mediation analyses was conducted\. The study evaluated subjective financial anxiety, BNPL usage \(frequency, dependence, hedonic preference, and delinquency\), impulsive buying tendency, and objective financial literacy\. Results Analyses reveal an absence of significant correlation linking financial anxiety to operational usage frequency or overarching bill dependence\. However, a robust positive covariation emerges between heightened financial anxiety and both the allocation of credit toward non\-essential hedonic consumption and the propensity for debt delinquency\. Impulsive buying tendency acts as a stable trait that shows a statistical indirect association in this cross\-sectional relationship, and objective financial literacy does not significantly moderate this association\. Discussion The structural pattern characterizes maladaptive digital default primarily as an emotion\-regulation phenomenon—associated with the convergence of subjective insecurity and impulsive traits—rather than a mere deficit in mathematical knowledge\. This highlights the need to incorporate behavioral friction elements into fintech interfaces\.
