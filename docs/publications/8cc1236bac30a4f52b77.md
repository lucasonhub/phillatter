# Systemic immune dysregulation and local barrier failure as predictors of septic shock in acute appendicitis: a large\-scale multicenter interpretable machine learning study

**Autores:** Yunfeng Chen; 芮云云

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background The transition from localized acute appendicitis to systemic septic shock is driven by complex immune and metabolic dysregulation combined with anatomical barrier failure\. Accurately predicting early hemodynamic deterioration remains clinically challenging\. This study aimed to develop an interpretable multimodal machine learning model by integrating systemic immunological profiles with local sonographic characteristics\. Methods This multicenter cohort study included 3000 patients with acute appendicitis\. Following rigorous data reduction using the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator regression, eight supervised machine learning algorithms were constructed\. The optimal predictive algorithm was comprehensively evaluated across training, internal testing, and independent external validation cohorts\. The SHapley Additive exPlanations framework was utilized to provide transparent model interpretability, and the final algorithm was deployed as a dynamic digital calculator\. Results Six independent predictors were identified, including the Systemic Immune Inflammation Index, C\-reactive protein to albumin ratio, glucose to lymphocyte ratio, Prognostic Nutritional Index, periappendiceal fluid, and the sonographic loss of the submucosal echogenic layer\. The Random Forest model demonstrated the highest discriminative performance among the evaluated algorithms, achieving an area under the curve of 0\.914\. The model’s discrimination, calibration, and decision\-analytic performance were consistently evaluated in the internal testing and external validation cohorts\. SHAP analysis indicated that systemic inflammatory indices and sonographic features reflecting local disease severity made important contributions to the model’s predicted risk of septic shock\. These findings should be interpreted as predictive associations rather than evidence of biological causality or synergy\. Conclusion This multicenter study developed and externally validated an interpretable machine learning model for early risk stratification of septic shock\. The publicly accessible calculator may support individualized risk assessment; however, prospective impact evaluation is required to determine whether its clinical implementation facilitates earlier resuscitation or improves patient outcomes\.
