# One Inequality, Five Hazards: A Unified Dimensionless Screening Number and a Reporting Checklist for Reactive\-Hazard Assessment

**Autores:** Tae Hun Kwak; Kyungjun Kang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Process\-safety practice screens reactive hazards with separate dimensionless criteria─the Damköhler number, the Frank\-Kamenetskii and Semenov self\-heating criteria, Stoessel criticality from the adiabatic temperature rise, and the atmospheric exclusion distance\. Applied in isolation they hide a shared algebra and leave practitioners without a common disclosure standard\. This Commentary makes one modest claim and one practical one\. The modest one: these five criteria share the algebraic form N = load/threshold, hazardous at N ≥ 1, each recovered by naming its load and threshold\. We do not claim a physical unification\. Normalization keeps the ratio and discards the hazard dimension it came from, so equal N need not mean equal hazard; and the thresholds differ in kind─a bifurcation the equations single out \(Frank\-Kamenetskii, Semenov\), an agreed convention \(the 200 K severity cut\), or an externally fixed exposure limit\. The thermal Biot number shares the algebra but selects a modeling regime rather than measuring a hazard, and is excluded\. The practical claim is a one\-page reporting checklist fixing what any screening statement must disclose, worked through a hazardous and a safe example\. Applied to twelve published reactive materials, the number and the categorical NFPA\-704 instability rating disagree for 31% of strictly comparable pairs─a descriptive statistic on a small, nonindependent set \(n = 12\)─because a categorical rating encodes ease of initiation while N orders thermal severity\. An uncertainty analysis shows classification is far more sensitive to the choice of threshold than to calorimetric error, which is why the checklist requires the threshold’s provenance\.
