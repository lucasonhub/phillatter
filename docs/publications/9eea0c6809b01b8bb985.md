# Conceptualising AI\-reform professional learning communities in Chinese schools through the lion dance metaphor

**Autores:** Shiyu Pu; Xiaokun Shi

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

As artificial intelligence \(AI\) moves into school education at increasing speed, we still know relatively little about how schools organise AI\-related change amid policy expectations, technological uncertainty and teachers’ everyday work\. Based on a qualitative case study of an AI education pilot school in Beijing, China, this article examines the formation and operation of an AI\-reform professional learning community \(PLC\)\. Data were generated through semi\-structured interviews, school\-based observations and school documents\. Through inductive thematic analysis, four recurring role functions were identified and subsequently conceptualised through the structural metaphor of the Chinese lion dance: drummers, lion heads, lion tails and audiences\. The findings show that school leaders translated policy expectations and paced implementation; teacher leaders carried visible exploratory and pedagogical work; non\-lead teachers extended reform through cautious and selective participation; and parents, while remaining outside the formal community, shaped ethical boundaries and conditions of trust\. The article conceptualises AI\-reform PLCs as issue\-specific and role\-differentiated communities that organise policy translation, teacher learning, leadership, participation and ethical negotiation around emerging technological change\. It contributes a culturally grounded account of how school\-based AI reform is collectively coordinated rather than simply implemented by individual teachers\.
