# Faculty\-supervised AI\-supported BOPPPS\-CBL and performance on a simulated neurological\-emergency assessment among junior\-college medical interns: a batch\-stratified randomized educational study

**Autores:** Junhuai Zhang; Zhanqin Xiao; Xiang Gu; Qi Lang; Chanxi Chen; Yaxi Chen

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background County hospitals and township health centers in China provide frontline care for time\-sensitive neurological emergencies, and three\-year junior\-college clinical medicine programs are an important pathway into this workforce\. We evaluated a faculty\-supervised artificial intelligence \(AI\)\-supported BOPPPS \(Bridge\-in, Objectives, Pre\-assessment, Participatory Learning, Post\-assessment, and Summary\) and case\-based learning \(CBL\) instructional package among junior\-college medical interns\. Methods In this prospective, batch\-stratified randomized educational study, 118 interns from 12 monthly rotation batches were assigned to AI\-supported BOPPPS\-CBL \( n = 58\) or conventional BOPPPS\-CBL \( n = 60\) during a one\-month neurology rotation\. The intervention used standardized DeepSeek prompts for Socratic questioning, error\-focused feedback, and structured reflection under faculty supervision\. The primary endpoint was the total score on a locally developed four\-station simulated neurological\-emergency assessment \(0–100\)\. Secondary outcomes included a written examination, while learner\-reported questionnaire items were exploratory\. Regression models adjusted for rotation batch, baseline academic performance, and digital literacy\. The study was not prospectively registered; no public trial registration identifier or registry URL exists, and no publicly time\-stamped protocol or statistical analysis plan was available\. Results All 118 participants completed the primary assessment\. Mean total scores were 82\.07 in the AI\-supported group and 76\.43 in the control group, with an adjusted between\-group difference of 5\.67 points \(95% CI, 2\.86–8\.49; P &lt; 0\.001; adjusted Cohen&\#x27;s d = 0\.74\)\. In secondary station\-specific analyses, only lesion localization met the multiplicity\-adjusted threshold \(adjusted mean difference, 2\.58 points; 95% CI, 1\.25–3\.91; P &lt; 0\.001; adjusted d = 0\.71\)\. A Station 2\-specific ICC was not estimated\. Post hoc sensitivity analyses excluding the station containing reused case contexts yielded standardized estimates of similar magnitude but could not exclude cueing or carry\-over\. Written\-examination estimates were smaller and imprecise\. Exploratory learner\-reported findings indicated greater perceived preparation\-time burden in the AI\-supported group\. Conclusion Assignment to the faculty\-supervised AI\-supported BOPPPS\-CBL package was associated with higher scores on a locally developed simulated neurological\-emergency assessment than conventional BOPPPS\-CBL\. These findings do not establish transfer to workplace clinical performance or patient outcomes\. Multicenter studies using independent assessment cases, longer\-term follow\-up, and workplace\-based outcomes are warranted\.
