# Effect of Glyceryl Trinitrate Enriched Cardioplegia on Patient Outcomes in Low Ejection Fraction Patients undergoing Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting

**Autores:** Muhammad Rizwan Naeem; Chanda Naseem

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Coronary artery bypass graft surgery in patients with low left ventricular ejection fraction results in an increased risk for myocardial damage and hemodynamic instability after the procedure\. Cardioplegia formulation is one of the factors that can affect myocardial protection during heart surgery\. Addition of glyceryl trinitrate \(GTN\), which acts as a nitric oxide donor, to cardioplegia formulation may be beneficial for myocardial perfusion\. Objective: To compare early postoperative outcomes between patients receiving standard blood cardioplegia and those receiving GTN\-enriched blood cardioplegia during CABG\. Methods: In this quasi\-experimental study, 64 patients were involved in CABG and divided into two equal groups of 32 each\. While the control group was treated with conventional blood cardioplegia, the treatment group was provided GTN\-containing blood cardioplegia at 1 mg/L\. The indicators measured in this study included CK\-MB after surgery, ventilation time, inotropic support, ICU stay, and postoperative complications\. For statistical significance, the tests conducted by researchers were used, with p&lt;0\.05 being statistically significant\. Results: The levels of postoperative CK\-MB were lower in the GTN group compared to the control group, although the results were not statistically significant \(median 39\.0 vs\. 42\.5 U/L, p=0\.066\)\. However, the period of mechanical ventilation was significantly shorter in the GTN group \(median 5\.0 vs\. 6\.0 h, p&lt;0\.001\)\. No statistical difference was found in the overall inotropic score and ICU stay\. One case of acute kidney injury was observed in the control group while no patient experienced acute kidney injury in the GTN group\. Less patients underwent IABP and direct current cardioversion in the GTN group, even though the results were not statistically significant\. Conclusion: GTN\-enriched cardioplegia had a shorter postoperative period of mechanical ventilation\. Despite lower levels of CK\-MB postoperatively and fewer complications in the GTN\-enriched group, these differences did not reach statistical significance\. It is necessary to conduct more randomized studies in order to prove the efficacy and safety of the use of GTN\-enriched cardioplegia in patients with reduced LVEF\.
