# Validation of generative artificial intelligence techniques for synthetic data generation in multiple sclerosis research: A comparison with real\-world evidence from the Italian multiple sclerosis registry

**Autores:** Pietro Iaffaldano; Saverio D’Amico; Giuseppe Lucisano; Massimiliano Copetti; Tommaso Guerra; Maria Assunta Rocca; Francesco Paolo Patti; Giovanna De Luca; Diana Ferraro; Rocco Totaro; Vincenzo Brescia Morra; Giuseppe Salemi; Emilio Portaccio; Matteo Foschi; Matilde Inglese; Maria Gabriella Coniglio; Clara Grazia Chisari; Francesca Caputo; Damiano Paolicelli; Mario Alberto Battaglia; Matteo Giovanni Della Porta; Victor Savevski; Mattia Delleani; Filippo Emanuele Colella; Elisabetta Sauta; Maria Pia Amato; Massimo Filippi; María Trojano

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: The use of generative artificial intelligence to create synthetic data is an emerging strategy to enhance real\-world evidence\. Objective: To assess the validity of artificial intelligence\-generated synthetic data in replicating data collected in the Italian Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders Register for the evaluation of progression independent of relapse activity risk under early intensive treatment and escalation treatment strategies\. Methods: Artificial intelligence generative models were trained on a sub\-cohort of 1666 Italian Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders Register patients to generate a synthetic dataset of 4878 patients\. Synthetic vAlidation FramEwork, Clinical Synthetic Fidelity, and Nearest Neighbor Distance Ratio were applied\. Comparisons between treatment strategies were conducted using both real and synthetic data, focusing on progression independent of relapse activity risk\. Results: The synthetic data demonstrated fidelity and privacy preservation\. Treatment\-effect estimates for escalation treatment versus early intensive treatment were similar across the real and synthetic datasets\. Cox proportional hazards models showed that synthetic data preserved the robustness of the estimates for the risk of a first progression independent of relapse activity event\. Associations with radiological risk factors were broadly similar across the real and synthetic datasets\. Conclusion: Artificial intelligence\-generated synthetic data reliably replicated treatment\-effect outcomes of Italian Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders Register data, supporting their potential use in clinically defined research settings\.
