# DeepHopping\-LTE: A Deep Reinforcement Learning Framework for Adaptive Frequency Hopping Spread Spectrum \(FHSS\) in Dense 4G Cellular Networks

**Autores:** Nkechinyere Eyidia; Smiles Chiweikpe Obiajunwa

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid growth of mobile devices and dense 4G deployments has intensified interference and congestion, challenging network reliability, spectrum efficiency, and quality of service\. This study proposes DeepHopping\-LTE, a deep reinforcement learning\-based adaptive frequency hopping framework designed to dynamically optimize frequency selection in dense cellular networks\. By leveraging a deep Q\-network \(DQN\), the framework balances exploration and exploitation using an epsilon\-greedy policy, converging to optimal hopping sequences to minimize interference while maximizing throughput\. Simulations and field data from two urban locations demonstrated that DeepHopping\-LTE significantly outperforms traditional static FHSS and heuristic frequency hopping methods\. The framework achieved a stable signal\-to\-interference\-plus\-noise ratio \(SINR\) averaging 15–17 dB, compared to 5 dB for static methods, more than doubling spectrum efficiency to 5\.5 bits/s/Hz under high load conditions\. Packet delivery ratio also improved dramatically, ranging from 94% to 98%, while traditional FHSS averaged 75%, reflecting superior reliability and quality of service\. Throughput improvement ratios reached 1\.6 under peak user density, and interference reduction ranged from 40% to 70%, confirming effective mitigation of environmental noise\. Additionally, the adaptability index reached 0\.95, demonstrating near\-instantaneous response to dynamic network changes, whereas heuristic and static methods lagged at 0\.65 and 0\.15, respectively\. The results validate that reinforcement learning can transform frequency hopping from a static approach into an intelligent, adaptive solution capable of optimizing network performance in real\-time\. By continuously observing network states and selecting optimal channels, DeepHopping\-LTE not only mitigates interference but also enhances spectrum utilization, reliability, and user experience\. These findings provide a practical foundation for policy implementation in dense urban networks, supporting guidelines for adaptive frequency management, interference monitoring, and reinforcement learning deployment in 4G and future 5G environments\. In conclusion, DeepHopping\-LTE addresses the critical challenges of dense cellular networks, offering a scalable, intelligent, and resilient framework that surpasses traditional methods, enabling efficient and reliable communication in high\-density mobile ecosystems\.
