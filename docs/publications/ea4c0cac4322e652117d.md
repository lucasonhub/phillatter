# Johnson, Jamison: Toward a Theory of Coherent Existence \- Journal 1 \- A Philosophical Framework for Subjective Experience, Suffering, Community, and the Nature of Meaning

## Descrição do feed

This paper presents a philosophical framework for the structure of conscious life, organized around five linked concepts: Jays, subjective coherence, the Center, Intent, and the ecology of coherence\. The Jay is proposed as the basic unit of analysis: a discrete, meaning\-laden moment of conscious experience with three features \(a perceiver, a felt quality, and an orientation toward or away from coherence\)\. Jays arise wherever a perspective meets the world, differ in weight, and accumulate over a lifetime into the patterns recognized as character\. What a Jay leaves, its Trace, passes on through what a life makes and does and is taken up by others into Jays of their own\. Subjective coherence is the degree to which accumulated Jays form an integrated whole; it is distinguished from shallow happiness and from comfort, and treated as dynamic\. The Center is the stable orientational core from which experience is organized, and Intent the expression of that core in action\. Each life is argued to keep its own interior clock, and outlook to bear on outcomes through appraisal, expectancy, and the responses of others, with perceived and physical effects kept distinct\. Suffering is analyzed, in its most common form, as a signal of misalignment between Jays and Center, and community as the ecology in which coherence is sustained or eroded\. The central claim is that the progressive positive coherence of one’s Jays, in authentic relation with others, is what it means to live well\. The account draws on phenomenology, Jungian psychology, Buddhist philosophy, Stoic ethics, attachment theory, predictive processing, global workspace theory, and research on the default mode network\. The paper is the first of four journals that advance a single thesis: that there is one consciousness, beyond perception and present within every subject, of which every mind is a perspective\. It states that thesis at the outset and leaves its defense to the companion journals; its own task is the structure of a life as it is lived from inside\. \(direct link \)
