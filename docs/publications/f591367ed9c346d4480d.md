# ENGINEERING EDUCATION IN NIGERIA: CHALLENGES, REFORMS, AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS

**Autores:** Henry Azuka Ifeachor; Chima Elijah Uwaoma; Ikechukwu Chukwukadibia Agha; Onwualia Promise Ebubechukwu; Eneh Christian Chukwuemeka

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Engineering education in Nigeria is critical for national technological development and economic transformation\. However, the sector faces persistent challenges, including inadequate funding, obsolete curricula, poor laboratory facilities, a shortage of qualified academic staff, and weak industry‑academia linkages\. This article provides a comprehensive analysis of the current state of engineering education in Nigerian universities and polytechnics\. Using a mixed‑methods approach comprising a review of policy documents, institutional surveys, and comparative benchmarking with global best practices, the study identifies key bottlenecks\. Results show that only 38% of accredited engineering programmes meet minimum laboratory equipment standards, and student‑to‑lecturer ratios exceed 35:1 in 70% of institutions\. Additionally, less than 20% of engineering graduates undergo industrial training of acceptable quality\. The article presents a detailed table of accreditation status from the Council for the Regulation of Engineering in Nigeria \(COREN\) and another on graduate employability\. Findings indicate a widening gap between curricular content and industry needs, leading to high graduate unemployment despite reported skill shortages\. Recommendations include a sustainable funding model, mandatory curriculum revision every five years, establishment of regional engineering hubs, and a compulsory one‑year industry residency for lecturers\. Policy implications for the National Universities Commission \(NUC\) and COREN are discussed\. The article concludes that without urgent structural reforms, Nigeria’s vision of becoming a technologically driven economy by 2030 will remain unattainable\.
