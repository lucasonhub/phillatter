# Digital translation practices in university English learning: adoption, disciplinary variation, and AI\-assisted revision

**Autores:** Kevin Mario Laura\-De La Cruz; Astrid Camila Milagros Palacios\-Mamani; Sebastian Alejandro Flores\-Maquera; Pilar Valeria Zapana\-Aguilar; Paola Milagros Paredes\-Machaca; Lucia Esperanza Caceres\-Ramos; Enrique Jose Quispe\-Mello; Gina Maribel Valle\-Castro

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Machine translation and generative artificial intelligence \(AI\) are now common resources in English as a foreign language learning, yet evidence from Latin American higher education remains limited and often conflates tool use with learning impact\. This study characterized translation practices, tool preferences, print\-dictionary use, and AI\-assisted text revision among university students in southern Peru\. Methods A cross\-sectional survey was conducted with 435 students from 37 degree programs and seven faculties at a public university\. Questionnaire items were analyzed as separate indicators rather than combined into a latent scale\. Valid percentages with 95% Wilson confidence intervals were estimated, paired exact McNemar tests were used for within\-participant comparisons, faculty differences were assessed using Monte Carlo chi\-square tests with false\-discovery\-rate adjustment, and an exploratory logistic regression was fitted\. Results Use of AI or automated translators was reported by 94\.7% of respondents, and 92\.4% used translation applications or websites\. Both were more prevalent than print\-dictionary experience \(65\.4%; paired odds ratios 9\.86 and 7\.88, respectively; both p &lt; \.001\)\. Google Translate predominated across questionnaire contexts, whereas ChatGPT accounted for 7\.7% of responses to the general\-use item and 34\.0% to the AI\-focused item\. AI\-assisted revision was reported by 74\.1% and varied across faculties \(52\.2%\-96\.8%; Cramér&\#x27;s V = \.274, q &lt; \.001\)\. Although 75\.0% reported using print dictionaries rarely or never, 78\.0% supported their continued promotion\. Discussion The findings describe a heterogeneous institutional translation ecology in which widespread digital\-tool adoption coexists with disciplinary variation, context\-dependent tool preferences, and continued endorsement of print resources\. These findings do not establish learning effects\. They support pedagogical attention to verification, post\-editing, and independent language production, whose effectiveness requires direct evaluation\.
