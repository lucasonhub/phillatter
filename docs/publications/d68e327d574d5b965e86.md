# An AdS\_\{3\} Dual for Supersymmetric MHV Celestial Amplitudes

**Autores:** Igor Mol

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

As I am discontinuing my research on celestial holography, I shall be uploading to this repository the documents that I have written on the subject in the past couple of months, in the hope that they might be useful to other researchers\. The two documents appertaining to this repository would constitute my reply to the referee report and my resubmission to https://scipost\.org/submissions/scipost\_202509\_00048v2/\. In this new version of my manuscript &quot;An AdS Dual for Supersymmetric MHV Celestial Amplitudes,&quot; I presented a detailed discussion of the problem of reconstructing the distributional celestial amplitudes, characterising the tree\-level scattering of gravitons arranged into a maximally helicity\-violating \(MHV\) configuration in general relativity and \\mathcal\{N\}=8 supergravity, from the celestial leaf amplitudes, which I termed hyperbolic\-slice correlators in the present manuscript\. I gave an especial emphasis to the reconstruction theorems for the three\-point hyperbolic\-slice correlator, utilising a geometric reformulation of the Gel&\#x27;fand\-Shilov theory of homogeneous distributions, and the four\-point hyperbolic\-slice correlator, utilising the Dolan\-Osborn formalism\. Furthermore, I derived the reconstruction of the distributional celestial amplitudes from their leaf counterparts from a new formalism which I termed the Mellin\-Barnes representation of hyperbolic\-slice correlators, and the central observable of this new formalism is the so\-called celestial Mack correlator\. The key idea was to apply the theory of Symanzik and Mack to the Lorentzian hyperbolic\-slice correlators for gravitons\. I decided to dedicate some effort to the demonstration of the reconstruction theorems because, if I were to continue my research on flat\-space holography, I would utilise this approach as the starting point of a research programme that I regarded as a reply to Tropper&\#x27;s &\#x27;&\#x27;Challenges to Understanding Celestial Holography from the Bottom Up&\#x27;&\#x27;\. I believe that it might be possible to apply the Polyakov\-Mellin bootstrap, developed by R\. Gopakumar and A\. Sinha, to establish a Polyakov bootstrap for celestial holography, applied, however, to the hyperbolic\-slice, or leaf, amplitudes, rather than the distributional amplitudes by themselves\. In this manner, the reconstruction theorems would serve as the main tool translating between the n\-point functions generated by the celestial version of the Polyakov bootstrap and the observables appertaining to the celestial CFT\. Interestingly, it is possible to interpret Tropper&\#x27;s discussion as a subtraction ambiguity in the language of the Polyakov bootstrap\. Finally, the dynamical model that I would be proposing in this new version consists of a completion of the Abe\-Nair\-Park theory by adding a radiative sector which would provide the ‘hard’ part of the \\mathrm\{AdS\}\_\{3\} Witten diagrams of the celestial leaf amplitudes\. In this new version, I added a mathematically rigorous discussion of the ANP theory\. I also began the writing of an Appendix presenting a pedagogical introduction to the chiral determinant representation of the WZNW model, which is also incomplete\. In my forthcoming postings, I shall upload my manuscripts where I explain how to generate tree\-level \\mathrm\{N\}^\{k\}\\text\{\-MHV\} celestial amplitudes in \\mathcal\{N\}=4 SYM theory from the \\mathrm\{SL\}\(N,\\mathbf\{C\}\)/\\mathrm\{SU\}\(N\) WZNW model for all MHV levels k; how to describe celestial loop amplitudes in a celestial version of the BST formalism; how to generate those loop amplitudes from \\mathrm\{AdS\}\{3\} bosonic string theory; and how to construct coherent states for \\mathfrak\{bms\}\{3\} by employing an extension of the theory of Kirillov to groupoids that I have developed in the past year\. Proceeding in this manner, I hope to derive a certain masochistic pleasure from watching the work that I carefully cultivated and that I wrote with my own hand being lost in this jungle of artificial intelligence “slop\.” It is clear to me that my mistake was to have allowed myself to be deluded by the belief that the production of original and independent ideas and work was a necessary and sufficient condition for belonging to this community, whereas, in reality, factors completely unrelated to the latter are not only more important but also decisive\. I hope these manuscripts might be helpful to other researchers, but as Horace wrote, vitae summa brevis spem nos vetat incohare longam\.
