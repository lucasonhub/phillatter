# Gruica, Toma: Submission: Exegetical and Philosophical Commentaries on the Book of Job

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper offers an interdisciplinary reinterpretation of the Book of Job that goes beyond traditional readings centered on submission, obedience, or divine retribution\. Drawing on comparative theology, Pauline typology, and philosophical existentialism, the paper proposes that Job’s deepest cry is not for justice or reward, but for intimacy with a God who seems distant, indifferent, or even cruel\. While Islamic conceptions of submission \(islām\) frame suffering as a path to metaphysical alignment, Job rebels against the logic of passive surrender, seeking instead a Redeemer who will stand with him in the flesh\. This longing, articulated in Job 19:25\-27, is read typologically as a prefiguration of the incarnational logic found in the Gospel of John and Pauline theology, where divine love becomes accessible through mediation, embodiment, and friendship\. The article explores how Job, stripped of all worldly meaning, confronts not merely divine silence but the trauma of metaphysical abandonment, thus opening a theological space in which suffering is no longer punishment but a site of relational hope\. In refusing to be consoled by moral rationalizations, Job anticipates a theology of grace that transcends submission and redefines righteousness as the capacity to desire a God who desires us back\.
