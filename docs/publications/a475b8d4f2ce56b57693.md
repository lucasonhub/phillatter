# Sense\-Making phase transition framework for Affect

**Autores:** Miguel Bosch; Laura Fanfarillo; Gustavo Diez; Victor Gómez Mayordomo; J\. Ricardo Arias\-Gonzalez; Belén Valenzuela

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Contemporary accounts of affect emphasize different perspectives, ranging from the regulation of bodily states in allostatic approaches to the relational and sense\-making character stressed by enactive theories\. Here, we show that affect can be made explicit within the sense\-making phase transition framework previously introduced for the perception of an inner sensation\. Within this same formal structure, each stable minimum of the sense\-making potential defines an affective state: its position determines valence, while its depth determines arousal\. Affect therefore is not introduced as an independent ingredient, but emerges as the associated state in the valence\-arousal space, providing a link between allostatic and enactive approaches to affect\. This formulation distinguishes valence intensity from arousal, allows for dominant and secondary affective states, and naturally accommodates mixed\-valence configurations beyond a strictly bipolar description\. Revisiting representative landscapes of the model, we show how neutral, uncertain, polarized, and mixed\-valence regimes arise as a function of contextual sensory information, learned historical evaluation, and externally supplied information\. We further show that the resulting valence\-arousal relation provides a theoretical basis for the upper\-open V\-shaped organization discussed in the affect literature, while also opening a natural route toward a dynamical description of affect and emotions\.
