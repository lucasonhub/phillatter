# Loss Curvature as a Sampling\-Free Proxy for Memorization in Diffusion Models

**Autores:** Shashank Sule; Xinyue Yu; Boonam Shin; Yuxuan Liu; Abolfazl Hashemi; Hayden Schaeffer

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Understanding the mechanism behind why diffusion models memorize their training data remains an open question in generative modeling\. In this paper, we examine this question from two perspectives: \(a\) the model\-level perspective concerning the onset of memorization in a model during training, and \(b\) the sample\-level perspective concerning the proclivity of an individual sample to be memorized by a given model\. We show that the so\-called input loss curvature, or the Frobenius norm of the Hessian of the diffusion model&\#x27;s training loss, is an effective metric for quantifying both perspectives on memorization\. In particular, we empirically demonstrate that input curvatures averaged across training and validation samples can identify when a model is likely to start regurgitating its training data when tracked over training time\. However, this metric is not sufficient for measuring an individual sample&\#x27;s likelihood of being memorized\. To address this perspective, we introduce a counterfactual definition of memorization in diffusion models, extending the analogous notion from supervised learning proposed by \[Feldman, 2019\]\. We prove that this counterfactual memorization is controlled by the input curvature of the leave\-one\-out model, thus establishing a direct theoretical link between a sample&\#x27;s local loss landscape and its generation likelihood\. We validate our derived bounds through several empirical experiments and show that input\-dependent curvature provides a proxy for memorization\.
