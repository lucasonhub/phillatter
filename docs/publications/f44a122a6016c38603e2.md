# Effects of within\-season temporal aggregation of weather variables on performance and interpretability in rice yield machine learning models

**Autores:** Noh\-Hyun Lee; Wonjae Jeong; Hyeon\-Ji Yang; Hana Cho; Hyeon\-Yeong Ju; Toshichika Iizumi; Ki‐Tae Kim

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

While crop responses are inherently stage\-dependent, machine learning yield models often rely on weather variables aggregated over the entire growing season\. This study evaluates how within\-season temporal aggregation of weather variables affects both predictive performance and interpretability in machine learning\-based rice yield modeling\. Using CYGMA \(Crop Yield Growth Model with Assumptions on Climate and Socioeconomics\)\-simulated potential yield as a synthetic response variable, we minimized confounding effects from observational noise and management variability, enabling a clearer evaluation of the role of within\-season weather aggregation\. Daily weather variables were averaged over the rice growing period after dividing each season into multiple equal\-length segments, and several machine learning algorithms were trained and evaluated using temporally independent validation\. Increasing within\-season temporal resolution improved model performance up to a moderate level, after which gains diminished\. Interpretation using SHAP \(SHapley Additive exPlanations\) and response diagnostics consistently indicated that yield sensitivity concentrates in specific windows of the season, with temperature\-related effects emerging most strongly early in the season and humidity\-related effects later\. Finer temporal aggregation sharpened the identification of window\-specific nonlinear and threshold\-like responses that were obscured under coarse seasonal averages\. Overall, moderate within\-season aggregation provides a practical balance between predictive skill and mechanistic interpretability, supporting more reliable identification of phenology\-relevant meteorological risks for climate\-impact assessment and decision support\.
