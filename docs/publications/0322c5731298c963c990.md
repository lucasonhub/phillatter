# BEYOND SOLVENTS: SOLID\-STATE ENGINEERING STRATEGIES FOR SOLUBILITY ENHANCEMENT OF POORLY WATER\-SOLUBLE DRUGS

**Autores:** Anjani Kumar Dwivedi; Ritesh Kumar Tiwari

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Poor aqueous solubility of active pharmaceutical ingredients remains a major challenge in pharmaceutical development, as it can lead to slow dissolution, inadequate drug absorption, variable bioavailability, and reduced therapeutic performance\. Conventional solubility enhancement techniques, including micronization, solvent\-based solid dispersion, and the use of solubilizing agents, may involve limitations related to solvent consumption, processing complexity, physical instability, and environmental sustainability\. Mechanochemistry has emerged as a promising solid\-state engineering approach that uses mechanical energy to modify the physicochemical properties of drug substances, often with reduced or no solvent use\. Objective: This review aims to explore mechanochemical approaches for enhancing the solubility and dissolution performance of poorly water\-soluble drugs\. It emphasizes the fundamental mechanisms, major processing technologies, formulation strategies, and recent developments that support the application of mechanochemistry in pharmaceutical formulation development\. Methods: The review examines mechanochemical techniques, including mortar–pestle grinding, ball milling, liquid\-assisted grinding, mechanochemical cocrystallization, co\-amorphization, and polymer\-assisted solid\-state processing\. It discusses the influence of drug properties, polymeric carriers, coformers, drug\-to\-carrier ratios, and processing parameters on formulation performance\. Analytical and characterization techniques, including Fourier\-transform infrared spectroscopy, powder X\-ray diffraction, differential scanning calorimetry, and microscopic methods, are considered alongside solubility measurements, dissolution testing, and physical stability assessment\. Results: Mechanochemical processing offers considerable potential for improving the apparent solubility and dissolution rate of poorly water\-soluble drugs through particle\-size reduction, crystal lattice disruption, defect formation, amorphization, and modification of drug–excipient interactions\. The effectiveness of these approaches depends on formulation composition, processing conditions, and the physical properties of the resulting solid\. The review also highlights challenges associated with recrystallization, moisture sensitivity, process reproducibility, and the translation of laboratory\-scale methods to industrial manufacturing\. Emerging applications of process analytical technology, predictive modelling, and continuous processing provide opportunities to improve process control and scalability\. Conclusion: Mechanochemistry represents a versatile and potentially sustainable strategy for improving the pharmaceutical performance of poorly water\-soluble drugs\. Its advantages include the potential to reduce solvent consumption, minimize waste, and enable targeted solid\-state modifications\. However, successful implementation requires systematic formulation optimization, comprehensive characterization, long\-term stability evaluation, and reliable scale\-up strategies\. Further research into standardized protocols, quality control, and industrial processing is essential to establish mechanochemistry as a reproducible and practical platform for pharmaceutical development\.
