# Rethinking School Readiness: Developmental, Ecological, and Pedagogical Perspectives

**Autores:** Esther AJAYI

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

School readiness has shifted, over the past several decades, from a narrowly cognitive construct centred on pre\-academic skill to a broadly developmental construct encompassing the child&\#x27;s cognitive, linguistic, social\-emotional, motor, and regulatory capacities in relation to the expectations of the settings into which the child will transition\. The shift has been particularly consequential for young children with developmental differences, for whom readiness is not a property of the child in isolation but a property of the child\-setting fit and of the supports available to bridge the gap between current capacity and setting demand\. This paper synthesises three traditions that have shaped contemporary understanding of early childhood readiness for diverse learners: developmental psychology, with its attention to the sequenced emergence of capacity; ecological and bioecological theory, with its attention to the nested systems within which development proceeds; and developmentally appropriate practice, with its attention to the pedagogical implications of developmental understanding\. The paper argues that these three traditions are complementary rather than competing, that each supplies a distinctive perspective on readiness for young children with developmental differences, and that their integration offers a principled foundation for early childhood special education\. The synthesis is conceptual rather than empirical\. the claims advanced are offered as a scaffold for practitioner reflection and for the design of further inquiry\.
