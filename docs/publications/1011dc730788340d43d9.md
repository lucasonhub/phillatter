# The Duke Myasthenia Gravis Clinic Registry: IV \. Does Race Affect the Outcome of Treatment of Myasthenia Gravis? A Single Center Retrospective Analysis

**Autores:** Mao Liu; Chunlu Ma; Michael William Lutz; Vern C\. Juel; Janice M\. Massey; Lisa D\. Hobson‐Webb; Shruti M\. Raja; Donald B\. Sanders

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

INTRODUCTION/AIMS: Few studies have investigated the role of race in the long\-term outcome of patients with myasthenia gravis \(MG\)\. The aim of this study is to analyze clinical characteristics and treatment outcomes related to race in patients with MG\. METHODS: This is a retrospective review of data from patients seen between 1980 and 2016 and followed for at least 2 years in the Duke MG Clinic\. Treatment goal \(TG\) was defined as achieving MGFA postintervention status of &quot;minimal manifestations&quot; or better\. Multivariable logistic regression analysis and Cox proportional hazards \(CPH\) modeling were performed to assess the effect of race on TG by adjusting for other variables\. RESULTS: Among 820 patients \(661 White, 81%; 159 Black, 19%\), Black patients had a younger median age at disease onset, female predominance, higher rate of muscle\-specific tyrosine kinase antibodies and thymectomy, and less non\-corticosteroid immunosuppressant use\. The percentage of White patients in the Registry is greater than the mean in the North Carolina census between 1980 and 2010 \(81% vs\. 77%, p = 0\.014\)\. Disease duration and MGFA class at first visit, thymectomy, follow\-up duration, median time to TG, TG achievement and maintenance, median time to TG loss, and corticosteroid/PLEX/IVIG use were similar in the two racial groups\. Neither multivariable logistic regression nor CPH modeling identified race as an independent predictor of TG\. DISCUSSION: Although racial differences were observed in patients with MG, long\-term outcomes were similar in Black and White patients\. Our study suggests that following treatment guidelines is important in achieving favorable MG outcomes\.
