# Adaptive Reinforcement Learning\-Based Dynamic Scheduling for Steel Reheating Furnaces: A Scalable Queue\-Driven Framework

**Autores:** Yanhe Jia; aowen liu

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Reheating furnace scheduling problem \(RFSP\) is a critical issue in hot\-rolling production, where stochastic order arrivals and service processes pose significant challenges to production efficiency and cost management\. The RFSP is plagued by inherent stochasticity in order arrivals and service times\. This study proposes an adaptive queue\-driven dynamic adjustment scheduling \(AQ\-DAS\) based on reinforcement learning for dynamic reheating furnace scheduling\. The system is modeled as an M/PH/C queueing system and formulated as a Markov decision process \(MDP\)\. By incorporating adaptive learning\-rate and adaptive exploration mechanisms into Q\-learning, the proposed method aims to minimize long\-term operating costs while balancing queueing, idle, and switching costs\. Simulation experiments are conducted to compare AQ\-DAS with CBS2, Q\-learning, SARSA, Double Q\-learning, DQN, and PPO\. For the five\-furnace system, AQ\-DAS achieves the lowest mean total operating cost, reducing it by 76\.6%, 12\.5%, 43\.6%, 37\.9%, 10\.2%, and 18\.6%, respectively\. It also maintains competitive throughput and high furnace utilization and substantially reducing switching frequency\. Sensitivity analysis shows that AQ\-DAS remains competitive under variations in the relative weights of queueing, idle, and switching costs\. Robustness experiments under demand fluctuations and equipment disturbances demonstrate low ARPDs of 6\.04%, 13\.47%, and 10\.84% for demand, equipment, and overall conditions, respectively\. Furthermore, scalability experiments with 5–20 furnaces demonstrate that AQ\-DAS maintains its cost advantage as the system scale increases, with cost reductions ranging from 8\.8% to 79\.5% across the benchmark methods, while requiring substantially less training time than DQN and PPO\. The proposed framework provides a scalable alternative to analytical optimization for high\-dimensional reheating furnace scheduling problems and offers a data\-driven decision\-support approach for dynamic capacity adjustment in steel production\.
