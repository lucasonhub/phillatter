# Does psychology have a truth\-telling problem? Survivance as truth\-telling pedagogy in psychology education

**Autores:** Louis P\. A\. Busch

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Truth\-telling and reparative praxis have become central concerns in higher education, particularly in settler\-colonial contexts where public institutions have denied, minimized, or excluded histories of violence, dispossession, and exploitation\. Psychology education holds a specific responsibility within this work as psychological research, assessment, diagnosis, and practice have participated in colonial projects through racialized theories of intelligence and health, clinical pathologization of systemic harms, extractive research, and training models that position Euro\-Western knowledge as universal\. Symbolic response and deficit framing limit reconciliation\-oriented teaching when institutional language, course content, and professional training leave colonial authority intact or position Indigenous Peoples primarily through harm\. This article adapts survivance and health survivance scholarship into a pedagogical framework to guide curriculum design, testimony protocols, case teaching, research assignments, supervision discussions, and assessment in psychology education\. Practical tools, including a survivance\-informed planning matrix and a psycholonization audit, are proposed to help students examine colonial authority in ordinary psychological practices and revise professional tools and approaches accordingly\. Ultimately, reparative truth\-telling in psychology education must extend beyond awareness and content inclusion toward accountable changes in professional preparation, institutional practice, and relationships with Indigenous Peoples\.
