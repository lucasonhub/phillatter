# To construct a model for elderly care services for multiethnic minorities in Kunming, Yunnan Province: a cross\-sectional study

**Autores:** Fangli Ma; Hongyong Fu; Siriluck Jittrabiab; Thanawat Imsomboon

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Population aging has become a major global public health and social issue, and China is one of the countries experiencing the most rapid population aging\. According to recent demographic statistics, the proportion of older adults in China continues to increase, creating substantial challenges for the country&\#x27;s health care and elderly care systems\. There may be a significant association between overall functional ability and elderly care satisfaction among rural older adults\. Objective: It was to examine the relationship between functional ability and elderly care satisfaction among rural older adults and to provide evidence for developing interventions aimed at improving functional ability and enhancing subjective satisfaction with elderly care\. Methods: A cross\-sectional survey was conducted among 433 older adults aged 65 years and above in ethnic villages of Yunnan Province, China\. Descriptive statistics were used to characterize demographic characteristics, living conditions, and functional ability\. Chi\-square tests and one\-way analysis of variance \(ANOVA\) were employed to examine the associations between demographic factors, functional ability, and elderly care satisfaction\. Results: One\-way ANOVA showed significant differences in overall functional ability among older adults with different levels of elderly care satisfaction \(F = 3\.047, p = 0\.029\), indicating that higher functional ability was associated with greater satisfaction with elderly care\. Conclusion: Functional ability is an important determinant of elderly care satisfaction among older adults in rural ethnic villages\. Improving older adults&\#x27; functional ability through health support, daily care services, and skill training may effectively enhance their subjective satisfaction with elderly care and improve their overall quality of later life\.
