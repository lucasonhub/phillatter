# An explainable machine learning framework with trend\-sensitive features for predicting high\-potential merchants in mobile payment ecosystems

**Autores:** JrJung Lyu; Chun\-Hung Hsieh

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Mobile payment platforms face a persistent decision\-support challenge: payment service providers \(PSPs\) must identify high\-potential merchants from large merchant bases without relying solely on retrospective transaction scale\. This study develops an explainable decision\-support framework for merchant prioritization by combining growth\-trajectory feature engineering, XGBoost classification, and SHAP\-based interpretability\. Using 61,280 merchant\-month records from 3,939 merchants of a leading payment service provider between January 2022 and March 2025, the framework predicts merchant potential while translating model outputs into decision\-usable explanations\. Merchant potential is operationalized through a polar\-group label: merchants in the top 20% of cumulative future gross merchandise value \(GMV\) over the prediction horizon are treated as high\-potential and those in the bottom 20% as low\-potential, so the term “high\-potential” denotes this top\-quantile future\-GMV definition rather than current transaction size\. To establish methodological rigor, the framework is benchmarked against rule\-based, linear, ensemble, and stacking baselines, and a feature\-group ablation is conducted to isolate the contribution of trend\-sensitive variables\. The proposed framework attains strong predictive performance on the held\-out test set \(AUC = 0\.841; AP = 0\.868; F1 = 0\.783; Precision@100 = 0\.91; Lift@40% = 1\.61\) while outperforming logistic regression, random forest, LightGBM, raw\-GMV XGBoost, and rule\-based screening on operational precision\. SHAP analysis indicates that the nine\-month GMV trend slope and the six\-month average GMV jointly shape merchant potential, with growth trajectory providing incremental predictive value beyond static scale\. The study contributes to merchant analytics by reframing explainability as a structured, auditable decision\-support layer rather than as a supplementary visualization\. For PSPs, the proposed framework supports transparent merchant prioritization, internal auditability, and targeted resource allocation while avoiding overdependence on opaque prediction scores or simple GMV\-based heuristics\. As boundary conditions, the results are drawn from a single PSP and a polar\-group \(top\-versus\-bottom\-quantile\) sample rather than the full merchant population, and the decision\-support layer is assessed structurally through mapping consistency and rank stability rather than through user studies, formal audits, or measured business outcomes\.
