# The final count\-down: estimating the total value of all the world&\#x27;s natural resources and an estimate of how long they may last

**Autores:** Harald Ulrik Sverdrup; Anders Nordby; Ole van Allen; Anna Hulda Ólafsdóttir; Kristín Vala Ragnarsdóttir; Wouter Van Dieren

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction We have attempted a final count\-down of all resources available to human society in future and converted this to monetary value for 2026\. Methods Simulations using the WORLD7 Integrated Assessment Model explore how long the available resources will support wealth and value creation in the global society\. Results The estimates made suggests that about 75% of the world&\#x27;s value creation has its origin in natural resources, and about 2 / 3 of the value creation has its origin in finite, fossil natural resources, fossil energy, fossil metals, and fossil materials\. Our results suggests that a system reconfiguration, including thorium\-based technologies, would be required for making society last more than 1,000 generations\. The most valuable resources in analysis are the energy resources, but these are partly dependent on the availability of technology metals and materials\. Thorium appears to be the single most valuable energy resource on the list, provided thorium\-based power plants are adopted, and based on the energy price\. The gross revenue from resource extraction and the profit value of the annual production was estimated\. The recoverable resource stocks of natural resources and the annual extraction rates were estimated\. Discussion Our estimates were combined with earlier estimates of the value of the ecosystems on the Earth and estimates of the value of the geo\- and biosphere to society\. The model simulations point to the importance of a long\-term material circularity, minimizing losses of material and preserving resource quality with time\. The simulation outputs suggest the material resources will last about 250–400 years from the global industrialization in 1900 to about 2350\. It appears that adjusting the settings of the present system is not sufficient for a fully sustainable society\. The final conclusion is that natural resources are root currency of the human civilization, equivalent to what we call money, concurrent with Odums 1996 seminal book\.
