# A lightweight knowledge distillation framework for few shot unsupervised industrial surface anomaly detection and localization

**Autores:** Xian Gao

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Industrial surface anomaly detection under data\-scarce, unsupervised conditions requires models that are both label\-efficient and computationally lightweight\. This paper presents LKDF\-AD, a knowledge\-distillation framework combining a frozen pretrained teacher with a compact student network \(385,168 parameters\), trained using only k = 1–10 normal reference images per category, without any labeled anomalies\. Evaluated across 15 MVTec AD categories and five shot counts, the framework achieves 77\.88–86\.19% image\-level and 90\.59–94\.87% pixel\-level AUROC, and generalizes to the independent BTAD benchmark \(73\.6–90\.99% image\-level AUROC\), with a larger BTAD pixel\-AUROC/PRO gap flagged as an open question\. Measured end\-to\-end \(teacher plus student together, as deployed\), the framework uses 1\.93× fewer parameters \(11\.56 M vs\. 22\.35 M\) and achieves 1\.57× lower inference latency \(4\.436 ms vs\. 6\.954 ms\) than a full\-size STFPM\-style baseline trained under an identical protocol\. Under category\-level paired testing \(using 15 independent category means, the statistically appropriate unit given repeated measurements across shot counts\), neither the image\-level nor the pixel\-level advantage over STFPM reaches statistical significance \(two\-sided p = 0\.7197 and p = 0\.188, respectively\)\. Consequently, the framework’s established advantage over STFPM is its directly measured computational efficiency \(1\.93× fewer end\-to\-end parameters, 1\.57× lower end\-to\-end latency\), rather than accuracy\. In contrast, against the RegAD\-inspired baseline, both accuracy advantages remain highly significant under the same corrected test \(two\-sided p = 0\.00201 for image\-level and p = 0\.00153 for pixel\-level\)\. A random, completely untrained student achieves only near\-chance performance \(52\.7% image\-level, 46\.6% pixel\-level AUROC\), confirming that the observed localization signal reflects genuine distillation rather than an architectural artifact\. A dedicated investigation of the framework’s weakest category \(screw, near\-chance performance\) tested a rotation\-sensitivity hypothesis directly across the full test set; the result was a significant correlation in the direction opposite to that hypothesized, and we report the mechanism as unresolved rather than settled\. These results position the framework as a genuinely lightweight, empirically characterized — rather than definitively superior — approach to few\-shot unsupervised industrial anomaly detection\.
