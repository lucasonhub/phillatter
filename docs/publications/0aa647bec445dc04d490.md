# Exploring immune\-endothelial interactions in neuroinflammation and vascular dysfunction

**Autores:** Zitian Wang; Zezheng Kang; Lei Wu; Jinglin Hu; Xinhua Chen; Tuoxian Tang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Neuroinflammation and cerebrovascular endothelial dysfunction are traditionally studied as parallel processes confined to individual disease categories\. Yet a growing body of evidence indicates that they operate as components of a tightly coupled, self\-amplifying circuit: the immune\-endothelial\-neural axis\. Within this axis, the peripheral immune system, cerebrovascular endothelium, and neural networks continuously modulate one another across disease contexts\. This Review summarizes current knowledge on the bidirectional immune\-endothelial interactions that underlie blood\-brain barrier disruption and neuroinflammation across cerebrovascular disorders, neuroimmune diseases, and neurodegenerative conditions\. A systems\-level comparison across disease categories identifies shared pathological hallmarks alongside disease\-specific signatures of tempo, vascular scale, and regional vulnerability\. Emerging therapeutic strategies are considered along the three limbs of the axis: immunomodulation, endothelial protection and barrier stabilization, and neural recovery, including traditional Chinese medicine as a multitarget approach\. By framing neurological disorders through the immune\-endothelial\-neural axis, this Review proposes a unifying framework that bridges disease boundaries and identifies common mechanistic targets for future translational research\. The framework is offered as a testable proposition in which the relative contribution of each limb differs between disorders\.
