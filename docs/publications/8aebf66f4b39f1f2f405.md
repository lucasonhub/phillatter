# Artificial Intelligence Adoption in African Academic Libraries: Opportunities, Constraints, and a Framework for Context\-Sensitive Implementation

**Autores:** Olabisi Dorcas OGUNGBENRO; Omolabake Eunice Akinyemi; Blessing Hunbadon Asue

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence \(AI\) technologies is transforming scholarly communication and knowledge\-sharing practices in academic libraries worldwide\. However, academic libraries in Nigeria and other African contexts continue to experience challenges related to limited visibility of research outputs, fragmented dissemination systems, uneven institutional repository development, information overload, and restricted access to scholarly resources\. This paper presents a conceptual and analytical review of the strategic role of AI in strengthening scholarly communication and knowledge\-sharing practices in African academic libraries\. Drawing on recent scholarly literature, policy documents, and conceptual perspectives on digital transformation, the study examines the application of emerging AI technologies, including machine learning, natural language processing, recommender systems, and research analytics tools, in enhancing knowledge organisation, research discovery, open access integration, repository visibility, collaboration, and research evaluation\. The paper further analyses contextual barriers affecting AI adoption, including inadequate digital infrastructure, funding limitations, professional capacity gaps, algorithmic bias, data privacy concerns, and weak institutional policy frameworks\. Based on these analyses, the paper proposes a context\-sensitive AI adoption framework that integrates technological readiness, institutional capacity, ethical governance, and policy support as essential dimensions for sustainable scholarly communication transformation\. The paper concludes that strategic investment in digital infrastructure, librarian capacity development, inclusive data governance, and coordinated institutional policies is critical for ensuring that AI contributes to equitable, accessible, and sustainable knowledge\-sharing ecosystems in African academic environments\.
