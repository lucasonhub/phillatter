# Prevalence and Antibacterial Susceptibility Pattern of Bacterial Isolates from Patients with External Ocular Infections in Three Tertiary Hospitals in Anambra State, Nigeria

**Autores:** 

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The human eye serves as the main sensory organ for vision, enabling perception of the world in remarkable detail\.Ocular infections are common and their morbidity can vary from mild, self\-limiting to sight\-threatening infections\.These infections have been known to cut across various age groups and gender\.The study aimed to determine the prevalence and antibacterial susceptibility pattern of bacterial isolates from external ocular infections in three tertiary hospitals in Anambra State, Nigeria: A hospital based cross\-sectional study was conducted at three tertiary hospitals in Anambra State\.A total of 384 ocular specimens were collected\.The bacteria were isolated using culture methods and identified by biochemical and molecular methods\.Antibiotic susceptibility test was carried out using disc diffusion techniques\.A structured questionnaire was used to obtain socio\-demographic characteristics of the patients\.Demographic distribution revealed that bacterial isolation was statistically significant in gender, residence and occupation but not significant across the different age groups\.Bacterial isolates were identified as Staphylococcus epidermidis, Staphylococcus aureus, Klebsiella variicola and Escherichia coli\.Staphylococcus aureus was the most prevalent pathogen \(47\.3%\) and conjunctivitis was the most common bacterial external ocular infection \(43\.6%\)\.The antibacterial susceptibility test revealed that the S\. aureus and S\. epidermidis were 100% sensitive to vancomycin, chloramphenicol and cefotaxime\.E\. coli was 100% sensitive to all tested antibacterial drugs except chloramphenicol, ceftriaxone and cefotaxime and K\. variicola was 100% sensitive to all except vancomycin and ciprofloxacin\.The prevalence of ocular infection was high with Conjunctivitis being the dominant\.Gram positive bacteria accounted for 82% of isolates with S\. aureus being the predominant isolate\.Vancomycin, chloramphenicol and cefotaxime were most effective against gram positive isolates while the penicillins, aminoglycosides and levofloxacin were most effective against gram negative isolates\.Routine bacterial diagnosis using culture, biochemical and molecular tests is essential for guiding empirical and definitive treatments and limiting antibiotic resistance in external ocular infections\.
