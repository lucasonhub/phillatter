# USER VERIFICATION THROUGH CALLIGRAPHIC NOISE ANALYSIS IN HANDWRITTEN SIGNATURES

**Autores:** André Luis Borsato Sanchez; Rodrigo Capobianco Guido

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Biometric user verification assesses whether an observed behavioral sample is consistent with a claimed identity\. Acoustic signals generated during handwritten signatures may convey information about the interaction among the pen, paper, and supporting surface\. This study examines a calligraphic\-noise verification architecture using 260 recordings from ten participants, evenly divided between development and external evaluation\. Signal representation combines the temporal descriptors A3 and B3, based on normalized positions of cumulative energy and zero crossings, yielding 38 features\. Two processing chains, with and without the discrete wavelet transform, and three decision strategies were investigated: a radial\-basis\-function support vector machine, a random forest, and weighted score fusion with weights of 0\.75 and 0\.25\. Decision thresholds were calibrated under a policy designed to limit false acceptances\. In the external evaluation of the chain without wavelet processing, score fusion accepted 92 genuine and eight impostor attempts, producing false rejection and false acceptance rates of 29\.23% and 0\.68%, respectively\. The random forest produced only four false acceptances but rejected 56 genuine attempts\. The findings support the experimental applicability of the architecture within the investigated acquisition setting, but do not establish across\-session generalization or isolate the contributions of individual descriptors
