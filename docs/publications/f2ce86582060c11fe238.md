# Integrating Generative Artificial Intelligence into Institutional Arbitration: A Comparative Analysis of Procedural Readiness and Due Process Between India and Singapore

**Autores:** Surya Saxena; Mr\. Deepak Bansal; M Taneja; Mr\. Vishal Bansal; Ms\. Akriti Gupta

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence \(GenAI\) into international commercial arbitration introduces transformative procedural efficiencies alongside complex legal and constitutional challenges\. This comprehensive research paper explores the regulatory, procedural, and doctrinal implications of utilizing Large Language Models \(LLMs\) and automated legal reasoning tools within arbitral decision\-making workflows\. By establishing a robust comparative framework between India and Singapore, the study critically evaluates how institutional rules, statutory mandates, and judicial oversight manage the intersection of cutting\-edge technology and fundamental guarantees of natural justice\. The paper begins by contrasting Singapore’s mature, digitally integrated arbitral ecosystem—anchored by the SIAC 2025 Rules and advanced institutional governance protocols—with India’s rapidly evolving domestic framework comprising institutions such as the India International Arbitration Centre \(IIAC\) and the Mumbai Centre for International Arbitration \(MCIA\)\. It investigates the non\-delegation doctrine, examining whether over\-reliance on AI\-generated legal reasoning and draft awards violates an arbitrator&\#x27;s core duty of independent evaluation under Section 34 of the Indian Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, and Singapore’s International Arbitration Act\. Furthermore, the study analyzes severe cybersecurity and data governance vulnerabilities, contrasting India&\#x27;s Digital Personal Data Protection \(DPDP\) Act compliance requirements with Singapore&\#x27;s commercial data protocols during cross\-border digital discovery\. Finally, the research evaluates award enforceability hurdles under the New York Convention framework\. It scrutinizes how national courts handle procedural challenges related to algorithmic opacity, data hallucinations, and public policy exceptions\. Ultimately, the paper provides actionable regulatory reform recommendations and institutional guidelines for emerging arbitral seats to safeguard due process integrity and procedural fairness while safely embracing technological innovation\.
