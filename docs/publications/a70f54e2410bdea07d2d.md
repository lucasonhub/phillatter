# Tailoring soy protein isolate–alginate double emulsion composite gel microcapsules for cheese\-like texture and probiotic protection in plant\-based cheese analogues

**Autores:** Toshifumi Udo; Ethan He; Gopinath Mummaleti; Yang Jiao; Jinru Chen; Rakesh K\. Singh; Fanbin Kong

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

While the market for plant\-based dairy alternatives is expanding rapidly, most products mainly focus on the sensory attributes of traditional dairy items and lack functional fortification, such as the incorporation of probiotic bacteria\. The objective of this study was to develop and characterize novel water\-in\-oil\-in\-water \(W/O/W\) double emulsion\-based gelled microcapsules for Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG \(LRGG\) using a soy protein isolate \(SPI\) \(3\.0%–6\.0% w/w\) and sodium alginate \(SA\) \(0\.5%–2\.0% w/w\) composite with Ca 2\+ cross\-linking, aiming to tailor and customize the microcapsules for cheese\-like textural characteristics from spread to semi\-hard types, while preserving probiotic viability for incorporation into plant\-based cheese analogues\. Using Response Surface Methodology \(RSM\) under a 3 2 full factorial design with center\-point replication, the complex interactions between the wall materials and their effects on physical, textural, and functional properties were systematically evaluated\. The results revealed that SA concentration strongly enhanced bulk firmness and particle size \( D 50 \) through robust calcium cross\-linking\. In contrast, SPI played a crucial role in providing protection against simulated gastrointestinal fluids during digestion testing\. Ultimately, this study demonstrates that the SPI\-SA system bridges the gap between macroscopic structural integrity and biological viability, providing a tunable structural base for probiotic\-fortified plant\-based cheese analogues\.
