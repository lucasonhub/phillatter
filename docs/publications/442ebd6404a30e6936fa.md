# JAUNDICE FORM OF LEPTOSPIROSIS IN OBSTETRIC PRACTICE: A CLINICAL CASE OF MATERNAL DEATH

**Autores:** Viacheslav M\. Husiev

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction\. The icteric form of leptospirosis in pregnant women is rare but severe, potentially leading to multi\-organ failure and a high risk of maternal and fetal death\. Clinical manifestations are often nonspecific and may mimic preeclampsia or other pregnancy complications, complicating early diagnosis\.Aim\. To increase medical professionals’ awareness of the clinical course and pathomorphological findings of icteric leptospirosis in pregnancy to support timely diagnosis and clinical management\.Materials and methods\. A retrospective analysis was conducted of a maternal death in a 25\-week pregnant woman with the icteric form of leptospirosis\. The study included anamnesis, clinical manifestations, laboratory findings, and autopsy results, with macro\- and histological examination of the liver, kidneys, lungs, and placenta\.Results\. On admission, the patient had jaundice, hyperthermia \(39\.2 °C\), tachycardia, arterial hypotension, and oliguria\. Laboratory tests indicated severe liver and kidney injury \(bilirubin 175 µmol/L, ALT 86 U/L, AST 95 U/L, creatinine 149 µmol/L, urea 15\.8 mmol/L, thrombocytopenia 74×10⁹/L, leukocytes 15\.4×10⁹/L, C\-reactive protein 18 mg/L\)\. Despite intensive therapy, multi\-organ failure, disseminated intravascular coagulation \(DIC\), and septic shock developed, resulting in death\. Autopsy confirmed a severe infectious\-toxic process: focal hepatic necrosis, dystrophic and necrotic changes in the kidneys, venous congestion and pulmonary edema, and severe infectious\-inflammatory damage of the placenta with villous edema, trophoblast necrosis, and extensive hemorrhages\.Conclusions\. Clinical, laboratory, and morphological findings indicate a severe course of the icteric form of leptospirosis in pregnancy and confirm the link between systemic infection, impaired uteroplacental circulation, and maternal death\. Early involvement of an infectious disease specialist and intensive therapy is critically important\.
