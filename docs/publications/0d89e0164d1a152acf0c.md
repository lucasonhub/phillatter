# Scalable and Trustworthy Machine Learning for Spatiotemporal Events: From Neural Point Processes to Foundation Models

**Autores:** Zihao Zhou

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Spatiotemporal events, such as earthquakes and aftershocks, epidemic case reports, geotagged social activity, and vehicle trajectories, are discrete occurrences in continuous space and time\. They arrive at irregular intervals, interact across space, and depend strongly on their history, properties that defeat models built for regularly\-sampled data\. This dissertation develops machine learning methods for such events across two eras of the field, unified by a single concern: trustworthiness in both models and evidence\. In the first era, neural point processes faced a fundamental obstacle: the likelihood contains an intractable integral of the intensity over space and time, and approximating it numerically places error inside every gradient step\. This dissertation refuses that compromise\. DeepSTPP sidesteps the integral by design, decoding a deep latent process into a kernel\-mixture intensity whose integral is available in closed form, which accelerates training by two orders of magnitude over Neural\-ODE baselines\. Auto\-NPP computes temporal likelihood integrals exactly for free\-form neural intensities through automatic integration: parameterize the antiderivative and recover the intensity by differentiation\. Its spatiotemporal extension, AutoSTPP, reduces the volume integral to line integrals\. Under the stated regularity and identifiability conditions, the derivative\-network class retains its approximation capacity, and maximum\-likelihood estimation consistently recovers the true influence function\. In the second era, foundation models arrived, shifting the bottleneck from model construction to evidence about capability\. A controlled, hypothesis\-driven study of large language models in time series anomaly detection tests several standing conjectures\. For most tested anomaly types, models detect anomalies more accurately from plots than from numeric text; chain\-of\-thought prompting does not improve detection; and the tested repetition\- and addition\-based accounts do not predict the observed behavior, which varies sharply across model families\. STEventsBench then provides the first standardized benchmark for anomaly detection on irregular\-interval spatiotemporal events, aggregating more than 45 million public records across nine public datasets with controlled anomaly injection and unified evaluation\. Exact event models and anomaly detectors can therefore be compared under one protocol\. Together, these contributions argue that trustworthy machine learning for spatiotemporal events rests on exact likelihoods for models and controlled, reproducible measurement for claims\.
