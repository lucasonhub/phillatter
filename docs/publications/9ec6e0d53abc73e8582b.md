# Single‐Cell Resolution Mapping of Osteoarthritis‐Associated Chondrocyte Dynamics Using a High‐Density CMOS MEA Platform

**Autores:** Neha Deshpande; Pauline Coquart; Bram Theuns; Susanne Cremery; Jaime Rodolfo V\. Garcia III; Julien Verplanken; Dennis Lambrechts; Dries Braeken; Liesbet Lagae; Johanna Bolander

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Fibrosis is a major cause of chronic tissue dysfunction and contributes substantially to morbidity and mortality worldwide\. Osteoarthritis \(OA\), affecting over 590 million people, is a prevalent fibrotic joint disease for which disease\-modifying therapies remain lacking, partly because disease progression remains incompletely understood\. As human\-relevant in vitro models, including organ\-on\-chip and microphysiological systems, become increasingly important, non\-invasive technologies capable of continuous cellular monitoring are urgently needed\. Here, we implement high\-density complementary metal\-oxide\-semiconductor \(CMOS\) multielectrode array \(MEA\)\-based impedance profiling as a label\-free platform for longitudinal assessment of OA\-relevant chondrocyte remodeling\. Human primary chondrocytes cultured on CMOS\-MEA chips exhibited impedance increases that correlated with electrode occupancy and extracellular matrix \(ECM\) deposition\. Spatial impedance mapping enabled visualization of injury\-response dynamics, while oxidative stress induced progressive impedance decline accompanied by apoptosis, morphological remodeling, and chondrocyte dedifferentiation, evidenced by reduced collagen II and SOX9 expression and increased collagen I expression\. In contrast, osteogenic de\-differentiation produced increased impedance associated with ECM remodeling and matrix mineralization\. Together, these findings support high\-density CMOS\-MEA impedance profiling as a non\-invasive approach for monitoring cellular remodeling and provide a foundation for future integration of spatially resolved impedance sensing into advanced microphysiological systems for OA and other fibrotic diseases\.
