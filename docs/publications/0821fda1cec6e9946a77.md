# Experimental and technoeconomic comparison of honeycomb pad and earthen pot evaporative cooling for photovoltaic panels

**Autores:** Vednath P\. Kalbande; Man Mohan; Kishor S\. Rambhad; Amol Bhikaji Kakade; Laukik Purushottam Raut; Byungmin Ahn

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract This study experimentally compares an uncooled photovoltaic \(PV\) panel with two low‐cost evaporative cooling configurations under identical outdoor conditions in Nagpur, India\. A system comprises a pump assisted honeycomb‐pad evaporative cooling system \(PV‐HCEC\) and a passive earthen pot evaporative water‐cooling system \(PV‐EPEWC\)\. PV‐HCEC was tested at water flow rates of 1, 3, and 5 LPM, whereas PV‐EPEWC operated through natural thermosyphon circulation\. The reference panel reached maximum internal and front surface temperatures of 63 and 68°C, respectively\. At 5 LPM, PV‐HCEC reduced these temperatures to 49 and 47°C, while PV‐EPEWC reduced them to 54 and 52°C\. The maximum electrical efficiency increased from 10\.71% for the reference panel to 18\.06% for PV‐HCEC and 16\.01% for PV‐EPEWC\. The maximum thermal and overall efficiencies were 69\.65% and 82\.89% for PV‐HCEC and 62\.97% and 75\.80% for PV‐EPEWC, respectively\. PV‐HCEC achieved an electricity cost of 5\.44 INR kWh −1 , an annualized LCOE of 7\.06 INR kWh −1 , a simple payback period of 6\.258 years, an energy payback time of 1\.52 years, and net CO 2 mitigation of 6\.16 t year −1 \. The results demonstrate that PV‐HCEC provides stronger thermal and techno‐economic performance, whereas PV‐EPEWC offers a simpler pump‐free option for decentralized and resource‐constrained applications\.
