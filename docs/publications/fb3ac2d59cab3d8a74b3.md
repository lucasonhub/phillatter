# Institutional fragmentation and rule\-of\-law coordination in the Guangdong\-Hong Kong\-Macao Greater Bay Area: a maritime soft power perspective

**Autores:** Juanjuan Wan

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction International assessments of maritime cities have shifted from hard infrastructure indicators toward institutional quality metrics such as maritime finance and law and attractiveness and competitiveness\. The Guangdong\-Hong Kong\-Macao Greater Bay Area \(GBA\), with its distinctive “one country, two systems, and three jurisdictions” configuration, faces gaps in cross\-jurisdictional rule connectivity that may limit its maritime soft power—a challenge that existing single\-jurisdiction assessment frameworks do not fully capture, highlighting the need for rule\-of\-law coordination\. Methods Using The Leading Maritime Cities of the World assessment framework as a benchmark, this policy and practice review combines a structured review of assessment data with comparative legal analysis to identify the mechanisms through which gaps in cross\-jurisdictional rule connectivity may affect the maritime soft power of GBA cities, as reflected in their international assessment results\. Results GBA cities display a split profile of maritime strengths—strong port performance in the Mainland port cities and comparatively stronger maritime finance and law capacity in Hong Kong—that is consistent with incomplete cross\-jurisdictional complementarity\. Three mechanisms are identified: uncertainty in the application of law, regulatory arbitrage and competition in standards, and the costs of judicial coordination\. In response, three institutional arrangements are proposed: a rule\-of\-law coordination pilot zone, a marine environmental dispute resolution center, and a cross\-border data interoperability mechanism\. Discussion Drawing on cross\-jurisdictional governance practices in the Rhine–Scheldt basin and the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey, the article argues that “rule\-of\-law coordination” merits consideration as a regional indicator in international maritime\-city assessment systems\. The proposed framework addresses a gap in current evaluations of multi\-jurisdictional regions\. The analysis identifies plausible mechanisms consistent with the observed ranking patterns rather than estimating causal effects\.
