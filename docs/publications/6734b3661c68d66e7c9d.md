# Forest green\-up in the Pearl River Basin: trends, driving factors, and relationships with net primary productivity

**Autores:** Shijie Yang; Zhaoju Zheng; Xueming Zhao; Zhipeng Zhang; Xiaohui Cui; Yuan Zeng

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The spring green\-up date \(GUD\), a critical indicator of ecosystem response to climate change, profoundly influences terrestrial productivity\. However, the complex drivers of GUD remain elusive, while the contentious relationship between GUD and net primary productivity \(NPP\) challenges the universal validity of the hypothesis that earlier green\-up enhances productivity\. Using MODIS NDVI data \(2000\-2020\), we quantified forest GUD dynamics in the Pearl River Basin and combined random forest, SHAP, and structural equation modelling to identify drivers and assess GUD\-NPP relationships\. GUD was advanced by approximately 5 days, with 81\.8% of forestland pixels advancing and 18\.2% delaying\. Only 5\.39% of advances showed significant trends, whereas 30\.45% of delays were concentrated near croplands and developed areas\. The random forest model explained 58\.2% of GUD variation\. Elevation, precipitation, and soil potassium and phosphorus were dominant; elevation\-induced thermal gradients outweighed ambient temperature effects, with elevations above 1,200 m exhibiting markedly delayed GUD\. Spatially, GUD had a positive correlation with NPP, indicating that later GUD was associated with higher forest NPP, possibly because of early\-spring cold snaps and frost\. Temporally, areas with stronger GUD delays tended to show greater NPP increases\. This study identified key controls on subtropical forest phenology and revealed a GUD\-NPP trade\-off\.
