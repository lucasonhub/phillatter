# Musaget, Navi: The Symbiotic Author as an Institutional Category

**Autores:**

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_\. 2026Governance of generative AI increasingly distinguishes among AI\-generated content, AI\-assisted content, human\-authored text, disclosure, and eligibility, yet these categories often classify different institutional objects\. This paper proposes the Symbiotic Author as a work\-relative human authorship category for cases in which an identifiable human author participates in a materially consequential, bidirectionally conditioned creative relation with a generative system\. The category does not depend on the percentage of machine\-generated language retained in the final artifact\. A work may contain no surviving machine\-generated prose while its development has been materially shaped through sustained human–system interaction; conversely, extensive machine\-generated prose may result from one\-directional generation without a qualifying symbiotic relation\. The framework distinguishes textual provenance from creative causal provenance and treats substantive human uptake, redirection, rejection, and other forms of causal participation as analytically relevant even when machine output is not retained\. It further separates the historical classification of the creative relation from declaration, creative provenance, evidentiary corroboration, and institutional eligibility\. On this account, disclosure and surviving records make a process institutionally legible but do not constitute the process itself, while eligibility remains an independent normative decision of the adopting institution\. The paper develops boundary cases, an anti\-anthropomorphism constraint, a proportionate corroboration model, and a compact operational protocol for journals, prizes, publishers, and related cultural institutions\. The proposal does not treat generative systems as human\-equivalent co\-authors and does not determine copyrightability, originality, literary quality, or institutional admission\. Its narrower contribution is an institutional architecture for describing the human author when materially consequential human–AI interaction becomes part of a work’s causal development without displacing the human authorial locus\. \(direct link \)
