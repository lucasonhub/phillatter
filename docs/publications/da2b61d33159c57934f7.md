# Neuronal\-Like Negative Capacitance of Biofilms from Common Microbial Pathogens Using Electrical Impedance Spectroscopy

**Autores:** Adhvaidhi Kuntran; Victor Carneiro da Cunha Martorelli; Malek Arnold; Rok Krašovec; Ian S\. Roberts; Thomas Andrew Waigh

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract We studied electrical impedance spectroscopy of biofilms from Gram\-negative bacteria, Gram\-positive bacteria and a fungus: Escherichia coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Enterococcus faecalis, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Staphylococcus epidermidis and Candida albicans\. We show that low\-frequency negative capacitance appears across all species under small DC bias voltages\. The negative capacitance is not organism\-specific, but rather a general property of biofilm\-electrode systems driven by non\-equilibrium ionic and interfacial processes\. An equivalent circuit model incorporating a constant phase element and an inductive branch reproduces the impedance response, capturing both interfacial heterogeneity and delayed ionic dynamics\. While interfacial electrode–electrolyte coupling shapes the spectra, the behaviour is consistent with biologically regulated ion transport\. Previous work demonstrated that ion channel–mediated electrical activity in E\. coli biofilms can generate inductive\-like responses, providing a mechanistic contribution for the observed negative capacitance\. These results establish negative capacitance as a robust and broadly conserved feature of microbial biofilms\. Electrical sensors could be optimized to measure negative capacitance as a proxy for the formation of living biofilms e\.g\. to detect the biofouling of catheters\.
