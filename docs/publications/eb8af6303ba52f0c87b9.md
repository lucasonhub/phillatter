# Self\-reported understanding and attitudes of healthcare workers and caregivers toward artificial intelligence in pediatric healthcare in a children’s hospital in China: a cross\-sectional study

**Autores:** Siyu Chen; Yang Li; Qi\-Gang Ding; Dong\-Xin Huang; Qiuyu Tang; Xue Kong

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective To investigate the self\-reported understanding and attitudes of healthcare workers and caregivers toward the application of artificial intelligence \(AI\) in pediatric healthcare in a children’s specialty hospital, and to compare differences between the two groups\. Methods This single\-center cross\-sectional questionnaire survey was conducted at Fujian Children’s Hospital from January 2026 to June 2026\. A self\-designed questionnaire was used to assess healthcare workers’ and caregivers’ self\-reported understanding, trust, risk perception, and willingness to accept AI applications in pediatric healthcare\. Respondents were divided into groups according to their identity\. Categorical variables were compared using the chi\-square test or Fisher’s exact test, while continuous variables were compared using the independent\-samples t test or the Mann–Whitney U test\. Results A total of 261 respondents were included, comprising 159 caregivers and 102 healthcare workers\. Healthcare workers had a higher level of self\-reported understanding and a higher frequency of use of AI applications in pediatric healthcare than caregivers \( p &lt; 0\.01\); however, they also showed stronger perceptions of data security risks \( p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Caregivers reported greater trust in AI\-assisted medical decision\-making than healthcare workers \( p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Most respondents believed that AI could not replace pediatric healthcare workers, with no statistically significant difference between the two groups \( p = 0\.166\)\. Regarding potential advantages, healthcare workers were more likely to agree that AI could reduce human error \( p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Regarding potential limitations, healthcare workers expressed greater concern about the risks of misdiagnosis, lack of humanistic care, and data privacy and security issues \( p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Conclusion In this single\-center children’s specialty hospital, healthcare workers reported greater self\-rated understanding and more frequent use of AI, together with stronger awareness of several potential risks, whereas caregivers showed greater trust in AI\-assisted medical decision\-making despite lower self\-rated understanding\. These findings should be interpreted cautiously and require confirmation in larger multicenter studies before being generalized to broader populations in China\.
