# Budget\-Constrained PID Tuning for a Line\-Following Differential\-Drive AGV Using Latin Hypercube Sampling and a Cubic Polynomial Response Surface

**Autores:** Mastang; Bakr Ahmed Taha; Retna Apsari; Muhammad Mukhlisin; Eni Dwi Wardihani; Norhana Arsad

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Physical tuning of proportional–integral–derivative \(PID\) controllers for line\-following automated guided vehicles \(AGVs\) is a costly black\-box optimization problem because each candidate gain vector must be evaluated through a physical episode\. This study proposes a fixed\-budget framework that combines Latin hypercube sampling \(LHS\) with a multivariate cubic polynomial response surface \(CPRS\)\. The framework was evaluated on a Raspberry Pi\-based differential\-drive prototype operating on circle and 8shape tracks using 20 paired random seeds\. Its performance was compared with Random\-CPRS, LHS\-NoCPRS, Gaussian\-process Bayesian optimization \(GP\-BO\), particle swarm optimization \(PSO\), and a genetic algorithm \(GA\)\. On the circle track, LHS\-CPRS achieved the lowest observed mean objective value \(0\.1727\), followed by GP\-BO \(0\.1896\)\. On the 8shape track, GP\-BO achieved the lowest observed mean \(0\.6451\), followed closely by LHS\-CPRS \(0\.6501\)\. The Friedman omnibus tests detected significant overall differences among the methods on both the 8shape and circle tracks\. Holm\-adjusted paired Wilcoxon tests showed that LHS\-CPRS significantly outperformed Random\-CPRS, LHS\-NoCPRS, PSO, and GA on both tracks, with large rank\-biserial effect sizes \(0\.610–0\.924\), whereas its differences from GP\-BO were not statistically significant\. In independent frozen\-gain test episodes, LHS\-CPRS achieved the lowest observed mean for 8shape to circle transfer \(0\.2790\) and the second\-lowest mean for circle to 8shape transfer \(0\.7748\), for which PSO achieved the lowest mean \(0\.7601\)\. Overall, the results demonstrate that LHS\-CPRS is highly competitive under a fixed evaluation budget and provide evidence of component\-level ablation effects and cross\-track consistency within the tested laboratory conditions\.
