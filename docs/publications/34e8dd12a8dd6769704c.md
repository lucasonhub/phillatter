# Physics\-guided machine learning fragility assessment of self\-centering\-braced RC bridges using CatBoost demand surrogates

**Autores:** Mohammad Hasan Gharavipour; Mohammadreza Ahadpour Khaneghah; Vahid Broujerdian

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study proposes a physics\-guided machine learning framework for the seismic fragility assessment of reinforced\-concrete bridges equipped with self\-centering braces \(SCBs\), with conventional restrainers used as the benchmark system\. Incremental Dynamic Analyses \(IDAs\) were performed in OpenSeesPy to build a demand\-capacity database covering eight engineering demand parameters \(EDPs\)\. Eight machine learning models, including artificial neural networks, random forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost \(CTB\), support vector regression, k\-nearest neighbors, and linear regression, were optimized using Optuna and compared in terms of predictive performance\. Among them, CTB provided the best overall accuracy, with cross\-validated mean R² values greater than 0\.97 for all EDPs\. The CTB\-based demand predictions were combined with lognormal capacity models to define probabilistic exceedance labels for different damage states\. These labels were then used to train a physics\-guided neural network \(PGNN\) for fragility curve generation\. The PGNN incorporates monotonicity and damage\-state ordering constraints into the loss function, together with gradient guidance derived from SHAP and ICE analyses, to produce smooth, monotonic, and non\-crossing fragility curves\. Component\-level fragilities were assembled into system\-level fragility curves using a series\-parallel OR/AND formulation\. At the system level, the median Sa\(1\.0 s\) for Slight damage increased from 0\.066 g for the restrainer bridge to 0\.219 g for the SCB bridge \(\+229\.5%\), while the Moderate\-state medians differed by only 1\.9%\. For Extensive damage, the SCB median intensity was 7\.7% lower than that of the restrainer bridge\. At Sa\(1\.0 s\) = 1\.5 g, the SCB configuration showed 15\.6% and 24\.2% higher exceedance probabilities at the Extensive and Complete damage states, respectively\. Overall, the proposed CTB\-PGNN framework reduces the limitations of fixed\-form regression models and provides physically consistent fragility estimates, offering a useful tool for retrofit prioritization and performance\-based seismic design\.
