# Profiling forward playing styles in big five European football leagues through PCA and cluster analysis

**Autores:** Chuanqi Xia; Dawei Cao; Jun Ye

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Attacking contributions in elite football are multi\-dimensional, and broad forward\-line labels often obscure functional variation within nominally homogeneous groups\. This exploratory study mapped the technical\-function space of forward\-line players in Europe’s Big Five leagues using principal component analysis \(PCA\) and clustering, and evaluated the extent to which the data support discrete categories versus continuous variation\.Publicly available FBref player\-season data for the 2021–2022 and 2022–2023 seasons were analysed for 381 attacking player\-season observations from 331 players with at least 900 league minutes\. Sixteen technical process indicators were reduced using PCA, and three retained, standardised component scores were used for clustering \(K\-means, K = 2–5\), with additional fuzzy c\-means and archetypal analyses to assess profile distinctness\.Three PCA components explained 62\.7% of the variance and were interpretable as ball\-carrying progression, creative playmaking and penalty\-area presence; parallel analysis supported retention of these three components\. The primary finding across multiple validation approaches is that the technical\-function data among forward\-line players were more consistent with continuous variation than with sharply separated, temporally stable clusters\. K = 2 had the highest silhouette coefficient, whereas a five\-region descriptive summary provided a more differentiated heuristic framework\. However, fuzzy c\-means showed that the median maximum membership across the five regions was 0\.505, with 68\.5% of player\-seasons below 0\.60, indicating substantial overlap\. Cross\-season centroid replication for the five\-region solution was markedly weaker than for smaller\-K solutions, further supporting the view that the data were more consistent with continuous variation than with temporally stable categories\.The five regions were associated with descriptive differences in npxG \+ xA, npxG/Shot and G \+ A, but these associations reflect differences in chance access and shot quality and are not independent validation of the regions as distinct player types\. Non\-penalty goals relative to non\-penalty expected goals did not differ significantly across regions\.These findings indicate that technical tendencies within forward\-line players are more consistent with continuous variation in chance access and contribution than with fixed, discrete roles\. The three PCA dimensions offer a more stable framework for describing functional variation among forwards\. The five\-region heuristic summary is an exploratory, communication\-oriented device for navigating this continuous space, not a validated player taxonomy\.
