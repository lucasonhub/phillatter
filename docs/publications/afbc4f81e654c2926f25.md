# Coal dust\-stimulated macrophage\-derived extracellular vesicles promote thyroid cancer cell phenotypes involving miR\-205\-5p, CAMK1G, and NF\-κB\-associated signaling

**Autores:** Feng Zhao; Tianqing Ma; Chi Zhang; Hongzhen Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Occupational coal dust exposure causes persistent pulmonary inflammation, but the mechanisms by which it may influence thyroid cancer progression remain unclear\. Macrophage\-derived extracellular vesicles \(EVs\) may participate in intercellular communication under particulate stress\. Objective This study investigated whether EVs released by coal dust\-stimulated macrophages alter thyroid cancer cell behavior and examined the involvement of miR\-205\-5p, CAMK1G, and NF\-κB\-associated signaling\. Methods Coal dust was physicochemically characterized before use\. EVs were isolated from PMA\-differentiated THP\-1 macrophages following coal dust exposure and characterized by transmission electron microscopy, nanoparticle tracking analysis, and Western blotting\. Small\-RNA sequencing, bulk RNA sequencing, TCGA\-THCA analysis, and the GSE274674 single\-cell dataset were integrated with RT\-qPCR, nuclear–cytoplasmic fractionation, dual\-luciferase reporter assays, and genetic rescue experiments\. Coal dust\-exposed C57BL/6J mice and PBMC\-humanized NSFG mice bearing TPC\-1 xenografts were used for in vivo evaluation\. Results PKH26\-labeled EV\-associated material was detected in TPC\-1 cells, and EV preparations increased cell viability, clonogenic growth, migration, and invasion while reducing apoptosis\. miR\-205\-5p was enriched in these EVs and accumulated predominantly in the nuclear fraction of recipient cells\. Inhibition of miR\-205\-5p attenuated the EV\-associated malignant phenotypes\. Integrated transcriptomic analyses prioritized CAMK1G and NF\-κB\-related signaling\. EV treatment increased endogenous CAMK1G expression and the phosphorylation of p65 and IκBα\. CAMK1G knockdown reduced these changes, whereas CAMK1G re\-expression partially restored the effects suppressed by miR\-205\-5p inhibition\. The reporter assay showed sequence\-dependent repression of the isolated CAMK1G 3′\-UTR construct, whereas endogenous\-expression and rescue experiments supported a positive functional association; therefore, these findings are not interpreted as a simple direct positive regulatory relationship\. In mice, coal dust exposure was associated with pulmonary abnormalities, altered serum FT4 and TPO\-Ab levels, increased thyroid CAMK1G expression, and enhanced pulmonary CD206 immunoreactivity\. In PBMC\-humanized mice, coal dust exposure or experimentally administered macrophage\-derived EVs increased xenograft growth, whereas CAMK1G knockdown attenuated tumor burden\. Conclusion These findings support a functional association between coal dust\-stimulated macrophage\-derived EV signaling and thyroid cancer progression involving miR\-205\-5p, CAMK1G, and NF\-κB\-associated activity\. However, direct physiological trafficking of endogenous lung\-derived EVs to thyroid or tumor tissue was not demonstrated\.
