# Friction properties of boundary film formed by polymer additives in wide\-area contacts with nanoscale gap

**Autores:** Wataru Yagi; Toshiyuki Tsuchiya; Naoki Yamashita; Tomoko Hirayama

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Polymer additives, originally used as viscosity index improvers, have attracted increasing attention as friction\-reducing lubricant additives because of their ability to adsorb onto surfaces through polar groups\. Although adsorbed polymer films are known to play an important role in friction under boundary lubrication conditions, how their interfacial properties change during sliding and how these changes affect tribological behavior remain unclear\. To elucidate the relationship between the interfacial behavior of polymer additives and friction characteristics, we investigated changes in the coefficient of friction and oil film thickness using lubricating oils containing polymethyl methacrylate \(PMA\)\. A custom\-built friction tester capable of applying unidirectional sliding over a contact area of approximately 1 mm 2 was used to evaluate PMA additives with different numbers of polar groups and molecular weights, with a base oil and an olefin copolymer \(OCP\) used for comparison\. The base oil and OCP\-containing oil exhibited a nearly constant dynamic coefficient of friction, whereas all PMA\-containing oils exhibited lower friction accompanied by stick\-slip behavior at low shear velocities\. No clear difference in the coefficient of friction was observed between PMA additives with different numbers of polar groups\. However, the oil film thickness of the PMA additive with fewer polar groups decreased with increasing load\. Although the coefficient of friction was nearly the same for high\- and low\-molecular\-weight PMAs, the oil film thickness of the high\-molecular\-weight PMA decreased after friction compared with that before friction\. These results suggest that the number of polar groups affects the stiffness of the adsorbed polymer film in the load direction, whereas molecular weight affects the oil film thickness\. For high\-molecular\-weight PMA, shear\-induced desorption or rearrangement of a weakly formed topmost layer may reduce the thickness of the remaining strongly adsorbed film\. These findings clarify the relationship between the interfacial behavior of polymer additives and friction characteristics and provide insights into the design of polymer additives for friction reduction\.
