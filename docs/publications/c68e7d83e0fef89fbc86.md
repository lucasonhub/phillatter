# Injury surveillance, on\-field rehabilitation and return\-to\-play criteria: A survey of current practices in male professional football

**Autores:** Benjamin John Dixon; Jill Alexander; Damian Harper

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Injuries in professional football present complex challenges to multiple stakeholders\. Subsequent rehabilitation processes are guided by epidemiological research and rehabilitation frameworks providing objective criteria informing decision\-making\. Processes must constantly evolve to provide support and minimise subsequent injury risk\. The aims of this exploratory study were to investigate current practices in injury surveillance, on\-field rehabilitation \(OFR\) and return\-to\-play \(RTP\) criteria in male professional football\. In total, 81 practitioners responded to an online survey consisting of 34 questions divided into four sections: 1\) practitioner demographics, 2\) injury data collection and monitoring, 3\) OFR and RTP criteria, and 4\) decision\-making process for return\-to\-training and RTP\. Results indicate that for injury data collection, variability exists in rehabilitation end\-date classification, post\-rehabilitation timeframes, and a potential disparity between subsequent injury classification and reporting\. A range of external running load metrics and objective targets are used to monitor and progress OFR, indicating lack of consensus and availability of validated objective criteria\. A multidisciplinary, shared decision\-making approach is adopted, including input from players; however, communication was highlighted as a key area to improve\. Findings of this study may be beneficial for practitioners developing internal rehabilitation procedures and for identifying future research gaps that could result in positive rehabilitation outcomes\.
