# Neuroethnology as a tool to comprehend the connections of the Self with the Surrounding: A Conceptual Enquiry with Hermeneutic Analysis\.

**Autores:** Prakash Chandra Panda; Soumya Tripathy; Pratyush Mishra; B Jagadish; Sanjeeta Nayak; Kartika Chandra Parida

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: The relationship between the self and its surroundings is often conceptualised as a distinction between individuals and the external world\. Objective: This study employs hermeneutic analysis to explore neuroethnology and introduce the term as a means of understanding how the self\-differentiates from its surroundings while remaining interconnected\. Methods: A modified Delphi\-informed consensus process, conducted over seven sessions, identified six analytical domains\. From 471 pooled records, 78 sources were selected for iterative thematic analysis and hermeneutic synthesis\. Findings: The analysis differentiates between culture and ethnoculture, transmission and incorporation, embeddedness and embodiment, and biological embedding and inherited cognition\. Ethnoculturally available meanings and practices become self\-specific through embodiment and ethnocognition, which organise attention, appraisal, and actions\. Recurrent neurocognitive and somatocognitive patterns, understood as plastic rather than fixed, may confer pre\-reflective self\-relevance prior to the emergence of an explicit self\-concept\. The term “chronopersonalisation” is introduced to describe the progressive temporal localisation of behavioural ecology into characteristic yet adaptable self\-organisation\. Reflexivity is considered both a method and a subject, enabling the organization derived from surroundings to be examined and reorganized\. Indic accounts of lineage and self\-enquiry are acknowledged as epistemic antecedents of this mode of enquiry\. Conclusion: Neuroethnology complements neuroanthropology, cultural neuroscience, and neurophenomenology by focusing on the evolving self–surrounding relationship as one of its units of enquiry\.
