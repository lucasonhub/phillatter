# Lessons learned from recruitment and implementation of the Perceived Diet Quality Study

**Autores:** Tameka I\. Walls; Alicia S\. Landry; Fatemeh Malekian; Gabriela E\. Rodriguez; Jessica Lee Thomson

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Online surveys offer rapid, cost\-effective, and convenient tools for data collection\. However, online survey research carries risk, particularly for fraudulent behavior\. The objective of this study was to describe lessons learned from implementing an online survey designed to test and validate a comprehensive tool for measuring perceived diet quality\. Methods Participants were recruited via flyers posted at various community organizations\. Recruitment flyers contained a QR code to access the recruitment survey\. The study consisted of two surveys, the Perceived Diet Quality Questionnaire \(PDQ\-Q\) and Diet ID TM , a dietary assessment, tool\. Email reminders were sent to participants who had not completed the surveys within specified time frames\. Chi square tests were used to determine whether participant characteristics differed by reminder status \(no reminder vs\. ≥1 reminder\)\. Results Of the 1,337 recruitment surveys submitted, 962 individuals were enrolled in the study, with 605 \(63%\) and 591 \(61%\) valid and complete PDQ\-Q and Diet ID TM surveys submitted\. Majority reasons for not enrolling 375 individuals in the study included duplicate name, email address or telephone number \(73%\) and suspicious or invalid contact information \(15%\)\. The mean number of reminders sent to participants who completed the PDQ\-Q and the Diet ID TM was 1\.8 \(SD = 0\.9\) and 1\.7 \(SD = 1\.4\)\. For both surveys, the percentage of participants who needed ≥1 reminder increased as age increased \(PDQ\-Q: 21% for 18–29 years vs\. 35% for ≥60 years; Diet ID TM : 10% for 18–29 years vs\. 22% for ≥60 years\)\. Conclusions While QR codes and mobile\-first surveys increase accessibility and reduce participant burden, they also introduce risks to data integrity resulting in the need for verification checks\. Incorporating verification procedures may reduce redundant submissions and improve efficiency\. The observed pattern of diminishing returns after two completion reminders indicates that reminder schedules can be optimized to balance participant engagement\. Participant characteristics associated with the need for reminders suggest that fully digital protocols may disadvantage some groups\. Future research may involve testing recruitment and retention strategies, evaluating cost\-effectiveness of number of email reminders vs\. completion percentage, and examining how tailored engagement influences participation among underrepresented populations\.
