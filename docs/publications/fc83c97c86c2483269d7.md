# Development of a small batch flexible station for packaging waste sorting in circular economy applications

**Autores:** Branko Lukić; Miloš Petrović; Nikola Knežević; Zaviša Gordić; Arso M\. Vukićević; Kosta Jovanović

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Efficient packaging waste sorting is an important requirement for implementing circular economy strategies, yet many automated sorting solutions are designed for large\-scale, high\-throughput recycling facilities and are not well suited to small\-batch industrial environments characterized by limited waste volumes, frequent material changes, and the need for rapid system reconfiguration\. Methods This paper presents the design, implementation, and laboratory validation of a modular collaborative robotic workstation for small\-batch packaging waste sorting\. The proposed system integrates a collaborative robot, RGB\-D vision system, conveyor\-based material flow, and a ROS2\-based control architecture within a unified perception–decision–manipulation framework\. The perception pipeline combines class\-agnostic segmentation using the Segment Anything Model \(SAM\) with lightweight MobileNetV2\-based classification for material recognition and is extended with optional modules for geometric reasoning and damage assessment\. The proposed architecture reduces dependence on task\-specific segmentation training and provides semantic information relevant to grasp planning, safety\-aware task allocation, and human–robot collaboration\. Results The workstation was validated under controlled laboratory conditions using three representative packaging waste categories: plastic bottles, aluminium cans, and Tetra Pak containers\. The SAM \+ MobileNetV2 perception pipeline achieved an accuracy of 0\.93, precision of 0\.93, recall of 0\.92, and F1\-score of 0\.92\. System\-level experiments demonstrated successful integration of RGB\-D object localization, conveyor tracking, camera\-to\-robot coordinate transformation, and robotic pick\-and\-place execution\. In contrast to conventional high\-throughput sorting systems, the proposed workstation emphasizes modularity, flexibility, and collaborative operation for small\-batch industrial packaging waste streams\. In addition, workstation\-level manipulation tests over 200 grasp attempts resulted in a 92\.0% grasp success rate, an 89\.0% end\-to\-end sorting success rate, and an estimated sorting rate of 5\.73 picks/min under controlled conveyor\-based conditions\. Discussion The results demonstrate the feasibility of integrating foundation\-model\-based perception with collaborative robot in a modular workstation intended for small\-batch industrial packaging waste sorting\. The proposed approach emphasizes flexibility, modularity, and reduced reconfiguration effort for variable waste streams\. The results provide a basis for further development toward adaptive and human–robot collaborative sorting, while broader validation under more diverse, cluttered, and industrially representative conditions remains necessary\.
