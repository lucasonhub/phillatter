# Workplace Stress, Psychological Safety, and Mental Well\-Being Among Healthcare Professionals: A Psychosocial Safety Climate Framework

**Autores:** Mst Shahinur Begum; Shekh Mohammad Mostafa; Banasree Roy Urmi; Sinigdha Islam; Md Saiful Islam Bhuiyan

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Healthcare professionals report persistently elevated levels of workplace stress and burnout, yet organizational interventions targeting stress in isolation have shown inconsistent effects on staff mental well\-being\. This paper proposes a conceptual framework in which psychosocial safety climate, senior management&\#x27;s demonstrated commitment to employee psychological health, functions as the organizational\-level antecedent that shapes both job demands and team psychological safety among healthcare professionals, which in turn determine workplace stress and, ultimately, mental well\-being\. Drawing on psychosocial safety climate theory, the Job Demands\-Resources model, Edmondson&\#x27;s construct of team psychological safety, and Keyes&\#x27; two\-continua model of mental health, the framework proposes that team psychological safety operates through two distinct mechanisms: a direct positive effect on well\-being, and a buffering effect that weakens the otherwise negative relationship between workplace stress and well\-being\. Six formal propositions are derived to guide future empirical testing\. The framework is developed as a companion piece to a related conceptual paper on digital transformation and employee engagement in healthcare organizations, addressing the same described healthcare workforce population from a stress and well\-being perspective, together constituting a cohort\-based conceptual research pair\. Implications for healthcare workforce policy and leader development are discussed, alongside a proposed survey\-based validation agenda\.
