# An explainable graph transformer framework integrating structural reasoning and visual attention for interpretable nail disease diagnosis

**Autores:** R\. Bhuvanya; Sujithra Thandapani; M\. Mohamed Iqbal; S\. Adikrishanan

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Automated nail\-disease classification remains a challenging task as different nail disorders present subtle and overlapping variations in color, texture and morphology\. This paper introduces GraphX, an explainable graph\-transformer framework that improves the baseline Graph Vision Transformer by incorporating image\-specific graph construction, graph\-transformer fusion, and attention guidance during training\. The Adaptive Graph Construction module constructs a sparse graph per image based on the cosine similarity of spatially and texture enriched patch representations and retains the eight most similar neighbors per patch\. The Cross\-Attention Fusion module allows reciprocal refinement between graph\-based structural embeddings and transformer\-based contextual tokens\. The Explainability\-Aware Attention module learns to match the attention distribution with continuous lesion\-prior maps generated from local intensity contrast and color deviation, providing weak spatial guidance without dermatologist\-annotated lesion mask\. In classification, GraphX achieved 94\.97% accuracy and macro\-averaged one\-vs\-rest ROC\-AUC of 0\.98, which outperformed the baseline models evaluated\. Explainability was measured with Grad\-CAM visualization, normalized attribution entropy, and deletion–insertion faithfulness testing\. The results demonstrate strong internal classification performance and provide preliminary evidence of model’s attention patterns, highlighting the potential of GraphX as a transparent framework for automated nail disease classification\.
