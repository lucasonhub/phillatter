# Learner autonomy in Ethiopian secondary EFL classrooms: observed teacher practices and student perceptions

**Autores:** Esayas Teshome Taddese; Habtamu Adem; Mitiku Tasisa Dinsa; A Shorouk

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Learner autonomy is widely recognized as a goal of modern language education\. However, in Ethiopian EFL contexts, the extent to which autonomy is promoted remains largely unknown\. This study investigated the relationship between teachers’ autonomy\-supportive practices and learner autonomy in Ethiopian secondary school EFL classrooms, a context marked by large classes, teacher\-centered instruction, and exam\-driven curricula\. Employing an explanatory sequential mixed methods design with quantitative priority \(QUAN→qual\), the research involved 412 students and 34 teachers from eight public schools in Gurage Zone, Central Ethiopia\. Data were collected through classroom observations \(Teacher Autonomy\-Supportive Practices Observation Schedule, TAPOS\), student perception surveys \(Student Perception of Autonomy Support Scale, SPASS\), and a learner autonomy inventory \(Learner Autonomy Inventory, LAI\)\. Because students were nested within teachers, quantitative analyses distinguished between student\-level and teacher\-level variables and employed two\-level linear mixed\-effects modeling and a 2\-1\-1 multilevel mediation model\. Qualitative data from interviews and focus groups provided complementary insights\. Findings revealed low levels of observed autonomy\-supportive practices \(mean = 2\.1/5\), with “providing rationales” most common \(68% of lessons\) and “encouraging self\-initiation” least common \(4%\)\. Students’ perceived autonomy support was relatively low when autonomy\-specific items were analyzed \(mean = 2\.7/5\), while their self\-reported autonomous behaviors were also low \(mean = 2\.4/4\)\. The multilevel model indicated that teacher autonomy\-promoting practices were positively associated with learner autonomy, while students’ perceptions of autonomy\-supportive practices and prior English achievement were also positively associated with learner autonomy\. Class size showed a negative association with learner autonomy\. The multilevel mediation analysis indicated a significant indirect association between teacher autonomy\-promoting practices and learner autonomy through students’ perceptions of autonomy support\. Qualitatively, students desired autonomy but felt unprepared, while teachers viewed autonomy as culturally challenging but not impossible\. The study concludes that an “autonomy gap” exists, necessitating teacher training that addresses cultural beliefs and systemic constraints and promotes gradual, structured approaches to learner autonomy\.
