# A multi\-source EO Framework for assessing permafrost thawing impact on land degradation in the Arctic region

**Autores:** Ionut Sandric; Mirela Vasile; Marina Virghileanu; Florin Miron; Alexandru Berbecariu; Laura Tîrlă; Radu Irimia; Teona Daia\-Creinicean; Carmen Bizdadea; Diana Onose; Ana Maria Popa; Răzvan Popescu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

EarthTrends is a multi\-source Earth Observation framework developed to assess the impact of permafrost thaw on land degradation across the Arctic\. Rapid warming is accelerating processes such as thermokarst formation, vegetation change, slope instability, and risks to infrastructure, yet monitoring these dynamics requires integrating heterogeneous satellite datasets and analytical methods\. EarthTrends addresses this challenge through a modular Python architecture that fuses long\-term satellite archives \(Landsat\), Copernicus Sentinel data, MODIS time series, and high\-resolution optical imagery with advanced statistical and artificial intelligence approaches\. The framework quantifies ecosystem change using multi\-sensor vegetation index time series analysed through linear regression, Mann–Kendall trend tests, and Long Short\-Term Memory \(LSTM\) models to detect non\-linear dynamics and anomalies\. Thermokarst features are automatically mapped from high\-resolution imagery using a U\-Net deep learning segmentation model\. Land degradation susceptibility is further evaluated by modelling soil freeze–thaw cycles as proxies for slope instability and landslide potential\. Environmental indicators are intersected with socio\-economic datasets to estimate impacts on settlements and infrastructure\. Preliminary results show that the thermokarst detection component is operational and capable of identifying surface subsidence features, while freeze–thaw analyses reveal anomalies linked to areas of increased degradation risk\. Vegetation trends highlight spatially heterogeneous ecosystem responses to warming across the Arctic\. Overall, EarthTrends demonstrates the feasibility of an integrated, scalable framework for monitoring permafrost\-driven environmental change and its societal implications\. The system is intended for pan\-Arctic application and will be released as an open\-source tool to support research, monitoring, and decision\-making related to climate\-induced land degradation\.
