# Knowledge Transfer in Early Modern England: The Role of Culinary Collections and Household Investigation Using THE WASPAS Method

**Autores:** Muniyammal Sakthivel; M\. Ramachandran; Arunambigai Ramesh

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examines the progress of English agriculture during the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, focusing on labor productivity, grain yields, and the expansion of farm size\. Using estate surveys, land tax assessments, and Arthur Young’s data, it explores how larger farms improved labor efficiency and contributed to agricultural transformation\. The research also compares English agricultural performance with French and Russian agriculture, highlighting international differences in farming practices and productivity\. Furthermore, the study evaluates five varieties of English, namely Old English, Middle English, King James Bible English, Colonial American English, and Scots English, using the WASPAS method\. Four evaluation parameters—rich vocabulary, historical importance, complex grammar, and obsolete vocabulary—are considered to assess their relative significance\. The findings indicate that Colonial American English achieved the highest ranking, whereas Old English received the lowest\. Colonial American English demonstrated the highest preference value under the WASPAS approach\. These results illustrate the usefulness of multi\-criteria decision\-making methods in comparative linguistic evaluation and provide insights into the historical development and distinctive characteristics of English language varieties\.
