# FALCON\-XR: Flexible and hybrid low\-precision floating\-point compute engine for energy\-efficient XR perception acceleration

**Autores:** Vijay Pratap Sharma; Shekhar Venkatpurwar; Shubham Kumar; Ratko Pilipović; Mukul Lokhande; Santosh Kumar Vishvakarma

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The convergence of extended reality \(XR\) and artificial intelligence is drivingincreasingly demanding on\-device perception workloads, yet the accelerators thatserve them must meet strict constraints: low latency for responsiveinteraction and energy\-efficient desing suitable for wearable devices\. These workloads, particularly within the XR perception pipeline, exhibitheterogeneous numerical sensitivity, motivating hybrid\-precision compute that canbalance accuracy against energy\. This paper presentsFALCON\-XR, a runtime\-adaptive low\-precision floating\-point accelerator forenergy\-efficient and low latency XR perception\. The architecture is built around the HybridLow\-Precision Floating\-Point Compute Engine \(HL\-FPCE\), which supports FP4 and FP8execution through a shared reconfigurable datapath that operates either as a singleFP8 MAC unit or as two parallel FP4 MAC units within a five\-stage pipeline\.Implemented in 28\\,nm CMOS, the design achieves a 1\.78\\,GHz operating frequencywithin a 5908\\,$\\mu$m\\textsuperscript\{2\} area and a 29\.42\\,mW power envelope,yielding a compute efficiency of 4\.12\\,pJ/op, an energy\-delay product of$0\.93 \\cdot 10^\{\-5\}$\\,fJ$\\cdot$s, an energy efficiency of 79\.68\\,TFLOPS/W, and acompute density of 122\.7\\,TFLOPS/mm\\textsuperscript\{2\}\. Evaluation acrossrepresentative XR perception workloads demonstrates that FP8 execution preservesnear\-baseline accuracy while FP4 delivers substantial reductions in latency andenergy at the cost of acceptable accuracy degradation, confirming the suitabilityof FALCON\-XR for real\-time, power\-constrained XR edge deployment\.
