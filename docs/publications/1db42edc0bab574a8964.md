# Physics\-Informed Vector LSTM for Multi\-Horizon Hub\-Height Wind Speed Forecasting Across Multiple Sites

**Autores:** Jianguo Yan; Zhe Li; Chuangye Li; Zhihua Ding; 黄翔超; Guohua Ke; Wenzhe Zhang; Bin Song; Shifeng Ding

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate hub\-height wind\-speed forecasting is a prerequisite for the safe and economical integration of wind power into the electricity grid, because forecasting errors propagate directly into imbalance penalties, reserve sizing, and turbine wear, and short\-term predictions \(one to twenty\-four hours ahead\) are of direct operational value\. The literature on wind\-speed and, more broadly, multivariate time\-series forecasting has advanced through three overlapping waves, namely classical statistical and physical models, shallow machine\-learning regression, and deep sequence models, which share a common trajectory of replacing hand\-crafted meteorological relationships by data\-driven estimators\. Four shared limits recur across this work: deep architectures treat covariates as exchangeable tokens and under\-use the physically grounded relationships among synchronous variables; they optimize pure accuracy without a mechanism that keeps the forecast physically consistent with the atmosphere; evaluations concentrate on generic benchmarks where the short\-term optimum is often a linear or persistence baseline, so the benefit of complex inductive biases at operational horizons is unclear; and wind\-speed studies seldom isolate an explicit physical prior from the recurrent or attention backbone\. This work contributes \(a\) a reproducible multi\-site benchmark of eight forecasting strategies, namely persistence, ARIMA, gradient\-boosted trees, a plain LSTM, and four further deep architectures including PatchTST, DLinear, iTransformer, and the proposed physics\-informed PIV\-LSTM, evaluated at five horizons on four real sites; \(b\) a physics\-informed vector \(PIV\) that augments the network input with an ordinary\-least\-squares linear reconstruction of the target from its known covariates, and regularizes training with a decaying consistency loss, implemented on an LSTM backbone \(PIV\-LSTM\); and \(c\) an ablation that quantifies where the physical prior helps and where it does not, together with an honest assessment of the results, including where a classical model remains competitive and the long\-horizon regime in which every model loses skill\.
