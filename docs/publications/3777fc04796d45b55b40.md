# IoT\-Based Battery Charging and Swapping Stations for Electric Vehicles: A Review of Architectures, Reservation, Security, and Challenges

**Autores:** U\. B\. Malkhandale; Sejal Mahato; Shruti Kale; Aryan Warkhedkar; Vansh Khonde; Khushi Ramtake

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Battery swapping has re\-emerged as a practical alternative to plug\-in charging for electric vehicles \(EVs\), particularly for electric two\-wheelers used in delivery, ride\-hailing, and shared\-mobility services, where vehicle downtime directly reduces earnings\. As swapping networks grow, a station can no longer be a passive rack of chargers: it must continuously know which batteries are charged, reserved, or faulty, and it must release a battery only to the user entitled to it\. This paper reviews battery charging and swapping stations from this Internet of Things \(IoT\) viewpoint\. It \(i\) compares energy\-replenishment models and the commercial and policy landscape; \(ii\) surveys studies of station operation, planning, and user acceptance; \(iii\) examines IoT sensing, communication, and cloud architectures for real\-time batterystatus monitoring; \(iv\) analyzes reservation, authentication, and access\-control mechanisms and their cybersecurity implications; and \(v\) discusses charging safety and evaluation metrics\. A rulebased battery\-state model and a low\-cost prototype that combines an ESP32 controller, Li\-Po battery bays, RFID/PIN authentication, servo\-actuated gates, a Firebase database, and a ReactJS dashboard are presented as an illustrative reference implementation\. The reviewed studies largely address operation, planning, or acceptance separately; the review identifies open challenges in integrated real\-time monitoring, interoperability, battery\-health estimation, security, and standardized evaluation\.
