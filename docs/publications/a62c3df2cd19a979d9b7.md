# Competitive phenotype and metabolite repertoire of the endophytic Diaporthe sp\. PBS30\-7

**Autores:** Ling Yang; Chuancheng Wei

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Endophytic fungi represent potential sources of antifungal agents, but the phenotypic and chemical basis of their interactions with competing fungi remains incompletely understood\. Here, physiological characterization, time\-resolved confrontation assays, cell\-free culture\-filtrate testing, untargeted chemical profiling, and molecular docking were integrated to characterize the competitive phenotype of the endophytic fungus Diaporthe sp\. PBS30\-7, isolated from the medicinal plant Pereskia bleo \(Kunth\) DC\., and to prioritize candidate metabolites for subsequent investigation\. Diaporthe sp\. PBS30\-7 exhibited condition\-dependent growth and a temporally asymmetric competitive phenotype against two Penicillium species, characterized by sustained colony expansion, progressive restriction of target\-fungal growth, and pronounced hyphal abnormalities\. Cell\-free culture filtrates retained antifungal activity against both target fungi, supporting a contribution of extracellular chemical factors to the overall antagonistic phenotype\. Feature\-based molecular networking of a culture\-associated agar–mycelium extract resolved a chemically diverse repertoire that included putatively annotated beauvericin\-type cyclodepsipeptides, diketopiperazines, and unresolved molecular families\. Molecular docking further prioritized this candidate chemical space, with beauvericin derivatives displaying particularly favorable target\-dependent docking profiles toward CYP51, Fks1, and the ABC transporter, whereas cyclo\(L\-Tyr\-L\-Tyr\) emerged as a notable diketopiperazine candidate\. Together, these findings prioritize candidate metabolites potentially associated with the competitive phenotype of Diaporthe sp\. PBS30\-7 and provide a foundation for future mechanistic studies of endophytic fungal antagonism\.
