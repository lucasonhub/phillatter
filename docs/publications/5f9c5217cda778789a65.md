# Visualizing literary inquiry: a curriculum framework for digital\-humanities pedagogy in English literature

**Autores:** Xinyu Xian

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Literature courses commonly use close reading to guide students through individual passages\. Because class time is limited and reading must be selective, however, students may find it difficult to observe patterns across an entire work\. Drawing on the change of scale associated with distant reading, this article proposes a five\-module curriculum framework for using digital text analysis and visualization in English literature teaching\. The modules address language and style, thematic language, chapter\-level discourse, character relations, and cross\-textual comparison\. Each module is designed to help students notice a pattern, formulate a literary question, return to relevant passages, and consider how analytical choices shaped what the visualization shows\. Demonstrations based on The Scarlet Letter and The Great Gatsby show that materials for all five modules can be prepared using frequency and concordance analysis, chapter trends, a lexical\-family heatmap, a character network, and normalized cross\-textual comparison\. They also make clear that visual patterns depend on choices about words, textual units, and coding, and cannot by themselves establish a literary interpretation\. The framework thus offers teachers a way to connect digital analysis with close reading and methodological reflection\. Its classroom feasibility and effects on student learning remain to be evaluated\.
