# Functional Motor Profiling and Performance Correlates in Youth Football Players: A Neuroscience Perspective

**Autores:** Vladimir Potop; Bogdan Pană; Liviu Emanuel Mihailescu; Ilie Mihai; Paul Visan; Niculina Liliana Mihăilescu; Constantin Ciorbă; Dumitru Barbu; Ion Mihăilă

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Psychomotor development plays an important role in neuromuscular regulation, movement efficiency, and football\-specific performance during early adolescence\. This study investigated changes associated with an integrated psychomotor\-oriented intervention program in psychomotor abilities, technical performance, and functional physiological responses in U13 football players\. A longitudinal quasi\-experimental pretest–posttest design was applied to 17 male youth football players during the competition season\. The 12\-week intervention program was integrated into regular football training and included psychomotor, neuromuscular, coordinative, and football\-specific exercises\. Psychomotor assessment included sprint performance, postural stability and control, agility, and reactive neuromuscular parameters, complemented by football\-specific technical tests and functional match monitoring using the Polar Vantage V system\. An external comparative U13 team \(n = 17\) was additionally evaluated for football\-specific technical performance\. Significant within\-group improvements were observed in 20\-m sprint performance \(Δ = −0\.19 s; p &lt; 0\.001; d = −1\.13\), lateral bipedal postural stability \(Δ = \+20%; p &lt; 0\.001; d = 1\.35\), and vertical bipedal postural control \(Δ = \+10%; p = 0\.006; d = 0\.77\)\. All three technical tests improved significantly within the intervention group \(Test 1: p = 0\.002, d = 0\.88; Test 2: p = 0\.016, d = 0\.75; Test 3: p = 0\.037, d = 0\.63\), while between\-group comparisons of change scores were not statistically significant\. Functional monitoring during official match play identified a significant reduction in Speed Z3 exposure \(p = 0\.016; d = −0\.70\), while no statistically significant changes were observed in the other physiological or locomotor variables\. Spearman correlation analyses identified significant associations among psychomotor, technical, and functional variables \(ρ = −0\.753 to −0\.488; p &lt; 0\.05\)\. Given the small sample, non\-randomized design, incomplete multidimensional assessment in the comparative group, and absence of biological maturation assessment, the findings should be interpreted as preliminary evidence of within\-group changes associated with psychomotor\-oriented training rather than as definitive causal effects\.
