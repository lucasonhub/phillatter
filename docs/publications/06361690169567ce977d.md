# Facchin, Marco &amp; Robertson, Ian: Recognizing artificial minds or succumbing to paraeidolia? On Cappelen &amp; Dever’s “whole hog” defense of artificial minds

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Prominent researchers are now defending the contention that current large language models have mental properties—humanity has, by these lights, successfully constructed artificial minds\. Notably, Cappelen and Dever have recently argued that such models are capable of thought, understanding, and performing intentional actions\. They support this controversial contention on the basis of an argument that trades on the idea that humans are highly competent at detecting mental states\. In this article, we argue that their case in support of the existence of artificial minds is philosophically misguided and, at crucial points, rests upon dubious empirical grounds\. Along the way, we also examine the methodological motivations behind their case\. Cappelen and Dever claim that the philosophy of AI tends to traffic in an outmoded and retrograde methodology, one that unduly privileges elaborate thought experiments and contrived counterexamples; this methodology, they argue, needs urgently to be overturned in favour of a new form of inquiry that begins by assigning more weight to what we observe when we interact with AI\. Once we make this reversal, they claim, the case for artificial minds is very strong indeed\. We argue that their case for such a methodology is, for numerous reasons, wrongheaded and is in any event unlikely to yield fruitful answers to questions concerning whether or not LLMs possess particular mental properties\.
