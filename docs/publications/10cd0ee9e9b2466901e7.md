# Nutritional dynamics of weeds in agroecosystem and their utilization in the agro\-food industry within a circular economy framework

**Autores:** Arshiya Dogra; Neha Sharma; Sunny Sharma

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Edible weeds are becoming more widely acknowledged that edible weeds are underutilized natural resources with substantial potential to promote circular bio\-economy initiatives and sustainable food systems\. Many weed species have nutritional content, a variety of bioactive chemicals, and numerous uses in human nutrition, livestock feeding, and value\-added agro\-food products, while being historically considered unwanted elements of agricultural landscapes\. The nutritional makeup, phytochemical profile, functional characteristics, and agro\-food usage of a few edible weed species that are frequently found in agricultural habitats are all critically summarised in this narrative review\. Major scientific databases, such as Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed, ScienceDirect, and Google Scholar, were used to retrieve literature published between recent years\. The selection of species was based on their documented edible use, the availability of peer\-reviewed information about their nutritional composition and potential applications, and their frequent occurrence as agricultural weeds\. Evidence suggests that edible weeds are abundant in vitamins, minerals, phenolic compounds, flavonoids, carotenoids, essential amino acids, dietary fibre, weeds are abundant in vitamins, minerals, phenolic compounds, flavonoids, carotenoids, essential amino acids, dietary fibre, and other phytochemicals with antibacterial, anti\-inflammatory, antioxidant, and metabolic regulating qualities\. Although interesting biological activities have been shown in experimental research, there is still a dearth of solid clinical evidence to support many of these health advantages\. Edible weeds provide prospects for value\-added applications in circular agricultural systems, such as livestock feeding, compost generation, natural food additives, and nutraceutical development, in addition to human nutrition\. However, before broader commercialisation is possible, issues with food safety, anti\-nutritional elements, processing method uniformity, regulatory approval, and customer acceptance must be resolved\. In conclusion, rather than taking the place of traditional crops, edible weeds should be seen as complementary resources that help diversify food systems, increase resource\-use efficiency, and support sustainable agricultural growth\. To enable their responsible incorporation into resilient agro\-food systems, future research should give priority to clinical validation, standardised processing technologies, safety assessment, economic feasibility studies, and commercialisation strategies\.
