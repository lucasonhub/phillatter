# Performance evaluation and interpretability of a Qwen LangGraph agent for AI written and AI rewritten text detection

**Autores:** Nyashadzashe Tamuka

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract The widespread adoption of large language models has increased the need to distinguish human\-authored text from AI\-generated content, including cases in which generated text is subsequently rewritten or paraphrased\. In practice, many detectors output only a single AI\-likelihood score or percentage, offering limited transparency and little indication of whether a passage appears directly AI\-written \(AIW\) or AI\-rewritten \(AIR\)\. This study presents an agentic detection framework for two connected tasks: binary provenance detection \(Human versus AI\) and conditional subtype detection \(AIW versus AIR\)\. The dataset combined the Human\-ChatGPT Comparison Corpus \(HC3\) and the ai\-text\-detection\-pile with 4,348 pre\-generated AIR records produced by a single GPT\-based rewriting workflow\. The proposed Qwen–LangGraph AI agent was compared to a HashingVectorizer–TF\-IDF–SGD classifier, DeBERTa\-v3\-xsmall, and ELECTRA\-small\. On the binary test set, DeBERTa\-v3\-xsmall achieved the best discrimination \(99\.4995% accuracy and 99\.9911% AUROC\), whereas the agent achieved 98\.8816% accuracy and 99\.9187% AUROC\. On the AI\-only test set \(10,769 AIW; 1,274 AIR\), the agent obtained the highest threshold\-based accuracy \(96\.7367%\), macro\-F1 \(90\.0357%\), and AIR F1 \(81\.8643%\), but its AUROC \(83\.2507%\) and AUPR \(79\.7336%\) were lower than those of the DeBERTa\-v3\-xsmall and ELECTRA\-small transformers\. Paired DeLong and McNemar analyses with Holm correction showed that the agent improved threshold\-based AIR performance over the lexical baseline\. Interpretability was operationalised through lexical weights, linguistic and out\-of\-distribution diagnostics, sentence\-level indicators, and route traces\. The findings support the use of AI as decision support, not as automated proof of plagiarism or misconduct\.
