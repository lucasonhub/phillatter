# Assessment of vulnerability and economic losses of typical Chinese ports under the influence of tropical cyclones

**Autores:** Tianni Wang; Tina Ziting Xu; Wen Zhang; Mark Ching‐Pong Poo

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The shipping industry is vital to global trade, with China’s ports leading in scale for years\. However, the growing threat of climate change, especially tropical cyclones, poses serious risks to port operations, infrastructure, and supply chains, resulting in substantial economic losses\. This study assesses the resilience and economic impacts of tropical cyclones on five key Chinese ports, using Shanghai, Shenzhen, Qingdao, Xiamen, and Qinzhou as case studies\. It develops a comprehensive index system that accounts for resilience, efficiency, and importance\. The study utilizes expert surveys, entropy weighting, the Analytic Hierarchy Process \(AHP\), and Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution \(TOPSIS\)\. Results show Shanghai Port is the most vulnerable, while Xiamen and Qinzhou rank lower\. Economic loss assessments \(2014–2023\) reveal severe seasonal impacts, with cargo\-handling fee losses being the most significant\. Findings suggest that effective berth planning and staff training can improve disaster prevention\. It extends prior vulnerability assessments by coupling a multi\-dimensional Port Vulnerability Index with an Economic Loss Estimation Model, quantifying direct and reputational losses across stakeholders and showing that high network importance does not always align with the largest economic losses\. This research provides a robust evaluation framework that enables policymakers to strengthen port resilience in the face of escalating climate risks\.
