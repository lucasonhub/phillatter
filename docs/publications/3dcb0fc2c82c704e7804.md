# WTe2 Weyl semimetal conical microarray enabled quantum\-enhanced terahertz photocurrent sensing for organophosphorus toxin detection in simulated body fluid

**Autores:** Xu Zhang; Mingxin Li; Chunting Wu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

As a representative topological quantum system, type\-II Weyl semimetals break the fundamental bandgap limitation of conventional semiconductor photodetectors and deliver giant intrinsic nonlinear optical response originating from topologically protected Weyl points and Fermi arc surface states, providing a disruptive material platform for advanced room\-temperature terahertz \(THz\) optoelectronics\. Building on this quantum material advantage, we develop a room\-temperature THz sensor based on a periodic WTe 2 conical microarray for quantitative detection of the organophosphorus neurotoxin chlorpyrifos in simulated body fluid \(SBF\)\. Each microcone measures 80 µm in base diameter and 60 µm in height, arranged with a 100 µm center\-to\-center pitch on a Si/SiO 2 substrate with Au/Ti electrodes\. A 140 GHz continuous\-wave THz source illuminates the chip from above, and the photocurrent is read out by a Keithley 2400 digital source meter via two tungsten\-carbide microprobes\. Leveraging the geometric near\-field concentration effect at the cone tips, the proposed architecture synergistically enhances the second\-order nonlinear photocurrent rectification originating from the Weyl\-point band structure of WTe 2 , as confirmed by CST electromagnetic simulation\. The underlying sensing mechanism relies on chlorpyrifos\-induced dielectric screening and interfacial charge transfer at the WTe 2 surface, which modulate the THz\-to\-DC rectification efficiency\. Current responsivity \(Ra\) and noise\-equivalent power \(NEP\) are extracted from illuminated and dark current–voltage \(I–V\) characteristics as a function of chlorpyrifos concentration over a bias range of 7\.0–8\.0 V\. The sensor achieves a limit of detection \(LOD\) of 12 ng/mL across a dynamic range of 0\.05–100 µg/mL in 0\.7% saline SBF, with a logarithmic calibration fit of R 2 = 0\.991\. Compared with conventional semiconductor and thermal THz detectors, the topological\-quantum\-material device realizes room\-temperature high\-sensitivity detection without relying on interband transition or cryogenic cooling, demonstrating fundamentally superior performance stability and broadband adaptability\. This work demonstrates that microstructured Weyl semimetal heterostructures enable quantum\-enhanced THz biochemical sensing, providing a new device architecture for label\-free, reagent\-free toxin detection in complex aqueous environments\.
