# QAR\-Based Modeling of Aircraft Ground\-Taxi Fuel Consumption and CO2 Emissions: Determinants, Prediction and Operational Implications

**Autores:** Jinglei Yu; Ruiyu Dong; Dongcheng Chen; Xinbin Zhao; Yinger Zheng; Yiqin Bai; Chaofeng Shao

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The airport ground taxiing phase is identified as a significant contributor to fuel consumption and carbon emissions within the aviation transport system\. However, refined management and research remain challenged by data gaps and model imprecision\. A research framework is proposed, combining high\-precision Quick Access Recorder \(QAR\) data, statistical analysis, and machine learning\. Main\-engine fuel consumption differences across aircraft types, airports, and taxi\-in and taxi\-out operations were analyzed to identify the principal determinants of taxiing fuel consumption\. A direct taxi\-time–fuel baseline and an Adaptive Mechanism Learning \(AML\) model were constructed to predict aircraft taxiing fuel consumption\. The AML model achieved a test\-set coefficient of determination \(R2\) of 0\.9672, while aircraft\-specific R2 values exceeded 0\.97 for A320, A350, and B777 aircraft\. The results highlight the importance of taxiing duration and engine operating state in estimating main\-engine fuel consumption and associated CO2 emissions\. The findings provide a basis for refined fuel\-consumption assessment and operational decision support for greener airport operations\.
