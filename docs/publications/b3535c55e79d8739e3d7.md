# Early spatial pain contraction and moderate\-to\-severe knee pain 12 months after total knee arthroplasty: a retrospective cohort study

**Autores:** Jun Liu; Wenheng Chen; Shan Gao

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background While total knee arthroplasty \(TKA\) generally provides excellent pain relief, a substantial minority of patients develop persistent postsurgical pain\. This study investigated whether the early postoperative contraction rate of whole\-body spatial pain extent was associated with moderate\-to\-severe knee pain 12 months after TKA\. Methods In this retrospective cohort study of patients undergoing primary unilateral TKA, participants completed whole\-body pain drawings at 2 weeks, 6 weeks, and 3 months postoperatively\. The Pain Extent Index \(PEI\) was calculated using a semiautomated image\-processing workflow\. The primary exposure was the patient\-specific rate of PEI change between week 2 and month 3, determined via linear mixed\-effects modeling\. The primary outcome was moderate\-to\-severe operated\-knee pain at 12 months\. Logistic regression evaluated this association, adjusting for baseline covariates and static pain measures\. Results At 12 months, 92 patients \(14\.7%\) reported moderate\-to\-severe knee pain\. Slower spatial pain contraction was significantly associated with higher odds of 12\-month pain \(adjusted odds ratio per 1\-SD slower contraction, 1\.42; 95% CI, 1\.03–1\.95\)\. This association remained significant even after adjusting for baseline risk factors and static pain intensity and extent at both week 2 and month 3\. Slower contraction was also correlated with poorer 12\-month joint\-related functional outcomes, including lower KOOS\-JR scores \( P &lt; 0\.001\)\. Conclusion Slower contraction of spatial pain extent during the subacute recovery phase was associated with moderate\-to\-severe knee pain and inferior functional outcomes 1 year after TKA\.
