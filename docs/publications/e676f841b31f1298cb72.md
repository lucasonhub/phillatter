# Predicting reproductive\-phase nutrient replenishment in Cannabis sativa L\. via a transpiration\-driven mass\-balance framework

**Autores:** Kit Powell; William L\. Bauerle

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Fixed nutrient recipes often overlook developmental shifts in uptake and the coupling between transpiration and ion delivery\. We tested a transpiration\-driven mass\-balance framework to predict nutrient replenishment during the reproductive phase in two cultivars of Cannabis sativa L\. \(CJ2, First Light\)\. Plants received a solution prepared from a constant recipe; water use was tracked gravimetrically, leachate was periodically sampled, and organs were harvested for ICP–OES/flow injection analysis and biomass composite partitioning\. Water\-use efficiency \(WUE\) peaked at 4\.5 g L −1 in CJ2 and 3\.4 g L −1 in First Light, while solution drawdown of N, P, and K declined from early to late flowering\. Leachate chemistry resolved cultivar\- and stage\-dependent transitions between net nutrient removal \(net removal\) from solution and net accumulation above input\. In First Light, N shifted from 130 mg L −1 net removal to \+109 mg L −1 enrichment and K from 192 mg L −1 net removal to \+195 mg L −1 enrichment; in CJ2, N ranged from 90 mg L −1 net removal to \+101 mg L −1 enrichment and K from 102 mg L −1 net removal to \+195 mg L −1 enrichment\. Phosphorus differences were smaller \(First Light: 58 to 13 mg L −1 ; CJ2: 42 mg L −1 removal to \+2 mg L −1 enrichment\)\. Mg net removal was greater in First Light than CJ2 \(37 vs\. 16 mg L −1 \)\. Significant cultivar main effects were detected for P, K, Ca, and Fe \(p ≤ 0\.05\), whereas significant date x cultivar interactions were detected for Mg and Mn \(p ≤ 0\.05\)\. Across the four harvest intervals retained for mass\-balance comparison, biomass\-weighted predictors frequently produced the lowest absolute percent deviation \(APD\), although performance was element\- and cultivar\-dependent\. Single\-organ tissue element concentrations misrepresented whole\-plant replenishment estimates: leaf Ca and Mg increased while leaf N declined, flowers and roots accumulated P, and stems and roots diverged in K\. Predicted input concentrations derived from organ biomass\-weighted tissue elemental content improved the alignment between replenishment estimates and measured net solution removal\. Biomass\-weighted means excluding floral tissue predicted most element replenishment amounts well and performed most consistently in CJ2, whereas biomass\-weighted means including all organs more frequently minimized APD in First Light, with absolute percent deviations&lt;10–13%\. Collectively, these results indicate that reproductive\-phase nutrition in Cannabis sativa L\. is well represented by a flux\-based mass\-balance replenishment framework informed by organ biomass\-weighted nutrient composition\.
