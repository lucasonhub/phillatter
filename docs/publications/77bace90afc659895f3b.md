# Transfer Learning in Bandits from Unknown Adaptive Policies: Problem\-Dependent Lower and Upper Bounds

**Autores:** Adrien Prevost; Timothee Mathieu; Waris Radji; Odalric\-Ambrym Maillard

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Transfer learning in stochastic multi\-armed bandits is typically studied assuming that prior data forms a fixed offline dataset or is generated by a known source\. In practice, historical observations come from a previous adaptive decision process whose policy is unknown\. We study this setting for bounded rewards in $\[0,1\]$\. Before the online game starts, the learner receives, for each arm $k$, $N&\#x27;\_k$ observations drawn i\.i\.d\.\\ from an unknown distribution $ν&\#x27;\_k$, collected by an arbitrary and possibly data\-dependent strategy, so the sample sizes $N&\#x27;\_k$ may be random\. Source and target means satisfy $\|μ\_k\-μ&\#x27;\_k\|&lt;L\_k$ for known constants $\(L\_k\)\_k$\. We derive an asymptotic problem\-dependent lower bound and propose KL\-UCB\-Transfer, which combines online and historical data and achieves logarithmic regret with an improved leading constant whenever $μ&\#x27;\_a&lt;μ\_1\-L\_a$, and recovers the classical KL\-UCB guarantee otherwise\. Since bounding the source\-target discrepancy is often unrealistic, we relax the assumption to the one\-sided condition $μ\_k\-μ&\#x27;\_k&lt;L\_k$ and show that logarithmic regret can survive under misspecification, characterizing three regimes ranging from improvement to a degraded logarithmic bound to a loss of guarantee\. Simulations illustrate the practical benefits of exploiting process\-generated historical data\.
