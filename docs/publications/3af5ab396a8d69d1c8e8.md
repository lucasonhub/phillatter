# Metaphor, multimodality and generative AI: Synthetic images prompted by figurative expressions in anti\-immigration discourse

**Autores:** Christopher Hart

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Complex social processes like migration are frequently understood via metaphors\. In right\-wing media and far\-right political discourses, two metaphors that abound draw on flood and invasion frames to problematise immigration and legitimise hostile attitudes and actions towards it\. In so far as metaphor involves the construction of mental imagery, it is intimately bound with visual experience, including semiotically mediated visual experience\. In a semiotic landscape increasingly populated by synthetic images, I argue that the meaning of metaphors may be modulated by AI\-generated visual content and that this is especially important in contexts of political communication\. The study investigates images generated by prompts instantiating an immigration is flood versus an immigration is invasion metaphor to inquire if and how they differ by source\-frame and what are the ideological implications of the images returned\. Following an initial pilot study, several specific semiotic features are analysed, including colour, properties of the figure \(size, shape, orientation\) and ground elements \(environment, securitising features and frame\-consistent features like fire and water\)\. Results show significant differences between metaphorical frames and highlight the potential that AI\-generated images have to reflect and reinforce ideological positions\.
