# Mendelian randomization of gut microbiota and exploratory testing of preventive effect of commercial Rothia mucilaginosa in constipated mice

**Autores:** Peishi Jiang; 杨小清; 张城浩; Yuwei Li; Chen Xu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Constipation is linked to gut microbiota dysbiosis, but causal microbial contributors and tractable bacterial interventions remain incompletely defined\. This study first performed two\-sample Mendelian randomization using Dutch Microbiome Project gut\-microbiome Genome\-Wide Association Study \(GWAS\) data \( N = 7,738\) and a European constipation GWAS \( N = 411,623\) to identify gut taxa linked to constipation\. Then, in vitro metabolomics and a loperamide\-induced constipation mouse model were used in male C57BL/6 mice to exploratorily test whether the commercial Rothia mucilaginosa strain BNCC363031 prevents loperamide\-induced constipation\. In an exploratory two\-sample MR screen across 207 gut taxa, genetically predicted Rothia was nominally associated with lower constipation risk \[odds ratio \( OR \) = 0\.916, p = 0\.0041\], and the species\-level Rothia mucilaginosa showed a concordant nominal protective association \( OR = 0\.921, p = 0\.0232\)\. In mice \( n = 8 per group\), preventive gavage partially restored loperamide\-suppressed defecation: the cross\-day mean fecal pellet count in the last 3 h per day was higher in the Rothia mucilaginosa group \(RG\) than in model controls \(MCs\) \(7\.94 vs\. 4\.41; p = 0\.0306\) but lower than in normal controls \(NCs\) \(13\.16; p = 0\.00162\), whereas body weight; small\-intestinal transit; and immunohistochemical zonula occludens\-1 \(ZO\-1\), Claudin\-1, and mucin 2 \(MUC2\) H\-scores were unchanged\. A fecal 16S V4 signal corresponding to R\. mucilaginosa was detected at a very low abundance in only 2/8 RG mice; in vitro metabolomics identified candidates of secreted metabolites, including acetic acid\. This study provides a reference for research on Rothia mucilaginosa in the context of constipation\.
