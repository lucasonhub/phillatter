# An orthogonal projection\-based iterative KNN classifier for sentiment analysis on small datasets

**Autores:** Soufiane El Mrabti; Amal Hjouji; Jaouad El\-Mekkaoui; Adil Hachmoud; Mohamed Lazzar

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

State\-of\-the\-art Sentiment Analysis \(SA\) models that achieve high performance typically require substantial training datasets\. To address this limitation, this paper introduces novel and efficient K\-Nearest Neighbors \(KNN\) variants for SA, particularly for scenarios involving limited, sparse, and high\-dimensional data\. Firstly, we propose the Orthogonal Projection\-based Iterative KNN classifier \(OPIKNN\), where the classification of unseen samples is performed based on their proximity to the pseudo\-nearest neighbors of the respective classes\. In the proposed classifier, the extreme\-nearest neighbors of the unseen samples are identified iteratively to determine the pseudo\-nearest neighbor using the orthogonal projection, which helps capture the class distribution more effectively\. Secondly, building upon OPIKNN, we develop the Fuzzy OPIKNN \(FOPIKNN\) classifier to address the problem of data uncertainty and reduce the effects of noise in limited datasets\. FOPIKNN uses a refinement step that shifts pseudo\-nearest neighbors toward class centroids based on their fuzzy membership degree to these classes to provide a robust class representation\. Extensive experimental results on six real\-world SA datasets from Kaggle and Amazon demonstrate the effectiveness and reliability of the proposed approaches in comparison with six state\-of\-the\-art KNN\-based variants and four benchmark Machine Learning \(ML\) classifiers\. In particular, the classification accuracy achieved by OPIKNN and FOPIKNN improves, on average, by 5\.8% and 7\.3%, respectively, relative to the standard KNN classifier\. Even more notably, the macro $$F\_1$$ \-score increases by 8\.5% and 9\.8%, respectively, demonstrating consistent performance gains across both balanced and imbalanced data distributions\.
