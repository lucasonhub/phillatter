# Chudnoff, Elijah: A Priorism in Moral Epistemology

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-19 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy\_\. forthcomingA priorism in moral epistemology holds that at least some moral judgments can be justified independently of sensory experience, by reason alone\. This article surveys the surrounding debates\. It begins with two influential arguments in favor of a priorism: an epistemic regress argument from Ewing and the British intuitionist tradition, and Kant’s argument from the metaphysics of morals\. Next up are two challenges to the regress argument\. The first derives from recent defenses of moral Inferentialism, the second derives from recent defenses of moral empiricist views on which moral judgments can be based on moral perception or emotion\. The article then compares rival explanations of a priori justification \(pure reasoning, epistemic necessity, understanding, and intuition\) and identifies the burdens each faces, such as how beliefs get based on understanding and the problem of morally abhorrent intuitive appearances\. It next examines how metaethical views, including non\-naturalism, primitivism, analytic naturalism, and constructivism, bear on a priorism\. Finally, it asks whether a priori justification has distinctive theoretical or practical significance, taking into account recent discussions of whether the a priori/a posteriori distinction is epistemically shallow\.
