# Digital Capability as a Mediator between Technology–Organization–Environment Factors and E\-Governance Integration: Evidence from the UAE Ministry of Energy and Infrastructure

**Autores:** Ibrahim Ahmed Alshamsi; Wan Nurul Karimah Wan Ahmed

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

E\-governance integration has become a strategic priority for governments seeking to improve interoperability, service delivery, and organizational efficiency through digital transformation\. Despite significant investments in digital technologies, empirical evidence remains limited regarding how Technology–Organization–Environment \(TOE\) factors contribute to e\-governance integration through the development of digital capability, particularly within the public sector of the United Arab Emirates \(UAE\)\. This study examines the mediating role of digital capability in the relationship between TOE factors and e\-governance integration in the UAE Ministry of Energy and Infrastructure \(MOEI\)\. A cross\-sectional survey was conducted among 236 MOEI employees involved in digital transformation and e\-governance activities across Abu Dhabi, Dubai, and Sharjah\. Data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling \(PLS\-SEM\) to assess the measurement model, structural relationships, and proposed mediation effects\. The findings indicate that organizational and environmental factors have significant positive effects on digital capability, whereas technology factors do not significantly affect digital capability\. Furthermore, digital capability has a significant positive effect on e\-governance integration and significantly mediates the relationships between organizational factors and e\-governance integration, as well as between environmental factors and e\-governance integration\. However, digital capability does not significantly mediate the relationship between technology factors and e\-governance integration\. These findings extend the TOE framework by demonstrating that digital capability functions as a critical organizational mechanism through which organizational and environmental conditions are translated into stronger e\-governance integration\. The study offers practical implications for public\-sector organizations by emphasizing the importance of organizational readiness, leadership commitment, workforce digital competencies, and supportive institutional environments for strengthening sustainable e\-governance integration\.
