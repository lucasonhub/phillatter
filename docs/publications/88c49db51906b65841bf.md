# Ai\-driven Automation, Universal Basic Income and Global Social Security Systems

**Autores:** Adams Adah Otakwu

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose: This article examines the adequacy of international labour, human rights and trade\-law frameworks for managing the digital labour transition driven by artificial intelligence, with attention to Universal Basic Income as a possible component of future social protection\. Methodology: Using a qualitative documentary research design, the study analyses international legal instruments, International Labour Organization standards \(ILOs\), United Nations \(UN\) human rights frameworks and World Trade Organization \(WTO\) materials through a four\-theme thematic coding structure\. Results: International law provides important foundations for technological labour transition through rights to work, social security, non\-discrimination and social dialogue, but does not establish Universal Basic Income as an international legal entitlement, while international trade rules facilitate technological diffusion without a comprehensive framework for distributing the social costs of displacement\. Conclusions: The article argues for an integrated international framework linking artificial intelligence governance, labour\-market transition, social protection, digital trade, skills development and sustainable fiscal mechanisms, while allowing states flexibility in determining whether Universal Basic Income should complement existing social security systems\. Limitations: The study relies on documentary evidence rather than empirical labour\-market data from affected workers, and rapidly evolving artificial intelligence capabilities mean exposure estimates may shift before policy frameworks are implemented\. Contributions: The article offers a conceptual bridge linking labour law, human rights law, trade governance and fiscal policy around managing technological transition while protecting human dignity\.
