# Anti\-Ro52/TRIM21 seropositivity in interstitial lung disease: effect modification by disease subtype and myositis\-specific antibody context

**Autores:** Junfeng Su; Yan Du; Chen Zhu; Haodong Zhou; Huyan Wang; Yinjun DU; Qiyuan Wang; Zexin Chen; Hanjun Cao; Man Huang; Jing Xue

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Anti\-Ro52/TRIM21 shows contradictory prognostic associations in interstitial lung disease \(ILD\), protective in some cohorts and adverse in others\. We evaluated whether it acts as an independent predictor or a phenotypic marker and whether its effect is modified by disease subtype and myositis\-specific antibodies\. Methods We retrospectively identified 538 adults with radiologically confirmed interstitial lung disease \(228 anti\-Ro52\-positive\) from a 2010–2020 single\-center cohort\. Cox proportional\-hazards models were sequentially adjusted for demographic, smoking, malignancy, and pulmonary\-function covariates\. Pre\-specified effect modification by connective tissue disease \(CTD\) status and myositis\-specific antibodies was assessed by likelihood\-ratio interaction tests with false\-discovery\-rate correction\. Robustness to missing data was supported by multiple imputation \( m = 20\) and a missing\-not\-at\-random \(MNAR\) tipping\-point analysis\. Results Ro52 positivity was associated with younger age, CTD predominance, and lower complement levels\. Pre\-specified effect modification was suggested: anti\-Ro52 trended toward lower mortality in CTD\-ILD \(HR 0\.73, 95% CI 0\.49–1\.07, P = 0\.104\) but did not independently increase mortality within the anti\-MDA5\-positive subgroup \(HR 1\.73, 95% CI 0\.66–4\.54\)\. Instead, the adverse prognostic effect of anti\-MDA5 was numerically greater among anti\-Ro52\-positive than Ro52\-negative patients \(MDA5 HR 10\.57, 95% CI 3\.60–31\.03 vs\. 2\.56, 95% CI 0\.97–6\.74\), although the formal interaction term did not reach statistical significance \( P = 0\.055\)\. The fully adjusted \(Model 6\) HR was 0\.72 \(95% CI 0\.52–0\.99; P = 0\.045\); further adjustment for CTD status \(Model 7\) attenuated the association to non\-significance \(HR 0\.75, 95% CI 0\.54–1\.04, P = 0\.091\), and IPCW weighting and time\-varying\-age analyses were likewise non\-significant; malignancy independently predicted mortality \(HR 1\.81, 95% CI 1\.15–2\.88; P = 0\.012\)\. Conclusion Anti\-Ro52 is a context\-dependent phenotypic marker of immune\-mediated ILD, not a universal prognostic biomarker\. Its prognostic association is heterogeneous across ILD phenotypes: it is not independently protective in CTD\-ILD \(adjusted HR 0\.73, P = 0\.104\), whereas the adverse prognostic effect of anti\-MDA5 was numerically greater among anti\-Ro52\-positive than Ro52\-negative patients \(MDA5 HR 10\.57 vs\. 2\.56\), although this interaction did not reach statistical significance \( P = 0\.055\); CTD\-status adjustment similarly attenuated the Ro52 association \(HR 0\.75, P = 0\.091\)\. These findings support stratified prognostic models integrating Ro52 with autoantibody and disease\-subtype context\.
