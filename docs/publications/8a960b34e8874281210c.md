# Digital technologies and operational efficiency: evidence from small and medium enterprises in Lagos State, Nigeria

**Autores:** Chituru Nkechinyere ALU; Alice Anese Shiyanbola; Oluwatoyosi Tolulope Olurin; Olalere Mayowa David; Chinedu Gandolph Ahannaya; Oluwaseun Atejioye; Itse Okoro

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Digital technologies enable businesses to secure and manage information, which can be accessed at any time, allowing authorised users to make decisions more efficiently\. Despite this potential, many SMEs in Nigeria are unable to fully maximise digital technologies, which limits their operational efficiency and competitiveness\. This paper examined the impact of digital technologies on operational efficiency of SMEs in Lagos State, Nigeria, using a quantitative survey research design\. A structured questionnaire was used to collect data from 166 SME owners, and both stratified and purposive sampling techniques were employed\. Data were analysed using descriptive and inferential\-regression analyses, while the paper was anchored on the technology acceptance and diffusion of innovation theories\. The findings indicate that artificial intelligence and cloud computing play significant roles in product forecasting and improving business processes, but do not significantly contribute to inventory management\. The paper further reveals that the effects of digital technologies are situational, requiring appropriate system integration, personnel capacity building, and alignment of operational processes\. These findings can guide Lagos State SME managers and policymakers in enhancing efficiency, competitiveness, and sustainable business development\.
