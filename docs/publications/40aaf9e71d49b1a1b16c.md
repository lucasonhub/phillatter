# Microvascular resistance reserve: insights into discordant resting and hyperemic epicardial coronary physiology

**Autores:** Aswin Babu; Isobelle Goldrick; Billy Broadbent; Rania Faeq; Charley Ann Budgeon; Roopa Krishnamoorthy; Primero Ng; Cara Barnes; D\. Sankhesara; Avadhesh Saraswat; I\. Shiekh; James Marangou; Richard F\. Alcock; Rachel C\. Orme; Carl Johann Schultz; Graham S\. Hillis; J\. Spiro

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Discordance between resting and hyperemic epicardial physiology is commonbut mechanistically unclear\. Microvascular resistance reserve \(MRR\), a novelindex of vasodilatory capacity, may provide insight\. We aimed to characterizethe prevalence and microvascular substrate of FFR/RFR discordance, andwhether MRR is independently associated with discordant phenotypes\. Methods We retrospectively analyzed 891 vessels from 723 consecutive patients with paired resting full\-cycle ratio \(RFR\) and fractional flow reserve \(FFR\) measurements \(June 2018–October 2024\)\. Vessels were categorized as concordant or discordant\. An operator\-selected subset of 210 underwent coronary flow reserve \(CFR\), index of microvascular resistance \(IMR\), and MRR assessment\. Multivariable logistic regression identified predictors of each discordant phenotype \(FFR−/RFR\+ and FFR\+/RFR−\)\. Results Discordance was present in 195 vessels \(22%\); 121 \(13\.6%\) FFR−/RFR\+ and 74 \(8\.3%\) FFR\+/RFR−\. Discordance was more common in the LAD \(25% vs\. 17%, OR 1\.68, 95% CI 1\.20–2\.36, p = 0\.003\), driven predominantly by the FFR−/RFR\+ phenotype \(OR 2\.43, 95% CI 1\.56–3\.78, p &lt; 0\.001\)\. In the microvascular subset \( n = 210\), reduced CFR \(OR 0\.77, 95% CI 0\.59–0\.97, p = 0\.039\) and reduced MRR \(OR 0\.81, 95% CI 0\.65–0\.97, p = 0\.037\) were independently associated with the FFR−/RFR\+ phenotype, while neither predicted the FFR\+/RFR− phenotype\. However, among 45 discordant vessels with microvascular data, MRR provided greater discrimination than CFR \(AUC 0\.712, 95% CI 0\.539–0\.884 vs\. 0\.608, 95% CI 0\.434–0\.783, p = 0\.018\)\. Discussion These exploratory, hypothesis\-generating findings suggest that impaired microvascular vasodilatory reserve, expressed predominantly as elevated resting flow, may contribute mechanistically to the FFR−/RFR\+ phenotype\.
