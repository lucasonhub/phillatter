# Advanced age at onset is associated with distinct functional brain reorganization in multiple sclerosis

**Autores:** Nicolò Tedone; Paola Valsasina; Alessandro Meani; Paolo Preziosa; Marta Grosselle; Federica Esposito; Massimo Filippi; Maria Assunta Rocca

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Functional network reorganization supports compensatory mechanisms in multiple sclerosis, but aging and later disease onset may alter this balance\. Late\-onset multiple sclerosis \(age at onset ≥50 years\) represents a relevant subgroup to examine how disease onset in older age is associated with functional connectivity abnormalities\. In this cross\-sectional study, we investigated resting\-state functional connectivity alterations in late\-onset multiple sclerosis versus adult\-onset multiple sclerosis and their associations with clinical, cognitive, and structural MRI measures\. Thirty\-four late\-onset multiple sclerosis, 169 sex\- and disease duration–matched adult\-onset multiple sclerosis, and 163 age\- and sex\-matched healthy controls underwent clinical, neuropsychological \(Brief Repeatable Battery of Neuropsychological Tests\), and MRI assessment including resting\-state functional MRI\. Independent component analysis quantified resting\-state functional connectivity within ten large\-scale networks\. Between\-group differences were tested using sex\- and scanner\-adjusted voxel\-wise models \(p&lt;0\.001, uncorrected, cluster extent ≥10 voxels\); resulting abnormal clusters were then examined for exploratory uncorrected associations with Expanded Disability Status Scale, disease duration, cognitive performance, T2\-hyperintense lesion volume, and normalized brain volume, without correction for multiple comparisons\. At MRI acquisition, compared with the adult\-onset multiple sclerosis group, the late\-onset multiple sclerosis group was significantly older \(median age: 60\.5 \[IQR: 56\.6–66\.1\] versus 35\.6 \[IQR: 30\.0–40\.1\] years; pFDR=0\.006\), showed higher Expanded Disability Status Scale score \(pFDR=0\.016\) and higher T2\-hyperintense lesion volume \(pFDR=0\.028\) despite similar disease duration \(median: 4\.5 \[IQR: 1\.7–13\.2\] versus 6\.0 \[IQR: 2\.1–11\.8\] years; pFDR=0\.894\), whereas no significant between\-group cognitive difference was found\. Both multiple sclerosis groups showed widespread functional connectivity abnormalities versus healthy controls, partly overlapping and partly distinct\. Adult\-onset multiple sclerosis displayed functional connectivity increases in the executive control and occipital default mode networks\. Late\-onset multiple sclerosis showed diffuse functional connectivity alterations, including decreases in the left fronto\-parietal, thalamic, and occipital default mode networks\. Exploratory uncorrected correlation analyses included 117 tested associations\. In late\-onset multiple sclerosis, decreased functional connectivity in the left fronto\-parietal network related to lower normalized brain volume and worse cognition \(p=0\.02–0\.03\), whereas altered thalamic network connectivity related to higher Expanded Disability Status Scale, longer disease duration, and higher T2\-hyperintense lesion volume \(p=0\.01\-0\.02\)\. These associations did not survive correction for multiple comparisons and should therefore be considered hypothesis\-generating\. These findings suggest that late\-onset multiple sclerosis was associated with a distinct resting\-state functional connectivity profile compared with adult\-onset multiple sclerosis within a cross\-sectional, age\-matched healthy control framework, possibly reflecting the combined contribution of multiple sclerosis\-related damage, age\-related vulnerability, and the more severe clinical profile of late\-onset multiple sclerosis\.
