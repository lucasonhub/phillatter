# Lysine lactylation in glioma: from metabolic stress to epigenetic remodeling and therapeutic resistance

**Autores:** Xiaosong Hu; Li Yang; Yanghao Hu; Hongjuan Cui

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Glioma is characterized by profound metabolic reprogramming and sustained lactate accumulation; however, the mechanisms by which lactate\-rich metabolic stress is translated into stable malignant phenotypes remain incompletely understood\. Lysine lactylation \(Kla\), a recently identified post\-translational modification, has emerged as a critical nexus linking lactate metabolism to durable epigenetic and protein\-state adaptations\. In glioma, accumulating evidence indicates that Kla contributes to multiple dimensions of tumor biology, including oncogenic transcriptional activation, glioma stem\-like cell \(GSC\) maintenance, immunosuppressive remodeling, and therapeutic resistance\. Mechanistically, histone Kla rewires metabolic stress\-responsive transcriptional programs, whereas non\-histone Kla executes rapid, transcription\-independent responses by directly modifying key regulatory proteins involved in DNA repair, signal transduction, and metabolic adaptation\. This review systematically summarizes current progress on the lactate\-Kla axis in glioma, encompassing the basis of lactate metabolism, the molecular regulatory network of Kla, and the distinct yet coordinated functions of histone and non\-histone lactylation\. We further discuss how Kla contributes to temozolomide \(TMZ\) resistance, radioresistance, and chemoradiotherapy adaptation, and outline a hierarchical therapeutic framework targeting this pathway\. Despite rapid advances, important areas requiring further investigation include subtype\-specific variation in lactate–Kla biology and the potential crosstalk between Kla and other post\-translational modifications\. A better understanding of these issues will help define the biological significance of the lactate–Kla axis and guide the future development of lactylation\-based therapeutic strategies in glioma\.
