# Horiuchi, Shinnosuke: Enabling later life: a four\-pillar framework for AI, robotics, and human enhancement in super\-aged societies

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_AI and Society\_:1\-12\. forthcomingArtificial intelligence \(AI\) and robotics are increasingly proposed as responses to population aging, care shortages, social isolation, and cognitive decline\. Yet technologies for older adults are often evaluated mainly by how well they compensate for loss or manage risk\. Using a theory\-driven conceptual synthesis, this article proposes a different orientation: AI\- and robotics\-enabled sociotechnical arrangements should be assessed by how they expand or constrain later\-life agency\. Drawing together gerontechnology, social and assistive robotics, smart\-home monitoring, digital ageism, AI ethics, human enhancement, and capability\-oriented accounts of aging and disability, this article develops a four\-pillar evaluative framework comprising substantive opportunity, practical control, relation, and responsibility\. It argues, first, that in super\-aged societies, AI and robotics help constitute agency, dependency, care, and responsibility rather than merely serving pre\-existing needs\. Second, enhancement in later life should be understood not as youthfulness or optimization but as widening meaningful possibilities under changing bodily and social conditions\. Third, the ethical quality of a technology depends on the wider arrangement in which it is designed, deployed, maintained, interpreted, and refused\. The framework translates these claims into design and governance questions for researchers, care institutions, and policymakers\. It shifts evaluative attention beyond narrow deficit management to examine how AI\-mediated environments support people in aging with dignity, relation, and room for revision and refusal\. \(direct link \)
