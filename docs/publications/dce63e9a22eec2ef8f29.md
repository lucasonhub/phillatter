# A Brain Uptake Constant for Mathematical Modeling of Cerebral Radiopharmaceutical Uptake: A Synthetic Computational Study

**Autores:** Matheus dos Santos Farias

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Compartmental and tracer\-kinetic models are widely used to describe radiopharmaceutical uptake in brain PET and SPECT, but in simple analytical models the fraction of transferred activity that reaches the brain is seldom isolated as a parameter of its own\. This study defines a dimensionless Brain Uptake Constant, $\\psi$, as the uptake fraction in a two\-stage first\-order model of cerebral radiopharmaceutical activity and examines its behaviour in a purely synthetic setting\. Activity in the systemic pool, $A\(t\)$, is lost at a first\-order rate $k$; cerebral activity, $A\_c\(t\)$, receives a source term $\\psi k A\(t\)$ and is lost at an effective decay rate $\\lambda$\. The closed\-form solution was evaluated directly for $A\_0 = 100$\~MBq, $k = 0\.25$\~min$^\{\-1\}$, $\\lambda = 0\.05$\~min$^\{\-1\}$ and $\\psi = 1\.00$, 0\.75, 0\.50 and 0\.25 over 0\-60\~min\. Deterministic Gaussian fields were used to show the spatial effect of $\\psi$ as surface plots and heat maps\. Each curve rises, passes through a single maximum at $t\_\{\\mathrm\{peak\}\} = 8\.047$\~min and then decays\. The peak time does not depend on $\\psi$, whereas the peak activity \(66\.87\~MBq for $\\psi = 1$\) and the 0\-60\~min time\-integrated activity scale linearly with $\\psi$\. In the spatial fields, $\\psi$ changes the amplitude but not the prescribed Gaussian widths\. No clinical, PET or SPECT data were used, so the results say nothing about clinical validity\. The model is offered as a simple parameterization that still has to be tested against measured dynamic imaging data with independently estimated kinetic parameters\.
