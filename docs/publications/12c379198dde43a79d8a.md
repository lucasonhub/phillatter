# PLASMON\-ENHANCED OPTICAL PROBES TOWARDS POINT\-OF\-CARE TESTING

**Autores:** Yingjie Hang

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Enzyme\-linked immunosorbent assay \(ELISA\) is a common method for protein biomarker detection but requires venipuncture samples sent to central labs, causing delays of hours or days\. In contrast, point\-of\-care \(POC\) devices like paper lateral flow strips \(PLFSs\) use finger\-prick samples, deliver results within 20 minutes, enable immediate decisions during visits, allow longitudinal biomarker tracking, and improve accessibility in resource\-limited settings\. PLFSs are simple, low\-cost, and easy to use; however, conventional PLFSs suffer from limited sensitivity and poor interference resistance, especially in blood samples\. To address these issues, this dissertation develops plasmon\-enhanced optical probes, studies plasmonic enhancement mechanisms, and engineers PLFS structures, resulting in improved sensitivity and reduced interference in human fluid testing\. First, a sandwich\-structured nanoparticle \(Ag\-core@silica@dye@silica\-shell\) has been designed as a visible\-light plasmonic fluorescence probe for nasal swab fluids\. Finite\-Difference Time\-Domain \(FDTD\) simulations show that plasmon enhances fluorescence via excitation and emission \(Purcell effect\) mechanisms\. These probes are integrated into PLFSs enabled sensitive detection of SARS coronavirus nucleoprotein and clinical COVID\-19 samples, outperforming colorimetric PLFS\. Second, tuning fluorescence into the near\-infrared \(NIR\) biological window reduces light absorption, scattering, and autofluorescence in blood testing, though NIR probes often have lower quantum efficiency\. FDTD simulations combined with machine learning identified key factors for plasmonic enhancement, guiding design of an optimal SiO₂ core\-Au shell NIR probe\. This approach reduced experimental trials and costs\. Third, NIR surface\-enhanced Raman scattering \(SERS\) offers fingerprinting peaks and high interference resistance in blood\. However, tuning plasmonic bands to NIR while maintaining electromagnetic enhancement at practical particle sizes is challenging\. Charge transfer plasmon \(CTP\) modes enhance fields and allow spectral tuning but were previously limited to solid substrates\. A scalable synthesis of Au\-Ag heterostructure nanoparticles with tunable CTP modes in colloids has been developed, boosting SERS signals\. Fourth, these Au\-Ag nanoparticles served as NIR SERS probes integrated into a PLFS with an “on\-strip” blood plasma separation filter and surface functionalization to improve signal\-to\-noise ratio\. The resulting PLFS detected HIV in clinical samples with sensitivity surpassing commercial lab instruments and existing POC kits\. In summary, this work advances understanding of plasmonic fluorescence and SERS enhancements, informs future probe design, and introduces three new plasmonic probes with broad applications in optical assays and bioimaging\. The PLFS platforms equipped with the optical probes developed offer versatile solutions for POC testing\.
