# Delay Complexity Dimension: Learning with Delayed and Censored Labels

**Autores:** Madhava Gaikwad

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

We study PAC learning of binary classifiers when labels arrive after random delays and are censored beyond an observation window $W$\. How many samples does this observation model require? We propose a distribution\-dependent answer, the Delay Complexity Dimension \(DCD\)\. The DCD combines the VC dimension with the exact label\-information rate of the observation channel\. Every learner needs $Ω\(\\mathrm\{DCD\}/\\varepsilon^2\)$ samples\. An oracle pseudo\-label ERM matches this rate on the instance family of the lower bound, up to constants and logarithmic factors\. On that family the DCD is within a constant factor of the classical censored\-ERM complexity, and whether the DCD rate is achievable where the two separate remains open\. The theory also yields a quantization scheme under which delay information lowers the residual label variance, an expedition policy with a $\(1\-1/e\)$ guarantee for its version\-space objective, and a phenomenon with no analogue in the no\-delay setting\. Under a fixed collection horizon, a moderate window can strictly beat immediate labels\. Experiments on synthetic data and the Criteo conversion logs support these predictions\. A budget\-matched test of a companion labeler\-diversity prediction is reported as a negative result\.
