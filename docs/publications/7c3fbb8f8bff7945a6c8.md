# Development of a multilingual speech\-to\-text translation model to support artisans’ business communication

**Autores:** O\. S\. Ojo; O\. Ajayi; O\. E\. Oyebode; O\. Oladipo

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Effective communication between artisans and customers remains difficult in multilingual commercial settings in which English interacts with indigenous and contact languages such as Yoruba and Nigerian Pidgin\. This study developed and evaluated a lightweight English\-to\-Yoruba and English\-to\-Nigerian Pidgin speech translation system for artisans’ everyday business communication\. The system is a five\-phase cascaded architecture comprising automatic speech recognition \(ASR\), text normalisation, an intermediate linguistic representation, language\-specific translation, and text\-to\-speech \(TTS\) synthesis\. Its hierarchical staging follows the processing principles of the Hierarchical Efficient Neural Transducer for Speech Recognition and Translation \(HENT\-SRT\), while the neural components of that design are replaced by transparent retrieval mechanisms suited to resource\-constrained devices\. A domain\-specific parallel corpus of 2,000 artisan oriented expressions per target language, built from a needs analysis with artisans in southwestern Nigeria and covering automotive repair, pricing negotiation, service delivery and customer relations, supports translation through exact bilingual lookup, with a fallback to fuzzy retrieval that combines sequence similarity and Jaccard token overlap under a confidence threshold\. The system was evaluated on 15 spoken utterances using word error rate \(WER\) for recognition and BLEU and METEOR for translation, reported at corpus and per\-utterance levels with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals\. Yoruba achieved mean BLEU and METEOR of 0\.93 and 0\.93, and Nigerian Pidgin 0\.90 and 0\.92\. Mean WER was approximately 0\.00 on the 14 scorable utterances\. Thirteen of 15 utterances \(87%\) were translated perfectly into both languages; one utterance failed completely and one failed partially in Pidgin\. Mean ASR latency was 1\.60 s, and translation accounted for less than 1% of end\-to\-end wall\-clock time\. The confidence intervals for the two languages overlapped substantially, so no reliable performance difference between them could be established at this sample size\. The findings establish the feasibility of a lightweight, transparent speech translation pipeline for in\-domain artisan communication and identify numeric expression handling, held out evaluation, and neural extension as the priorities for future work\.
