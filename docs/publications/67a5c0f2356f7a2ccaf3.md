# Toward “Obesityomics”: Domain‐Specific Omics in Precision Obesity Medicine with a Brief AI Perspective

**Autores:** Nadia Abu Samra; Sara G\. I\. Suliman; Mohammed Al\-Hadad; Dorret Irene Boomsma; Fadwa Alhantoobi; Maithah Ebraheim AlSamahi; Haneen Abufarajallah; Alia Al Tikriti; Zeyar Aung; Selena E\. Richards; Abdulrahim A\. Sajini; Mira Mousa; Fatima Sulaiman; Wael M\. Osman

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: Obesity&\#x27;s global rise has outpaced the effectiveness of &quot;one\-size\-fits\-all&quot; prevention and treatment strategies\. The promise of precision medicine lies in addressing the etiological and biological diversity of obesity and transforming it into actionable endotypes\. OBJECTIVES: In a narrative review, we analyze &quot;obesityomics,&quot; looking at the contributions each omics layer makes to understanding and managing obesity\. The areas we evaluate are genomics \(heritability, polygenic risk, and monogenic forms\), epigenomics \(development and environment imprinting\), transcriptomics \(tissue\-specific programs of adipose, liver, muscle, and hypothalamus\), proteomics \(signaling networks and adipokines\), metabolomics and lipidomics \(fuel partitioning, lipotoxicity, and metabolic inflection points\), and microbiome science \(metabolites, host\-microbe interactions, and microbial ecology\)\. Our domain\-by\-domain \(single\-omics\) approach demonstrates how disease taxonomy, causal pathways, and biomarkers can be refined and targeted therapeutically\. DISCUSSION: We argue that &quot;obesityomics&quot; is a pragmatic approach to aligning omics modalities to the right questions at the right time, not a wholesale merger of datasets\. Artificial intelligence \(AI\)\-including machine learning and deep learning\-should be used in conjunction with quality control, feature selection, modest data integration, and obesity intervention prediction\. Among the key challenges in managing sensitive multilayer data are harmonization, standardization, reproducibility, equitable representation, and governance\. The translation priorities include prospective studies related to patient\-centered outcomes, biomarker panels that are clinic\-ready, interoperability standards, and cost\-effectiveness evaluations\. CONCLUSION: The review maps concrete steps to assist people with obesity in making better choices by identifying what each omics domain excels at and introducing a brief AI perspective\.
