# Women’s voice in the South African entrepreneurial ecosystem: implications for support interventions

**Autores:** Sinqobile Joseph Mkhwanazi; Reward Utete

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction While there are numerous claims that women entrepreneurship is in a precarious state after the recent upheavals, the situation of their businesses has not been sufficiently examined\. Despite the subject&\#x27;s pertinence, to the best of the authors&\#x27; knowledge, the existing literature gives scant attention to the current situation of women entrepreneurs in South Africa in the post\-pandemic era\. Grounded in Resource\-Based Theory and Institutional Theory, this study empirically investigates the current state of women entrepreneurs in Durban\. Methods Adopting the quantitative research approach, data were collected using a closed\-ended questionnaire, of which 349 SMEs&\#x27; responses were subjected to both descriptive and inferential analysis\. Exploratory Factor Analysis \(EFA\) using Principal Axis Factoring with Promax rotation identified the underlying constructs, followed by reliability and multiple regression analyses\. Results Entrepreneurial Constraints \(EC\), Entrepreneurial Support \(ES\), Entrepreneurial Participation \(EP\), and Business Environment and Regulatory Climate \(BERC\) emerged as the key constructs\. The findings reported low satisfaction with entrepreneurial support, particularly financial, infrastructural, training and guidance support\. EC was the most prominent challenge, with inadequate capital, skills deficits and low profitability being highly reported\. Regression analysis showed that ES and BERC were positively associated with EP, while EC was negatively associated with EP\. Collectively, the predictors explained 46\.3% of the variance in EP\. Discussion The findings indicate that women’s entrepreneurial participation is shaped by the interaction of resource availability and institutional conditions\. Strengthening financial assistance, skills development, mentorship, infrastructure, guidance and the regulatory environment may address the multidimensional barriers affecting women entrepreneurs\.
