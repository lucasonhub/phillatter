# Clinical characteristics, treatment patterns and long\-term outcomes of warm and mixed autoimmune hemolytic anemia: insights from a real\-world study

**Autores:** Junan Zhang; Yuan Li; Jiewen Qiu; 郑以州; Meili Ge; Fengkui Zhang; Jun Shi; Xin Zhao

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective Autoimmune hemolytic anemia \(AIHA\) represents a heterogeneous group of autoimmune disorders, with limited large\-scale real\-world evidence from Asian populations\. This study aimed to characterize the clinical characteristics, treatment patterns, and long\-term outcomes of patients with warm AIHA \(wAIHA\) and mixed AIHA \(mAIHA\)\. Methods This single\-center retrospective study screened 629 patients with AIHA hospitalized in China between 2014 and 2023\. After excluding 32 patients with cold AIHA and 31 with DAT\-negative AIHA, 566 patients \(449 wAIHA and 117 mAIHA\) were included, with a median follow\-up of 57 months\. Results Compared with wAIHA, mAIHA patients presented with earlier onset, poorer bone marrow compensation, and cold AIHA\-like features despite comparable hemoglobin levels\. Secondary AIHA was more prevalent in mAIHA \(39\.3% vs\. 22\.5%\)\. Glucocorticoids remained the first\-line treatment with similar response rates in both subtypes, while rituximab was the predominant second\-line therapy\. Treatment strategies and outcomes with multiple emerging therapies were also summarized\. Median event\-free survival was 17 months, with relapses mainly occurring during treatment tapering or infections\. Estimated overall survival \(OS\) rates at 1, 3, and 5 years were 96%, 92%, and 89%, respectively, with infections and disease progression being leading causes of mortality\. Independent risk factors associated with OS included age ≥60, hemoglobin at onset &lt;6 g/dL, bone marrow responsiveness index &lt;121, male sex, and an IgM\+IgA\-mediated serological pattern, whereas cold agglutinin titers demonstrated limited value in predicting OS or treatment response\. Conclusions mAIHA and wAIHA share comparable glucocorticoid response and long\-term survival despite distinct clinical and serological characteristics\. This large real\-world cohort delineates the profiles of wAIHA and mAIHA and identifies baseline factors associated with OS to support individualized management\. Emerging therapies present promising prospects in AIHA management\.
