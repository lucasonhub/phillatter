# Artificial Intelligence as a Second Reader for Focused Point\-of\-Care Ul\-trasound \(POCUS\) in the Emergency Department: A Scoping Review:

**Autores:** Milad Bakhshi; Saba Aghajanai; Aliraza Majidi

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Artificial intelligence \(AI\)/ machine learning \(ML\) as a second reader for focused point\-of\-care ultrasound \(POCUS\) may address two key emergency department \(ED\) challenges: operator variability and workflow delays\. This review maps the existing literature on the application of AI/ML as a second reader for POCUS in the ED\. Methods: We included studies on AI/ML for POCUS interpretation, acquisition guidance, or quality assessment in the ED/urgent care settings, with AI as a second reader supporting clinical decisions\. We excluded fully autonomous AI, non\-emergency settings, healthy volunteers, phantom data, and non\-English publications\. Two reviewers independently screened records from five databases \(PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, IEEE Xplore; 2020– August 17 2026\), extracting data on study characteristics, POCUS application, AI role, performance metrics, technology readiness level \(TRL\), and clinical implementation status\. Results: Of 1,884 records identified, 44 studies met inclusion criteria\. Lung ultrasound was the most frequent application \(38\.6%\)\. AI’s primary role was as an interpretation assistant \(77\.3%\)\. Models demonstrated high diagnostic performance \(e\.g\., sensitivities ≥95% for hemoperitoneum and DVT detection\), with one study achieving 100% sensitivity and specificity for pneumothorax detection, significantly outperforming 11 expert clinicians\. However, most studies were at a preclinical validation stage \(TRL: 4–5; 56\.8%\), with only 18 studies \(40\.9%\) testing prototypes in live clinical workflows\. Major limitations identified were small, single\-center datasets, reliance on expert\-acquired images, and lack of investigation into critical real\-world challenges like domain shift and automation bias\. Conclusions: AI for POCUS in the ED shows strong diagnostic performance in controlled research settings but remains distant from robust clinical integration\. The field is characterized by a predominance of early\-stage validation studies\. Future research must prioritize multi\-center, multi\-device external validation, inclusion of novice\-generated data, prospective workflow integration, and explicit safety evaluations to translate technical promise into reliable clinical impact\.
