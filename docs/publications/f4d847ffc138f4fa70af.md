# Concept Representations in Vector Space: Quantifying Sharedness and Idiosyncrasy of Meaning

**Autores:** Simon Josef Durstewitz; Denis Buehler

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Concepts are the basic units through which we navigate the world and communicate meaning\. But do you and I really mean the same thing when we think or speak about a concept? The present study develops a measurement framework for modeling and analyzing interindividual variation in concept representations through sentence embeddings, dense vector representations mapping entire utterances into a shared geometric space\. The framework is developed in a theoretical argument and validated, demonstrated, and subjected to robustness checks in a pre\-registered study\. Short personal definitions, elicited from $N = 125$ participants, are encoded as sentence embeddings and submitted pairwise to a crossed random effects model yielding two concept\-level quantities: sharedness \($S\_b$\), the expected dissimilarity between two randomly drawn individuals, and idiosyncrasy \($I\_b$\), the person\-specific share of total dyadic variance\. Two validity tests support the approach: the embedding space fully recovers concept and domain organization from participant definitions even with the target word excluded, and a parameter\-free classifier achieves 0\.630 accuracy in a within\-participant extensional prediction task against a chance baseline of 0\.500 and a consistency ceiling of 0\.893\. The framework finds theoretically interpretable variation in $S\_b$ and establishes $I\_b$ as a reliable source of dyadic variance across concepts\. Results replicate across three encoders varying in architecture and capacity, providing a reproducible and scalable instrument for the empirical study of meaning variation\.
