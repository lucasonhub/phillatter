## Resumo

Abstract The indicator cell assay platform \(iCAP\) is a blood\-based diagnostic approach that uses standardized cells as biosensors to transduce complex circulating signals into cell\-based gene\-expression profiles for multivariate disease classification\. We developed an iCAP for early detection of Alzheimer’s disease \(AD\-iCAP\) by incubating plasma from patients with early\-stage AD \(mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia\) and non\-affected controls with standardized neurons and training classifiers on transcriptomic responses\. Modeling features were restricted to a predefined 84\-gene panel derived independently by analyzing 5xFAD mouse plasma in the AD\-iCAP\. In this exploratory study, a model was trained and tested using 191 retrospectively collected human plasma samples across three cohorts\. The classifier achieved AUC = 0\.64 \(95% CI 0\.51–0\.78\) in an external validation set during model development \(n = 82\) and AUC = 0\.77 \(95% CI 0\.57–0\.96\) in a blind test set \(n = 23\)\. The multivariate model incorporated 5 genes including AKT3 , GPR50 , PALLD , RGMA , and PCDH10 , implicated in AD, neuronal signaling and/or cytoskeletal dynamics\. The cell\-based approach is orthogonal to direct detection of circulating amyloid and tau, and these exploratory findings support further evaluation of AD\-iCAP in prospective studies for combinatorial diagnostics and patient stratification for clinical trials\.
