# Embodiment\-Classifier Guidance: Transferring Frozen VLAs Across Robot Dynamics without Demonstrations

**Autores:** Devin Han; Hershraj Niranjani

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Flow\-based policies such as $π\_0$ are trained on a fixed set of embodiments, and transferring them to new ones is an open problem\. To adapt a frozen $π\_0$ to a new embodiment without additional demonstrations or parameter updates, we train a lightweight embodiment classifier to distinguish successful action chunks on the source body from those on the target body, using a few calibration tasks\. On transfer tasks that $π\_0$ saw on its source body but the classifier was never trained on, we nudge each denoising step of $π\_0$ with the classifier&\#x27;s gradient, which estimates the direction from the distribution of successful actions on the source body toward that on the target body\. On the LIBERO simulation benchmark with four modified controllers, embodiment\-classifier guidance raises $π\_0$&\#x27;s average success on the target bodies from 72% to 80% across 12 suite\-controller pairs, with a 31% relative reduction in failures \(11 of 12 gain; 5 significant after Holm correction\)\. In simulation, guidance is data\-efficient, needing few target\-body rollouts per task, which makes it promising for sim\-to\-real transfer, where rollouts are costly\. Guidance is mixed on weak wrists and harmful on a locked wrist, when the classifier lacks data or favors infeasible motions\. Collecting more target\-body data with guided $π\_0$ can fill such gaps: on one weak\-wrist arm, guided $π\_0$ solved a task on which unguided $π\_0$ had zero successes in the original data, and retraining the classifier on the guided successes raised success from 52\.5% to 70\.7%\.
