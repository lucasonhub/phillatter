# Predischarge blood count and nutritional risk marker patterns associated with 30\-day unplanned readmissions for hematological toxicity after radiotherapy for nasopharyngeal carcinoma: a multicenter cohort study

**Autores:** Guifei Zhou; Qianxi Sha; 赵丽红; Suren Rao Sooranna; Ying Wang; Huiying Tan

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Early unplanned readmissions after radiotherapy\-based treatment may reflect residual toxicity and gaps in supportive care coordination\. We examined associations between predischarge hematological and albumin marker patterns and 30\-day hematological toxicity\-related unplanned readmission in nasopharyngeal carcinoma \(NPC\)\. Methods This multicenter retrospective cohort study included 607 patients discharged after hospitalization involving radiotherapy\-based treatment at three tertiary hospitals in southwestern China between December 2018 and March 2026\. The primary complete\-case sample comprised 588 patients with predischarge absolute neutrophil count \(ANC\), platelet count \(PLT\), and albumin measurements\. Core markers were ANC &lt;1\.5 × 10⁹/L, PLT &lt;200 × 10⁹/L, and albumin &lt;35 g/L\. We used association rule mining and Firth penalized logistic regression\. Chemotherapy and supportive care variables were examined in sensitivity analyses, and all\-cause 30\-day unplanned readmission was a supportive secondary outcome\. Results Based on the chronologically first readmission within 30 days, 77 of 607 patients \(12\.7%\) were classified as having an unplanned readmission; 58 of the 77 \(75\.3%\) were classified as hematological toxicity\-related\. In the primary sample, hematological readmission rates were 4\.9%, 13\.1%, and 17\.5% for zero, one, and two or more core markers, respectively\. Adjusted odds ratios \(ORs\) were 2\.86 \[95% confidence interval \(CI\) 1\.50–5\.46\] and 4\.33 \(95% CI 1\.82–10\.30\) for one and two or more markers versus zero markers\. Corresponding ORs were 2\.93 and 4\.55 after chemotherapy adjustment and 2\.59 and 3\.47 after supportive care adjustment\. The all\-cause analysis showed a similar gradient \(OR 2\.65, 95% CI 1\.52–4\.61; OR 3\.18, 95% CI 1\.43–7\.05\)\. Conclusion Routine predischarge measures were associated with early readmission risk, but the findings do not establish causality or a clinical decision rule\. Prospective external validation is required before clinical use\.
