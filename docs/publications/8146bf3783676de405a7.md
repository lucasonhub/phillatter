# Brevican immunoreactivity in the mouse cochlea during development and following noise\-induced hearing loss

**Autores:** So Young Kim; Nuri Jin; Ho Sun Lee; Min\-Hyun Park

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Perineuronal nets \(PNNs\) regulate synaptic stabilization and neural circuit development in the central nervous system\. Although BCAN\-associated extracellular matrix specializations have been described in the cochlea, their developmental organization and response to acoustic injury remain poorly understood\. Here we characterized BCAN\-associated extracellular matrix in the developing and adult mouse cochlea and examined changes following noise\-induced hearing loss \(NIHL\)\. HAPLN1 and neurocan were broadly detected around inner hair cells from postnatal day 7 \(P7\), whereas BCAN immunoreactivity was not detected at P7 but was detectable around the inner hair cell \(IhC\) region at P14\. RNA in situ hybridization and exploratory analysis of published single\-cell transcriptomic datasets characterized developmental patterns of BCAN mRNA signals in spiral ganglion\-associated cell populations\. Three\-dimensional cochlear clearing and light\-sheet imaging qualitatively revealed BCAN immunoreactivity surrounding a subset of cell bodies in the auditory nerve transitional region of adult cochleae\. Following permanent\-threshold\-shift–inducing noise exposure, BCAN immunoreactivity was markedly reduced in the IhC region and appeared less prominent around cell bodies in the auditory nerve transitional region\. These findings further characterize BCAN\-associated extracellular matrix patterns in the mouse cochlea and describe developmental changes in BCAN immunoreactivity and its reduction following severe NIHL\.
