# Traceable Burned\-Area Evidence for Territorial Governance: Scenario\-Based multi\-pyDEMATEL and ABBI Mapping in the Vesuvian Interface

**Autores:** Salvatore Polverino; Elif Gizem Yetkin; Hourakhsh Ahmad Nia

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

MODIS/EFFIS burned\-area archives provide reproducible information on wildfire occurrence, perimeter geometry, administrative attribution, mapped area, date fields, and land\-cover composition\. However, their translation into proportionate municipal documentation, monitoring, coordination, and territorial\-management evidence remains only partially formalized, especially in volcanic–urban interfaces where relatively compact burned\-area records may still generate non\-negligible administrative pressure\. This study develops a scenario\-based multi\-pyDEMATEL workflow for assessing Administrative Burned\-Area Burden in the Vesuvian interface, southern Italy\. Rather than modelling burn severity, hydrogeomorphic susceptibility, legal responsibility, or field\-validated damage, the analysis reframes MODIS/EFFIS records as traceable administrative evidence for multi\-criteria decision\-chain screening\. Four bounded objectives are addressed: \(i\) to construct a GIS\-ready burned\-area support for the Vesuvian interface while retaining Napoli and Campania layers as contextual references; \(ii\) to derive ten non\-hydrogeomorphic Administrative Burned\-Area descriptors describing burned\-area share, recurrence, large\-event dominance, administrative concentration, protected\-area exposure, agricultural exposure, urban/interface exposure, forest–shrubland signature, event\-duration burden, and reporting/update lag; \(iii\) to structure these descriptors through a scenario\-based multi\-pyDEMATEL ensemble comprising 121 scenario\-specific DEMATEL computations, and to audit the resulting fingerprints through PCA; and \(iv\) to translate the resulting Administrative Burned\-Area Burden Index into municipal ABBI classes suitable for QGIS visualization, documentation prioritization, and verification\-oriented monitoring\. The results indicate a marked concentration of Vesuvian burned\-area burden across a limited number of event and municipal supports, while the DEMATEL/PCA outputs help distinguish stable and divergent descriptor configurations within the administrative evidence system\. The resulting ABBI layer is therefore interpreted as a governance\-sensitive, GIS\-ready decision\-support product, not as a hazard map, burn\-severity product, legal\-liability surface, municipal\-responsibility boundary, or DEM\-derived susceptibility model\. The framework provides a reproducible audit chain for converting open burned\-area archives into cautious and interpretable territorial\-management evidence\.
