# Acrolein target\-linked toxicological perturbation and tumor\-immune microenvironment heterogeneity in colorectal cancer

**Autores:** Gongming Cheng; Liuqun Shan; Wenjie Shi; Xucheng Liu; Xuewen Tao; Jianbo Han

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Acrolein is a highly reactive aldehyde generated endogenously during lipid peroxidation and encountered through environmental and dietary exposures\. Although acrolein has established electrophilic, genotoxic, and oxidative\-stress effects, the extent to which acrolein\-linked gene perturbation is represented in colorectal cancer \(CRC\) transcriptomes and relates to tumor immune\-stromal heterogeneity remains incompletely characterized\. Methods We integrated direct human acrolein target evidence from the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database \(CTD\) with TCGA\-COAD/READ bulk RNA\-sequencing profiles and a single\-cell RNA\-sequencing subset from GSE231559\. The CTD curation yielded 179 unique direct human targets\. Bulk analyses included 624 primary\-tumor and 51 solid\-tissue normal profiles\. Single\-cell analyses included six tumor and three normal libraries, yielding 24,128 retained cells assigned to eight broad marker\-supported compartments\. Target dysregulation burden, immune\-stromal expression programs, and library\-aware single\-cell target localization were evaluated as transcriptomic summaries\. In addition, RT\-qPCR was performed in HCT 116 colorectal carcinoma cells to experimentally evaluate four measurable CTD targets \(NFKBIA, GSTP1, VIM, and GPX4\) after acrolein exposure\. Results The bulk tumor\-normal analysis identified 5,594 differentially expressed genes at FDR &lt; 0\.05 and \|log2 fold change\| ≥ 1\. Of 179 CTD acrolein targets, 155 were measurable in the bulk dataset and 43 met the differential\-expression threshold\. Mean absolute normal\-reference target burden was higher in tumors than normal tissue \(1\.96 vs\. 0\.75; difference 1\.21; Welch p = 3\.62e\-42\)\. Within primary tumors, target burden correlated negatively with T\-cell and endothelial programs \(rho = −0\.27 for each\), myeloid \(rho = −0\.20\), fibroblast\-ECM \(rho = −0\.19\), and antigen\-presentation programs \(rho = −0\.20\), and positively with cell cycle \(rho = 0\.20\)\. In single\-cell data, descriptive mean target scores were highest in myeloid cells \(0\.455\), fibroblasts \(0\.424\), and endothelial cells \(0\.344\)\. Cross\-scale effects for 154 shared targets showed modest concordance \(rho = 0\.31, p = 6\.95e\-05\)\. RT\-qPCR showed vehicle\-normalized relative expression of 2\.18 ± 0\.14 for NFKBIA, 1\.83 ± 0\.13 for GSTP1, 1\.57 ± 0\.15 for VIM, and 0\.71 ± 0\.09 for GPX4 after acrolein exposure \(mean ± SD of three biological replicates\)\. Conclusions Acrolein target\-linked expression perturbation is detectable in CRC and is associated with immune\-stromal heterogeneity across bulk and single\-cell transcriptomic scales\. RT\-qPCR provides targeted experimental support for acrolein\-responsive expression changes in NFKBIA, GSTP1, VIM, and GPX4 in HCT 116 cells\. These findings support a toxicogenomic framework for hypothesis generation but do not measure acrolein exposure in patients, establish causal toxicological effects in human tumors, or demonstrate a specific intercellular signaling mechanism\. Additional mechanistic, protein/adduct, dose\-response, and independent\-cohort validation will be required to determine the biological significance of the observed associations\.
