# Mangrove litter mixing alters elemental composition more frequently than mass loss, with species\-specific patterns across nitrogen\-enrichment plots

**Autores:** Zhouxiang Zheng; Qijuan Cao; Yuxi Chen; Mingxuan Zheng; 刘志彦 LIU Zhiyan; Yisheng Peng

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Mangrove litter decomposition contributes to coastal carbon and nutrient cycling, yet changes in litter mass may not fully reflect changes in elemental composition\. This study investigated litter decomposition and C: N stoichiometry in relation to species identity, nitrogen enrichment, and litter mixing in a subtropical mangrove wetland\. Methods Two field experiments were conducted on Hailing Island, southern China\. A previously reported nitrogen\-enrichment experiment included an untreated control and three nitrogen\-input levels \(22\.85, 114\.24, and 228\.48 g N m \-2 yr \-1 \)\. As each treatment was represented by a single plot, the results were examined descriptively\. A separate litter\-mixing experiment examined three mangrove species \( Sonneratia apetala , Kandelia obovata , and Avicennia marina \) and their two\- and three\-species mixtures\. Litter mass remaining, total organic carbon \(TOC\), total nitrogen \(TN\), total phosphorus \(TP\), and C:N ratios were measured during decomposition\. Results S\. apetala and A\. marina decomposed faster than K\. obovata , with differences corresponding to their initial litter chemistry\. Mass loss varied among nitrogen\-enrichment plots without a consistent response to increasing nitrogen input\. Compared with the control, mean aboveground\-litter TP was 14\.02% higher and N was 11\.53% lower in the high\-nitrogen plot\. In the litter\-mixing experiment, significant deviations from weighted single\-species expectations occurred in only 20% of mass\-remaining comparisons, compared with 65%, 70%, and 55% for TOC, TN, and TP, respectively\. Most elemental deviations occurred during the early and intermediate stages of decomposition\. The three\-species mixture did not consistently show greater mass loss than the two\-species mixtures\. Discussion Litter mixing produced more frequent changes in elemental composition than in mass remaining, with responses varying among species combinations\. These findings suggest that litter mass loss alone may not adequately reflect changes in nutrient composition during mangrove litter decomposition\. The nitrogen\-enrichment results also showed variation in litter stoichiometry, although these observations require confirmation through replicated field experiments\.
