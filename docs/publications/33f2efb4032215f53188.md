# Perioperative immunogenomics and clinical outcomes under ERAS\-based nursing care in muscle\-invasive bladder cancer

**Autores:** Lu\-Lu Xin; Jin\-jin Guo; Xin\-ying Zheng; Zhaoyang Li

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Surgical trauma is associated with perioperative immune perturbation in muscle\-invasive bladder cancer \(MIBC\)\. Enhanced Recovery After Surgery \(ERAS\) combines multidisciplinary perioperative management with structured nursing care, but its immunogenomic associations remain incompletely characterized\. Methods This retrospective historical\-control cohort included 126 MIBC patients undergoing radical cystectomy with Bricker ileal conduit \(ERAS\-based care, n = 63; conventional care, n = 63\)\. Serial peripheral immune profiling was performed at T0 \(preoperative\), T1 \(24 h postoperative\), and T2 \(POD7/discharge\)\. Tumor analyses included multiplex immunofluorescence, tumor mutational burden \(TMB\), and NanoString immune\-gene profiling; spatial transcriptomics was performed in 24 specimens \(12/group\)\. Clinical outcomes included 30\-day infectious complications and survival, while biomarker and immunogenomic analyses were considered exploratory\. Results ERAS\-based care was associated with a less pronounced postoperative CD8 \+ T\-cell decline \(22\.8% vs\. 41\.6%, P &lt; 0\.001\) and lower T1 serum IL\-6 \(43\.2 vs\. 87\.4 pg/mL, P &lt; 0\.001\)\. An exploratory four\-marker T1 immune index \(CD8 \+ count, IL\-6, sPD\-L1, and NK activity\) showed predictive performance for 30\-day infectious complications \(AUC = 0\.84, 95% CI 0\.76–0\.92\)\. In resection specimens, the ERAS cohort had a higher proportion of the immune\-inflamed tumor immune microenvironment \(TIME\) phenotype \(52\.4% vs\. 28\.6%, P = 0\.012\)\. Exploratory survival analyses also identified associations with an eight\-gene immune signature and TMB status; these findings require validation\. Conclusion In this retrospective cohort, ERAS\-based perioperative nursing and multidisciplinary care were associated with differences in systemic immune recovery, postoperative outcomes, and cross\-sectional tumor immune features\. The proposed immune\-marker and gene\-signature findings are exploratory and require independent validation before clinical implementation\.
