# Ergonomic risk factors for wrist pain among smartphone\-using university students: A cross\-sectional study

**Autores:** M Shoadiqin; Hendrik Hendrik

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Smartphone use has become integral to university students’ academic and social activities, but prolonged use combined with non\-ergonomic wrist positions and body postures may increase biomechanical strain and contribute to wrist pain\. This study examined ergonomic risk factors associated with wrist pain among smartphone\-using university students\. Methods: A quantitative observational cross\-sectional study was conducted from June to July 2026 among undergraduate Public Health students at Universitas Tamalatea Makassar\. A total of 78 students were recruited using accidental sampling from a population of 336 students\. Smartphone use, wrist position, and body posture were assessed using an ergonomics\-based questionnaire referring to Rapid Upper Limb Assessment principles, while wrist pain was measured using the Numeric Rating Scale\. Associations were analyzed using Pearson’s Chi\-square test, with odds ratios \(ORs\) and 95% confidence intervals \(CIs\) estimated from the 2×2 contingency tables\. Results: Wrist pain was reported by 64 students \(82\.1%\)\. High smartphone use was associated with greater odds of wrist pain than low use \(OR=3\.44; 95% CI: 1\.03–11\.51; p=0\.038\)\. A non\-ergonomic wrist position showed the strongest association with wrist pain \(OR=10\.14; 95% CI: 2\.52–40\.78; p&lt;0\.001\)\. Non\-ergonomic body posture was also significantly associated with wrist pain \(OR=4\.77; 95% CI: 1\.34–16\.98; p=0\.011\)\. Conclusion: Smartphone\-related ergonomic exposures were significantly associated with wrist pain among university students, particularly non\-neutral wrist positioning\. Campus\-based ergonomic education should emphasize neutral wrist alignment, appropriate posture, reduced prolonged smartphone use, and regular rest breaks to minimize musculoskeletal strain\.
