# Dose–response association and predictive value of plasma aldosterone concentration for cardiometabolic multimorbidity in patients with hypertension: a retrospective cohort study

**Autores:** Zhiqiang Zhang; Nanfang Li; Qing Zhu; Junli Hu; Jing Hong; Wenbo Yang; Pan Zhou; 马慧敏; Rui Ma; Ting Wu

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background The purpose of this study was to investigate the relationship between cardiometabolic multimorbidity \(CMM\) and plasma aldosterone concentration \(PAC\) among patients with hypertension, to characterize the potential dose–response pattern, and to assess whether the inclusion of PAC into a conventional risk model improves risk reclassification and clinical utility\. Methods Between January 2010 and January 2026, 7,677 hospitalized hypertensive patients without CMM at baseline were included in this retrospective cohort analysis\. The follow\-up period was 3\.52 years on average\. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression and restricted cubic spline \(RCS\) analyses were performed to examine the association between PAC and the risk of incident CMM\. The incremental predictive value of PAC was evaluated for 3\-year risk prediction using time\-dependent ROC analysis with inverse probability of censoring weighting \(IPCW\), survival\-based net reclassification improvement \(NRI\), integrated discrimination improvement \(IDI\), calibration assessment, and decision curve analysis \(DCA\)\. Based on the potential interaction observed in the subgroup analysis, RCS analyses stratified by serum potassium level and a bivariate penalized spline model were used to characterize the joint nonlinear associations of serum potassium and PAC with the risk of CMM\. The Boruta algorithm was additionally applied for feature selection and assessment of variable importance\. Results During follow\-up, 1,949 incident cases of CMM were documented\. Patients in the highest quartile of baseline PAC \(Q4\) had a considerably greater cumulative likelihood of CMM than those in the other quartiles, according to Kaplan–Meier analysis \(log\-rank P &lt; 0\.05\)\. In the multivariable Cox regression analysis, each 1\-ng/dL increment in PAC was associated with a 2% higher hazard of incident CMM \(hazard ratio \[HR\], 1\.02; 95% confidence interval \[CI\], 1\.02–1\.03\)\. Compared with the lowest quartile \(Q1\), the adjusted HRs were 1\.19 \(95% CI, 1\.04–1\.36\) for Q2, 1\.32 \(95% CI, 1\.16–1\.52\) for Q3, and 1\.65 \(95% CI, 1\.45–1\.87\) for Q4, with a significant trend across quartiles \( P for trend&lt;0\.001\)\. RCS analysis demonstrated a significant nonlinear association between PAC and the risk of CMM \( P for nonlinearity &lt; 0\.001\), identifying an estimated inflection point at approximately 15\.34 ng/dL, above which the risk curve showed an upward trend\. Subgroup analyses indicated that the PAC–CMM association may differ by serum potassium status \( P for interaction &lt; 0\.05\)\. After PAC was added to the baseline prediction model, the time\-dependent AUC increased from 0\.678 to 0\.735 \( P &lt; 0\.001\), with a net reclassification improvement of 0\.372 and an integrated discrimination improvement of 0\.038 \( P &lt; 0\.05\)\. DCA suggested that incorporating PAC may provide additional estimated net benefit across clinically relevant threshold probabilities\. The Boruta feature\-selection algorithm identified PAC as an important predictor, and the overall findings remained consistent across subgroup and sensitivity analyses\. Conclusion The possibility of a CMM incident was positively correlated with PAC in a nonlinear fashion, and this correlation differed depending on serum potassium levels\. Incorporating PAC into a conventional risk model was associated with improved predictive performance and greater net clinical benefit, suggesting that PAC may provide additional prognostic information for cardiometabolic risk stratification among patients with hypertension\.
