# Trace Evolution Bench: Measuring Whether Agents Improve from Their Own Environment Traces

**Autores:** Fangfei Li; Chenyang Zhao; Long Wang; Feng Tian; Lv Guo

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Self\-evolving agents are usually scored by endpoint Gain: held\-out success after an update minus success before\. That difference can credit a frozen rescore as evolution\. We introduce Trace Evolution Bench \(TEB\), a protocol that separates that rescore from a context or weight update, together with three frozen 80/20/40 packs\. An update is accepted only when validation does not fall, held\-out identifiers never enter the update, and a text update is not ranked with a weight update\. The primary scoreboard is coding, graded by hidden unit tests in a closed workspace\. On that pack \(40 held\-out tasks, one family\), DeepSeek v4 Pro under no\_evolve never changes its policy yet moves 7\.5, 5\.0, and 12\.5 percentage points across three seeds\. Of nine text\-update cells inspired by GEPA, ACE, and ExpeL, seven have positive excess above that seed&\#x27;s own band, and one gepa\_llm seed moves down by the width of its band \(\-12\.5, regression 0\.48\)\. GLM and MiniMax do not reproduce that seven\-of\-nine pattern\. No method stays above its own band on every seed of all three models: GLM has two paired cells above their own bands, and MiniMax expel\_llm seed 44 is a third \(regression 0\.30\)\. The methods were fixed on a validation screen before held\-out was read\. Office, graded by rule\-bounded delivery, and manufacturing table QA are separate packs: office DeepSeek does not exceed its own bands \(widest 17\.5 points\), and manufacturing has no paired positive excess beyond its seed band\.
