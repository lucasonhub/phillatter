# Advancements in Fonio \(Digitaria exilis\): Emerging Insights into Horticultural Development, Bioactive Composition, Functional Properties, and Drug Delivery Potential

**Autores:** 

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Fonio \(Digitaria exilis\) is an ancient orphan cereal of West Africa which has been recently highlighted in the scientific literature around the world because of its unique nutritional characteristics, its resistance to the adverse abiotic conditions and also its potential therapeutic uses\.The present review summarizes and consolidates the knowledge regarding the horticultural and genomic development, bioactive composition, functional properties, and pharmaceutical delivery systems from peer\-reviewed publications published between 2020 and 2025\.The genomic progress contains the release of a high quality tetraploid reference genome, the understanding of independent domestication pathways for white and black fonio, and agro\-morphological characterization of germplasm diversity\.Bioactive Profiling shows the presence of sulfur containing amino acids \(methionine and cysteine\) at about 150 and 350 mg/kg respectively, flavones such as apigenin and luteolin at about 150 and 350 mg/kg respectively, B group vitamins and essential minerals including iron \(3\-10 mg/100g\) and zinc \(1\.5\-4 mg/100g\)\.The functional properties achieved are antioxidant \(after germination\), antidiabetic \(animal studies\), anti\-inflammatory \(flavonoids\), and glutenfree\.In the pharmaceutical sector, the cross\-linked fonio starch nanoparticles \(180\-320 nm\) have been shown to have high encapsulation efficiency \(72\-84%\) and have released ibuprofen over 12 hours, whereas unmodified nanoparticles have released ibuprofen in 4\-6 hours\.Together, they indicate that fonio is a climate smart crop with great nutraceutical and pharmaceutical potential\.But limitations are still there such as low yield \(150\-500 kg/ha in traditional agriculture\), shatter and labor intensive post\-harvest processing, high content of phytic acid \(0\.5\-1\.4 g/100g\) which reduces the bioavailability of minerals\.The future research needs encompass molecular breeding by advanced
