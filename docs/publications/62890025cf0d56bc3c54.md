# A multiview video–text model detects obstructive coronary stenosis and predicts 1\-year cardiovascular events from angiography

**Autores:** Sarra Harrabi; Yichen Wu; Omar Majwal; Gabriel Asslo; George Azar; Geoffrey H\. Tison; Minhaj Ansari; Miloš Vukadinovic; David Ouyang; Joshua P\. Barrios; Jacques Delfrate; Robert Avram

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Invasive coronary angiography is the reference standard for diagnosing coronary artery disease, yet assessment is subjective, and existing artificial\-intelligence tools typically assess stenosis in individual frames or videos and do not integrate the full multiview angiographic study\. Here we show that DeepCORO\-CLIP, trained on 203,808 angiographic videos and reports from 28,117 patients, interprets whole multiple\-video angiograms at once\. It detects obstructive stenoses \(≥70%\), with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve \(AUROC\) of 0\.89 internally and externally, and identifies chronic total occlusion \(AUROC = 0\.90 to 0\.95\), intra\-coronary thrombus and calcification \(AUROC = 0\.90 to 0\.91\)\. It also predicts 1\-year cardiovascular events \(AUROC = 0\.79, 95% CI of 0\.74 to 0\.84, versus 0\.65 for a model derived using clinical variables\) and left ventricular ejection fraction \(mean absolute error 7\.3% versus 8\.8% for a previous model\), both achieved using coronary angiography alone\. In a blinded review of 365 evaluations across 288 angiograms, its reports were clinically usable in 76% of evaluations versus 88% for cardiologists, with comparable false positives \(17% versus 14%, P = 0\.38\); residual missed stenoses reflected an adjustable threshold rather than an absent signal\.
