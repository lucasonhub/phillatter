# Evaluation of Two Combined Analytical Approaches to Assess the Impact of Rare Variants on Severe COVID\-19

**Autores:** Francesca Rosamilia; Giada Recchi; Davide Cangelosi; Sergio Decherchi; Francesca Lantieri; Stefania Croci; Carlo Salvarani; Andrea Cavalli; Marta Rusmini; Isabella Ceccherini; Paolo Uva

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Severe COVID\-19 displays substantial inter\-individual variability that cannot be fully explained by common genetic variants, suggesting a contribution of rare functional variation\. We performed whole\-genome sequencing in 191 Italian patients \(96 moderate, 95 severe\) to investigate rare coding variants \(minor allele frequency &lt;1% and &lt;5%\) via complementary network\-based and machine learning approaches\. Classical single\-variant and burden tests did not identify significant associations after multiple testing correction\. However, Network\-based Heterogeneity Clustering \(NHC\) revealed that multiple gene clusters were enriched in severe cases, particularly those involving RNA polymerase II–mediated transcription, RNA metabolism, and vesicle\-mediated transport pathways\. Gene\-level rare variant analysis via the variable threshold method identified five genes significantly associated with severe disease \(FDR &lt; 0\.05\), namely, EIF4G3, GTF2F2, LY96, COX6A1, and STK11, which are involved in transcriptional and translational regulation, innate immune signaling, and mitochondrial function\. Unsupervised biclustering significantly stratified patients by severity, and a decision tree model highlighted the burden of DOCK10 variants as a key discriminator between phenotypes\. Convergence between the network\-based and machine learning results further supported the robustness of the identified signals\. Overall, our integrative framework underscores the contribution of rare variants to COVID\-19 severity and highlights the coordinated perturbation of interconnected molecular pathways as a determinant of clinical outcome\.
