# A Fuzzy Multi\-Criteria Decision\-Making Approach for Managerial Appointments in Public Health Institutions

**Autores:** Memiş KARACA

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: Healthcare services are fundamental to public welfare and social sustainability\. The quality, accessibility, continuity, and efficiency of healthcare delivery largely depend on effective institutional governance and competent leadership\. This study aims to develop an objective and systematic evaluation model for managerial appointments in public health institutions in order to strengthen healthcare service performance and ensure merit\-based leadership in service delivery processes\. Methods: A multi\-criteria decision\-making \(MCDM\) framework was developed based on criteria identified from the healthcare management literature, expert evaluations, and contractual managerial appointment regulations in public health institutions\. To address uncertainty and ensure consistency in expert judgments, the Fuzzy Full Consistency Method was applied to determine the relative importance of the defined criteria\. The model integrates sector\-specific healthcare service requirements into a structured decision\-support system\. Results: The analysis revealed that equipment\-related criteria, which directly influence the quality, efficiency, and sustainability of healthcare service delivery, had the highest importance weight \(0\.460\)\. Personal criteria were assigned the lowest weight \(0\.360\)\. The findings indicate that institutional capacity and service\-related competencies are prioritized over individual characteristics in strengthening healthcare service governance\. Conclusion: By focusing on healthcare service quality and institutional performance, this study presents the first fuzzy MCDM\-based model specifically designed to support managerial appointments in public health institutions\. The proposed framework contributes to improving healthcare service effectiveness, promoting merit\-based governance, and enhancing accountability and transparency in public healthcare management systems\.
