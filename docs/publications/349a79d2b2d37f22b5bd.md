# Digital Risks and AI\-Enabled Responses in Adolescent Mental Health: A Bibliometric and Science\-Mapping Analysis

**Autores:** Ecem Çi̇çek Gümüş

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: This study mapped digital risks and AI\-enabled responses in adolescent mental health, quantified publication balance, and tested explicit domain integration within single records\. Method: Scopus metadata \(frozen August 9, 2026; exported August 15, 2026\) covering articles, reviews, and conference papers were analyzed via bibliometric mapping\. Temporal trends evaluated 2015–2025 \(complete years\), reporting 2026 provisionally\. Records were classified as Risk\-only, Response\-only, or Both\. Robustness checks included audits, manual validation of Both records, sensitivity analyses, VOSviewer mapping, thematic evolution, burst detection, and network validation\. Results: Of 4,804 retrieved records, 1,623 formed the primary corpus \(1,335 in 2015–2025: 833 Risk\-only, 495 Response\-only, 7 Both\)\. The response\-to\-risk ratio rose from 0\.04 \(2015\) to 1\.19 \(2025\), when response output first surpassed risk\. Manual review confirmed only 5 direct bridges in 2015–2025 \(0\.37%\) and 9 overall \(0\.55%\); integration remained &lt;1% across sensitivity analyses\. Keyword mapping revealed distinct cyberbullying/victimization, AI/digital\-mental\-health, and machine\-learning/suicide\-prediction clusters, with recent growth in AI, digital phenotyping, and LLMs\. Conclusion: Although AI response research has expanded rapidly, explicit co\-framing of digital risk and AI response within single publications remains rare and independent of 2026 data\.
