# Cooperative metaheuristic feature selection and out\-of\-fold weighted ensemble learning for chronic kidney disease prediction from a reduced attribute panel

**Autores:** Amaresh Ayyanahalli Math; Ananda Murthy Meenakshi Sundaram

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Chronic kidney disease is diagnosed from a broad laboratory panel whose size and composition are open to optimization\. This study pairs a cooperative hybrid metaheuristic for attribute selection with a weighted voting ensemble\. Genetic, swarm, and annealing sub\-populations evolved in parallel over one shared fitness landscape, exchanged elites every five iterations, and were refined by annealing applied as a local search on the incumbent; the attributes retained across the elite band formed the subset, which fed four heterogeneous learners whose probabilities were fused using weights tuned from out\-of\-fold predictions only\. Imputation, search, learners, and weights were confined to the training partition of a 10 times five\-fold cross\-validation, and every single\-operator variant, four classical selectors, and the unreduced panel were evaluated under that identical protocol\. The ensemble reached 99\.00% accuracy, an area under the curve of 0\.9994, and an F\-measure of 99\.19% from 11 of the 24 attributes, dropping 53% of the assays at a cost of two\-thirds of a percentage point that is not statistically significant once the dependence between cross\-validation folds is modeled\. Accuracy held at 98\.00% when a further fifth of all values was deleted, and an independent cohort of 200 records recorded as discretized intervals returned 98\.00%\. The pipeline is statistically equivalent to the strongest single learner and to the strongest classical selector while using fewer than half the attributes\.
