# Primary enteric\-type adenocarcinoma of the renal parenchyma in a patient with bilateral renal malrotation: a case report and review of the literature

**Autores:** Yuanqi Wu; Haoke Zheng; Gaurab Pokhrel; Rongyu Zhao; Shouchang Wu; Shuanbao Yu; Xuepei Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Primary enteric\-type adenocarcinoma of the renal parenchyma is an exceptionally rare malignancy, accounting for less than 1% of renal epithelial tumors\. Due to its morphological and immunohistochemical overlap with metastatic gastrointestinal malignancy, establishing a primary renal origin presents a considerable diagnostic challenge\. Radical nephroureterectomy has traditionally been the standard surgical treatment\. Case presentation We report a 47\-year\-old asymptomatic female with an incidental 68 mm cystic\-solid left renal mass detected during a trauma evaluation\. Imaging suggested Bosniak category IV on computed tomography and cystic teratoma on magnetic resonance imaging\. Notably, the patient had bilateral renal malrotation\. Serum tumor markers \(CEA and CA19\-9\) were within normal limits\. The patient underwent successful robotic\-assisted partial nephrectomy with a warm ischemia time of 15 minutes\. Histopathology revealed adenocarcinoma with an enteric immunophenotype: positive for CK20, CDX\-2, SATB2, and Villin, with complete loss of PAX\-8 and GATA\-3\. Postoperative gastrointestinal endoscopy and 18 F\-FDG PET\-CT excluded primary gastrointestinal or distant occult malignancies, confirming a primary renal parenchymal origin\. At one\-month follow\-up, the patient showed no evidence of recurrence without adjuvant therapy\. Conclusion This case represents the first reported association of primary enteric\-type renal adenocarcinoma with bilateral renal malrotation and demonstrates that robotic\-assisted partial nephrectomy can be a feasible nephron\-sparing approach for selected patients with this aggressive tumor\. A systematic diagnostic approach including surgical resection, immunohistochemical profiling, and exclusion of gastrointestinal metastasis is essential for accurate diagnosis and optimal management\.
