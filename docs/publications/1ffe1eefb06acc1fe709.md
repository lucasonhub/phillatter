## Descrição do feed

\_Agency in the Age of Algorithms\_\. 2026This essay argues that the hard problem of consciousness retains structural significance in the age of advanced AI, even if AI never achieves consciousness\. Consciousness has historically functioned not as a metaphysical substance requiring proof, but as an unavailable\-to\-inspection anchor that stabilizes attribution of responsibility and authorship\. Its opacity, not its presence, was its strength\. As algorithmic systems increasingly mediate decisions without possessing this presumed interiority, the social practices that depend on consciousness as a boundary marker — blame, praise, accountability — lose their structural support\. The paper reframes the hard problem from an epistemic obstacle to a normative boundary, showing that the most destabilizing scenario is not machines becoming conscious, but remaining non\-conscious while displacing human decision\-making authority\. \#\#\#\# This essay is the first in a two\-part series examining the structural conditions under which classical candidates for the ground of agency have been weakened in algorithmically mediated environments\. AAA\-01 pairs with AAA\-02 \(Behavior Is Not Agency\) as independent arguments converging on a shared conclusion developed in AAI\-01 \(Delegation Is Not Distribution\)\. The concept introduced here — consciousness as structural anchor rather than metaphysical fact — is later formalized in AAI\-03 \(The Causal Lateness of Consciousness\)\. Together, the AAA and AAI series constitute an eliminative derivation showing that interruptibility, not consciousness, behavior, or intention, is the minimal structural condition for moral concepts to retain causal force\.
