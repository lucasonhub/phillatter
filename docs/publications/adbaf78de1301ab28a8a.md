# Bulgaria, Jamel: Structural\-Constructivist Architecture of the Core Emotion Framework: A Comprehensive Scientific, Applied, and Computational Evaluation

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

The scientific investigation into the architecture of human affect has historically been defined by a fundamental theoretical schism, often characterized as a century\-long conflict between discrete emotion models and dimensional frameworks\. Discrete models, such as those proposed by Paul Ekman and Carroll Izard, argue for biologically hardwired, universal emotional categories presumed to have evolved as distinct adaptive responses to environmental challenges\. These models posit that specific neurological circuits are dedicated to primary emotions, which manifest through universal facial expressions and physiological signatures\. Conversely, dimensional models, often associated with James Russell and Lisa Feldman Barrett, prioritize a continuous spectrum of valence and arousal\. These frameworks suggest that what individuals categorize as discrete emotions are essentially cognitive constructions layered atop simpler physiological states, or &quot;core affect,&quot; which varies along dimensions of pleasantness and activation\. The Core Emotion Framework \(CEF\), conceptualized and formalized by independent researcher Jamel Bulgaria, emerges as a sophisticated structural\-constructivist resolution to this historical dichotomy\. The framework integrates interoceptive physiology, computational operations, and mechanisms of therapeutic change into a single, testable model\. Rather than treating emotions as static states, descriptive labels, or passive qualitative experiences, the CEF defines them as active, functional operators—internal mechanisms that modulate cognitive, somatic, and conative systems\. This shift in ontology represents a radical departure from viewing emotions as static states to viewing them as active functional processes that generate, regulate, and express human emotional capability\. \(direct link \)
