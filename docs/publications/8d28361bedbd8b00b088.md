# Controlled Evaluation for Numeric\-Field Forgery Detection in SROIE Receipts using Fine\-Tuned EfficientNetV2\-S

**Autores:** Tousi Adak; Priyanshu Mukherjee; Sudipta Das; Jahir Paik

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Altered amounts, dates, and identifiers on retail receipts represent a practical source of expense and tax fraud\. However, public receipt datasets such as Scanned Receipts OCR and Information Extraction \(SROIE\) contain authentic receipts but do not provide forged counterparts, limiting controlled evaluation of receipt\-forgery detection methods\. Chakraborty \(2025\) reported 97\.1% accuracy using a convolutional neural network \(CNN\)\-based receipt\-forgery detection framework\. Building on this research context, the present study investigates forgery detection at the numeric\-field level, focusing on receipt components that directly carry financial information\. Using SROIE, a receipt\-grouped benchmark of paired authentic and synthetically forged numeric\-field crops is constructed\. Two controlled forgery mechanisms are considered: splicing a donor numeric field from the same receipt and erasing and re\-rendering a field with a specified proportion of its digits changed\. Authentic and forged crops undergo the same Joint Photographic Experts Group \(JPEG\) compression procedure, and receipt\-level grouping is used for training, validation, and test partitioning at approximately 70/15/15\. An ImageNet\-pretrained EfficientNetV2\-S is fine\-tuned end\-to\-end using linear warm\-up, cosine learning\-rate decay, and exponential moving average \(EMA\) weights, with model selection based exclusively on validation accuracy\. On the held\-out test set of 4,602 crops, comprising 2,301 authentic and 2,301 forged samples, the reference detector achieves 98\.09% accuracy, 99\.42% precision, 96\.74% recall, an F1\-score of 0\.9806, and an area under the receiver operating characteristic \(ROC\) curve \(AUC\) of 0\.9971 at a fixed decision threshold of 0\.5 in a single run with seed 42\. Most remaining errors correspond to missed forgeries\. The reported accuracy is numerically higher than the 97\.1% reported by Chakraborty \(2025\), but direct comparison is limited by differences in datasets, sample construction, and evaluation protocols\. Gradient\-weighted Class Activation Mapping \(Grad\-CAM\) analysis indicates attention to numeric text regions but also, in some cases, to crop boundaries and background regions, suggesting that synthetic generation artifacts may contribute to the observed performance\. As the study relies on synthetic forgeries without multiple baseline architectures, ablation experiments, multiple random seeds, or real\-world forged\-receipt evaluation, the findings are limited to the defined synthetic tampering processes rather than general real\-world forgery\-detection performance\.
