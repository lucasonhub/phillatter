# Divergent CMV and EBV co\-infection impact in adult and pediatric hematopoietic stem cell transplantation recipients

**Autores:** Osama A Alsmadi; Hasan Hashem; Khalid Halahleh; Jude Jarrah; Yazan Talab; Iyad Yasin Sultan; Maysa Al Hussaini

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Cytomegalovirus \(CMV\) and Epstein\-Barr virus \(EBV\) reactivations are major complications after hematopoietic stem cell transplantation \(HCT\), but co\-infection epidemiology and outcomes in combined adult\-pediatric cohorts remain poorly defined\. Materials and methods This retrospective single\-center study analyzed 941 consecutive HCT recipients \(2015–2023\)\. CMV and EBV were quantified by whole\-blood qPCR \(positivity ≥153 and ≥433 copies/mL, respectively\)\. CMV testing was weekly until day \+100 post\-HSCT; EBV testing followed clinical indication at the treating physician’s discretion\. IgG serostatus is not routinely tested at KHCC for post\-transplant monitoring\. Acknowledging some missing values, we evaluated the incidence, risk factors, and survival impact of CMV/EBV co\-infection using chi\-square tests, Kaplan\-Meier analysis, and multivariable logistic and Cox proportional\-hazards regression\. Results Among 941 recipients \(56\.5% adult, 43\.5% pediatric\), adults received 89\.1% of CMV tests \(838/941\) and 76\.6% of CMV infections \(105/137\), whereas pediatric patients accounted for 59\.8% of EBV infections \(76/127\); EBV testing covered 65\.5% \(616/941\)\. Co\-infection incidence was 7\.6% \(70/916\)\. Co\-infection was significantly associated with allogeneic transplant \(p=0\.004\), haploidentical donor \(p=0\.010\), reduced\-intensity conditioning \(p=0\.017\), and acute graft\-versus\-host disease \(aGVHD\) \(p=0\.028\), but not with malignancy status \(54/70 \[77\.1%\] vs\. 577/760 \[75\.9%\] malignant; p=0\.819\); haploidentical \(vs\. related\) donor type remained independent on multivariable analysis \(adjusted OR 2\.01, 95% CI 1\.18–3\.41, p=0\.0098\)\. CMV monoinfection was associated with adult age and haploidentical transplantation; EBV monoinfection with pediatric age, non\-malignant disease, and aGVHD\. Pediatric 3\-year overall survival \(OS\) was superior \(78\.4% vs\. 64\.6%; median follow\-up 41\.8 months\)\. The difference in 3\-year OS between co\-infected \(67\.3%\) and non\-co\-infected \(70\.9%\) patients did not reach statistical significance \(Kaplan–Meier log\-rank p = 0\.4046; crude mortality by Pearson chi\-square, p = 0\.286\); chronic GVHD was the only independent predictor of OS \(adjusted HR 0\.016, 95% CI 0\.002–0\.150, p=0\.0003\)\. Conclusion CMV and EBV post\-HCT exhibit distinct, age\-associated risk profiles, and co\-infection defines a high\-risk phenotype linked to intensive immunosuppression and aGVHD\. Three\-year OS did not differ significantly between groups; given the observational design, selection bias from non\-uniform EBV testing, and possible type II error, this should not be taken as evidence that pre\-emptive strategies eliminate the survival impact of co\-infection\. Findings support age\- and risk\-adapted viral monitoring\.
