# Letchford, Nicholas James: Love and Philesōtia: A Homeorhetic Account of Love, Ethics, and Orientational Practice

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This essay develops a companion account of Homeorhetic Love and Philesōtia within Signal\-Bifurcation Theory \(SBT\)\. It argues that Love is not an emotion, nor a moral command, nor a romantic ideal\. It is a structural condition of conscious participation grounded in the homeorhetic equilibrium of living organisation\. In the biological setting, the condition later cast in conscious life as Love is a homeorhetic mode in which bounded variation remains within stability\-supporting limits between stasis and overshoot\. Within Semepoietic consciousness, at the basal level of valence\-weighted sensorimotor coupling, this same condition becomes available as immediate, non\-instrumental participation, where later narrational outcome\-demand recedes\. Homeorhetic Love is not identical with every instance of successful biological regulation\. Living organisation may regulate through defence, withdrawal, urgency, or repair without Love being the appropriate description\. Love names the more specific condition in which homeorhetic participation becomes consciously available without symbolic projection, possession, or outcome\-demand becoming primary\. Valence and regulation remain active; what loosens is their conversion by Signal\-Narration into a demand that reality, another person, or the future resolve itself according to a narrationally imposed condition\. The essay further argues that many influential philosophical and psychological accounts of love have not fully described this underlying condition\. Instead, they circle love as it appears once rendered and miscategorised by Signal\-Narration: as conditions already converted from structural relation into stories of lack, projection, possession, idealisation, or demand\. Such misattributions belong to the paradox of human estrangement and reflection permitted by symbolic bifurcation\. They have yielded the drama of Shakespeare and the emotional resonance of Yeats, yet in SBT terms they remain definitions and extrapolations that circle, and often remain structurally incomplete in relation to, the condition proposed here as Homeorhetic Love, whose power to settle emotive tension and dissonance is a precondition of unconditionality\. The philosophical tradition is examined in two movements\. The first considers Plato, Aristotle, Schopenhauer, Nietzsche, and Freud, whose accounts variously treat love in terms of lack, drive, virtue, affirmation, compensatory projection, or symbolic appropriation\. The second comprises accounts that resist sentimentalism and drive\-reduction and attempt to locate something non\-instrumental at the core of love: Spinoza, Fromm, Frankfurt, and Murdoch, each of which approaches aspects of the structural condition without fully formulating it in these terms\. Eastern traditions, in particular Taoist accounts of wu wei and Buddhist accounts of mettā within the brahmavihārās, offer further structural parallels examined on their own terms\. The latter part of the essay introduces Philesōtia not as part of the ontology, but as a logical orientational proposal articulated from the structure of the framework itself: a mode of practice through which a narrationally engaged organism may seek patterns of awareness that draw symbolic overshoot back toward immediacy\. From this basis, the essay re\-situates morality and ethics within Semepoietic continuity\. Semepoiesis carries no intrinsic moral topology\. Human living and social systems nevertheless remain non\-indifferent to what supports or threatens their coherence\. Ethical systems, mythic overlays, religious dogma, and related forms are therefore interpreted not as discoveries of cosmic moral law, but as narrational codifications of continuity\-supporting and continuity\-threatening patterns within an impartial unfolding\. This does not derive moral obligation directly from ontology\. Philesōtia remains an orientational proposal rather than a cosmic imperative\.
