# Fujie, Naoto: Post\-Active\-Inference Experimentally Delimiting the Operational Conditions of Active\-Inference Representations Across Worlds, Tasks, Memory Regimes, Hysteresis, and Structural Capacity

**Autores:**

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026Active Inference provides a powerful formal framework for inference, action, and adaptation under a generative model\. This paper asks a different question: under what operational conditions do the primitives through which an Active\-Inference system is described—agent, boundary, observation, variable, model, prediction error, and policy—remain stable, become non\-redundant, enter transition, or cease to provide an adequate description? We develop Post\-Active\-Inference not as a successor controller, but as an experimental program for making those conditions themselves measurable\. In Phase 1, a preregistered confirmatory simulation study comprising 55,680 runs across 30 fresh seeds found that Active Inference remained a strong controller throughout much of the tested space, while reproducible transitions in representational validity were concentrated primarily in observation and action\-interface perturbations rather than world\-side topology, coupling, memory, or structural perturbations\. Operational tracking did not generally outperform Active Inference, and several development\-stage effects failed to replicate, including a proposed dissociation between representational failure and preserved control advantage\. Phase 2 extends the analysis across multiple world families, graph realizations, tasks, a finite Bayesian structure\-learning Active\-Inference comparator, memory\-retention regimes, continuous perturbation paths, and structural\-capacity measures\. Structure learning did not uniformly dominate adaptive Active Inference and could retain high model confidence under complete observational failure\. History re\-entry was negligible under rapidly decaying memory but became non\-redundant under long\-memory conditions in development experiments\. A preregistered hysteresis effect reproduced directionally but failed its predefined practical\-effect threshold\. By contrast, structural\-capacity experiments confirmed that binary reachability can remain almost unchanged while effective accessibility is substantially reorganized: target\-region inbound edge capacity and inbound weight fell by approximately one half while mean path distance increased\. The results do not falsify Active Inference and do not establish a universally superior alternative\. They instead support a methodological shift: the operational conditions under which Active\-Inference primitives become valid, unstable, transitional, or dispensable can themselves be experimentally investigated\. More generally, nominal possibility and effective operational accessibility must be distinguished: a state can remain formally reachable while the capacity, support, and pathways through which it can be reached have already been substantially reorganized\. \(direct link \)
