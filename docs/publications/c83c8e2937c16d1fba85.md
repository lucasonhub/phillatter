# Precision biologics in pediatric inflammatory bowel disease: Current evidence and emerging predictors

**Autores:** Mohammed Al‐Beltagi; Nermin Kamal Saeed; Adel Salah Bediwy; Samir Hasan; Yasser Salah El\-Sawaf; Reem M Elbeltagi

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Pediatric inflammatory bowel disease \(IBD\) presents with significant heterogeneity in disease phenotypes, underlying biology, and therapeutic outcomes, often presenting with more aggressive disease and unique developmental considerations compared to adult\-onset IBD\.Despite the impact of biologic therapies, substantial variability in treatment response, including primary non\-response and secondary loss of response, remains a major clinical challenge in this vulnerable population, underscoring the critical need for precision medicine approaches to guide individualized biologic selection in children\.This narrative review critically evaluates the evidence supporting predictive biomarkers for biologic response in pediatric IBD, distinguishes prognostic from treatment\-predictive markers, compares candidate biomarkers across biologic classes, and identifies priorities required for clinical translation\.This narrative review critically evaluates the evidence supporting predictive biomarkers for biologic response in pediatric IBD, distinguishes prognostic from treatment\-predictive markers, compares candidate biomarkers across biologic classes, and identifies priorities required for clinical translation\.A narrative evidence synthesis was undertaken using PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, and Web of Science through June 2026, prioritizing pediatric studies and selectively incorporating adult evidence when pediatric data were unavailable or insufficient\.The therapeutic landscape for pediatric IBD has expanded to include anti\-tumor necrosis factor agents, vedolizumab, ustekinumab, and selective interleukin\-23 inhibitors like risankizumab\.Meanwhile, advances in biomarker discovery, therapeutic drug monitoring \(TDM\), genomics, transcriptomics, microbiome science, multi\-omics, and artificial intelligence \(AI\) have driven efforts to evaluate treatment selection\.While various candidate predictors \-including clinical characteristics, inflammatory biomarkers, pharmacokinetic parameters, genetic variants, transcriptomic signatures, immune profiles, and microbiome\-derived markers \-have been identified, the majority of molecular, microbiome, and AI evidence remains exploratory and is largely derived 5 / 153 from adult studies, with no single biomarker currently offering sufficient standalone predictive accuracy for routine clinical use\.TDM remains the most clinically validated approach for treatment optimization, particularly in the pediatric population where pharmacokinetic variability is high\.Evidence also suggests that response predictors may differ across biologic classes, necessitating biologic\-specific approaches\.Future precision medicine strategies will increasingly investigate integrated multi\-omic platforms and AI\-driven predictive models rather than individual biomarkers, moving toward disease endotypes as hypothesis\-generating frameworks\.Emerging technologies such as single\-cell and spatial transcriptomics, radiomics, digital pathology, and digital twins are being explored to better understand biologic selection and dynamically adapt therapy\.Precision biologic selection in pediatric IBD is an evolving and critical goal\.This review concludes that while current biomarker approaches have limitations, integrating diverse biomarkers, microbiome data, TDM, and AI offers a framework to optimize treatment selection\.This approach aims to ultimately enable &quot;the right biologic for the right child at the right time&quot; and improve long\-term outcomes for this vulnerable population by systematically addressing the translational gap from research to clinical practice\.
