# Evaluation of Depressive Symptoms in Children Aged 3\-6 Years: A Parent\-Report Study

**Autores:** Mine Cengiz; Yusuf Aktepe; Burcu Demir Gökmen

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND/AIMS:This study aimed to evaluate parent\-reported depressive symptoms in children aged 3\-6 years and to examine the relationship between these symptoms and the socio\-demographic characteristics of the children and their parents\. MATERIALS AND METHODS:A descriptive cross\-sectional study was conducted from January to March 2025 and included 236 parents who were recruited by simple random sampling\.Data were collected using a socio\-demographic information form and the child depressive symptom scale\.The results were summarized as counts, percentages, means, and standard deviations, and comparisons were made using Independent Samples t\-tests, one\-way analysis of variance, Kruskal\-Wallis, and Mann\-Whitney U tests\. RESULTS:Depressive symptom levels varied significantly among children, differing by age, sex, and child health\-related factors, such as allergic or chronic diseases\.In addition, family\-related factors, particularly parental chronic illness, were associated with higher levels of depressive symptoms \(p&lt;0\.05\)\. CONCLUSION:Children&\#x27;s depressive symptom levels differ according to certain child and family characteristics\.The findings suggest that, when assessing the psychosocial well\-being of young children, family\-related characteristics, in addition to child\-related factors, may also be taken into consideration\.Further studies with larger sample sizes, longitudinal designs, and intervention\-based approaches are needed to evaluate the potential benefits of psychoeducation and support programs for parents\.
