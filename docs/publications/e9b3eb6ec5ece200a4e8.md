# Moore, Terrence: Perception as Active Self‑Construction: A Foundational Capacity for Flourishing

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Perception is often treated as the passive reception of sensory data, a process that merely informs an already‑constituted self\. This paper argues for a different view: perception is an active, constructive capacity that simultaneously builds a coherent self and supplies the causal information needed for intentional agency\. Drawing on phenomenological \(Husserl, Merleau‑Ponty\), enactive \(Varela, Thompson\), and predictive processing \(Clark, Friston\) traditions, the paper defends a constructive‑enactive account of perception\. It shows that perception is not only epistemic \(providing mental maps of the world\) but also ontological – it participates in the ongoing self‑construction of the agent\. Because perception makes causal pathways legible, it is the epistemic gateway to interdependence\. Consequently, institutional visibility – the condition that makes causal consequences traceable – is a moral necessity\. A society that blocks perception of causal relations \(an obscuring‑form society\) directly undermines the cognitive capacity that underlies flourishing\.
