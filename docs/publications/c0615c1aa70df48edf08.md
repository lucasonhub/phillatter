# Effect of the CP@clinic program \(Community Paramedicine at Clinic program\) on health care utilization from administrative data during and after the intervention: a cluster\-randomized controlled trial

**Autores:** Gina Agarwal; Melissa Pirrie; Manasvi Sai Vanama; Zheng Jing Hu; John Michael Paterson; Lehana Thabane; Ricardo N\. Angeles; Francine Marzanek

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction The study objective was to compare the effects of the Community Paramedicine at Clinic \(CP@clinic\) program versus usual care on building\-level health service utilization during a 12\-month trial period and 12 months post\-trial\. Methods This study employed a longitudinal, repeated cross\-sectional design within a cluster\-randomized controlled trial \(RCT\)\. It involved 30 apartment buildings across five Ontario communities, housing low\-income older adults\. Buildings were paired and randomized to receive either the CP@clinic intervention or continuing with usual care\. Data was derived from ICES\-maintained administrative health databases, linked using unique identifiers\. The sample included all residents aged 55 \+ in the study postal codes from the Ontario Health Insurance Plan \(OHIP\) Registered Persons Database \(RPDB\), excluding those aged 120\+, ineligible for OHIP, and those with no health care encounters within 7 years\. The primary health care utilization outcome was emergency department visits by ambulance and other outcomes included hospital admissions and primary care visits\. Results CP@clinic buildings showed significantly lower hospital admission rates during both the 12\-month RCT period \(adjusted incidence rate ratio \[aIRR\]: 0\.75 \[0\.61,0\.91\]\) and the 12\-month post\-RCT period \[aIRR: 0\.82 \(0\.70,0\.96\)\] compared to usual care buildings\. Primary care visits were lower in CP@clinic buildings during the RCT \(aIRR: 0\.93 \[0\.88,0\.99\]\) but higher in the post\-RCT period \(aIRR: 1\.05 \[1\.00,1\.10\]\)\. No significant differences were found in emergency department visits or the number of days admitted between groups in either period\. Discussion CP@clinic was linked to a sustained reduction in hospital admissions and an increase in primary care visits beyond the RCT time period, reflecting a shift from reactive to preventive care among older adults residing in social housing\. The lasting impact beyond the RCT period suggests that the CP@clinic Program can improve health system efficiency, primary care access, and reduce health inequities in vulnerable populations\. Trial registration ClinicalTrials\.gov, Identifier: NCT02152891
