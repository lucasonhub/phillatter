# Structural diagnosis and evolution of urban resilience in dryland oasis urban agglomerations from an eco\-immunological perspective

**Autores:** Yingyue Hou; Qiang Hu; Ümüt Halik

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Dryland oasis urban agglomerations face increasing drought risk, but gains in long\-term adaptive capacity may not translate into stronger structural resilience because additive indices allow strong dimensions to offset weak ones\. This study developed a partially non\-compensatory four\-dimensional super\-trapezoid resilience model \(TE4\) integrating resistance, recovery, adaptive capacity, and institutional learning and preparedness, and applied it to ten city\- and county\-level units on the Northern Slope of the Tianshan Mountains during 2014–2024\. The traditional composite index increased by 5\.09% and adaptive capacity by 29\.38% between the 2014 and 2024 endpoints, whereas the TE4 endpoint value in 2024 was 1\.32% lower than that in 2014; however, its downward temporal trend was not statistically significant\. Potential resilience rose by 9\.28%, recovery capacity fell by 8\.03%, and the unrealized resilience gap widened by 15\.67%, indicating a modeled recovery\-related conversion deficit\. Removing NDVI\-derived indicators barely altered TE4 rankings \(Spearman’s ρ = 0\.983\)\. Panel models did not show short\-term NPP buffering by higher TE4 under stronger drought, while pre\-event recovery capacity showed the strongest positive but statistically inconclusive association with post\-drought NPP recovery\. TE4 is therefore a structural diagnostic tool for identifying capacity deficits and prioritizing recovery\-oriented disaster risk reduction rather than a direct predictor of annual ecosystem productivity\.
