# Machine learning–based validation of Human Phenotype Ontology \(HPO\) terms for systemic autoinflammatory diseases using the Eurofever Registry: the ODINO project

**Autores:** Caterina Matucci‐Cerinic; Giacomo Cavalca; Matteo Vergani; Roberta Caorsi; Marielle van Gjin; Guilaine Boursier; Eugenia Mosci; Giovanni Fiorito; Paolo Uva; Marco Gattorno

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background The Human Phenotype Ontology \(HPO\) terms for systemic autoinflammatory diseases \(SAIDs\) were revised in 2022, but their diagnostic accuracy has not yet been validated in real\-life cohorts\. Objectives To evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of HPO terms in a real\-life cohort of patients enrolled in a large international registry for SAIDs through a machine learning \(ML\) approach\. Methods Clinical and genetic data were extracted from the Eurofever Registry \(EF\) for 2,576 patients\. Due to limited sample sizes for rarer conditions, analyses focused on FMF, MKD, TRAPS, CAPS and PFAPA\. The 223 clinical EF variables were codified into HPO terms, including terms with missing HPO correspondence\. Supervised ML models \(Elastic Net, k\-Nearest Neighbors, Random Forest, and XGBoost\) were trained to classify the five diseases using both multiclass and one\-vs\-rest \(OVR\) strategies\. Results A full HPO correspondence was found for 185 EF terms\. Clinically relevant variables frequently missing from HPO, including “ancestry” and “fever length”, were incorporated into the final models\. On the held\-out test set, the XGBoost OVR framework achieved the following recall values: 87\.2% for FMF, 90\.4% for PFAPA, 81\.1% for CAPS, 55\.7% for TRAPS, and 55\.6% for MKD\. Feature importance analyses highlighted “fever length” as key discriminative variable\. Error analysis revealed an enrichment of patients carrying the R92Q TNFRSF1A variant among false\-negative predictions for TRAPS\. Unsupervised analysis showed no clear separation between FMF patients carrying one versus two pathogenic MEFV mutations\. Conclusions HPO\-based phenotypic modeling supports accurate classification of the 5 most frequent recurrent fevers included in this study\. The HPO database should be enriched by including the missing terms, especially “fever length”, “ancestry”, and the symptoms frequencies according to Eurofever\. An ontology\-driven diagnostic tool applicable to both clinical research and computational settings for recurrent fevers is now available\.
