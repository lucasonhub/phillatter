# Painful arthroplasty with disproportionate pain: Recognition and management of complex regional pain syndrome

**Autores:** Stephanie O’Palko; Alexander G\. Burbelo; Andrew James Murphy; Matthew Bullock

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Persistent pain following total hip arthroplasty and total knee arthroplasty is most commonly attributed to periprosthetic joint infection, aseptic loosening, instability, or other mechanical complications\.However, complex regional pain syndrome \(CRPS\) represents an uncommon but important cause of disproportionate postoperative pain that is frequently underrecognized, highlighting the importance of timely and accurate diagnosis and management\.This narrative review summarizes the current evidence regarding the epidemiology, pathophysiology, diagnosis, and management of CRPS following total joint arthroplasty, with particular emphasis on challenges unique to the arthroplasty population\.The Budapest criteria remain the primary clinical framework for diagnosis after exclusion of infection and mechanical failure\.Current management emphasizes early recognition, multidisciplinary rehabilitation, physical therapy, and multimodal pharmacologic treatment\.Although sympathetic nerve blocks are commonly incorporated into treatment algorithms and may provide temporary diagnostic or therapeutic benefit in selected patients, contemporary systematic reviews demonstrate limited high\-quality evidence supporting sustained clinical efficacy\.Available arthroplasty\-specific literature consists primarily of case reports and small observational studies\.Orthopaedic surgeons should maintain a high index of suspicion for CRPS in patients with persistent pain that is disproportionate to expected recovery after total hip arthroplasty or total knee arthroplasty, particularly when standard diagnostic evaluation is unrevealing\.Early recognition and appropriate evaluation may facilitate timely management of CRPS following arthroplasty\.Further prospective studies are needed to define optimal diagnostic pathways and clarify the role of sympathetic nerve blocks in arthroplasty\-associated CRPS\.
