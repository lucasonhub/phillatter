# Severity\-dependent and sex\-biased dysregulation of A\-to\-I RNA editing in COVID\-19 reveals CTSS as a key regulatory target

**Autores:** Jingyuan Wang; Zhao Ya\-di; Li\-Ping Wang; Yu\-Qing Ren; Jia\-Nuo Li; Teng Wang; Hua\-Kang Zhang; Feng Zhu; Jian‐Huan Chen; Yunyun Jin

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

SARS\-CoV\-2 infection causes diverse clinical outcomes, with men facing higher risks of severe disease\. Adenosine\-to\-inosine \(A\-to\-I\) RNA editing mediated by ADAR enzymes governs innate antiviral immunity, yet its contribution to COVID\-19 severity and sex\-biased disease remains unclear\. We performed epitranscriptomic profiling on 126 blood samples from non\-COVID controls and COVID\-19 patients spanning different disease severities\. We detected 1,466 differentially RNA\-edited sites in 514 genes linked to clinical outcomes\. Although ADAR expression increased in COVID\-19 patients overall, it was strongly reduced in ICU patients compared with non\-ICU patients, and this shift was far more prominent in males\. This severity\-associated and sex\-biased ADAR/ADARB1 downregulation coincided with decreased global RNA editing and perturbed expression of immune\-related edited transcripts\. Cathepsin S \(CTSS\) was identified as a central regulatory node: elevated A\-to\-I editing within its 3&\#x27; UTR in ICU patients stabilizes CTSS mRNA\. Without stratifying by disease severity, ADAR expression and global editing showed no significant sex differences; the large sex divergence only appeared among ICU patients\. This indicates severity\-dependent, not infection\-innate, disruption of A\-to\-I editing, disproportionately amplified in critically ill males\. CTSS 3&\#x27;UTR editing may serve as a biomarker for patient stratification, revealing a mechanistic connection between epitranscriptomic changes and male\-biased severe COVID\-19\.
