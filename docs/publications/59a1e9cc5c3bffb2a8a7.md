# Clinically relevant chromosomal abnormalities and chromosomal polymorphisms in 7,530 patients undergoing reproductive genetic counseling: a retrospective cytogenetic study in a Chinese Han population

**Autores:** 吴松航 Wu Songhang; Hongyan Shang; Wenhua Wang; Dan Chen; Wennan Wu

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Chromosomal aberrations are important causes of reproductive disorders, but their clinical interpretation is often complicated by the presence of benign chromosomal polymorphisms that may obscure the true diagnostic yield of karyotyping\. The aim of this study was to evaluate peripheral blood karyotyping in patients undergoing reproductive genetic counseling and to distinguish clinically relevant chromosomal abnormalities from chromosomal polymorphisms\. Methods A total of 7,530 patients who visited the reproductive center and the obstetrics and gynecology genetic clinic of the First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University from January 2016 to December 2022 were included\. Peripheral blood karyotyping was performed using lymphocyte culture, routine chromosome preparation, and G\-banding\. Cytogenetic findings were classified into clinically relevant chromosomal abnormalities and chromosomal polymorphisms\. Chromosomal polymorphisms were analyzed separately and were not counted as the primary diagnostic outcome\. Correspondence analysis was performed using an observed\-count matrix linking major clinical manifestations with representative karyotypic findings\. Results A total of 552 abnormal cytogenetic findings were detected, corresponding to an overall cytogenetic finding rate of 7\.33% \(552/7,530\)\. Among them, 246 cases were classified as clinically relevant chromosomal abnormalities, giving a primary diagnostic rate of 3\.27% \(246/7,530\), whereas 306 cases were chromosomal polymorphisms, accounting for 4\.06% of the total cohort and 55\.43% of all abnormal cytogenetic findings\. In correspondence analysis, the first two dimensions explained 77\.9% and 15\.2% of the total inertia, respectively\. The global chi\-square test indicated a non\-random association between clinical manifestations and representative karyotypic findings \(chi\-square = 69\.92, df = 16, P &lt; 0\.001\)\. Conclusion Peripheral blood karyotyping remains a useful first\-line cytogenetic test in reproductive genetic counseling\. Clinically relevant chromosomal abnormalities and chromosomal polymorphisms should be reported and interpreted separately\. Phenotype\-guided interpretation may improve the clinical value of karyotyping and help identify patients who require additional molecular confirmation or multidisciplinary counseling\.
