# Fujie, Naoto: The Philosopher Operational\-Shape Machine Heterogeneous Ignition, Mutual Transformation, Extinction, Reflexive Reconstruction, Reality Effects and Penetration, Xenoscience, and the PyTorch–Xeno–Ziran Experimental Programme

**Autores:**

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper develops the Philosopher Operational\-Shape Machine \(POSM\) as a framework for investigating the historical transmission, mechanical transformation, causal persistence, retrospective reconstruction, and possible extinction of heterogeneous operations associated with philosophy\. The framework does not presuppose a philosopher, philosophical subject, or fixed taxonomy of philosophical operations\. It distinguishes three potentially independent trajectories: historical transmission, the persistence of causal capacities across heterogeneous media, and subsequent reflexive reconstruction\. These trajectories can diverge, interact, transform, or cease under different configurations and temporal conditions\. The study examines twelve historical philosophical trajectories, including Sellars, Brandom, Simondon, Whitehead, Deleuze, Luhmann, Brassier, Sacilotto, Wolfendale, McDowell, Kant, and Hegel\. Their theoretical constraints are investigated through computational reconstructions, counterfactual interventions, mechanism ablations, historical reinsertion, and comparisons with generic predictive, rule\-based, and belief\-revision models\. The experimental programme includes exploratory simulations of heterogeneous operational mechanisms, causal intervention, multi\-agent disagreement, historical retention, observational constraints, and downstream effects\. Positive and negative results are retained, including cases where generic computational models reproduce or outperform philosopher\-associated surrogate mechanisms\. The paper distinguishes successful computational implementation from philosophical fidelity, historical inheritance, justificatory entitlement, and autonomous philosophical operation\. It does not claim to have demonstrated the faithful computational realization of twelve philosophical systems\. Its central contribution is a conditional account of how historically transmitted philosophical operations may persist, transform, fragment, become retrospectively reconstructible, or lose their operational conditions across machine\-mediated scientific and institutional environments\. \(direct link \)
