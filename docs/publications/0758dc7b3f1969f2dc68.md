## Descrição do feed

\_The Physical Reality Module Embodied Knowledge, Situated Intelligence, and the Human Ground of Artificial Mind\_ 5\. 2026Yann LeCun has argued that large language models suffer from a fundamental deficit: they manipulate symbols without grounding in physical reality, a condition he describes as a &quot;cat level gap&quot; in machine intelligence\. The dominant response to this diagnosis is architectural — build world models, embodied robots, sensorimotor feedback loops\. This essay proposes a philosophically prior intervention, while carefully distinguishing two levels at which &quot;grounding&quot; can be understood: the level of the model&\#x27;s internal representations, where LeCun&\#x27;s architectural program operates and where the present proposal makes no claim, and the level of the extended cognitive system, where it does\. Drawing on Donna Haraway&\#x27;s situated knowledges \(1988\), Merleau\-Ponty&\#x27;s phenomenology of the body\-subject \(1945\), Andy Clark and David Chalmers&\#x27;s extended mind thesis \(1998\), and Paulo Freire&\#x27;s pedagogy of the oppressed \(1970\), it introduces and theorizes the concept of the Physical Reality Module \(PRM\): the embodied human agent whose consequence\-tested, body\-inscribed, situationally grounded knowledge constitutes the form of intelligence that AI systems cannot generate from within themselves but can, in partnership, draw upon\. The essay argues that PRMs are not rare or exceptional; they are distributed across the global population, present wherever human beings have been forced by circumstance to develop deep, reliable knowledge of how the world actually works\. Crucially, the essay argues that the populations most systematically excluded from AI development — refugees, survivors of displacement, workers in the informal economy, communities of the Global South — are disproportionately rich in PRM\-type knowledge, and that the failure to recognize and integrate this knowledge constitutes not merely a technical limitation but a structural injustice\. The essay concludes by proposing, as a testable hypothesis rather than a settled conclusion, a framework for understanding the human\-AI partnership as a complementary cognitive system in which the PRM&\#x27;s embodied grounding and the AI&\#x27;s pattern\-vast generativity together constitute a form of intelligence neither could achieve alone\. \(direct link \)
