# Reproducibility of Machine Learning Accuracy for Chemically Stabilised Soils using an Open UCS Database

**Autores:** Vedprakash Chandrakant Maralapalle; Abdullah Ansari; Vinay Ashok Rangari; Dhirajkumar Lal; Shrikant Shinde

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Machine learning models for the unconfined compressive strength \(UCS\) of lime and cement stabilised soils often report test coefficients of determination \(R 2 \) above 0\.9\. Cross\-validation is widely used to tune these models, but the headline score is usually taken from one train\-test split, and its sensitivity to that split is rarely reported\. This study examines that sensitivity on an openly licensed database of 194 records\. A single 80:20 split was repeated 200 times with fixed hyperparameters, and five model configurations were compared under ten repeats of five\-fold nested cross\-validation, together with monotonic binder constraints, empirical interval coverage and a leverage\-based applicability domain\. Across the 200 splits, test R 2 ranged from −0\.235 to 0\.962 \(mean 0\.782, SD 0\.181\)\. Under nested resampling a regularised multilayer perceptron \(R 2 = 0\.880 ± 0\.103\) outperformed extreme gradient boosting \(0\.810 ± 0\.195\), reversing the single\-split ranking\. Monotonic constraints removed physically inadmissible binder responses with no measurable loss in R 2 or RMSE and an MAE increase of 0\.007 MPa\. Naive bootstrap intervals covered 33\.3% of observations against a nominal 90%, rising to 87\.2% with out\-of\-fold residuals, and five records fell outside the applicability domain\. Model interpretation agreed with the original study despite the differences in accuracy\. Reporting accuracy as a distribution, with coverage and domain checks, is recommended for small geotechnical databases\.
