# Digital Empathy Scale: A Validity and Reliability Study

**Autores:** Abdullah Yıldırmaz

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Online communication platforms now constitute one of the principal arenas of interpersonal exchange; constraints on nonverbal signaling, asynchronous turn\-taking, and varying degrees of anonymity together reshape how individuals interpret others&\#x27; states and decide whether to act on them\. The present research developed and examined the psychometric properties of the Digital Empathy Scale \(DES\), a 12\-item instrument intended to capture empathic orientation in online contexts, using a sequential three\-phase design\. In the first phase \(N = 300\), a 29\-item pool was submitted to principal axis factoring; the analysis returned a one\-factor solution explaining 45\.6% of the total variance \(KMO = \.892; Bartlett χ²\(66\) = 1464\.63, p &lt; \.001; factor loadings \.57–\.68\)\. In the second phase \(N = 417\), confirmatory factor analysis on an independent sample supported the proposed structure \(CFI = \.945; TLI = \.928; RMSEA = \.072; SRMR = \.050\) and yielded external validity evidence \(general empathy r = \.599; digital well\-being r = \.367; social desirability r = \.164; α = \.929; split\-half r = \.871\)\. In the third phase \(N = 80\), the scale exhibited substantial temporal stability across a two\-week interval \(r = \.817, p &lt; \.001\)\. Taken together, the findings provide convergent psychometric evidence supporting the DES as a sound instrument for assessing empathy within digital communication settings\.
