# Teacher\-Facing Generative AI and Workplace Mental Health in Higher Education: Perceived Demands and Resources

**Autores:** Liliya V\. Batyuk; Volodymyr Borysov; Iryna Hlazkova; Yana Romantsova; Olena Каrasova; Оксана Миколаївна Дуброва; Олена Ронжес

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

To read the full\-text Open Access article and download the PDFs, please visit our Issue portal at Paradigm \(Walter de Gruyter\): https://reference\-global\.com/article/10\.56508/mhgcj\.v9i1\.374 Introduction: Workplace mental health promotion in higher education requires attention to how digital technologies reshape teaching demands and resources\. Purpose: To identify perceived demands and resources surrounding teacher\-facing generative artificial intelligence \(AI\) and interpret their implications for teachers’ mental health promotion\. Methodology: An exploratory mixed\-methods study combined a survey of 150 participants \(56 English as a foreign language \(EFL\) teachers and 94 students/student\-tutors\) with 12 teacher interviews at two Ukrainian universities in January\-February 2026\. Descriptive statistics and four exploratory Fisher exact tests with Holm adjustment were integrated with thematic analysis of 30 selected translated interview extracts\. Job Demands\-Resources and self\-determination theories informed a post\-hoc interpretation\. Results: Among teachers, 53\.6% rated an AI platform for EFL teachers \(Twee\) very useful for preparation\-time saving and 57\.1% for task variety\. Across the mixed sample, access \(27\.3%\), quality assurance \(22\.0%\), curriculum alignment \(20\.0%\), and training/time \(20\.0%\) were the leading constraints\. Interview extracts linked anticipated workload relief to individual student support, while highlighting learning demands and the importance of professional judgment, empathy, and mentoring\. Conclusions: These perceptions identify work\-design issues relevant to mental health promotion during AI implementation\. Participatory decisions, protected learning and recovery time, and accessible psychosocial support are priorities for institutional consideration
