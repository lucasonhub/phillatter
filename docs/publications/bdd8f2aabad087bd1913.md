# The Application of People Analytics Based on Predictive Modeling to Reduce Employee Turnover in the Banking Sector

**Autores:** Romario Tuhepary; Vanessa Victoria Micibaroe; Clara Febriani Wader; Julian Haumahu

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Employee turnover presents a significant strategic and financial challenge in the banking sector, leading to substantial recruitment costs, loss of institutional knowledge, disruption of branch operations, and diminished team productivity\. Traditional human resource management \(HRM\) practices rely primarily on retrospective metrics, exit interviews, and managerial intuition, which are descriptive and ill\-suited for anticipating future departure risks\. This paper investigates the application of People Analytics and predictive machine learning modeling to identify employee attrition risks and support proactive retention strategies in banking institutions\. Drawing on recent literature \(2022–2026\), theoretical frameworks \(Resource\-Based View, Human Capital Theory, and Technology\-Organization\-Environment framework\), and empirical benchmarks, this study evaluates multi\-source employee data taxonomies, comparative predictive modeling algorithms \(Logistic Regression, Decision Trees, Support Vector Machines, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting\), key turnover drivers, organizational benefits, implementation challenges, and ethical/privacy safeguards\. The literature reveals that non\-linear ensemble models, particularly Gradient Boosting \(ROC\-AUC 0\.906\) and Random Forest \(ROC\-AUC 0\.891 / 0\.890\), consistently outperform parametric baseline models in predicting turnover probability\. Empirical feature importances highlight job satisfaction, overtime, career development opportunities, compensation growth, and tenure length as the primary predictors of attrition\. Furthermore, this paper analyzes regulatory requirements under the EU Artificial Intelligence Act \(Regulation EU 2024/1689\), which classifies AI workforce analytics as high\-risk, necessitating explainable AI \(XAI/SHAP\), continuous bias auditing, and human\-in\-the\-loop governance\. Finally, a structured six\-layer implementation framework is proposed to assist banking organizations in transitioning from reactive reporting to evidence\-based talent retention\.
