# Kim, GeonWoo: A Lower\-Bearer Count Is Not a Total Census: The Exactness Burden for Multi\-Agent AI

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

An evaluator correctly identifies every lower bearer in a multi\-agent artificial intelligence \(AI\) system and finds that none has a property P\. What warrants extending that verdict to all relevant bearers in the system? This paper identifies the Exactness Burden: the warrant for excluding further bearers when a completed lower census is used as an exact total\. The analysis applies across individuation criteria and separates three counting achievements: engineering decomposition, lower individuation, and total\-census closure\. The warrant can come from a higher\-level audit with justified coverage, a justified flatness premise, or a justified exhaustive composition or exclusion rule\. Two controlled models make the burden concrete\. F₀ and N preserve the same n lower bearers, engineering count, specified public task record, and selected performance, yet receive different collective verdicts under the same criterion\. That criterion is thin: it individuates organizations by their recurrent completion relations, in which retained state guides activity whose results return to update what is retained\. Every return relation in F₀ is local; N also completes a return relation across members while local completion continues\. Under uniform cross\-level application, N therefore contains a distinct collective bearer, establishing at least n \+ 1 and refuting exactly n before a wider census is complete\. Matched counts and task\-level evidence thus cannot decide the collective verdict; the additional bearer calls for assessment of P or justified exclusion under the receiving inquiry’s criteria\. Comparisons with individuation theories and an architectural audit connect candidate recognition to grounds for completeness\.
