# Quantitative CT\-Derived Imaging Features Improve Preoperative Risk Stratification of Intraductal Papillary Mucinous Neoplasms

**Autores:** Tae Seung Lee; Hong Min Shin; In\-Ho Park; Jae Seok Bae; In Rae Cho; Woo Hyun Paik; Wooil Kwon; Dong Ho Lee; Haeryoung Kim; Ji Kon Ryu; Jin Ho Choi; Sang Hyub Lee

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: Accurate preoperative identification of high\-grade dysplasia \(HGD\) or malignancy in intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms \(IPMNs\) remains challenging using conventional guideline\-based imaging criteria\. Quantitative computed tomography \(CT\)\-based features may detect subtle structural changes preceding overt radiologic findings\. This study aimed to evaluate whether the pancreatic index \(PI\) and additional three\-dimensional CT\-derived quantitative features improve prediction of HGD or malignancy in IPMNs\. METHODS: This single\-center retrospective study included patients who underwent surgical resection for pathologically confirmed IPMNs between January 2001 and August 2024 and had available preoperative pancreas\-protocol CT\. Three\-dimensional quantitative parameters were analyzed\. RESULTS: Among 333 patients, 204 had low\-grade dysplasia \(LGD\), 56 had HGD, and 73 had malignancy\. PI decreased with increasing histologic grade \(75\.6 ± 10\.2% in LGD, 74\.5 ± 12\.0% in HGD, 70\.5 ± 12\.6% in malignancy; p = 0\.003\)\. Pancreatic Hounsfield unit standard deviation \(HU\-SD\) was significantly higher in HGD and malignancy than in LGD \(25\.6 ± 5\.9, 29\.0 ± 8\.7, and 29\.6 ± 6\.2, respectively; p &lt; 0\.001\)\. A combined model integrating guideline\-based criteria with PI, CI, cystic HU\-SD, pancreatic HU\-SD, and pancreatic duct volume demonstrated better discrimination than the guideline\-only model \(area under the curve 0\.800 vs\. 0\.760; p = 0\.007\)\. CONCLUSIONS: Quantitative CT\-derived imaging features provide incremental value for predicting HGD and malignancy when integrated with conventional guideline\-based criteria\. These noninvasive imaging biomarkers may enhance risk stratification and provide additional information to support clinical decision\-making when integrated with conventional criteria\. Further external validation is required to establish their clinical utility in routine practice\.
