# Alhosseini Almodarresieh, Seyed Alireza: Consciousness in Large Language Models: A Critical Review and Operationalization of the ‘Reverse Consciousness’ Hypothesis

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

The question of whether large language models \(LLMs\) can attain consciousness has moved from philosophy to public discourse, yet it remains empirically unresolved\. This paper critically reviews the application of three major theories of consciousness—Integrated Information Theory \(IIT\), Global Workspace Theory \(GWT\), and Higher\-Order Theories \(HOT\)—to Transformer architectures, finding that current evidence does not support the attribution of consciousness\-related properties to LLMs\. We introduce the Reverse Consciousness hypothesis not as a claim that LLMs are conscious, but as a falsifiable research program: the conjecture that language\-trained systems may develop internal representations that are structural analogs of conscious processing patterns, traversing the path language → representation rather than experience → language\. We present a synthetic experiment demonstrating that Transformers lack the persistent state required for a stable self\-model, and we propose three operational tests for evaluating this hypothesis in future work\. We conclude that the question remains open, but the burden of evidence lies with those who would attribute consciousness to systems that lack recurrent feedback, persistent identity, and embodied interaction\. Keywords: artificial consciousness, large language models, philosophy of mind, IIT, global workspace theory, Transformer architecture, self\-model, reverse consciousness\.
