# Niami, Morteza: The Phenomenology of the Mask and Finite Continuance: An Ontological\-Ethical Reading of Stanley Kubrick&\#x27;s Eyes Wide Shut

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This article develops an ontological\-ethical reading of Stanley Kubrick&\#x27;s Eyes Wide Shut \(1999\) through the concepts of Finite Continuance and Dynamic Mask Theory\. It argues that the film&\#x27;s philosophical centre is not the revelation of what lies behind the mask, but the question of whether finite subjects can continue responsibly when complete access to the other is impossible\. Engaging critically with existing Kubrick scholarship—psychoanalytic, feminist, philosophical, and Deleuzian—the article proposes a new framework that moves beyond readings of the film as a dream, a conspiracy, or a study in jealousy\. The mask is analysed as a mediating structure that temporarily organises identity, desire, social position, vulnerability, and access\. The masked ritual at Victor Ziegler&\#x27;s mansion is examined as an extreme configuration of Form\-Monopoly, in which institutionalised form regulates who may appear, participate, recognise, and remain within a given relational field\. Four scene\-specific analyses—of the Ziegler party, the confession sequence, the masked ritual, and the final exchange—demonstrate how Kubrick&\#x27;s cinematic form \(lighting, framing, sound design, duration, and camera movement\) enacts the very ontological leakage the article theorises\. A Deleuzian reading of the film&\#x27;s slow temporality further reveals how the time\-image suspends sensory\-motor continuity, opening experience to the pure optical and sonic situations that structure epistemic opacity\. The article anticipates and addresses three potential criticisms of the Finite Continuance framework: its alleged neglect of social and political dimensions, its resistance to normative evaluation, and its potential cultural particularity\. Alice Harford&\#x27;s confession concerning an imagined sexual encounter is distinguished from evidence of participation in the ritual: her desire is significant precisely because an imagined possibility can transform the relational field without becoming an external event\. The concluding exchange between William and Alice is interpreted through Responsible Suspension and the Ethics of Sufficiency\. Their relationship does not survive because uncertainty is eliminated, but because exhaustive certainty ceases to function as the necessary condition of continuation\. Eyes Wide Shut thus becomes a philosophical investigation into how finite subjects can preserve relational agency when complete access to another person remains impossible\.
