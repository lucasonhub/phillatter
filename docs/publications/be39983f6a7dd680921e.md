# Design and optimization of an Ag/Cu–black phosphorus/Franckeite heterostructure for high\-sensitivity surface plasmon resonance biosensing

**Autores:** Jacob Wekalao; Tobias Otieno

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Surface plasmon resonance \(SPR\) biosensors enable label\-free, real\-time detection, but simultaneously achieving strong field confinement, near\-critical coupling, and a high figure of merit \(FoM\) remains challenging, particularly when low\-loss silver must be protected from aqueous media\. Methods A CaF 2 /Ag/Cu/black phosphorus \(BP\)/Franckeite Kretschmann heterostructure was modeled at λ = 633 nm using the frequency\-domain Maxwell wave equation and the transfer matrix method under TM\-polarized angular and phase interrogation\. The Ag and Cu thicknesses were optimized individually and jointly, size\-dependent Drude damping corrections were applied, and performance was benchmarked against a Cu\-only reference with identical BP/Franckeite overlayers\. Fabrication tolerance to BP layer number and Franckeite thickness was also evaluated\. Results Joint optimization identified a near\-critical\-coupling configuration at approximately 31 nm Ag and 7 nm Cu, with a minimum reflectance of 1\.1 × 10 −3 within the sensing window\. Over 1\.328–1\.336 RIU, the sensor reached a peak angular sensitivity of 372°/RIU and a FoM of 47\.0 RIU −1 at n s = 1\.332, approximately 62% and 78% higher than the Cu\-only reference\. Phase interrogation reached 29,200°/RIU, corresponding to a minimum detectable refractive\-index change of approximately 3\.4 × 10 −7 RIU\. The 1/e evanescent\-field penetration depth was 178–201 nm\. Size\-dependent damping reduced the FoM by approximately 5%, and realistic 2D\-layer thickness variations reduced it by no more than approximately 10%\. Discussion The Ag/Cu/BP/Franckeite architecture is a promising platform for label\-free biosensing, including carcinoembryonic antigen \(CEA\) detection, subject to experimental validation of long\-term stability, surface functionalization, and thermally compensated phase readout\.
