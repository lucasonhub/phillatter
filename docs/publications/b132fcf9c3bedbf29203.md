# Integrating Systemic Immune\-Nutritional Indices with Anatomical Staging to Indicate Occult Bone Metastasis in Breast Cancer

**Autores:** M\. Genc; Bilal Çağrı Bozdemir

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: Current oncological guidelines restrict routine whole\-body bone scintigraphy \(WBS\) in asymptomatic, early\-stage breast cancer patients, creating a critical clinical blind spot for occult skeletal metastases\. Because tumor\-driven systemic inflammation and nutritional depletion prepare a permissive pre\-metastatic bone niche, this study investigated the diagnostic association of the Naples Prognostic Score \(NPS\)—a composite immune\-nutritional index—with bone metastasis in breast cancer\. Materials and Methods: This retrospective cohort study evaluated 104 female breast cancer patients\. Clinical characteristics, laboratory parameters, and blinded consensus WBS evaluations by two independent nuclear medicine physicians were meticulously analyzed\. The independent predictive power of systemic biomarkers was assessed using multivariable logistic regression\. Diagnostic accuracy was evaluated via Receiver Operating Characteristic curve analysis\. Results: Overall, bone metastasis was identified in 30\.8% \(32/104\) of the cohort\. Multivariable analysis revealed that a high NPS \(score 3\-4\) is an independent indicator associated with bone metastasis \(Adjusted Odds Ratio: 7\.16, 95% Confidence Interval: 1\.62–31\.72; p=0\.010\)\. Integrating the high NPS into a baseline clinical model improved the diagnostic accuracy \(Area Under the Curve\) from 0\.864 to 0\.892\. Crucially, among early\-stage \(American Joint Committee on Cancer Stage I\-II\) patients conventionally excluded from routine imaging, a high NPS unmasked a remarkably high metastasis rate of 42\.9%, compared to just 17\.6% in the low NPS group\. Conclusion: To our knowledge, this is one of the first studies to demonstrate that the NPS may serve as a promising, non\-invasive triaging biomarker for bone metastasis\. By mathematically reflecting the host&\#x27;s systemic immune\-nutritional status, the baseline NPS provides a cost\-effective triaging tool that may address the current staging gap\. It may help refine imaging selection by ensuring that high\-risk early\-stage patients receive prompt skeletal screening while sparing low\-risk individuals from unnecessary radiation exposure and financial toxicity\.Keywords: Host inflammation, Naples Prognostic Score, Oncology triage, Pre\-metastatic niche, Systemic Immune\-Inflammation Index, Whole\-body bone scintigraphy\.
