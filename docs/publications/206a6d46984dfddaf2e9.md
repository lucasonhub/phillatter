# Cohort\-relative knee pain, walking performance, and risk of total knee arthroplasty: An Osteoarthritis Initiative analysis

**Autores:** Amr Khafagy; Mohammed Safwat Hamza

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objectives: The objective of the study is to examine whether cohort\-relative knee pain and 400\-m walking performance are associated with subsequent total knee arthroplasty and whether preserved walking identifies a lower\-hazard subgroup among higher\-pain knees\. Methods: This was a secondary analysis of the Osteoarthritis Initiative, comprising 4,247 participants contributing 8,494 knees\. Knees were grouped by cohort\-median splits of Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Arthritis Index pain \(&gt;2/20\) and 400\-m walk time \(&gt;297\.8 s\) into four phenotypes: Lower Pain/Faster Walk \(reference\), Lower Pain/Slower Walk, Higher Pain/Faster Walk, and Higher Pain/Slower Walk\. A knee\-specific marginal Cox model with cluster\-robust variance estimated the cause\-specific hazard of first arthroplasty, with death as a competing event, adjusted for age, sex, body mass index, Kellgren–Lawrence grade ≥2, depressive symptoms, and subcohort\. Results: Over a median of 14\.0 years, 658 first arthroplasties and 540 competing deaths occurred\. Both higher\-pain phenotypes showed elevated hazard versus reference \(Higher Pain/Faster Walk 2\.58 \[2\.03–3\.28\]; Higher Pain/Slower Walk 2\.23 \[1\.76–2\.84\]; both p &lt; 0\.001\); Lower Pain/Slower Walk did not \(1\.16 \[0\.91–1\.48\]; p = 0\.24\)\. Neither the direct higher\-pain contrast \(1\.16 \[0\.93–1\.44\]; p = 0\.19\) nor the pain × walk interaction \(0\.75 \[0\.54–1\.03\]; p = 0\.07\) was significant\. Nine\-year cumulative incidence, with competing mortality, was 3\.8%, 6\.0%, 15\.4%, and 17\.6%\. Adjustment for medial joint\-space width attenuated but did not remove the associations\. Conclusion: Knee pain above the cohort median was associated with a higher hazard of subsequent arthroplasty, whereas dichotomized 400\-m walk performance did not clearly discriminate hazard among higher\-pain knees\. These cohort\-relative findings, based on a single non\-knee\-specific walking measure, are hypothesis\-generating and carry no implication for arthroplasty appropriateness\.
