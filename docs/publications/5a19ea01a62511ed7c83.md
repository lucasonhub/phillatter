# Cross\-sectional study of eating behaviours, physical activity and depression in college students in China

**Autores:** Yunfeng Bai; Boyue Zhao; Yuan Liu; Feng Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examined the associations of eating behaviours and physical activity \(PA\) with depressive symptom severity and the mediating role of self\-efficacy among college students in Northwest China\. A cross\-sectional study is performed from 2022 to 2023 across ten colleges in five provinces in Northwest China\. Eating behaviours, physical activity, depressive symptom severity, and self\-efficacy are assessed using self\-developed eating behaviour items, the Physical Activity Rating Scale\-3 \(PARS\-3\), the Patient Health Questionnaire\-9 \(PHQ\-9\), and the General Self\-Efficacy Scale \(GSES\), respectively\. Multivariable linear regression and mediation analyses are performed\. The final analysis includes 7,041 participants\. Using a PHQ\-9 cutoff of ≥10, 8\.79% screen positive for probable depression\. In the fully adjusted model, frequent breakfast skipping \(β = 2\.57, 95% CI 2\.26\-2\.88\), late\-night snacking \(β = 2\.11, 95% CI 1\.63\-2\.58\), irregular meals \(β = 4\.55, 95% CI 4\.05\-5\.05\), and strong preferences for high\-calorie \(β = 1\.43, 95% CI 1\.04\-1\.81\), sweet \(β = 0\.71, 95% CI 0\.24\-1\.19\), and spicy foods \(β = 1\.52, 95% CI 1\.22\-1\.82\) are associated with higher PHQ\-9 scores, as is frequent alcohol consumption \(β = 3\.52, 95% CI 2\.99\-4\.04\)\. Higher PA is associated with lower PHQ\-9 scores \(β = −0\.03, 95% CI \-0\.03 to \-0\.02\)\. For strong sweet food preference, the indirect effect through self\-efficacy is 0\.29 \(95% CI 0\.11\-0\.48\), accounting for 40\.22% of the total effect\. Specific eating behaviours and PA are associated with depressive symptom severity, and self\-efficacy may partly mediate these associations\.
