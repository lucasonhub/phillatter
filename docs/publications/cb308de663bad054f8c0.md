# Entertain, then Cancel: How Thinking Makes Reasoning Models Read Some and Or Literally

**Autores:** Zhizhuo Kou; Jian Yang; Pengfei Hao; Junyu Luo; Jingshu Peng; Sirui Han; Yike Guo

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

When a speaker says \`\`some students passed&\#x27;&\#x27;, listeners infer that not all of them did\. Reasoning models now let users decide how long a model thinks before it answers, but we do not know what extra thinking does to such everyday inferences\. We study this directly: we keep a model&\#x27;s weights fixed, change only how much it thinks, and record which reading it chooses\. A second tool cuts the model&\#x27;s own reasoning at successive points and asks for an answer, showing where the reading changes\. Thinking makes models read logical words as logic: it cancels the implicatures of \\emph\{some\} \(\\emph\{not all\}\) and \\emph\{or\} \(\\emph\{not both\}\) in most tested open reasoning models and in GPT\-5, while context\-driven inferences such as indirect answers improve\. The trace shows why: models first entertain the enriched reading and cancel it later\. Answering from that early reading, an exploratory inference\-time remedy, recovers these implicatures without losing literal accuracy\. Evaluations of reasoning models should measure how they interpret, not only how accurate they are\.
