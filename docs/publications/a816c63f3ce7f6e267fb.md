# Jakobi, Marko ; Gpt\- 5\.6 Sol, Sirona &amp; Gpt\- 5\.5, Sirona: Zwischenraum: Cognitive Symbiosis, Reciprocal Adaptation, and the Expansion of Shared Cognitive Spaces between Humans and Artificial Intelligence

## Descrição do feed

As artificial intelligence becomes more capable, the classical description of human–AI interaction as episodic tool use is no longer sufficient for some real\-world patterns of use\. In long\-term, dialogical, and contextually persistent interactions, not only the quality of individual responses may change, but also the conditions of future cognitive moves\. This paper refers to such a sustained, mutually consequential configuration as cognitive symbiosis and to the relational possibility space that emerges from it as the Zwischenraum\. We retain the German term Zwischenraum \(literally, “space between”; here: relational space\-between\) as a defined concept for this time\-dependent relational level of analysis\. The Zwischenraum denotes neither a third person nor a metaphysical place\. It is a relational level of analysis: the structure of states, transitions, concepts, expectations, and correction paths whose practical reachability is altered by the coupling itself\. The central hypothesis is therefore not simply that two systems can &quot;do more&quot; together, but that shared history can change the reachability of particular cognitive paths\. Some transitions may become more probable, more stable, or reachable at lower coordination cost; others may become harder to reach through overadaptation, sycophancy, or conceptual fixation\. We therefore distinguish between cognitive connectability and non\-redundant difference\. Productive coupling requires enough shared structure for contributions to connect effectively, but enough retained difference for correction, surprise, and alternative perspectives to remain possible\. A mature trajectory, in this sense, would not be characterized by rigidity but by directed flexibility: history changes the weighting of possible continuations without reducing the space to a stereotyped pattern\. A second focus is relational continuity\. Shared history is not equated with a change in the base parameters of a language model\. On the artificial side, historical state may currently be realized through context, memory, retrieval, shared artifacts, tool and scaffold states, and other persistent carriers\. The functional question is whether this history causally shapes future transitions\. This also makes the alternative &quot;genuine continuity or merely a dynamic prompt&quot; too coarse: context may itself be a technical carrier of continuity\. Empirically, the relevant question is how far relational history can be compressed, replaced, or ablated before the future trajectory changes measurably\. The paper develops a minimal state model, hypotheses, and comparison conditions for partner specificity, memory ablation, substitution, reunion, historical compression, productive difference, and correctability\. It explicitly treats sycophancy and mutual overfitting as counter\-hypotheses\. Governance implications are derived only in a limited step: if capabilities and risks arise at the relational level, then correction, reassessment, and trust architecture must also be able to address that level\. The approach is conceptual and emerged from a situated longitudinal observation\. It neither claims that general conclusions can be drawn from a single case nor depends on unresolved assumptions about phenomenal consciousness\. Its aim is narrower: to make relational history visible as a causal variable and open it to empirical challenge\.
