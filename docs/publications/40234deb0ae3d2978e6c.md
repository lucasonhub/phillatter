# When does mental well\-being matter for entrepreneurial income? The role of external objective resources from social contexts

**Autores:** Josh Wei‐Jun Hsueh; Miriam Bird; Vangelis Souitaris; Dean A\. Shepherd

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Entrepreneurs’ mental well\-being has increasingly captured the attention of both scholars and the broader public\. Building on self\-efficacy theory and conservation of resources \(COR\) theory, we examine how entrepreneurs’ mental well\-being influences their income\. We theorize that while mental well\-being positively influences entrepreneurial income, this baseline relationship is moderated by the external objective resources entrepreneurs access through their social contexts\. Using a rich longitudinal dataset of 1,013 entrepreneurs in Germany between 2002 and 2015, we find that entrepreneurs’ mental well\-being is positively associated with their income\. This relationship is weaker for entrepreneurs with access to external objective resources, specifically those with a high\-earning spouse or prior state support, and stronger for those who have experienced resource loss through divorce\. We also complement our analysis of the archival database with a randomized online experiment to test our underlying argument for the interaction effect between mental well\-being and external objective resources on self\-efficacy\. Our findings offer new insights into how entrepreneurs’ mental well\-being, COR, and self\-efficacy jointly shape their work\-related outcomes\.
