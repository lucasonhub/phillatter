# Validation of a computer\-vision milking parlour monitoring system

**Autores:** Guardado Servellón; David L\. Renaud; Nolan Davies; Madyson Dailey; Kelsey L\. Spence; T\.J\. DeVries; Rita Couto Serrenho

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial intelligence can help identify suboptimal milking practices and provide reports without time\-consuming visits\. The objective of this study was to validate a computer\-vision milking parlour monitoring system \(CVS, Cattle Care Inc\.\) by comparing its reports against trained human observers \(n = 2, comparison method\)\. A camera was installed in the parlour of two dairy farms in Ontario, Canada, one operating a herringbone parlour and the other a parallel parlour\. Four one\-hour segments \(2 per farm\), selected by the CVS, were independently analysed by human observers and the CVS\. Prep\-lag time, average unit\-on time, turn time, and milking protocol deviations were assessed in each shift and compared between methods\. For continuous variables, Lin’s concordance correlation coefficients \(CCC\) were calculated to evaluate the agreement between CVS and human observers; Bland–Altman plots were used to assess systematic bias\. For categorical variables, the F\-score was calculated to assess event\-detection performance\. The CCC for unit\-on time \(n=44 milking turns\) was 0\.999 \(95% CI: 0\.999\-0\.999\), for prep\-lag time \(n = 214 cows\) was 0\.995 \(95% CI: 0\.994–0\.996\) and for turn time \(n = 25 turns\) was 0\.998 \(95% CI: 0\.997–0\.999\)\. All protocol deviations flagged by the human observers \(n = 51 events\) were detected by the CVS; six towel misuse events detected by the CVS were missed by the human observers\. An F\-score of 1 was obtained for the CVS method\. Under the conditions tested, the CVS demonstrated high agreement with human observations for prep\-lag time, unit\-on time, turn time, and milking\-protocol deviations, while also identifying some events not detected by human observers\. The assessed technology may complement human expertise by enabling more efficient and streamlined assessment of milking parlour performance\.
