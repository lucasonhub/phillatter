# CopyQuad: Quadrupedal Skill Transfer from a Single Side\-View Video Without a Depth Model

**Autores:** Mohamad H\. Danesh; Raina Hu; Mengxue Xu; Hsiu\-Chin Lin

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Video\-to\-robot pipelines for quadrupedal imitation insert a learned lifting stage, either a monocular depth model or a per\-clip 3D reconstruction, to recover the one axis a side\-view camera cannot see\. We present CopyQuad, which omits that stage and pins the missing axis to the robot&\#x27;s own stance width instead\. The setting is side\-view footage of sagittally dominated motion, where limb motion is almost entirely in the image plane\. A frozen 2D keypoint detector and a fixed analytic retargeting then turn a single uncalibrated clip into a robot reference whose foot targets are reachable by construction, so no per\-clip trajectory optimization is needed\. One phase\-indexed reward structure, whose only per\-behavior freedom is three scalars from a shared sweep, learns a dog walk, a horse walk, a horse trot and two non\-periodic gestures, all deployed zero\-shot on hardware\. We then measure what omitting the axis costs\. In a training\-free rigidity test, a video depth model varies the distance between the animal&\#x27;s hips by $40$ to $107\\%$ of its own mean and a single\-image metric model by $22$ to $58\\%$, though that distance cannot move\. Motion capture puts the true lateral paw travel of a dog walk at $4\.5$ cm peak to peak, a mean deviation near $1\.4$ cm against the $3\.5$ cm of lateral foot error our policies already leave, so the discarded axis sits below the pipeline&\#x27;s resolution\. Depth repaired to feasibility never beats the constant, and the unrepaired estimates put the robot on the ground\.
