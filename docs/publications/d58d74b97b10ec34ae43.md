# Physics\-Guided Graphical Signatures and Machine Learning for In\-Cylinder Pressure Sensor Health Monitoring

**Autores:** Mahmoud Elkhodr; Abdallah AL Sabbagh; Bilal Youssef

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In\-cylinder pressure sensors \(ICPSs\) in internal combustion engines provide high\-value feedback for closed\-loop combustion control, but their exposure to severe thermal and mechanical conditions creates a need for reliable onboard health monitoring\. This study develops a physics\-guided diagnostic framework in which compression\-stroke pressure measurements are transformed through a two\-dimensional graphical\-signature representation before machine\-learning classification\. An experimentally calibrated and validated diesel\-engine simulation platform was used to generate 4800 independent runs spanning ten trapped\-mass operating levels\. Sensor degradations comprised positive and negative pressure offsets from 10 to 50 kPa and crank\-angle timing delays from 1∘ to 5∘, each at five severity levels\. Nested cross\-validation, feature ablation, severity\-resolved analysis, leave\-one\-trapped\-mass\-level\-out validation, and a repeated\-seed robustness check were used\. The pressure\-signature character showed markedly lower trapped\-mass dependence than the physical variables \(0\.76% versus 90\.2–99\.7% variance explained\)\. A single\-character RBF\-SVM achieved approximately 90\.9% balanced accuracy and 95% specificity in nested run\-level validation and retained 90\.9% balanced accuracy under the unseen\-trapped\-mass test, outperforming physical\-only features \(78\.8%\) and the five\-feature model \(87\.2%\)\. Repeating the full nested procedure across six random seeds yielded 90\.94% ± 0\.18 percentage points balanced accuracy, indicating low split sensitivity\. Fault sensitivity increased with severity and approached 99–100% from approximately 30 kPa or 3∘ upward\. These results support graphical\-signature compression as a compact, operating\-condition\-robust representation for machine\-learning\-based ICPS health monitoring\.
