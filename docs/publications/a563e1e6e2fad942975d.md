# Boether, Olivier: Kahneman’s Error: Dual\-Process Theory, Instrumental Rationality, and the Foreclosure of the Determination of Ends

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Daniel Kahneman’s heuristics\-and\-biases program and his dual\-process model of cognition transformed the study of human reasoning by mapping the systematic ways in which fast, intuitive judgment \(System 1\) departs from the norms of slow, deliberative reasoning \(System 2\)\. This treatise argues that, for all its achievement, the program rests upon a foreclosure\. Its catalog of “errors” and “biases” presupposes a normative standard of rationality, and that standard is purely instrumental: it is the logic of coherence, probability, and expected\-utility maximization, which assesses whether means are efficiently fitted to ends while taking the ends themselves as given\. Kahneman can therefore certify that an agent reasons correctly toward a goal; he possesses no faculty that determines which goals are worth having\. The determination of ends is the office of the philosophical self, and it is absent from the model\. By installing instrumental rationality as the standard of good thinking, Kahneman forecloses the philosophical self not by omission, as in Damasio, nor by elimination, as in Sapolsky, but at the level of the norm itself\. Drawing upon the author’s Double Helix of Understanding and the multiplicative model of human conduct, the paper proposes a tripartite correction in which the two systems are governed by a philosophical self that evaluates ends\. The errors Kahneman catalogs are errors of means; the deeper error is the framework’s inability to recognize an error of ends\.
