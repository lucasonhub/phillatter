# Striatal network dysfunction underlying speech impairment in early Parkinson’s disease

**Autores:** Caitlin Paige Rogers; Sandy Mournet; Cécile Galléa; Emma Biondetti; Graziella Mangone; Sara Sambin; Lydia Chougar; Nicolas Villain; Rahul Gaurav; Isabelle Arnulf; Marie‐Odile Habert; Dijana Petrovska‐Delacrétaz; Jean\-Christophe Corvol; Habib Benali; Marie Vidailhet; Stéphane Lehéricy; Laetitia Jeancolas

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract The pathophysiology underlying Parkinson’s disease \(PD\) speech impairments and its potential sex differences remain unclear\. We investigated sex\-specific alterations in speech and striato\-cortical networks in 104 patients with early PD \(68M/36F\), and 44 healthy controls \(23M/21F\), using quantitative speech markers and multimodal neuroimaging\. Speech features related to prosody, phonation, articulation, fluency, and rhythm, were extracted from voice recordings; striatal dopaminergic loss was quantified using DaT\-SPECT, and functional connectivity between striatal and cortical language networks was analyzed with resting\-state fMRI\. Results showed that PD\-related speech impairments were more pronounced in males\. Functional connectivity was globally reduced in PD, but female patients exhibited higher caudate\-cortical connectivity\. In males, speech deficits were associated with both dopaminergic loss and connectivity disruption, whereas in females, only connectivity changes were involved\. These findings revealed sex\-specific pathophysiological mechanisms in early PD speech disorders, emphasizing the need for sex\-stratified approaches in research and clinical practice\.
