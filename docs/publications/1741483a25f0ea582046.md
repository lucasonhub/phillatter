# Evolutionary roots of hyperactivity: The transition from hedonism to laboriousness as an environmental mismatch hypothesis

**Autores:** Giovanna Azevedo Rodrigues; Marcelo Eduardo Caixeta

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Hyperactivity is usually studied as a symptom of attention\-deficit/hyperactivity disorder rather than as a continuous dimension whose expression depends on the environment\. This article aims to propose the hedono\-laboral transition hypothesis, according to which the impairment associated with the hyperactive dimension arises from a mismatch between a motivational profile oriented toward near\-term return and the delayed\-reward ecology of contemporary adult life\. This is a theoretical essay supported by a narrative review, prioritizing publications from 2021 to 2026\. The literature indicates that, in foraging societies, the passage from play to work is progressive and has blurred boundaries; that hyperactivity is associated with reduced waiting capacity, altered time perception and greater boredom proneness; that reinforcement improves performance more in those with the disorder than in their peers; that the trait favors starting a venture but not sustaining it; and that interventions based on proximal reinforcement, physical exercise and family structure have small to medium effects\. Genomic findings and the fluctuating course of the condition impose limits, which are incorporated into a weak version of the mismatch that does not assume an ancestral advantage of the trait\. Three propositions are presented, each with a testable prediction, along with six rehabilitation principles centered on remaking, in a playful and gradual way, the transition from hedonism to laboriousness, with family engagement and a structured routine\. The hypothesis organizes scattered findings, is open to refutation and complements established treatment without replacing it\.
