# Stress\-testing consentAI: a pipeline for evaluating a conversational educational adjunct to surgical informed consent

**Autores:** Anthony Abou Mrad; Jayanth S\. Pratap; Aviona Conti; William Azar; Paola Vargas; Natalie Moreno; Joseph Cheaib; Mansour Riachi; Lisa Marie Knowlton; David P\. W\. Rastall; Gabriel A\. Brat; Melissa S\. Camp; Lilah F\. Morris; Anne Ehlers; Dana Alexa Telem; Peter Najjar; Andrew MacGregor Cameron; Jeffrey K\. Jopling

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Informed consent is delivered inconsistently, and the comprehension it should produce is often poor\. We built ConsentAI, a conversational educational adjunct that guides patients through a surgeon\-authored consent form and gates progression on demonstrated understanding\. Before any patient\-facing trial, we asked how such an agent can be evaluated at scale, and which methods can be trusted\. Eight clinicians scored 50 simulated laparoscopic cholecystectomy sessions on a 10\-item rubric\. We tested four LLM judge architectures against this clinician reference under a pre\-specified, imbalance\-robust plan\. No single judge reproduced clinicians’ failure calls\. Only jury aggregation improved sensitivity \(0\.421; 95% CI, 0\.26–0\.61\), and no architecture reached a usable triage point\. A constant pass\-everything labeler topped raw agreement and AC1 yet scored zero on Matthews correlation and Cohen’s kappa\. Evaluating clinical conversational agents therefore requires multiple clinician ratings and imbalance\-robust metrics, treating clinician adjudication as a measurement layer, not ground truth\.
