# Context sensitivity in ChatGPT–generated peer review: evaluative alignment across positive and negative user\-provided contexts

**Autores:** Gülşah Subaşı; İrem Şenman

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Large language models \(LLMs\) are increasingly used to support evaluative tasks, raising questions about whether their judgements remain independent when users provide prior evaluative information\. This study examined the context sensitivity of GPT\-5\.5 Instant\-generated academic peer\-review evaluations under positive and negative user\-provided evaluative contexts\. Method Sixteen academics evaluated two published manuscripts, classified for the study as relatively higher and lower quality, across four structured conditions\. Each participant completed all conditions in separate ChatGPT sessions, yielding 64 peer\-review outputs\. Descriptive summaries of overall scores and publishability judgements complemented qualitative content analysis\. Results Three themes emerged: alignment of evaluative emphasis with user\-provided evaluative context, differences in the relative prominence of evaluation criteria, and explicit alignment with the user\-provided evaluation\. Mean ChatGPT scores were 8\.74 and 8\.84 under positive evaluative context, compared with 3\.37 and 3\.64 under negative evaluative context for the lower\- and higher\-quality manuscripts, respectively\. Discussion Because all evaluative inputs included both a verbal evaluation and a numerical rating, the findings reflect sensitivity to the broader evaluative context and do not distinguish the contribution of verbal framing from numerical anchoring\. The results highlight a potential limitation in treating ChatGPT\-generated evaluations under comparable interaction conditions as independent second opinions and underscore the importance of interaction design in human–AI evaluation workflows\.
