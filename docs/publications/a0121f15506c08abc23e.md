# From past to present and toward the future: a mechanism\-informed clinical framework for COVID\-19\-associated headache and post\-viral pain

**Autores:** Cheng He; Yan Xue; Yao Liu; Zhou\-Can Xu; Rui\-Qi Zhao; Jia\-Yi Sun; Meng Jian\-feng; Meng\-Ruo Song; Zhijun Zhang; Qun\-Feng Gui

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Headache is a prominent acute and persistent neurological manifestation of COVID\-19, representing both as a significant clinical challenge and a valuable model for understanding the neurotropic effects of SARS\-CoV\-2\. This review synthesizes evidence showing that the epidemiology of COVID\-19 headache is distinctly shaped by younger age, female sex, and a prior history of headache disorders; its clinical phenotypes are diverse, strongly associating with chemosensory dysfunctions such as anosmia; and its core pathophysiology involves neuroinflammation \(e\.g\., cytokine\-mediated pathways\), trigeminovascular activation, and sex\-specific neuroendocrine\-immune interactions\. Although acute headache often responds to conventional analgesics, persistent cases—a major component of Post\-Acute Sequelae of COVID\-19 \(PASC\)—necessitate a multidisciplinary management approach\. This contrasts with the typically self\-limiting headache following vaccination\. Future research should prioritize longitudinal studies to define chronicity, mechanistic investigations into sex\-specific pathways, and the development of phenotype\-specific, personalized therapeutic strategies\.
