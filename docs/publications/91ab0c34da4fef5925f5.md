# Forest bathing as a pathway toward ecological identity: a community case study

**Autores:** Kelly G\. Guilbeau; Kathryn Dietzway

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Climate and ecological disruption can affect wellbeing, social relationships, and connection to place; when experienced across generations, these widespread disruptions can lead to collective trauma, becoming culturally embedded in identities shared at a community\-level\. This paper proposes a theoretical model demonstrating the reciprocal relationship between collective trauma and ecological dissociation, defined as diminished capacity for relational connection with the more\-than\-human world\. We then present Acadiana Forest Bathing, a free, community\-based initiative in south Louisiana, as an application of forest bathing to operationalize the theoretical underpinnings of this reciprocal cycle\. Inviting self\-guided and group\-based sensory experiences in outdoor settings, the program aims to cultivate ecological identity through embodiment, agency, belonging, and connection at individual, community, and ecological scales\. Initial lessons learned and suggestions for future programming are explored, as well as discussion of feasibility to systematically evaluate the conceptual application of the theoretical model, such that this place\-based programming can integrate lived experiences with community\-level interventions to address the ecological dissociation resulting from collective trauma\.
