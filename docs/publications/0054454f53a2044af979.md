# Size and geometry effects on the flow\-rate–torque trade\-off of a drainage impeller in a submerged mechanical defoamer

**Autores:** Fan Wang; Youqiang Jiang; Youjie Qiu; Wei Liu; Yangyang Huai

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Rotating drainage impellers must balance suction\-driven flow against shaft loading, yet which geometric parameters govern this flow\-rate–torque trade\-off remains poorly established\. This work addresses this question for the drainage impeller of a submerged mechanical defoamer, focusing on the single\-phase hydrodynamics underlying froth capture rather than directly modelling foam rupture\. Thirty configurations spanning four geometric parameters — blade number Z, impeller diameter D, impeller height H and blade outlet angle β — were generated by maximin orthogonal Latin hypercube sampling and evaluated by three\-dimensional steady\-state CFD for drainage flow rate and torque\. Gaussian\-process surrogates with leave\-one\-out cross\-validation then supported conditional\-response analysis, Sobol sensitivity indices and surrogate\-based multi\-objective Bayesian optimization\. The results show that impeller diameter dominates the dimensional responses, with first\-order Sobol indices of 0\.91 for flow rate and 0\.96 for torque\. After dimensional normalization removes the leading size\-scaling effects, diameter still governs the flow coefficient, whereas impeller height emerges as the principal secondary factor for the torque coefficient, indicating that size mainly sets the operating point while the residual geometry modulates the normalized loading\. A representative Pareto compromise design \(Z = 8, D = 81\.11 mm, H = 30 mm, β = 120°\) was verified by CFD within 6% of the surrogate predictions; relative to the original impeller it raises the flow rate by 25\.5%, a gain attributable mainly to the enlarged diameter rather than to improve dimensionless flow capacity\. Water experiments at 1000 rev/min confirmed the CFD predictions for both impellers, with flow\-rate deviations within 5% and torque deviations within 10%\. Flow\-field analysis relates the higher throughput to an enlarged suction\-related low\-pressure region, smoother blade\-passage flow and reduced local wake dissipation\. The framework provides a practical basis for drainage\-impeller design and, more generally, for separating size and geometry effects in CFD\-based optimization under limited simulation budgets\.
