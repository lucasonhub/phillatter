# Workshops as a boundary\-spanning tool: the case of the Network\-Forum on Biodiversity Research Germany

**Autores:** Jonas Geschke

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Science\-policy interfaces in the field of biodiversity have undergone significant progress in the recent years, becoming important spaces for communication and consultation\. The establishment of the Intergovernmental Science\-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services \(IPBES\) is just one such example\. Here, focusing on the Network\-Forum on Biodiversity Research Germany \(NeFo\) as a case study, the role and dynamics of workshops as a boundary\-spanning tool are explored\. Using social network and cluster analysis as well as quasibinomial regression, participation data from 42 NeFo workshops held between 2010 and 2018 are analyzed\. The workshop co\-participation network comprises 471 named institutions, 14 freelancers, and 79 participants without a declared institutional affiliation, from 70 countries\. The results describe how NeFo’s workshops contributed to the development of a German network and forum for biodiversity research\. The participation trends show a network diversification, including a significant increase in the relative participation of business institutions\. This indicates a move towards more integrative and participatory knowledge exchange processes\. Whilst only a few institutions act as brokers between different parts of the network, most institutions took part in only a few workshops\. The findings therefore illustrate how workshops can bring otherwise disconnected institutions into a shared participation network and how boundary organizations can occupy structurally central positions within it\. Based on the analytical findings and practical experiences from NeFo, implications for the design of workshops intended to connect actors across sectors and support biodiversity science\-policy processes are derived\.
