# Assessing Behavioral and Environmental Facets of Obesity Unobtrusively Utilizing Digital Tools: Introducing the BETTER4U Indicators

**Autores:** Chrysa Episkopou; Dimitrios Aletras; Afroditi Kita; Aleksandra Łuszczyńska; Maria Kafyra; Eva Karaglani; Yannis Μanios; Maira Bes‐Rastrollo; Anna E Ek; Ioannis Ioakeimidis; Ioanna Panagiota Kalafati; Ana Isabel Rito; Alexis Kyriacou; Anestis Dougkas; Julie‐Anne Nazare; Louise Seconda; Gillian Santorelli; Vassilios Fanos; Karri Silventoinen; Terho J\. **Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Understanding obesity involves evaluating numerous contributing factors\. Collecting the necessary data for this can impose a significant burden on both research participants during clinical studies and real\-life deployments of digital tools aiding obesity management\. The BETTER4U project \(European Union&\#x27;s Horizon Europe, Grant Agreement ID 101080117\) introduces 36 behavioral and 9 living environment indicators designed to unobtrusively and efficiently capture daily life through passive monitoring using market\-available smartphones and smartwatches while accounting for environmental confounders\. This concise set, intended for dietitians, physical activity experts, and healthcare professionals as a standard for assessing obesity\-related behavior, summarizes the behavioral and environmental profile of a person with obesity/overweight\. We present the selected indicators, their selection and implementation methodology and the high\-level plan for their verification, describing their conceptual foundations and technical realization\. These indicators can be deployed in a modular framework: by transforming raw sensor data into structured, interpretable measures they provide a scalable foundation for long\-term monitoring of obesogenic behaviors, individual assessment, and evaluation of interventions targeting physical activity, diet, stress, and sleep\. The indicators are intended for an expert\-facing dashboard within the BETTER4U Intervention Platform, a participant\-side interface within the BETTER4U application, and as input to the BETTER4U AI\-based causal model, supporting relationship modeling between behavior, environment, and obesity outcomes to inform personalized interventions\. This work lays the basis for data\-driven validation of these indicators following the &quot;BETTER4ALL Pilot Study&quot; \(ClinicalTrials\.gov ID: NCT07120802\), ahead of their use in the BETTER4U RCT among individuals with obesity/overweight\.
