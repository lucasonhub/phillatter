# Early Postnatal Androgenic Activity Inhibition Produces Differential Long‐Term Alterations in Anxiety‐Like Behavior in Young Adult Male and Female Wistar Rats

**Autores:** Ulises Primo; José Manuel Fernández‐García; Rocío García\-Úbeda; Paloma Collado; Beatriz Carrillo; Helena Pinos

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Sex differences in anxiety\-like behavior are influenced by gonadal steroids, yet the contribution of early postnatal androgen signaling and testosterone metabolic pathways to the long\-term organization of these behaviors remains unclear\. Wistar male and female rats received daily s\.c\. injections from postnatal day \(P\) 1 to P5 of vehicle, flutamide, letrozole, or finasteride\. In young adult animals, locomotor activity and anxiety\-like behavior were evaluated via the open field test \(OFT\) and elevated plus maze \(EPM\)\. Females showed higher locomotor activity than males in both tests\. Control females showed a less anxiety\-like profile than control males in the EPM\. Flutamide treatment abolished sex differences in EPM open\-arm anxiety\-related measures and increased the number of freezing episodes and their duration in the OFT\. However, finasteride treatment preserved the sex differences observed in the control groups in anxiety\-like measures\. Finally, aromatase inhibition abolished sex differences in anxiety\-like behavior and induced an anxiolytic\-like effect in males, as indicated by increased open\-arm entries relative to control males\. These findings indicate that androgen\-related pathways during early postnatal life shape adult anxiety\-like behavior in a sex\-dependent and pathway\-dependent manner and highlight aromatization as a key mechanism in the organization of EPM behavioral outcomes in males\.
