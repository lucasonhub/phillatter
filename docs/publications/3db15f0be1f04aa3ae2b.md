# Axelkrans, Erik: Consciousness as Projection: An Ontological Alternative to Emergence

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Contemporary theories of consciousness are largely structured around some form of emer\- gence, according to which subjective experience arises from sufficiently complex physical pro\- cesses\. Despite decades of theoretical refinement, emergentist approaches continue to face per\- sistent conceptual difficulties, including explanatory gaps, category errors between physical de\- scription and phenomenal experience and unresolved ambiguities concerning ontological status\. This paper develops an alternative framework in which consciousness is understood not as an emergent property of matter but as a projection from a deeper generative domain\. The analysis is situated within a generative–projective ontology \(GPO\), established in previous works and summarized here to provide architectural context\. Within this framework, experiential struc\- tures possess real internal organization and coherence, while remaining ontologically distinct from both physical entities and irreducible mental substances\. The paper focuses on the Consciousness Projection Sector \(CPS\) of GPO\. No new physics, neurobiological assumptions or empirical claims are introduced\. A minimal formal element, the Consciousness Ontology Equation \(COE\), is presented solely as a structural indicator of projectional coherence, not as a mechanistic or computational model\. By reframing consciousness in projectional rather than emergent terms, the paper offers a conceptually parsimonious alternative that preserves the reality of experience while avoiding the explanatory liabilities of both reductionism and substance dualism\. On this view, the central problem of consciousness is reconceived not as one of emergence but of ontological coexistence between irreducible domains grounded in a common generative structure\. The proposed framework aims to clarify the ontological status of consciousness and to provide a stable foundation for future theoretical work in consciousness studies\. \(direct link \)
