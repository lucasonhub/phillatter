# Case Report: Severe acute hypermagnesemia following inadvertent bath agent ingestion rapidly resolved by emergent hemodialysis

**Autores:** Chien\-Chou Chen; Jun\-Nan Chen; Tzu\-Hsien Yang; Shih\-Hua Lin

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Severe acute hypermagnesemia from excessive magnesium \(Mg 2\+ \) exposure is rare but life\-threatening and requires prompt recognition\. We report a 54\-year\-old previously healthy man who presented to the emergency department with acute altered consciousness and generalized weakness\. Physical examination showed stupor, hypothermia, hypotension, and generalized areflexia\. Initial laboratory testing demonstrated mixed respiratory and metabolic acidosis, a normal serum creatinine concentration, and severe hypermagnesemia \(serum Mg 2\+ concentration, 15\.8 mg/dL\)\. The initial 12\-lead ECG demonstrated diffuse ST\-segment depression, and an artificial intelligence\-enabled electrocardiography \(AI\-ECG\) model identified an estimated mortality risk above the 99th percentile\. Nasogastric decompression retrieved minimal gastric contents, and initial treatment with intravenous calcium, saline, and a loop diuretic did not produce meaningful neurological improvement\. Emergent hemodialysis resulted in rapid recovery of consciousness and a decrease in serum Mg 2\+ to 4\.9 mg/dL during treatment, together with neurological improvement and a marked decline in AI\-ECG\-predicted mortality risk\. Subsequent history\-taking indicated inadvertent ingestion of approximately 100 mL of a private\-label artificial hot\-spring mineral preparation, for which spa personnel reported a nominal formulation of 30% magnesium chloride, 33% magnesium sulfate, and 10% potassium sulfate\. He recovered without residual complications\. This case emphasizes that a normal serum creatinine concentration at presentation does not preclude life\-threatening magnesium toxicity after a large acute Mg 2\+ exposure\. Timely hemodialysis should be considered when severe symptoms persist despite initial therapy\.
