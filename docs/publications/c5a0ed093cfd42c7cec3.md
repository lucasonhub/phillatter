# Cover plants fixed large amounts of atmospheric N, contributing to the N balance in irrigated fruit crops in the Brazilian semiarid region

**Autores:** Renata Janaína Carvalho de Souza; Vanderlise Giongo; Plínio Barbosa de Camargo; Jéssica Rafaella de Sousa Oliveira; Edilândia Farias DANTAS; Juscélia da Silva Ferreira; Cybelle Souza de Oliveira; Jessyca Adriana Gomes Florêncio da Silva; Paulo Ivan Fernandes‐Júnior; Everardo Valadares de Sá Barretto Sampaio; Ana Dolores Santiago de Freitas

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Cover crops, or cover plants, especially legumes, can counterbalance the depletion in soil organic matter, the low supply of nitrogen from the organic reserves and the losses of the inorganic fertilizers in irrigated fruit crops\. Methods We tested two cover crop mixtures, one with more and the other with less legume proportions, and one treatment where the spontaneous weeds were left to grow, before planting melon or intercropping in a mango orchard\. Biomass of the cover crops and the weeds were cut and incorporated or left on the soil surface\. Results In general, incorporation or surface placement did not significantly differ\. The cover crop aboveground biomass production in the mixtures varied from 5\.82 to 11\.95 Mg ha −1 y −1 while the spontaneous weeds biomass varied from 2\.18 to 6\.56 Mg ha −1 y −1 \. The N contents of the cover crop mixtures varied from 125 to 333 kg ha −1 y −1 , while those of the spontaneous weeds from 25 to 160 kg ha −1 y −1 \. The N fixation varied from 56 to 106 kg ha −1 y −1 in the cover crop mixtures and from 17 to 102 kg ha −1 y −1 in the spontaneous weeds\. Discussion These amounts are of the same order as those exported with the fruits\. Thus, cover crops, especially those with high legume proportions, are promising practices in tropical irrigated fruit production system, deserving further agronomic and economic studies to validate their recommendation to producers\.
