  exhibits comparatively weaker hydrogen adsorption together with strong structural
  stability. The progressive decrease in adsorption strength with increasing hydrogen

  investigations. The findings highlight the potential of metal-functionalized SiCNTs

# First\-principles investigation of hydrogen adsorption and storage mechanisms on Ni\- and Pt\-decorated SiCNTs toward green energy applications

**Autores:** Vusala Nabi Jafarova; Suleyman Bashirov; Ionuţ Cristian Scurtu; Aynur N\. Jafarova; A\.J\. Ahmadova; R\. A\. Hasanova; F\. A\. Bayramova

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Hydrogen storage and efficient hydrogen adsorption issues still pose significant frontiers for next\-generation clean energy applications\. The study provides a comprehensive first\-principles investigation of the hydrogen adsorption on pristine and metal\-decorated silicon carbide nanotubes \(SiCNTs\)\. Methods Structural stability and adsorption behaviour, electronic structure and charge redistribution mechanisms were systematically investigated using the spin\-polarized density functional theory within the GGA\-PBE framework\. Successive hydrogen adsorption was investigated by considering one, two, and three H 2 molecules\. Results Furthermore, ab initio molecular dynamics simulations performed at 300 K confirmed the thermal stability of both Ni–SiCNT\+H 2 and Pt–SiCNT\+H 2 systems, with no noticeable metal desorption or severe structural distortion observed during the simulation period\. The calculated binding energies of −1\.44 eV for Ni and −4\.10 eV for Pt confirm stable metal anchoring on the SiCNT surface, leading to enhanced hydrogen adsorption behavior compared with pristine SiCNT\. For a single H 2 molecule, the adsorption energies follow the order Ni–SiCNT \(−1\.991 eV\) &gt; Pt–SiCNT \(−1\.747 eV\) &gt; pristine SiCNT \(−0\.184 eV\)\. As the hydrogen loading increases, the average adsorption energy gradually decreases to −1\.353 and −0\.882 eV/H 2 for Ni\-decorated SiCNT and to −0\.930 and −0\.614 eV/H 2 for Pt\-decorated SiCNT, indicating progressive saturation of the metal adsorption sites while preserving favorable hydrogen uptake\. The total and partial density of states analyses indicate that metal decoration adds electronic states at the Fermi level, promoting orbital hybridization between metal d\-states and hydrogen 1s\-states\. In addition, Ni 3d orbitals show pronounced hybridization with H 1s states, whereas Pt 5d states exhibit comparatively weaker electronic coupling and a more balanced electronic structure\. Mulliken population, Bader charge, and electron\-density\-difference analyses consistently reveal that Ni induces stronger and more localized charge redistribution than Pt, whereas Pt exhibits a more delocalized and moderate electronic response\. The Bader charge analysis further demonstrates that the average charge redistribution per H 2 molecule decreases with increasing hydrogen loading for both metals, confirming progressive saturation of the adsorption centers\. Discussion The present results demonstrate distinct hydrogen adsorption behavior for Ni\- and Pt\-decorated SiCNTs, where Ni promotes stronger adsorption, while Pt provides a comparatively stable adsorption interface with moderate interaction strength\. Overall, the combined structural, electronic, and charge analyses demonstrate that Ni decoration provides superior hydrogen activation and adsorption capability, whereas Pt decoration exhibits comparatively weaker hydrogen adsorption together with strong structural stability\. The progressive decrease in adsorption strength with increasing hydrogen loading suggests improved adsorption reversibility; however, a definitive assessment of reversible hydrogen\-storage performance requires further thermodynamic and kinetic investigations\. The findings highlight the potential of metal\-functionalized SiCNTs for hydrogen adsorption and activation\-related clean energy applications\.
