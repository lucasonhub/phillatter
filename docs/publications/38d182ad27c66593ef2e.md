# Sex steroids differentially modulate SARS\-CoV\-2\-induced inflammatory responses in endothelial and immune cells

**Autores:** Sandra Bižić‐Radulović; Tijana Subotički; Ana Kovačević; Emilija Živković; Olga Popovic; Olivera Mitrović Ajtić; Miloš Diklić; Snežana Jovanović; Vladan P\. Čokić; Bojana B\. Beleslin‐Čokić

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Sex\-related differences in coronavirus disease 2019 \(COVID\-19\) severity suggest an important role for sex steroids in regulating immune and endothelial responses to severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 \(SARS\-CoV\-2\)\. Methods Receptor expression, inflammatory signaling, and monocyte transendothelial migration were assessed by RT\-PCR, immunoblotting, and Boyden chamber assay, respectively\. HULEC\-5a and primary human immune cells were exposed to in vitro \-amplified SARS\-CoV\-2\. Samples were collected between June 2020 and April 2022, with sample sizes based on primary sample availability and independent experiments\. Results SARS\-CoV\-2 differentially regulates estrogen receptor gene expression, decreasing it in endothelial cells \(p&lt;0\.05\) while increasing it in immune cells \(p&lt;0\.01\)\. SARS\-CoV\-2 generally activated inflammatory signaling pathways, including NFκB, STAT3, and p38 MAPK, in THP\-1 monocytic cells \(up to 1\.9\-fold\)\. The NFκB response was augmented by the androgen receptor agonist dihydrotestosterone \(DHT, 44\.6%\) and attenuated by the androgen receptor antagonist flutamide \(44\.1%\)\. In endothelial cells, DHT twice reduced SARS\-CoV\-2 activation of the p38 MAPK signaling pathway\. β\-estradiol inhibited \(2\.2\-fold\), whereas DHT stimulated \(4\.9\-fold\) NFκB signaling pathway in neutrophils isolated from patients with COVID\-19\. SARS\-CoV\-2 activation of inflammatory signaling pathways deteriorates over time in monocytes from patients with COVID\-19 \(p&lt;0\.05\)\. Furthermore, DHT significantly increased trans\-endothelial migration of monocytes \(p&lt;0\.001\) and detachment of SARS\-CoV\-2 exposed endothelial cells \(p&lt;0\.01\), suggesting a potential role for androgen\-responsive pathways in endothelial dysfunction and immune cell trafficking\. Discussion These findings elucidate the molecular mechanisms driving male\-biased COVID\-19 severity and underscore the potential of targeting androgen\-responsive pathways as a therapeutic strategy to mitigate severe viral inflammation and endothelial dysfunction\.
