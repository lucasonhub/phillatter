# The Impact of Retrieval Augmented Generation on the Reliability of Large Language Models

**Autores:** 

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Large Language Models \(LLMs\) have demonstrated remarkable fluency but remain fundamentally unreliable for knowledge\-intensive tasks due to hallucination, temporal staleness, and lack of verifiability\.Retrieval\-Augmented Generation \(RAG\) has emerged as a dominant architectural paradigm that grounds generation in external evidence\.This paper provides a comprehensive investigation into how RAG reshapes LLM reliability across factual accuracy, citation fidelity, temporal freshness, and robustness to noise\.We benchmark five RAG variants against closed\-book baselines using secondary datasets including Natural Questions, TriviaQA, HotpotQA, and RGB\.Results show an average 23\.7% reduction in hallucination rate and 31\.2%improvement in citation grounding when using advanced retrieval with self\-reflection\.However, we identify critical failure modes: retriever\-generator misalignment, context distraction, and over\-reliance on irrelevant passages\.We propose a reliability framework and demonstrate that corrective and modular RAG designs significantly mitigate these risks\.Our findings establish RAG as necessary but not sufficient for trustworthy deployment\.
