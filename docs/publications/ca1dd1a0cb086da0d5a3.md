# Clinical outcomes and prognostic factors in pediatric acute myeloid leukemia with monocytic differentiation: a single\-center cohort study from Egypt

**Autores:** Tarek Ahmed Abdelrehim Ahmed; Mahmoud Hammad; Sonya Soliman; Nahla Elsharkawy; Sherine Ibrahim Salem; Sonia Ahmed; Alaa Elhaddad

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Acute myeloid leukemia \(AML\) with monocytic differentiation \(FAB M4/M5 subtypes\) is a distinct subtype of pediatric AML characterized by aggressive clinical behavior and common extramedullary involvement\. Despite therapeutic advances, outcomes remain suboptimal, particularly in resource\-limited settings\. The study aimed to evaluate the clinical characteristics, treatment outcomes, and prognostic markers in pediatric patients with AML, FAB M4/M5 subtypes\. Patients and methods A retrospective cohort study analyzing 193 patients with AML, FAB M4/M5 subtypes diagnosed at the Children’s Cancer Hospital, Egypt\-57357 \(CCHE\-57357\) between January 2016 and December 2021\. Results The cohort comprised 131 patients with M4 and 62 with M5 morphology\. Median follow\-up was 62\.0 months among survivors and 37\.6 months for the entire cohort\. Three\- and five\-year OS were 53\.1% and 48\.3%; three\- and five\-year EFS were 48\.2% and 45\.5%\. Five\-year CIR was 23\.8%\. Forty\-eight patients \(24\.9%\) died during induction\. Multivariate Cox regression identified MRD positivity after first induction \(HR 2\.868, 95% CI 1\.133–7\.260, p = 0\.026\) and monosomy 7 \(HR 6\.206, 95% CI 2\.692–14\.306, p &lt; 0\.001\) as independent adverse prognostic factors\. Conversely, inv\(16\)\(p13\.1;q22\) or t\(16;16\)\(p13\.1;q22\); CBFB::MYH11 was an independent favorable prognostic factor \(HR 0\.253, 95% CI 0\.108–0\.592, p = 0\.002\)\. 14 high\-risk pediatric AML patients underwent HSCT post\-induction; three experienced adverse outcomes, comprising relapses \( n = 2\), and acute GVHD \( n = 1\)\. Conclusion Genetic findings and treatment response were associated with outcome, supporting a complete diagnostic assessment\. Improving survival requires precise risk stratification, MRD\-guided therapy, and robust supportive care\.
