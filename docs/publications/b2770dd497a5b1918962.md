# Mediated Givenness and Scientific Object\-Formation: Infrared and Ultraviolet Radiation Beyond Direct Perception

**Autores:** Nuoheng Du

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Scientific instruments make imperceptible phenomena knowable, but it remains unclear whether such access enlarges perception itself or only other forms of epistemic availability\. This article develops a layered relational realist account of mediated givenness to explain how scientific objects become available without being constituted by human access\. It distinguishes three functional layers of mediation—embodied\-phenomenal, translational\-display, and integrated\-substitutive—and four directional modes of availability: existential, structural, predictive, and qualitative\. On this account, translational\-display technologies convert object\-dependent effects into bodily receivable carriers, whereas integrated\-substitutive technologies become part of the perceptual medium that organizes a subject’s experiential field\. The article further reconstructs scientific object\-formation as a sequence from object\-dependent effects and carrier appearance to mediated anchoring, operational stabilization, communal validation, and theoretical integration\. Historical cases involving Herschel’s infrared and Ritter’s ultraviolet experiments illustrate how imperceptible effects became stable public objects of inquiry through measurement, calibration, comparison, and theoretical incorporation\. The analysis shows that science can expand existential, structural, and predictive access without producing new qualitative experience\. It therefore locates the limits of direct perception in embodied or technologically integrated perceptual organization while treating broader cognition as extendable through reliable technical, symbolic, mathematical, and theoretical mediation\.
