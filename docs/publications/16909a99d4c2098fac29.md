# Sustainable Solutions through Self\-Sufficient Energy Houses Powered by Hybrid Renewable Energy Systems

**Autores:** Jorge Contreras; Carlos Armenta\-Déu

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This article analyzes the energy self\-sufficiency in a paired house using a hybrid renewable energy system installed in one house and using the second house for a null energy balance\. The article searches for sustainable systems reducing energy dependence on fossil fuels and greenhouse gas emissions\. The study is based on the application of the fundamental equations of energy, thermal, and electrical balance for a domestic installation\. This system integrates a domestic wind turbine and a photovoltaic array, assisted by a battery bank as an energy storage system to compensate for energy imbalances during a power outage\. The proposed design relies exclusively on electricity to meet all thermal and electrical demands, including heating, domestic hot water, air conditioning, appliances, and lighting\. The design employs an air source heat pump for domestic hot water, heating, and air conditioning; this unit directly connects to the hybrid renewable energy system, and the battery bank assists the hybrid system when needed\. The battery is charged and discharged according to the daily energy balance\. A simulation is performed under various operating conditions to evaluate the proposed design feasibility\. The study includes experimental tests to verify the simulation’s validity and the reliability of the system design\. Experimental tests show good agreement between simulation and actual performance, with an accuracy higher than 99% \( σ ave = 2\.126\), and demonstrate the feasibility of energy self\-sufficiency in housing with hybrid renewable energy sources\. Sustainable energy installations require highly efficient systems to supply thermal energy, such as high\-performance aerothermal heat pumps\.
