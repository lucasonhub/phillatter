# Physiologically\-based pharmacokinetic model to predict pharmacokinetics of KY0467 in humans and guide dose selection in infants and children

**Autores:** Panpan Xie; Yanyan Yang; Xuemei He; Ming Yang; 陈知行; Xi Weng; Aixin Shi

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Hand, foot and mouth disease \(HFMD\) caused by enterovirus 71 \(EV71\) remains a severe threat to infants and young children, with no specific antiviral therapy currently available\. KY0467 is a novel EV71‐targeting antiviral drug under clinical development, but clinical data are limited to healthy adults, and appropriate pediatric dosing regimens are urgently needed\. Methods In this study, a physiologically based pharmacokinetic \(PBPK\) model was developed and validated in rats and beagles, extrapolated to humans, and verified using clinical pharmacokinetic \(PK\) data from healthy adults\. The model was then scaled to infants \(28 days–23 months\) and children \(2–5 years\) using age‐dependent physiological parameters to predict effective doses matching adult exposure at 30 mg/day\. Results Predicted plasma concentration‐time profiles agreed well with observed values, and all mean fold errors \(MFE\) for T max , C max , and AUC 0‐t were within 0\.5‐ to 2‐fold\. Population simulations identified effective doses of 0\.5 mg/kg/day for infants and 9 mg/day for children\. Conclusions In conclusion, the adult PBPK model of KY0467 was successfully developed and validated for the first time, and the extrapolated pediatric model could be used to predict pediatric dosing of KY0467, which will provide invaluable references for the design of future clinical trials and its rational use in the pediatric population\.
