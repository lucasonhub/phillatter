# Phase\-specific neuromechanical signatures of 40\-m sprint performance: an integrated two\-dimensional kinematic and surface electromyographic profiling study

**Autores:** Xin Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Sprint performance reflects coordinated mechanical and neuromuscular behavior across distinct sprint phases\. This study identified phase\-specific neuromechanical signatures of 40\-m sprint performance and tested whether integrated kinematic and electromyographic information added predictive value beyond direct spatiotemporal determinants of running velocity\. Methods In this cross\-sectional study, 160 young adults classified as sprint\-trained athletes or physically active non\-sprinters completed three maximal 40\-m sprints\. Split times were recorded every 10 m\. Four synchronized high\-speed cameras \(240 frames·s \-1\) and wireless surface electromyography of six lower\-limb muscles captured sagittal\-plane kinematics, muscle excitation, coactivation, and excitation–movement coupling across four sprint phases\. Velocity\-adjusted mixed\-effects models and one\-dimensional statistical parametric mapping assessed discrete and waveform differences\. Reliability\-qualified features were analyzed using elastic\-net models with repeated nested five\-fold cross\-validation\. Mean 40\-m velocity was the primary outcome\. Step length and step frequency formed the direct spatiotemporal benchmark; ground\-contact and flight times were added in an extended benchmark\. Sprint\-training\-status classification was secondary and exploratory\. Results Sprint\-trained athletes completed 40 m faster than non\-sprinters \(5\.20 ± 0\.31 vs 5\.95 ± 0\.40 s\)\. Their profile showed longer normalized steps, shorter contact times, phase\-specific trunk and lower\-limb configurations, greater early\-phase gluteus maximus and vastus lateralis excitation, and greater later\-phase biceps femoris preactivation and medial gastrocnemius excitation\. In the complete synchronized sample \( n = 152 \), the integrated non\-spatiotemporal model achieved R 2 = 0\.74 and RMSE = 0\.34 m·s \-1 , versus R 2 = 0\.60 /RMSE = 0\.43 for kinematics and R 2 = 0\.55 /RMSE = 0\.45 for sEMG\. Adding neuromechanical features to the direct benchmark improved R 2 from 0\.72 to 0\.86 and reduced RMSE from 0\.36 to 0\.25 m·s \-1 \(paired reduction 0\.11, 95% CI 0\.07–0\.15\)\. Improvement persisted for the extended benchmark \( R 2 0\.76→0\.87; RMSE 0\.33→0\.24\)\. Transition and high\-speed features accounted for 82% of coefficient magnitude, not variance explained\. Exploratory classification achieved AUC = 0\.94 and balanced accuracy = 0\.89\. Conclusion Forty\-metre sprint performance was characterized by a phase\-specific neuromechanical signature that provided incremental out\-of\-fold predictive information beyond direct and closely related spatiotemporal velocity metrics\. External validation is required before broader generalization\.
