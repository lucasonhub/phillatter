# Changes in general and physics\-related professional competence facets of early childhood educators

**Autores:** Michaela Gelewski; Simone Lehrl; Stefanie Schnebel

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Play\-based science learning opportunities are considered a promising approach to strengthening science education in early childhood\. Professional development \(PD\) is increasingly recognized as important in this context, yet empirical evidence on play\-based physics PD remains limited\. In particular, there is a lack of PD programs addressing play\-based science learning, and empirical evidence remains limited regarding how play\-based physics PD programs support early childhood educators&\#x27; general and domain\-specific professional competencies\. Addressing this gap, the present study examines self\-reported pre\-post changes in selected professional competence facets within the PhysikSPIEL PD program\. Methods Participants \( N = 42\) were early childhood educators from Germany and kindergarten teachers from Switzerland, enrolled in the 2024 or 2025 program cycle, each lasting approximately three months\. Using a pre\-post design and a standardized self\-report questionnaire, the study assessed motivational orientations \[self\-efficacy expectations \(including those related to SST\), self\-concept, enthusiasm\] and pedagogical beliefs \(co\-constructive teaching and learning beliefs\)\. Results Analyses indicated self\-reported pre\-post increases in domain\-specific self\-efficacy expectations \(including those related to SST\), academic self\-concept, and enthusiasm, whereas co\-constructive teaching and learning beliefs remained stable\. Discussion Given the pre\-post design, the relatively small and self\-selected sample, and the reliance on self\-report measures, these findings should be interpreted cautiously\. Overall, the study provides preliminary evidence that participation in a play\-based physics PD program may be associated with changes in selected self\-reported motivational competence facets\.
