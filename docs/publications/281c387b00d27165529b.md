# Mystical experiences mediate the effects of psilocybin\-assisted psychotherapy \+ mindfulness\-based stress reduction on trait mindfulness and depression symptoms: mechanistic results from a randomized controlled trial

**Autores:** Kevin Byrne; Madeleine M\. Odette; Eric L\. Garland; Benjamin R\. Lewis

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

ABSTRACT Background Healthcare workers experienced amplified depression and burnout during COVID\-19\. Both Mindfulness Based Stress Reduction \(MBSR\) and psilocybin have been shown to reduce depressive symptoms in this population, however the psychological mechanisms by which adding psilocybin to mindfulness training improves depression and trait mindfulness remain incompletely characterized\. Drawing on the Mindfulness\-to\-Meaning Theory \(MMT\), we hypothesized that psilocybin\-occasioned mystical experiences would serve as the primary mechanism linking psilocybin\-assisted therapy to improvements in trait mindfulness and depressive symptoms\. Methods Frontline physicians and nurses \(N=25\) with COVID\-related depression were randomized 1:1 to 8\-week MBSR alone or MBSR plus group psilocybin\-assisted therapy \(25mg\)\. Trait mindfulness \(FFMQ\-15\), savoring capacity \(BSI\), and nondual awareness \(NADA\-trait\) were assessed at baseline and 2 weeks\. FFMQ\-15 was also assessed at 6 months post intervention\. Mystical experience intensity \(MEQ\-30\) was assessed post\-dosing session or post MBSR mindfulness retreat\. Mediation analyses tested whether MEQ\-30 mediated treatment effects on mindfulness and depression changes\. Results Twenty\-two participants completed 2\-week assessments\. ANCOVA showed significant between group effects favoring the MBSR \+ psilocybin treatment arm on mindfulness gains as measured by the FFMQ\-15 \(F\(1,19\)= 5\.53, FDR\-corrected p=0\.045\) and the BSI \(F\(1,19\)=9\.08, FDR\-corrected p= 0\.02\) at the 2 week primary endpoint\. In exploratory mediation analyses, intensity of mystical experience showed significant indirect effects on both change in trait mindfulness and depressive symptoms\. Conclusions Adding group psilocybin intervention to an MBSR curriculum augmented mindfulness gains\. Exploratory mediation analyses identified significant indirect effects of mystical experience intensity on both trait mindfulness and depressive symptom change; these findings require confirmation in larger samples\.
