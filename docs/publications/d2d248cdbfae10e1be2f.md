# DBCM\-Oral: Clinical Knowledge\-guided Dual\-scale Cross\-modal Ordinal Learning for Explainable Oral Cancer Histopathology Classification

**Autores:** Vinaya R\. Kudatarkar; Annapurna P Patil; Savita K\. Shetty

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate severity\-aware diagnostic classification of oral lesions from histopathological images is important for diagnosis, treatment planning, and prognosis assessment\.Existing deep learning approaches predominantly focus on conventional multiclass classification and often overlook ordered diagnostic\-severity relationships, integration of clinical knowledge, model interpretability, and predictive uncertainty\.To address these limitations, this study proposes DBCM\-Oral, a clinical knowledge\-guided cross\-modal ordinal learning framework for explainable ordered diagnosticcategory classification\.The proposed framework comprises six major components: Histopathology Patch Normalization and Quality Control \(HPNQ\), a Dual\-Scale Histopathological Encoder \(DSHE\) based on ConvNeXt\-Tiny and GhostNetV3, an Adaptive Cross\-Modal Fusion Framework \(ABMFM\), Semantic Clinical Feature Alignment \(SCFA\) using Contrastive Language\-Image Pretraining \(CLIP\)\-based pathology prompts, an Ordinal Severity Estimation Head \(OSEH\), and an Explainable Uncertainty\-Aware Decision Support module\.In contrast to conventional softmax classifiers, the proposed ordinal learning strategy models an adopted diagnostic\-severity ordering among Normal tissue, Oral Submucous Fibrosis \(OSMF\), Well\-Differentiated Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma \(WDOSCC\), Moderately Differentiated Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma \(MDOSCC\), and Poorly Differentiated Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma \(PDOSCC\), without assuming that these categories represent a universal longitudinal disease trajectory\.Experimental evaluation was conducted using the ORCHID dataset comprising 300,000 histopathological patches\.In the predefined reference run, DBCM\-Oral achieved a patch\-level accuracy of 95\.7%, a macro F1\-score of 95\.7%, and an AUC of 0\.9982 on the patient\-disjoint ORCHID evaluation partition\.Across five independent training runs, the framework achieved a mean accuracy of 95\.7% ± 0\.31% and a mean macro F1\-score of 95\.6% ± 0\.29%\.The proposed method was additionally compared with representative CNN\-, Transformer\-, ordinal\-regression\-, and pathology vision\-language\-based baselines under the same evaluation protocol\.The results demonstrate the effectiveness of combining dual\-scale histopathological representation learning, clinical semantic alignment, ordinal diagnostic\-severity modelling, uncertainty estimation, and explainable decision support for oral lesion classification\.
