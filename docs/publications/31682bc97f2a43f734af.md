# Deep Vein Thrombosis Risk Prediction after Total Hip Replacement at Kandou Hospital

**Autores:** Ira Frayanty Sarewa; Djony Edward Tjandra; Billy Karundeng

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Deep vein thrombosis \(DVT\) is a clinically significant complication following total hip arthroplasty \(THA\), requiring a clear distinction between observed postoperative events and model\-based risk estimates\. This prospective cohort study enrolled 62 patients who underwent THA at Prof\. Dr\. R\. D\. Kandou Hospital in Manado, Indonesia, between January 2023 and December 2024, with a 90\-day follow\-up period\. Clinical characteristics, Wells scores, D\-dimer levels, fibrinogen levels, and Doppler ultrasonography findings were recorded\. Descriptive findings indicated that 46 participants \(74%\) were male, 48 \(77%\) had elevated D\-dimer levels, and all documented Doppler ultrasonography examinations yielded normal findings\. A Monte Carlo simulation comprising 10,000 iterations was also performed using a logistic regression equation with coefficients reportedly derived from published literature rather than estimated from the study cohort\. The simulation yielded a median predicted DVT prevalence of 7\.3%, with a 95% prediction interval of 0%–29%\. However, this estimate represented a simulation\-derived outcome rather than the observed incidence of postoperative DVT\. The available manuscript did not report the actual number of incident DVT events during the 90\-day follow\-up and contained an unresolved inconsistency between the exclusion of patients with active cancer and the reported inclusion of seven participants with active cancer\. \[AUTHOR DATA REQUIRED: Confirm the number of observed DVT events during follow\-up and clarify the eligibility criteria concerning active cancer\.\] Overall, the study provided descriptive information on clinical characteristics and potential DVT risk factors in a local THA population, alongside an exploratory simulation\-based risk estimation framework\. Nevertheless, cohort\-specific predictive associations, model discrimination, and calibration could not be established without verification of the observed clinical outcomes, model coefficients, and estimation procedures\.
