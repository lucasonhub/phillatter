# Response\-function\-conditioned inverse design enables disorder as a design dimension in porous materials

**Autores:** Wei Zhao; Ran Chen; Jiaxiu Zhang; Liangliang Nan; Song Wang; Honghua Chen; Hanyang Yu; Songcan Chen; Mingqiang Wei

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Disordered porous materials offer broad mechanical\-response spaces, but their non\-periodic three\-dimensional architectures make inverse design ill\-posed: structurally distinct configurations can produce similar stress\-strain curves, while scalar descriptors such as porosity cannot resolve the underlying topology\. Here, we introduce a response\-function\-conditioned inverse design framework that learns directly from XCT\-derived closed\-cell aluminium\-foam architectures and targets complete nonlinear stress\-strain responses rather than individual properties\. A point\-cloud encoder and forward surrogate establish the structure\-response relation, while conditional generation and gradient\-based refinement search the learned structural space for manufacturable candidates\. Trained on 844 XCT\-derived subvolumes spanning porosities of 0\.4034–0\.8935, the forward model achieves a pointwise R² of 0\.9918 and a median curve\-level NRMSE of 3\.015% on a parent\-specimen\-separated test set\. For targets admitting multiple accepted solutions, inverse\-designed candidates retain a median response NRMSE of 0\.0358 while exhibiting a median pairwise structural distance of 0\.2328, demonstrating non\-trivial one\-to\-many design\. Finite\-element analyses and compression tests on additively manufactured architectures across three aluminium\-alloy systems further confirm physical realizability\. These results establish full\-response conditioning as a practical route for designing real disordered porous materials beyond predefined unit cells\.
