# Lithostratigraphically\-constrained landslide susceptibility modeling in red\-bed basins using a bayesian probability\-based mixture probabilistic neural network

**Autores:** Zidan Liu; Aihua Yi; Gen Huang; Qi Li; Tongyi Zheng

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Regional landslide susceptibility prediction commonly treats diverse failure mechanisms as a single statistical entity, an oversimplification that fails in lithologically complex red\-bed basins, where retrogressive and translational landslides follow fundamentally distinct hydromechanical laws\. Here we develop a mechanism\-divergent framework that integrates frequency ratio factor screening, Bayesian probability for uncertainty\-aware dataset construction, and a mixture probabilistic neural network to explicitly fuse multi\-modal susceptibility indices\. The approach is validated in Sanshui District, a subtropical red\-bed basin in China, using a 15\-year multi\-source inventory\. Random forest and logistic regression are coupled with frequency ratio and Bayesian strategies to independently predict each landslide type\. Bayesian\-enhanced models consistently outperform their frequency\-ratio counterparts, with Bayesian random forest achieving the highest accuracy\. The mixture probabilistic neural network captures spatial heterogeneity through distance\-weighted neural connections, markedly improving regional mapping and attaining an area under the curve of 90\.2%, substantially higher than the 66\.7% of conventional unified models\. Shapley additive explanation analysis identifies lithology, annual precipitation, and road proximity as dominant controls; lithostratigraphic Groups 1, 7, 12 and 13 exhibit the highest susceptibility\. This framework offers a lithostratigraphy\-informed, mechanism\-aware pathway for landslide risk assessment in complex soft\-rock terrains\.
