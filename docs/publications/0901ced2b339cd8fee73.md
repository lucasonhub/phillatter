# Fabrication of green and powerful magnetic nanocomposite for the removal of oil from seawater: Optimization using Response Surface Methodology

**Autores:** Asmaa M\. Abdel Rahim; Batool Alawi Mohammed Al\-Alwiat

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Traditional techniques for adding modifiers to solid support materials are time\-consuming and require significant energy and materials\. This study focuses on modifying agricultural waste using a quick, simple, and easy solid\-phase extraction approach\. This work reports the design, fabrication, and laboratory evaluation of Alfalfa straw biomass \(Alf\. S\) decorated with magnetic iron\-oxide nanoparticles \(Fe3O4 MNPs\) for oil\-water separation\. This novel magnetic nanocomposite adsorbent was synthesized via in situ coprecipitation\. FTIR, XRD, and SEM characterized the newly developed nanocomposite \(Fe3O4 MNPs@Alf\. S biomass\)\. In this study, the Box\-Behnken experimental design \(BBD\) was used to determine the optimal values of three independent variables affecting oil removal efficiency from seawater: the amount of Fe3O4 MNPs@Alf\. S biomass, the amount of oil in grams, and adsorption time \(min\)\. The maximum adsorption capacity was 1300 mg/g with 300\.0 mg of adsorbent, 10\.0 mL of used motor oil, and an adsorption time of 1 min\. The data also confirm successful fabrication and oil adsorption, as the high R2 value and close proximity to R2\(adj\) indicate that the BBD model was significant\.
