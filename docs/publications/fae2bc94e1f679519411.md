# Relative bench\-press strength and jump power are associated with distinct tracking\-based in\-game shares in professional ice hockey

**Autores:** Peter Spornberger; Hendrik Kolbert; Thimo Wiewelhove

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Ice hockey is an intermittent, high\-intensity team sport, yet the transfer from preseason off\-ice testing to tracking\-derived in\-game impact metrics remains underexplored in professional settings\. Objective To examine associations between preseason off\-ice fitness and tracking\-derived 5\-on\-5 in\-game impact in a Deutsche Eishockey Liga \(DEL\) team across three seasons\. Methods Retrospective analysis of 28 male professional skaters \( N = 50 player\-seasons after listwise deletion, up to three observations per player\)\. Predictors were 30 m sprint, countermovement jump \(CMJ\), relative single\-leg strength \(Bulgarian split squat 1RM per body mass, SLS/BM\), relative upper\-body strength \(bench press 1RM per body mass, BP/BM\), estimated maximal oxygen uptake \(VO₂max\), body fat percentage, and age\. Outcomes were two team\-relative share metrics derived from the league&\#x27;s official player\-tracking system: the relative expected\-goals\-for percentage \(xGF%Rel\), defined as a player&\#x27;s on\-ice share of expected goals minus the team&\#x27;s share with the player off the ice, capturing chance quality; and the relative Fenwick\-for percentage \(FF%Rel\), the corresponding on\-minus\-off share of unblocked shot attempts, capturing territorial play\. Ordinary least squares models included season fixed effects and player\-clustered robust standard errors; multiplicity across predictors was controlled using the Holm procedure\. Sensitivity models additionally adjusted for playing position\. Results Model fit was modest \(adjusted R 2 : 0\.16 for xGF%Rel; 0\.21 for FF%Rel\)\. After Holm correction, BP/BM was positively associated with xGF%Rel \( β = \.021, p\_Holm = \.011\)\. For FF%Rel, CMJ was positively associated \( β = \.018, p\_Holm = \.012\) and estimated VO₂max negatively associated \( β = −\.019, p\_Holm = \.035\)\. Sensitivity analyses preserved the direction and approximate magnitude of all three associations\. Statistical support was uniform only for CMJ–FF%Rel, which remained significant throughout; the BP/BM association did not survive allometric scaling and the estimated\-VO₂max association fell just above the adjusted threshold after author exclusion\. Conclusions In this single\-team DEL cohort, preseason off\-ice fitness showed metric\-specific rather than global associations with 5\-on\-5 relative share outcomes\. Relative upper\-body strength aligned with chance\-quality share, while lower\-limb explosiveness aligned with territorial share; the inverse VO₂max association likely reflects role and deployment context rather than detrimental effects of aerobic capacity per se \. Findings are associative, not causal, and warrant validation in larger multi\-team datasets with explicit usage/context adjustment\.
