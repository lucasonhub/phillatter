# Anchored Targeted Projection Pursuit for Hypothesis\-Driven Quality Control of Drill\-Cuttings Geochemical Data

**Autores:** Yupeng Li; S\. Shahrani; Zhaoxian Yuan

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Wellsite X\-ray fluorescence \(XRF\) analysis of drill cuttings provides multi\-element profiles for chemostratigraphy, well correlation and reservoir characterization, but the data are exposed to artefacts of the drilling workflow, such as drilling\-fluid residue, carry\-over between samples and detector drift\. Quality control \(QC\) usually relies on principal component analysis \(PCA\), which ranks samples by variance rather than geological meaning, or on supervised classifiers, which need labels that do not exist at the QC stage\. In practice, analysts often suspect a problem and can point to a few samples that show it\. We introduce anchored targeted projection pursuit \(anchored TPP\), which builds its projection target from these anchors alone and leaves all other samples unconstrained\. The fit reduces to a weighted orthogonal Procrustes problem with a closed\-form solution and no tuning parameters\. It remains well posed when variables outnumber samples, and its loadings name the elemental contrast behind each flag\. We test it on a synthetic XRF benchmark and on a semi\-synthetic experiment that plants a potassium chloride \(KCl\) brine mud signature into real cuttings data from an unconventional hydrocarbon well\. Anchored TPP detects contamination that PCA and single\-element thresholds miss, and log\-ratio pre\-treatment more than doubles its recovery\. Positive\-\-unlabelled learning recovers marginally more \(0\.55 of 12 hidden samples; 95% confidence interval 0\.07\-1\.03\), but anchored TPP is interpretable, tuning\-free and fast enough for interactive use\. Because all contamination labels are planted, field validation against laboratory\-confirmed intervals remains necessary\.
