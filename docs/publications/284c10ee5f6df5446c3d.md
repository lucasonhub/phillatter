# Case Report: Endometrial carcinoma harboring a rare POLE p\.T278K variant and dual MSH6 frameshift variants

**Autores:** Yuxuan Yang; Huimei Luo; Huixing Yan; Haoping Yu; Xinyue Ma; Qiuli Teng; Longxin Zhang; Jinwen Ye; Zeng Yuan; Qing Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background POLE \-mutated endometrial carcinomas are associated with an excellent prognosis and have become an integral component of the molecular classification of endometrial carcinoma\. However, the pathogenicity of many rare non\-hotspot POLE exonuclease\-domain variants remains uncertain, particularly when they occur in tumors harboring additional molecular alterations that may confound molecular classification\. Methods Histopathologic findings and immunohistochemical expression of mismatch repair proteins and p53 were evaluated\. Targeted next\-generation sequencing of tumor tissue was performed to characterize genomic alterations, microsatellite instability, tumor mutational burden, and the mutational spectrum\. The non\-hotspot POLE p\.T278K variant was evaluated using the León\-Castillo scoring framework\. MLH1 promoter methylation was assessed by methylation\-specific PCR to further characterize the subclonal MLH1/PMS2 loss\. Case summary A 59\-year\-old woman with endometrioid endometrial carcinoma underwent total hysterectomy with surgical staging\. Targeted next\-generation sequencing identified a rare non\-hotspot POLE exonuclease\-domain variant, c\.833C&gt;A \(p\.T278K\), together with two MSH6 frameshift variants, c\.3261del \(p\.F1088Sfs\*2\) and c\.3261dup \(p\.F1088Lfs\*5\)\. The tumor demonstrated an ultramutated genomic profile with a tumor mutational burden of 370\.0 mutations/Mb and an MSI\-H phenotype\. Immunohistochemistry demonstrated subclonal loss of MLH1, PMS2, and MSH6 with retained MSH2 expression; MLH1 promoter methylation was also detected\. Conclusion This case adds clinicopathological and molecular evidence supporting the biological significance of the rare POLE p\.T278K variant and illustrates a practical approach for interpreting uncommon POLE exonuclease\-domain variants in endometrial carcinomas with multiple molecular classifiers\. Accurate identification of the dominant molecular classifier is critical for appropriate molecular classification, prognostic assessment, and clinical management\.
