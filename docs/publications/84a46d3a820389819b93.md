# Collected: Trauma, Wonder, and the Birth of the Modern Museum Movement

**Autores:** Cheryl Harned

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

This dissertation investigates the life and collecting practices of Joseph Allen Skinner \(1862–1946\), a Holyoke, Massachusetts silk manufacturer, philanthropist, and childhood survivor of the Mill River Flood disaster of 1874, and the museum he created in South Hadley, Massachusetts, which he bequeathed to Mount Holyoke College upon his death\. Drawing on material culture studies, the history of emotions, and scholarship in trauma and neuroscience, it argues that Skinner&\#x27;s lifelong collecting habits and eventual museum\-making were shaped by two intertwined emotional responses to catastrophic loss: trauma and wonder\. Chapter II, &quot;Trauma Collected,&quot; examines Skinner&\#x27;s accumulation of Mill River flood relics—illustrated newspapers, silk remnants, a six\-ton chunk of a broken dam—as material responses to disaster that persisted across seven decades\. Chapter III, &quot;Wonder Collected,&quot; traces his childhood rock collecting, fascination with optical devices, and engagement with World&\#x27;s Fair objects as expressions of wonder—trauma&\#x27;s twin—arguing that collecting wondrous objects became a primary strategy for processing loss\. Chapter IV, &quot;Birth of the Modern Museum Movement,&quot; chronicles Skinner&\#x27;s journey from nervous prostration in 1900 to his museum&\#x27;s opening in 1932, demonstrating how he rebuilt himself by rebuilding everything around him: churches, a mountain, a college, and finally a historic village assembled from the ruins of towns flooded to create the Quabbin Reservoir\. Ultimately this dissertation argues that the Joseph Allen Skinner Museum occupies a liminal space between the nineteenth\-century cabinet of curiosity and the modern professional institution, representing a moment when museums could still serve simultaneously as personal memory houses and public educational spaces, and that understanding this history enriches our broader understanding of how trauma and wonder shaped American collecting and museum\-making at the birth of the modern museum movement\.
