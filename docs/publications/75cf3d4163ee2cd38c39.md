# Urban energy peaking in China: timing, persistence, rebound risk, and regional heterogeneity

**Autores:** Yinghan Liu; Nga Wun Lau; Johnny F\. I\. Lam; Guanqiuyue Chen

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Determining whether urban energy demand has entered a persistent post\-peak phase is important for assessing China’s energy transition\. A historical maximum alone, however, does not establish a durable turning point because energy consumption may subsequently rebound or remain close to its previous peak\. Methods Using a balanced panel of 336 Chinese prefecture\-level cities from 2000 to 2022, this study develops a diagnostic framework that distinguishes historical maxima from effective peaks and evaluates post\-peak persistence, rebound risk, transition status, and trend changes\. The baseline definition classifies an effective peak as an earlier historical maximum followed by an average energy\-consumption level over the final three years that is at least 5% below that maximum\. The classification is further assessed using alternative thresholds, persistence windows, moving\-average specifications, a pre\-pandemic endpoint, and trend\-break analysis\. Results Of the 336 cities, 202 recorded their historical maximum in 2022, while 94 satisfy the baseline effective\-peak criterion and 88 are classified as stable post\-peak\. Eastern cities show a substantially higher effective\-peak share than central, western, and northeastern cities\. The pre\-pandemic analysis indicates that peak classification is sensitive to the sample endpoint, while trend\-break results show that slower energy\-demand growth is considerably more common than an absolute reversal in energy consumption\. A small group of cities also exhibits rebound risk after an earlier decline\. Discussion These findings show that a historical maximum is insufficient to establish a persistent post\-peak transition and that Chinese cities remain at markedly different stages of energy\-demand adjustment\. The proposed framework is diagnostic rather than causal and provides a consistent basis for distinguishing stable post\-peak transitions from temporary declines and rebound\-prone trajectories\. The results highlight the importance of accounting for persistence and regional heterogeneity when assessing urban energy\-transition progress\.
