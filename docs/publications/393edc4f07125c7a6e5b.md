# Evaluating the Performance of Electrocardiography and Immunological Markers \(IL\-6, TNF\-α, and C\-Reactive Protein\) in the Diagnosis of Left Ventricular Hypertrophy Using Echocardiography as the Gold Standard

**Autores:** Hameed A\. Alhibaly; Basama Edan Kadum; Ban Waheed Hussein Bdair; Satar Jabbar Rahi Algraittee; Abdul Amir H\. Kadhum

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Aim: To evaluate the diagnostic performance of ECG criteria and inflammatory markers—interleukin\-6 \(IL\-6\), tumor necrosis factor\-alpha \(TNF\-α\), and C\-reactive protein \(CRP\)—in detecting LVH\. Materials and Methods: A total of 241 patients with concentric LVH confirmed by ECHO were included and categorised into ECG\-positive and ECG\-negative groups\. Additionally, 50 age\-matched controls without LVH were recruited\. The diagnostic performance of Sokolow–Lyon, Cornell voltage, and Peguero–Lo Presti ECG criteria was assessed using sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy\. Serum levels of IL\-6, TNF\-α, and CRP were analysed across different levels of left ventricular mass index \(LVMI\)\. Results: Significant differences were observed between ECG groups in interventricular septal thickness, left ventricular diameter, and LVMI \(p &lt; 0\.05\)\. ECG criteria demonstrated low sensitivity \(10\.37%–20\.75%\) but high specificity \(up to 100%\), with limited overall accuracy\. In contrast, IL\-6, TNF\-α, and CRP levels were significantly elevated in patients with severe LVH \(p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Conclusions: ECG criteria show high specificity but limited sensitivity in diagnosing LVH\. Elevated inflammatory markers correlate strongly with disease severity, suggesting their potential role in improving diagnostic accuracy\.
