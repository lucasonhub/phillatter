# Kohl, Tobias Martin: Do I Exist? What Philosophy, Neuroscience, and Physics Know About Your Self in the 21st Century \(EXIST Series\)

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This work develops a naturalistic and anti\-Platonist account of the ‘I’ as the process of experiencing, and answers the title question with a qualified yes: the ‘I’ exists, here and now, as a process bound to one body on one worldline—but it is not a thing, has no location in the brain, and does not survive the substrate on which it runs\. The account is built on dual\-aspect monism reaching back to Spinoza: experiencing is the inner side of neuronal convergence, not a second item alongside it, just as the hollow of a vase is not a second thing beside the vase\. Three concepts carry the argument\. Substrate asymmetry holds that a gap in experiencing decides nothing by itself—it looks the same in sleep as in death—and that what matters is solely whether the physical substrate outlasts the gap\. Token binding holds that the ‘I’ is bound to this one substrate on this one trajectory in spacetime, not to a pattern that could be instantiated elsewhere\. The experienced now is shown to be a window of a few seconds, assembled after the fact and retrospectively dated—not a defect, but the condition under which streams of unequal latency can become a single moment at all\. The τ\-criterion, inherited from The Unowed Universe, holds that whatever lacks rest mass lacks proper time and therefore cannot live through anything\. From these follows a two\-way disjunction for any claim of survival after death: transfer fails on dual\-aspect monism, replication fails on substrate asymmetry\. The position is tested against clinical findings \(deep sleep and the perturbational complexity index, the cerebellum, split\-brain, hydranencephaly, dissociative identity disorder\), against the anatomy of neuronal synchrony and the efference copy, and against the physics of proper time and the thermodynamic arrow\. The work evaluates twenty contemporary and classical positions, each presented on a standardized index card—including Dennett, Metzinger, Parfit, Chalmers, Damásio, Solms, Seth, Frankish, Baars, Dehaene, Tononi, Friston, Hume, and the Buddhist concept of non\-self—and positions itself as Type\-B materialism in Chalmers’s taxonomy, against Platonist conceptions of the self as a Form, against substance dualism, against pattern and bundle theories, and against eliminative illusionism\. Consequences are drawn for free will \(the question dissolves rather than being answered\), for the notion of “false” experiencing \(inapplicable to an inner side\), for theodicy \(moot, since experiencing without suffering would not achieve the same biological performance\), and for the Last Judgment\. What is lost on this account is not the ‘I’ but the homunculus—the captain on the bridge; the ‘I’ turns out to be not less than assumed but something else: the inner side of its substrate, inseparable from it and unique\. Methodologically, the work was co\-written with Claude, an artificial intelligence developed by Anthropic; the collaboration is openly declared and the AI figures as a test case for the criteria the book develops\. This is the first volume of the EXIST series, in which each volume answers one existence question with a definite verdict\. \(direct link \)
