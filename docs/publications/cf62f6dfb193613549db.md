# Data sharing for adaptive user interfaces: The influence of health constraints

**Autores:** Henrik Laumert; Miriam Grates; Martina Brandt

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

As household appliances become more digital and assistive, adaptive functions may provide new opportunities to support individuals with health\-related limitations in activities of daily living \(ADL\)\. Such systems, however, depend on users’ willingness to provide behavioral data that enable personalized assistance\. While previous research has primarily focused on technology acceptance and use, health\-related differences in willingness to share usage data for adaptive assistive systems have received little attention\. Using data from a population\-representative survey in Germany \(n = 949\), this study investigates whether and to what extent ADL limitations influence willingness to share usage data with assistive systems that enable adaptive user interfaces for household appliances such as ovens and washing machines\. Logistic regression analyses show that individuals with such limitations are more likely to be willing to share usage data\. This effect persists even after accounting for a broad set of potential confounders like differences in technology experience, personality traits, and sociodemographic characteristics\. The findings extend current research beyond technology acceptance and use by demonstrating a previously underexplored association between health\-related limitations and willingness to participate in the data\-driven processes underlying personalized assistance\. This greater willingness to share data suggests potential for adaptive household technologies to provide tailored assistance for this target group, while highlighting the need for further research into the mechanisms shaping data\-sharing willingness and how voluntary and informed participation can best be supported across cultural contexts\.
