# Maternal serum alpha\-fetoprotein, fetal fraction, and prognostic nutritional index in relation to adverse pregnancy outcomes

**Autores:** Pınar Tuğçe Özer; Meriç Balıkoğlu; Gülin Okay; Mehmet Özer

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objectives: To evaluate the association of maternal serum alpha\-fetoprotein \(AFP\) with adverse pregnancy outcomes and to investigate its relationship with fetal fraction \(FF\) and the Prognostic Nutritional Index \(PNI\) as potential indicators of placental function and maternal inflammatory\-nutritional status\. Methodology: This retrospective study included 192 singleton pregnancies followed at a tertiary center between 2020 and 2024\. Maternal serum AFP, NIPT\-derived fetal fraction, and PNI were evaluated in relation to preeclampsia, proteinuria, and gestational diabetes mellitus\. Continuous variables were assessed for normality with the Shapiro\-Wilk test and compared using the independent\-samples t\-test or Mann\-Whitney U test, as appropriate\. Categorical variables were compared using the chi\-square or Fisher’s exact test\. A two\-sided p&lt;0\.05 was considered statistically significant\. Results: Preeclampsia occurred in 5\.7%, proteinuria in 13\.0%, and gestational diabetes mellitus in 29\.2% of pregnancies\. Fetal fraction was higher in pregnancies with preeclampsia \(11\.70 \[8\.65\-14\.20\] vs\. 7\.60 \[5\.60\-10\.10\]; p=0\.007; rank\-biserial r=0\.48\)\. Proteinuria was associated with lower AFP levels \(28\.30 \[25\.30\-39\.00\] vs\. 35\.10 \[30\.20\-44\.50\]; p=0\.029; r=\-0\.27\) and higher hemoglobin levels \(11\.80 \[11\.20\-12\.40\] vs\. 11\.40 \[10\.75\-12\.00\] g/dL; p=0\.042; r=0\.25\)\. AFP, fetal fraction, and corrected PNI did not differ significantly according to gestational diabetes status\. Conclusions: Maternal serum AFP was not significantly associated with preeclampsia or gestational diabetes mellitus\. However, lower AFP levels were associated with proteinuria, and higher fetal fraction was associated with preeclampsia\. These findings should be interpreted as exploratory associations and require confirmation in larger, appropriately adjusted studies\.
