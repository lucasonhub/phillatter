# Uncertainty\-aware explainable multi\-horizon building load forecasting with reservoir computing

**Autores:** Adriá Serra; Alberto Ortiz; Vincent Canals

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The European Union \(EU\) is at a pivotal juncture in its energy policy, driven by global energy transitions and the pressing need to combat climate change\. Buildings, which account for approximately 40% of total energy consumption and 36% of carbon dioxide emissions in the EU, represent a critical area for optimization\. This study proposes an advanced explainable multi\-horizon building demand forecasting framework based on Reservoir Computing \(RC\), enhanced with Monte Carlo dropout\(MCd\) to quantify predictive uncertainty and an out\-of\-distribution \(OOD\) detection mechanism for robust interval forecast validation\. The framework leverages the computational efficiency of RC, combined with probabilistic forecasting techniques, to deliver accurate multi\-step energy load predictions across diverse operational conditions\. Experimental results demonstrate improved forecast measured by multiple metrics, stable prediction intervals that increase under OOD scenarios, and enhanced reliability compared to baseline models\. The integration of Explainable Artificial Intelligence \(XAI\) techniques, Local Interpretable Model\-agnostic Explanations \(LIME\), provides detailed insights into the temporal and contextual drivers of energy demand\. Key benefits include improved decision\-making for energy managers, enhanced occupant comfort, optimized HVAC \(Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning\) operations, and seamless integration of renewable energy sources\. This framework aligns with the EU’s European Green Deal target of carbon neutrality by 2050, offering a scalable solution for smart building energy management\. The methodology also supports regulatory compliance, reduces operational costs, and mitigates risks associated with unpredictable energy demand fluctuations\.
