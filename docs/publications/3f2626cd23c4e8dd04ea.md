# High\-grade inflammation, metabolic abnormalities, and incident type 2 diabetes: a dynamic matched prospective cohort study

**Autores:** Yulong Lan; Dan Wu; Xiong Ding; Lingyu Xu; Hui Zhou; Mengyi Zheng; Zegui Huang; Peng Fu; Zekai Chen; Wei Wang; Shouling Wu; Youren Chen

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Aims To investigate the association between high\-grade inflammation and incident type 2 diabetes \(T2D\) and assess how this risk varied according to metabolic abnormalities and risk factor control\. Methods Within the Kailuan cohort, 9,347 individuals with high\-grade inflammation \(hsCRP ≥10 mg/L\) between 2006/2007 and 2016/2017 were dynamically matched by age and sex at a prespecified 1:4 ratio to 31,584 non\-inflamed participants\. Multivariable Cox regression models adjusted for demographic, lifestyle, renal, lipid, blood pressure, and adiposity\-related factors were used to estimate hazard ratios \(HRs\) for incident T2D overall, across metabolic and risk factor strata\. Results Participants with high\-grade inflammation had greater adiposity and more adverse metabolic profiles than matched non\-inflamed participants\. During a median 10\.1 years of follow\-up, T2D developed in 17\.7% of participants with high\-grade inflammation and 13\.1% of matched non\-inflamed participants\. High\-grade inflammation was associated with a 24% higher adjusted risk of T2D \(HR 1\.24, 95% CI 1\.16–1\.33\)\. Diabetes risks were further elevated when high\-grade inflammation coexisted with metabolic abnormalities, ranging from 2\.25 for overweight/obesity to 4\.24 for impaired fasting glucose\. Management of BMI, waist\-to\-height ratio, or blood pressure largely attenuated the excess risk, whereas favorable smoking or renal function control alone was insufficient\. Conclusions High\-grade inflammation was associated with a higher risk of T2D, particularly in the presence of metabolic abnormalities\. Maintaining a healthy weight and optimal blood pressure may help attenuate this risk, highlighting the potential importance of integrating high\-grade inflammation and metabolic status in diabetes risk assessment and prevention\.
