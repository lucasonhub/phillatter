\_Artificial Societies\_ 21 \(3\)\. 2026Cyborgization has moved from the realm of science fiction and laboratory experiments into a developing market with medical, industrial, and consumer segments\. The article proposes an analytical matrix built around five basic models of cyborgization: restorative, industrial, cognitive\-communicative, everyday, and experimental\. Each model is described through invasiveness, functional purpose, infrastructure dependence, level of commercialization, accessibility, and regulatory maturity\. On this basis, the authors compare the approaches of major technological leaders, including the United States, the European Union, Japan, China, Israel, and South Korea\. The analysis shows that cyborgization can reduce industrial injuries, improve labor productivity, and lower long\-term healthcare costs, but it also creates a new form of structural inequality, the enhancement divide, and raises serious concerns about the control of neuro\- and biometric data\.
