  freedom. In lives: the communist party-states caused deaths counted in tens of millions

  §7.1). The famine of the Great Leap Forward killed between about 15 and 45 million
  people, and its harvest records were written not only by officials who feared to
  report less than the plan but by officials who competed to report more. In the Great
  Terror of 1937–38 some 680,000 to 750,000 people were shot, and regional leaders
  petitioned Moscow for larger quotas of executions than they had been assigned. In
  virtues: a regime that could not tell loyal praise from interested praise demanded
  proofs of loyalty that only harm to others could supply, and so taught whole societies
  to prove their fidelity by betraying their neighbours, their colleagues and their
  parents — and left behind a trust between persons that was still measurably lower
  a generation after the regimes fell. In freedom: every act of frank speech acquired
  a price, every denunciation a reward, and the people who could have checked the
  power were converted, one proof at a time, into its accomplices. The article establishes
  six results. The worm is a negative verifier (D1): unlike a silent captured channel,
  which adds nothing to the detection of a forgery, worm display subtracts from it
  and adds to the reliance placed on the forged record. Worm inflation (T1): the inflation
  of a harvest report has a compelled part, the target an official must report, and
  a worm part, the excess he reports to be seen; the worm part alone can cross the
  threshold of famine, and when next year’s target is set from this year’s report,
  the worm’s excess becomes the next official’s floor. The floor ratchet (T2): wherever
  display is rewarded by rank, the floor of safety rises by the worms’ own excess,
  so that worm status is unstable and yesterday’s zealot becomes today’s laggard.

  power that must still verify loyalty is driven to demand displays that destroy the
  displayer’s outside options, and the cheapest such displays are harms to others
  — denunciation, then complicity. Conversion by complicity (T4): the acts the spiral
  demands are those that turn opportunists into believers. The chain (C2): discretion
  over forged gifts is the wage the power pays its middle layer for display, so worming
  upward and gift-forging downward are one transaction. The article states where the
  mechanism does not run — where a verifier survives, where praise keeps its information,
  where loyalty is fixed by birth, where display is demanded only as submission, and
  where allocation is by rule — and shows that at the peak of terror, when intensity
  no longer separates the worm from the frightened, initiative still does: the regional
  leaders who asked Moscow for larger quotas of executions in 1937 were raising the
  floor, not standing on it. The mechanism is not peculiar to communism; National
  Socialist and military regimes ran it too, and the article says so. Its thesis is
  narrower and harder to answer: the Leninist party-state was the only regime type
  that removed every boundary of the mechanism at once — it made itself the sole dispenser
  of livelihood, defined orthodoxy by a line that moved so that anyone could fall
  from it, taught denunciation to children as a virtue, and abolished every verifier
  that could have stopped the spiral. Its catastrophes were therefore not the work
  of a few bad leaders, nor the excesses of local zealots, nor the product of fear
  alone. They were the output of a structure its founders chose to build. The article
  is also an instrument against that structure. Its results give a diagnosis that
  can be applied from documents — the initiative criterion identifies the agents who
  raised the floor of repression rather than standing on it. Its five boundaries are
  five defences, each of which stops the spiral: an independent verifier, allocation
  by public rule, plural sources of livelihood, protection for speech that goes up

  the four defences on which apologists for communist rule still rely: that its founders

# Mattos, José Caetano de: THE DENOUNCING WORM: How the Communist Party\-State Turned Flattery into Complicity — the Floor Ratchet, the Spiral of Proof and the Worm Inflation of the Harvest Record

**Autores:**

## Descrição do feed

Every tyranny has had its flatterers\. The communist party\-state did something no court before it had done on such a scale: it turned flattery into denunciation, denunciation into complicity, and complicity into conviction, and it did so across a third of humanity for most of a century\. This article shows how, by joining three results of the Philosophy of Virtues that have so far stood apart: the abolition of the verifier \(The Communist Paradise, Forged, \#130\), the forged gift \(Bread for Silence, \#131\) and the virtue worm, the person who defends a seized power by praising its trivia \(The Virtue Worm, \#132\)\. The cost was paid in lives, in virtues and in freedom\. In lives: the communist party\-states caused deaths counted in tens of millions — about 27 to 64 million in the documented episodes of six countries alone \(\#130, §7\.1\)\. The famine of the Great Leap Forward killed between about 15 and 45 million people, and its harvest records were written not only by officials who feared to report less than the plan but by officials who competed to report more\. In the Great Terror of 1937–38 some 680,000 to 750,000 people were shot, and regional leaders petitioned Moscow for larger quotas of executions than they had been assigned\. In virtues: a regime that could not tell loyal praise from interested praise demanded proofs of loyalty that only harm to others could supply, and so taught whole societies to prove their fidelity by betraying their neighbours, their colleagues and their parents — and left behind a trust between persons that was still measurably lower a generation after the regimes fell\. In freedom: every act of frank speech acquired a price, every denunciation a reward, and the people who could have checked the power were converted, one proof at a time, into its accomplices\. The article establishes six results\. The worm is a negative verifier \(D1\): unlike a silent captured channel, which adds nothing to the detection of a forgery, worm display subtracts from it and adds to the reliance placed on the forged record\. Worm inflation \(T1\): the inflation of a harvest report has a compelled part, the target an official must report, and a worm part, the excess he reports to be seen; the worm part alone can cross the threshold of famine, and when next year’s target is set from this year’s report, the worm’s excess becomes the next official’s floor\. The floor ratchet \(T2\): wherever display is rewarded by rank, the floor of safety rises by the worms’ own excess, so that worm status is unstable and yesterday’s zealot becomes today’s laggard\. The spiral of proof \(T3\): praise is cheap, so it soon carries no information; a power that must still verify loyalty is driven to demand displays that destroy the displayer’s outside options, and the cheapest such displays are harms to others — denunciation, then complicity\. Conversion by complicity \(T4\): the acts the spiral demands are those that turn opportunists into believers\. The chain \(C2\): discretion over forged gifts is the wage the power pays its middle layer for display, so worming upward and gift\-forging downward are one transaction\. The article states where the mechanism does not run — where a verifier survives, where praise keeps its information, where loyalty is fixed by birth, where display is demanded only as submission, and where allocation is by rule — and shows that at the peak of terror, when intensity no longer separates the worm from the frightened, initiative still does: the regional leaders who asked Moscow for larger quotas of executions in 1937 were raising the floor, not standing on it\. The mechanism is not peculiar to communism; National Socialist and military regimes ran it too, and the article says so\. Its thesis is narrower and harder to answer: the Leninist party\-state was the only regime type that removed every boundary of the mechanism at once — it made itself the sole dispenser of livelihood, defined orthodoxy by a line that moved so that anyone could fall from it, taught denunciation to children as a virtue, and abolished every verifier that could have stopped the spiral\. Its catastrophes were therefore not the work of a few bad leaders, nor the excesses of local zealots, nor the product of fear alone\. They were the output of a structure its founders chose to build\. The article is also an instrument against that structure\. Its results give a diagnosis that can be applied from documents — the initiative criterion identifies the agents who raised the floor of repression rather than standing on it\. Its five boundaries are five defences, each of which stops the spiral: an independent verifier, allocation by public rule, plural sources of livelihood, protection for speech that goes up against power and no reward for reports that go sideways against peers, and the opening of archives after the regime falls\. And its results answer, one by one, the four defences on which apologists for communist rule still rely: that its founders meant well, that its crimes were local excesses, that its people were merely afraid, and that its evils were the work of a few\. The same structure now has new bodies, in the states that communist parties still govern, in states that score civic conduct and reward the reporting of others, and in artificial systems that make praise costless at scale\. Three tests are registered; none has been run\. Relevance The question why so many ordinary, intelligent people under the communist states denounced their neighbours, inflated their reports and took part in campaigns they privately doubted has usually been answered with fear\. Fear explains a great deal, but it does not explain the excess: the official who reported a yield ten times the plan, the colleague who denounced before he was asked, the believer who was made rather than born\. This article explains the excess structurally, without assuming that the people concerned were unusually wicked, and it shows how the excess killed\. The answer matters for the present for three reasons\. First, the mechanism is not historical: every institution that rewards displayed loyalty and cannot verify it is under the same pressure to demand costlier proofs, and every institution that rewards the reporting of colleagues is building the first step of the spiral\. Second, states that score the conduct of their citizens, such as China’s Social Credit System, publish the floor of safety as a number and attach rewards to exceeding it; the floor ratchet predicts what follows\. Third, artificial intelligence now makes praise effectively free to produce in any quantity\. By the spiral of proof, a power that wishes to read loyalty will be pushed faster towards proofs that cost the prover something real, and in political life the costliest proofs have always been paid by other people\. The answer also matters because communism is not over\. Communist parties still hold power over about a fifth of humanity, and the apologetics of the twentieth\-century regimes — that their founders meant well, that their crimes were the excesses of local zealots or of a few bad leaders, that their peoples were merely afraid — are still taught\. This article gives the reader a structural answer to each, and a set of defences derived from the mechanism itself \(§11\.1\), so that what destroyed so many lives can be recognised at its first step and stopped before its last\. For individuals the article offers what the programme offers throughout: a description of the act before which a person can stop\. The spiral begins with a sentence of praise that costs nothing and ends with a signature beneath another person’s name\. Every step in between is taken by someone who could have spoken frankly instead\. \(direct link \)
