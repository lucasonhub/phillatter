# Association between self\-efficacy and AI dependence among medical postgraduates: a multicenter cross\-sectional study with exploratory prediction

**Autores:** Meng\-Pan Li; Meng\-Jian Ren; Jiang\-Peng Wu; Zhaohui Li; Tong\-Tong Liu; Hao\-Ran Chen; Heng\-Yi Guo; Wencai Liu; Yafei Jiang; Ling\-Feng Yu; Xiao\-Jun Ma

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Generative artificial intelligence \(AI\) is increasingly used in postgraduate medical education and research, but the relationship between self\-efficacy and dependence\-like reliance on AI remains insufficiently characterized\. We examined the association between continuous self\-efficacy and AI\-dependence scores and separately explored prediction of sample\-relative higher AI\-dependence scores\. This multicenter cross\-sectional online survey included 1,546 valid questionnaires from medical postgraduates at six participating Chinese universities between January and March 2026\. Self\-efficacy was assessed using a five\-item study\-specific academic measure, and AI dependence was assessed using a direct Chinese translation of the 22\-item Artificial Intelligence Addiction Scale for Researchers\. Analyses used multivariable linear regression with HC3 standard errors, restricted cubic splines, model diagnostics, and item\-overlap sensitivity analyses\. Exploratory prediction used the sample median score of 70 as a non\-diagnostic boundary and a fixed 70:30 training/test split for internal validation\. Among 1,546 participants, 938 \(60\.7%\) were women and 579 \(37\.5%\) studied clinical medicine\. In the primary adjusted model, each 5\-point higher self\-efficacy score was associated with a 5\.296\-point lower AI\-dependence score \(95% CI, \-5\.918 to \-4\.674; P &lt; 0\.001\)\. Restricted cubic splines supported the overall association \(chi\-square = 287\.10; df = 3; P &lt; 0\.001\) and modest nonlinearity \(chi\-square = 8\.79; df = 2; P = 0\.012\)\. After removal of clearly overlapping items from both instruments, the Model 3 estimate changed by 2\.1% \(beta = \-5\.187; 95% CI, \-5\.800 to \-4\.575\)\. The exploratory model achieved an AUC of 0\.875 \(95% CI, 0\.845–0\.906\) in the held\-out internal test set\. Higher self\-efficacy was inversely associated with continuous AI\-dependence scores among medical postgraduates\. These findings suggest that AI\-literacy education may need to support autonomous task engagement and critical verification of AI outputs, but the cross\-sectional design precludes causal interpretation\. The exploratory prediction analysis was internally validated only, used a sample\-relative boundary, and requires independent validation before practical use\.
