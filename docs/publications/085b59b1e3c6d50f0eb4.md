# Eco\-feminism in the modern world: Gender, environment and social transformation

**Autores:** V Bhagya Jyotsna

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Eco\-feminism is an interdisciplinary intellectual and social movement that connects gender equality, environmental sustainability and social justice\. It examines how systems of patriarchy, economic exploitation, unequal resource distribution and environmental degradation can reinforce one another\. Emerging prominently during the 1970s and 1980s, eco\-feminism has developed from an early emphasis on the symbolic relationship between women and nature toward broader approaches involving environmental justice, political ecology, intersectionality and sustainable development\. Contemporary environmental crises such as climate change, biodiversity loss, water scarcity, pollution and food insecurity have renewed interest in eco\-feminist perspectives\. Women, particularly those living in rural and economically vulnerable communities, may experience environmental changes differently because of unequal access to land, income, technology, political institutions and natural resources\. At the same time, women are important agents of environmental protection, community resilience, sustainable agriculture and climate action\. The United Nations also recognizes that women&\#x27;s participation in environmental decision\-making can strengthen climate\-resilient policies\. This article examines the relationship between gender and environment, the evolution of eco\-feminist thought, women&\#x27;s environmental leadership, climate change, environmental justice and the possibilities for social transformation\. It argues that modern eco\-feminism should move beyond the assumption that women are naturally closer to nature and instead emphasize equality, participation, rights, knowledge, intersectionality and democratic environmental governance\. Eco\-feminism is an interdisciplinary perspective that examines the interconnected relationship between the oppression of women and the exploitation of nature\. It argues that patriarchal, hierarchical, and exploitative social structures often contribute to both gender inequality and environmental degradation\. In the modern world, climate change, deforestation, pollution, water scarcity, loss of biodiversity, and unsustainable development have created serious social and ecological challenges, with women—particularly those in rural and marginalized communities—often experiencing disproportionate impacts\. Women play important roles in agriculture, household resource management, community health, and environmental conservation, making their participation essential to sustainable development\. This article examines the significance of eco\-feminism in understanding contemporary gender and environmental issues and explores its potential contribution to social transformation\. It highlights the importance of women&\#x27;s environmental knowledge, community participation, political representation, and access to natural and economic resources\. The study also emphasizes that environmental sustainability cannot be achieved without addressing gender inequality, poverty, social exclusion, and unequal access to resources\. Eco\-feminism promotes an alternative development approach based on ecological sustainability, social justice, equality, care, cooperation, and democratic participation\. The article concludes that strengthening women&\#x27;s rights, leadership, and participation in environmental decision\-making can contribute significantly to building a more just, inclusive, and ecologically sustainable modern society\.
