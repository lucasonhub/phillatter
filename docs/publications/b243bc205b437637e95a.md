# Use of quality\-of\-care and patient\-safety policies in the WHO European region: an ecological cross\-sectional exploratory analysis of policy adoption and health outcomes

**Autores:** Liesbeth Borgermans; Ana Marques Domingues; Anna Crispo; Valter R\. Fonseca; Lenio Capsaskis; Jennifer Hall; João Breda

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective To assess the implementation of quality\-of\-care and patient\-safety policies across the WHO European Region and their association with population health outcomes\. Methods This study used an exploratory, ecological cross\-sectional design\. We constructed a summary count \(0–9\) of nine national quality\-of\-care and patient\-safety policies to capture the breadth of policy adoption, and used exploratory multivariable linear regression to examine cross\-sectional associations with selected outcomes, adjusting for health\-workforce density and public spending on health\. Results Many countries lacked national action plans for quality and patient safety, with only around one\-third implementing both\. Implementation of a national quality\-of\-care plan was associated with higher healthy life expectancy \(1\.68 additional years; p unadjusted = 0\.033, p = 0\.144\), a lower probability of premature death from cardiovascular disease, cancer, diabetes and chronic respiratory diseases \(−3\.33 percentage points; p unadjusted = 0\.049, p = 0\.144\), and reduced under\-five mortality \(−4\.06 deaths per 1,000 live births; p unadjusted = 0\.023, p = 0\.144\)\. Implementing a greater number of national policies was associated with small increases in healthy life expectancy \(0\.29 years per additional policy; p unadjusted = 0\.043, p = 0\.167\) and lower under\-five mortality \(−0\.72 deaths per 1,000 live births per additional policy; p unadjusted = 0\.018, p = 0\.162\), while associations with other outcomes were weaker and often non\-significant\. Conclusion Given the ecological cross\-sectional design, limited adjustment and multiple testing, these findings should be treated as exploratory and non\-causal and used as a basis for more detailed longitudinal research\.
