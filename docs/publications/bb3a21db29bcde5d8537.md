# ECONOMIC, LEGAL AND CYBERNETIC DETERMINANTS OF INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY PROTECTION IN NEURAL NETWORK MODELS IN THE CONTEXT OF THE DIGITALISATION OF THE ECONOMY

**Autores:** Олександр Сергійович Кушнерьов; Р\.І\. Андрієнко; Yevhenii Kravchenko

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction\. The exponential growth of the market for neural network technologies and artificial intelligence systems poses critical challenges for intellectual property protection bodies, as traditional legal mechanisms are unable to ensure the economic security of innovations\. Artificial intelligence’s capacity for unauthorised replication of algorithmic architectures results in significant financial losses, destabilising the commercialisation of R&amp;D and leading to substantial lost profits\. Purpose\. The aim of the study is to conduct an economic, legal and cybernetic assessment of current methods for protecting intellectual property relating to neural network models, as well as to develop comprehensive institutional tools for minimising financial risks and ensuring a sustainable level of capitalisation for innovative projects in the field of artificial intelligence\. Methods\. The methodological framework combines systems analysis, the comparative law approach and the tools of institutional economics\. Results\. The results demonstrate that the key object of protection, which generates added value, is the configuration of a trained neural network \(topology, weights, biases\), the legal uncertainty surrounding which hinders the capitalisation of innovative projects\. The technical and economic inefficiency of traditional digital watermarks has been established due to the high risks of asset devaluation during attacks; conversely, the cost\-effectiveness of proactive approaches based on the analysis of neuronal functionality has been substantiated\. Hybrid monetisation models \(royalties, SaaS subscriptions\) and organisational barriers \(centralised hosting, API access\) have been systematised, which reduce the transaction costs associated with subsequent legal defence\. Prospects\. The economic case for implementing a special \(sui generis\) legal regime for trained models has been demonstrated, as has the critical need for global harmonisation of regulatory policies to ensure the competitiveness of the artificial intelligence market\.
