# Artificial Intelligence‐Optimized Image Biomarker Identification for Sinonasal Inverted Papilloma Diagnosis Using Image‐Enhanced Versus White‐Light Endoscopy

**Autores:** Zhenxiao Huang; Cheng Li; Min Li; Ying‐Hong Zhang; Guimin Zhang; Qian Huang; Zufei Li; Michael T\. Chang; Bing He Zhou

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: This study aims to compare image feature identification and diagnostic performance between artificial intelligence\-aided image\-enhanced endoscopy \(using the Storz Professional Image Enhancement System, SPIES\) and conventional white\-light endoscopy \(WLE\) for sinonasal inverted papilloma \(SIP\)\. Methods: A total of 163 patients were investigated using Storz Professional Image Enhancement System \(SPIES\) endoscopy \(SPIES Spectral A\[SA\]/Spectral B\[SB\]\) and WLE\. From each patient, one or two images \(only in the presence of bilateral lesions\) were obtained, and a large number of potential features were extracted from WLE, SA, and SB\. Independent t\-tests and LASSO methods were used to feature selection\. Machine learning \(ML\) models were trained and evaluated for each channel of images\. Results: The Random Forest \(RF\) algorithm on SPIES SB mode achieved superior performance: accuracy = 0\.86, sensitivity = 0\.89, specificity = 0\.83, AUC = 0\.86, and Kappa = 0\.71\. SB significantly outperformed SA \(accuracy = 0\.77\) and WLE \(accuracy=0\.75\)\. ROC analysis confirmed SB&\#x27;s diagnostic superiority \(area under curve \[AUC\]: SB = 0\.86 vs\. SA = 0\.77, WLE = 0\.75\)\. Conclusions: SPIES SB mode demonstrated the highest diagnostic accuracy and AUC for SIP identification, highlighting its potential as a superior imaging modality\. SPIES endoscopy exhibits superior diagnostic accuracy and predictive performance compared to conventional WLE in the diagnosis of SIP\. However, the single\-center design and limited sample size necessitate validation through multicenter studies with larger cohorts\.
