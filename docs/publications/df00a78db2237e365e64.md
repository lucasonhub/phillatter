# One Cardiac Myosin Inhibitor Mechanism, Three Gateways: What a Follow\-On Candidate&\#x27;s Regulatory Path Reveals About Parallel Health Canada, FDA, and EMA Clinical Trial Authorization

**Autores:** Vraj Modi; Pruthvirajsinh Sindha; Kishan Rajput; Sukhpreet Singh Maan; Peivand Pirouzi

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Cardiac myosin inhibitors are a recently established mechanistic class that reduces excess cardiac contractility in obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy \(oHCM\) by allosterically and reversibly modulating myosin motor activity, with the first agent in this class having received FDA approval in late 2025\. This article uses a hypothetical new multicentre Phase 2 clinical trial of a follow\-on cardiac myosin inhibitor candidate, sponsored by a fictional company unrelated to the approved product&\#x27;s marketing authorization holder, to compare the Clinical Trial Application \(CTA\) process required by Health Canada, the Investigational New Drug \(IND\) process required by the U\.S\. Food and Drug Administration, and the Clinical Trial Application process required by the European Medicines Agency under Regulation \(EU\) No 536/2014\. Because an approved product already exists in this class, the comparison pays particular attention to how each region&\#x27;s reviewers are expected to treat the established drug\-drug interaction and echocardiographic monitoring precedent from that approved product&\#x27;s development when evaluating a new candidate acting through the same mechanism\. The article addresses the operational challenges specific to developing a follow\-on candidate in a class with an approved precedent, the ways artificial intelligence tools are used, and can responsibly be used, in preparing and administering this kind of regulatory package, and closes with recommendations for regulatory operations teams managing cardiac myosin inhibitor candidates across these three jurisdictions\.Keywords: cardiac myosin inhibitor; obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy; Clinical Trial Application; Investigational New Drug; Regulation \(EU\) No 536/2014; echocardiographic monitoring; regulatory artificial intelligence
