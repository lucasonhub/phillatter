# Predicting pathogen\-positive blood culture episodes at two hours using electronic health record data

**Autores:** Radu C\. Grovu; Hala Soomro; Nathan Gelinas; Michael Rossi; Sean F\. Monaghan

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Most blood cultures obtained early in hospitalization do not yield a pathogen\. We evaluated prediction 2 h after the first blood\-culture record using MIMIC\-IV\. Adults with an initial routine culture record within 24 h of hospital presentation were eligible if alive and still receiving care at prediction\. The cohort included 145,914 encounters, 99\.3% eventually admitted, and 6,404 pathogen\-positive episodes \(4\.39%\)\. Comparisons at 2, 4 and 6 h supported the earliest tested prediction time because later gains were small and uncertain\. The primary XGBoost model was evaluated in 11,523 test encounters; 242 episodes were positive \(2\.10%\)\. AUROC was 0\.819 \(95% CI 0\.792–0\.844\), average precision 0\.124 \(0\.096–0\.167\), and Brier score 0\.0201\. The top 5% of predicted risk contained 81 positives among 577 encounters, giving 14\.0% positivity and 6\.68\-fold enrichment\. Laboratory values, particularly lymphocyte percentage, contributed most to prediction\. Secondary analyses assessed the added value of previous culture results\. Mean predicted risk was 3\.78%, compared with 2\.10% observed\. This 2\-h model was able to concentrate pathogen\-positive episodes in higher\-risk groups, but calibration and transportability require further validation before clinical use\.
