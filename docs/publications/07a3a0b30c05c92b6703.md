# The osteoimmune cell atlas of peri‐implantitis: A narrative scoping review

**Autores:** Emilio Alfredo Cafferata; Anton Sculean; Frank Schwarz

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND/AIM: Peri\-implantitis represents a complex osteoimmune disorder characterized by dysregulated cellular ecosystems that drive chronic inflammation and accelerated peri\-implant bone loss\. MATERIALS AND METHODS: This article was designed as a narrative scoping review to map and integrate the available evidence from transcriptomics, single\-cell RNA sequencing \(scRNA\-seq\), spatial transcriptomics, flow cytometry, and immunohistochemistry to construct a comprehensive cellular atlas of peri\-implantitis\. KEY FINDINGS: neutrophil subsets with NETosis\-associated activity, inflammatory macrophage states, altered Th17/Treg\-related immune responses, and expanded B\-cell/plasma\-cell compartments forming organized adaptive immune niches\. Proposed molecular mechanisms include NF\-κB\-related inflammatory activation, CXCL13\-associated adaptive immune organization, NETosis\-related pathways, and RANKL\-mediated osteoclastogenic signaling\. However, several of these pathways remain incompletely validated in human peri\-implantitis tissue\. Critical knowledge gaps include limited direct evidence for NLRP3 inflammasome activation, uncharacterized type I interferon signaling, unresolved hierarchies of cellular RANKL sources, and limited functional data for rare immune populations\. CONCLUSIONS: The peri\-implantitis osteoimmune microenvironment constitutes a highly organized, self\-reinforcing inflammatory ecosystem\. Progress toward precision therapeutic strategies requires spatial multiomics at subcellular resolution, longitudinal human studies, and functional ex vivo validation models to interrogate the self\-sustaining circuits identified here\.
