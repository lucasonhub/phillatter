# Perceived social support may mediate adiposity outcomes after nutrition and exercise intervention in adolescents with overweight

**Autores:** Tianyu Gao; Zitong Ye; Ying Xiao

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study evaluated whether an 8\-week nutrition and exercise intervention improved adiposity and estimated basal metabolic rate outcomes in adolescents with overweight and explored perceived social support and exercise self\-efficacy as psychosocial mediators of outcomes at three\-month follow\-up\. Ninety\-four adolescents aged 10–14 years from Guangzhou, China, were randomized 1:1 to the intervention or usual\-care control group; 78 participants \(39 per group\) provided complete data for the analyses reported here\. The intervention included supervised aerobic or resistance exercise three times weekly for 30 min per session and a 15\-min group nutrition education class once weekly for 8 weeks\. Controls received usual\-care health advice without scheduled exercise or nutrition sessions\. BMI, fat mass, and estimated basal metabolic rate \(BMR\) were measured by bioelectrical impedance at baseline, 8 weeks, and three\-month follow\-up\. Perceived social support and exercise self\-efficacy were evaluated as candidate mediators\. ANCOVA\-framework linear models and exploratory parallel mediation analyses with 5,000 bootstrap resamples were used\. At 8 weeks, the intervention group had lower BMI and fat mass and higher estimated BMR than the control group after adjustment for baseline values and covariates; the reported 95% confidence intervals excluded zero\. In the three\-month mediation models, perceived social support showed significant specific indirect effects for BMI, fat mass, and estimated BMR\. In contrast, no specific indirect effect through exercise self\-efficacy was detected because its 95% confidence interval included zero for each outcome\. The intervention was associated with favorable adiposity and estimated BMR outcomes, and exploratory mediation analyses identified perceived social support as a potential pathway for maintained outcome differences at three months\. Because physical activity and dietary behavior were not measured during follow\-up, these findings should not be interpreted as evidence that adolescents sustained physical activity or dietary behavior after the program\. Clinical Trial Registry: ChiCTR2500110816\.
