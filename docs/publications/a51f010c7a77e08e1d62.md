# Why this AI app? The impact of generative artificial intelligence on educators’ choices in educational apps

**Autores:** Emma Liptrot; Adam Kenneth Dubé

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In elementary education, generative artificial intelligence \(GenAI\) is being incorporated into educational applications, despite still\-emerging evidence of its effects on children’s learning and development\. Educators, who are already overwhelmed with choice in the commercial app space, may increasincly have to decide whether to incorporate GenAI\-enabled apps for their classrooms\. Thus, the present study investigates how mentioning GenAI in app store descriptions impacts K\-6 educators’ selection of educational apps\. Over two studies, K\-6 educators \(each n = 101\) from Canada, the US, and the UK viewed and evaluated eight researcher\-created educational math apps with and without GenAI\. Study 1 tested whether mentioning GenAI in app descriptions affected educators’ app evaluations but found no effect\. In Study 2, results from a repeated measures MANOVA revealed that educators rated apps without AI higher than those mentioning GenAI, but there were no significant effects of GenAI on likelihood to download or willingness to pay for apps\. A hierarchical cluster analysis identified three distinct groups of educators: 1\) selectors \(n = 28\), who exhibited a preference for downloading, rating, and paying for apps including GenAI; 2\) agnostics \(n = 59\), who exhibit minimal preferences; and 3\) avoiders \(n = 14\), who prefer apps without AI\. Most educators were agnostic to the use of GenAI in educational apps, highlighting the need to support educators’ informed decisions about when to adopt GenAI tools and encourage critical evaluation of GenAI’s role in education\. More research is needed to understand how educators’ GenAI preferences relate to their attitudes, knowledge, and use of GenAI\.
