# Unpacking the Multidimensional Urbanization–Power System Resilience Nexus: Explainable and Causal Machine Learning Evidence from China

**Autores:** Daran Cai; Ziheng Shangguan; Yuanyuan Gao; Donghao Yang

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Whether urbanization strengthens or undermines power\-system resilience remains unclear because its demographic, spatial, economic, and digital dimensions may generate fundamentally different effects\. Using panel data from 31 Chinese provincial\-level regions from 2011 to 2023, this study integrates XGBoost with province\-grouped cross\-validation, SHAP, and accumulated local effects to uncover predictive importance, nonlinearities, and interactions, followed by double machine learning and causal forest for effect estimation\. Land urbanization emerged empirically as the most influential predictor, exerting an overall negative and markedly nonlinear effect on power\-system resilience\. Importantly, the negative conditional relationship is substantially weaker at higher levels of population urbanization, industrial upgrading, and digitalization, revealing systematic heterogeneity across urban development conditions\. Although the estimated negative relationship weakened nationally between 2011 and 2023, marked provincial heterogeneity persisted\. These findings reveal urbanization–power\-system resilience as a conditional and spatially differentiated nexus and suggest that coordinating land expansion with population concentration, economic upgrading, and digital capacity is critical for limiting resilience losses\.
