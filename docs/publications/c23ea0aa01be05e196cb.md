# Inaccurate Wind Power Forecasting During Extreme Weather Events Using Normalized Machine Learning

**Autores:** Dr\. Manoj Singh Amisha Nandansingh Negi

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Wind\-power prediction is reasonably accurate in normal weather but degrades during high winds, gusts and rapid changing conditions, and a model fitted to one turbine does not directly transfer to turbines of other capacities\. This paper presents an extreme\-weather\-aware, capacity\-normalized framework that converts 10\-minute SCADA and meteorological data into turbine power with Random Forest regressor\. The model predicts power as a fraction of rated capacity, uses features such as air density, density\-corrected wind speed, gust factor, rapid wind changes and one\-hour variability, labels statistically extreme conditions with thresholds learned from training data, and gives extreme observations five times the training weight\. The framework is demonstrated on a dataset of 11,520 records from site \(coastal, flat inland, hilly and plateau\) with 2\.0–5\.0 MW turbines, using a per\-site chronological 80/20 split\. On the unseen test period the model reached an NMAE of 2\.01%, an NRMSE of 2\.55% and an R² of 0\.992, with an aggregate energy error of −0\.06% \(414\.42 MWh predicted versus 414\.68 MWh actual\)\. Errors rose under extreme conditions \(NMAE 2\.25% versus 1\.97% in normal conditions\), confirming that extreme periods should be reported separately\. Sensitivity test showed that the extreme weighting did not reduce extreme\-condition error on this dataset\.
