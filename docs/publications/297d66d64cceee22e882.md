# Machine learning and artificial intelligence for antigen discovery and immunogen design: A critical appraisal of computational strategies in medical microbiology

**Autores:** Shoaib Manzoor

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Conventional vaccine development is slow, costly, and prone to attrition, with target antigen selection representing a major bottleneck\. Machine learning \(ML\) and artificial intelligence \(AI\) are increasingly proposed to accelerate vaccine development\. Aim: To critically evaluate ML\- and AI\-supported strategies for antigen discovery, epitope mapping, and immunogen engineering against bacterial, viral, fungal, and parasitic pathogens\. Methods: Guided by PRISMA 2020, literature from January 2020 to April 2026 was retrieved from PubMed/MEDLINE, Web of Science, Scopus, and bioRxiv\. Eligible studies described computational tools for antigen/epitope prediction or immunogen design, reported performance metrics, and, where relevant, experimental confirmation\. Methods were grouped into five categories and assessed for architecture, accuracy, validation, and limitations\. Results: Three developments shape the field: deep\-learning epitope predictors \(MUNIS, ROC\-AUC ≈0\.980; GraphBepi\); AlphaFold 3\-based modeling of TCR\-pMHC and antibody\-antigen complexes; and generative platforms \(RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN, MESODID\) yielding experimentally verified antibodies and multiepitope scaffolds\. Integrated pipelines \(PLGDL, VaxSeer\) and safety/developability filters permit end\-to\-end candidate ranking\. Obstacles include sparse, demographically biased training data, limited conformational epitope accuracy, neglected glycosylation, and frequent laboratory failure of designed proteins\. Conclusion: AI methods have advanced from concepts to experimentally supported tools that can accelerate antigen identification\. Translation into licensed vaccines will require repeated laboratory validation, standardized reporting, and training sets reflecting population diversity\. Relevance for Patients: By compressing antigen selection, these strategies may bring vaccines against endemic, emerging, and refractory pathogens to clinical evaluation sooner, while attention to dataset bias and in silico safety screening can promote equitable and safe products\.
