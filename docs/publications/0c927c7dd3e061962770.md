abstract: 'In correctional settings, substance use care is often evaluated on the
  basis of observed abstinence or reduced use during custody, treatment attendance,
  readiness for change, and motivation to sustain recovery. Yet constrained access
  to substances does not demonstrate that stress and reward regulation have recovered.
  Custody may involve uncertainty, limited autonomy, disrupted sleep, and social disconnection;
  release reintroduces drug-associated cues while adding pressures related to housing,
  stigma, income, and unstable treatment access. These phases are commonly managed
  as separate administrative episodes, although they may constitute a single biologically
  and socially continuous transition.We argue that the prison-to-community transition
  should be investigated as a neuropeptide-sensitive allostatic sequence. We define
  the "carceral allostatic amplifier" as an exposure-linked, within-person worsening
  of stress-regulatory phenotypes across custody and re-entry, potentially accompanied
  by coordinated changes in candidate neuropeptide measures. It is a conditional and
  falsifiable construct, not a biological label: it predicts neither uniform harm
  across institutions nor a common trajectory for all incarcerated people. Its purpose
  is to identify when abrupt environmental change, interrupted care, and preexisting
  dysregulation may converge.We are unaware of prospective human studies that repeatedly
  measure neuropeptide trajectories from custody through community re-entry. This
  Opinion therefore integrates evidence from stressrelated addiction neurobiology
  (1-6) and post-release vulnerability (7)(8)(9)(10)18) to define a testable research
  agenda. It should not be interpreted as evidence that incarceration produces a specific
  peptide profile.Addiction involves adaptations in reward circuitry and recruitment
  of stress processes in the extended amygdala. CRF, dynorphin-kappa opioid receptor,
  and orexin signaling form the primary stress-promoting candidates in this framework;
  NPY, nociceptin, and context-dependent oxytocin are counter-regulatory candidates
  (1)(2)(3)(4)(5)(6). Their roles vary by receptor, circuit, substance, and addiction
  stage. Peripheral concentrations are indirect and cannot be equated with synaptic
  release, receptor activity, or signaling within a specific brain circuit.The framework
  is transdiagnostic at the level of stress regulation, but it should not be assumed
  to operate identically across substances or populations. Alcohol, opioid, stimulant,
  and polysubstance use disorders require stratified analyses, as do co-occurring
  trauma, mental illness, chronic pain, sex-related biology, age, and treatment exposure.
  Opioid use disorder offers a pragmatic initial test because correctional-care evidence
  is strongest for medication continuity and post-release outcomes (8-10, 15, 16,
  19); findings should not be generalized automatically to other disorders.Risk is
  concentrated immediately after release; recent linked data show particularly high
  opioidoverdose rates during the first two weeks (18). A program assessed only at
  discharge may therefore appear successful before community cues and structural adversity
  have tested what was achieved in custody (7)(8)(9)18). Return to use may then be
  attributed to motivation alone, even though vulnerability may have accumulated before
  release. Follow-up should align with the transition rather than administrative convenience.The
  prospect of a single blood marker for relapse is attractive but premature. Plasma,
  serum, saliva, and other peripheral specimens are not interchangeable, and their
  concentrations may not represent central signaling. Circadian timing, nutrition,
  medication, acute stress, sex-related biology, specimen handling, extraction, storage,
  and assay procedures can influence results. Oxytocin illustrates this uncertainty:
  peripheral measurement is methodologically sensitive, and a recent trial found no
  added benefit of intranasal oxytocin beyond alcohol behavioral couple therapy (11)(12)(13)(14).We
  therefore propose a within-person network hypothesis. Under sustained adversity,
  greater recruitment of stress-promoting systems together with insufficient counter-regulation
  may accompany hyperarousal, poor sleep, reduced social safety, stress-reactive craving,
  and diminished recovery motivation. Housing, stigma, relationships, income, and
  treatment access are not background "noise" or nuisance covariates; depending on
  the causal question, they may be exposures, moderators, mediators, or outcomes.
  Figure 1 depicts hypothesized associations rather than an established causal pathway.Figure
  1 summarizes the framework. Within-person changes may be more informative than a
  single group average, and medication continuity and trusted care may attenuate adverse
  trajectories. No peptide panel should be considered clinically useful unless it
  adds reproducible predictive value beyond contextual conditions, symptoms, and treatment
  continuity.Three potential uses must remain distinct. A mechanistic correlate may
  illuminate a pathway without predicting an individual outcome. A prognostic biomarker
  must add validated predictive value beyond available clinical and social information.
  A treatment-response biomarker must identify differential benefit from a defined
  intervention. Candidate neuropeptides are far from satisfying the latter two standards.
  The immediate question is whether coordinated, context-linked trajectories are reproducible-not
  whether a threshold can classify an individual.The model yields four prespecified
  predictions. First, within-person changes in stress-regulatory phenotypes will cluster
  around defined transitions-entry, stable custody, release preparation, and re-entry-rather
  than remain static. Second, greater exposure to sleep disruption, social threat,
  cue exposure, or treatment interruption will be associated with less favorable trajectories,
  whereas medication continuity and trusted care will attenuate them. Third, repeated
  measures will explain more variation in proximal outcomes than a single pre-release
  value. Fourth, the construct will be weakened if standardized assays show no reproducible
  transition-related pattern or no incremental information beyond contextual and clinical
  measures.An informative design would follow participants at custody entry, during
  stable custody, before release, within 72 hours after release where feasible, during
  weeks 1-2, and at days 30 and 90. Investigators should prespecify the specimen matrix
  and standardize collection time, fasting and medication status, extraction, storage,
  freeze-thaw exposure, batch allocation, limits of detection, and blinded duplicate
  assays. Biological observations should be paired with validated measures of sleep,
  stress, social safety, craving, cue exposure, housing, and treatment continuity.Analyses
  should prioritize a small set of primary systems and outcomes, with other tests
  labeled exploratory. Directed causal diagrams should distinguish time-varying exposures,
  confounders, mediators, and outcomes. Multilevel models should estimate within-person
  change; missingness and loss to follow-up require sensitivity analyses; multiplicity
  requires correction or hierarchical modeling; and any prediction model requires
  external validation. Altered tolerance, drug availability, infection, nutrition,
  medication, and acute stress remain competing explanations.Medication for substance
  use disorder must never be withheld for mechanistic convenience. Consent must be
  voluntary and separated from discipline, privileges, parole, release decisions,
  and access to care. Data governance should specify ownership, access, retention,
  secondary use, and a prohibition on disclosure to correctional or law-enforcement
  decision makers. Biospecimens and linked data should not be repurposed for punishment,
  surveillance, or individualized release prediction. People with lived experience
  should participate in protocol design, oversight, and interpretation.A staged program
  would reduce false discovery. Feasibility studies should establish recruitment,
  specimen stability, assay reliability, retention, and acceptability. Confirmatory
  cohorts should preregister directional hypotheses and seek external replication.
  Only reproducible associations should advance to intervention studies; well-designed
  null findings would remain informative tests of the model.The practical priority
  is continuity, not biomarker-guided care. Evidence-based medication should continue
  across custody and release (8-10, 15, 16, 19). Sleep stabilization, predictable
  routines, dependable social connection, naloxone access, housing support, and timely
  community linkage are justified by clinical and ethical evidence independent of
  any biomarker. Humane care must never become conditional on a biological result.Neuropeptide-targeted
  treatment remains experimental. Nivasorexant entered human trials for nonaddiction
  indications, but efficacy for addiction has not been established (17). A newer selective
  orexin-1 antagonist has progressed from preclinical substance-use models toward
  early human investigation, without clinical efficacy evidence to date (20). Oxytocin
  findings also remain inconsistent (14). The defensible near-term agenda is therefore
  mechanism-focused research layered onto established medication, housing, harm reduction,
  and psychosocial care-not a replacement for those interventions.The carceral allostatic
  amplifier is deliberately falsifiable. It would be weakened if repeated, standardized
  measures showed no reproducible transition-related pattern, added no information
  beyond contextual measures, or failed to track proximal phenotypes after rigorous
  control for confounding. It would be strengthened by convergence across repeated
  assays, sleep and behavioral phenotypes, and experimental stress responses. Even
  convergent peripheral findings would remain correlates unless stronger causal designs
  supported mediation.The model offers three contributions: it explains why observed
  non-use under constraint may overstate recovery; it integrates structural exposure
  with stress biology without treating either as sufficient; and it redirects biomarker
  research from static classification toward dynamic transitions. Its limitations
  are substantial. Human neuropeptide measurement is difficult, translation from animal
  models is incomplete, correctional environments are heterogeneous, and observational
  studies are vulnerable to selection, attrition, measurement error, and time-varying
  confounding.Feasibility and generalizability will vary across prisons, jails, remand
  settings, jurisdictions, sexes and genders, racial and ethnic groups, ages, immigration
  contexts, and lengths of custody. Release timing changes, follow-up is difficult,
  and health systems differ in medication access, privacy protections, and community
  linkage. Multisite studies should document these institutional and structural conditions
  rather than statistically erase them.We do not advocate neuropeptide screening of
  incarcerated people. We propose the prison-tocommunity transition as a rigorous
  test of when vulnerability changes, for whom, and through which regulatory pathways.
  One conclusion does not require biomarker validation: pharmacological, relational,
  and environmental continuity should be preserved across the gate. Neuropeptide science
  will be useful only if it clarifies how to strengthen that continuity rather than
  reducing addiction to a molecule.'
