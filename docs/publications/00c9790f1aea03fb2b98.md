# Study buddy learning is associated with academic success in undergraduate science courses

**Autores:** Achol Jones; Gabrielle Reznik; Al Rohet Hossain; Veronica Dudarev; Bowen Hui; Patrice Belleville; Warren Williams; Eden Fussner; James T\. Enns

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

for undergraduate science students\. The study tested three hypotheses: \(i\) Do students benefit academically from a formalized study buddy peer learning program? \(ii\) Do mentors and protégés benefit equally from participation in the study buddy program? \(iii\) Does heterogeneity in group members&\#x27; initial academic performance have an effect on benefits from participation in the study buddy program? To test these hypotheses, we implemented a cohort\-based study buddy program in which high\-scoring students \(mentors\) and lower\-scoring peers \(protégés\), following their first assessment scores in a given course, were invited to form study groups\. We compared the performance of participants versus non\-participants on subsequent assessments, while examining separately the effects for mentors and protégés\. Results showed \(i\) significant grade benefits for participants over non\-participants, \(ii\) similar participation benefits for students in mentor and protégé roles, and \(iii\) no interaction of competence\-based heterogeneity on these effects\. Although this study design was unable to assess the nature of the study buddy interactions, the grade performance data highlight the program as a cost\-effective and scalable approach to enhance students&\#x27; educational experiences\. The data also emphasize the value of reducing barriers to collaborative learning in higher education\.
