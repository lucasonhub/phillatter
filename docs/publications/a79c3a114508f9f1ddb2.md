# Sub‐Second Ratiometric Fluorescence Imaging of Histamine Dynamics in Living Cells via a Supramolecular Probe

**Autores:** Chenming Chan; Yujie Han; Yufei Song; Chunlei Jiang; Qi‐Wei Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

ABSTRACT Histamine \(HA\) is a critical neurotransmitter, yet its intracellular dysregulation remains difficult to monitor due to the slow kinetics and environmental sensitivity of traditional fluorescent probes\. Herein, we report a supramolecular ratiometric probe engineered via the host–guest self‐assembly of cationic fluorophores with cucurbit\[7\]uril \(CB\[7\]\)\. By precisely modulating the cationic locus within the guests, we achieved a 2:1 \(host:guest\) stoichiometric complex \( CB\[7\] 2 ⊃GA‐1 \) that displays a distinct 60 nm bathochromic shift\. Functioning via a competitive displacement mechanism, this probe demonstrates exceptional selectivity and an ultrafast response time of 0\.96 s \( k obs = 3\.56 s − 1 \)\. We successfully utilized this system to visualize real‐time HA fluctuations in living mast cells with sub‐second resolution\. Notably, our imaging revealed a quantitative correlation between nigericin‐induced cytoplasmic HA elevation and mast cell rupture\. These findings validate the probe&\#x27;s efficacy in tracking cellular inflammatory responses and highlight its potential as an early‐warning indicator for drug‐induced cytotoxicity\. Consequently, this work offers a robust tool for dissecting HA‐related pathophysiology and establishes a generalizable design paradigm for the rapid, ratiometric sensing of biologically relevant analytes\.
