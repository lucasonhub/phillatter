# Wound\-healing activity of Laportea decumana leaf extract in a polymicrobial\-infected wound model in rats

**Autores:** Muhammad Haryandi; SEPTRIANI BANGALINO&\#x27;; Nurhasni Hasan; Yulia Yusrini Djabir

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Polymicrobial wound infections remain a major clinical challenge due to prolonged inflammation and impaired tissue repair\. Laportea decumana, a traditional medicinal plant with reported antimicrobial and anti\-inflammatory properties, has not been well studied in polymicrobial\-infected wounds\. This study evaluated the wound\-healing effects of topical L\. decumana leaf extract in a rat model of polymicrobial\-infected cutaneous wounds\. Standardized 6\-mm dorsal wounds were inoculated with a mixed suspension of Staphylococcus aureus, Staphylococcus epidermidis, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa\. After a 48\-h infection establishment period, rats received once\-daily topical treatment for 14 days with L\. decumana extract \(2%, 4%, or 8%\) or a negative control\. Wound healing was assessed by macroscopic wound closure and histopathological evaluation\. Wound closure increased progressively in all groups\. On day 2, the 8% extract group showed greater mean wound closure than the control \(41\.64% vs\. 24\.26%\)\. By day 14, wounds in all groups approached near\-complete closure \(95\.45–95\.94% in treated groups vs\. 92\.57% in the control\), with no statistically significant differences\. Histopathological analysis demonstrated treatment\-associated improvements in wound architecture, including enhanced re\-epithelialization, better\-organized granulation tissue, and reduced inflammatory cell infiltration, most prominently in the 8% extract group\. Although L\. decumana leaf extract did not significantly accelerate macroscopic wound closure, it promoted favorable histological features of tissue repair in polymicrobial\-infected wounds\. These findings underscore the importance of histological endpoints in complex infection models and support further investigation of L\. decumana as a natural topical therapy for infected wound management\.
