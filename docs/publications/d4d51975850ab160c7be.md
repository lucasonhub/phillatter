# Connectomic signatures advance the understandings of transdiagnostic heterogeneity in psychosis

**Autores:** Yu Du; Jingjing Zhang; Ting Ma; Chenfei Ye

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Current consensus\-driven diagnostic systems poorly capture the marked heterogeneity and high comorbidity of psychiatric disorders, necessitating brain\-based transdiagnostic frameworks to advance mechanistic understanding and clinical translation\. Connectome\-wide association studies have consistently identified widespread large\-scale dysconnectivity across diagnostic boundaries, yet normative modeling and meta\-analytic evidence highlights substantial inter\-individual variability in the spatial topography and magnitude of connectomic deviations\. In this Perspective, we bridge this divide between cross\-disorder commonalities and individualized pathology by integrating connectomic overlap and computational biotyping into a top\-down, four\-tiered hierarchical architecture\. This framework progresses from macro\-scale transdiagnostic topological scaffolds, through meso\-scale phenotypic triangulation, to data\-driven biotypes and micro\-scale clinical translations, delineating bidirectional interplay between shared network constraints and heterogeneous pathological diversification\. Building on this architecture, we outline strategies leveraging multi\-cohort data harmonization and healthy\-anchored normative biotyping to refine robust cross\-disorder circuits and reproducible neurobiotypes\. Lastly, we propose a translational roadmap to transition connectomic stratification toward prospective, treatment\-relevant clinical validation in precision psychiatry\.
