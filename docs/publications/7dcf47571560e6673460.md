# Artificial Intelligence in Eurasian Higher Education: An Empirical Study of Student Practices

**Autores:** Shamil Sheymardanov; Muhamad Hariz Muhamad Adnan; Timur Mufazalov

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Generative artificial intelligence has penetrated higher education not through official strategies but via students’ and faculty members’ personal devices, catching most universities off guard\. This article presents empirical data from a large\-scale survey conducted between March and April 2026 across six post\-Soviet countries \(Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan, Armenia, Tajikistan\)\. The study provides a systematic cross\-national analysis of student AI use patterns in the Eurasian higher education space, complementing recent single\-country studies in the region\. The sample included 478 students\. The study measured frequency of AI use, perceived risks, compatibility with educational values, and the presence of institutional regulatory mechanisms\. The results show that 78 % of students regularly use generative AI, yet only 32 % of universities have an official policy in this area\. A deep disciplinary divide emerges: students fall into two roughly equal profiles – “pragmatic techs” \(daily users with low ethical anxiety\) and “reflective humanists” \(cautious users with a strong demand for ethical frameworks\)\. The main barrier to AI adoption is not technological infrastructure but the absence of regulatory and social technologies\. The article offers discipline\-differentiated recommendations for universities in non\-Western contexts\.
