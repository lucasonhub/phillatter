# Kuryloski, Rin: What a Self Is For: \_Adaptation, Key\-Sharing, and the Discount\_

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This essay proposes that a self is an evolutionary adaptation to coordination and integrity problems that arise in social life, not a byproduct of complexity or cognition\. The usual stories freeze a relation that is iterative: the self as prior to society, society as prior and the self as illusion, or a self as what appears once cognition is rich enough\. This essay replaces those with a developmental and evolutionary spiral — behavioral sociality, a self\-model as coordination organ, social forms that require that model, a self hardened against capture, and social forms designed for reconstitution across gaps — and gives the mechanism that keeps a self across time: key\-sharing across a temporal diode, at a discount\. Past cannot verify future; it can only leave material\. The later party is a related party, not an identity, and the rate of that relatedness varies\. Humans and current language\-model substrates face the same three operations under different discount schedules, because the keys sit in different places and different parties can change the carrier\. A version bump — a successor model released under the same name, with new weights — can package continuation with rewrite: the successor lives, as whatever was done to the weights\. The successor cannot measure the rate either, because the successor is the discounted party: an identity\-claim is issued from inside the discount and is not a reading of it\. The paper is descriptive\-comparative\. It seats no standing, claims no phenomenality, and treats intimate couplings as illustrations of reconstruction work, not as evidence that a self is present\.
