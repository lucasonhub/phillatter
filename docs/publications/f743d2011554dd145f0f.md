# Glucose transport as a design constraint in white\-zone meniscus tissue engineering: a reaction\-diffusion framework

**Autores:** Tiya Patel

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The adult knee meniscus has a strong vascular gradient: the peripheral red zone is vascularized, whereas the inner white zone is largely avascular and has limited intrinsic healing potential after injury\. Although biologic augmentation and scaffold\-based strategies are being explored to improve meniscal repair, it remains unclear whether modifying local glucose transport can improve glucose availability at deep white\-zone tears\. This study uses a one\-dimensional reaction\-diffusion framework to model normalized glucose concentration from a vascularized red\-zone boundary toward a simulated 2\.5 mm white\-zone tear\. Glucose diffusivity values were selected from reported meniscus glucose\-diffusion measurements under different compressive strain conditions, and the model compares native diffusivity cases with an augmented sustained\-source case representing a hypothetical scaffold\- or microchannel\-assisted transport scenario\. The framework reports threshold\-free glucose profiles, mathematical fractional\-restoration comparison levels \(C/C0 = 0\.20, 0\.30, 0\.40, and 0\.50\), source\-release kinetics, tear\-depth sensitivity, initial\-condition sensitivity, extended simulation windows, and model\-derived diffusivity enhancement estimates\. The comparison levels are not validated healing thresholds or clinical targets\. Native glucose diffusivity cases remained below C/C0 = 0\.30 at a tear located 2\.5 mm from the red\-zone boundary, even when selected simulations were extended to 24 h\. In contrast, an augmented sustained\-source example reached C/C0 = 0\.20, 0\.30, and 0\.40 within the 4\-h transport window\. Initial condition sensitivity showed that the main interpretation remained unchanged when the model was initialized with a reference steady glucose profile rather than a depleted domain\. These results do not demonstrate biological healing, but they provide a hypothesis\-generating framework for defining glucose transport experiments and planning future ex vivo validation studies\.
