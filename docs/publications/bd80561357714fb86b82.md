## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026Forecasting normally represents a future in variables selected at the time of prediction\. But what happens when the distinctions that organize a forecast do not retain their predictive work across the interval being forecast? This paper develops predictive\-variable non\-conservation as a special case of operation\-indexed representational adequacy\. A representation is adequate only relative to an operation, configuration, and interval; representations equivalent for passive prediction may diverge under intervention, control, recovery, resource constraints, or later configuration change\. The paper distinguishes unknown values, model uncertainty, structural breaks, concept drift, feature evolution, conceptual disruption, and changes in the non\-redundancy of predictive distinctions\. It further separates three claims that are often conflated: schema revision may be selectively necessary without yielding an absolutely adequate representation, and an adequate representation need not constitute a uniquely warranted variable decomposition\. A sequence of synthetic correctness and stress experiments tests these distinctions, including a retained negative result in which predictive success fails to uniquely recover a privileged representation\. Exploratory evidence from an incomplete Ziran runtime development snapshot additionally shows that predictive adaptation and control success can dissociate\. The paper connects these results to David Roden’s account of posthuman disconnection, Sellarsian and Brandomian distinctions between causal tracking and conceptual\-inferential articulation, feature evolution, forecasting under structural change, and recent causal representation learning\. A preregistered confirmatory computational protocol is specified in which fixed\-schema, feature\-adaptive, schema\-revising, and capacity\-matched policies are compared across configuration crossings\. The resulting claim is deliberately limited: some failures of prediction may concern not only unknown future values or changing dynamics, but the failure of present representational distinctions to remain adequate for the operations required by a later configuration\. \(direct link \)
