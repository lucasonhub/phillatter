# Sarcopenia in Patients with Axial Spondyloarthritis: Associations with TNF Inhibitor Therapy and Exploratory Ultrasound Findings

**Autores:** Cagla Karaoglu; Özlem Pehlivan; Halise Hande Gezer; Rıdvan Sivritepe; Fatma Kulalı; Sema Basat

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background/Aims: Sarcopenia has gained increasing attention as a potential comorbidity in axial spondyloarthritis \(axSpA\)\. This study aimed to assess the frequency of sarcopenia in patients with axSpA and to investigate its association with tumor necrosis factor inhibitor \(TNFi\) therapy\. A secondary objective was to explore the potential value of ultrasound\-based muscle measurements in identifying sarcopenia\. Materials and Methods: This cross\-sectional study enrolled 62 adult patients with axSpA who were evaluated during routine rheumatology follow\-up\. Clinical data, including disease activity, functional status, and spinal mobility, were recorded\. Muscle strength, physical performance, and body composition were assessed in accordance with current sarcopenia definitions\. Selected thigh and lumbar muscle parameters were measured using musculoskeletal ultrasound\. Statistical analyses included comparative tests and regression modeling\. Results: Probable sarcopenia was identified in 22\.6% of patients, while 12\.9% met the criteria for sarcopenia; no cases of severe sarcopenia were detected\. Sarcopenia was observed less frequently among patients receiving TNFi therapy compared with those not treated with TNFi\. TNFi use was associated with a reduced likelihood of sarcopenia\. Ultrasound measurements of the rectus femoris muscle showed modest associations with sarcopenia status, although their diagnostic performance was limited\. Conclusion: Sarcopenia is common in patients with axSpA\. In this cross\-sectional study, TNFi use was associated with a lower prevalence of sarcopenia, although a causal relationship cannot be established\. Ultrasound\-derived muscle parameters showed limited discriminatory performance and should be considered exploratory\.
