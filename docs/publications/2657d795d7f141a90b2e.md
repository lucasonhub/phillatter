# Spatial divergence of climate–vegetation relationships in Florida: A 43\-year multi\-method analysis \(1982–2024\)

**Autores:** Anil Kumar Mandal; Aman Kumar Thapa; Madan Thapa Chhetri; Hongbo Su; Yan Yong; Fred Bloetscher

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Attributing long\-term vegetation change to climate is difficult in subtropical ecosystems, where temperature and precipitation act simultaneously, are confounded by abrupt disturbances, and where statewide averages can conceal offsetting associations\. Here we apply a multi\-method convergence\-of\-evidence framework to a sensor\-harmonized 43\-year record \(1982–2024\) of NOAA CDR NDVI, temperature, and precipitation across Florida, which spans cool temperate forests to warm subtropical wetlands\. Modified Mann–Kendall tests with false\-discovery\-rate correction detected monotonic trends; pixel\-level correlation, spatial\-error regression, and lagged cross\-correlation evaluated association strength, direction, and timing; Empirical Orthogonal Function decomposition of deseasonalized anomalies separated persistent climatic signals from episodic disturbance, with the leading mode \(58\.4% of variance\) tracking latitudinal warming and a lower\-order mode \(PC3\) tracking hurricane impacts\. Florida warmed by \+0\.033 °C yr⁻¹, significant in 99\.6% of land pixels, while precipitation showed no field\-significant trend\. The temperature–greenness association was positive across essentially the entire state, marginally stronger in the south; localized negative responses were confined to specific southern wetland and urban\-fringe pixels rather than forming a systematic north–south reversal\. The state\-mean precipitation–NDVI correlation was negative at zero lag and reversed from r = −0\.263 to r = \+0\.102 when incoming shortwave radiation was partialed out, consistent with cloud\-associated light suppression rather than water limitation—a phenomenon we term the Water Paradox\. SPI/SPEI–NDVI correlations were near zero, and Everglades water levels were largely decoupled from wetland greenness \(r = 0\.08, n\.s\.\), indicating that inundation acts as a localized threshold modifier rather than a dominant suppressor\. Climate–vegetation models for comparable high\-rainfall subtropical systems may therefore need to account for spatially divergent temperature sensitivities and treat precipitation as an energy constraint rather than a uniform benefit\.
