# Employee Acceptance of Artificial Intelligence in Logistics Operations: Evidence from a State\-Owned Energy Distributor in Sri Lanka

**Autores:** Savindya Gunawardhana; Dineshi Sashikala; Tharindu Gunathilake

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial Intelligence \(AI\) adoption in logistics depends not only on technological capability but also on employees’ willingness and ability to use AI\-based systems\. This study examines the factors influencing employees’ behavioural intention to use AI in logistics operations through the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology \(UTAUT\)\. A positivist, deductive, quantitative and cross\-sectional survey design was used in a state\-owned energy distribution enterprise in Sri Lanka\. The population comprised 93 employees, with 75 targeted and 63 usable responses obtained\. The data were analysed using descriptive statistics, Cronbach’s alpha, Pearson correlation and multiple regression in SPSS\. Performance expectancy, effort expectancy and facilitating conditions had statistically significant positive effects on behavioural intention, whereas social influence was not statistically significant\. Facilitating conditions had the strongest standardized effect \(β = 0\.326, p = 0\.008\)\. The regression model explained 67\.0% of the variance in behavioural intention \(R² = 0\.670\)\. The findings highlight the importance of organisational readiness, practical support, system usability and visible performance benefits when introducing AI into regulated logistics operations\. The study contributes context\-specific evidence from a state\-owned energy distributor in Sri Lanka while recognizing that the single\-organization, cross\-sectional design limits broader generalization\.
