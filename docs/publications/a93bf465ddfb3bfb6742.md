# Integrating cultural heritage into inland waterway revitalization: spatial heterogeneity and differentiated planning strategies in Henan, China

**Autores:** Hu Li; Qingtao Bai; Yating Song; Xian Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The revival of inland navigation creates new opportunities to align cultural heritage activation with spatial planning across river basins, yet the relationship between river\-network environments and contemporary heritage vitality remains underquantified\. Using 371 nationally protected heritage sites in Henan Province, we constructed a Heritage Site Vitality Index \(HSVI\) from seven min–max\-normalized cultural, social, and economic indicators using weights derived from the analytic hierarchy process\. The analysis progressed from heritage spatial organization to river\-network–vitality configurations and then to site\-level associations, using pure\-spatial DBSCAN, bivariate spatial analysis, ordinary least squares \(OLS\), geographically weighted regression \(GWR\), and locally compensated ridge GWR \(LCR\-GWR\)\. DBSCAN revealed a dominant central\-western agglomeration belt extending from Zhengzhou to Luoyang and a more dispersed peripheral pattern\. At the 5\-km grid scale, heritage\-vitality density and river\-network density showed a positive global spatial association \(bivariate Moran’s I = 0\.2109; permutation p = 0\.001\)\. High vitality–high river\-network configurations occurred in parts of the central and northern plains, high river\-network–low vitality configurations in parts of the eastern and southern plains, and several heritage agglomerations displayed high vitality despite low river\-network density\. Adjusted R 2 increased from 0\.1201 for OLS to 0\.6000 for GWR, showing that geographically varying coefficients captured substantially more variation in heritage vitality\. Coefficients for heritage agglomeration, riverbank\-line richness, and riparian greenness changed sign across the province, while their spatial patterns remained relatively consistent under LCR\-GWR\. Together, the regional configurations and local coefficients show how heritage organization, riverbank form, and riparian ecology differentiate river\-network–vitality relationships across the province, providing a diagnostic basis for differentiated heritage activation and spatial planning\.
