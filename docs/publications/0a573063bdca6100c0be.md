# Effectiveness of “Follow the Track”\- a video\-game\-based intervention on awareness and attitude regarding road safety among adolescents in Bhopal\- a Quasi\- Experimental study

**Autores:** Lily Podder; Dona Benny; Choudhary Sarla; Deepanjali Chandel; Dikhchya Pokhrel; Diksha Riju Zade; Dimpal Garg; Diya Kumari Malav; Diya Nagar

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objectives The study evaluated the effectiveness of “Follow the Track,” a video\-game\-based educational intervention, on awareness and attitude regarding road safety among secondary and higher secondary school students\. Material and methods A quasi\-experimental non\-equivalent pretest–post\-test control\-group design was adopted with 298 students \(control = 140, experimental = 158\) selected through nonprobability convenience sampling technique\. Data were collected using a structured awareness questionnaire and a 5\-point Likert scale to assess attitude regarding road safety\. The experimental group received the “Follow the Track” video game intervention three times \(on alternate days\), with each session lasting approximately 15\-20 minutes\. The video game consisted of two levels: level 1 focused on a memory\-based flip game on recognition and recall of road safety symbols, and level 2 encompassed an interactive quiz module with 20 structured questions on road safety rules, safe driving practices, and first aid management of road traffic accidents\. Data were analyzed using descriptive and inferential statistics with International Business Machines \(IBM\) Statistical Package for the Social Sciences \(SPSS\) version 26\.o Results Following the intervention, students in the experimental group showed statistically significant improvement in both awareness \(t = −23\.87, df = 157, p &lt;0\.001\), and attitude \(t = −44\.147, df = 157, p &lt;0\.001\) regarding road safety\. Inter\-group analysis by ANCOVA revealed a significant difference in post\-test awareness between the experimental and control groups \(F \(2,295\) = 997\.54, p &lt;0\.001\), with a large effect size \(partial η 2 = 0\.87\) and also in post\-test attitude scores \(F \(2,295\) = 151\.604, p &lt;0\.001\) between the control and experimental group, with a large effect size \(partial η 2 = 0\.915\)\. Conclusion The findings indicated that the video\-game\-based educational intervention was significantly effective in enhancing road safety awareness and promoting positive attitudes among adolescents, highlighting the value of integrating innovative digital learning strategies into school health education programs\.
