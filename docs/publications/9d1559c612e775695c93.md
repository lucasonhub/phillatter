# High\-resolution topology\-preserving instance segmentation network for vision\-based measurement system of dense welding material stubs

**Autores:** Xinyu Chen; YuChao Chen; Yan Ren; Guangming Lv; Meng Zhang; Wang Yuan

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Accurate counting and morphological analysis of welding electrodes and wire stubs are essential for warehouse data management in welding factories\. However, the visual recognition of welding material stubs in real\-world industrial applications is frequently hindered by issues such as missed detections, false detections, and imprecise boundary localization\. To address these metrological and visual perception challenges, this paper proposes a novel High\-resolution Topology\-Preserving Instance Segmentation Network \(HTP\-Net\)\. The proposed end\-to\-end architecture seamlessly integrates three core designs to realize precise decoupling and robust counting of dense targets\.Specifically, a Wavelet\-Driven Edge\-Preserving \(WEP\) module is designed to retain subtle physical boundaries without loss during feature downsampling\. Concurrently, a Long\-Range Topology Representation Block \(LTR\-Block\) mitigates topological disconnections resulting from extreme target slenderness by global context modeling\. Finally, an Orientation\-Driven Instance Segmentation Head \(ODI\-Seg\) leverages rotational geometric priors to fundamentally resolve excessive NMS and overlapping mask artifacts\. Notably, across both our custom and public datasets of dense slender materials, HTP\-Net achieves substantial performance gains over existing methods, improving mAP 50 scores by at least 1\.2% and up to 55\.3%\. Furthermore, we developed an intelligent counting system for welding materials powered by the proposed model, which has been deployed and validated in a real\-world factory environment\. Extensive experiments demonstrate that the integrated system achieves average recognition accuracies of 99% and 94% for electrode and wire stubs, respectively\.
