# Demographic Differences in Automated Facial Attractiveness Prediction: Evidence from SCUT\-FBP5500 and MEBeauty

**Autores:** Aila Sajid Bhangoo; Usama Younus; Haven Yi\-Suh Shin; Dr\. Asma Hamid Awan

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In this work, we examine automated facial beauty prediction \(FBP\) models to evaluate demographic variation in human attractiveness scores, facial landmark preprocessing, and cross\-dataset prediction error\. We perform multistage quantitative sociotechnical audits using SCUT\-FBP5500 and a 1,962\-image MEBeauty subset, including DLib landmark\-extraction analysis, intersectional ordinary least squares \(OLS\) regression, observed\-rating inter\-rater variability analysis, and a five\-seed ResNet\-50 cross\-dataset evaluation\. DLib landmark extraction was successful for 1,929 of 1,962 MEBeauty images, with an overall failure rate of 1\.7%\. The highest observed subgroup failure rate was among Middle Eastern men \(4\.2%; 2/48\)\. In the symmetry\-adjusted OLS model \(N = 1,929, R² = \.022\), no gender, ethnicity, or gender × ethnicity coefficient was statistically significant at the Benjamini–Hochberg correction\. At the same time, blur was the sole significant image quality control \(p=\.006\)\. Subgroup RMSE estimates across datasets ranged from \.470 to \.512\. Black men had the highest point estimate \(RMSE = \.512\), but prespecified image\-level bootstrap comparisons with Asian men \(difference = \.002, 95% CI \[−\.09, \.09\]\) and White men \(difference = \.022, 95% CI \[−\.05, \.09\]\) both included zero\. These results show how disaggregated sociotechnical auditing can be useful and also emphasize the significance of disentangling descriptive subgroup variation from statistically supported demographic disparities\.
