# Eslami, Abolhassan: Defecation as Extended Mind: A Psychoanalytic\-Ecological Synthesis of the Anus as Cognitive Interface

## Descrição do feed

\_TBA\_\. forthcomingThis article proposes a theoretical synthesis of Freudian psychoanalytic theory, ecological psychology, the free energy principle, and the Deleuzian concept of the Body without Organs to reconceptualize the anus as an active cognitive interface rather than a passive excretory orifice\. Drawing on Freud&\#x27;s anal stage of psychosexual development \(Freud, 1962\) and Winnicott&\#x27;s concept of the body ego \(Winnicott, 2006\), alongside ecological psychology&\#x27;s notion of the body as a site of affordance perception \(Gibson, 1979\) and Friston&\#x27;s active inference framework \(Friston, 2010; Parr et al\., 2022\), this article argues that defecation constitutes a unique cognitive cycle in which the body expels matter that has been classified as internal, thereby updating its predictive models of itself\. The anus, in this view, functions as a dynamic Markov blanket—a statistical boundary that regulates the exchange between internal and external states \(Kirchhoff et al\., 2018\)\. Through expulsion, the body achieves a form of self\-cleansing that is also self\-reinitialization\. This article further incorporates Deleuzian scholarship on the Body without Organs as an unstratified field of potentialities \(Eslami, 2025\), arguing that the anus operates as a threshold where stratified organization encounters unformed flows, and that defecation is a process of deterritorialization and reterritorialization \(Deleuze &amp; Guattari, 1987\)\. The article concludes that the brain&\#x27;s engagement with waste is not merely metabolic but epistemic, reducing variational free energy by simplifying the body&\#x27;s ecological predictions \(Friston, 2010\)\.
