# Reporting\-lag\-aware machine learning framework for short\-term forecasting of respiratory disease activity: a sentinel hospital study in Guangzhou

**Autores:** Zhiheng Luo; Wenzhou Duan; Yukun Lin; Huirong Wang; Hongmei Liu; Yunting Liu

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Background Reporting delays in laboratory confirmation constrain timely situational awareness in respiratory disease surveillance\. This study aimed to develop and evaluate a reporting\-lag\-aware machine learning framework for short\-term, age\-stratified forecasting of respiratory disease activity under real\-world surveillance constraints\. Methods We analyzed 1004 days \(November 1, 2023 to July 31, 2026\) of surveillance data from a national sentinel hospital in Guangzhou, China, including physician\-recorded or laboratory\-supported influenza, COVID\-19 and Mycoplasma pneumoniae cases, fever clinic visits and meteorological variables\. Given a routine 2\-day reporting lag, an XGBoost model predicted the 7\-day\-ahead case increment\. Most count\-derived predictors were normalized, while the latest observable case count was retained as a level anchor\. The shrinkage\-calibrated XGBoost forecast was combined with a rolling SARIMA forecast\. Comparators included naive rules, SARIMA, an oracle\-weather SARIMAX benchmark, LSTM and Transformer models\. Performance was assessed using RMSE, MAE, R 2 , directional accuracy, alert sensitivity, positive predictive value and prediction\-interval coverage over 363 scored target dates within a 371\-day calendar evaluation window\. Results The framework achieved RMSE values of 16\.50, 12\.64 and 6\.14 in the total population, adults and children, respectively, with corresponding R 2 values of 0\.640, 0\.694 and 0\.353\. It significantly outperformed persistence in the total and adult series \(Diebold\-Mariano p = 0\.001 and 0\.003\), but improvements over a 7\-day moving average or rolling SARIMA were not statistically significant\. No comparison was statistically significant in children\. For the proposed framework, directional accuracy was 67\.2% to 73\.5%, and sensitivity for identifying days on which activity reached or exceeded twice the development\-period median was 58\.1% to 78\.4%\. The absolute\-count specification performed worse over the extended evaluation period \(RMSE = 20\.32; R 2 = 0\.453\), during which fever clinic attendance declined by 31\.5% and the case\-to\-visit ratio increased by 31\.6%\. Conclusions A reporting\-lag\-aware framework based on routine data provided modest supplementary 7\-day situational awareness\. Its improvement over persistence was approximately one case per day in MAE, with no significant advantage over a 7\-day moving average or rolling SARIMA\. Performance depended on the stability of count\-based predictors under shifts in care\-seeking behaviour, supporting prospective multi\-site validation before broader use\.
