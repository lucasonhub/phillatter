# Association between infertility prevalence and exposure to heat stress: an ecological study based on the Universal Thermal Climate Index

**Autores:** Dian Wang; Jiahua Qian; Xiaoming Zhang; Chunbao Mo; Yihao Chen; Yuhe Si; Yicheng Wang; Jianan He; Ma Jianxiong

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Repeated exposure to heat stress may adversely affect reproductive health, yet the population\-level relationship between heat stress and infertility has not been well characterized\. Methods We conducted a global ecological analysis across 174 countries and regions from 1990 to 2021\. Heat exposure was quantified as the annual number of days falling into four Universal Thermal Climate Index categories: moderate, strong, very strong, and extreme heat stress\. We additionally constructed a weighted score combining very strong and extreme heat stress\. Associations with male and female infertility prevalence were estimated using linear mixed\-effects models\. Results Each additional day of very strong and extreme heat stress was associated with increases in male infertility ASPR of 1\.292 \(95% confidence interval \[CI\]: 0\.675–1\.908\) and 1\.654 \(95% CI: 0\.214–3\.096\) per 100,000 population, respectively\. The corresponding increases in female infertility ASPR were 2\.126 \(95% CI: 0\.785–3\.467\) and 7\.133 \(95% CI: 3\.940–10\.323\) per 100,000 population, respectively\. Higher weighted heat\-stress scores were also consistently associated with greater infertility prevalence\. Conclusions These findings suggest that recurrent heat\-stress exposure may be associated with a higher population burden of infertility and support consideration of reproductive health in climate\-change adaptation and heat\-protection strategies\.
