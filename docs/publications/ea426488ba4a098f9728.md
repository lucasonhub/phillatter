# RELAY: Selective Self\-Distillation with Adaptive Hand\-off for Rubric\-Based Reinforcement Learning

**Autores:** Kunwang Zheng; Xinyu Sun; Yu Cao; Shuocheng Li; Lei Cheng; Ying Liu; Qilong Zhang; Jinlong Chen; Hua Yang; Qianglong Chen

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Rubric\-based reinforcement learning grades a response against many written criteria but collapses them into one reward, which discards the detail the rubric carries and gives no GRPO reward gradient when a group&\#x27;s responses tie\. A frozen teacher that reads the rubric as privileged context provides token\-level supervision, but we observe an early plateau in the gains over GRPO\. RELAY uses advantages and rewards to select positive\-advantage and low\-scoring zero\-advantage rollouts, and monitors the distillation loss to decide when to retire the teacher\. Stage 1 combines selective distillation from a frozen teacher with GRPO; a plateau detector then removes the teacher, and Stage 2 continues with GRPO alone\. Calibrated on one model\-domain pair and reused unchanged in three others, the plateau detector fires between steps 122 and 171\. RELAY scores highest in\-domain among the compared methods in all four settings, ahead of GRPO on RaR\-Medicine by 4\.62 points with Qwen3\-1\.7B and 6\.02 with Qwen3\-4B\. On Qwen3\-1\.7B/Medicine, removing the teacher from a shared checkpoint outperforms keeping, weakening or refreshing it\. Distilling selected rollouts covering about half the response tokens also outperforms distilling all rollouts at a matched average coefficient\.
