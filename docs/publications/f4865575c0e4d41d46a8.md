# Governance quality and the renewable\-electricity transition: evidence from a 195\-country panel, 2000–2023

**Autores:** Simon Baradziej

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Whether the quality of a country’s institutions shapes how fast it decarbonizes its power sector remains contested, and much of the evidence comes from single regions or now\-dated samples\. This study links the six World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators to the renewable share of electricity generation for 195 countries over 2000–2023 \(4,359 country\-years\), drawing renewable data from Our World in Data and Ember, and it separates cross\-country differences from within\-country change\. The unadjusted association is negative, because low\-income, hydropower\-rich countries report high renewable shares\. Conditioning on income turns the association positive across countries, near 13 percentage points per one\-unit rise in the governance index, but that cross\-country association is fragile: it falls by about a quarter and loses significance once natural\-resource rents and region fixed effects are added \(9\.99, 95% CI \[−1\.47, 21\.46\]\)\. Within countries, two\-way fixed\-effects estimates show no association on average \(−1\.6, p = 0\.50\)\. The six dimensions are effectively one factor \(the first principal component absorbs 87% of their variance\), so no single dimension can be credibly singled out\. A within\-country interaction suggests the governance slope rose over time \(about 0\.39 percentage points per year in the baseline model\), but this pattern does not survive allowing each country its own linear renewable trend \(0\.005, p = 0\.98\) or a first\-difference specification, so it cannot be read as a genuine strengthening of the governance effect\. Taken together, the panel provides little robust evidence that governance quality drives the electricity transition: the cross\-country association is confounded by endowments, the within\-country association is null on average, and the apparent temporal strengthening is sensitive to differential country trends\. The estimates are associational\.
