# Classification of diamond color grades based on portable 785 nm Raman spectroscopy

**Autores:** Kang Ma; Guoqiang Fang; Kai Hu; Wuliji Hasi

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Visual color grading of Ib\-type HPHT lab\-grown diamonds is subjective and poorly repeatable, because adjacent\-grade color differences in the colorless to near\-colorless range lie at the limit of visual resolution\. This work presents a rapid, non\-destructive method that couples portable 785 nm Raman spectroscopy with machine learning to discriminate D, E, F, G, and J diamond color grades\. Seven classification models were compared on the raw spectra, after training\-set augmentation, and after hyperparameter optimization\. The KNN model reached the best internal held\-out accuracy of 96\.0%\. External validation with a PCA\-KNN pipeline reached 92\.9% exact\-grade accuracy on 14 unknown HPHT diamonds, against 85\.7% for the PCA\-RF model; the only misclassification was a single E\-grade stone assigned to D\. Control experiments attribute this transferability to the projection rather than to the classifier: the full 671\-channel spectrum reproduced the 96\.0% internal accuracy but recovered only 8 of the 14 stones externally, whereas the two retained principal components \(77\.0% internally\) recovered 13 of 14\. Lorentzian analysis identified the band near 1354 cm⁻¹ as the diamond first\-order Raman line at 1332 cm⁻¹, with a systematic calibration offset of \+21\.9 ± 0\.4 cm⁻¹ and an FWHM of 7\.1 ± 0\.6 cm⁻¹\. The grade information is therefore carried by the intensity distribution of the Raman profile rather than by peak positions, and portable 785 nm Raman spectroscopy with a two\-component PCA\-KNN pipeline offers an auxiliary check on the visual grading of lab\-grown diamonds, although the present validation rests on a single reference stone per grade\.
