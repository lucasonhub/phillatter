# Measurement of contextual factors in clinical practice and research: development of an expert\-informed toolkit

**Autores:** Jennifer A\. Bent; Chad E\. Cook; Antoine Bailliard; Joel E\. Bialosky; Felicity L\. Bishop; Elisa Carlino; Luana Colloca; Jorge Eduardo Esteves; Alessandra Garcia; Amy W\. McDevitt; Vitaly Napadow; David Newell; Alvisa Palese; William R Reed; Giacomo Rossettini

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Contextual factors \(CFs\) can influence therapeutic encounters and clinical outcomes, yet their measurement remains fragmented, lacking a unified framework to guide tool selection\. To identify, appraise, and synthesize CF measurement instruments into an expert\-informed clinical toolkit and identify current measurement gaps\. An international panel of 15 experts evaluated 227 instruments across five domains \(patient/practitioner characteristics, relational dynamics, environment, and treatment\) using a hybrid COSMIN\-informed appraisal design\. Instruments were screened for content validity and psychometric robustness\. Twenty instruments were selected for inclusion in the toolkit\. Despite identifying high\-quality instruments for stable traits, our analysis revealed critical measurement gaps in dynamic, state\-responsive processes\. Specifically, there is an absence of validated, clinically feasible tools for capturing real\-time clinician affective states, non\-verbal synchrony, therapeutic touch, and moment\-to\-moment environmental or procedural interactions\. This study provides the first expert\-informed framework for assessing CFs\. Future research should prioritize developing state\-responsive instruments capable of capturing these dynamic elements as they unfold in real\-world settings to better understand the contextual drivers of recovery\.
