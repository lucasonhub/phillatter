# Farmers market as public quick commerce infrastructure: a conceptual policy framework for digital transformation

**Autores:** Arunachalam Panchatcharam SriVidhya; Paul J\. Mansingh

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid digitalization of food retail through private quick commerce platforms has transformed urban food consumption patterns in India\. Despite the investments in digital infrastructure, Uzhavar Sandhai \(Farmers Market\), a farmer\-to\-consumer direct marketing system in Tamil Nadu, is still limited to traditional physical markets\. This study suggests a strategic rethinking of Farmers Market as a public quick commerce infrastructure layer, incorporating digital marketplaces, hyperlocal logistics and cooperative governance to boost farmer market competitiveness and promote public value, food system resilience and equitable digital market access\. In this study, the Farmers Market Digital Platform model is developed by applying the conceptual policy design approach to convert the Farmers Market platform into a decentralized public quick commerce network\. This study identifies the institutional, technological and governance reforms necessary to support the digital transformation of farmer markets using publicly governed digital infrastructure\. This reorientation of Farmers Market to public digital infrastructure could form the policy roadmap for platformization of agriculture in Tamil Nadu, which would benefit small farmers, urban consumers, and municipalities and help alleviate inefficiencies in supply chains\. As a conceptual policy framework, the proposed model has not been empirically tested and requires future pilot implementation and validation before adoption\. The study contributes to the evolving literature on agricultural digitalization by offering a vision for the potential redesign of existing public agricultural markets as digitally enabled public service infrastructure, which can enhance farmer welfare and urban food system resilience\.
