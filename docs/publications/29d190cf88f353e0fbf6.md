# Jones, J\. A\.: The Architecture of Thinking: A Structural Model of Cognitive Activity

**Autores:**

## Descrição do feed

This paper introduces the Substrate\-independent Operator Model \(SOM\), a theoretical framework that explains cognitive activity as an emergent consequence of system organization rather than as a product of specific material substrates, symbolic representations, or task\-bound computational processes\. Building upon the broader Structural Identity Architecture \(SIA\), the model proposes the Thinking Space as a continuously active topological domain generated through the interaction of functional structural operators, including operator zones, fluid domains, crystalline regions, transition membranes, and resonance lines\. Within this framework, thoughts are not treated as stored symbolic entities or discrete computational objects\. Instead, they are modeled as transient emergent condensations arising within a dynamic activity landscape\. Cognitive processes emerge through the continuous interaction of structural operators that regulate stabilization, transformation, compatibility, persistence, and cross\-regional continuity\. The paper develops a formal account of attractor formation, resonance\-based stabilization, emergence\-window dynamics, task\-oriented overlays, and baseline self\-organization\. These mechanisms are used to reconstruct a range of cognitive phenomena, including sequential thought formation, creative ideation, flow states, rumination, hyperfluidity, and other dynamic cognitive transitions\. The resulting framework advances a substrate\-independent theory of cognition that relocates explanation from neural implementation and symbolic information processing to the organizational conditions necessary for the emergence and persistence of cognitive structure\. The paper further examines implications for synthetic cognition, neuromorphic systems, autonomous agents, and the development of postbiological forms of intelligence\. \(direct link \)
