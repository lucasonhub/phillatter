# Acute effects of bench press conditioning performed with different velocity\-loss thresholds on punch performance in elite adolescent boxers

**Autores:** Penglin Diao; Jinxuan Bao; Xiaoyu Liu; Zhaoqian Li; Yunsong Liu; Xundian Liu; Yayi Jiang; Yinkai Zhang; Ziyu Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Velocity\-loss \(VL\) thresholds may help regulate the balance between potentiation and fatigue during post\-activation performance enhancement protocols; however, their effects on boxing\-specific performance remain unclear\. This study investigated the acute effects of bench press conditioning regulated by different VL thresholds on straight\-punch performance in adolescent boxers\. Methods Eighteen male boxers \(17\.6 ± 0\.7 years\) completed four conditions in a randomized, counterbalanced crossover design: an active control condition \(CON; two sets of 10 bench press repetitions using a habitual warm\-up load below 30% one\-repetition maximum \[1RM\]\) and three VL conditions consisting of two sets of bench press at 85% 1RM terminated at 10% \(VL10%\), 20% \(VL20%\), or 30% \(VL30%\) velocity loss\. Lead\- and rear\-hand peak punch velocity \(PPV\), peak punch force \(PPF\), and an estimated peak punch\-power index \(PPP\) were assessed at baseline and 4, 8, 12, and 16 min after conditioning\. Outcomes were analyzed using linear mixed\-effects models, followed by Dunnett\-adjusted comparisons of each VL condition with CON\. Additional Tukey\-adjusted pairwise comparisons were performed among VL10%, VL20%, and VL30%\. Results Significant condition × time interactions were observed for rear\-hand PPV, lead\- and rear\-hand PPF, and lead\- and rear\-hand PPP \(all p ≤ 0\.046\), but not for lead\-hand PPV \( p = 0\.391\)\. At 8 and 12 min, VL10% and VL20% produced higher rear\-hand PPV, PPF, and PPP and higher lead\-hand PPP than CON \( p ≤ 0\.039; Hedges’ g = 0\.835–1\.267\)\. In contrast, VL30% produced lower rear\-hand PPV, lead\- and rear\-hand PPF, and rear\-hand PPP than CON at 4 min and remained lower for selected outcomes at 8–12 min \( p ≤ 0\.046; g = −2\.439 to −0\.814\)\. Direct VL comparisons showed no significant differences between VL10% and VL20%, whereas both conditions produced higher values than VL30% for several outcomes at selected time points \( p ≤ 0\.045\)\. Most between\-condition differences were no longer evident at 16 min\. Conclusion Under the present protocol in the 18 male adolescent boxers studied, VL10% and VL20% were associated with favorable differences in several punch\-performance outcomes after 8–12 min of recovery, whereas VL30% was associated with lower performance during the earlier recovery period\. Direct comparisons showed no significant differences between VL10% and VL20%, whereas both conditions produced higher values than VL30% for several outcomes at selected time points\. However, because repetition volume was not matched, these findings do not isolate the effect of VL threshold or establish an optimal threshold\.
