# Examining sources of variability in repeated testing of the rewards positivity and P300 ERP components

**Autores:** Katherine Boere; Mathew Rocha Hammerstrom; Jeff Hopkins; Isaac K\. Barss; Rae Dauphinee; Patrick J\. Montgomery; Olav E\. Krigolson

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Event\-related potentials \(ERPs\) provide millisecond\-resolution measurements of cognitive and affective processes, making them a foundational tool in human neuroscience research\. A clear understanding of their reliability, stability, and sensitivity is therefore essential for interpreting experimental results with confidence, particularly in multi\-task protocols where several ERPs are measured in sequence\. The present study examined three sources of variability for two widely used components, the P300 and the rewards positivity: test\-retest reliability, systematic amplitude changes across repeated measurements, and order effects\. Methods Twenty\-six adults completed five cognitive assessments distributed over approximately 2 h, with each assessment containing a visual oddball task and a two\-armed bandit task; task order was independently randomized within each assessment\. Reliability was quantified using intraclass correlation coefficients \(ICCs\), changes across repeated assessments were tested with linear mixed\-effects models, and order effects were evaluated using mixed\-effects models accounting for repeated observations within participants\. Results The P300 demonstrated excellent reliability across all five assessments \(ICC = 0\.91\)\. The rewards positivity showed a clear divergence between conditional and difference\-wave measures: the difference wave was moderately reliable \(ICC = 0\.52\), while the underlying win \(0\.92\) and loss \(0\.92\) waves demonstrated excellent reliability\. Rewards positivity amplitude showed no evidence of a linear change across assessments, whereas P300 amplitude increased systematically across the testing period\. No evidence of order effects was observed for either component, indicating that task order did not meaningfully influence ERP amplitude within sessions of this length\. Discussion Together, these findings characterize the reliability and time\-related variability of the P300 and rewards positivity during repeated within\-session testing and provide practical guidance for multi\-task EEG protocols\.
