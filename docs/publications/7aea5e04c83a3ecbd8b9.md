# Evaluation of Terminal Aerodrome Forecasts across Indian airports using key aviation meteorology parameters

**Autores:** Neeti Singh; Anoop Kumar Mishra; Sourav Adhikari; Gajendra Kumar; C\. S\. Tomar

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Terminal Aerodrome Forecasts \(TAFs\) are central to aviation meteorology, offering structured shortduration predictions of weather at aerodromes and nearby airspace\.In India, the four Meteorological Watch Offices \(MWOs\) at Delhi, Chennai, Mumbai, and Kolkata also serve as Aerodrome Meteorological Offices, issuing short\-term \(9 h\) and extended \(30 h\) TAFs\.The present study evaluates the Automated Aviation Forecast Verification System \(AFVS\) as an operational tool for real\-time, objective verification of TAFs across Indian MWOs\.Traditionally, TAF verification relied on manual evaluation, which was subjective, time\-intensive, and limited in scale\.AFVS replaces this with an automated, algorithm\-driven protocol aligned with International Civil Aviation Organization \(ICAO\) standards\.Over eight years \(2017\-2024\), AFVS was validated against Meteorological Aerodrome Report observations for six critical parameters: wind direction, wind speed, visibility, precipitation, cloud amount, and cloud base height\.Results show high forecast skill for precipitation \(93\.42%\), cloud amount \(99\.31%\), cloud base height \(98\.71%\), and wind speed \(88\.61%\), exceeding or approaching ICAO&\#x27;s 80% benchmark\.Visibility \(70\.20%\) and wind direction \(61\.89%\) showed lower accuracy due to their inherent variability\.The findings demonstrate AFVS as a robust and scalable verification framework that enhances reproducibility, efficiency, and standardised monitoring, while offering pathways to adaptive forecast improvements and stronger aviation safety systems\.
