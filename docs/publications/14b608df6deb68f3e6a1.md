# Diabetes‐Related Foot Disease as the Principal Reason for Hospital Admission: 5 Years of Data From a Major Tertiary Hospital in Aotearoa New Zealand

**Autores:** Michele Garrett; Rinki Murphy; Arier Chi\-Lun Lee; Peter Anthony Lazzarini; Timothy W Kenealy

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OBJECTIVE: To examine hospitalisations caused by diabetes\-related foot disease \(DFD\) at the largest tertiary hospital in Aotearoa New Zealand\. METHODS: We analysed hospital admission data for DFD\-caused hospitalisations from Auckland City Hospital \(ACH\), from 1 July 2014 to 30 June 2019, for people 15 years or older, using ICD10\-AM discharge codes and amputation procedure codes\. DFD\-caused hospitalisations were defined where DFD was the principal reason for admission\. Demographic variables and length of stay were also collected\. RESULTS: During the study period, out of the total 11,222 hospitalisations in persons with diabetes, 1605 \(14\.3%\) were DFD\-caused hospitalisations, which included 265 \(16\.5%\) minor amputations and 94 \(5\.9%\) major amputations\. There were a total of 71,033 hospital bed days in persons with diabetes, of which 16,992 \(23\.9%\) hospital bed days were due to DFD\. Median length of stay in days was 4 \(IQR: 1\-8\) for persons with diabetes; 7 \(3\-14\) for DFD\-caused hospitalisations, 13 \(8\-22\) for minors and 17 \(13\-26\) for major amputations\. The DFD\-caused hospitalisation rate per 1000 persons with diabetes increased by 86% from 6\.2 \(95% CI: 5\.2, 7\.2\) in 2014/15 to 11\.5 \(10\.2, 12\.8\) in 2018/19 and combined minor and major amputation rates increased 82% from 1\.1 \(0\.7, 1\.5\) to 2\.0 \(1\.4, 2\.5\)\. The rate of first DFD\-caused hospitalisation was highest for Māori at 7\.5 \(5\.8 9\.5\), followed by Pacific at 5\.3 \(4\.5, 6\.2\) and lowest for European/Other at 3\.7 \(3\.3, 4\.1\) per 1000 person\-years with diabetes\. CONCLUSION: DFD\-caused hospitalisations and amputations made up nearly a quarter of all hospital bed days used by people with diabetes and nearly doubled over 5 years\. The highest rates were found in adults of Māori and Pacific ethnicity\. Most DFD\-caused hospitalisations did not involve amputations \(78%\)\. The high and increasing burden of DFD\-caused hospitalisations suggest surveillance could monitor necessary changes to preventative foot care services\.
