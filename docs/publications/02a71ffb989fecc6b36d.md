# Integrated assessment of agricultural and processing\-related chemical hazards across the food chain: glyphosate occurrence and lipid changes in ready\-to\-eat corn\-based foods

**Autores:** Wendy Jannette Ascencio\-López; María Teresa Zayas\-Pérez; T\. Alexandra Ferreira; Ricardo Munguía\-Pérez; Guadalupe Virginia Nevárez‐Moorillón; Teresa Soledad Cid\-Pérez; Raúl Ávila\-Sosa

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Ready\-to\-eat foods may accumulate chemical hazards from both agricultural production and food processing\. These hazards converge in the same food but are usually evaluated separately\. This study assessed both types of hazard together in corn\-based foods consumed on a Mexican university campus, as an integrated food\-chain approach\. Methods Ten tortilla and ten tortilla chip samples were collected from campus cafeterias and food stalls\. They were screened for glyphosate by high\-performance liquid chromatography with diode\-array detection \(HPLC\-DAD\), as an indicator of agricultural chemical hazards\. Dietary exposure was estimated with a conservative upper\-bound scenario and expressed as a hazard quotient \(HQ\) relative to the acceptable daily intake \(ADI\)\. Six paired frying\-oil samples, collected before and after frying, were analyzed by Fourier\-transform infrared spectroscopy \(FTIR\) to estimate iodine value \(IV\) as an indicator of lipid unsaturation\. Results Glyphosate signals were detected in 8/10 tortilla and 10/10 tortilla chip samples \(90% overall\)\. Screening\-level concentrations ranged from 0\.107 to 1\.994 mg kg −1 \. Detected concentrations were above the Mexican maximum residue limit \(MRL\) for corn grain \(0\.1 mg kg −1 \) and below the Codex Alimentarius MRL for maize \(5 mg kg −1 \); both limits were used only as regulatory reference points\. HQ values were 0\.0573 for women and 0\.0511 for men, so estimated exposure was below the ADI under the assumed scenario\. IV decreased significantly after frying \(mean decrease 5\.00 units; 95% CI 1\.30–8\.71; p = 0\.0178\)\. Discussion Ready\-to\-eat corn\-based foods may carry both agricultural residues and processing\-related lipid changes\. HQ &lt; 1 does not imply absence of risk\. The frequent detection of glyphosate supports continued monitoring and confirmatory analysis by liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry \(LC–MS/MS\)\. This integrated approach can support food safety surveillance in institutional food environments\.
