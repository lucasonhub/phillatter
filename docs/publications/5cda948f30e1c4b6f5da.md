# Interdependent urban economies: a network analysis of socio\-economic relationships between street vendors and formal businesses in Ratnapura, Sri Lanka

**Autores:** Oshini Kularathne; Lakshika Meetiyagoda; Kokila Sooriyagoda; Nimesh Akalanka

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction The struggle between street vendors and authorities over access to public spaces in urban areas has drawn the attention of scholars for a long time\. In the case study of Ratnapura in Sri Lanka, we aim to make visible the social network infrastructure of street vendors with a focus on two major objectives: \(1\) Exploration of the nature of the relationship between street vendors and formal urban space, and \(2\) Evaluation of the importance of street vendors for the functions of formal urban spaces\. Methods The primary data collection methods were systematic observations and semi\-structured interviews with 30 participants selected through snowball sampling\. The collected data were used to develop activity maps by adapting Håkansson’s Economic Network Model \(ENM\) and Social Network Analysis \(SNA\)\. Results The results revealed four types of relationships among the street vendors and formal shop owners—direct, indirect, uni\-directional, and bi\-directional—based on the three dimensions of actor bonds, activity links, and resource ties\. The findings further demonstrate that street vendors interact directly with a wide range of actors and serve as bridges or intermediaries within the urban economic network\. Moreover, the results indicate that street vendors do not merely survive in public spaces; they also generate indirect benefits, provide essential services, and support the functioning of other actors within the formal urban system\. Discussion These findings contribute novel insights to social network analysis research by demonstrating the patterns of socio\-economic relationships between street vendors and formal urban actors\. Moreover, this research encourages urban planners and policymakers to recognize the contribution of street vendors to urban economic development\.
