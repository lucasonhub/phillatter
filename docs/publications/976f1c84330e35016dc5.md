# Disk winds from T Tauri stars \- physics and observables

**Autores:** Ana Inés Gómez de Castro

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Magnetically driven disk winds are fundamental to protoplanetary disk evolution, regulating angular momentum transport, stellar accretion, disk dispersal, and the environment in which planets form\. Magnetohydrodynamic \(MHD\) models provide the theoretical framework for understanding how large\-scale magnetic fields launch, accelerate, and collimate these outflows, while establishing direct links between the underlying physics and observable diagnostics\. This review presents an overview of analytical magnetized disk\-wind models developed primarily for T Tauri stars\. We summarize the physical foundations of magnetized outflows, from early magnetized wind theories to self\-consistent accretion–ejection solutions, emphasizing the roles of magnetic geometry, angular momentum extraction, magnetic lever arms, mass loading, and non\-ideal MHD effects in determining wind structure and efficiency\. We review the observational diagnostics predicted by analytical models, including spectroscopic, kinematic, thermo\-chemical, and molecular signatures that constrain the wind\-launching region, acceleration, collimation, and angular momentum transport\. We also briefly discuss how modern numerical simulations extend analytical theory by incorporating non\-ideal MHD, realistic thermodynamics, radiative transfer, chemistry, dust evolution, and time\-dependent processes that cannot be treated self\-consistently within the assumptions of analytical models\. By linking MHD theory, analytical solutions, observations, and numerical simulations, this review emphasizes the enduring importance of analytical disk\-wind models as the conceptual framework for interpreting magnetized outflows from protoplanetary disks and their role in the evolution of young planetary systems\.
