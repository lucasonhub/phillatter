# The illusion of outsourcing learning: A learner\-centered perspective on AI in education

**Autores:** Markus H\. Hefter

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Generative artificial intelligence \(genAI\) is rapidly transforming education, but not the cognitive mechanisms through which humans learn\. However, genAI’s effects on learning are often discussed in terms of technological capabilities rather than the cognitive processes through which learning occurs\. In this paper, I build on learner\-centered accounts for evaluating genAI in education\. Through an educational psychology and cognitive science lens, I argue that technologies can influence learning only insofar as they support or interfere with learners’ active cognitive processing\. This perspective is particularly important because genAI can increasingly perform cognitive operations on learners’ behalf, such as generating explanations, organizing information, and solving problems\. Such capabilities may indeed increase efficiency, but they may also deprive learners of cognitive activities essential for learning\. Therefore, the key question for genAI in education is not whether genAI improves learning, but whether the cognitive operations delegated to genAI are operations that learners need to perform themselves for the intended learning to occur\. The same genAI function may therefore support learning under one learning objective and undermine it under another\. Thus, performing better with genAI support should not be mistaken for learning that remains once the support is removed\. Moreover, explicit learning objectives do not capture all cognitive, metacognitive, motivational, social, or developmental opportunities afforded by an activity\. Educational psychology is uniquely positioned to determine which cognitive operations learners need to perform themselves and which may be delegated to genAI\. It can also help identify what may be lost when such operations are outsourced\.
