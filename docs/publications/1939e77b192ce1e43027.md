# Case Report: Cytomegalovirus\-associated granulomatous lung disease in an immunocompetent preterm infant

**Autores:** Yiping Zhou; Yaru Zhang; Rufang Zhang; Yucai Zhang; Yun Cui

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Granulomatous lung disease caused by cytomegalovirus \(CMV\) has not been described in immunocompetent infants\. We report a 5\-month\-old boy, born at 31 \+5 weeks of gestation, with a 2\-month history of isolated cough and normal growth\. Contrast\-enhanced chest computed tomography showed a multiloculated cystic mass in the right upper lobe\. CMV DNA was detected in whole blood \(1\.41 × 10 4 copies/mL\) and urine \(1\.44 × 10 6 copies/mL\)\. Intravenous ganciclovir for 2 weeks produced no radiological change; because a tumor could not be excluded, video\-assisted thoracoscopic right upper lobectomy was performed\. Histology showed extensive necrosis with well\-formed epithelioid granulomas and multinucleated giant cells\. Stains for acid\-fast bacilli and fungi and Epstein\-Barr virus\-encoded RNA in situ hybridization were negative\. To exclude an infectious granulomatous disease, metagenomic next\-generation sequencing \(mNGS\) of the resected tissue identified CMV at 1,143 reads per million as the only pathogen\. The postoperative course was uncomplicated, with no recurrence at 6 months\. CMV produced a localized granulomatous mass mimicking a congenital lung lesion or neoplasm, and tissue mNGS provided the decisive microbiological evidence\. When a preterm infant has an unexplained chronic pulmonary lesion, CMV should be considered, and mNGS of pathological tissue can establish the diagnosis\.
