# Remote Sensing Applications in Land Use, Land\-Use Change and Forestry \(LULUCF\)

**Autores:** 

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** book

## Resumo

This Special Issue highlights the evolving role of remote sensing in monitoring land use, land\-use change, and forestry \(LULUCF\), a critical component of greenhouse gas \(GHG\) accounting and climate policy\. As demands for transparent and spatially explicit reporting increase, Earth observation \(EO\) data—particularly from programmes such as Copernicus—are becoming central to national inventories and monitoring, reporting, and verification \(MRV\) systems\. The contributions demonstrate a clear shift from experimental research to operational applications\. High\-resolution satellite data \(e\.g\., Sentinel\-2\), cloud\-based platforms such as Google Earth Engine, and machine learning methods—especially Random Forest—are widely used to produce consistent, large\-scale land\-use classifications\. A recurring theme is the integration of multi\-source datasets, including spectral, topographic, and ancillary information, to improve accuracy and better represent complex land\-use categories\. The papers also emphasize validation, methodological harmonization, and alignment with LULUCF reporting frameworks\. Overall, this collection underlines the growing maturity and policy relevance of EO\-based approaches for robust, scalable, and transparent LULUCF monitoring\.
