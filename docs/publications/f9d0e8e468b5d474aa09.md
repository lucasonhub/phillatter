# The Significance of Gut Microbiome Health Axis: Exploring the Synbiotic Role of GOS and Lactobacillus Reuteri in Supporting Brain, Skeletal and Muscular Development

**Autores:** Naeyema Sadia; Khan Nahid; Shams Maksud; Khaleeda Akter; Shuma Riam; Shahana Begum; Rebecca Akhter; Saiful Alam

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The human gut microbiome is a complex ecological community that plays an important role in physiological development, immune maturation, metabolic homeostasis, and organ function, particularly during early life\. The first years after birth represent a critical developmental window when microbial colonization and dietary interactions can influence long\-term health\. Emerging evidence indicates that the gut microbiome extends beyond digestion and functions as a communication network connecting intestinal health with distant organs through the gut microbiome health axis\. This axis integrates microbial metabolites, immune signaling, endocrine regulation, and neural pathways to influence brain, skeletal, and muscular development\. Disruption of early\-life microbial balance has been associated with impaired gut comfort, altered immune responses, metabolic disturbances, and developmental challenges\. Synbiotics, defined as combinations of beneficial microorganisms and selectively utilized substrates that provide synergistic health effects, have gained attention as nutritional strategies for supporting healthy microbiome development\. Among these, galacto\-oligosaccharides \(GOS\), a well\-characterized prebiotic carbohydrate, and Lactobacillus reuteri, a probiotic species associated with gastrointestinal health, represent a promising combination\. GOS selectively stimulates beneficial bacteria, particularly Bifidobacterium species, supporting short\-chain fatty acid \(SCFA\) production, intestinal barrier integrity, and microbial diversity\. Meanwhile, L\. reuteri may contribute through competitive exclusion of potentially harmful microorganisms, immune modulation, enhancement of intestinal tolerance, and production of bioactive compounds that influence host physiology\. Recent randomized clinical evidence suggests that young\-child nutritional formulations containing L\. reuteri and GOS may modulate gut microbial composition and support musculoskeletal developmental parameters, highlighting the potential value of targeted microbiome interventions during early growth\. The gut\-brain axis is among the most extensively studied microbiome\-mediated pathways\. Microbial metabolites, including SCFAs, neurotransmitter\-related compounds, and immune mediators, may influence neuronal development, cognitive function, and neuroinflammatory regulation\. The gut\-bone axis illustrates how microbial metabolites can influence mineral absorption, osteoblast activity, vitamin metabolism, and skeletal remodeling\. Improved nutrient utilization and SCFA production may support calcium availability and help regulate inflammatory processes\. Emerging evidence also supports a gut\-muscle axis, through which microbial composition may influence muscle metabolism, nutrient utilization, protein synthesis, and physical development\. This review examines the gut microbiome health axis and the mechanistic role of the GOS and L\. reuteri synbiotic combination in supporting early\-life gut comfort and development, with particular emphasis on brain, skeletal, and muscular outcomes\.
