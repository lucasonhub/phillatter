# Genomic discovery and functional characterization of novel antimicrobial and anti\-Plasmodium peptides in the malaria vector Anopheles gambiae

**Autores:** Cairé Barreto; Mary Kefi; George Dimopoulos

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The innate immune system of Anopheles gambiae , the primary human malaria vector, utilizes a diverse array of factors and mechanisms to target Plasmodium parasites along with other microbes\. However, the current understanding of its antimicrobial peptide \(AMP\) defenses is limited to only a few canonical members, thereby underestimating the true complexity of the mosquito’s immune response\. To explore the breadth and functional specificity of An\. gambiae AMP\-mediated immunity, we conducted a comprehensive in silico genomic survey, identifying thirty\-six previously uncharacterized putative AMPs\. Transcriptional profiling revealed distinct developmental and tissue\-specific expression patterns, ranging from strict larval specificity to enrichment in the salivary glands and Malpighian tubules\. Functional characterization of synthetic peptides validated the four novel broad\-spectrum AMPs, Anolarvin, Konopsin, Sialicin, and Cryptophelin\. Notably, Konopsin was identified as an Immune Deficiency \(IMD\) pathway\-regulated effector enriched in Malpighian tubules, while the larval\-specific Anolarvin appeared essential for developmental homeostasis\. Mechanistically, these AMPs demonstrated significant diversity, with some acting as non\-lytic bacteriostatic agents and others functioning through membrane permeabilization\. Crucially, the salivary gland\-enriched AMPs Sialicin and Cryptophelin exhibited potent in vitro activity against Plasmodium falciparum sporozoites, reducing parasite viability by \~81% and \~75%, respectively\. These findings reveal a mechanistically diverse and spatially specialized mosquito AMP\-mediated immunity\. The discovery of endogenous AMPs with anti\-sporozoite activity provides promising new candidates for the development of malaria transmission\-blocking interventions\.
