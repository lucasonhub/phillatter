# Two dissociable axes of brain hierarchical dynamics track universal and population\-specific effects of mindfulness

**Autores:** Victor Oswald; David Zarka; Riku Johannes Ihalainen; Gulshan Kumar; Rikkert Hindriks; Auretta Sonia Kummar; Helen Correia; Hakuei Fujiyama; Saketh Malipeddi; Ravindra P Nagendra; Arun Sasidharan; Bindu M Kutty; Steven Laureys; Prejaas K\. B\. Tewarie

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

How mental training reshapes the temporal architecture of large\-scale brain dynamics and how these mechanisms differ between healthy and clinically vulnerable populations remains poorly understood\. Time\-averaged neural measures collapse the multi\-scale nature of brain activity, obscuring the dynamic processes through which behavioural interventions reorganise hierarchical brain function\. Here, applying a sliding\-window Temporal Irreversibility Index to source\-reconstructed EEG across three complementary cohorts \(n = 141\), we decompose hierarchical brain trajectories into three orthogonal temporal descriptors and uncover two dissociable, mechanistically interpretable axes of intervention\-induced plasticity\. Cumulative meditation practice drives a universal, pan\-cerebral reduction in the exploratory breadth of hierarchical dynamics, tracking dose\-dependently with lifetime hours of practice\. Temporal persistence is bidirectionally tuned by 8\-week mindfulness\-based interventions in a population\-specific way: reduced in healthy meditators but increased in trauma\-exposed individuals, revealing that comparable MBI protocols engage qualitatively distinct network mechanisms depending on baseline clinical status\. A joint Hidden Markov Model further links these dynamical shifts to distinct microstate substrates, and longitudinal changes selectively track improvements in a single facet of mindfulness, attentional presence in daily life\. Together, these findings establish a mechanistic framework for how behavioural training reorganises non\-equilibrium brain dynamics, with direct implications for tailoring mindfulness\-based interventions to individual clinical profiles\.
