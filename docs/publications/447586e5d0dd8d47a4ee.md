# FAREF: Exploring Consolidation and Acquisition Effects of Procedural Scaffolding in Argument Evaluation

**Autores:** Hori Rashid; Nawras Khudhur; Tola Abdulsattar Faraj; Tsukasa Hirashima

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Despite extensive research on critical thinking instruction, approaches for supporting the maintenance of procedural argument\-evaluation performance, particularly among struggling learners, remain underexplored\. This exploratory study evaluated the Formalization and Argument Reasoning Evaluation Framework \(FAREF\), a flowchart\-guided procedural scaffold integrating the Triangular Logic Model\. In a quasi\-experimental pretest\-posttest design, 29 undergraduates received FAREF instruction \( $$n = 15$$ \) or conventional instruction \( $$n = 14$$ \)\. An omnibus item\-level analysis yielded a significant three\-way interaction between condition, initial skill, and pre\-test response state, $$\\chi ^2\(1\) = 4\.08$$ , $$p =\.043$$ , consistent with a consolidation\-specific benefit for lower\-skill learners; the interaction was fragile to single cases \(leave\-one\-out range: $$p =\.024$$ –\.127\) and marginal when initial skill was modeled continuously \( $$p =\.053$$ \)\. No acquisition effect was detectable, and an equivalence test showed the study was insensitive to effects smaller than $$\\pm\\;0\.10$$ on the proportion scale\. The pre\-test performance clustered near the chance level of the binary format, bounding all inferences\. The findings are hypothesis\-generating and motivate a powered, randomized replication with a validated instrument\.
