# Osteopathic manipulative treatment as an adjunct therapy in a pediatric patient with chronic pancreatitis and visceral hyperalgesia: a case report

**Autores:** Avrohom Levy; Haili Bach; Fernando Rafael Medina Carbonell; Aniruddh Setya

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Chronic abdominal pain arising from visceral hyperalgesia is difficult to manage, and the use of osteopathic manipulative medicine in pancreatitis\-associated visceral hyperalgesia is rarely described\. This case adds to the literature by highlighting a potential integrative, mechanism\-informed approach for pain modulation in a complex pediatric patient\. Case A 17\-year\-old male with chronic pancreatitis in the setting of a heterozygous SPINK1 N34S \(c\.101A &gt; G; p\.Asn34Ser\) variant, presented with acute\-on\-chronic abdominal pain severe enough to require escalating opioid therapy; imaging demonstrated calcifications, edema, and peripancreatic fluid collections\. A multimodal treatment plan was initiated that included gabapentin, nortriptyline, and targeted osteopathic manipulative treatment\. A secretin\-enhanced MRCP obtained during a prior admission one month earlier demonstrated pancreatic calcifications, edema, and peripancreatic fluid collections\. During the current hospitalization, serum lipase decreased from 979 U/L on admission to 90 U/L by hospital day 3, when gabapentin, nortriptyline, and targeted osteopathic manipulative treatment were initiated as part of a multimodal treatment plan\. The patient had two sessions of osteopathic treatment and they were each associated with reductions in pain without adverse effects\. As\-needed opioid medications were discontinued by discharge as part of a multimodal therapy plan\. The patient remained pain\-free at one\-month follow\-up with minimal opioid use, suggesting a potential role for osteopathic manipulative medicine as part of a multimodal strategy in the treatment of chronic pancreatitis related pain\. At three\-month follow\-up, the patient reported pain rated 4/10 occurring several times per week and subsequently underwent total pancreatectomy with islet autotransplantation for definitive management\. Conclusion This case describes an association between OMT and short\-term pain improvement within a multimodal treatment strategy for chronic pancreatitis\-related visceral pain\. Although causality cannot be established from a single uncontrolled observation, the findings support further investigation of OMT as a potential adjunctive therapy in pediatric patients with chronic pancreatitis and secondary visceral hyperalgesia\.
