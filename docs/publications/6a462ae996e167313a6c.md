# Association between the triacylglycerol–glucose index, TyG\-BMI and hypertension in children and adolescents: a cross\-sectional study with machine\-learning\-based risk stratification

**Autores:** Jialiang Zhu; Chen Zhang; Jialin Sun; Huiyu Du; Wenxin Zhao; Gang Tian; Jingge Zhao; Dandan Tian; Mengwei Wang; Zhanfeng Liu; Min Liu; Yibin Hao

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Hypertension in children and adolescents is closely linked to obesity and metabolic disturbance\. The triacylglycerol–glucose \(TyG\) index and TyG\-BMI are surrogate markers of insulin resistance, but their associations with pediatric hypertension remain incompletely characterized\. Methods This cross\-sectional study included 25,886 children and adolescents aged 7–17 years from 18 prefecture\-level cities in Henan Province, China\. The TyG index was calculated as ln\[triglycerides \(mg/dL\) × fasting plasma glucose \(mg/dL\)/2\] and TyG\-BMI as TyG × BMI\. Hypertension was defined as a blood pressure at or above the 95th percentile for age, sex and height \(mean of the last two of three measurements\)\. Logistic regression \(Models I–IV\) estimated odds ratios \(ORs\) per 1 SD and across quartiles, with non\-linearity and effect modification assessed on both scales\. Eleven machine\-learning models were developed on a stratified 70:30 split, with imputation and feature selection performed strictly within the training set and discrimination \(area under the receiver\-operating characteristic curve, AUC\) evaluated in the held\-out validation set\. Results Among 25,886 participants, 1,798 \(6\.95%\) had hypertension\. Median TyG was 8\.05 \(IQR 7\.77–8\.35\) and median TyG\-BMI 154 \(IQR 133–180\)\. Each 1\-SD increment in TyG was associated with hypertension in all models \(Model IV 1\.306 \(95% CI 1\.145–1\.489\)\), and the association was graded across quartiles; TyG\-BMI showed stronger associations \(Model IV 2\.109 \(95% CI 1\.985–2\.241\)\)\. No multiplicative product term was significant after correction, whereas on the additive scale all six relative excess risk estimates were positive with confidence intervals excluding zero\. In the held\-out validation set, discrimination was moderate \(best AUC 0\.7544, 95% CI 0\.7302–0\.7777, for TyG and 0\.7534, 0\.7298–0\.7759, for TyG\-BMI\), and adding either index to the same baseline model already containing BMI increased discrimination only marginally \(about 0\.003 in AUC\)\. Conclusions Higher TyG and TyG\-BMI were associated with hypertension in Chinese children and adolescents, more strongly for TyG\-BMI\. No multiplicative interaction survived correction, but all additive\-scale estimates were positive, so effect modification cannot be excluded\. Machine\-learning models built from routinely available variables showed only moderate discrimination and remain hypothesis\-generating tools for risk stratification rather than validated prediction models\. Longitudinal studies are needed to assess causality\.
