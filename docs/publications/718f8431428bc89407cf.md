# Siderophores and siderophore\-associated metabolites from Antarctic Pseudomonas sp\. ANT\_H12B contribute to iron and phosphorus mobilization in soils with contrasting properties

**Autores:** Xiaomeng Chen; Marcin Musiałowski; Klaudia Dębiec\-Andrzejewska; Robert Stasiuk; Luisella Celi; Michela Schiavon; Maria Martin; Silvia Celletti

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Iron \(Fe\) oxy\(hydr\)oxides regulate soil Fe and phosphorus \(P\) cycling, but how bacterial siderophores and co\-produced metabolites mobilize these nutrients across pH remains unclear\. We hypothesized that siderophore\-mediated Fe complexation and P mobilization depend on pH and mineral crystallinity and that siderophore\-associated metabolites \(SAM\) are more effective than purified siderophores\. A purified pyoverdine\-like siderophore fraction from Antarctic Pseudomonas strain ANT\_H12B was chemically and spectroscopically characterized and evaluated for Fe³ \+ complexation from FeCl 3 between pH 5 and 9\. Iron mobilization from ferrihydrite \(Fh\), goethite \(Gt\), and hematite \(Hm\) was examined at pH 5\.5 and 8\.5 across mineral concentrations of 0\.5–2 g L⁻¹; purified siderophores and SAM were compared in four P\-deficient soils of contrasting pH\. Iron³ \+ complexation increased from 6% at pH 5 to 10% at pH 9, reflecting ligand deprotonation\. Ferrihydrite showed the greatest dissolution, although Fe release at pH 8\.5 was 30% and 37% lower than at pH 5\.5 at 1 and 1\.5 g L⁻¹, respectively\. Conversely, Gt dissolution at 2 g L⁻¹ was 40% higher at pH 8\.5, whereas Hm showed the lowest dissolution, reaching 3 and 1\.9 μM at pH 5\.5 and 8\.5, respectively\. Purified siderophores moderately increased Olsen\-P in neutral and high\-Fe alkaline soils, whereas SAM increased Olsen\-P approximately 1\- to 26\-fold across all soils, with the greatest effect in high\-Fe alkaline soil\. SAM also increased soluble P 11\- to 40\-fold and NaOH\-EDTA\-extractable P by 25–104% relative to controls\. The results support our hypothesis, showing that siderophores contribute to Fe and P mobilization but act more effectively within SAM, where organic acids provide complementary dissolution and sorption\-competition mechanisms\. This finding agrees with previous ANT\_H12B studies reporting improved nutrient availability and plant performance and supports using unpurified metabolite mixtures for sustainable P management\. Indeed, retaining the co\-produced mixture could reduce purification requirements and preserve complementary functions relevant to sustainable P management\.
