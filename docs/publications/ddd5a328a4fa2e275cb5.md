# Sinus tarsi adipose tissue as a neuro\-adipose niche in progressive collapsing foot deformity: neural innervation and stem cell properties

**Autores:** Antonio Mazzotti; Alberto Arceri; César de César Netto; Francesca Salamanna; Luca Cavazza; Gabriele Bilancia; Cesare Faldini; Gianluca Giavaresi; Francesca Veronesi

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose The adipose tissue of the sinus tarsi has traditionally been viewed as a passive structure\. Emerging evidence indicates it may function as a biologically active, innervated microenvironment contributing to proprioception and tissue homeostasis\. As a rich source of mesenchymal stem cells, it also holds regenerative potential\. However, its neural characteristics and stem cell properties remain underexplored\. Methods Adipose tissue samples were obtained from 20 patients undergoing surgery for symptomatic flatfoot deformity \(progressive collapsing foot deformity \- PCFD\) and stratified by age \(&lt;18 vs\. ≥ 18 years\) and sex\. Histology \(H&amp;E\) and immunohistochemistry \(S100, p75, PGP9\.5\) assessed neural elements, with quantitative analysis of immunopositive areas\. Adipose\-derived stem cells \(ADSCs\) were isolated and evaluated for viability \(Alamar Blue\) and osteogenic differentiation \(gene expression: RUNX2, ALPL, COL1A1, BGLAP\)\. Results Histology revealed preserved architecture with age\-related stromal expansion, more pronounced in males ≥18 years\. Neural markers are localized primarily to the stromal/vascular compartment, with p75 predominant across groups \(p &lt; 0\.0001\)\. Younger females showed highest neural marker expression; significant age\-associated decline in patients with PCFD occurred in S100 and p75\. ADSCs from younger females exhibited superior viability and early osteogenic marker expression, while aging correlated with reduced proliferation and a shift toward mature osteogenic phenotype, especially in males\. Conclusion The sinus tarsi harbors a dynamic neuro\-adipose niche with age\- and sex\-dependent neural and stem cell features\. This tissue may link proprioception with regenerative capacity and represents a promising autologous cell source for foot and ankle applications\.
