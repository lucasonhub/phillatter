# Positive\-unlabeled graph ranking for assembly\-center\-oriented resilience\-green sustainability evaluation of China Railway Express node cities

**Autores:** WanYu Wang; KeHong Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

China Railway Express \(CR Express\) has become an important rail\-based logistics corridor for improving Eurasian connectivity, regional openness, and sustainable freight organization\. However, evaluating its node cities remains challenging because assembly\-center labels are incomplete, city performance is network\-dependent, and logistics development is increasingly shaped by resilience and green\-sustainability requirements\. This study proposes a positive\-unlabeled graph ranking framework for CR Express node\-city evaluation\. The framework integrates statistical indicators, logistics operation variables, railway logistics relation structures, ecological governance indicators, and policy\-text semantic representations\. Existing assembly centers are treated as observed policy\-designation positive anchors, while other node cities are modeled as unlabeled samples rather than negative cases\. Accordingly, the model output is interpreted as relative assembly\-center\-oriented planning suitability under the observed operational, network, resilience, green\-sustainability, and policy characteristics rather than as a causal estimate or a probability of future official designation\. To reduce post\-treatment information leakage, both ex\-ante validation using 2011–2019 data and current assessment using 2011–2023 data are conducted\. Experiments on 33 CR Express node cities show that the proposed model achieves an NDCG@5 of 0\.932 ± 0\.025, with auxiliary AUC and macro\-F1 values of 0\.948 ± 0\.015 and 0\.875 ± 0\.021, respectively\. Changsha, Wuhan, Jinan, Shenyang, and Hefei are identified as unlabeled cities with relatively high model\-based planning suitability\. The results indicate that railway logistics node evaluation benefits from jointly modeling incomplete labels, composite logistics relations, operational resilience, policy semantics, and green\-sustainability constraints\.
