# Fishbone diagram strategy in algebraic word\-problem instruction: senior high school students’ achievement and perceptions

**Autores:** Asare Charles; Innocent Akpage; Daniel Aboagye; Isaac Davor

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Algebraic word problems continue to pose significant challenges for senior high school students due to difficulties in interpreting problem statements, identifying mathematical relationships, and applying appropriate solution strategies\. This study examined achievement differences between students taught algebraic word problems using the Fishbone Diagram Strategy and those taught using the conventional method and examined students’ perceptions of the strategy\. A quasi\-experimental pre\-test\-post\-test non\-equivalent control group design was employed with 128 Senior High School students, comprising 64 students in the experimental group and 64 students in the control group\. The experimental group received instruction using the Fishbone Diagram Strategy, while the control group was taught using the conventional method over six weeks\. Data were collected using an Algebraic Word Problems Achievement Test and a perception questionnaire\. Data were analysed using descriptive statistics, Mann–Whitney U tests, Wilcoxon Signed\-Rank tests, ANCOVA, and an ANCOVA applied to ranked scores as a sensitivity analysis\. After controlling for pre\-test achievement, ANCOVA revealed a statistically significant difference in adjusted post\-test achievement between the instructional groups, F\(1, 125\) = 44\.673, p &lt; \.001, partial η 2 = \.263, favouring the experimental group\. The findings suggest that the Fishbone Diagram Strategy is a promising learner\-centered instructional approach that was associated with higher achievement and positive learning experiences in algebraic word problem solving among the participating students\.
