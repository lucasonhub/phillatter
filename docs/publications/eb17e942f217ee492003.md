# Optimist Deep Research: An Iterative, State\-Centric Agent for Autonomous Literature Research

**Autores:** Gihan Panapitiya

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Most retrieval\-augmented systems treat literature search as a single retrieval step followed by summarization\. Real literature research works differently: the investigator&\#x27;s understanding of the question changes as they read, new subquestions appear, and evidence is weighed, not just collected\. We present Optimist Deep Research, an open\-source agent that runs literature research as an iterative evidence\-acquisition process over an explicit, mutable research state\. A research question is broken into a tree of subquestions, each served by search intents that are translated into source\-specific queries over arXiv, OpenAlex, Crossref, PubMed, Semantic Scholar and the web\. Results are merged into a canonical, provenance\-preserving document registry through a conservative four\-tier deduplication hierarchy\. The registry is expanded by citation snowballing until a saturation criterion is met, and then screened, ranked and allocated to subquestions round\-robin\. Documents are read in parallel into a structured evidence store of claims with support, location, type and strength\. A critic reviews the state along eight dimensions and returns research actions rather than a binary verdict: it can add, split, rephrase, deprioritize or close subquestions, so the research plan is an output of the investigation as well as an input\. The final report is written section by section from the evidence store, and every citation resolves to a retrieved document\. We describe the design, the deterministic machinery that keeps a language\-model pipeline auditable, and a case study that read 28 of 237 retrieved documents in about three and a half minutes\. We also report weaknesses the case study exposed\.
