# Machine learning\-based association analysis and cross\-sectional classification of physical exercise behaviors and mental health among vocational medical students

**Autores:** Saisai Tian; Shuai Meng

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Psychological distress is common among vocational medical students, yet how multidimensional exercise behavior relates to their mental health remains coarsely described\. This cross\-sectional study surveyed 1,127 students at three vocational medical colleges in China between September and November 2024, applying machine learning to two analytically separate tasks: describing association patterns between exercise profiles and psychological status, and classifying concurrent psychological risk\. Exercise behavior was measured with the IPAQ Short Form plus purpose\-written items on modality, motivation, social context and temporal regularity; psychological status with the GAD\-7, PHQ\-9, PSS\-10 and SWLS, combined into three ordered categories that are operational rather than diagnostic\. Random forest ranking, Apriori rule mining and K\-Means profiling agreed on three points: weekly frequency carried the most information among behavioral variables; frequent moderate\-intensity aerobic activity co\-occurred most often with low anxiety and depression; and four partially overlapping profiles emerged\. XGBoost classified best on an internal hold\-out test set of 226 students, one fifth of the full sample \(accuracy 0\.889, 95% CI 0\.842 to 0\.924; macro\-averaged AUC 0\.943\), yet reached only 0\.769 sensitivity for severe distress, and accuracy fell to 0\.858 under leave\-one\-college\-out validation\. Because the design is cross\-sectional and validation internal, these findings describe associations rather than supply a screening tool\.
