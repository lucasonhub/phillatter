Ditzingen: Reclam\. pp\. 281\-294\. 2026The chapter on &quot;AI and Afterlife&quot; explores the ethical and psychological implications of artificial intelligence \(AI\) in enabling digital forms of afterlife, where deceased individuals are &quot;resurrected&quot;, or continue to be &quot;present&quot;, through virtual avatars, chatbots, or other AI\-based simulations\. The article discusses case studies and asks whether such technologies aid in coping with grief or prolong emotional distress\. It emphasizes the need for ethical frameworks to govern these applications, as unregulated use may hinder healthy mourning by fostering an escapist &quot;parallel reality&quot; that denies the finality of death\. The rise of the so\-called Digital Afterlife Industry highlights the commercialization of AI\-generated posthumous interactions\. These services, trained on a deceased person&\#x27;s data, can produce eerily lifelike but unpredictable outputs that risk misrepresentation or emotional harm\. Philosophers like Daniel Dennett \(2023\) caution that such &quot;counterfeit people&quot; threaten human autonomy and truth, as AI\-generated personas may distort legacies or exploit vulnerabilities\. Against this background, the chapter examines religious and transhumanist perspectives\. While religions offer metaphysical comfort, transhumanism proposes technological transcendence, advocating for human enhancement or uploads of what seems like digital consciousness\. However, philosophers like Paula Boddington \(2025\) argue that disembodied existence remains speculative, as identity and agency are deeply tied to physicality\. In its conclusion the article questions whether digital afterlife technologies fulfill humanity&\#x27;s ancient desire for immortality or merely complicate grief and ethical boundaries\. It calls for critical reflection on how we wish to be remembered and whether AI should mediate that memory\. While these innovations may offer solace, they also demand rigorous ethical oversight to prevent exploitation, historical distortion, or the erosion of human dignity in the face of mortality\. \(direct link \)
