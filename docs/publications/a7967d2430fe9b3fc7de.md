# Concentration of injury and fatality harm at repeat\-active highway–rail grade crossings: A multi\-state permutation and machine learning analysis

**Autores:** Charles Luanga Ayimbila Akpakia; Obed Ayiku Tetteh; Emmanuel Anu Thompson; Haofei Guo; Wu Jinyu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Highway–rail grade crossing incidents remain a transportation safety concern, yet prior studies have focused mainly on crash occurrence, severity, and hotspot detection, leaving limited evidence on how injury\-or\-fatality harm concentrates at crossings with repeated incidents\. Using Federal Railroad Administration \(FRA\) Form 57 records from six U\.S\. states during 2013–2025, this study quantified repeat\-site harm concentration and evaluated leakage\-controlled surveillance ranking\. The final cohort comprised 8,286 incidents, of which 3,250 involved at least one injury or fatality\. Repeat\-active crossings, defined as crossings with at least two incidents, accounted for 56\.3% of documented harm and exceeded the randomized benchmark \(p = 0\.013; false\-discovery\-rate\-adjusted p = 0\.026\)\. The harm\-count Gini coefficient across all observed incident\-involved crossings was 0\.630, indicating an unequal distribution of documented harm\. Leakage\-controlled rolling\-origin histogram gradient boosting validation yielded a mean area under the precision–recall curve \(AUPRC\) of 0\.721, while unseen\-crossing evaluation yielded 0\.693; separate leave\-one\-state\-out logistic validation yielded 0\.674\. Conceptually, the study formalizes repeat\-site harm concentration as distinct from incident frequency and separates surveillance prioritization from causal safety mechanisms\. The framework supports repeat\-site screening and prioritization for further inspection and engineering assessment without implying causal infrastructure deficiencies or intervention effectiveness\.
