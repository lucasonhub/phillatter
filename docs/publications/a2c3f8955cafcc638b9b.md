# Air\-to\-Water Condensation Farming and Fogponics for &quot;Zero\-Watering&quot; Horticulture in India: A Review of Technologies, Crop Suitability and Readiness for El Niño\-Driven Water Scarcity

**Autores:** Jadala Shankaraswamy

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

India produced an estimated 369\.05 million tonnes of horticultural produce from 29\.48 million ha in 2024–25\. Its per\-capita water availability, however, has fallen to about 1,486 m³ \(2021\) and is projected to reach 1,367 m³ by 2031, below the 1,700 m³ water\-stress threshold\. Greenhouse warming is expected to strengthen the inverse El Niño–monsoon relationship\. Future El Niño years may therefore cause crop\-yield losses about 30% larger than those observed historically, and nearly 200 Indian districts have been identified as highly vulnerable in 2026\. This review examines two complementary pathways that decouple high\-value horticulture from rainfall and groundwater: \(i\) air\-to\-water condensation farming, in which atmospheric water generators \(AWGs\), sorbent\-based harvesters, dew condensers or fog collectors, preferably solar\-powered, supply condensate to the root zone of sheltered crops; and \(ii\) fogponics, an aeroponic variant that delivers nutrient solution as a 5–30 µm fog to suspended roots\. Published evidence shows that solar\-driven atmospheric water can irrigate leafy vegetables with growth comparable to tap water, that fog collectors can yield 2\.9–3\.3 L m⁻² day⁻¹ \(annual mean\) at favourable coastal sites, and that aeroponic seed\-potato systems are commercially established in India\. Specific energy demand nevertheless ranges from about 0\.02 kWh L⁻¹ in humid air to 3\.64 kWh L⁻¹ in very dry air, and levelized water costs of US$ 0\.09–0\.40 L⁻¹ have been reported\. These figures confine the approach, at present, to high\-value, small\-footprint protected cultivation, nurseries and propagation\. A crop\-suitability matrix for Indian fruits, vegetables and flowers, an agro\-climatic zoning framework, a sizing procedure and a research roadmap are proposed\. &quot;Zero watering&quot; is best understood as zero dependence on irrigation from surface or groundwater, not zero water input\. The technology can serve as climate\-insurance infrastructure for nurseries and protected horticulture during El Niño years\.
