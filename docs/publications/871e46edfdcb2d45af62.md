# Initial Development and Content Validity of an Occupation\-Focused Assessment: Occupations in Higher Education Transitions Inventory

**Autores:** Karen M\. Keptner; Rachel Rogers; Alyssa Chandler Lee; Daniel Martin

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

University students face occupational disruption during post\-secondary transitions, yet existing campus screening tools leave occupational challenges largely unassessed\. No comprehensive, occupation\-focused screening instrument exists for this population\. To describe the theoretical development and content validation of the Occupations in Higher Education Transitions Inventory \( OHETI \), a screening tool assessing university student adaptation across 10 domains of occupation grounded in the Theory of Occupational Adaptation \(OA\) a five\-phase iterative development process was used: \(a\) initial item generation; \(b\) preliminary expert review for face validity \( N = 3 occupational therapists with expertise in OA theory\); \(c\) tool reconceptualization based on expert feedback; \(d\) cognitive interviews with undergraduate students for face validity and comprehensibility \( N = 10\); and \(e\) formal content validation using a Content Validity Index \(CVI\) rating procedure with \( N = 6\) occupational therapy professionals with clinical and academic experience with emerging adult students\. Initial item generation yielded 68 items across seven OA constructs\. Expert feedback prompted a fundamental restructuring, producing 116 items across 10 occupations, covering aspects of relative mastery: satisfaction, effectiveness, and efficiency\. Revision based on student cognitive interviews resulted in 122 items\. Formal content validation produced I\-CVI scores ranging from 0\.667 to 1\.0, with S\-CVI scores of 0\.9516 for clarity and 0\.9435 for relevance\. Two items were removed, resulting in a final tool of 120 scorable items across 10 occupations\. The OHETI demonstrates strong content and face validity as a theoretically grounded, occupation\-focused screening tool for university students\. Online administration and individualized feedback distinguish it from existing instruments\. Psychometric validation is needed before broad implementation\.
