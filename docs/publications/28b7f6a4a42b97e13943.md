# Modern non\-invasive methods for the diagnosis of scoliosis in children

**Autores:** Andrey I\. Perepelkin; Е\. В\. Власова; Ahmed Ghulam

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Scoliosis is the most common orthopedic disease in childhood, affecting 3–5 % of children and adolescents\. Traditional radiography, despite its status as the “gold standard”, is associated with cumulative radiation exposure, which is particularly significant for the growing organism\. Over the past decades, numerous diagnostic methods that do not use ionizing radiation \(hereinafter referred to as radiation\-free\) have been developed, but their clinical application remains limited\. The aim of the study: To conduct a systematic analysis of modern non\-invasive methods for diagnosing scoliosis in children, evaluate their diagnostic accuracy, clinical effectiveness, advantages and limitations, and determine prospects for implementation in clinical practice\. Research methodology: An analysis of domestic and foreign literature from 1973 to 2025 was conducted\. Searches were performed in PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, IEEE Xplore, and eLibrary\.ru databases\. The review includes data from randomized controlled trials, systematic reviews, meta\-analyses, and clinical guidelines\. The results of the research: The review includes relevant sources covering the epidemiology, etiopathogenesis, and classification of scoliosis\. The main non\-invasive diagnostic methods are analyzed: low\-dose radiography \(EOS\), ultrasound \(Scolioscan\), surface topography \(including moiré topography, raster stereography, and computer optical topography\), 3D scanning, magnetic resonance imaging, as well as neurophysiological methods\. According to recent meta\-analyses, ultrasonography demonstrates the highest degree of correlation with radiography, while surface topography also shows consistently high agreement of results\. Conclusion: Modern methods that do not use ionizing radiation represent an effective alternative to traditional radiography, especially for screening examinations and dynamic monitoring\. Ultrasonography and surface topography are the most promising radiation\-free methods\. Further development of artificial intelligence technologies will improve the accuracy and reproducibility of these methods and expand their availability for widespread clinical practice\.
