# Does climate adaptation policy deliver a health dividend? Exposure, modeled health burden, and defensive capacity in China&\#x27;s climate\-adaptive city pilots

**Autores:** Cun Wang; Hantao Gong

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Climate policy is widely expected to yield a health dividend: cleaner air can improve health without a simultaneous expansion of defensive capacity\. We distinguish exposure reduction from defensive adaptation in China&\#x27;s 2017 climate\-adaptive city pilot, using a 2011–2022 panel of 264 cities, including 21 treated units\. A difference\-in\-differences design examines fine particulate matter \(PM2\.5\) exposure, while the Global Exposure Mortality Model translates exposure into modeled pollution\-related health burden\. Physicians, hospital beds, and employee\-insurance enrolment measure selected defensive inputs\. The pilot is associated with a 3\.87 μg/m 3 lower PM2\.5 \(95% confidence interval −6\.49 to −1\.25; wild\-bootstrap P = 0\.0067\)\. The estimate remains negative after pollution\-convergence adjustments but is smaller and imprecise in the matched sample\. The central accounting model implies an approximately 8\.10% lower burden \(conditional 95% confidence interval −11\.32 to −4\.76%\), not an observed mortality reduction\. Average capacity estimates are imprecise and cannot establish the absence of defensive adaptation\. A physician\-based coupling calibration admits positive and negative contributions and cannot bound the total defensive response\. Among nine pre\-policy capacity interactions, telecommunications intensity is positively associated with bed expansion \(\+4\.40 per 10,000 residents; pointwise 95% confidence interval 2\.47–6\.32; adjusted P = 0\.021\), although pre\-trend concerns limit causal interpretation\. Other interactions remain uncertain\. The main estimate is close to an illustrative detection threshold, warranting caution with non\-significant findings\. The results support an exposure\-related health co\-benefit under explicit accounting assumptions\. Assessment incentives, coordination, and financing are possible explanations for uneven responses, but their roles are not identified by these estimates\.
