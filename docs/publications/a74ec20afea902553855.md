# Tri\-axial shear performance of urban dredged silt stabilized with a biochar\-modified alkali\-activated slag binder

**Autores:** Kaiyan Wen; Yuhua Zhan; Qiang Feng; Mengting Wei; 汤智伟; Ziyao Wei; Yulong Zhao

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Urban dredged silt has a weak structure and poor water stability, which limit its direct infrastructure reuse\. In this study, we evaluate a biochar\-modified alkali\-activated slag \(BAS\) binder comprising corn\-straw biochar, ground granulated blast\-furnace slag \(GGBS\), and carbide slag\. Response surface methodology was used to assess the effects of binder composition on unconfined compressive strength \(UCS\)\. The numerical optimization module recommended 13\.26% GGBS, 8\.25% carbide slag, and 4\.30% biochar by total specimen mass\. The measured 28 days UCS of this combination was 6\.25 MPa, compared with the software\-reported prediction of 6\.32 MPa\. For the combined analysis, the normalized internal dry\-mass ratio was adopted as the BAS formulation for the consolidated un\-drained tri\-axial program at different binder dosages, water contents, and curing ages\. At 28 days and 18% water content, cohesion and friction angle were 169\.4 kPa and 38\.5 ° at 5% BAS and 195\.2 kPa and 42\.5 ° at 15% BAS, respectively\. Among the tested water contents, both parameters were the highest at 18%\. Their increments became small after 60 days of curing\. At 15% dosage, 18% water content, and 28 days, BAS gave c = 195\.2 kPa and ϕ = 42\.5 ° , whereas the cement comparison group gave c = 191\.7 kPa and ϕ = 42\.1 ° \. Relative to alkali\-activated slag without biochar and the lime comparison group, BAS cohesion was 16\.40% and 23\.62% higher, respectively\. The corresponding friction\-angle values were 12\.73% and 17\.40% higher, respectively\. Taken together, these findings position BAS as a promising clinker\-free strategy for valorizing industrial and agricultural by\-products through the sustainable stabilization and infrastructure reuse of urban dredged silt\.
