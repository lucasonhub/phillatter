# Case Report: An MDT\-guided multimodal protocol for large\-area stroke plateau — integrating neuromodulation with multimodal assessment

**Autores:** Xiao\-li Wu; Xiao\-qian Xia; Chang\-qing Ye; Yun\-lei Wang; Xing\-Zhu Li; Lixu Liu; Fu\-biao Hang; Jing\-song Liu; Yichuan Song; Yue Zhang; Xiao\-xia Du

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective This case report investigates the feasibility and potential value of an individualized precision rehabilitation protocol for large\-area stroke, integrating multimodal neuromodulation under the guidance of a multidisciplinary team \(MDT\) and dynamic assessment\. Methods A 38\-year\-old male with right hemiplegia and global aphasia following large left hemispheric infarction underwent an 8\-month MDT\-managed program\. A closed\-loop “assess\-decide\-intervene\-re\-assess” framework was implemented, featuring periodic multimodal evaluations \(clinical scales, DTI, fMRI, EEG, 3D gait analysis\)\. The intervention dynamically combined conventional therapy with staged neuromodulation: repetitive Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation \(rTMS\) for aphasia and intermittent Theta Burst Stimulation \(iTBS\) for motor disorder, Brain\-Computer Interface \(BCI\)\-robotic training and task\-oriented Virtual Reality \(VR\) for upper limb function, and biomechanical correction with custom insoles based on gait analysis\. Results Notable functional improvements were observed: LOTCA increased by 16 points, CRRCAE subscale improved by 60%–90%, FMA increased from 20 to 56, FMA\-B reached 12, and MBI improved from 40 to 91\. Neuroimaging revealed increased fractional anisotropy \(FA\) in the corpus callosum and right corticospinal tract, and elevated ALFF/ReHo in right frontal\-temporal areas\. Event\-related desynchronization \(ERD\) decreased during BCI training, and gait parameters improved\. Conclusion In this patient with large\-area stroke who showed limited response to conventional rehabilitation, an MDT\-guided, dynamically adjusted multimodal intervention was temporally associated with concurrent recovery across cognitive, language, motor, and daily living domains\. The concomitant neural changes suggest possible trans\-hemispheric network reorganization\. These observations are hypothesis\-generating and, while limited by the single\-case design, provide a preliminary rationale for future investigations into precision neurorehabilitation approaches in similar complex cases\. Clinical trial registration ChiCTR2300069203\.
