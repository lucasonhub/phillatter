# Moore, Terrence: Becoming an Ethical Self

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This is Chapter 1 of Noura: Justice, Flourishing, and Commonwealth\. The chapter argues that every theory of justice presupposes an image of the human person, and that the foundational question of political philosophy is therefore not what rules should govern already existing individuals but how a human being becomes an ethical self\. Drawing on research in philosophy, psychology, and neuroscience, the chapter defends a constructive\-enactive account of perception: perception is not passive reception but active construction, and the self is not a given but a continuously constructed narrative achievement\. The chapter defines the self as a cognitively constructed entity perceived to possess an identity ontologically distinct from other identifiable entities within a world co\-constructed through the same perceptual processes; the other as a cognitively constructed entity perceived to possess an identity ontologically distinct from the self; and otherness as the perception of another self whose ways of acting differ significantly and saliently from one&\#x27;s own\. Self and otherness are introduced as interdependent co\-arisings—neither reducible to the other, neither prior to the other\. In moral development, one&\#x27;s perception of otherness develops with one&\#x27;s perception of self; they arise together, develop together, and dissolve together\. The definitions describe the conventional stages; at peak development, neither self nor otherness persists\. Because the self is constructed, it can develop or stagnate\. Moral development is therefore the development of the self that reasons and cares—a process that unfolds through stages of increasing perceptual adequacy and expanding scope of care\. The chapter introduces flourishing as the integrated exercise of three core capacities—cognitive, agentive, and moral—and argues that freedom is not the absence of interference but the presence of informed agency\. It then shows why four structural conditions—Visibility, Symmetry, Contestability, and Repair—are necessary for interdependent human flourishing, and why institutional opacity is the political form of perceptual distortion\. The task of a just commonwealth is to create the conditions under which persons can become ethical selves—selves capable of perceiving interdependence, caring for others, and acting with informed agency\. The chapter serves as the foundation for the book&\#x27;s larger argument that justice is the study of the conditions under which persons develop into caring agents capable of flourishing together within an interdependent world\.
