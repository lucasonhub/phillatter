# From simulation to reality: Empirical validation of the superiority of da in indexing sensitivity in recognition\-memory experiments\.

**Autores:** Adva Levi; Caren M\. Rotello; Yonatan Goshen‐Gottstein

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The selection of an accuracy \(sensitivity\) measure is a pivotal decision faced by researchers who use binary judgments\.An abundance of measures has been developed to index sensitivity independent of participants&\#x27; bias\.However, recent Monte Carlo simulations ran in our lab found that all commonly used measures confound sensitivity with bias, with the exception of our proposed version of the signal\-detection measure d\-sub\-a \(denoted d a \), based on confidence ratings rather than binary responses\.Nevertheless, empirical confirmation in real\-world experiments is needed to validate any measure\.To this end, we ran two large\-scale recognition studies that can serve as a proof of concept to all binary tasks\.Sensitivity was not manipulated at encoding, while we manipulated bias at test\.When comparing these iso\-sensitive conditions, valid measures should yield erroneous significant results at approximately 5% \(or α\)\.Using resampling, we repeatedly ran the experiment to derive rates of Type\-I errors\.Only d a , but not P r , A′, or d′, demonstrated the desired 5% rate of Type\-I errors across different experimental designs, sample sizes, and number of trials\.We also used our data to assess statistical power\.We compared d a &\#x27;s ability to detect genuine sensitivity differences and found detection rates similar to all other measures tested\.Combined with our simulation results, these empirical findings provide compelling evidence that d a is a valid and powerful sensitivity measure\.Our findings offer a validated measure that can reduce the hazards of the measurement crisis that plague recognition\-memory research\.Our large data sets, available online, provide opportunities for future analyses\.
