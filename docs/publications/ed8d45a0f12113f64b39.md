# Hybrid ARIMA\-Prophet\-LSTM with SHAP Interpretation for Forecasting Outpatient Visits by Medical Specialty

**Autores:** Vlandimir Welem L; Sani Muhamad Isa

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Accurate forecasting of outpatient visits is important for hospital staffing, scheduling, facility utilization, and resource allocation\. Outpatient demand in the study data shows strong weekly seasonality and holiday\-related variation, creating challenges for both forecasting accuracy and model interpretation\. Objective: This study evaluates ARIMA, Prophet, LSTM, and four hybrid configurations for daily outpatient\-visit forecasting across five medical specialties and interprets the best\-performing hybrid model using SHAP\. Method: Historical daily outpatient\-visit data from Internal Medicine, Orthopedic and Traumatology Surgery, Cardiology, Pediatrics, and Neurology were modeled using ARIMA, Prophet, LSTM, ARIMA\-Prophet, ARIMA\-LSTM, Prophet\-LSTM, and ARIMA\-Prophet\-LSTM\. Time\-series cross\-validation with an expanding window was used, with MAE, RMSE, and MAPE as performance metrics\. Friedman and post hoc Nemenyi tests were applied for model comparison, and SHAP&\#x27;s GradientExplainer was used for the LSTM component of Prophet\-LSTM\. Results: Prophet achieved the best performance in four specialties, whereas Prophet\-LSTM was best in Neurology\. The triple hybrid was significantly worse than Prophet \(p = 0\.017\) and did not differ significantly from ARIMA\-Prophet \(p = 0\.9996\)\. SHAP identified day\-of\-week cyclical features as the most influential predictors across specialties, while national holidays showed low global importance but were associated with large errors in specific cases\. Conclusion: Greater model complexity did not improve forecasting accuracy in these data\. Prophet provided the strongest overall performance, while Prophet\-LSTM remained a competitive, interpretable hybrid alternative\.
