# Latent profiles of 24\-h movement behaviors and bone health at one\-year follow\-up in Chinese preschool children

**Autores:** Yuhan Li; Sen Cao

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Physical activity, sedentary behavior and sleep together occupy a fixed 24\-h day, yet their relation to bone health in early childhood has largely been examined one behavior at a time, or through adherence to separate guideline thresholds\. Person\-centred approaches that characterise the whole\-day composition have not been applied to bone health in preschool children\. Methods A total of 109 children \(mean age 4\.33 years; 44\.0% girls\) from three kindergartens in Changsha, China, were followed for 1 year\. Sedentary behavior, light and moderate\-to\-vigorous physical activity were measured by hip\-worn accelerometry over seven consecutive days, and sleep was reported daily by parents and recorded by class teachers including the weekend days\. The four behaviors were closed to 1,440 min/day, expressed as isometric log\-ratio coordinates, and summarised by latent profile analysis\. Bone health was indexed by the tibial speed of sound \(SOS\) from quantitative ultrasound, expressed as a z \-score for age, sex and ethnicity\. Covariates were related to profile membership by the bias\-adjusted three\-step approach, and profiles to the SOS z \-score by the Bolck–Croon–Hagenaars approach in baseline score and 1\-year follow\-up score models\. Results Three profiles were identified: Sedentary \( n = 31\), Low\-active, long\-sleeping \( n = 7\) and Active \( n = 71\)\. Older children were more likely to belong to the Active profile than to the Sedentary profile \(OR 2\.164 per year, 95% CI 1\.012 to 4\.630\), whereas sex, socioeconomic status score and body mass index z \-score were not associated with profile membership\. At baseline the SOS z \-score did not differ across profiles\. At 1\-year follow\-up, children in the Active profile scored lower than those in the Sedentary profile \(adjusted difference −0\.462, 95% CI −0\.774 to −0\.150, p = 0\.004, Holm\-adjusted p = 0\.016\), whereas the Low\-active, long\-sleeping profile did not differ \(−0\.256, 95% CI −0\.844 to 0\.332\)\. Conclusion An association between 24\-h movement profile membership and bone health was present at 1\-year follow\-up but not at baseline\. The whole\-day movement composition therefore merits consideration in bone health research and promotion in the preschool years\.
