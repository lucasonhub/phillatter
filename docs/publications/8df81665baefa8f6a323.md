# Pavement Texture Characterization and Skid\-Resistance Evaluation Methods: A Review

**Autores:** Chuan Chen; Haoyuan Luo; Wei Chen; Na Yue; Xiaoyi Ran; Jiekai Wu

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Pavement texture and skid resistance are central to road safety and surface maintenance decisions, yet the literature remains fragmented across texture acquisition, texture characterization, direct friction measurement, texture\-based prediction, and surface\-treatment applications\. Recent reviews have addressed several of these topics individually, while cross\-stage integration of method selection, evidence comparability, and surface\-layer service behavior remains limited\. This paper presents a structured narrative review of pavement texture characterization and skid\-resistance evaluation\. A corpus of 115 sources, including peer\-reviewed articles, theses, technical reports, and current standards, was organized around five linked stages: texture acquisition, texture characterization, direct friction measurement and harmonization, texture\-based friction prediction, and evaluation under functional surface layers or special service conditions\. The review explicitly distinguishes measured skid\-resistance quantities from indirect texture\-derived predictions and compares methods using a predefined ordinal rubric for accuracy/evidence, efficiency, cost accessibility, standardization, and interpretability\. The synthesis shows that standardized macrotexture measures and direct friction devices are mature for their intended outputs, whereas microtexture characterization, cross\-device harmonization, and model transferability remain less consistent\. Multiscale and contact\-effective texture descriptors generally provide stronger physical relevance than single depth indices, but their field standardization is limited\. Machine\-learning models improve predictive capability when sufficient data are available, although heterogeneous datasets and validation strategies constrain generalization\. Surface treatments and special environments further alter texture evolution and measurement transferability\. Five research priorities are therefore identified: uncertainty\-aware harmonization, multiscale contact\-effective descriptors, physics\-informed and transferable learning, synchronized network\-level sensing, and surface\-treatment\-specific durability protocols\.
