# Effects of forest therapy on cognitive and executive functions

**Autores:** Felipe Jerez\-Salas; Valentina Luksic\-Cataldo; Cristal Pavez\-Álvarez

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Forest therapy has been proposed as a non\-pharmacological intervention with potential benefits for cognitive functioning, particularly attentional and executive processes\. Objective: To systematically synthesize the available evidence on the cognitive effects of forest therapy in cognitively healthy adults\. Methods: A systematic review was conducted following PRISMA guidelines\. Experimental studies assessing objective cognitive outcomes after forest therapy interventions were included\. Results: Nine studies were included\. Preliminary positive trends were observed in attentional performance, autonomic relaxation, and psychological/affective states\. Evidence regarding processing speed and executive flexibility remains sparse and non\-significant\. Findings were constrained by high risk of bias, small samples, and heterogeneity\. Conclusion: Forest therapy provides preliminary, low\-certainty evidence for attentional recovery and emotional regulation within cognitive\-behavioral frameworks\. Current findings are hypothesis\-generating and require confirmation through rigorous, pre\-registered randomized trials\.
