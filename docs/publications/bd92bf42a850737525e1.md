# Radial Tiny Golden Angle UTE \(tyGA‐UTE\) for Cardiac MRI in the Small Animal Model

**Autores:** Hao Li; Patrick S\. Metze; Pengxi Han; Benzheng Wei; Volker Rasche

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Robust cardiorespiratory motion correction is critical for high\-quality ultra\-high\-field \(UHF\) preclinical cardiac magnetic resonance imaging \(MRI\)\. Whereas radial tiny golden angle \(tyGA\) sampling, data\-driven self\-gating \(sg\), compressed sensing \(CS\), and ultrashort echo time \(UTE\) are individually well\-established techniques, their integration into a unified acquisition and reconstruction pipeline, together with systematic validation at 11\.7 T remains insufficiently explored\. This study presents and validates a UHF\-optimized tyGA\-based UTE workflow that enables high\-temporal\-resolution sliding\-window dynamic imaging \(SW\-tyGA\-UTE\) and high\-spatial\-resolution cardiorespiratory self\-gated cine imaging \(Gated\-tyGA\-UTE\) from a single continuous acquired tyGA acquisition\. All experiments were performed on an 11\.7\-T small\-animal MRI system in 16 healthy wild\-type mice\. Cardiac functional parameters, image quality metrics \(signal\-to\-noise ratio \[SNR\] and contrast\-to\-noise ratio \[CNR\]\), and inter\-/intra\-observer reproducibility were systematically compared between the proposed tyGA\-UTE pipeline and two navigator\-gated reference techniques: retrospectively gated FLASH and UTE methodologies\. SW\-tyGA\-UTE and Gated\-tyGA\-UTE reconstructions yielded global left ventricular functional parameters comparable to those obtained with the reference methods \(all p &gt; 0\.05\)\. All functional metrics showed excellent intra\-observer \(ICC = 0\.94\-0\.99\) and inter\-observer \(ICC = 0\.90\-0\.98\) reproducibility, with ejection fraction showing the highest measurement consistency\. Image quality was evaluated using an image\-based within\-ROI noise quantification approach that captures both thermal noise and reconstruction\-related artifacts\. In this analysis, the Gated\-tyGA\-UTE protocol showed no statistically significant differences in left ventricular blood\-pool SNR, myocardial SNR, or CNR relative to the reference sequences\. In contrast, the high\-temporal\-resolution sliding\-window reconstruction showed significantly reduced image quality across all three metrics, consistent with the expected spatiotemporal trade\-off of undersampled dynamic imaging\. This study validates a robust, UHF\-optimized tyGA\-UTE imaging workflow that generates both high\-fidelity quantitative cine data and ultra\-fast dynamic cardiac images from a single acquisition\. The unified pipeline eliminates the need for sequential repeated scans, minimizes anesthesia\-related physiological variability, and improves experimental efficiency, providing a practical tool for preclinical cardiac phenotyping, longitudinal studies, and drug screening\.
