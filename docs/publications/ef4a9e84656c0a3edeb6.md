# Through the Kaleidoscopic Lens of Art: a STEM education pathway within Italy’s PCTO framework

**Autores:** Rosa Coluzzi; Vito Imbrenda; Licia Fanti; Wanda Traino; Massimo De Carlo; Vincenzo Camardelli; Andrea Smilzo; Michele Cordisco; Giovanna Limone; Lorenzo Amato; Giuseppe Calamita; Emanuele Ciancia; Ilaria Gandolfi; Angela Perrone; Salvatore Lucente; Valeria Giampaolo

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Interdisciplinary approaches can create opportunities for connecting scientific observation with artistic interpretation and environmental communication\. The “Kaleidoscopic Lens of Art: Imaging the Environment” project was implemented within Italy&\#x27;s PCTO \(Pathways for Transversal Skills and Career Guidance\) framework through collaboration between the Art High School “Mario Festa Campanile” \(Melfi\-Southern Italy\) and researchers from CNR\-IMIOT \(National Reserach Council of Italy \- Istituto di Metodologie Integrate per l&\#x27;Osservazione della Terra\)\. The pathway involved 27 third\-year high school students from the Figurative Arts, Architecture and Environment, and Audiovisual and Multimedia tracks and focused on the translation of environmental concepts, geospatial data, and satellite imagery into artistic forms\. The pathway combined scientific lectures, field and laboratory activities, visual analysis, conceptual re\-elaboration, and artistic production\. It is presented here as a descriptive, practice\-based case study, with attention to the transformations occurring between scientific source material and artistic representation\. The resulting works included mixed\-media paintings, architectural visualizations, digital photomontages, and audiovisual products addressing landscape transformation and human\-environment relationships\. The comparison across the three artistic tracks shows that shared scientific source materials were translated into distinct modes of artistic re\-elaboration: material and chromatic abstraction in Figurative Arts, spatial and scalar reinterpretation in Architecture and Environment, and digital and narrative re\-composition in Audiovisual and Multimedia\. This comparison suggests that artistic practices shape which features of scientific representations are selected and emphasized, and how their environmental meanings are expressed\. The study documents a science\-to\-art workflow that may inform the adaptation and systematic evaluation of similar interdisciplinary initiatives in other educational contexts\.
