# When civic duty feels personal: psychological ownership as a pathway to prosocial behavior

**Autores:** Rini Rahmawati; Asrid Juniar; Meiske Claudia; Muhamad Rusmin Nuryadin; Megi Nediawan; Liko Noor Rafianto Rahadhian; Muhammad Rayyan Adhiyani; Vina Tampi

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Tax compliance is increasingly understood as a form of prosocial behavior shaped by internal moral orientations and individuals’ psychological attachment to public institutions rather than solely by external enforcement mechanisms\. This study examines how civic moral obligation influences prosocial behavior through psychological ownership while considering the moderating role of institutional trust\. A quantitative survey design was employed, and data were collected from 180 individual taxpayers in Tapin Regency, South Kalimantan, Indonesia\. The data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling \(PLS\-SEM\)\. The results indicate that civic moral obligation positively affects both psychological ownership and prosocial behavior, suggesting that individuals who internalize tax compliance as a moral responsibility are more likely to develop a sense of ownership toward public institutions and engage in prosocial behavior\. Psychological ownership also significantly enhances prosocial behavior and partially mediates the relationship between civic moral obligation and prosocial behavior, highlighting its role as an important mechanism through which moral values are translated into action\. Furthermore, institutional trust strengthens the positive relationship between psychological ownership and prosocial behavior, indicating that higher levels of trust amplify the behavioral consequences of psychological attachment to public institutions\. These findings emphasize the importance of integrating moral, psychological, and institutional factors in promoting sustainable voluntary tax compliance\.
