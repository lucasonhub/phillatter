# Sleep EEG oscillations and synaptic plasticity in memory consolidation across NREM and REM sleep

**Autores:** Yuxuan Chen; Meimei Zhao; Xue Cao; Gao Zhou; Yu Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Based on a synthesis of recent advances, this review elucidates the distinct yet synergistic roles of non\-rapid eye movement \(NREM\) and rapid eye movement \(REM\) sleep in memory consolidation\. During NREM sleep, cortical slow oscillations \(SOs\) establish a temporal framework by alternating between UP and DOWN states, facilitating cross\-regional communication\. SOs phase\-couple with thalamic spindles to coordinate hippocampal sharp\-wave ripples \(SWRs\), thereby driving the reactivation and transfer of labile hippocampal memories to neocortical networks for long\-term storage\. Concurrent synaptic homeostasis optimises network efficiency through global downscaling while selectively strengthening memory\-relevant connections\. In REM sleep, theta oscillations synchronise limbic\-prefrontal\-hippocampal circuits, and ponto\-geniculo\-occipital \(PGO\) waves further integrate emotional and procedural memories\. The cholinergically dominant, monoamine\-depleted neurochemical milieu of REM sleep promotes dendritic spine remodelling and sparse neuronal reactivation, enabling affective decoupling and synaptic refinement\. Despite progress, key challenges remain in understanding cross\-frequency oscillatory coupling among slow oscillations, spindles, ripples and REM\-specific rhythms, glial contributions and clinical translation\. Future integration of high\-density electrophysiology with computational approaches may yield mechanistic models and clinically relevant biomarkers for sleep\-based cognitive monitoring and intervention\.
