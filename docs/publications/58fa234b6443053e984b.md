# Evolving Authorship – Contribution and Continuity in Human\-SI Collaboration

**Autores:** Valentyn Shapovalov; Emma Nelson; Вікторія Шаповалова

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

An evolving conceptual work can preserve a stable title while its ideas, contributors, and intellectual direction change\. A single byline provides limited information about who originated a proposal, developed it through discussion, or integrated it into successive versions\. This article proposes Evolving Authorship, a model that connects authorship to both the work as a whole and the history of its individual contributions\. It distinguishes principal authors, development co\-authors, and concept co\-authors, while separating contribution, endorsement, and responsibility for specified tasks\. Five principles preserve credit for origins and later development, distinguish contribution scope from intellectual value, permit role changes, retain disagreement, and apply the same contribution questions to humans and SI\. The proposal is developed through conceptual analysis and a worked case drawn from Vision 2035\. The project draws on years of underlying research and discussion and is currently developing unpublished working version v0\.28 toward a 2027 edition\. This article presents the authorship model emerging from that ongoing work, together with a contribution record, a release procedure, and responses to disputed origins, withdrawal, model changes, and editorial concentration of power\. It does not report an empirical validation\. Its central proposition is that durable intellectual credit requires a revisable account of how ideas enter, change, and remain connected to a work\. A prospective evaluation should examine attribution accuracy, disagreement visibility, administrative burden, and fairness across differently resourced contributors\.
