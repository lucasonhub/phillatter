# Molecular Docking Stufy Of Secondary Metabolites From Turmeric \(Curcuma longa\) As Potential MAO\-A Inhibitors For Antistress Therapy

**Autores:** Yulanda Annisa Syafa Fatmawati; Tatiana Siska Wardani; Siwi Hastuti

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Monoamine Oxidase A \(MAO\-A\) is a key enzyme in neurotransmitter metabolism, making its inhibition a strategic approach for antistress drug development\. Turmeric \(Curcuma longa\) secondary metabolites possess potential therapeutic properties as MAO\-A inhibitors\. This study aims to evaluate the potential of turmeric secondary metabolites as MAO\-A inhibitors through in silico molecular docking, pharmacokinetic profiling \(ADME\-Tox\), drug\-likeness evaluation, and subsequent structural modification of the lead compound\. A computational approach was employed using 70 secondary metabolites from Curcuma longa L\. Molecular docking was performed via PyRx\-AutoDock Vina, followed by ADME\-Tox and drug\-likeness evaluation using SwissADME, pkCSM, and Toxtree, while structural modification was designed using ChemDraw\. Demethylcurcumin emerged as the best candidate among seven top compounds, demonstrating a favorable binding affinity of \-9\.3 kcal/mol and an RMSD of 1\.001 Å\. Structural modification yielded a derivative, \(4E,6E\)\-1\-\(3,4\-dihydroxyphenyl\)\-7\-hydroxyocta\-4,6\-dien\-1\-one, which exhibited improved drug\-likeness, pharmacokinetic profiles, and toxicity parameters compared to the parent compound\. Demethylcurcumin shows strong potential as a natural MAO\-A inhibitor, and its structural derivative provides an enhanced pharmacological and safety profile for further antistress drug development\.
