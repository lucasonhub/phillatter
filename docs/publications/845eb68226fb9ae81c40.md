# Additive manufacturing of nuclear materials under irradiation extremes: thermal\-mechanical performance, qualification challenges, and future research directions

**Autores:** Irfan Nazir Wani

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This review examines additive manufacturing \(AM\) of nuclear materials\. Three core advantages of AM for nuclear reactor components are identified: high geometric complexity, short lead times, and tunable microstructures\. Five mainstream AM technologies are reviewed: laser powder bed fusion \(LPBF\), directed energy deposition \(DED\), electron beam powder bed fusion \(EB\-PBF\), wire arc additive manufacturing \(WAAM\), and binder jetting\. Focusing on four material classes \(austenitic and ferritic\-martensitic steels, oxide dispersion\-strengthened \(ODS\) alloys, refractory metals and plasma\-facing materials, and silicon carbide composites\), the review establishes process–structure–property–performance \(PSPP\) relationships\. The core emphasis is on thermomechanical behavior under extreme operating conditions: temperatures up to 1,000 °C \(Generation IV reactors\), transient heat fluxes exceeding 10 MW m \-2 \(fusion devices\), and neutron irradiation up to 200 displacements per atom \(dpa\)\. AM\-specific irradiation failure mechanisms are analyzed\. Four critical unresolved problems are identified: \(i\) thermal stability of AM\-specific dislocation structures under service conditions, \(ii\) conflicting reports on irradiation swelling resistance between AM and wrought alloys, \(iii\) absence of standardized qualification pathways, and \(iv\) limited neutron irradiation data, with existing data restricted to doses below 10 dpa\. Finally, the review highlights knowledge gaps concerning synergistic effects among irradiation, corrosion, and thermomechanical loading and proposes prioritized directions for future research\.
