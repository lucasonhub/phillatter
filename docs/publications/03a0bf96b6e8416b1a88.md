# “It Works, but I Don’t Know Why”: Uncritical Use, Verification Behavior and Perceived Understanding in Non\-CS Students’ Generative AI\-Assisted Programming Projects

**Autores:** YeEun Jang; Jung\-In Choi

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Generative artificial intelligence \(GenAI\) enables students with little programming experience to produce working software, raising the concern that learners may build artifacts they cannot explain\. This convergent mixed\-methods study examined how uncritical GenAI use and verification behavior \(VB\) relate to novices’ self\-reported understanding of their own AI\-assisted projects\. Participants were undergraduates from predominantly non\-computing majors in two offerings of a general\-education Python course; 64 provided complete survey data and 68 written reflections were thematically analyzed\. Uncritical use \(UR\) was associated with a larger self\-reported product–understanding gap \(GAP; r = 0\.43, p &lt; 0\.001\) but showed little association with perceived understanding \(PU\), whereas VB was associated with PU \(r = 0\.45, p &lt; 0\.001\) but showed little association with the gap\. Tests of dependent correlations confirmed that these associations differed\. In the reflections, execution\-based checking was the most commonly reported verification strategy \(47 of 68\), whereas comprehension\-oriented strategies were less common\. Because all measures were self\-reported and cross\-sectional, the findings are correlational; they suggest that instruction should explicitly scaffold comprehension\-oriented verification in AI\-integrated introductory programming\.
