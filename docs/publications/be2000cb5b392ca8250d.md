# Attentional differences in autism – the Priority Monotropism account

**Autores:** Heather May Annan

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

I will introduce the heterogeneity problem and explain that it poses a serious threat to essentialist theories of autism\. I will explain that the heterogeneity problem of autism has two prongs\. The first prong of the heterogeneity problem is that autism is associated with a wide range of seemingly disparate symptoms and behaviours that do not share a clear conceptual link\. The second prong of the heterogeneity problem is that there is individual variance between the symptom profiles of autistic individuals\. I will introduce the following essentialist theories of autism: Weak Central Coherence theory, Theory of Mind theory, Enhanced Perceptual Functioning theory, and Executive Dysfunction theory\. I will demonstrate that these theories are unable to overcome the heterogeneity problem\. I will then introduce a further essentialist theory of autism—Monotropism\. I will show that while Monotropism has the potential to overcome the heterogeneity problem, it is insufficient in its current form\. I will claim that this is largely due to the lack of a clear definition of key terms, such as ‘attention’ and ‘interest’\. This is especially problematic since this theory proposes that the core essence of autism is a difference in attentional style preference\.
