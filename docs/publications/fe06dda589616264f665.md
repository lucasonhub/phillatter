# Taiwanese Bankruptcy Prediction with LASSO and Default\-Put\-Inspired Random Forests

**Autores:** Zhuo Ren

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Bankruptcy prediction is a critical but unusually demanding classification problem because failed firms are rare and the cost of a missing is often larger than the cost of a false alarm\. This study examines 6,819 Taiwanese firm observations described by 95 financial indicators, of which 220 \(3\.23%\) are labeled bankrupt\. Using a stratified 60%/20%/20% split, this study applies LASSO to select 25 accounting variables and compares supervised, ensemble, and anomaly\-detection models under a recall\-focused measure framework\. A repeated LASSO filtering is applied and takes five additional structural\-credit features into consideration, including an American default\-put value approximated by a neural\-network surrogate trained on least\-squares Monte Carlo prices and an analytical terminal\-shortfall probability; 25 total predictors are again retained\. Among baseline models, random forest provides the strongest AUC\-precision balance \(AUC 0\.9548; precision 0\.3971; recall 0\.6136\)\. The refitted credit proxy\-augmented random forest without SMOTE raises average precision from 0\.3451 to 0\.3790 without changing recall\. Its SMOTE variant produces an AUC of 0\.9574, average precision of 0\.4198, and recall of 0\.6364\. In conclusion, findings of this paper demonstrate a superior performance for ensemble models \(e\.g\. Random Forest\) and an efficient feature engineering framework which can be further extended and integrated under structural credit theory\.
