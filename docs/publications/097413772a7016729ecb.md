# Problematic social media use and academic procrastination: a review of theories and boundary conditions

**Autores:** Chenan Lu

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Problematic Social Media Use \(PSMU\) is associated with academic procrastination, but this association is not universal, and the conditions under which it holds have not been systematically examined\. This narrative review organises the empirical evidence by boundary condition rather than by theoretical account, drawing on Temporal Motivation Theory, a control\-theory account of self\-regulatory failure, and the I\-PACE model\. Studies were included only if they measured social media use as the exposure, academic procrastination as the outcome, and the proposed condition as a mediator or a moderator; a condition was retained only if at least three studies met these criteria\. Three conditions qualified: low self\-control, gender, and low grit\. All three are characteristics of the person rather than of the situation, and no situational condition met the criteria\. Identifying who is vulnerable would allow academic support to be targeted at the capacities that determine whether use becomes academic procrastination, rather than offered as general advice about reducing screen time\.
