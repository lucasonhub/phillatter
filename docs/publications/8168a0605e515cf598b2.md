# The potential for habitat support structures to address habitat deficits in a declining marine ecosystem

**Autores:** Hanna Koch; William C\. Sharp; John H\. Hunt

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Concurrent local and global stressors are rapidly degrading marine ecosystems\. Consequences of this degradation include widespread reductions in the availability, quality, and structural complexity of habitats\. For example, coral reef habitats across the Western Atlantic/Caribbean are flattening and becoming more structurally homogeneous as warming waters, severe bleaching events, unprecedented disease outbreaks, deteriorating water quality, and hurricanes continue to drive stony coral mass mortality\. This is problematic because the physical structure of a habitat profoundly influences its associated biodiversity and ecosystem functioning\. Structurally complex habitats support greater species richness and abundance as they have more microhabitats and ecological niches available for life history functions that support survival and reproduction\. Conversely, habitat loss and degradation can drive reductions in the abundance and diversity of organisms, which negatively impacts the health of our ecosystems, as well as the goods and services they provide\. While active restoration of species that create biogenic structure is underway, efforts may be slow to be effectively upscaled, and sources of environmental decline and mortality continue unabated\. Complementary or synergistic management interventions involving the deployment of habitat support structures designed to maximize ecological function, promote biodiversity, and instantaneously increase relief and complexity may ameliorate certain habitat deficits related to fish and invertebrate life histories\. Improving habitat availability and quality may in turn improve conditions and resources in an area, thereby strengthening ecosystem resilience and functioning\. Considering the configuration of habitat support structures to support species’ ontogenetic movements may provide further ecosystem benefits by promoting persistence across multiple life stages and therefore production\. Herein, we provide a perspective on the potential utility of such structures to mitigate habitat deficits in the Florida Keys \(U\.S\.A\), using nearshore hardbottom and offshore coral reef habitats as examples\.
