# Household\-Context Adverse Childhood Experiences and Co\-occurring Psychiatric Diagnoses and Symptom Severity Among US Adolescents

**Autores:** Yasin Çalışkan; Abas Haşimoğlu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: Adverse childhood experiences \(ACEs\) are a well\-established risk factor for psychiatric disorders; yet, most research has focused on one diagnosis at a time\. The association between household\-context ACE burden and both the number and severity of co\-occurring disorders was examined in a national sample of US adolescents\. Methods: Using data from the 2024 National Survey of Children’s Health, 20,003 adolescents aged 12–17 years were analyzed\. A cumulative ACE score was grouped as none, 1–2, or 3 or more\. The primary outcome was the number of co\-occurring diagnoses across 4 core conditions—depression, anxiety, behavioral/conduct disorder, and attention\-deficit/hyperactivity disorder \(ADHD\)—analyzed with design\-based ordinal logistic regression; symptom severity within each diagnosis was a secondary outcome\. All models accounted for the survey design and were adjusted for age, sex, race, family income\-to\-poverty ratio, and caregiver mental health\. Results: Adverse childhood experience burden showed a graded association with comorbidity burden\. Relative to adolescents with no ACEs, the adjusted odds of a higher comorbidity category were 1\.65 \(95% CI, 1\.45–1\.89\) among those with 1–2 ACEs and 3\.34 \(95% CI, 2\.76–4\.05\) among those with ≥3 ACEs\. Each additional ACE was associated with greater symptom severity for depression, anxiety, and ADHD \(adjusted odds ratio range, 1\.16–1\.22\), though not for behavioral/conduct disorder\. A binary sensitivity analysis gave consistent results\. Conclusion: Among adolescents, a greater ACE burden was associated with a broader and more severe burden of psychopathology across diagnoses rather than being concentrated in a single disorder\. Adolescents with a high ACE burden may represent a higher\-risk clinical profile warranting broader assessment\.
