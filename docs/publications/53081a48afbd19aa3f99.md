# Algorithmic authority in Spain: relative deprivation, hierarchy, and support for AI rule

**Autores:** Mingji Liu

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial intelligence is increasingly used in public administration\. Whether citizens would grant it authority over decisions normally entrusted to representative institutions is a separate political question\. Using cross\-sectional data from a quota\-stratified Spanish survey, this article examines support for AI political authority\. Respondents evaluated three propositions: that AI should make important parliamentary decisions, that it should oversee lawmaking, and that AI\-based political decision\-making would reduce concerns about corruption\. We conceptualize endorsement of these propositions as bounded algorithmic exit: openness to relocating political judgment from representative institutions in specified domains\. The analysis combines descriptive estimates and multivariable models with an ordinal latent\-variable model estimated using WLSMV\. In the attention\-check\-passing sample \(N = 1,490\), support was uncommon: 15\.2% scored above the scale midpoint, and 5\.4% scored at least 5 on all three items\. Across full\-distribution models, support was most consistently associated with greater relative deprivation, stronger social dominance orientation, more right\-leaning ideology, and younger age\. The corruption proposition attracted more agreement than either proposition assigning AI direct political authority\. The study distinguishes AI as an administrative tool from AI as a political authority and connects support for AI rule to technocracy, social hierarchy, and democratic accountability\.
