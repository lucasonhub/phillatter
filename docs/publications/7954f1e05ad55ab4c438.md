# Divergent migratory strategies along the East Asian insect flyway: A trade‐off between flight power and atmospheric dependence

**Autores:** 高博雅 GAO Boya; Ye‐Chen Wang; Jun‐Sheng Zhu; Yi‐Yang Zhang; Chen Zhou; Beibei Wang; Don R\. Reynolds; Jason W\. Chapman; Yan Wu; Gao Hu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Mass migration within the East Asian insect flyway is a globally significant ecological phenomenon, yet how insects with different flight capabilities utilize divergent strategies to exploit the same wind system remains a critical puzzle\. We hypothesize that these strategies arise from a fundamental trade\-off between active trajectory control and strategic atmospheric exploitation\. To test this, we integrated long\-term \(11\-year\) field observations with atmospheric trajectory modeling to quantitatively assess the migratory performance of two species with disparate flight capacities: the oriental armyworm moth \(Mythimna separata\), a relatively powerful flier, and the brown planthopper \(Nilaparvata lugens\), a weak flier\. Our simulations reveal that the strong\-flying M\. separata, which initiates its northward migration from mid\-May, actively influences its trajectory, leveraging its self\-powered flight to overcome slower, less directionally stable winds\. In contrast, the weaker\-flying N\. lugens, which migrates about a month later, synchronizes its migration with a period of faster, more stable wind currents, allowing it to compensate for its limited flight power\. These findings provide a mechanistic demonstration of how a trade\-off between self\-propelled power and atmospheric selectivity shapes migratory success\. Ultimately, this study establishes that flight capacity is a key determinant of how migratory insects interact with their atmospheric environment, which in turn impacts their contrasting life histories within a shared flyway\.
