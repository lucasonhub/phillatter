# Jovanovic, Vladisav: What Is Consciousness?: Orientation, Self\-Modeling, and Burden\-Bearing Experience

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Consciousness remains theoretically contested, and no single functional or phenomenological description currently settles what consciousness is\. This paper develops a layered, human\-centered proposal rather than a universal definition\. The first layer concerns adaptive orientation: conscious experience helps organize salience, action, and flexible response in a changing world\. The second concerns situated self\-organization: experience is embodied and structured by ongoing interaction with an environment\. The third concerns identification with self\-models, roles, and narratives that can become psychologically costly to revise\. The fourth concerns self\-relevant correction: evidence can matter not only informationally but through loss, shame, conflict, grief, or changed action\. The fifth, called burden\-bearing witness, is presented as a mature human capacity to remain present to consequential information without reducing experience to either detached report or defensive closure\. These layers are not claimed to be necessary and sufficient conditions for phenomenal consciousness\. Evolutionary and active\-inference approaches provide neighboring accounts of orientation and flexible control, while contemporary theories of consciousness remain plural\. The comparison with artificial systems is therefore evidential rather than categorical: fluent self\-report does not establish consciousness, but the absence of the specifically human developmental arc described here does not establish non\-consciousness either\. Artificial consciousness should be assessed through theory\-derived indicators and converging evidence\.
