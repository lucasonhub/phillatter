# The design–stewardship disconnect in urban greening governance: a structured evidence synthesis

**Autores:** Xiaoyan Ye; Yian Xu; Jinyuan Tu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

While global investments in urban green infrastructure \(UGI\) are accelerating, the long\-term performance of these nature\-based solutions is increasingly compromised by a critical disconnect between initial design and post\-construction stewardship\. To unpack this governance blind spot, this study analyzes 62 peer\-reviewed publications\. Operating under a specific methodological boundary condition, we applied a threshold\-based selection criterion focusing exclusively on high\-impact \(Q1\) journals\. Consequently, this study is explicitly positioned as a structured synthesis of the mainstream theoretical discourse shaping current global UGI policy, rather than a globally exhaustive empirical census\. Records were coded across five critical dimensions: funding, governance, aesthetics, equity, and technology\. Our synthesis reveals frequent co\-occurrences of governance and funding deficits, particularly exacerbating technology gaps and spatial inequity\. Crucially, we identify a structural paradox: capital\-intensive designs frequently intersect with profound institutional and financial barriers, whereas naturalized designs recurrently face strong esthetic friction\. We conceptualize this phenomenon as the “CAPEX\-OPEX divide” in urban environmental governance\. Based on these systemic associations, we advance a comprehensive framework comprising three core stewardship propositions—administrative under\-resourcing, esthetic conflict, and unequal stewardship burdens\. This synthesis shifts the academic and policy focus from mere UGI creation to sustainable, equitable long\-term maintenance\.
