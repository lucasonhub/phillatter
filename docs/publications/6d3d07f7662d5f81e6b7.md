# STK39 is associated with endothelial dysfunction and inflammatory activation under hypoxic conditions in moyamoya disease

**Autores:** Rui Liang; Zhenyu Zhou; 張耀仁; Xun Ye; Youjia Tang; Shihao He; Yuanli Zhao

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective Moyamoya disease \(MMD\) is a cerebrovascular disease whose mechanism relatively unknown and can cause vascular occlusion and hyperplasia\. This study aimed to explore molecular heterogeneity associated with the expression of acetylation\-related genes in MMD and to identify candidate genes potentially involved in vascular endothelial dysfunction\. Methods Public MMD transcriptomic datasets were analyzed using a predefined acetylation\-regulatory gene set to identify expression clusters and prioritize cluster\-associated candidate genes\. Functional enrichment, immune\-infiltration, and predicted miRNA–mRNA network analyses were performed to characterize candidate\-associated molecular features\. Serum STK39 and MTMR12 concentrations were assessed by ELISA in a preliminary study of three patients with MMD and three healthy controls\. STK39 was further investigated in human brain microvascular endothelial cells \(HBMECs\) using siRNA\-mediated knockdown under normoxic and hypoxic conditions, with Trichostatin A \(TSA\) treatment included as an exploratory pharmacological intervention\. Results Consensus clustering was used to classify MMD into two subtypes, and five key subtype\-related genes were identified: STK39, NEO1, PKNOX1, GOLT1B, and MTMR12\. In the validation cohort, serum STK39 and MTMR12 levels were significantly decreased in patients with MMD\. In vitro , STK39 knockdown promoted HBMEC migration, inhibited proliferation, and increased the expression of HIF\-1α, p\-STAT3, cleaved Caspase\-3, IL\-6, CCL2, and VCAM\-1 under hypoxic conditions, whereas TSA treatment partially reversed these changes\. Conclusion Acetylation\-related genes are closely associated with the molecular heterogeneity of MMD\. Among them, STK39 appears to participate in hypoxia\-related endothelial dysfunction and inflammatory activation\. These findings provide new insight into the role of acetylation\-related pathways in MMD pathogenesis and suggest potential targets for future mechanistic studies\.
