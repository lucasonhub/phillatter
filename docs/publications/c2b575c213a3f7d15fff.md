# High\-throughput computational discovery of multi\-component Ni\-rich cathodes with suppressed lattice breathing and delayed H2–H3 transition

**Autores:** Tien Quang Nguyen; Nobuyuki Zettsu; Michihisa Koyama

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

We propose a high\-throughput computational framework for the discovery of multi\-element layered oxide \(MELO\) cathodes for lithium\-ion batteries, combining machine\-learning interatomic potentials with multi\-objective structural screening\. Starting from a Ni\-rich layered LiNiO 2 \-derived R 3 ¯ m framework, a chemically diverse compositional space is constructed through multi\-element substitution in the transition\-metal layer, together with explicit sampling of antisite defects and Li/vacancy configurations\. Large\-scale configurational sampling and delithiation pathway generation are performed, followed by structural relaxation and energy evaluation using a universal neural\-network interatomic potential, enabling efficient exploration of more than 2400 delithiated structures across 220 MELO compounds\. We introduce physically interpretable descriptors that capture key structural responses during electrochemical cycling, including c\-axis evolution, volume change, and the H2–H3 phase transition\. These descriptors are combined in a multi\-objective Pareto screening scheme to identify compositions that simultaneously optimize structural stability and phase\-transition behavior\. Validation against density functional theory confirms the reliability of the proposed workflow in predicting lattice deformation and transition trends\. The screening identifies a set of Pareto\-optimal MELO compounds, including LiNi 0\.8 Co 0\.08 Mn 0\.08 \(AlMoZn\) 0\.04 O 2 , LiNi 0\.8 Co 0\.08 Mn 0\.08 \(CrMoZn\) 0\.04 O 2 , and LiNi 0\.8 Co 0\.08 Mn 0\.08 \(AlMoV\) 0\.04 O 2 , exhibiting reduced lattice breathing, moderated volume change, and delayed H2–H3 phase transition compared with conventional NCM811\. Thermodynamic analysis further reveals favorable Li/vacancy mixing energetics and reduced phase\-separation tendencies in the selected compounds, while precursor reaction and elemental cohesive energies indicate their potential thermodynamic accessibility\. This work establishes a generalizable computational strategy for accelerated discovery of structurally robust multi\-component cathode materials\.
