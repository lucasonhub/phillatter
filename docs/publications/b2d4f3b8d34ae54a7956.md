# Sustainable and Synergistic Energy Management for Large Model Computing

**Autores:** Jingbo Hao; Yang Tao

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid growth of foundation models, most prominently large language models \(LLMs\), is turning the electricity consumption of computing infrastructure into a first\-order constraint on the artificial intelligence \(AI\) industry\. This review synthesizes recent literature on three levers for reconciling large\-model computing with power\-system limits: green energy supply, green computing, and computing\-energy synergy \(CES\), i\.e\., the coordinated operation of computing infrastructure and power systems\. Beyond earlier descriptive accounts, the review makes four contributions: \(1\) comparative assessments that condense the energy\-supply options for AI data centers and the principal energy\-efficiency techniques, including quantization, pruning, knowledge distillation, batching, dynamic voltage and frequency scaling \(DVFS\), and scheduling, into single\-view tables with their performance, latency, cost, energy, and carbon trade\-offs; \(2\) an explicit introduction of standard sustainability metrics \(PUE, CUE, WUE, energy per token, and carbon emissions per workload\) as the quantitative backbone for comparing the surveyed approaches; and \(3\) a substantially deepened treatment of CES, comparing centralized, distributed, and hybrid algorithmic paradigms, formulating a representative joint scheduling problem, and analyzing feasibility under engineering constraints such as renewable\-supply uncertainty, storage limits, grid congestion, workload deadlines, latency requirements, and electricity and carbon price volatility; and \(4\) a conceptual framework, that links supply\-side options, the sustainability\-metrics backbone, demand\-side workload flexibility, and power\-system engineering constraints into a single coordination view\. Three research gaps are identified: integrating 24/7 carbon\-free electricity procurement with flexible workload scheduling; co\-scheduling delay\-tolerant training with latency\-sensitive inference; and embedding power\-system constraints and market signals into CES algorithms\. The evidence reviewed indicates that green energy lowers the operational emissions of large\-model computing but does not by itself deliver continuous carbon\-free operation; green computing techniques can substantially reduce energy per unit of computation at accuracy or latency costs; and CES is a promising coordination paradigm whose large\-scale feasibility still needs to be demonstrated under realistic engineering constraints\.
