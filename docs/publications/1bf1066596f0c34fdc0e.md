# Novel grazing strategies increase milkweed \(Asclepias spp\.\) availability and monarch \(Danaus plexippus\) abundance in rangelands

**Autores:** Ellysa R\. Johnson; Emma C\. Donahey; Katherine C Kral\-O’Brien; Jason P\. Harmon; Kevin K K Sedivec; Torre J\. Hovick

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Monarch butterfly \( Danaus plexippus \) populations are declining, emphasizing the need for conservation throughout their range\. An understudied and underutilized area for conservation includes rangelands managed with livestock grazing\. Although this region is on the edge of the eastern monarch population range, rangelands can provide billions of milkweed \( Asclepias spp\.\) stems, the host plant for monarch butterflies\. A better understanding of the dynamics between milkweed, monarchs, and grazing strategies are necessary, especially since previous research has shown how milkweed stem abundance changes based on grazing management\. Our objectives were to 1\) quantify the influence of three livestock grazing strategies on milkweed and flowering forbs and 2\) determine how adult and juvenile monarch abundance is influenced by milkweed and flowering forbs\. Methods Our study was conducted at the Central Grasslands Research Extension Center in North Dakota from 2021\-2022\. We examined three grazing strategies that varied in their spatial and temporal disturbance patterns \(modified twice\-over rest\-rotation \[MTORG\], patch\-burn grazing \[PBG\], and season\-long grazing \[SLG\]\)\. Results We found higher milkweed abundance in MTORG compared to PBG and SLG, and milkweed abundance was not impacted by drought during the study\. Floral resources were generally more abundant in MTORG, but we detected a significant interaction between year and grazing regime\. Floral resources declined dramatically in PBG in 2021 because of the drought but recovered quickly the following year and matched floral resource availability in the MTORG strategy in 2022\. Both adult and juvenile monarch abundance were positively correlated with milkweed abundance\. Discussion Despite adult monarchs needing both milkweed for reproduction and nectar resources for fueling adult flight, the best explanation for changes in adult monarch abundance was milkweed abundance\. Overall, we found rangelands can benefit monarchs, both during juvenile and adult life stages, by providing vital plant resources\. However, the magnitude of benefit will depend on the spatial and temporal influence of grazing disturbance on host plant availability\.
