# Ulcerative colitis reporting across medications: FAERS analysis and cross\-species transcriptomic triangulation

**Autores:** Huan Long; Caihua Duan; Jiusi Liu; Nanxi Xiao; Jiang Huang; Huilei Zeng; Zhenling Gao; Yao Chen

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Ulcerative colitis \(UC\) is reported with medications from different therapeutic classes\. We examined these reporting associations together with recorded clinical context and tested whether experimental cytokine\-blockade signatures align with human UC mucosal expression\. Methods FAERS reports from 2004Q1–2024Q4 were restricted to health professionals and primary\-suspect medications\. Ten previously identified candidates were evaluated using unique report–generic counts, reporting odds ratios \(RORs\), proportional reporting ratios, and a supportive Bayesian information component\. Sensitivity analysis excluded complete reports recording UC as an indication\. Directional signatures from five antibody\-versus\-isotype contrasts in a 42\-sample mouse colitis study were scored in two external human cohorts \(87 UC/21 controls and 74 active UC/11 controls\), using 12,109 common one\-to\-one orthologs\. Ten primary score comparisons underwent joint Benjamini–Hochberg correction\. Results Among 7,300,798 eligible FAERS reports, 11,352 included UC\. Eight candidates met the specified reporting criteria; seven retained support after UC\-indication exclusion\. Secukinumab and isotretinoin had 204 and 130 UC reports, with RORs of 4\.49 \(95% CI 3\.90–5\.16\) and 3\.79 \(3\.19–4\.51\)\. Valganciclovir decreased from nine to three reports after exclusion and became imprecise\. In human mucosa, experiment\-2 IL\-17A and IL\-17RA blockade signatures scored lower in UC in both cohorts; experiment\-1 IL\-17RA showed a positive difference in only one cohort\. IL\-23p19 and IL\-12/23p40 blockade signatures scored lower in UC in both cohorts\. These patterns persisted across the planned signature\-size sensitivity analysis\. Conclusion Event\-centered analysis differentiated reporting associations by their indication context\. External experimental and patient data did not support a uniform UC\-like transcriptional pattern following IL\-17 blockade, refining the mechanistic interpretation of the reporting signals\. The findings identify priorities for drug\-specific clinical investigation without estimating incidence or establishing causality\.
