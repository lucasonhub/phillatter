# FFA\-SegGPT: A Two\-Stage Cross\-Modal AI System to Optimize the Clinical Workflow of Diabetic Retinopathy FFA Interpretation

**Autores:** Fan Gan; Xian Chen; Yinan Shao; Henghui Li; Wanyun Chen; Weiguo Qin; Xin Long; Huimin Fan; Hanying Hu; Zihan Yu; Kaiwen Chen; Nuo Xu; Liping Chen; Jian Cao; Jing Li; Zhipeng You

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose: The purpose of this study was to develop an interpretable, two\-stage artificial intelligence \(AI\) system \(fundus fluorescein angiography \[FFA\]\-SegGPT\) designed to optimize the clinical workflow of diabetic retinopathy \(DR\) FFA interpretation and reduce the risk of factual inaccuracies common in end\-to\-end models\. Methods: We developed a cross\-modal system that decouples lesion quantification from report generation\. A segmentation module first explicitly segments non\-perfusion areas \(NPAs\) and neovascularization \(NV\)\. These segmentation maps then serve as visual constraints to guide an evidence\-based report generator, ensuring diagnostic text is strictly grounded in detected lesions\. Results: In a simulated clinical workflow, AI\-aided reporting significantly reduced mean interpretation time by 50% \(from 8\.13 ± 0\.47 to 4\.09 ± 0\.21 minutes, P &lt; 0\.001\) and improved median report quality \(4\.0 vs\. 3\.5, P &lt; 0\.001\) compared to manual interpretation\. Objectively, the model achieved high Dice Similarity Coefficients \(NV = 85\.35% and NPAs = 86\.49%\) and maintained high semantic fidelity for report generation \(BERTScore = 0\.91\), with robust generalization demonstrated across three external cohorts\. Conclusions: FFA\-SegGPT enhances both the efficiency and quality of DR diagnostics\. By decoupling lesion quantification from report generation, the system provides a transparent, dual\-output mechanism that reduces the risk of factual inaccuracies, offering an interpretable workflow to facilitate safe clinical adoption\. Translational Relevance: This interpretable AI system optimizes the clinical workflow of DR by providing verifiable lesion segmentation and automated reporting, potentially reducing clinician workload and standardizing diagnostic quality\.
