# Genetic programming control of quasiperiodic and chaotic oscillations in a thermoacoustic system

**Autores:** Bo Yin; Zhijian Yang; Yu Guan; Stéphane Redonnet; Vikrant Gupta; Larry K\.B\. Li

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Self\-excited thermoacoustic oscillations remain a key obstacle to low\-emission combustion, especially in time\-aperiodic regimes where quasiperiodic and chaotic dynamics hinder conventional controller design\. We experimentally demonstrate data\-driven suppression of such aperiodic thermoacoustic oscillations in a flame\-driven Rijke tube using genetic programming \(GP\)\. In closed\-loop form, the GP\-discovered control laws are static nonlinear feedback maps that generate, in real time, actuation commands from a sensor signal\. Performance is evaluated by a cost function that penalises both the thermoacoustic amplitude \(state cost\) and the actuation effort \(input cost\), enabling efficiency comparisons\. We benchmark GP closed\-loop control \(GCC\) against GP open\-loop control and conventional open\-loop harmonic forcing\. In the quasiperiodic regime, all three strategies achieve nearly 90 percent sign 90 % $90\\,\\%$ amplitude reduction, but the closed\-loop strategy is the most efficient\. The low\-amplitude controlled state is dominated by a preferred \(high\-gain\) mode of the open\-flame describing function, and can persist even after control deactivation, indicating basin\-of\-attraction switching\. In the chaotic regime, GCC again outperforms both open\-loop strategies, achieving the largest thermoacoustic amplitude reduction with minimal actuation effort\. Synchronisation analysis reveals phase trapping, drifting and locking between pressure and heat\-release\-rate fluctuations, while the 0–1 test and complexity–entropy diagnostics reveal that the GP\-suppressed state retains measurable signatures of deterministic chaos\. Overall, this study shows that GP control can provide an efficient model\-free tool for suppressing aperiodic thermoacoustic oscillations by autonomously exploiting high\-receptivity flame pathways with state\-dependent actuation\.
