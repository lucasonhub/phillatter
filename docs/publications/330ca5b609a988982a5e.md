# Moderating Effect of Tax Fairness on Determinants of Tax Morale Amongst SMEs Tax Payers in Thika Town, Kiambu County in Kenya

**Autores:** Konse Sarah Akey; Dr\. Joel Tuwey; Naomi Chepkorir Koske

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Tax morale, understood as the intrinsic willingness of taxpayers to comply with tax obligations, is important to voluntary tax compliance because enforcement alone may not explain why similarly situated taxpayers behave differently\. This study examined the moderating effect of tax fairness on the relationship between taxpayer knowledge, tax complexity, taxpayer tolerance and tax morale among SME taxpayers in Thika Town, Kiambu County, Kenya, during the period September 2024–September 2026\. The study was primarily anchored on Utilitarian Theory of Tax Evasion, with Social Learning Theory and Economic Deterrence Theory providing complementary explanations of taxpayer behavior\. An explanatory cross\-sectional research design was adopted\. The target population comprised 3,700 registered SME taxpayers in Thika Town\. A sample of 139 SME taxpayers was determined using Nassiuma’s formula and selected through proportionate stratification by business sector followed by simple random sampling\. Primary data were collected using a structured questionnaire containing five\-point Likert\-scale items covering taxpayer knowledge, tax complexity, taxpayer tolerance, tax fairness and tax morale\. Of the 139 questionnaires distributed, 120 were completed and returned, representing an 86\.3% response rate\. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, multiple linear regression and hierarchical regression at the 5% significance level\. The reported regression results indicate that taxpayer knowledge was positively associated with tax morale \(β = 0\.283, p&lt;; 0 \.001\), while tax complexity was negatively associated with tax morale \(β = 0\.325, p&lt;; 0\.001\) and taxpayer tolerance was positively associated with tax morale \(β = 0\.450, p&lt;; 0\.001\)\. The direct\-effect model explained 66\.4% of the variation in tax morale \(R²=0\.664; F \(3,116\) = 76\.327, p&gt;; 0 \.001\)\. In the full moderation model, the interaction between taxpayer knowledge and tax fairness was significant \(β = 0\.268, p = 0\.021\), as was the interaction between tax complexity and tax fairness \(β = 0\.221, p =0\.029\) and taxpayer tolerance and tax fairness \(β = 0\.478, p = 0\.046\)\. The findings indicate that perceived tax fairness changes the strength of the relationships between the three determinants and tax morale\. The study recommends targeted taxpayer education, simplification of tax procedures and communication, and measures that strengthen perceptions of fairness in the tax system\. Because the study used cross\-sectional self\-reported data, the regression findings are interpreted as statistical associations rather than definitive causal effects\.
