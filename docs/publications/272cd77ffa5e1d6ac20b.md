# Meditation in an academic medical institution to improve student mental health: a pilot study

**Autores:** Marie Vazquez Morgan; Suzanne Tinsley

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction : Anxiety is common among graduate and professional students\. Meditation has been shown to reduce anxiety symptoms, and virtual reality \(VR\)\-guided meditation has emerged as a more immersive approach that may increase engagement\. This pilot study evaluated the feasibility, adherence, and preliminary anxiety outcomes associated with VR\-guided and audio\-guided meditation among health professions students\. Methods Feasibility was assessed using recruitment, enrollment, retention, assessment completion, intervention delivery, and attendance\-data capture\. Adherence was defined as the number of QR\-code\-recorded meditation\-session attendances during the 8\-week intervention\. Anxiety outcomes were assessed using the GAD\-7\. Within\-group changes were examined using paired t tests and Wilcoxon signed\-rank tests, and between\-group differences in change scores were explored with an independent\-samples t test\. Spearman correlation analysis examined the association between attendance and reduction in anxiety\. Results Thirty\-five students were assessed for eligibility, 22 were randomized, and all completed the intervention and post\-intervention assessment\. Both modalities were delivered as planned, and 226 session attendances were recorded\. Participants in the VR condition completed a mean of 12\.00 recorded sessions \(SD = 5\.42; range, 1–17\), corresponding to 75\.0% of the prescribed minimum, whereas participants in the audio\-guided condition completed a mean of 8\.55 sessions \(SD = 5\.22; range, 1–18\), corresponding to 53\.4% of the prescribed minimum\. GAD\-7 scores decreased significantly within both groups\. A linear mixed\-effects model demonstrated a significant main effect of time \( z = 6\.80, p &lt; \.001\) but no significant Condition×Time interaction \( z = −0\.28, p = \.783\)\. Consistent with this finding, the difference in change scores between groups was not significant, t\(20\) = 0\.27, p = \.786, Cohen&\#x27;s d = 0\.12\. Recorded attendance was not associated with improvement in GAD\-7 scores\. Conclusion Both meditation modalities were successfully implemented, and all participants completed the study\. Recorded attendance was greater in the VR condition; however, adherence varied substantially, and attendance was measured through check\-in records only\. Anxiety symptoms decreased in both groups, but the absence of a control group and small pilot sample preclude conclusions regarding efficacy or equivalence\.
