# Making GenAI revision accountable: learner evaluation, uptake, and revision quality in source\-based academic writing

**Autores:** Huican Huo; Yanfan Cheng; Yue Peng; Weiwei Xie

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Generative artificial intelligence \(GenAI\) can provide fluent revision suggestions, but source\-based writing requires learners to judge whether those suggestions preserve source meaning, evidence, attribution, task requirements, and intended stance\. This classroom\-based study examined 40 second\-year English majors completing two source\-based writing tasks\. The dataset comprised 160 drafts, 80 reflections, 793 traceable revision episodes, and independent ratings of 80 D1–D2 pairs\. Cross\-classifying 131 reflection\-linked rationales with uptake showed that language\-clarity reasons were concentrated in local language uptake, trust or uncertainty frequently accompanied checked uptake, and efficiency or time was concentrated in weakly checked uptake\. Excluding 34 risk\-unclear episodes, substantive\-risk suggestions were more often associated with visible checking than local\-only suggestions \(32\.5% vs\. 18\.9%; participant\-clustered GEE, OR = 2\.07, 95% CI \[1\.48, 2\.91\], p &lt; 0\.001\), although the authorial\-stance contrast was not robust\. Within substantive\-risk episodes with determinate consequences, visible checking was associated with higher odds of local improvement \(84\.6% vs\. 39\.6%; adjusted OR = 8\.11, 95% CI \[4\.88, 13\.47\], p &lt; 0\.001\) and lower odds of degradation\. D2 texts were rated higher than D1 texts, but absolute rater agreement was moderate, ICC\(A,k\) = 0\.625, and no participant–task\-level uptake proportion was significantly associated with whole\-text gain after multiplicity correction\. The findings are consistent with risk\-calibrated accountability as an observational design principle; they do not establish a causal effect of visible checking\.
