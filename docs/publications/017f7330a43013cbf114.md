# Bridging the gap: Competency\-driven health informatics education for future healthcare leaders: a PhD study

**Autores:** A B Chikware; Nicolette Vanessa Roman; Eugene Lee Davids

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Information and communications technologies are becoming integral to modern healthcare, advancing the adoption of health informatics and transforming the competencies required of healthcare professionals\. This digital shift necessitates a re\-examination of healthcare education to ensure alignment with evolving practice demands\. This study explores healthcare professionals’ perceptions of health informatics education and its contribution to developing digital competencies\. Thirty\-one participants were recruited through purposive and snowball sampling strategies, representing eight healthcare disciplines: medicine, nursing, social work, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, dietetics, pharmacy and dentistry\. Data were collected through semi\-structured interviews and analysed using thematic analysis\. Participants identified emerging aspects of digitisation within healthcare, such as digital health\-record systems, artificial intelligence and open\-source applications, that require enhanced digital skills training\. However, limited attention to health informatics within undergraduate education and insufficient workplace training were reported as key barriers to digital competency development\. Despite these limitations, participants expressed a strong interest in digital innovation, particularly following their experiences during the COVID‑19 pandemic\. This presents a strategic opportunity to strengthen health informatics education\. The study concludes that integrating health informatics competencies through updated curricula, blended learning methods, and targeted professional development is essential for preparing healthcare professionals to effectively engage with digital transformation in healthcare\. Furthermore, by default of being born in a highly digital transformation era, this generation of healthcare professionals is strategically placed to drive healthcare digitisation and future innovations\.
