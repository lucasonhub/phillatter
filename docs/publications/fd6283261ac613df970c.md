# Airway foreign body removal in children: warning about shortcuts

**Autores:** Maria Majori; Alessandro Marchioni

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Foreign body aspiration remains one of the most life\-threatening emergencies in pediatric airway management, requiring prompt intervention to prevent rapid progression to severe hypoxemia\.Although rigid bronchoscopy continues to be recognized as the gold standard for the removal of airway foreign bodies in children, recent technological advances have expanded the role of flexible bronchoscopy, fostering an ongoing debate regarding the optimal therapeutic approach\.In this Perspective, we argue that this discussion should move beyond a simplistic comparison between rigid and flexible techniques\.Instead, the primary distinction lies between clinically stable patients with distally located foreign bodies and life\-threatening central airway obstructions, in which rigid bronchoscopy remains indispensable\.We critically examine the current evidence supporting flexible bronchoscopic techniques, including the use of disposable bronchoscopes and cryotechnology, highlighting their potential advantages as well as their intrinsic mechanical and clinical limitations in pediatric patients\.We further discuss the growing concern that the increasing reliance on flexible bronchoscopy may reflect a progressive loss of expertise in pediatric rigid bronchoscopy rather than a true evolution of standards of care\.Ultimately, technological innovation should complement, rather than replace, proficiency in rigid airway management\.Ensuring immediate availability of both techniques within appropriately trained multidisciplinary teams remains essential to guarantee the highest standards of safety for children with airway foreign body aspiration\.Although relatively uncommon, inhaled foreign bodies \(IFBs\) represent one of the most life\-threatening emergencies in pediatric airway management, predominantly affecting children aged 1\-2 years \(1, 2\)\.At this age, the millimetric caliber of the airways predisposes aspirated objects to cause the occlusion of the trachea or main bronchi\.Most of the IFBs in pediatric patients, moreover, consist of organic, foodrelated materials prone to hygroscopic expansion, facilitating the progression from partial to complete airway obstruction\.Furthermore, patients in this cohort exhibit heightened vagal sensitivity, predisposing them to laryngospasm and bradycardia\.These physiological factors, combined with high oxygen consumption rates and reduced functional residual capacity \(FRC\), significantly accelerate the onset of hypoxia \(3\)\.In addition, the small caliber of the pediatric airway implies that even minimal reductions in lumen result in exponential increases in airflow resistance, as described by Poiseuille&\#x27;s law\.The increased compliance of the pediatric chest wall further reduces airway support, promoting dynamic airway collapse during forceful inspiratory efforts, particularly in the setting of obstruction \(Figure 1\)\.Consequently, prompt and effective intervention is imperative, as fatal outcomes may rapidly occur due to severe hypoxemia\.
