# Machine learning\-based pathomics signature as a novel digital marker for C\-X\-C motif chemokine ligand 9 expression and prognostic assessment in breast cancer

**Autores:** Wen Huang; Mengna Zhan; Juan Yu; Lei Ren; Junxian Du; Yingyong Hou; Rongkui Luo

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objectives: C\-X\-C motif chemokine ligand 9 \(CXCL9\) is a critical mediator of tumor\-infiltrating lymphocyte recruitment and a potential biomarker in breast cancer \(BC\)\. This study aimed to develop a machine learning\-based handcrafted pathomics model to forecast survival outcomes by characterizing CXCL9\-related morphological signatures from routine hematoxylin and eosin \(H&amp;E\) slides\. The biological relevance and clinical utility of the derived pathomics score \(PS\) were subsequently evaluated to provide an objective framework for risk stratification\. Material and Methods: We developed a pathomics model integrating quantitative H&amp;E image features with machine learning in 791 Cancer Genome Atlas\-BC patients\. Feature selection using minimum redundancy maximum relevance and recursive feature elimination was performed exclusively within the training set, and a gradient boosting machine model was constructed to output a PS representing the predicted probability of CXCL9 expression\. Model performance was assessed in the training, testing, and external validation cohorts\. Associations of PS with survival, immune infiltration, cancer\-immunity cycle \(CI cycle\) activity, tumor mutational burden, and molecular subtypes were investigated through integrated bioinformatics\. Results: CXCL9 expression was confirmed as a significant prognostic factor for overall survival \( P = 0\.037\)\. The pathomics model demonstrated stable performance for predicting CXCL9 status in training, testing, and external cohorts \(area under the curves: 0\.741, 0\.729, and 0\.704, respectively\)\. The PS significantly correlated with CXCL9 expression, stratified patients into distinct prognostic groups \( P = 0\.012\), and remained a significant protective factor in multivariate analysis \(hazard ratio = 0\.66, P = 0\.048\)\. Mechanistically, high PS was associated with immune\-inflamed features, characterized by increased natural killer cells and CD8\+ T\-cell infiltration, enhanced CI cycle activity, and elevated TMB\. Furthermore, the PS effectively reflected clinical heterogeneity, with triple\-negative BC exhibiting the highest scores compared to other molecular subtypes \( P &lt;0\.001\)\. Conclusion: This pathomics framework provides an imaging\-derived indicator of CXCL9 expression and may support prognostic stratification in BC\. Its association with immune\-inflamed characteristics suggests potential relevance for immune phenotype assessment, but its value for predicting immunotherapy response requires validation in immunotherapy\-treated cohorts\.
