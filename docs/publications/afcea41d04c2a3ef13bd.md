# Niami, Morteza: Intensity as an Ontological Variable Erosion, Reconfiguration, and Finite Continuance

**Autores:**

## Descrição do feed

Intensity has occupied a significant position in modern and contemporary philosophy, particularly in accounts of duration, affect, difference, embodiment, becoming, and material transformation\. From Bergson’s analysis of qualitative multiplicity and duration to Deleuze’s ontology of intensive difference, and from phenomenological accounts of lived affectivity to contemporary approaches to process and thermodynamic organization, intensity has increasingly been treated as a constitutive dimension of change rather than as a simple measure of magnitude\. This article develops a distinct ontological conception of intensity within the framework of finite continuance\. It proposes that intensity is an emergent relational index concerning the pressure exerted upon a finite organization relative to its effective capacity for reconfiguration and its higher\-order capacity to preserve the possibility of further transformation within a determinate temporal field\. Every finite entity exists as a provisional stabilization within an erosive field and therefore depends upon constitutive exchange, regulation, and the preservation of future reconfigurative capacity\. Intensity becomes ontologically decisive when the exercise of present reconfigurative capacity begins to consume the meta\-capacity required for subsequent transformation\. The article distinguishes input intensity, phenomenological intensity, and erosive intensity; differentiates reconfigurative capacity from meta\-capacity; specifies operational dimensions of erosive intensity; and formalizes intensity as a heuristic relational index\. It develops the Threshold of Sufficiency as a dynamic transition region rather than a fixed numerical boundary\. The article further distinguishes the present conception from Deleuzian intensive difference by separating questions of individuation and actualization from questions concerning the preservation of future reconfigurability\. Its relation to thermodynamics is likewise specified through analogy and limited structural correspondence rather than physical identity\. The resulting account situates intensity between phenomenology, process ontology, and thermodynamic thinking while advancing a specific thesis: the ontological significance of intensity lies in the way present pressure modifies the future capacity of a finite entity to become otherwise\.
