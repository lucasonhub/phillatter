abstract: '_Zenodo_. 2026Whether a confirmed result is established should not depend
  on who or what produced it. This paper states that sentence as a principle and places
  it in a short chain of inferences. The establishment of a result is a function of
  the record of confirmations alone, and is therefore invariant when the provenance
  of production is replaced while the content and the confirming acts are held fixed.
  Responsibility and attribution behave differently: they attach to the human who
  supplies purpose, judgment and authority, and they covary with that person. The
  two belong to different layers. Establishment is epistemic; the treatment of a result,
  that is, its publication, its credit and its private rights, is institutional, and
  the allocation of private rights is set by law and is not decided here. Because
  anyone with the ability to check can carry out a confirmation, peer review is read
  as a delegated form of inspection, a contribution to the record of confirmations
  but not a constituent of establishment, in the way that no certificate is needed
  to recognise a person who is plainly dead. We derive the consequences for priority,
  which attaches to the dated public record of the posit, and we delineate the principle
  from intellectual property rights. We then describe a virtual instance, the Court
  of Provenance Invariance, together with a Register of Confirmations. Since the academy
  is itself a party to the question of how results of differing provenance are treated,
  the review of invariance must sit outside the party. Within the range of sources
  examined, we find no description of this concept; this is a claim of non-detection
  and not of absence. The paper does not judge any particular result, does not say
  whether artificial intelligence should hold rights, and does not propose a new right.
  The formal model is given in Appendix A, and its finite instances are verified by
  exhaustive enumeration in Appendix B. The paper also separates three questions that
  are easily run together: who is the effective producer of a result, who bears responsibility
  for it, and whether it is established. Human involvement in the conditions of generation
  does not entail being the effective producer, a machine''s effective role does not
  discharge the responsibility that the human holds, and the establishment of a result
  does not require the determination of its effective producer. The invariance covers
  every coordinate of the provenance of production, including the standing of the
  producer, while the independence of a confirmation remains part of the record. The
