# Dual\-Functional SiO x Cross\-Point Devices Enabled by Top Electrode Engineering for Ultralow\-Current Resistive Memory, Ultrasensitive Sarcosine Sensing, and Artificial Intelligence\-Assisted Biomarker Classification

**Autores:** Sourav Roy; Long Nguyen Minh Le; Abhijit Aich; Punnatorn Boonyoung; Wachiraporn Poomeesap; Tingyang Shen; Yi\-Pin Chen; Shih\-Yin Huang; Siddheswar Maikap

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract In the pursuit of ultra\-low\-power neuromorphic and bioelectronic systems, this work investigates two complementary SiOx\-based resistive\-switching architectures with engineered top electrodes: a Cu/CuAl structure employing metallic\-filament CBRAM and an IrOx/SiOx/W cross\-point architecture employing vacancy\-mediated switching\. Introducing a Cu–Al alloy interfacial layer into a Cu/SiOx/W structure enables ultralow\-current operation with an energy consumption of ∼0\.03 fJ/bit, endurance exceeding 109 program/erase \(P/E\) cycles, and a memory window \(MW\) greater than 2500\. Further advancement to a three\-dimensional IrOx/SiOx/W cross\-point architecture significantly improves device reliability, achieving endurance beyond 1010 P/E cycles at operating currents of 10 and 50 μA while maintaining stable switching for more than 103 cycles at an ultralow current of 200 nA with an MW exceeding 3 × 104\. Beyond memory functionality, the porous IrOx top electrode enables label\-free biomarker sensing through its stable redox states and super\-Nernstian pH response\. TEM, HRTEM, and FESEM analyses reveal a porous nanostructured IrOx architecture that facilitates efficient oxygen exchange, proton transport, and redox activity\. The resulting platform achieves ultrasensitive sarcosine detection with a limit of detection as low as ∼10 fM\. To examine the feasibility of automated interpretation of sensing signals, current–time responses were analyzed using a Convolutional Spiking Neural Network \(CSNN\)\. The model achieved an overall classification accuracy of 95\.8%, together with a sensitivity of 96\.02% and specificity of 100%\. These results demonstrate that complementary top\-electrode\-engineered SiOx architectures can provide ultralow\-power memory operation, while the IrOx/SiOx/W cross\-point platform further enables ultrasensitive biomarker detection and AI\-assisted signal analysis, highlighting their potential for future bioelectronic and neuromorphic systems\.
