# DO FUSION MODULES AND COMPOUND LOSSES REALLY HELP? A MULTI\-SEED ABLATION OF A HYBRID CNN–TRANSFORMER MODEL FOR 3D BRAIN TUMOR SEGMENTATION

**Autores:** G\. X\. Abdukadirova; M\. I\. Abduqodirova

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Hybrid CNN–Transformer models for brain tumor segmentation are usually justified by ablation studies in which each variant is trained once, although run\-to\-run variation can be as large as the reported gains\. This study re\-examines the components of the HyCTSeg model \- a parallel CNN and Swin Transformer encoder with bidirectional cross\-attention fusion \(CAFF\) and a four\-component loss \(Dice, cross\-entropy, focal, Tversky\) \- with a statistically powered design\. Nine variants were each trained with four random seeds on BraTS 2021 \(32 independent runs, 150 epochs\) on an identical data split, and compared by paired t\-tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction\. The multi\-seed design reduced the standard deviation to 0\.0006–0\.0018 Dice\. The full model ranked third of five architectures \(0\.8593 ± 0\.0015\); the Swin\-only encoder was best \(0\.8645 ± 0\.0016\) and exceeded the full model by 0\.0052 \(p = 0\.0096\), and simple additive fusion exceeded it by 0\.0032 \(p = 0\.0107\), both significant after correction\. The differences were concentrated in the enhancing tumor region\. The compound loss did not outperform Dice loss alone \(−0\.0004, p = 0\.38\)\. These negative results show that single\-run ablations can overstate the value of architectural components and argue for multi\-seed evaluation as standard practice\.
