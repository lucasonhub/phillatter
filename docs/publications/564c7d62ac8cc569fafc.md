# A review of methods for collecting crew fatigue data in civil aircraft cockpits

**Autores:** Yuntai Shi

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Flight fatigue has been identified as a key factor contributing to human\-factor\-related safety incidents in civil aviation, significantly affecting pilots&\#x27; cognitive and operational capabilities and posing a persistent threat to flight operational safety\. However, the confined cockpit environment, together with vibration, electromagnetic interference, and other distinctive physical conditions, imposes substantial technical constraints on the real\-time, continuous, and non\-intrusive collection of fatigue data\. Existing data acquisition methods differ considerably in sensor types, measurement indicators, and evaluation criteria, and the overall body of research remains fragmented, lacking a systematic review from the perspective of applicability in aeronautical engineering\. This study first employs literature review and analytical methods to systematically categorize approaches to collecting crew fatigue data in civil aircraft cockpits across four dimensions: technical principles, application scenarios, acquisition requirements, and outcome evaluation\. Second, a synthesis of empirical data reported in existing studies indicates that multimodal data fusion yields a substantially lower false\-classification rate than single\-indicator approaches and therefore offers greater value for early warning\. Future research should focus on strengthening hardware interference\-resistance design, developing localized real\-time processing algorithms, and promoting unified industry standards for data collection and evaluation, thereby providing a feasible pathway for the intelligent advancement of flight safety management\.
