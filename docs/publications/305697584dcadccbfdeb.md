  processes, and the development of artificial intelligence, coupled with growing
  geopolitical and economic uncertainty, mean that effective management is no longer
  solely the domain of economic, financial, or organizational knowledge. It is becoming
  increasingly evident that sound managerial decisions rest on an understanding of
  human beings – their emotions, motivations, cognitive limitations, and needs related
  to security, dignity, and belonging. It is precisely this perspective – combining
  theoretical reflection with practical experience – that forms the guiding thread
  of this monograph, prepared on the basis of the 6th Scientific Conference “New Vectors
  of Management: Behavioral Economics for Conscious and Diverse Organizations,” held
  as part of the series “The Digital Society and Concepts of Organizational Management.”

  integrating the achievements of cognitive and social psychology with classical economic
  theory, provides tools for better understanding decision-making processes, social

  through all the chapters of this monograph. The structure of the monograph reflects
  the multifaceted nature of the issues addressed – ranging from risk and digitalization,
  through artificial intelligence, legal regulation, geopolitics, and marketing, to
  diversity, employee well-being, and leadership from a cross-cultural perspective.
  The opening chapter, by M. Barna and Z. Novosad, is devoted to risk management in
  the context of digitalization. The authors present a comparative analysis of two
  methods used to support decision-making under conditions of uncertainty – correlation
  and regression analysis, and expert assessment methods – highlighting their methodological
  foundations, strengths, and limitations in application to higher education. On the
  basis of a survey study, they identify the need to combine study with work as the
  most significant educational challenge. In the second chapter, A. Dağdeviren addresses
  the accessibility of infrastructure for people with disabilities in the context
  of the Blue Flag programme criteria, examining its application on beaches in Türkiye.
  The author points to the need to ensure a continuous, safe route from parking areas
  to the water, as well as regular monitoring of infrastructure compliance with technical
  requirements, situating the chapter within a broader reflection on the inclusivity
  of public space. The chapter by J. Kloc shifts the discussion to artificial intelligence
  and behavioral management. The author presents the theoretical foundations of behavioral
  management and analyzes the opportunities and challenges associated with the use
  of machine learning algorithms, predictive analytics, and recommendation systems
  in monitoring and shaping employee behavior. A significant part of the discussion
  is devoted to the ethical dimension of these solutions – the protection of privacy,
  algorithmic transparency, and the risk of decision-making bias. The chapter''s conclusion,
  pointing to the need to maintain a balance between organizational effectiveness
  and respect for individual autonomy, resonates with the central theme of the entire
  monograph. M. Nowikowska examines the relationship between legal regulation and
  organizational culture as determinants of the effectiveness of compliance systems.
  The author shows that the effectiveness of mechanisms ensuring an organization''s
  compliance with the law and ethical standards does not stem solely from the quality
  of control procedures, but to a significant extent from the organizational culture
  within which they operate. It is emphasized that regulatory compliance is, in practice,
  co-shaped by the attitudes and values shared by an organization''s members. K. Palonka-Pulikowska
  addresses the issue of geopolitics and its growing impact on corporate management
  decisions. The author points to the gradual replacement of the globalization paradigm
  by a geopolitical approach, and to the growing importance of geoeconomics, which
  employs economic instruments to pursue political objectives. She analyzes the impact
  of sanctions, trade restrictions, embargoes, and export and import controls on the
  functioning of enterprises amid the transition from the existing international order
  toward a more fragmented and multipolar system, shaped to a large extent by the
  economic rivalry between the United States and China. The chapter raises the fundamental
  question of the limits within which a contemporary market economy can function in
  the face of the growing politicization of economic decisions. M. Róg-Giacosa shifts
  the discussion to the level of business relationships, analyzing the determinants
  of exhibitors'' perceived value of participation in B2B trade fairs in a declining
  industry, using the example of the Kids’ Time International Fair in Kielce. Using
  exploratory factor analysis, correlation analysis, and multiple linear regression,
  the author demonstrates that exhibitors'' marketing capabilities – rather than the
  quality of event organization – have a stronger effect on the business outcomes
  achieved, although organizational quality remains important for the overall evaluation
  of the event and for exhibitors'' willingness to recommend it. A. Banaszak addresses
  the behavioral shaping of attitudes toward old age as a tool for building inclusive
  organizations. Using the example of an original workshop programme, “Understanding
  Old Age,” which employs a geriatric-experience simulator, the author analyzes the
  mechanisms through which experiential education reduces ageism and stereotypes concerning
  older people. The chapter emphasizes the role of direct experience of the limitations
  associated with the aging process in fostering empathy and intergenerational solidarity.
  U. Anisiewicz combines a behavioral-economics perspective with an analysis of marketing
  communication, addressing the representation of women in advertising in the context
  of femvertising. Drawing on a survey study, the chapter examines the extent to which
  consumer responses to advertising messages that emphasize female empowerment are
  rational, and the extent to which they are shaped by cognitive heuristics. The chapter
  offers an interesting case study of how categories from behavioral economics intersect
  with marketing practice and the social perception of authenticity in advertising
  messages. W. Bobisz addresses the topic of mobbing as a contemporary threat in the
  workplace, a subject of considerable importance for human resource management. The
  author points out that a rapidly changing labor market, pressure to meet performance
  targets, and evolving management styles all contribute to the intensification of
  pathological behaviors, the consequences of which affect not only employees'' physical
  and mental health but also workplace atmosphere, staff turnover, and companies''
  financial performance. L. Weldemichael presents the results of a study on the impact
  of loyalty programmes on passenger satisfaction in the airline industry. Amid strong
  competition from Middle Eastern and European carriers, the author examines how individual
  elements of a loyalty programme influence customer satisfaction. H. Weldemichael,
  in turn, addresses the role of strategic management in enhancing the effectiveness
  of Ethiopian civil society and non-governmental organizations. The author examines
  these organizations'' managerial practices, the relationship between strategic planning
  and effectiveness, the role of leadership, and the barriers that hinder the implementation
  of strategic management in the non-governmental sector. T. Getahun presents a comparative
  analysis of the influence of national culture on the effectiveness of leadership
  styles in Ethiopia and Poland. D. Biały, in turn, broadens the scope of the issues
  discussed by introducing an original research perspective that contributes to the
  monograph’s exploration of the behavioral and organizational dimensions of contemporary
  management. The author examines the productivity paradox in organizations, focusing
  on the relationship between the growing number of information technology tools used
  in the workplace and the increasing prevalence of digital cognitive fatigue. The
  monograph concludes with a chapter by J. Nowicka, devoted to the analysis and comparison
  of logistics processes implemented at selected historic sites in the Wałbrzych Agglomeration.
  The chapter examines information flows, deliveries, warehousing, transportation,
  and stakeholder service. Drawing on statistical methods, the author highlights the
  importance of historical logistics practices both for the preservation of cultural
  heritage and for the improvement of contemporary logistics management practices.
  Although the chapters included in the monograph vary in terms of subject matter,
  methodology, and geographical scope, they share a common concern with understanding
  the mechanisms underlying human behavior in organizations and identifying ways to
  develop structures that are more aware, responsible, and inclusive. They contribute
  to the broader field of behavioral economics, understood not merely as a subdiscipline
  of economics but as an epistemological perspective that integrates insights from
  psychology, law, marketing, and management.'

## Resumo

Contemporary organizations operate under an unprecedented dynamic of social, economic, and technological change\. Globalization, the digitalization of managerial processes, and the development of artificial intelligence, coupled with growing geopolitical and economic uncertainty, mean that effective management is no longer solely the domain of economic, financial, or organizational knowledge\. It is becoming increasingly evident that sound managerial decisions rest on an understanding of human beings – their emotions, motivations, cognitive limitations, and needs related to security, dignity, and belonging\. It is precisely this perspective – combining theoretical reflection with practical experience – that forms the guiding thread of this monograph, prepared on the basis of the 6th Scientific Conference “New Vectors of Management: Behavioral Economics for Conscious and Diverse Organizations,” held as part of the series “The Digital Society and Concepts of Organizational Management\.” As Daniel Kahneman observed, dispelling the myth of the perfectly rational consumer, in his book Thinking, Fast and Slow: “Our thinking is far more prone to error and far more susceptible to emotion than we tend to assume\.” This observation, formulated over a decade ago, has gained renewed relevance today\. Behavioral economics, by integrating the achievements of cognitive and social psychology with classical economic theory, provides tools for better understanding decision\-making processes, social relations, and the mechanisms underlying the functioning of contemporary organizations\. It also teaches that the organizations of the future should be built on a foundation of empathy, diversity, responsibility, and conscious leadership – values that run through all the chapters of this monograph\. The structure of the monograph reflects the multifaceted nature of the issues addressed – ranging from risk and digitalization, through artificial intelligence, legal regulation, geopolitics, and marketing, to diversity, employee well\-being, and leadership from a cross\-cultural perspective\.
