# Efficacy of 0\.5 and 1% Quaternary Ammonium Silane Irrigant on Smear Layer Removal of Radicular Dentin – An In\-vitro Study

**Autores:** Vadde Uma Maheswari; Chinni Sunil Kumar; Lavanya Anumula; Swapna Sannapureddy; Pinnika Samarasimha Yadav; Dr\. D S S Vishnu Pavan

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Aim: To evaluate the effect of 0\.5 and 1% quaternary ammonium silane \(QAS\), and ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid \(EDTA\) on smear layer removal\.Methodology: Thirty\-six extracted mandibular premolars were standardized to 15 mm root length and instrumented to F3 using Protaper Gold files\.Samples were randomly assigned to three groups \(n = 12\): Group A \(0\.5% QAS\), group B \(1% QAS\), and group C \(17% EDTA\)\.Each canal was irrigated with 5 mL of the assigned solution for 5 minutes\.Smear layer removal at coronal, middle, and apical thirds was evaluated under a scanning electron microscope \(SEM\) using Rödig et al\. criteria\.Data were analyzed with one\-way ANOVA and Mann\-Whitney U test\.Results: Significant differences \(p &lt; 0\.05\) were observed in smear layer scores among all groups at coronal, middle, and apical third regions\.Group A consistently showed the highest smear layer scores \(3\.08, 3\.5, and 3\.75\), group B intermediate values \(2\.5, 2\.5, and 2\.75\), and group C the lowest \(1\.25, 1\.25, and 1\.42\)\.Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid demonstrated superior overall chelating action, while 1% QAS outperformed 0\.5% QAS in deeper regions\.Conclusion: Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid remains the most effective irrigant for smear layer removal\.Quaternary ammonium silane at 1% concentration showed improved efficacy compared to 0\.5% and offers additional antimicrobial and collagen\-preserving benefits\.Clinical significance: Quaternary ammonium silane, particularly at 1% concentration, demonstrated comparable smear layer removal in deeper canal regions while offering added antimicrobial and collagen\-preserving benefits\.These multifunctional properties suggest that QAS could streamline irrigation protocols by combining chelation, disinfection, and dentin stabilization in a single solution\.Adoption of QAS may reduce dentin erosion and enhance treatment longevity, though further clinical trials are required before routine use can be recommended\.
