# Fairness under Prediction Error in Predict\-then\-Optimize Pipelines

**Autores:** Hilda Adwubi Osei

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Fairness constraints in predict\-then\-optimize pipelines are necessarily enforced on predicted outcomes $\\hat\{y\}$, since true outcomes $y$ are unobserved at decision time\. We study whether a demographic parity constraint enforced this way continues to hold against $y$, and how the resulting violation scales with prediction error\. Using a budget\-constrained allocation problem solved as a linear program, we show empirically that the true\-outcome fairness gap grows with prediction error $σ$ and saturates, well fit by $c\(1 \- e^\{\-σ/τ\}\)$ with $c = 0\.1545 \\pm 0\.0103$ and $τ= 3\.9045 \\pm 0\.3745$; its mean is invariant to sample size while its variance decays at the standard $O\(1/\\sqrt\{n\}\)$ rate\. We derive a two\-part theoretical bound: a closed\-form local sensitivity result showing the gap&\#x27;s first\-order response to prediction error depends on only two individuals per group, validated against the solver to within three significant figures; and a universal, error\-independent ceiling following from the boundedness of the decision variable, confirmed consistent with but looser than the empirical saturation level\. On US Census income data, enforcing demographic parity on a trained predictor&\#x27;s output reduces an unconstrained disparity in true income to roughly one\-third of its original size, leaving 34\.4% $\\pm$ 3\.3% \(California\) and 35\.7% $\\pm$ 1\.8% \(Texas\) of the disparity intact and undetectable from the predictions alone \-\- consistent not only in mechanism but in approximate magnitude across two states with different demographics and income distributions, and stable across ten independent train/test splits in each\. A fairness constraint that appears fully satisfied at decision time can mask a substantial, systematic violation of the outcome it was meant to protect\.
