# Artificial Intelligence in Pharmaceutical Market Access and Evidence Generation: From Real\-World Evidence to Health Technology Assessment and Patient Access — A Scoping Review

**Autores:** Tushar Dinkar Shelke; Prashant Ambadas Garudkar

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial intelligence \(AI\) is emerging as a transformative technology in pharmaceutical market access and evidence generation, offering new opportunities to improve healthcare decision\-making through advanced data analytics and predictive modelling\. The integration of machine learning, natural language processing, deep learning, and generative AI has the potential to strengthen real\-world evidence \(RWE\) generation, health technology assessment \(HTA\), health economics and outcomes research \(HEOR\), pricing and reimbursement strategies, and patient access\. This scoping review aims to map the applications of AI across the pharmaceutical market access pathway, with particular emphasis on evidence generation, clinical and economic evaluation, and patient\-centred healthcare\. The review is designed to follow an established scoping review framework and the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta\-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews \(PRISMA\-ScR\)\. Relevant literature is intended to be identified through searches of PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Embase, and Web of Science, supplemented by relevant HTA agency publications\. The evidence will be organized according to AI techniques, application areas, reported benefits, implementation challenges, and research gaps\. Key areas of interest include AI\-assisted analysis of healthcare data, automated evidence synthesis, economic modelling, reimbursement planning, and patient stratification\. Despite these opportunities, challenges related to data quality, algorithmic bias, transparency, privacy, model validation, and regulatory acceptance may limit practical implementation\. The review aims to identify emerging applications and research priorities while emphasizing the importance of responsible AI governance, methodological rigor, and human oversight\. Overall, AI may support more efficient evidence generation and informed pharmaceutical market access decisions; however, its clinical, economic, and societal value requires further empirical evaluation\.
