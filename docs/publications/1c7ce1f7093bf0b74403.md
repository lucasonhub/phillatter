# Institutional support, relationships, and multicultural participation: response surfaces of university belonging and sociocultural engagement

**Autores:** Siyuan Luo; Hui Qu

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Universities are cultural settings in which institutional arrangements and interpersonal recognition jointly condition participation and belonging\. Methods This open\-data secondary analysis examined how perceived supportive campus environment and relationship quality combined in relation to university belonging and sociocultural engagement among health\-professions students at a culturally diverse university in the United Arab Emirates\. The released dataset contained 683 records; 661 complete cases entered both polynomial models\. Predictors were transformed to proportion\-of\-maximum\-possible scores\. Inference combined 2,000 bootstrap resamples, HC3 heteroskedasticity\-robust standard errors, duplicate\-record checks, and Cook&\#x27;s D influence sensitivity\. Results The models explained 47\.3% of belonging and 25\.6% of sociocultural engagement\. The joint\-resource slope was positive for belonging \[a1 = 3\.159, 95% bootstrap CI \(2\.596, 3\.602\)\] and sociocultural engagement \[a1 = 1\.721, CI \(1\.233, 2\.133\)\]\. Curvature, directional difference, and mismatch curvature were not consistently supported\. Removing two exact duplicate records or 41 observations above Cook&\#x27;s D &gt;4/ n retained the positive joint\-resource pattern\. Discussion Thus, stronger institutional support and relationships were jointly associated with both campus belonging and multicultural participation, without evidence for a unique matching effect\. The cross\-sectional measures do not directly observe identity co\-construction, so the contribution is a bounded quantitative account of conditions associated with culturally situated participation and belonging\.
