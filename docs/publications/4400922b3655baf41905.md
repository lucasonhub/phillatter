# Assessing Mental Health Without Relying on Psychiatric Data

**Autores:** Juan de Dios Sanjuán\-Antúnez; Ernesto Curbelo; Inmaculada Peñuelas‐Calvo; Enrique Baca‐García; Elena Pérez\-García; Hilario Blasco\-Fontecilla; David Delgado‐Gómez

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Approximately one in seven people worldwide is affected by a mental disorder, making early identification crucial for preventing symptom progression and chronicity\. However, diagnosis remains challenging, with discrepancies between referring physicians’ initial assessments and specialist psychiatric diagnoses reported in approximately half of cases\. In recent years, machine learning has shown promising results in mental health assessment, with supervised techniques such as decision trees, random forests, and support vector machines among the most widely used approaches\. However, these methods require large and representative datasets to capture the substantial heterogeneity of mental disorders, which can be difficult to obtain due to stigma, disease severity, and limited access to mental healthcare\. This work investigates the feasibility of identifying mental disorders using data exclusively from a non\-psychiatric population\. The underlying hypothesis is that this population exhibits greater homogeneity and can therefore be characterized using a variational autoencoder with a Gaussian latent space\. The proposed approach uses reconstruction errors from the non\-psychiatric training population as a reference, with substantially higher errors in new observations indicating a potential mental disorder\. Results show that the proposed VAE achieves performance comparable to that of the three aforementioned supervised techniques in the two dataset used\. For the first dataset, the accuracy, sensitivity, specificity and ROC\-AUC of the VAE were 0\.88, 0\.90, 0\.85 and 0\.95\. These performance measures were 0\.83, 0\.86, 0\.80, and 0\.88 for decision trees; 0\.88, 0\.91, 0\.85 and 0\.94 for random forest; and 0\.88, 0\.91, 0\.85 and 0\.95 for support vector machines\. Similar results were obtained for the second dataset\. Moreover, the progressive increase in reconstruction errors across populations may reflect greater deviations from the patterns observed in the non\-psychiatric population in groups with greater clinical complexity, supporting its potential use as a screening tool\.
