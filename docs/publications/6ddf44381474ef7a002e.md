# Intrinsic and Organizational Drivers of Civil Servants&\#x27; Work Motivation in Public Service Delivery: A Documentary Evidence Synthesis from Central Maluku, Indonesia

**Autores:** Pnielya Jenny Lessi; Wahab Tuanaya; H\.V\.R\. Pattimukay; Muhtar Muhtar

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Civil servants&\#x27; motivation is central to public\-service performance, particularly in geographically dispersed local governments\. This study identifies intrinsic motivational mechanisms and organizational conditions relevant to public\-service delivery at the Regional Personnel and Human Resources Development Agency \(BKPSDM\) of Central Maluku, Indonesia\. A qualitative documentary case study combined with an integrative evidence synthesis was employed\. A final evidence set of 21 sources was organized into four source groups, guided by a documented search logic and eligibility protocol\. Deductive and inductive coding were applied, and driver clusters were retained through three independent evidence\-stream criteria: broader evidence \(A\), Indonesian public\-sector empirical evidence \(B\), and direct Central Maluku/BKPSDM relevance \(C\)\. The findings show that recurrent intrinsic mechanisms include recognition and achievement, responsibility and autonomy, professional growth and competence, and public\-service meaning\. Organizational conditions include supervisory and organizational support, goal and administrative clarity, career development and non\-financial recognition, workable administrative systems, manageable job demands, supportive working conditions, and service\-user feedback\. These findings suggest that motivation should be managed through a complementary architecture that links meaningful work and psychological need satisfaction with organizational resources and constraints\. The documentary design provides a context\-grounded framework for field testing but does not estimate employee\-level causal effects\.
