Motivation and Goals of the ThesisConstrained by exceptionally small genomes, they must maximize the use of their resources and encode all the instructions necessary to enter host cells, replicate efficiently, evade the host immunity as much as possible and spread to new hosts\.RNA viruses are able to achieve this goal through elegant and often sophisticated strategies that make them compact and versatile just like swiss\-army knives \(Fig\. 1\)\.The central aim of this thesis is to investigate the potential of genomic features beyond the primary nucleotide sequence, such as RNA secondary and tertiary structures, as a new lens to understand viral biology\.Rather than viewing the viral genome merely as a linear script for protein production, this work embraces the idea that viruses are multifunctional systems, which must be studied across molecular, evolutionary, and ecological scales\.The following chapters apply this perspective to a diverse set of RNA viruses, using complementary approaches such as computational modeling, biochemical probing, phylogenetic analysis, and field\-based ecological surveillance\.
