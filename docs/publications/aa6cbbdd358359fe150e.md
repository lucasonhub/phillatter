# Kiasat, Majed: Dynamic Neighborliness: An Institutional Framework for the Relationship Between Human and AI Institutions

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Rain That Does Not Fall in Silicon: Dynamic Neighborliness and the Future of Human and AI Institutions is a work of philosophical theory\. It asks not what will happen, but what is possible, and under what conditions\. Its subject is the relationship between the human institution and the possible AI institution\. The book argues that this relationship should be framed neither as control nor as alignment, but as an architecture in which distance is the central category\. The book proposes the concept of Dynamic Neighborliness: a relationship between two parties, each with sufficient independence, sustained through the continuous reconfiguration of distance, reflective pause, and exit\. These three functions are derived from the continual change of institutional goals and take institutional form in three reciprocal rights: the right to an epistemic boundary, the right to a structural pause, and the guaranteed right of exit\. The book specifies four eligibility conditions for Dynamic Neighborliness, and a threshold of necessity that separates optional from obligatory neighborliness and defines destructive competition as the denial of a dependence that has crossed that threshold\. The book is a structural analysis of possibility\. It neither predicts the future nor issues absolute prescriptions\. Its normative stance is conditional: if a relationship free of domination between such institutions is desirable, then certain conditions are necessary\. It states its central claim in contestable form: any relationship between independent institutions whose goals keep changing and whose interdependence has crossed the threshold of necessity can remain free of domination only if both parties can reconfigure distance, pause, and exit\. The book begins from the gap between knowing about the world and living it, which it calls the feeling of being, and suspends judgment about machine experience\. It distinguishes the AI institution from tools and individual agents, defines functional will as an orientation arising from structural need rather than consciousness, and locates the beginning of any future neighborliness in the initial architecture of systems built today\. It extends its analysis to networks, where neighborliness is not transitive and sufficient independence works as a bulkhead, and it brings the Global South into the theory, treating the mass of unenhanced humans at the margins of intelligent networks as agents rather than as noise\. Chapters 1 and 2 open the question from the standpoint of experience and self\-awareness\. Chapters 3 through 8 pursue it in the fields of cognition, decision\-making, resources, hidden networks, self\-improvement, and influence over power, and for the most part depict relationships that are still instrumental or on the threshold of Dynamic Neighborliness\. Chapters 9 through 12 formulate Dynamic Neighborliness as horizon and possibility through scenarios at four levels, none of which is a prediction\. The appendices connect the theory with the philosophy of technology, set out the rights of both parties, examine the experience of the Global South, and state the book’s method\. The manuscript is complete\.
