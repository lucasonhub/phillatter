# Nausner, Peter: Enforcement is not authorisation: Who sets the limits that agent\-safety infrastructure enforces?

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Agent\-safety infrastructure is beginning to move the enforcement of limits out of the AI agent, onto separate hardware and into monitors the agent cannot get past\. This research note argues that the word authority, as the debate uses it, covers two things that are now moving separately: enforcement, which makes a limit hold, and setting, which determines where the limit lies\. A profile of outside instances measures them apart, among other dimensions by externality to the agent and to the operator, by power to enforce, by power to set, and by binding effect\. Applied to six cases from September 2026, from embedded evaluation and cross\-developer incident reports to California&\#x27;s verification statutes and NVIDIA&\#x27;s open agent\-safety reference design, it shows that observation is externalised widely, that enforcement leaves the agent but not the operator, and that outside setting exists for verifiers rather than for agents\. Proposals to limit the production of capability reproduce the pattern\. A bill adopted by the Austrian Parliament, proposed § 18a of the General Administrative Procedure Act on fully automated administrative acts \(not yet promulgated as of 12 October 2026\), shows what operator\-independent grounding, visible deployment and review can look like, and where they stop\. The note derives three requirements for limits that affect third parties, and a boundary: a system may enforce a limit that someone has set; it must not set a limit that no one has\. A postscript covers developments to 10 October 2026, including a New York City Council bill on third\-party validation and shut\-down capability\.
