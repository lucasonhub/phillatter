# AFFORDABLE PHARMACOGENOMICS IN BRAZIL&\#x27;S UNIFIED HEALTH SYSTEM: COST LEVERS, THE ROLE OF THE CLINICAL PHARMACIST AND THE FARMA\-GEN SUS IMPLEMENTATION MODEL

**Autores:** Joao Fernandes Floriano; María Elizabeth Menezes

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Clinical pharmacogenomic guidelines are available, but implementation in Brazil&\#x27;s Unified Health System \(SUS\) faces testing costs, fragmented care pathways and limited reuse of results\. Objective: To examine the determinants of pharmacogenomic costs and value in the SUS and to propose FARMA\-GEN SUS, an author\-proposed implementation model not adopted by the Ministry of Health, combining a multigene panel triggered by a first high\-yield clinical indication, interoperable records and pharmacist–prescriber collaboration\. Methods: Critical, proposal\-oriented essay integrating clinical trials, economic evaluations, Brazilian population studies and regulatory documents in force on 13 October 2026, supplemented by targeted PubMed and BVS/LILACS searches\. Published evidence, illustrative calculations and implementation proposals were considered separately; no primary clinical data were collected\. Synthesis: In PREPARE, clinically relevant adverse drug reactions among patients with actionable gene–drug interactions occurred in 21\.0% of the guided group versus 27\.7% of controls \(OR 0\.70; 95% CI 0\.54–0\.91\)\. The associated economic analysis identified the test price as a major cost driver, without establishing savings for Brazil\. The proposed model prioritises high\-yield clinical triggers, fit\-for\-purpose analytical coverage, structured recording, clinical interpretation and later reuse\. An illustrative CYP2C19–clopidogrel break\-even exercise used hypothetical clinical assumptions and a non\-significant effect from TAILOR\-PCI; with median public\-purchase prices \(2025–2026\), the annual incremental cost of ticagrelor far exceeded the break\-even ceilings against clopidogrel, whereas a counterfactual against universal ticagrelor suggested possible savings\. Results depend on effect size, medicine and test costs and the comparator; the exercise is not a cost\-effectiveness study\. Conclusion: Savings for the SUS have not been demonstrated\. FARMA\-GEN SUS should be evaluated in a prospective comparative pilot measuring health outcomes \(events avoided, QALYs\), safety, micro\-costed expenditure, access, implementation and budget impact before any scale\-up\.
