# Case Report: Four\-year durable remission, cerebral oligoprogression, and femoral head necrosis following severe recurrent immune checkpoint inhibitor\-related pneumonitis in a patient with stage IV lung adenocarcinoma

**Autores:** Yanjun Xue; Aizhen Xu; Xin Tong; 尹辛大; Lei Wei

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Severe immune checkpoint inhibitor\-related pneumonitis \(CIP\) typically leads to the permanent discontinuation of immunotherapy; however, its long\-term oncological outcomes and late drug toxicity remain poorly defined\. This report describes a patient with stage IV lung adenocarcinoma who, following recurrent grade 3 CIP, achieved durable systemic remission for more than 4 years and displayed a distinctive pattern of cerebral oligoprogression\. A 66\-year\-old male patient with stage IV lung adenocarcinoma achieved a partial response \(PR\) after four cycles of sintilimab combined with chemotherapy\. Three weeks after the last dose, he developed grade 3 CIP complicated by acute pulmonary embolism\. Recurrence occurred twice because of overly rapid corticosteroid tapering and was ultimately controlled through an ultralow tapering regimen \(50 mg/day prednisone initially, reduced by 5 mg per week\) combined with pirfenidone for antifibrotic therapy\. Following the resolution of CIP, no further systemic antitumor therapy was administered\. During 48 months of follow\-up, the patient maintained complete remission \(CR\) of extracranial metastases; the sole site of progression was a solitary cerebellar lesion, which exhibited three episodes of oligoprogression, each of which was successfully managed with either radiotherapy or bevacizumab\-based therapy\. At 34 months after the onset of CIP, the patient developed bilateral osteonecrosis of the femoral head \(ONFH\), with right\-sided slight collapse \(ARCO stage III\) and left\-sided stage I\. This case suggests a potential synergistic toxic mechanism between cumulative corticosteroid exposure \(approximately 7,300 mg prednisone equivalents\) and VEGF inhibition\-mediated microvascular injury\. This case also suggests that ultralow corticosteroid tapering combined with antifibrotic therapy can effectively manage refractory CIP; the dissociation between durable peripheral immunological memory and isolated central nervous system \(CNS\) progression supports the hypothesis of CNS immune privilege\. Finally, clinicians must remain vigilant about long\-term musculoskeletal toxicity in long\-term survivors of ICI therapy\.
