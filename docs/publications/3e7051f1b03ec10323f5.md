# MagNavAI: Physics\-Guided Feature Engineering for Magnetic Localization in GNSS\-Denied Environments

**Autores:** Murat Salim Karabinaoğlu

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract This study introduces a physics\-guided feature engineering \(FE\) framework for magnetic localization in GNSS\-denied environments, addressing a key limitation: raw geomagnetic measurements are weakly informative for spatial discrimination\. The proposed framework transforms corrected magnetic measurements into a high\-dimensional, physically interpretable feature space by encoding directional field properties \(magnitude, inclination, azimuth\), anomaly\-based deviations relative to IGRF and survey references, and multi\-scale statistical descriptors capturing local spatial structure\. This representation is designed to enhance the spatial discriminability of magnetic signatures and improve their utility for machine\-learning\-based localization\. The approach is evaluated on an open\-access aeromagnetic dataset using controlled Extra Trees configurations representing non\-feature\-engineered, physics\-guided, and map\-assisted feature settings\. The framework was evaluated using a nested leave\-one\-flight\-out \(LOFO\) protocol that separates complete flight trajectories between training, hyperparameter optimization, and testing\. Three Extra Trees configurations were examined: NFE\-No\-OGS, FE\-No\-OGS, and FE\-Map\-Assisted\. Under this stricter within\-region held\-out\-flight evaluation, the mean DRMS decreased from 20\.55 km for NFE\-No\-OGS to 15\.12 km for FE\-No\-OGS, corresponding to a 26\.4% reduction\. Improvement was observed for all six held\-out flights\. The map\-assisted variant further reduced the mean DRMS to 12\.92 km\. These results demonstrate that the proposed feature representation improves within\-region, held\-out\-flight generalization independently of OGS\-derived survey information, while survey\-reference features provide additional predictive value\. The results should therefore be interpreted as evidence of improved supervised magnetic fingerprint regression within the evaluated survey region rather than as cross\-region localization capability\. Overall, the revised results indicate that feature representation plays an important role in within\-region held\-out\-flight magnetic localization performance and should be evaluated independently from survey\-reference information\.
