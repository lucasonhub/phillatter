# ChemExplain: A Multimodal Benchmark for Explanatory Chemical Reasoning

**Autores:** Aotian Chen; Frazier N\. Baker; Reza Averly; Shan Zhang; Tianao Chen; Daniel Adu\-Ampratwum; Xia Ning; Yuan Xue

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Current chemistry benchmarks primarily evaluate outcome prediction or reaction pathway generation, but rarely test whether a model can explain why an observed chemical effect occurs\. We introduce ChemExplain, a benchmark of 1,180 multiple\-choice chemistry questions drawn from 383 peer\-reviewed papers\. Each question pairs a molecular or reaction image with an observed chemical effect and four candidate explanations, each associated with a different structural feature visible in the image\. Answering requires the model to perceive the depicted structure, apply chemical knowledge, and identify which feature most directly explains the effect, a capability we term explanatory attribution\. The correct feature\-mechanism relation is derived from supporting evidence in the source paper rather than invented by the question\-generation LLM\. We construct the benchmark automatically with a multi\-agent framework that applies progressive quality control at three stages and validate it with eight chemistry domain experts\. The strongest evaluated model reaches 78\.6% accuracy, leaving considerable room for improvement\. On a 600\-question expert\-reviewed subset, reasoning\-trace analysis classifies 71\.4% of the strongest model&\#x27;s errors as attribution failures\. Attribution is also the largest error category for the open\-source and chemistry\-specific models included in our analysis\.
