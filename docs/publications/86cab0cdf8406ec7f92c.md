# From Fabricated Citations to Unverified Journal Matches: Problems and Solutions in LLM Assisted Manuscript Generation

**Autores:** Indri Anjar Kartika Sari; Onno Widodo Purbo; Abi Julian Saputra Pratama; Rahardian Dwi Saputra

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The growing use of large language models \(LLMs\) in scholarly writing introduces risks of fabricated references, unsupported claims, and unreliable journal recommendations\. Evidence gating addresses these risks by allowing outputs only when verifiable conditions are met and requiring explicit abstention otherwise\. This study evaluates evidence gating through a design science research approach, examining scopus\-jurnal, an open\-source Python multi\-agent pipeline that retrieves literature, drafts IMRaD sections, validates citations, reviews manuscripts, and recommends journals\. Five evaluation episodes combined traceability audits and white\-box experiments covering 46 in\-text citations, 122 verification cases, 14 reviewer output formats, and 40 journal\-matching probes\. Results show that although the pipeline abstained when evidence was absent, weaknesses emerged between LLM outputs and deterministic code\. Two of 17 specified phases lacked implementation, restricting the pipeline to drafting framing sections\. Citation detection resolved only 31\.6% of valid APA citations, while fabricated citations were sometimes accepted\. Title matching accepted all 14 fabricated near\-miss titles and rejected eight of nine legitimate short titles\. Reviewer\-score parsing failed on seven of ten plausible formats\. A revised verification rule reduced false acceptance from 33\.3% to 2\.4% and false rejection from 43\.1% to 0%\. Journal\-matching experiments showed that scope evidence without a score threshold enabled safe abstention, whereas introducing a threshold eliminated abstention\. These findings emphasize tool\-level evaluation and contribute an audit protocol, seven design principles, a reference architecture, eight integrity\-preserving solutions, and five future research priorities\.
