# Optimising Irish Fen Habitat Mapping: An Evaluation of Temporal Windowing and Spectral Features Using Google Earth Engine and Random Forest

**Autores:** Ciara Farrell; Yuanyuan Pu; Shane Regan; Lizy Abraham; Owen Naughton

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

High spectral similarity and overlapping habitat boundaries present a substantial challenge for mapping and monitoring Ireland’s ecologically vital fen ecosystems\. This study establishes a reproducible remote sensing workflow by combining Google Earth Engine \(GEE\) with Sentinel\-2 imagery for Irish fen classification\. This study uses a Random Forest machine learning model to evaluate five temporal windows against three spectral feature sets across three distinct sites\. The results indicate that a growing season window \(June–September\) incorporating both bands and spectral indices captures peak phenological information more effectively than annual composites, producing high within\-site, pixel\-level overall accuracy across all study sites \(Scragh Bog: 95\.24%, Liskeenan Fen: 94\.13%, Ballymore Fen: 94\.43%\)\. Although raw spectral bands achieved marginally higher localised accuracies at Liskeenan \(\+0\.24%\) and Ballymore \(\+1\.65%\), the combined feature set was maintained as the standard workflow to ensure cross\-site stability, biophysical parameterisation, and resilience against localised soil background noise\. To accommodate narrow, fragmented geometries, training data was extracted via pixel\-level stratified sampling; while this introduces spatial autocorrelation, classification outputs showed coherent within\-site spatial patterns, although accuracy estimates remain subject to spatial autocorrelation\. Crucially, the Google Earth Engine\-based Random Forest model maintained stable within\-site classification performance when reducing the training\-testing ratio from a standard 80:20 split down to a highly constrained 50:50 split\. Ultimately, this workflow provides a cloud\-based framework for monitoring vulnerable wetland ecosystems under real\-world geometric and ground\-truth data limitations\.
