# Niami, Morteza: Finite Continuance: A Normative Theory of Agency Under Chronic Instability

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Contemporary theories of practical rationality predominantly assume that agents operate in environments where optimization remains a meaningful normative objective\. Whether framed as utility maximization, preference satisfaction, eudaimonic flourishing, or the realization of the good, these approaches presuppose a degree of environmental stability sufficient to render optimization feasible and rational\. However, prolonged instability, structural uncertainty, and chronic erosion increasingly characterize the lived condition of late modernity\. This article develops the Philosophy of Finite Continuance as a novel normative framework for agency under chronic instability\. The central thesis is that the preservation of agency itself—not utility maximization, not flourishing, not resilience—should constitute the fundamental criterion of rational action when environmental instability reaches chronic levels\. The theory introduces three interconnected mechanisms—Constructive Leakage, Responsible Suspension, and Silent Endurance—as adaptive strategies that regulate exposure to erosive conditions while maintaining the integrity of agency over time\. Through critical engagement with virtue ethics, bounded rationality, predictive processing, and existential philosophy, I demonstrate that optimization becomes normatively secondary once instability is chronic\. Rationality, under such conditions, is best understood as the intelligent regulation of vulnerability\. The article concludes by outlining implications for moral philosophy, political theory, cognitive science, and the emerging discourse on agency in the Anthropocene\. Keywords: finite continuance, responsible suspension, constructive leakage, silent endurance, agency, practical rationality, chronic instability, structural ethics, normative theory \(direct link \)
