# RANKING ALGORITHMS FOR MEDICAL INFORMATION SYSTEMS BASED ON NOMINAL DATA

**Autores:** Rajapboyeva Hulkaraxon Sotiboldiyevna

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid expansion of electronic health records \(EHRs\), hospital information systems, clinical decision\-support platforms, and digital patient registries has increased the need for reliable algorithms capable of processing heterogeneous clinical information\. A substantial proportion of medical information is nominal rather than numerical or ordinal\. Examples include diagnosis categories, medication classes, symptom types, admission reasons, procedure codes, departments, blood groups, and other categorical attributes\. Nominal categories have no inherent mathematical order; therefore, assigning arbitrary numerical codes to them may introduce artificial relationships and distort the results of statistical or machine\-learning models\. This article examines algorithms for solving ranking problems in medical information systems when the underlying information is nominal\. The methodological framework considers categorical\-data representation, frequency\-based ranking, probabilistic ranking, multinomial logistic regression, decision trees, random forests, support\-vector approaches, learning\-to\-rank methods, and rank aggregation\. Particular attention is given to one\-hot encoding and other appropriate representations of nominal variables, because preserving the absence of ordinal meaning is fundamental to valid modelling\. Published medical informatics studies demonstrate that ranking can support emergency triage, retrieval of relevant EHR information, clinical prediction, and prioritization of patient\-related information\. For example, a learning\-to\-rank study involving 90 EHR notes reported an AUC\-ROC of 0\.940 for ranking candidate medical terms, while a structured\-record relevance\-ranking study evaluated 1,010 medications and 2,913 problems from 99 patients\. These published results demonstrate the practical feasibility of ranking approaches in healthcare, although performance depends strongly on data quality, outcome definition, validation, calibration, and clinical context\. The article proposes a general algorithmic framework for nominal\-data ranking that combines categorical encoding, clinically meaningful feature construction, model training, ranking\-score generation, validation, and human oversight\.
