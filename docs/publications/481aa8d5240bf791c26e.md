# Toward Responsible and Sustainable AI Integration in Education: A Cross\-National Analysis of Factors Influencing Teachers’ AI Adoption Using TALIS 2024

**Autores:** Chenhan Zhao

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study investigates how AI\-specific and contextual constructs relate to teachers’ adoption of artificial intelligence, and whether these relationships vary across education systems\. Teachers are central to achieving Sustainable Development Goal 4 through responsible AI integration, yet most prior work relies on general technology acceptance frameworks and single\-system samples\. Drawing on TAM and UTAUT, this study relates AI use to perceived AI usefulness, perceived AI risks, and four contextual constructs: AI\-related training, teachers’ stress, institutional support, and school innovation climate\. This model was tested with TALIS 2024 data from 1766 lower secondary teachers in the United Arab Emirates and Shanghai, China\. Structural equation modeling and multi\-group SEM showed that training \(β=0\.401,p&lt;0\.001\) and perceived AI usefulness \(β=0\.250,p&lt;0\.001\) were the strongest direct constructs associated with AI use\. Contrary to expectations, perceived AI risks were positively associated with perceived usefulness in both samples \(China: β=0\.308,p&lt;0\.001; UAE: β=0\.390,p&lt;0\.001\), which suggests that teachers who recognize potential risks may also tend to acknowledge AI’s pedagogical value\. Multi\-group comparisons showed that institutional support was found to be associated with perceived usefulness only among Chinese teachers, whereas school innovation climate was found to be associated with perceived usefulness only among UAE teachers, though only the institutional\-support difference was significant in cross\-group tests\. These findings point to context\-sensitive professional development policies that support sustainable AI integration in education\.
