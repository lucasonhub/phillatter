# Assessment of change in drug\-induced parkinsonism in daily\-life gait using vision\-based machine learning

**Autores:** Benjamin Filtjens; Vida Adeli; Soroush Mehraban; Andrea Sabo; Babak Taati; Andrea Iaboni

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Drug\-induced parkinsonism is a potentially reversible movement disorder that may be subtle and difficult to monitor in residential dementia care\. Vision\-based machine learning analysis of gait may provide unobtrusive longitudinal monitoring around medication changes\. We conducted a two\-center cohort study of 61 residents with dementia receiving psychotropic medication\. Hallway gait sequences were recorded using ceiling\-mounted cameras and converted to three\-dimensional body pose\. A TabPFN classifier estimated UPDRS\-III item 29 gait severity for each walk\. We evaluated associations between acute antipsychotic \(AP\) and antidepressant \(AD\) dose reduction/cessation \(≥50% dose reduction or cessation, 0\-21 days after change\) and daily mean predicted gait severity using linear mixed\-effects models in ever\-exposed participants, with complementary equal\-weight paired analyses among participants observed in both medication states\. In mixed\-effects models, acute AP dose reduction/cessation was associated with lower predicted gait severity \(β=\-0\.127, p = 0\.022\), whereas AD dose reduction/cessation was not \(β=\-0\.060, p = 0\.210\)\. Equal\-weight paired analyses found no statistically significant participant\-average within\-person change among paired participants \(AP mean change = 0\.031, 95% CI \[\-0\.114, 0\.176\], p = 0\.653; AD mean change =\-0\.021, 95% CI \[\-0\.098, 0\.056\], p = 0\.575\), consistent with heterogeneous individual responses\. Vision\-based monitoring captured within\-participant gait variation around psychotropic dose\-reduction periods, but findings are associative\. Larger prospective studies with documented reasons for medication changes are needed before using such monitoring to guide medication decisions\.
