# Bart’s syndrome in a preterm neonate: A case report

**Autores:** Farhana Quyum; Asma Akter; Farzana Zulfia Khan; Nazmun Nahar

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Bart’s syndrome is a rare congenital disorder characterized by aplasia cutis congenita, widespread blistering, and nail dystrophy\. It is now recognized as a clinical manifestation of epidermolysis bullosa and has been reported in association with serious congenital abnormalities, including pyloric atresia, urethral stenosis, renal anomalies, rudimentary ear development, and facial dysmorphisms\. We present a male neonate, 2 nd child born of a consanguineous marriage, born at 34 weeks of gestation with a birth weight of 1590 g\. Physical examination revealed extensive bilateral congenital absence of skin over the lower limbs and left forearm, localized bullae, and nail abnormalities\. Associated findings included a right ear deformity and a small atrial septal defect \(ASD\) with a left\-to\-right shunt confirmed by echo color Doppler\. Management focused on rigorous conservative measures, including broad\-spectrum intravenous antibiotics, mupirocin\-impregnated dressings for wound care, and maintenance of hydration and temperature\. Despite timely and appropriate measures, the infant developed severe respiratory distress and died on the 3 rd day of life\. As ASD was not reported in Bart’s syndrome before, and small ASDs are common in neonates, this could be a chance association\. The extent of blistering at birth may not be a proper indicator of long\-term outcome\. However, the presence of associated congenital abnormalities significantly worsens the prognosis of Bart’s syndrome, underscoring the necessity of a thorough systemic workup and aggressive supportive care in these vulnerable neonates\.
