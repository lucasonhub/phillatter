  also significantly shorter, with a nonsignificant increase in the NASA-TLX score.
  Furthermore, a Neurobehavioural Performance Index (NBPI) for automation was proposed
  and validated to evaluate human and task performance and understand human OOTL through
  the underlying cognitive mechanisms. The results suggested that future cockpit automation
  designs should consider an effective task distribution for HAT. The second study
  of this thesis proposes an adaptive automation framework based on EEG spectral features
  and spatiotemporal deep learning with 2D/3D Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN)
  and Long-Short Term Memory (LSTM) to keep pilots in the loop. Our results indicated
  that the proposed two-stage 2D CNN-LSTM model with stacked images yields a test
  accuracy of 0.9918. To ensure safe applications in high-stakes domains like aviation,
  the Shapley additive explanations were adopted to strengthen the interpretability
  of the model. High delta and theta waves with low beta and gamma waves were found
  contributing positively to the OOTL state. It indicates that the classification
  aligns with the theoretical background and is trustworthy. The trustworthy adaptive
  deep learning model supports the dynamic automation configuration for improving
  human-automation collaboration in cruising flights. The model designed can support
  adaptive and dynamic automation configuration that tunes the level of automation
  to improve the human-automation collaboration in cruising flights. The third study
  of this thesis further advances intelligent automation through analysing the radio
  communication between pilots and air traffic controllers (ATCOs) using speech recognition
  and the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). For mutual verification,
  pilots are required to read back the instructions and clearance from ATCOs in a
  timely manner to mitigate communication errors. Hence, speech recognition was utilised
  to transcribe radio communication in the readback procedure to text and identify
  vocal cues of human OOTL with the reasoning ability of LLMs. Fine-tuning was performed
  to enhance the aviation-specific knowledge of the LLMs. Furthermore, a chain-of-thought
  (CoT) and fault-tolerant approach is adopted to design prompts to enhance the explainability
  for trustworthy applications of the model in safety-critical domains. To validate
  the framework in real-time, a case study with ten pilots is conducted on an Airbus
  A320 simulator. Our model successfully achieved an accuracy of 0.8722 and 0.8144
  in offline and real-time OOTL detection, respectively. Based on dynamic automation
  tuning, the proposed Voice2Vigilance framework contributes by offering a trustworthy,
  non-intrusive, and cockpit-ready solution to achieve HAT in flight operations. Summing
  up, this thesis examines human OOTL from multiple perspectives, including neuroergonomics,
  behaviour, and communication. It contributes to safer flight operations with AI-driven
  intelligent automation to keep pilots in the loop.

# Trustworthy intelligent automation for human\-automation teaming in flight operations

**Autores:** Cho Yin Yiu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Automation has been extensively used in flight operations to enhance work efficiency and alleviate the cognitive workload of operators during demanding tasks such as manual flying\. As automation has become increasingly reliable in the past decades, pilots are now supervisors of automation rather than operators\. However, pilots might experience a decline in vigilance over time and eventually become delinked from the control loop, posing a significant safety threat to flight operations\. Hence, identifying human out\-of\-the\-loop \(OOTL\) in the cockpit efficiently is critical to ensure flight safety while not overloading human operators\. Therefore, this thesis seeks an integrated cockpit automation solution with neuroergonomics, cockpit/air\-ground interaction, and artificial intelligence \(AI\) to achieve human\-automation teaming \(HAT\) through balancing human and task performance with a minimal trade\-off in cognitive workload\. The first study of this thesis focuses on an integrated empirical appraisal of human and task performance of pilots using electroencephalography \(EEG\), reaction time, and NASA Task Load Index \(TLX\)\. Twenty\-four cadet pilots were recruited to accomplish a flight task on an Airbus A320 flight simulator with different levels of automation \(LOA\)\. The results from the EEG indicated that partial automation raises pilots&\#x27; engagement and reduces pilots&\#x27; drowsiness\. The reaction time was also significantly shorter, with a nonsignificant increase in the NASA\-TLX score\. Furthermore, a Neurobehavioural Performance Index \(NBPI\) for automation was proposed and validated to evaluate human and task performance and understand human OOTL through the underlying cognitive mechanisms\. The results suggested that future cockpit automation designs should consider an effective task distribution for HAT\. The second study of this thesis proposes an adaptive automation framework based on EEG spectral features and spatiotemporal deep learning with 2D/3D Convolutional Neural Networks \(CNN\) and Long\-Short Term Memory \(LSTM\) to keep pilots in the loop\. Our results indicated that the proposed two\-stage 2D CNN\-LSTM model with stacked images yields a test accuracy of 0\.9918\. To ensure safe applications in high\-stakes domains like aviation, the Shapley additive explanations were adopted to strengthen the interpretability of the model\. High delta and theta waves with low beta and gamma waves were found contributing positively to the OOTL state\. It indicates that the classification aligns with the theoretical background and is trustworthy\. The trustworthy adaptive deep learning model supports the dynamic automation configuration for improving human\-automation collaboration in cruising flights\. The model designed can support adaptive and dynamic automation configuration that tunes the level of automation to improve the human\-automation collaboration in cruising flights\. The third study of this thesis further advances intelligent automation through analysing the radio communication between pilots and air traffic controllers \(ATCOs\) using speech recognition and the reasoning capabilities of large language models \(LLMs\)\. For mutual verification, pilots are required to read back the instructions and clearance from ATCOs in a timely manner to mitigate communication errors\. Hence, speech recognition was utilised to transcribe radio communication in the readback procedure to text and identify vocal cues of human OOTL with the reasoning ability of LLMs\. Fine\-tuning was performed to enhance the aviation\-specific knowledge of the LLMs\. Furthermore, a chain\-of\-thought \(CoT\) and fault\-tolerant approach is adopted to design prompts to enhance the explainability for trustworthy applications of the model in safety\-critical domains\. To validate the framework in real\-time, a case study with ten pilots is conducted on an Airbus A320 simulator\. Our model successfully achieved an accuracy of 0\.8722 and 0\.8144 in offline and real\-time OOTL detection, respectively\. Based on dynamic automation tuning, the proposed Voice2Vigilance framework contributes by offering a trustworthy, non\-intrusive, and cockpit\-ready solution to achieve HAT in flight operations\. Summing up, this thesis examines human OOTL from multiple perspectives, including neuroergonomics, behaviour, and communication\. It contributes to safer flight operations with AI\-driven intelligent automation to keep pilots in the loop\.
