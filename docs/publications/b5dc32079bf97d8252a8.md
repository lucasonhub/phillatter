# Moderate phosphorus reduction under split drip fertigation maintains maize yield and coincides with closer root–available phosphorus spatial correspondence

**Autores:** Changle Liu; Shuping Hu; 马达灵; Liqing Wang; Xiaofang Yu; Julin Gao

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Phosphorus \(P\) has low mobility in soil, so crop P acquisition depends on both P availability and its spatial accessibility to roots\. We hypothesized that moderate P reduction under split fertigation could maintain maize productivity by improving the coordination between available P and root distribution\. Methods A two\-year field experiment was conducted in irrigated spring maize in Inner Mongolia, China\. Split monoammonium phosphate fertigation was applied at 105 \(CP1\), 127\.5 \(CP2\), and 150 kg P 2 O 5 ha \-1 \(CP3\), together with a no\-P treatment \(CP0\) and conventional basal diammonium phosphate application at 150 kg P 2 O 5 ha \-1 \(CK\)\. Root length density and Olsen P were measured in paired soil blocks within the 0–40 cm soil profile at silking, together with plant P accumulation, biomass, grain yield, and P\-use efficiency\. Results CP2 increased Olsen P availability and root proliferation in the middle and deeper soil layers and showed closer spatial correspondence between root length density and Olsen P at 20–40 cm\. These changes were associated with greater post\-silking P accumulation and biomass\. Reducing P input by 15% maintained grain yield at the level of CP3 and increased yield by 4\.07% relative to CK, while apparent P recovery efficiency and P agronomic efficiency increased by 77\.36% and 54\.62%, respectively\. Further reducing P input to 105 kg P 2 O 5 ha⁻¹ decreased P accumulation, biomass, and yield, whereas 150 kg P 2 O 5 ha \-1 provided no additional yield benefit\. Discussion Under the conditions of this study, moderate P reduction under split fertigation maintained maize yield while reducing fertilizer input and enhancing P\-use efficiency, and this response was associated with closer spatial correspondence between root distribution and soil available P\.
