# Large Language Models for Cybersecurity in the Quantum Era: A Critical Review of Trustworthy AI, Emerging Threats, and Post\-Quantum Defense

**Autores:** Muhammad Awais; Muhammad Ayaz

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The convergence of two disruptive forces — large\-scale foundation models \(large languagemodels, LLMs, and multimodal systems\) and the maturing threat of quantum computing — is reshaping cybersecurityfrom both the defensive and offensive sides\. Large models are being embedded into security operations, vulnerabilitydiscovery, and threat\-intelligence pipelines, while quantum algorithms threaten to invalidate the public\-key infrastructureunderlying nearly all deployed cryptographic protocols\. Despite an explosion of publications in both areas, the literatureremains fragmented: AI\-for\-security research rarely engages with post\-quantum considerations, and post\-quantumcryptography \(PQC\) research rarely engages with the trustworthiness failures of the AI systems that will manage themigration\. Methods\. This paper conducts a critical, structured review synthesizing evidence from AI security, systemssecurity, and post\-quantum cryptography literature published primarily between 2020 and 2026\. We construct a multidimensional taxonomy of AI\-for\-cybersecurity paradigms, systematize large\-model\-specific trustworthiness failures —adversarial robustness, data poisoning, prompt injection, model extraction, hallucination, and supply\-chain risk — andmap the intersection between AI\-enabled defense and AI\-assisted post\-quantum migration\. Key findings\. Reportedperformance gains for LLM\-based security tools are frequently measured on narrow, contamination\-prone benchmarksthat do not reflect adversarial, non\-stationary production environments\. Prompt injection remains structurally unsolvedrather than merely under\-mitigated: reported task\-hijacking success rates against tool\-using agents rise sharply onceattackers tailor techniques to agent\-specific behavior\. Offensive robustness evaluation lags defensive\-capability evaluationby roughly an order of magnitude in surveyed agentic\-security papers\. AI\-assisted tooling for PQC migration —cryptographic discovery, code auditing, risk scoring — is nascent and inherits the reliability problems of the underlyingmodels it is built on\. Implications\. We argue the two challenges are entangled rather than parallel: securing the migrationto quantum\-resistant cryptography will depend on AI\-assisted automation at a scale classical tooling cannot match, yetthat automation must be trusted under exactly the adversarial conditions where current large models are weakest\. Weclose with a prioritized research agenda spanning evaluation methodology, quantum\-aware threat modeling, and cryptoagility tooling for 2026–2035\.Index Terms—artificial intelligence; cybersecurity; large language models; adversarial machine learning; promptinjection; post\-quantum cryptography; quantum computing; trustworthy AI; security operations; AI red teaming
