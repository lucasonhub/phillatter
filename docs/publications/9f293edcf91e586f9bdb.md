# MENTOR: Learning from Failure with On\-Policy Pedagogical Intervention

**Autores:** Guanlan Yue; Hang Yu; Xiaoyong Huang; Ruipeng Li; Wei Zhang; Bingchang Liu; Rui Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards \(RLVR\) is a key driver for Large Reasoning Models, but it is vulnerable to &quot;zero\-reward collapse&quot;, a training failure where a sudden increase in task difficulty causes the learning signal to vanish\. We argue that the conventional approach of discarding these failed trajectories is wasteful, as they contain valuable diagnostic information about the model&\#x27;s cognitive gaps, a bright side that can be leveraged for recovery\. Existing solutions, however, often regress to static, &quot;off\-policy\-style&quot; interventions like pre\-defined hint libraries or perform post\-hoc refinement, failing to provide the adaptive, forward\-looking guidance needed to escape the collapse\. To address this, we propose a paradigm shift towards on\-policy pedagogical intervention and introduce MENTOR, a multi\-agent framework that acts as a live mentor\. MENTOR diagnoses the root cause of failure in real\-time and dynamically generates targeted, instructional prompts to guide the learning agent&\#x27;s next attempt\. As a plug\-and\-play module, MENTOR integrates seamlessly into existing policy optimization pipelines like GRPO without modifying the training infrastructure\. Extensive experiments on math and code generation show that MENTOR effectively mitigates zero\-reward collapse, outperforming state\-of\-the\-art baselines\. Notably, it achieves massive average relative gains of up to 78\.3% on Llama\-3\.2\-3B\-Instruct over its base model\.
