# Multi\-source temporal fusion and multi\-station learning for early warning of diamondback moth infestation in Brassica crops

**Autores:** Dong Zhang; Jiale Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Meteorological variability influences diamondback moth \(DBM; Plutella xylostella \) infestation and the resulting damage to Brassica crops\. Accurate multi\-horizon forecasting requires models that preserve short\-lived meteorological exposures while integrating thermal timing, spatial dependence, and recent infestation dynamics within a transparent framework\. Methods The Temporal–Geospatial Pest Network \(TG\-PestNet\) forecasts the weekly percentage of inspected plants classified as DBM\-infested 1 to 4 weeks ahead at four Guangdong stations, from origin\-time information only\. It preserves hourly exposure, encodes lookback weeks on a thermal\-time axis, couples stations through a wind\-informed graph, and protects infestation history in a gated anchor\. Evaluation used 344 synchronized weeks, 66 later test origins, 13 history\-only comparators, retraining ablations, matched\-width controls, and input\-matched generic encoders\. Results and discussion TG\-PestNet achieved the lowest observed root\-mean\-square error, mean absolute error, and mean absolute percentage error in all 12 station–metric combinations, with mean reductions of 5\.9%–13\.0%\. Year\-ahead forecasts outperformed persistence in all four test years\. Removing the history anchor increased error by 14\.3% and remained significant after multiplicity correction, demonstrating its central contribution to forecast accuracy\. Meteorological information provided complementary predictive value: its occlusion increased the error by 9\.3%, and retraining without it increased the macro RMSE by 3\.6%\. TG\-PestNet also substantially outperformed the two input\-matched generic encoders, whose errors were approximately one quarter higher\. These results establish TG\-PestNet as an accurate, reproducible and auditable framework for multi\-station pest forecasting, with component\-level analyses that identify the dominant contribution of recent infestation history and characterize the predictive roles of high\-resolution meteorology and structured spatial information\.
