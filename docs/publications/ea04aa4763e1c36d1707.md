# AI\-driven “new migrants”: skill\-spatial mismatch and out\-migration intention

**Autores:** Di Wang; Boran Zhang; Junlong Chen

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

While AI profoundly reshapes the labor market, its spatial impact remains under\-researched\. Addressing this, this paper uses data from the China Migrant Population Dynamic Monitoring Survey and the China Patent Database across 250 prefecture\-level cities during 2014–2018 to examine AI&\#x27;s effect on labor out\-migration intention\. Findings reveal AI significantly increases out\-migration intention, especially among women and in non\-digital hubs or central/western regions\. Mechanistically, the results are consistent with a displacement channel involving routine jobs and a restructuring channel involving industrial shifts\. These channels can be understood through a “skill\-spatial mismatch” resulting from the changing spatial distribution of tasks\. Furthermore, regional aging and fiscal support amplify the positive relationship between AI and out\-migration intention\. The study concludes by emphasizing the need for a spatially inclusive employment policy framework as AI reshapes employment geography\. The findings concern workers&\#x27; stated propensity to leave, which may precede future mobility, rather than observed migration flows\.
