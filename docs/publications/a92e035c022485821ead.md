# A century of streamwater quality degradation by input from urban water infrastructure as revealed by tree\-ring records

**Autores:** Josiah M\. Sananda; Jay L\. Banner; Sneha Nachimuthu; Bryan A\. Black; J\. L\. Abella\-Gutiérrez; Kasey M\. Faust; Staci Loewy

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract The transfer of water from urban water networks to natural streams is a driver of streamwater quality degradation that likely occurs worldwide\. We develop a new proxy using tree\-ring strontium isotope ratios \( 87 Sr/ 86 Sr\) to reconstruct multidecadal histories of municipal\-water contributions to natural streams\. We apply this approach to three watersheds spanning highly urbanized, rapidly urbanizing, and rural conditions in central Texas, USA\. Municipal water has persistently dominated streamflow \(&gt;90%\) in the urban watershed since at least 1945, only 25 years after the initial expansion of Austin’s municipal water infrastructure network\. In the rapidly urbanizing watershed, the modeled municipal\-water component of stream baseflow increased from approximately 12% to 33% over only 35 years\- a 175% increase that coincided with an 85% increase in population density\. In contrast, municipal\-water contributions have remained low in the rural watershed since the 1840s\. The timing of Sr isotope increases in the urban and rapidly urbanizing watersheds indicates that municipal\-water contributions can substantially alter stream chemistry within just two decades of water infrastructure installation\. This proxy allows the reconstruction of water\-quality degradation over periods of urban growth in the 20 th and 21 st centuries, including periods that predate the initiation of modern water\-quality monitoring\. Our approach provides a tool for diagnosing the resilience of water infrastructure under accelerating urban growth and climate stress, informing proactive strategies to protect freshwater resources worldwide\.
