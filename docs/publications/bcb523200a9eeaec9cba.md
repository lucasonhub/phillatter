# Discipline and expression: piano education as a reflection of changing social values, 1850–2000

**Autores:** Baiyu Chen; Rongyi Tang

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examines how piano education functioned as both a mirror and mechanism for social transformation from 1850 to 2000, analyzing the dialectical relationship between pedagogical approaches and evolving societal values\. Through historical document analysis of pedagogical texts, institutional records, competition programmes, and cultural commentary, the research traces piano education’s evolution across four distinct periods: Victorian\-era mechanical discipline \(1850–1900\), scientifically\-informed modernist approaches \(1900–1950\), Cold War cross\-cultural exchanges \(1950–1980\), and digital\-era transformations \(1980–2000\)\. Findings reveal how piano pedagogy consistently manifested tensions between disciplinary control and expressive freedom, reflecting broader societal negotiations between institutional authority and individual agency\. Analysis of the persistent dialectic between technical standardization and artistic individualism demonstrates that successive generations reimagined the boundaries of personal autonomy within pedagogical frameworks, negotiating the scope for self\-expression against the demands of institutional conformity\. The research demonstrates that piano education served simultaneously as a conservative force reinforcing social hierarchies and a progressive medium enabling cultural transformation, offering insights into how aesthetic practices both reproduce and contest prevailing social values across different historical contexts\.
