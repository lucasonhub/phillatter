# Johnson, Jamison Todd: The Psychological Benefits and Downfalls of Legitimate and Illicit AI Use by Modern Man

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

What does artificial intelligence do to the people who use it, and what decides whether it helps or harms? This essay argues that intent is the variable that decides, and that intent gets written into a technology&\#x27;s design, defaults, and business model long before any single user touches it\. It works through the evidence in four movements\. The first is the material footprint of computing, from ENIAC to data\-center water and power, set against the substitution history of earlier technologies\. The second is the psychology of use: compulsive versus problematic use, randomized evidence that unguarded AI access harms learning while a guardrailed tutor does not, the erosion of source\-checking, and moral deskilling in automated public systems\. The third is a philosophical account of intent as the direction that gives a life and an institution its shape, drawing on Aristotle&\#x27;s final cause, prospection, Bratman&\#x27;s planning theory, and the Buddhist doctrine of cetanā, and tested against counterexamples\. The fourth is a speculative reflection on machine sentience, emanation, sub\-creation, and cognitive closure, which keeps explicitly separate what the evidence shows and what the author proposes\. The essay closes by arguing for building AI carefully and in the open, and for using it to help people create rather than to create for them\. \(direct link \)
