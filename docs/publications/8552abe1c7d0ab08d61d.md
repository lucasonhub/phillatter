# Machine learning–driven strength prediction and optimal waste glass powder dosage for concrete exposed to high temperatures

**Autores:** Yasin Onuralp Özkılıç; Osman Ahmed Umiye; Thamer Salman Alomayri; Fatih Yılmaz; Sadık Alper Yildizel; Ali İhsan Çeli̇k

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study explores the synergistic effects of the addition of waste glass powder \(WGP\) and elevated temperatures on the mechanical behavior of concrete, and proposes a machine\-learning\-based framework for mixture design optimization\. Six concrete mixtures containing 0–50% WGP were tested for compressive, flexural, and splitting tensile strengths over the temperature range 24–600 °C, and the results consistently showed an optimal WGP dosage of 10–20% at room temperature and a pronounced degradation in these strengths above 400 °C\. To model the strength evolution across the entire WGP\-temperature space, a physics\-aware data augmentation method and a multi\-output Random Forest surrogate model are developed and validated using mixture\-wise three\-fold cross\-validation, parity plots, Y\-randomization tests, SHAP\-based interpretability analysis, and bootstrap\-based uncertainty quantification\. The validated surrogate is then combined with NSGA\-II to determine WGP dosages that simultaneously maximize room\-temperature strength, high\-temperature residual capacity, and material efficiency\. Both prospective within\-domain experimental validation showed close agreement between the surrogate predictions and independently measured responses\. Contour maps and optimization results also consistently show that the 10–20% WGP dosage offers the best trade\-off between initial strength and thermal resilience\. The results demonstrate the two\-fold role of WGP as a micro\-filler in improving ambient performance and as a thermally sensitive phase governing degradation mechanisms at elevated temperatures\. Therefore, this work presents a validated ML\-based framework and practical design charts for optimizing WGP\-modified concrete for fire\-exposed or high\-temperature applications\. SEM analyses of the glass powder\-reinforced concrete sample exposed to 600 °C showed an increase in cracks and voids in the microstructure; EDX results indicated Ca enrichment with the dominance of O \(51\.49%\), Ca \(20\.78%\), and Si \(8\.17%\); FT\-IR spectrum showed the disappearance of the \~ 3400 and \~ 1650 cm⁻¹ bands and the weakening of the \~ 950–1000 cm⁻¹ band, confirming C–S–H degradation; and XRD analysis revealed a decrease in amorphous humps and the prominence of crystalline peaks, indicating that the structure became more crystalline and brittle at high temperature\. TGA results indicated that room\-temperature\-cured samples exhibited higher mass loss and progressive degradation at low temperatures, whereas samples pre\-exposed to 600 °C exhibited reduced mass loss and enhanced thermal stability, owing to prior dehydration and microstructural reorganization\.
