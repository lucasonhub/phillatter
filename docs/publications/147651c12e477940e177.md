# Distinguishing FDA Expedited Program Designations from Marketing Authorization: Orphan Drug, RMAT, and Priority Review in a Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy Cell Therapy

**Autores:** Mudra Gautamkumar Naik; Mansi Ketankumar Patel; Peivand Pirouzi

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

A persistent source of confusion in rare disease drug development, among sponsors, investors, and the public alike, is the conflation of expedited\-program designations with marketing authorization itself: a product can hold Orphan Drug, Rare Pediatric Disease, Priority Review, and Regenerative Medicine Advanced Therapy designations simultaneously, none of which constitutes or guarantees approval, and each of which serves a distinct and limited regulatory function\. This article uses an allogeneic cardiosphere\-derived cell therapy for Duchenne muscular dystrophy \(DMD\) to disentangle these designations precisely, with particular attention to the frequently misunderstood distinction between Priority Review, which shortens an agency&\#x27;s target review timeline, and Accelerated Approval, which permits approval based on a surrogate endpoint reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit; the product under review holds the former but has never been eligible for the latter, since no accepted surrogate exists for its mechanism\. The article develops this product&\#x27;s full designation and regulatory history as a worked example, proposes a real\-world evidence strategy appropriate to a product advancing entirely on traditional approval standards, and closes with recommendations for regulatory affairs professionals communicating designation status accurately to internal and external stakeholders\.Keywords: expedited programs; Priority Review; Accelerated Approval; Orphan Drug designation; Duchenne muscular dystrophy; regulatory artificial intelligence
