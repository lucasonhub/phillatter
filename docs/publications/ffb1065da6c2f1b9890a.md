# A Comprehensive Review of Medicinal Plants and their bioactive as Sources of Wound Healing Agents: Mechanism of Action, Recent Developments and Future Perspectives

**Autores:** Pravin Kumar Mourya; Mayank Kumar Yadav; Raziuddin Khan; Zeashan Hussain

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Cutaneous wound healing is a complicated and highly regulated biological mechanism that restores the skin&\#x27;s functional and structural integrity after an injury\. It represents a series of various phases: haemostasis, proliferation, inflammation and remodelling\. Disruption of any of the mentioned phases can impair tissue repair, resulting in persistent, non\-healing wounds that present serious economic and therapeutic difficulties\. Although conventional wound\-care therapies have improved patient outcomes, their effectiveness may be limited by factors such as antimicrobial resistance, adverse effects, high treatment costs, and delayed tissue regeneration\. Consequently, there is increasing interest in plants with therapeutic benefits and their bioactive compounds as safer, cost\-effective, and multifunctional alternatives for wound management\. Medicinal herbs have long been used in traditional medicine systems globally, and growing scientific evidence supports their therapeutic potential in promoting wound healing\. Flavonoids, phenolic acids, alkaloids, terpenoids, tannins, saponins, glycosides, and essential oils are examples of plant\-derived bioactive chemicals that display a variety of pharmacological actions, including angiogenic, antibacterial, anti\-inflammatory, antioxidant, and collagen\-stimulating properties\. These phytochemicals contribute to wound repair by key modulation of molecular pathways, including the upregulation of VEGF \(vascular endothelial growth factor\) and TGF\-β \(Transforming Growth Factor\-β\), thereby enhancing angiogenesis, fibroblast proliferation, extracellular matrix deposition, collagen synthesis, and re\-epithelialization\. In addition, many herbal compounds suppress pro\-inflammatory mediators, including TNF\-α \(Tumour Necrosis Factor\-α\), IL\-1β \(interleukin\-1β\), and iNOS \(inducible nitric oxide synthase\), thereby reducing oxidative stress and creating a favourable environment for tissue regeneration\. This review summarises an overview of medicinal herbs with their bioactive constituents as promising woundhealing agents\. It summarises the biological mechanisms underlying wound repair and highlights the therapeutic potential of plant\-derived compounds\. Furthermore, the review addresses current challenges, available preclinical and clinical evidence, and future research directions required for the successful translation of these natural products into evidence\-based wound management\. Collectively, the available literature suggests that medicinal plants represent a valuable source of multifunctional bioactive molecules with considerable potential for the development of safe, effective, and innovative wound\-healing therapies\.
