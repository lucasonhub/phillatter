# Para além da psicopatologia: o papel da organicidade nos comportamentos suicidários inaugurais no idoso

**Autores:** Rita Isabel Martins Ortiga; Catarina Eusébio; Elisa Catarina Fernandes Lopes; Luís Fonseca

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Suicidal risk increases with age, characterized by more lethal methods and less prior communication of intent\. Although psychopathology is a well\-established classic risk factor, many older adults make first\-ever suicide attempts without any psychiatric history, challenging traditional screening models\. Through a review and two clinical cases, we explore the role of organic factors in inaugural suicidal behaviors in older adults\. Clinical information was obtained through interviews and review of hospital records, with informed consent for publication\. A woman and a man, aged 73 and 77 respectively, both without previous psychiatric follow\-up, self\-inflicted severe penetrating abdominal trauma\. The first showed hypochondriacal concern with chronic constipation that had worsened and become refractory to treatment, amplified by a family history of gastrointestinal cancer\. Sleep deprivation in the preceding days likely facilitated the transition from somatic rumination to an impulsive act\. The second developed a similar preoccupation with constipation that began after hospitalization for decompensated cardiorespiratory disease, suggesting delirium as a precipitating factor\. Constipation is an independent predictor of suicidal behaviors, mediated by the gut–brain axis: the intestinal microbiome influences serotonin synthesis, systemic inflammation, and the psychological distress associated with persistent physical discomfort\. Additionally, sleep deprivation and delirium increase impulsivity by impairing inhibitory control and judgment mediated by the prefrontal cortex\. Thus, a multidimensional assessment of suicidal risk in older adults is essential, integrating physical and mental health, with particular attention to early detection of confusional states and management of distressing somatic symptoms\.
