# Effect of an Educational Intervention on Lower Limb Pain During the Hajj Mass Gathering: A Randomized Controlled Trial

**Autores:** Yasser Bouchouata; Latifa Tahiri; Salma Elboughabi; Safa Rakhami; Ayda Belmlih; Hajar Elgmiri; Saad Sihida; Nouhaila El\-Maimouni; Meryem Kardoudi; Moncef Ziani; Hammou Jaouad; Amina Chrif Alaoui; Mustapha Oufqir; Khadija Fajjary; Fatima Jassab; Rédouane Abouqal; Jihane Belayachi; Fatine Kronbi; Hanan Rkain; Mohammed Youbi; Samia Karkouri; Fadoua Allali

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction/objectives: Lower limb pain is common and disabling from age 50 onwards, yet prevention before predictable episodes of intense ambulatory load has never been tested\.We evaluated a pre\-departure therapeutic patient education program in pilgrims attending a mass gathering\.Method: A parallel\-group randomized controlled trial \(1:1\) was conducted in two Moroccan vaccination centers: 218 pilgrims aged 50\-75 years were enrolled in April 2025 and followed until late June 2025\.The intervention group received a therapeutic patient education program, comprising a one\-hour group education session plus an unsupervised home\-based adapted physical activity program and WhatsApp reminders; controls received none\.The primary outcome was lower limb pain on a visual analog scale \(0\-10\) 48 hours after the end of the peak activity period\.Analysis was by intention to treat, using linear mixed models, with blinded assessors and analyst\.Results: The primary outcome was available for 216 of 218 participants \(mean age: 62\.6 years; 147 \(67\.4%\) women\)\.At T2 \(48 hours after the end of the peak activity period\), lower limb pain was lower in the education group \(\-1\.75 points; 95% CI: \-2\.40 to \-1\.09; p&lt;0\.001\); a smaller difference was already present seven days before departure, and the difference persisted seven days after return\.The education group also had less limping \(24 \(22%\) versus 42 \(39\.3%\);p=0\.01\), less increased analgesic use \(33 \(30\.3%\) versus 48 \(44\.9%\); p=0\.027\), less foot pain \(11 \(10\.1%\) versus 24 \(22\.4%\);p=0\.01\), and better quality of life \(\+0\.059;p=0\.021\)\.Low back pain, healthcare use, and falls did not differ\.Conclusions: A pre\-departure therapeutic patient education program, evaluated as a whole, produced a statistically significant reduction in lower limb pain in adults aged 50\-75 years exposed to several days of intense walking\.The point estimate did not reach the 2\-point threshold set a priori, so a clinically important benefit can be neither confirmed nor excluded\.Predictable episodes of high ambulatory load may offer a practical window for resource\-light prevention, which requires confirmation against an active comparator\.
