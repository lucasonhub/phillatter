# Language management in the age of LLMs: AI tools in proofreading as emerging norm authorities?

**Autores:** Vít Dovalil; Adriana Hanulíková

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Who decides what counts as “correct” or standard language today? Correcting language has always been an act of authority\. This authority seems now to be quietly shifting toward large language models \(LLMs\), because they are routinely consulted for proofreading in educational, academic, and professional contexts, valued for the apparent ease with which they produce standardized, well\-formed texts\. However, which linguistic norm these models draw on when “correcting” language remains underexplored\. This is a critical issue, because less linguistically informed users are more likely to accept AI\-generated corrections uncritically\. In this paper, we examine such corrections and argue that treating LLMs as de facto norm authorities with regard to standard language is premature\. We first rethink standard language as a sociolinguistic concept that is negotiated among linguists, norm authorities, model speakers/writers, and codifiers \(authors of dictionaries, grammars etc\.\) rather than a fixed property of a language\. We then illustrate LLMs&\#x27; performance in proofreading empirically, by submitting a German text containing 13 targeted morphosyntactic phenomena for grammar correction to six LLMs \(eight model configurations\) at three time points\. Expectedly, results show variability both between and within models over time, as well as some consistency and several discrepancies with codification and model texts\. LLMs, we conclude, may be reasonably reliable for some morphosyntactic variables, and they could be treated as an autonomous norm authority if they can be shown to draw transparently on knowledge codified by reliable sources\.
