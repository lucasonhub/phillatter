# Case Report: Diagnostic dilemma: rebound thymic hyperplasia mimicking mediastinal relapse following chemoimmunotherapy in pediatric jejunal Burkitt lymphoma

**Autores:** Meifang Lin; Zengwang Zhang; Hao Zhang; Dongdong Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Pediatric sporadic Burkitt lymphoma \(BL\) typically presents with gastrointestinal involvement; primary mediastinal disease is exceedingly rare\. Rebound thymic hyperplasia \(RTH\) is a benign regenerative process following chemotherapy that can radiologically mimic mediastinal relapse, creating significant diagnostic challenges in post\-treatment surveillance\. Case presentation An 8\-year\-old male was diagnosed with St\. Jude stage III Burkitt lymphoma \(BL\) following the emergency resection of a jejunal mass\. After receiving risk\-adapted chemoimmunotherapy, he achieved a complete metabolic response\. Three months post\-treatment, routine surveillance computed tomography \(CT\) revealed a new anterior mediastinal soft tissue mass, magnetic resonance imaging \(MRI\) revealing T2 hyperintensity in the anterior mediastinal lesion without restricted diffusion, while subsequent PET/CT demonstrated mildly increased FDG uptake, raising immediate clinical concern for disease relapse\. To rule out malignancy, a CT\-guided percutaneous biopsy was performed\. Histopathological analysis revealed normal thymic microarchitecture containing Hassall’s corpuscles\. The final imaging and pathological findings supported a diagnosis of chemotherapy\-induced rebound thymic hyperplasia \(RTH\)\. Conclusion Cytotoxic therapy induces profound thymic involution, often followed by striking regenerative expansion upon treatment cessation\. Recognizing RTH is paramount to avoid misdiagnosing neoplastic recurrence and administering unwarranted therapies\. This case highlights need to integrate advanced imaging and tissue biopsy when evaluating new mediastinal opacities in pediatric lymphoma survivors\.
