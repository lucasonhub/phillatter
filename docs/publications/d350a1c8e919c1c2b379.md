# Kush, Jacob: Feeling as Licensed Operation: A stipulated identity, and two tests of grain

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

The Architecture of Intentional Activation gave a criterion of authored agency: a process is owned only when the executive mind deliberately activates it\. That paper set phenomenal character to one side\. This note states what feeling is in the same architecture\. Feeling is not a lamp bolted onto a job\-list\. For this license, specified jobs are felt operations\. Glow, in this paper, is the running total of the human template under load — not a universal prize a species reaches by accumulating clicks\. A microorganism is not a human with a low count\. A coma is not a working human with the cabin empty\. A life on autopilot can still be felt and still fail to be authored\. Need explains why the jobs are on the menu\. Identity\-by\-sum is what lets the menu explain feel\. Once that is in print, the microorganism is a contrast case, psychopathy is a channel test, and a dark full duplicate is not this license with the lights off\. It is a different object\. That is a constitutive claim, and a stipulated one\. It should be read as one\. This note is a further thesis, not a silent revision of Functional Authored Agency or The Architecture of Intentional Activation\. Those papers left phenomenal character conditional and defined authored agency without identifying it with feel\. Readers should keep that record\. What follows is the stronger identity claim, offered as a stipulation beside that record — not as something already proved there, and not as a derivation of why there is something it is like\. How a total is pictured, including one’s own, is treated separately in Imposed Image\. This note keeps occupancy and access apart\. That other note does not prove the identity here\.
