# Knowledge, attitudes, and practices among users of GLP\-1 receptor agonists in Saudi Arabia: a cross\-sectional study

**Autores:** Abdullah Ali Alshehri; Mohammed K\. Al\-Osaimi; Nawaf N\. Al\-Saeedi; Osama M\. Al\-Wagdani; Sultan S\. Al\-Zahrani; Fareed A\. Alharthi; Ghadah H\. Alshehri; Fahad Hassan Baali; Saeed Saad A\. Alghamdi; Wael Y\. Khawagi

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background The use of glucagon\-like peptide\-1 \(GLP\-1\) receptor agonists has increased rapidly in Saudi Arabia for weight management and glycaemic control\. However, concerns remain regarding inappropriate use, medication safety, and misconceptions surrounding these therapies\. This study assessed the knowledge, attitudes, and practices \(KAP\) of GLP\-1 receptor agonist users in Saudi Arabia and identified factors associated with these domains\. Methods A cross\-sectional study was conducted among adult GLP\-1 receptor agonist users in Saudi Arabia between December 2025 and March 2026\. Eligible participants were aged ≥18 years and had used semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, or tirzepatide within the previous 12 months\. Participants were recruited through purposive snowball sampling via social media platforms\. Descriptive statistics and binary logistic regression were used to assess KAP levels and associated factors\. Results A total of 310 participants were included\. The median age was 33 years \(IQR: 16\), and the median BMI was 33\.9 kg/m 2 \(IQR: 7\.1\)\. Tirzepatide was the most frequently used medication \(46\.1%\)\. Good practices were reported by 54\.8% of participants, whereas only 25\.8% demonstrated good knowledge and 33\.2% reported positive attitudes\. Knowledge, attitude, and practice scores were positively correlated \(ρ = 0\.278–0\.395; all p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Participants aged &gt;50 years were less likely to demonstrate good knowledge \(aOR = 0\.14, 95% CI: 0\.04–0\.82\) and positive attitudes \(aOR = 0\.29, 95% CI: 0\.09–0\.91\)\. Postgraduate education was independently associated with positive attitudes \(aOR = 6\.51, 95% CI: 2\.23–18\.99\) and good practices \(aOR = 4\.47, 95% CI: 1\.37–14\.60\), while female sex was associated with good practices \(aOR = 2\.06, 95% CI: 1\.28–3\.33\)\. Conclusion GLP\-1 receptor agonist users demonstrated generally favourable medication\-use practices but suboptimal knowledge and attitudes\. Higher knowledge was associated with more positive attitudes and better practices, highlighting the importance of patient education and healthcare professional counselling to support safe and informed use\.
