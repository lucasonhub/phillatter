# Perceived stress, dental environmental stress, and academic performance among dental students: a cross\-sectional study

**Autores:** Sheila Yaghmai; Jessica Owens; Reem Mahmoud; Eman Allam

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objectives This study investigated perceived stress and dental environmental stress among dental students, their relationships with academic performance, and the potential association of participation in the Integrative Mind\-Body Wellness Initiative \(IMBWI\) with students’ well\-being and academic outcomes\. Methods A cross\-sectional survey was conducted among dental students at the UCLA School of Dentistry\. Participants completed the Perceived Stress Scale \(PSS\-10\) and the Dental Environment Stress \(DES\) questionnaire, along with self\-reported demographic and academic measures, including academic performance\. Regression analyses were performed to identify factors associated with stress outcomes and academic performance\. Spearman&\#x27;s correlation analysis was used to evaluate associations between stress scores and academic performance, with statistical significance set at p &lt; 0\.05\. Results Mean \(SD\) PSS and DES scores were 21\.64 \(6\.74\) and 58\.03 \(15\.53\), respectively, indicating moderate stress levels\. Academic\- and personal\-related stressors represented the highest\-scoring DES domains\. PSS and DES scores demonstrated a moderate positive correlation \( ρ = 0\.65, p = 0\.001\)\. Academic ranking showed weak, non\-significant negative correlations with both PSS \( ρ = −0\.20, p = 0\.08\) and DES scores \( ρ = −0\.09, p = 0\.43\)\. Students reporting ≥10 weekly hours of self\-care activities had significantly lower odds of high environmental stress \(OR = 0\.19, 95% CI: 0\.05–0\.62, p = 0\.009\)\. Greater study time outside scheduled academic activities was similarly associated with lower odds of high environmental stress \(OR = 0\.20, 95% CI: 0\.05–0\.67, p = 0\.013\)\. Participation in the IMBWI demonstrated a non\-significant trend toward lower stress levels\. Conclusions Dental students demonstrated moderate levels of perceived and environmental stress\. Greater engagement in self\-care activities and independent study time was associated with lower environmental stress levels\. These findings support the importance of promoting student well\-being and supportive learning environments within dental education\.
