# Peripheral blood CD34\+ peak during mobilisation as an additional parameter to assess PBSC inoculum adequacy autologous HSCT

**Autores:** Giuseppe Milone; Giuseppe Milone; Emanuele Martorana; Maria Grazia Camuglia; Salvatore Leotta; Andrea Spadaro; Giulia Giuffrida; Gaetano Moschetti; Federica Galbo; Paola Scire; Giulio Antonio Milone; Giulio Antonio Milone; Giordana Brunetti; P\. Castorina

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background After autologous haematopoietic stem cell transplantation \(HSCT\), the infused CD34\+ cell dose is usually considered essential for assessing the adequacy of the peripheral blood progenitor cells \(PBSC\) inoculum\. Methods In a cohort of 302 patients who underwent autologous haematopoietic transplantation, we investigated whether the peak peripheral blood CD34\+ cell count during mobilisation could predict neutrophil engraftment\. Results In the overall cohort, the mean time to neutrophil engraftment was 12\.2 days, and the mean infused CD34\+ cell dose was 5\.1 × 10^6 CD34\+ cells/kg\. In univariate analysis, the CD34\+ cell dose \(p=0\.005\), the PB CD34\+ peak during mobilisation \(p=0\.0002\), the number of performed apheresis \(p=0\.03\), and the use of G\-CSF \(p=0\.001\) were significantly associated with time to neutrophil engraftment\. When factors were evaluated in multivariate analysis, the PB CD34\+ peak and use of G\-CSF remained significant\. In a ROC analysis, the PB CD34\+ peak predicted delayed engraftment with an AUC of 0\.695 \(95% CI, 0\.599\-0\.791\)\. Joint stratification by PB CD34\+ peak and infused CD34\+ dose identified subgroups at high risk of delayed engraftment\. Conclusion In patients treated by autologous HSCT and receiving an adequate dose of infused CD34\+ cells, the PB CD34\+ peak during mobilisation is independently associated with post\-transplant neutrophil engraftment\.
