# SIEM Logs as Digital Evidence: Reliability, Integrity, and Challenges in Cybercrime Investigations

**Autores:** Ernest Kobbie Doe

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Security Information and Event Management \(SIEM\) systems are widely used to collect, correlate, and analyse logs for cybersecurity monitoring and incident response\. However, the growing reliance on SIEM data during digital forensic and cybercrime investigations raises important questions about whether such logs can be treated as reliable digital evidence\. This paper examines the factors that influence the evidential value of SIEM logs through a conceptual research design supported by a structured integrative review of literature from cybersecurity, digital forensics, log management, incident response, and digital evidence preservation\. The analysis develops four propositions relating to source\-log quality, preservation and chain of custody, transparency of SIEM processing, and the effects of timestamp inconsistencies, weak access controls, incomplete logging, and deliberate manipulation\. The findings show that SIEM evidential reliability depends on the entire logging lifecycle and not just on the kind of SIEM platform used\. High\-quality source logs, strong integrity controls, clear chain of custody procedures, and transparent correlation and normalisation processes strengthen evidential value, while incomplete records, weak preservation practices, and manipulation reduce confidence in forensic conclusions\. The paper argues that SIEM platforms should be treated as part of a wider digital evidence ecosystem\. Organisations should therefore incorporate forensic requirements into logging, preservation, and security\-monitoring practices before incidents occur\. Future empirical research should test the proposed relationships across different SIEM platforms and investigative environments\.
