# Markerless pose\-derived movement quality assessment in physical education: a multi\-task student performance analysis

**Autores:** Lihua Cui; Huang Xuan; Wenpeng Zhan

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Markerless motion capture offers a practical opportunity to support objective movement assessment in physical education, but its use for student performance evaluation remains limited by concerns about landmark reliability, task variability, camera\-view effects, and insufficient validation\. This study developed and internally validated a markerless pose\-derived framework for evaluating student movement quality across common physical\-education tasks\. A publicly available synchronized marker\-based and markerless motion\-capture dataset was analyzed\. The final analytical dataset included 21 male physical\-education students, seven tasks, two camera views, and 294 eligible task\-view trials\. Markerless keypoints following the COCO\-17 anatomical landmark convention were reconstructed into frame\-level skeletons, cleaned using confidence\-based filtering, interpolated for short gaps, smoothed, and normalized by body scale\. Trial\-level features were extracted to quantify movement amplitude, symmetry, smoothness, rhythm, postural control, and detection reliability\. A Markerless Movement Quality Score was generated using equal\-weighted, expert\-weighted, and PCA\-derived scoring strategies\. Trials were stratified into low, moderate, and high pose\-derived movement\-quality groups within each task\-view combination\. Internal validation used grouped 5\-fold, leave\-one\-subject\-out, final subject holdout, leave\-one\-task\-out, and leave\-one\-view\-out strategies\. The pipeline reconstructed 951,067 frame\-level skeletons and more than 16 million keypoint observations\. Trunk and lower\-limb landmarks showed high reliability, with hips, knees, ankles, and shoulders demonstrating near\-complete tracking availability, whereas facial landmarks were less stable\. Task\-level features captured distinct movement signatures across walking, jogging, high knees, jumping jacks, lunges, and squats\. The scoring methods showed strong agreement, including Spearman correlations of 0\.97 between equal\-weighted and expert\-weighted scores, 0\.96 between equal\-weighted and final scores, and 0\.94 between PCA\-derived and final scores\. The PCA performance landscape showed partial separation of low, moderate, and high movement\-quality groups\. Machine\-learning validation was modest, with the best grouped 5\-fold Macro\-F1 reaching approximately 0\.39, and weaker cross\-view generalization\. The confusion matrix showed better recognition of low\-quality trials than moderate or high\-quality trials\. Markerless pose\-derived features can provide interpretable trial\-level descriptors of movement quality across physical\-education tasks, particularly when based on reliably detected trunk and lower\-limb landmarks\. However, predictive validation was modest, with limited discrimination between adjacent movement\-quality groups and weak generalization across some task and camera\-view conditions\. The internally derived MMQS groups were not independently expert\-rated, and the present results therefore support feasibility and internal methodological validation rather than reliable automated grading or generalizable performance prediction\. External validation against expert assessments, marker\-based trajectories, larger cohorts, and real classroom conditions is required before practical educational deployment\.
