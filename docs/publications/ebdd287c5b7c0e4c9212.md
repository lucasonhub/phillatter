  However, an understanding of hormonal modulators of behavior and metabolism occurred
  much later, with Berthold''s studies (Berthold, 1849;Sawin, 1996), which provided
  direct evidence for the effects of testicular secretions in domestic chickens (Soma,
  2006). Further, these studies parallel observations on the effects of testicular
  secretions for humans and other mammals (Handelsman, 2022). Most notable is the
  study by Brown-Sequard (1889) in which at 72 years of age he experienced rejuvenation
  from injecting himself with an aqueous extract of guinea pig and dog testes. Although
  a placebo effect, Brown-Sequard''s findings catalyzed interest in the effects of
  testicular compounds as a fountain of youth for aging. These studies illustrate
  the conserved mechanisms of the actions of steroid hormones in mammals and birds
  and highlight the convergent underpinnings of lifetime reproductive function across
  vertebrate organisms.Studies in poultry (Gallus gallus domesticus; Coturnix japonica)
  have provided a cornerstone for understanding developmental biology, neural basis
  of behavior (ex: testosterone effects, operant conditioning in pigeons), reproduction,
  disease processes, immune function, and establishing novel research models. Advances
  in poultry genetics allowed optimization of reproduction in egg layers and growth
  in meat producing birds. Interestingly, selected strains show significant differences
  in physiology, including metabolic endocrine characteristics, productivity, immunocompetence,
  and lifespan. The bursa of Fabricius, described by Bruce Glick and colleagues (Glick,
  1966) elucidated its critical role in immune function and demonstrated early steroid
  induced sex differences in chicks (Glick and Sadler, 1961). Beyond the rich literature
  on immune function, domestic species have provided tools for evaluating the movement
  of pathogens in wild birds and risk to domestic poultry (Prosser et al., 2016).
  This article focuses on conserved mechanisms directing endocrine and behavioral
  components of reproduction over the lifespan and responding to environmental modulators.Developmental
  processes in the chick embryo, first documented by Aristotle have provided the basis
  for understanding early development (Zagris, 2022). Detailed descriptions of developmental
  stages in the chick embryo became the standard for recognizing conserved mechanisms
  across classes of organisms (Hamburger and Hamilton, 1951). With development outside
  the maternal system, the chick embryo provides a versatile model for studies on
  molecular development, signaling pathways, differentiation of physiological systems,
  neurobehavioral pathways, and assessment of toxins. More recently, in vitro chick
  embryo shellless culture systems provide to opportunity to assess efficacy and toxicity
  of treatments for cancer on specific cells (Nipper et al., 2024). Transgenic embryos
  add capability to investigate specific physiological processes throughout embryonic
  development and over the lifespan (Henderson et al., 2025).Sexual differentiation
  in precocial birds occurs during embryonic development, with. gonadal and accessory
  gland development preceding activation of the hypothalamic-pituitarygonadal axis
  and sexual differentiation of behavior in Japanese quail (Figure 1). Altricial birds
  undergo a more extended sexual differentiation process, notably the sexually dimorphic
  song system (Adkins-Regan et al., 1990). As shown in Figure 1, birds dramatically
  differ in lifespan, with quail regarded as short-lived and long-lived species such
  as cranes, condors, vultures, eagles, and seabirds. Further, adult peak productivity
  varies with lifespan, which can be greatly attenuated by environmental stressors.
  Importantly, aging processes notably reproductive function mirror those in mammals
  with conserved mechanisms operating during age-related reproductive decline (Ottinger,
  2018;Comizzoli and Ottinger, 2021).Poultry live in a controlled environment, with
  minimal exposure to environmental chemicals and other stressors experienced by wild
  birds. In contrast, wild birds experience exposures to mixtures of environmental
  stressors (pollutants, habitat and resource loss, climate change; Ottinger and Geiselman,
  2023). Studies in the domestic chick and Japanese quail have provided a biomarkers
  of exposure, indicative of adverse physiological and behavioral effects of exposure
  to contaminants, with precocial species (domestic chicken, Japanese quail, mallard
  duck) serving as the standard for regulatory testing of industrial, agricultural,
  and residential use chemicals (Ottinger and Quinn, 2024). Beyond adverse effects
  of environmental chemicals on reproductive and metabolic systems, toxicants also
  impair cardiac development, chick locomotor and vocal behaviors, and molecular markers
  of toxicants (Quinn and Ottinger, 2008;Carro et al., 2013Carro et al., , 2018;;Legrand
  et al., 2021;Ottinger and Dean, 2022). Field birds are impacted by environmental
  crises, such as the Gulf oil spill (Perez et al., 2017). Using these biomarkers
  of exposure also provide the basis for developing a field friendly ''tool kit''
  for wild birds (Ottinger and Dean, 2022;Grace et al., 2024;Ottinger and Quinn, 2024).
  Further, many long-lived species have 1-2 chicks/year so that any adverse effects
  from pollutants and other stressors can severely impact population resilience (Kruger
  et al., 2022;Grace et al., 2024).Avian Biology has provided the platform for understanding
  embryonic development and neuroendocrine processes modulating sexually dimorphic
  behavior. Unique metabolic and respiratory system adaptations for flight and migration,
  bone physiology, and vestibular hair cell regeneration provide valuable models systems.
  Conserved Mechanisms are the hallmark of contributions of poultry science to vertebrate
  biology and biomedicine. In combination with insights associated with genetics and
  embryology, immunology, neural development, reproduction, and aging processes have
  produced essential information and platforms for understanding molecular mechanisms
  and biomedical applications.Birds are indicators of ecosystem resilience and overall
  planetary health and occupy many niches. For example, vultures play a critical role
  as scavengers (Wink et al., 2026). The concept of One Health encompasses these and
  other interrelationship of humans, wildlife, and the environment (Figure 1). Utilizing
  bioindicators of exposure (Exposome and exposomics; Figure 1) will enhance predicting
  risk and lifetime health (healthspan; Ottinger et al., 2024;Ottinger and Geiselman,
  2025). Building on the foundation of poultry research will ensure the health of
  domestic species and build an effective platform for conserving wild avian species.'
