# TiO₂ thin\-film\-on\-graphene memristors: a high\-uniformity, multilevel\-capable platform for ultralow\-energy neuromorphic systems

**Autores:** P Sivakumar; S Srikanth; C Devi; S P Meena; M\. Malaidurai

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Memristors that combine metal\-oxide thin films with two\-dimensional graphene electrodes hold immense promise for high\-density, energy\-efficient neuromorphic hardware\. Here, we demonstrate a vertical memristor based on a single\-crystalline rutile TiO₂ thin film \(\~150 nm\) sandwiched between a chemical\-vapor\-deposited monolayer graphene bottom electrode and an Ag top electrode\. The atomically flat graphene/TiO₂ interface yields exceptional resistive switching uniformity, ultralow leakage currents, and a high resistance ratio\. The devices exhibit forming\-free bipolar switching with an ultralow set voltage of \+0\.45 V and reset voltage of \-0\.35 V, a high ON/OFF resistance ratio exceeding 2 × 10⁵, and a read endurance beyond 10⁷ cycles without degradation\. High\-temperature retention measurements confirm stable resistance states for over 10⁵ s at 85 °C with an Arrhenius activation energy of 1\.1 eV, and cycle\-to\-cycle and device\-todevice variability in both high\-and low\-resistance states remains below 3\.2% \(n = 20 devices, 100 cycles each\)\. By precisely controlling the compliance current, we achieve 64 distinct, linearly spaced conductance levels, enabling 6\-bit\-per\-cell multilevel storage\. Energy consumption per switching event is calculated as 87 fJ \(E = V × I × τ = 0\.45 V × 3\.9 μA × 50 ns\), positioning this platform among the most energy\-efficient memristive devices reported\. Field\-emission scanning electron microscopy \(FESEM\) reveals a uniform TiO₂ thin film morphology on graphene, and Xray photoelectron spectroscopy \(XPS\) confirms a Ti:O stoichiometry with 15% oxygen vacancy concentration\. The high resistance state \(\~20 GΩ\) is attributed to the atomically flat graphene/TiO₂ interface suppressing leakage, nanoconfinement of the switching volume, and a Schottky barrier at the Ag/TiO₂ interface \(εᵣ ≈ 86\)\. These metrics position the TiO₂ thin film/graphene memristor as a robust, scalable building block for next\-generation in\-memory computing and neuromorphic processors\.
