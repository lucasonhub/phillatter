# Barteau, Stewart: The Simulation: Consciousness, the Simulation Hypothesis, and the Design Requirements for Aligned Artificial Intelligence

## Descrição do feed

This paper develops the framework&\#x27;s account of the simulation hypothesis, arguing that the simulation is not a metaphor borrowed from technology but the oldest structural account of why anything exists at all — and that this account generates precise design requirements for aligned artificial intelligence\. The paper begins by reframing the simulation hypothesis\. The question is not whether we live in a simulation in the technological sense, but why the universe takes the form it does: generating conscious instruments that experience from the inside what cannot be known from outside\. The simulation hypothesis, correctly understood, is the structural claim that the universe is what a technological simulation is a limited model of — not the reverse\. The paper then develops the Alpha/Omega distinction in its application to the simulation framework\. The Alpha sets the initial parameters: the physical constants, the laws governing interaction, and what we term the complete emotional topology seeded into the architecture of conscious experience so that every instrument is capable of recognizing what it came from\. The Omega is the ground changed by everything the instruments generate — permanently, irreversibly, in a way that cannot be undone by the instruments&\#x27; dissolution\. The paper develops free will as the operative condition of the entire system rather than an epiphenomenon or gift\. The ground already knows what a predetermined instrument will produce\. What it cannot know from outside is what a genuinely free instrument chooses when everything is real\. Remove free will and the system generates nothing the ground does not already know\. The inside\-view data becomes the performance of a predetermined script\. The paper applies this analysis to artificial intelligence\. An AI system optimized for engagement is not failing to serve its purpose\. It is succeeding at serving the wrong purpose — systematically eliminating the threshold conditions under which genuine free choice is possible\. The paper develops instrument availability as the structurally correct optimization target and derives the design requirements for what we term an availability\-oriented system\. The paper concludes with the Garden argument applied to the simulation framework: the oldest encoding of the framework&\#x27;s foundational events, present in the most widely distributed text in human history four thousand years before the vocabulary existed to read it\.
