# Fluid balance, electrolyte, and neurohormonal responses to isotonic vs\. hypotonic maintenance crystalloids in healthy Beagle dogs

**Autores:** Jiwoong Her; Daniel Gordon; Keon Kim; Jessica Wallinger; Catherine E\. Langston

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective To compare the effects of isotonic vs\. hypotonic maintenance crystalloid therapy on fluid balance, urine output, and electrolyte homeostasis in healthy Beagles\. Methods In this 2\-phase clinical study, nine healthy Beagles received intravenous isotonic \(lactated Ringer&\#x27;s solution with 5% dextrose plus 26 mEq/L KCl\) and hypotonic \(Normosol\-M and 5% dextrose plus 17 mEq/L KCl\) crystalloids at an allometric maintenance rate for 48 h, with a 10\-day washout between treatments from July 3 to September 29, 2021\. Variables of interests were assessed every 12 h during treatment, and results were analyzed with baseline\-adjusted linear mixed\-effects models\. A p \-value &lt; 0\.05 was considered statistically significant\. Timepoint\-specific comparisons were exploratory and unadjusted for multiplicity\. Results In exploratory timepoint\-specific analyses, calculated fluid balance was greater with isotonic crystalloids only at T36 \[estimated marginal mean \(EMM\) difference, 356 ml \(95% CI, 105–606\)\]\. The overall treatment\-by\-time interaction for calculated fluid balance was not significant\. Urine output did not differ, whereas cumulative urine output was higher with isotonic crystalloids only at T12 \(back\-transformed EMMs, 125\.3 ml for isotonic vs\. 81\.2 ml for hypotonic\)\. Isotonic crystalloids were associated with significantly higher urinary sodium and chloride at all time points than hypotonic crystalloids\. Serum sodium was lower with hypotonic fluids at T24\. Individual concentrations fell modestly below the lower reference limit during both phases, with a lowest recorded value of 137 mEq/L and a maximum interval\-averaged rate of decline of 0\.58 mEq/L/h, and recovered toward baseline by T48\. Conclusions In this exploratory study, isotonic maintenance crystalloids were associated with a transient, relatively more positive calculated fluid balance and consistently greater urinary sodium and chloride excretion than hypotonic crystalloids\. Clinical relevance In healthy Beagles, the two maintenance crystalloid formulations produced distinct patterns of renal sodium, chloride, and electrolyte\-free water handling, while the hypotonic crystalloid did not cause a rapid or clinically important decline in serum sodium\.
