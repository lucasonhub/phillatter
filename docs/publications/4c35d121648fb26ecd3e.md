# CAST: Credit Alignment for Cross\-Tokenizer On\-Policy Distillation

**Autores:** Jingru Yang; Jinhang Wu; Minjing Shi; Shengfeng He; Xuyan Li; Man Tan; Biye Li

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Cross\-tokenizer on\-policy distillation \(OPD\) requires aligning teacher and student sequences before their scores can be compared\. Decoded\-text alignment provides such correspondence at the chunk level, but leaves a distinct problem unresolved: credit alignment\. A chunk\-level signal specifies how much a span should change, but not how that signal should be distributed across student tokens, while tokens without teacher correspondence remain unconstrained\. We propose CAST \(Chunk\-aware credit Assignment with Selective Token Anchoring\), a shift\-or\-stay framework that addresses both issues\. For aligned chunks, CAST redistributes each chunk&\#x27;s advantage across student tokens using predictive uncertainty and local teacher\-signal smoothness, emphasizing learning opportunities supported by locally consistent teacher feedback while preserving the chunk&\#x27;s total advantage\. The resulting weights exactly solve a KL\-regularized credit\-allocation problem\. For unaligned positions, CAST selectively anchors the student to its initial policy through a KL penalty, limiting drift where teacher supervision is unavailable\. Because credit assignment is separated from chunk construction, CAST also applies to punctuation\-defined or fixed\-length chunks under shared tokenization\. Across six mathematics, science, and code benchmarks, CAST improves the average over the strongest evaluated baseline by $2\.45$ and $1\.32$ points for cross\-tokenizer transfer from Qwen3\-8B to Mistral\-7B and Llama\-3\.1\-8B, respectively, and by $2\.10$ points for shared\-tokenizer transfer to Qwen3\-1\.7B\.
