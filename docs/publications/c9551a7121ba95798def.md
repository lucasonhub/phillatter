# Augmenting colonoscopy with artificial intelligence and robotic assistance: a human\-factors scoping review toward a task\-based shared autonomy paradigm

**Autores:** Luigi Manfredi; Alfredo Annicchiarico; Gianpiero G Gravante

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Colonoscopy is central to colorectal cancer prevention but remains a high\-demand psychomotor and cognitive procedure, characterized by operator dependency, variable performance, and substantial physical and mental workload\. Increasing evidence links procedural quality and safety to clinician\-related factors, including musculoskeletal strain, fatigue, and non\-technical skills\. Concurrently, artificial intelligence \(AI\) and robotic technologies are emerging as potential tools to address these limitations\. We conducted a scoping review with narrative synthesis of literature on human factors, ergonomics, AI, robotic technologies, and shared autonomy in colonoscopy and surgical robotics\. Evidence synthesis: Colonoscopy performance remains limited by non\-negligible miss rates and variability driven by both technical and human factors\. Ergonomic constraints and cognitive workload contribute significantly to operator strain and may affect vigilance and decision\-making\. AI systems\-including computer\-aided detection \(CADe\), diagnosis \(CADx\), and quality monitoring\-have demonstrated improvements in detection metrics and procedural standardisation, although concerns regarding cognitive offloading and potential deskilling persist\. In parallel, emerging robotic and soft\-robotic platforms aim to enhance navigation, improve procedural ergonomics, and reduce operator musculoskeletal strain, although clinical evidence remains limited\. These technologies converge within a task\-based shared\-autonomy paradigm integrating physical, perceptual, and cognitive augmentation, with different procedural tasks operating at different levels of human\-system control\. A task\-based shared\-autonomy framework may provide a coherent model for integrating AI and robotics into colonoscopy while preserving human supervision and clinical judgement\. Future developments should prioritise human\-centred design, workflow integration, and multidimensional evaluation strategies to ensure safe and effective clinical implementation\.
