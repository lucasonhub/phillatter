# Flow partitioning modulates salt intrusion response to river discharge in a river\-network estuary

**Autores:** Xinhui Xie; Yanwen Xu; Jingxi Liang; Jie Yang; Wei Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Salt intrusion in river\-network estuaries is commonly related to upstream freshwater discharge, but the relationship may vary among channels due to flow redistribution within the estuarine network\. In the Modaomen Estuary of the Pearl River Delta, salt intrusion poses a serious threat to freshwater security during the dry season\. This study investigates how upstream flow partitioning modifies the relationship between salt intrusion length and discharge using hydrological and salinity observations combined with three\-dimensional numerical experiments\. A classical power\-law relationship between salt intrusion length and discharge was evaluated, and the influence of upstream flow partitioning was quantified using the discharge ratio between the West River and North River\. The results show that salt intrusion length decreases with increasing discharge, but the relationship between salt intrusion length and discharge exhibits systematic shifts under different flow partitioning conditions\. These shifts indicate that total discharge alone cannot fully represent the freshwater forcing experienced by the Modaomen channel\. Compared with the conventional power\-law relationship based solely on total upstream discharge, incorporating flow partitioning reduces the RMSE of salt intrusion length predictions by 29\.0%, from 3\.1 km to 2\.2 km\. The findings demonstrate that upstream flow redistribution is an important control on salt intrusion variability in river\-network estuaries\. Considering flow partitioning provides a physically interpretable representation of freshwater forcing and offers implications for salt intrusion assessment and freshwater management\.
