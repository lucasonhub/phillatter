# Severe Bacterial Infections and Sepsis in Children and Adolescents in São Paulo, Brazil: A Population\-Based Time\-Trend Study, 2015\-2025

**Autores:** André Luís Bastos Lima; Juan Silva Martins; Isadora Rita Carvalho; Cauê da Silva Manara; Stella Bernachi Rodrigues; Julia Pinheiro Almeida; Marcella Magliano Marins; Paola Zelada Florence; Ana Clara Fernandes

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Severe bacterial infections and sepsis are leading causes of pediatric hospitalization and death worldwide, yet recent Brazilian population\-based data spanning the COVID\-19 pandemic and distinguishing severe bacterial infection broadly from strict septicemia remain scarce\.Objectives: To describe hospitalization trends, mortality, and factors associated with in\-hospital death among children and adolescents with severe bacterial infections and sepsis in São Paulo State, Brazil, from 2015 to 2025, comparing the broad cohort with a strict\-sepsis subgroup\.Methods: Population\-based, repeated cross\-sectional study in São Paulo State, Brazil, 2015\-2025, using Hospital Information System of the Brazilian Unified Health System \(Sistema de Informações Hospitalares do Sistema Único de Saúde, SIH/SUS\) records for hospital admissions of individuals aged 28 days to 19 years\.The primary exposure was age group \(infant, preschool\-age, school\-age, adolescent\); the primary outcome was in\-hospital death\.Poisson regression estimated the annual trend in the number of admissions\.Crude relative risks \(RR\) were calculated directly from contingency data; adjusted RR \(aRR\) were obtained by predictive marginal standardization from a multivariable logistic model, with modified Poisson regression \(robust variance\) as a sensitivity analysis\.Results: Among 553,218 hospitalizations \(277,644; 50\.2% male\), overall case fatality was 1\.6% \(8765 deaths\)\.Compared with infants \(case fatality 1\.8%; 3038/170,495\), adolescents had over double the risk of death \(crude RR 2\.16, 95% CI 2\.06\-2\.27\),while preschool\-age \(RR 0\.47, 95% CI 0\.44\-0\.50\)and school\-age children \(RR 0\.59, 95% CI 0\.54\-0\.63\)had lower risk\.Male sex carried modestly higher risk \(RR 1\.16, 95% CI 1\.11\-1\.21\),and comorbidity \(49,952; 9\.0% of admissions\) more than tripled risk \(RR 3\.22, 95% CI 3\.07\-3\.38\)\.The number of admissions declined over the study period \(Poisson regression trend ratio 0\.96 per year, 95% CI 0\.96\-0\.97\),with a pronounced, non\-linear drop during the pandemic and partial recovery thereafter\.The strict\-sepsis subgroup comprised 27,181 admissions \(4\.9% of the cohort\) but had a case fatality of 14\.7% \(3985 deaths\), nine times that of the broad cohort\.Conclusions: Severe bacterial infections impose a substantial, age\-concentrated hospital burden among Brazilian children, while strict sepsis, though comparatively uncommon, remains markedly lethal\.Distinguishing these entities is important for surveillance and resource planning\.
