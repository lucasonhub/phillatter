# Genetic associations in the gut–lung axis: a Mendelian randomization study and in vitro assessment of sodium butyrate in non\-small cell lung cancer

**Autores:** Weili Yi; Li Qin; Dongning Huang; Fang Wu

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background The gut microbiota may influence host immunity, but the relationship between specific microbial genera and non\-small cell lung cancer \(NSCLC\) risk remains uncertain\. We evaluated genetically proxied microbial abundance and conducted separate exploratory experiments with sodium butyrate \(NaB\)\. Methods Two\-sample Mendelian randomization \(MR\) used genus\-level MiBioGen summary statistics and FinnGen/OpenGWAS NSCLC data \(finn\-b\-C3\_LUNG\_NONSMALL; 1,627 cases and 217,165 controls\)\. Instruments were selected at P &lt; 1 × 10 −5 with linkage disequilibrium clumping \(r 2 &lt; 0\.001; 10,000 kb\) and F &gt; 10\. Inverse variance weighting \(IVW\) was the primary estimator; Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate \(BH\-FDR\) correction was applied across 119 genera\. Sensitivity analyses included MR\-Egger, weighted median, mode\-based methods, heterogeneity, pleiotropy and leave\-one\-out analyses\. NaB, used as a representative microbial metabolite rather than a taxon\-specific product, was evaluated at 1 mM for 24 h in tumor\-conditioned medium \(TCM\) experiments\. Results Six genera showed nominal inverse IVW associations with NSCLC risk \(P = 0\.0120–0\.0498\): Alistipes, Methanobrevibacter, Bacteroides , Ruminococcus gauvreauii group, Ruminococcus 1 and Peptococcus \. None survived BH\-FDR correction \(q = 0\.6072–0\.9819\)\. Sensitivity tests did not detect heterogeneity or directional pleiotropy for these genera, but alternative estimators and leave\-one\-out analyses did not consistently retain nominal significance\. In THP\-1\-derived macrophages, NaB treatment altered polarization\-associated transcripts, reduced the TCM\-associated CCK\-8 signal and increased acetylated histone H3 \(Ac\-H3\) relative to TCM alone\. Conclusion The MR analysis identifies six suggestive microbial associations with NSCLC risk that require independent replication\. Complementary NaB experiments show changes in macrophage\-associated transcripts, metabolic viability and histone acetylation, providing a basis for further study of microbial metabolites in the tumor microenvironment\.
