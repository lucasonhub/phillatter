# GeoCloak: An Agentic Framework for Semantics\-Preserving Geolocation Privacy via Peripheral Clue Manipulation

**Autores:** Yixin Wu; Mingjie Li; Yun Shen; Michael Backes; Yang Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

The rapid advancement of large vision language models \(LVLMs\) has made geolocation inference from images highly accurate and accessible\. This poses serious privacy risks: a passive adversary with black\-box LVLM access can infer a user&\#x27;s location from a single photo with no metadata\. Existing protection methods rely almost exclusively on adversarial perturbation, which introduces perceptible visual artifacts and utility degradation\. We identify the visual\-geolocation asymmetry: geolocation\-decisive clues, such as street signs, tend to be visually peripheral but dominate location inference\. Exploiting this asymmetry, we propose GeoCloak, an agentic framework for semantics\-preserving geolocation privacy protection\. It acts as a clue\-aware planner that analyzes each image to identify geolocation\-relevant clues, dispatches targeted semantic edits to directly suppress peripheral clues, and autonomously configures adversarial perturbation to further suppress residual geolocation features, all under iterative utility\-aware feedback\. We evaluate GeoCloak on four diverse datasets spanning 108 countries and 576 cities against five LVLMs and three inference methods\. GeoCloak reduces overall city\-level accuracy from 41\.2% to 9\.9%, approximately half of the best\-performing baseline, while achieving substantially superior utility preservation both qualitatively and quantitatively\. We hope this work takes a meaningful step toward practical, semantics\-preserving geolocation privacy protection, giving users greater control over the location information inadvertently embedded in their shared images\.
