# Glioblastoma, IDH\-wildtype, with spontaneous transdural and calvarial invasion: a case report and literature review

**Autores:** Jianhang Lin; Jiayi Lin; Jiana Zhang; Haiqing Xie; Dawei Liu; Shaolei Guo

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Glioblastoma \(GBM\), IDH\-wildtype, is the most aggressive adult\-type diffuse glioma\. Although extracranial spread may occur after craniotomy, radiotherapy, or other interventions, spontaneous transdural and calvarial invasion before treatment is exceedingly rare\. We report a 37\-year\-old woman who presented with long\-standing right temporal\-parietal headache and acute worsening with vomiting\. Computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated a right temporal mass with intratumoral hemorrhage, peritumoral edema, and contiguous extension through the dura and temporal bone into the temporalis muscle\. The lesion was resected through a transcranial approach, and postoperative imaging showed gross total resection\. Histological examination demonstrated an infiltrating high\-grade glial neoplasm with brisk mitotic activity and palisading necrosis\. Immunohistochemical and molecular findings, including the absence of IDH1/IDH2 mutations, supported a diagnosis of glioblastoma, IDH\-wildtype, CNS WHO grade 4\. At 18 months after surgery, the patient remained alive\. This case highlights a rare pattern of primary extracranial extension in GBM and suggests direct contiguous transdural invasion as a plausible route of spread in the absence of prior intervention\.
