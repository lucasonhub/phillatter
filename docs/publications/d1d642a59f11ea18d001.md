## Descrição do feed

In Consciousness and the Limits of Objectivity: The Case for Subjective Physicalism\. Oxford: Oxford University Press\. pp\. 123\-151\. 2013This chapter argues that both the knowledge argument and the conceivability argument can be seen as two ways of claiming that the phenomenal facts are not deducible from the physical facts and so they therefore do not supervene upon them\. Several ways of refuting the argument are considered\. The first way maintains that because our reference to phenomenal states is particularly direct in cases of phenomenal knowledge, and deduction is an indirect way of coming to know something, deduction cannot get us phenomenal knowledge\. This is rejected because semantic directness neither guarantees epistemic directness or non\-deducibility\. The second account considers claims that phenomenal knowledge is non\-propositional knowledge by acquaintance\. This is rejected because phenomenal knowledge bears hallmarks of being knowledge\-that\. The author then proposes combining these two proposals, according to which acquaintance imports phenomenal states into propositions, thus providing epistemic directness of what it is like to have certain experiences\.
