# Groundwater resource vulnerability in the annaba coastal aquifer, northeastern Algeria: integrating hydrogeological and socio\-economic drivers for sustainable management

**Autores:** Samir Hani; Nabil Harrat; Hamzaoui Wahiba; Azzedine Reghais

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The Annaba coastal aquifer in northeastern Algeria represents a critical yet vulnerable water resource system under intensifying anthropogenic pressure, where per capita water availability has declined sharply from 1,500 m3/year in 1962 to 500 m3/year today\. Utilizing an integrated Driver\-Pressure\-State\-Impact\-Response \(DPSIR\) framework coupled with Artificial Neural Networks \(ANNs\), multivariate statistics, and Quantitative Human Health Risk Assessment \(QHHRA\), this study evaluates hydrogeological degradation and socio\-economic drivers across 21 municipalities\. An optimized Multi\-Layer Perceptron \(MLP 14\-7\-1\) neural network modeled groundwater abstraction with high precision \(R2 &gt; 0\.998, R &gt; 0\.999, RMSE &lt;0\.088 hm3/year\), identifying per capita income and tourism as the primary drivers of overexploitation\. Hierarchical Cluster Analysis \(HCA\) and Principal Component Analysis \(PCA\) delineated two distinct management zones: a high\-abstraction coastal cluster \(3 municipalities: Bouteldja, Pont Bouchet, and Salines\) requiring immediate supply augmentation, and an inland cluster \(18 municipalities\) dominated by land\-use pressures and diffuse pollution\. Health risk assessment revealed severe contamination thresholds: cadmium \(Cd\) ingestion via root vegetables yielded alarming Hazard Quotients \(HQ = 31\.9 for children and 13\.7 for adults\), while lead \(Pb\) reached HQ = 6\.64 in children\. In livestock scenarios, zinc \(Zn\) exposure via milk consumption exceeded safety limits \(HQ = 5\.23 for children and 2\.61 for adults\)\. Furthermore, dermal contact with Cd\-contaminated sludge presented unacceptable non\-carcinogenic risk \(HQ = 1\.59\) alongside carcinogenic risk \(ELCR = 9\.67\.10\-7\)\. These quantitative boundaries underscore the urgent need for targeted demand\-side management and spatial zoning within the proposed CWIMSAM model, advancing SDG 6 implementation in Mediterranean coastal basins\.
