# Meaning\-Full Leadership: Meaning\-Making Discourse in Christian Leadership Development Conferences in the United Kingdom and the United States of America

**Autores:** John D\. Coggin

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

This thesis explores the social construction of leadership through meaning\-making discourse at Christian leadership development conferences \(LDCs\)\. Existing research into leadership development, which is predominantly functionalist in approach, has largely overlooked the ways in which LDCs serve as meaning\-making sites and through their discourse construct concepts of leadership that affect personal identity, organisational structure, and social control\. This thesis recovers and develops Peter L\. Berger’s concept of plausibility structures as an analytical tool to describe how logics from various meaning\-making sources are utilised by speakers to construct an identity of ‘meaning\-full’ leadership at LDCs\. Using critical discourse analysis, it explores how social understandings of leadership were cultivated through the content of three LDCs hosted in 2022 by faith\-based groups\. These LDCs were held for in\-person audiences in the United Kingdom and the United States of America and broadcast around the world through streaming platforms and local screenings\. The thesis examines the ways in which transnational understandings of leadership were framed in conversation with neoliberal understandings of leadership, forged through grappling with the personal and organisational impacts of the COVID\-19 pandemic, and honed through developing strategies for church leaders to conceive of and exert power over their organisations and wider society\. It finds through this analysis that the task of leadership in the context of LDCs extends beyond the cultivation of practical skills for organisational management and constitutes an important aspect of personal identity\. It concludes that the model of ‘meaning\-full’ leadership advanced by the LDCs has simultaneously empowering and anxiety\-producing valences, with implications not only for the identity management of individual leaders but also for the lived social realities of the organisations they lead\.
