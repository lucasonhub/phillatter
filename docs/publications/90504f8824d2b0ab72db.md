# Johnson, Jamison: On Computing \- What the Machine Borrowed From the Brain, and Where It Stopped

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Do you remember the first computer you actually brought into your home? Starting from a family Packard Bell, an Encarta article on the moon landing, and an Apollo Guidance Computer far weaker than that 1994 desktop, this lyric\-philosophical essay traces how the machine was built from an idea about the brain: von Neumann&\#x27;s EDVAC draft described its parts in the language of McCulloch and Pitts&\#x27;s neurons and called its store a &quot;memory,&quot; a word the machine borrowed from us\. It follows the transistor, the integrated circuit, and the 4004 as a chain of inventions bundled from earlier ones, maps drive, RAM, and processor onto cortex, prefrontal cortex, thalamus, and Descartes&\#x27;s pineal, and then shows where the metaphor snaps: the brain has no von Neumann bottleneck, though it has narrows of its own\. What survives is the smaller claim of Clark and Chalmers&\#x27;s extended mind: we think with machines\. The essay reads the search index as everyone&\#x27;s slice of the world laid over everyone else&\#x27;s, revisits Armstrong&\#x27;s missing &quot;a,&quot; and closes on Boolean logic: the machine runs the IF, but a person leaned into it first\. \(direct link \)
