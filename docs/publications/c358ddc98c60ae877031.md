# Cross\-Lingual Robustness of Societal Bias Representations in Large Language Models: A Contrastive Activation Steering Analysis of Llama 2 and GPT\-OSS

**Autores:** Mehuly Chakraborthy; Antonio Rodà

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Large language models \(LLMs\) increasingly support consequential applications, making it important to examine whether they reproduce harmful social biases\. We thus present a bilingual, representation\-level study of bias and refusal behavior in Llama\-2\-7B\-Chat \(7B, dense\) and GPT\-OSS\-20B \(Mixture\-of\-Experts\) using English and Italian prompts, where the Italian material is a reviewed translation of the English material and therefore supports a controlled bilingual comparison rather than a culturally localized audit\. Using Contrastive Activation Steering, we construct gender, race, religion, and refusal vectors from paired stereotype/anti\-stereotype and refusal/non\-refusal prompts\. We compare unsteered, bias\-only, and bias\-plus\-refusal\-subtraction generation and analyze layer\-wise vector geometry\. Across the tested prompts and interventions, stereotype\-consistent completions remain elicitable in both models and languages\. Because the response\-level analysis uses seven held\-out templates and single\-annotator labels, these behavioral findings are exploratory\. The geometric analysis shows that, in the tested models, bias directions are partially aligned and become increasingly anti\-aligned with refusal in later layers\. Thus, visible refusal does not by itself establish that stereotype\-associated directions are absent\. The findings motivate representation\-level bilingual red\-teaming that evaluates refusal and bias jointly\.
