# Letters and Biological Process: Linguistic Structure Encodes Hierarchical Depth in the Gene Ontology

**Autores:** Sadiya Ahmad

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

MOTIVATION: We asked whether the natural language used to name Gene Ontology \(GO\) Biological Process terms encodes hierarchical position in the GO directed acyclic graph\. RESULTS: Semantic representations of term names, combined with UMAP visualization and k\-means clustering \(k=20\) recovered biologically coherent semantic groups whose depth distributions differed strongly \(Kruskal\-Wallis H=5244, p&lt;10\-300; η2=0\.217\)\. Term\-name length increased with depth \(Pearson r=0\.34, Spearman ρ=0\.37; slope ≈3\.2 letters per level\), and this positive scaling held under shortest\-, longest\-, and average\-path depth definitions\. Vocabulary entropy followed a bell\-shaped diversification\-specialization curve with an intermediate\-depth peak \(maximal at depth 4\) that lies outside a Monte Carlo null envelope across most depths, and null\-model tests supported a genuine linguistic\-hierarchical association\. AVAILABILITY AND IMPLEMENTATION: The go\_lingdepth Python package, regenerated result tables, and figure\-generation scripts are available at https://github\.com/Sadiya\-ISU/Letters\-and\-Biological\-Process and archived on Zenodo \(DOI: 10\.5281/zenodo\.21501473\)\. SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: Supplementary data are available at Bioinformatics online\.
