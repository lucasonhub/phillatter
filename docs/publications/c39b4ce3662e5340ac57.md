# Dimethyl ether\-induced degradation and failure of nitrile rubber seals in gas stove: Experiment and molecular dynamics simulation

**Autores:** Shanjie Zhao; Tao Feng; Jiarui Yang; Jinfeng Zhang; Keji Chen; Jingning Zhang; Kan Jin

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Illegal blending of dimethyl ether \(DME\) into liquefied petroleum gas \(LPG\) can compromise nitrile rubber \(NBR\) seals in gas stoves, increasing the risk of leakage\-related accidents\. Three representative sealing components, gas inlet sealing rings \(GISR\), rubber O\-rings \(OR\), and flat rubber gaskets \(FRG\), were investigated using pressurized liquefied DME/n\-butane immersion tests combined with measurements of mass change, hardness, compression set, tensile properties, SEM, FTIR, and molecular dynamics \(MD\) simulations\. DME exposure caused swelling, irreversible mass loss, hardening, and mechanical deterioration, with GISR showing the most severe degradation and OR the best resistance\. GISR reached a maximum mass loss of about 21%, a hardness increase of 95%, and a compression set of 38\.3%\. SEM revealed pores and microcracks, while FTIR showed marked decreases in ester\-related oxygen\-containing bands with the NBR backbone largely retained\. MD simulations indicated relatively high mobility of DME\-associated O atoms \(3\.86×10 \-7 m 2 /s\), while DME migration and local accumulation in free\-volume regions may perturb NBR chain packing and promote segmental rearrangement\. These results identify the degradation pathway governed by molecular penetration, additive extraction, property deterioration, and defect development rather than extensive chemical decomposition of the NBR backbone\. These findings provide a mechanistic basis for evaluating DME compatibility of NBR seals and reducing leakage and fire hazards in gas stoves\.
