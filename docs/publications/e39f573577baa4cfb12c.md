# Comparative genomic analysis of established and emerging anti\-tubercular drug targets reveals evolutionary conservation and antimicrobial resistance patterns in Mycobacterium tuberculosis isolates from Indonesia

**Autores:** Ni Made Puspawati; Iryanti Eka Suprihatin; Akshay Suthar; Ashaka Vansia; Rajesh Patel

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Multidrug\-resistant tuberculosis \(MDR\-TB\) remains a major public health threat in Indonesia, which ranks second globally in tuberculosis burden\. Whole\-genome sequencing \(WGS\) studies of drug\-resistant Mycobacterium tuberculosis in Indonesia have so far been analyzed largely in isolation, and no previous study has systematically compared the evolutionary conservation of classical, clinically validated resistance\-associated genes with newly proposed metabolic drug targets within a single, geographically diverse Indonesian isolate collection\. Methods We compiled 444 publicly available Mycobacterium tuberculosis whole genomes from four Indonesian whole\-genome sequencing studies spanning Java and Papua and analyzed them using TBProfiler for lineage assignment and genotypic drug\-resistance prediction\. Following exclusion of nine isolates for which an unambiguous single lineage could not be assigned, gene\-level sequences for ten classical resistance\-associated genes \( rpoB, katG, inhA, fabG1, embB, pncA, gyrA, gyrB, rpsL, rrs \) and ten emerging metabolic drug\-target genes \( guaA, guaB2, glnA1, gltB, dapB, folP1, bioA, panC, aroG, ilvB1 \) were reconstructed for 435 isolates and compared by multiple sequence alignment, variant calling, and functional annotation\. Results \(East\-Asian\) predominated \(46\.2% of isolates\) and was disproportionately associated with drug\-resistant genotypes \(χ² = 49\.07, df = 2, p &lt; 0\.001; Lineage 2 versus Lineage 4 odds ratio for any genotypic resistance 2\.69, 95% CI 1\.77–4\.07\)\. Overall, 28\.2% of isolates were genotypically multidrug\-resistant and 5\.4% were pre\-extensively drug\-resistant, with no mutations associated with bedaquiline, linezolid, or clofazimine resistance detected\. All twenty target genes were highly conserved \(89\.4–99\.6% invariant sites\), consistent with the clonal population structure of M\. tuberculosis \. However, the two panels differed substantially: classical resistance\-associated loci were significantly less conserved than emerging targets \(mean 96\.47% versus 99\.24%; p &lt; 0\.001\), carried a significantly higher SNP density \(20\.70 versus 6\.58 SNPs per kilobase; p = 0\.002\) and per\-site Shannon entropy \(p = 0\.009\), and accounted for 537 of the 670 variable sites detected across the panel despite comprising a similar total coding length\. The non\-synonymous\-to\-synonymous substitution ratio was also significantly higher among classical loci \(median 1\.67 versus 0\.96; p = 0\.033\), driven by the prodrug\-activating enzymes pncA \(49\.0\) and katG \(10\.4\) and by rpsL \(6\.0\) and rpoB \(2\.08\)\. Within the emerging panel, absolute variable\-site counts were uniformly low \(4–47 sites per gene\) and did not support discrimination between individual candidate genes\. Conclusions Across a geographically diverse Indonesian Mycobacterium tuberculosis population, therapeutically unexploited metabolic drug targets are markedly more conserved and carry a substantially lower mutational burden than classical resistance\-associated loci, whose elevated non\-synonymous variation is concentrated in genes whose inactivation confers resistance\. These findings support the emerging metabolic panel collectively as a low\-variability target space for future anti\-tubercular development, and establish evolutionary conservation combined with substitution\-pattern analysis as a quantitative framework for target\-space prioritization and genomic surveillance in high\-burden tuberculosis settings\.
