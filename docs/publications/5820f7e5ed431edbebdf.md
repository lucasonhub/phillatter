# Danganan, Lemuel: What are the Implications of the Right to Rescue? Scope, Status, and Procedural Entitlements in Rescue Conflicts

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Even if a person is in danger of dying, they might have more than just a reason for someone to help them; some theories hold that the person in danger has a right against a rescuer who is available\. But difficulties arise when there is a conflict of this kind\. For example, if both Anna and Ben would die unless Ruth takes action and Ruth is able to save only one, with no morally significant difference between Anna and Ben, Ruth acts in a way that is required by the moral principle of fairness by choosing to save Anna and letting Ben die\. If Ben had an individual right against Ruth to be rescued, then one has to ask: what happened to that right? The article points out a question which the literature on allocation generally tends to overlook\. Rescue theory has put forward a great deal of argument on lotteries, numbers, aggregation, chances, procedural claims, and the moral status of those who cannot all be saved \(Taurek 1977\)\. It has also put forward opposing views as to when rescue is required, when non\-rescue may be permissible, and when an endangered person has a claim\-right at all \(Pummer 2023\)\. Yet another question remains\. A theory cannot begin with an outcome\-directed right to successful rescue and then assert that the same right is completely fulfilled merely because the person has been given a fair chance, a vote, a hearing, or a procedure\. Some change must occur within the account\. The scope of the right might have been narrowed\. The right might still exist even if permissible action fails to realize it\. Or the entitlement in question might be about the procedure and not about the successful rescue\. I refer to these as scope revision, status persistence, and object revision\. The resulting rescue\-right trilemma is not another allocation rule; it is a test of what a theory means when it says that a person has an individual right to rescue\.
