# Machine\-Learning\-Enabled Design of Lab\-Scale Continuous Distillation Systems for Pharmaceutical Process Development

**Autores:** J\.C\. Turnage; Andres P\. Hyer; Kimberly E\. Penzer; James K\. Ferri

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Predictive tools for continuous distillation design lag behind upstream pharmaceutical synthesis─including AI\-enabled digital tooling for retrosynthetic route design─leaving column specification dependent on computationally expensive simulation or extensive experimentation\. This work presents a machine\-learning surrogate framework trained entirely on first\-principles mechanistic simulations, requiring no experimental distillation data, and eliminating the need for commercial tools such as ChemCad or AspenPlus\. Three surrogate models for binary tert\-butylamine/alcohol systems \(methanol, ethanol, isopropanol\) predict distillate and bottoms compositions with mean absolute errors below 0\.005 mol/mol and R2 &gt; 0\.999, completing a single prediction in under 1 ms─a 24\-fold speedup\. A coupled Pareto optimizer returns capital\-versus\-operating\-cost trade\-off curves in under 10 s, with over 93% of designs verified within ±0\.010 mol/mol\. Applied to a pharmaceutical case study, the framework correctly identifies isopropanol as the optimal solvent without prior thermodynamic knowledge\. The system\-agnostic architecture could readily integrate with open\-source large language model platforms to further lower the barrier to implementation\.
