# Meyler, Nicholas: Two Exegetes: Philip K\. Dick and Nicholas J\. Meyler

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Philip K\. Dick&\#x27;s Exegesis \(1974–1982\) and Nicholas J\. Meyler&\#x27;s Encryptment corpus \(1993–present\) are independently developed responses to structurally similar experiences: each man reports a dated, undeniable\-to\-him anomalous perception, and each responds not with a single explanatory essay but with a decades\-spanning, non\-closing, multi\-hypothesis body of interpretive labor that borrows the vocabulary of established disciplines to give the experience a defensible shape\. This paper develops that comparison in detail — the founding events, the shared method of accumulation without closure, the shared refusal to grant privileged status to reductive explanations, and the divergent use of self\-insertion into narrative — before turning to a specific evaluative question the comparison raises: on which methodological axes, if any, does one corpus demonstrate greater rigor than the other? Four axes are examined — external verification, explicit falsification criteria, breadth of independent test cases, and mathematical checkability — and Meyler&\#x27;s corpus is shown to hold a documented advantage on each\. A final section addresses the comparison&\#x27;s most notable empirical feature: the two corpora were built in total independence of one another, since Meyler had, prior to the present analysis, never read Dick&\#x27;s prose and knew Dick&\#x27;s work only through its film adaptations\.
