# Low\-temperature monolithic integration of patterned distributed Bragg reflectors for high\-resolution QD\-OLED microdisplays

**Autores:** Jin\-Wook Shin; Seung Wan Woo; Hyunsu Cho; 최수경; Jun Yong Kim; Hyunjun Jang; Gi Heon Kim; In‐Bok Baek; Byoung‐Hwa Kwon; Nam Sung Cho; Chun‐Won Byun; Hyoc Min Youn; Jin Sun Kim; Jong Ho Jeong; Jung Youn Bae; Jonghee Lee; Chan‐mo Kang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

With the increasing demand for hyper\-realistic displays, significant advancements have been reported in high\-resolution quantum dot–organic light\-emitting diode \(QD\-OLED\) technology owing to its wide color gamut and improved color purity\. However, achieving both high color purity and light extraction remains a critical challenge due to significant blue light leakage in high resolution QD\-OLED\. Here, we overcome these limitations by demonstrating a damage\-free, low\-temperature \( &lt; 100 °C\) monolithic integration of patterned distributed Bragg reflectors \(DBRs\) directly onto a QD\-OLED stack to effectively suppress blue leakage and enhance conversion efficiency\. DBRs fabricated from SiN x and SiO x using low\-temperature \( &lt; 100 °C\) plasma\-enhanced chemical vapor deposition demonstrate high reflectance of 95\.9% at 455 nm while effectively suppressing reflectance in the red and green regions\. A high\-precision, one\-step fluorine\-based dry etching process achieved a 4 μm pattern size with a near\-vertical taper angle of 89°, supporting pixel densities up to 3175 ppi\. Compared to the device without DBR, the monolithically integrated architecture exhibits a photon recycling effect, enhancing red and green emission intensities by 19\.7% and 10\.7%, respectively, while reducing blue leakage by approximately 90%\. Furthermore, the integrated device achieved a color gamut of 120\.5% relative to the sRGB standard\. These findings confirm that monolithically integrated DBRs serve as a robust and scalable solution for next\-generation high\-resolution QD\-OLED displays\.
