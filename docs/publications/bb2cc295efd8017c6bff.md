# Prognostic associations of PD\-L1 expression and BAP1 loss in pleural mesothelioma: a retrospective single\-center cohort from Colombia

**Autores:** Ricardo Elias Bruges; Carlos Carvajal; Angela Luna\-Meza; Marcela Nunez; Alejandra Navarrete; Rafael Santiago Parra\-Medina

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Evidence on the prognostic associations of programmed death\-ligand 1 \(PD\-L1\) and BRCA1\-associated protein 1 \(BAP1\) in pleural mesothelioma \(PM\) remains inconsistent, and joint data from Latin American cohorts are limited\. We evaluated these biomarkers in a Colombian cohort\. Methods This retrospective single\-center cohort included adults with histologically confirmed PM treated at the Instituto Nacional de Cancerología, Bogotá, from January 2011 through December 2023\. PD\-L1 was quantified using tumor proportion score \(TPS\), with ≥1% as the primary threshold; BAP1 was classified as retained or lost by nuclear immunoreactivity\. Overall survival \(OS\) was measured from pathologic diagnosis to death\. Event\-free survival \(EFS\) was measured from diagnosis to first documented progression or death; patients without an event were censored at last contact\. Kaplan–Meier methods and separate multivariable Cox models were used\. Results Sixty\-seven patients were included; 60/67 \(89\.6%\) had locally advanced or metastatic disease\. PD\-L1 TPS ≥1% was present in 26/67 \(38\.8%\), and BAP1 loss in 47/61 evaluable tumors \(77\.0%\)\. All 67 patients died\. Median OS was 9\.20 months \(95% CI, 6\.11–14\.13\), and median EFS was 6\.51 months \(95% CI, 5\.49–9\.13\)\. PD\-L1 TPS ≥1% was not clearly associated with OS \(adjusted HR, 1\.03; 95% CI, 0\.57–1\.86\) or EFS \(adjusted HR, 1\.11; 95% CI, 0\.62–2\.00\)\. BAP1 loss also showed no clear adjusted association with OS \(HR, 1\.57; 95% CI, 0\.78–3\.16\) or EFS \(HR, 1\.65; 95% CI, 0\.82–3\.33\)\. Conclusions Neither PD\-L1 TPS ≥1% nor BAP1 loss showed a clear adjusted association with survival\. These jointly measured estimates extend the evidence from an underrepresented Latin American population but require external validation; the exploratory ≥5% and ≥10% analyses were based on only 14 and 7 patients, respectively\.
