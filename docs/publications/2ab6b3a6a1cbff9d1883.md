# Advances in Robot Visual Gesture Interaction Technology from a Bio\-Inspired and Machine Learning Perspective

**Autores:** Mingming He

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

As robots increasingly enter open environments such as collaborative manufacturing, public services, and assisted care, the ability to accurately perceive human behavioral cues and interaction intentions has become a crucial foundation for advanced human\-computer interaction systems\. Visual gestures possess distinct characteristics, including non\-contact operation, clear spatial orientation, and low interaction burden\. However, their practical applications remain significantly affected by complex backgrounds, partial occlusions, individual user differences, and real\-time computing constraints\. This comprehensive article reviews robot visual gesture interaction technology from the innovative perspective of integrating biological visual attention mechanisms with modern machine learning paradigms\. First, the biologically inspired mechanisms and their computational representations, such as selective attention, saliency drive, and attention focus, are systematically outlined\. Subsequently, the main technological routes—including artificial features with traditional machine learning, deep feature learning, keypoint sequential modeling, and advanced attention\-based structural models—are thoroughly summarized, and their specific applicable conditions and inherent limitations are critically compared\. Furthermore, the specific application requirements and evaluation dimensions across diverse scenarios, such as industrial collaboration, service and social robots, and assistive robotics, are deeply analyzed\. Existing research clearly demonstrates that this dynamic field is rapidly expanding from simple single\-frame action classification to complex spatio\-temporal feature fusion and contextual intent understanding\. Ultimately, achieving open environment robustness, cross\-user generalization, real\-time lightweight processing, multi\-modal fusion, and stringent safety and reliability remains the primary focus of subsequent research endeavors in this domain\.
