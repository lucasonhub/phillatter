# Geometric Kernel Diversity: An Axiomatic Diversity Measure in Euclidean Space

**Autores:** Ditbul Ban; Younghun Roh; Julian Shun; Vihan Lakshman

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Quantifying the diversity of a set of objects is a fundamental problem in machine learning and statistics\. Mironov and Prokhorenkova \(2025\) recently showed that three axioms\-monotonicity, uniqueness, and continuity\-are crucial for a reliable diversity measure and that none of the standard metrics in the literature satisfy all three\. They also constructed two measures that do satisfy the axioms, but both are NP\-hard to compute, leaving open the question of whether efficient axiomatic diversity measures can exist\. We study a Euclidean\-restricted version of this question\. We introduce Geometric Kernel Diversity \(GKD\), which associates a radial kernel solid with each point and measures the volume of their union\. We show that canonical GKD satisfies all three Mironov\-Prokhorenkova axioms on Euclidean\-realizable inputs\. For canonical GKD, we give exact polynomial\-time algorithms in dimensions $d \\le 3$\. In arbitrary dimensions, we give a randomized algorithm that returns a $\(1 \\pm \\varepsilon\)$\-approximation with probability at least $1\-δ$ in time $O \\left\(\\frac\{nd\}\{\\varepsilon^2\}\\log\\frac\{2\}δ\\right\)$ for $n$ points\. The axiomatic guarantee applies to exact canonical GKD; the randomized algorithm approximates this deterministic quantity\. We further show that the two diversity measures of Mironov and Prokhorenkova \(2025\) remain NP\-hard on Euclidean\-realizable inputs and, unless $\\mathrm\{NP\} = \\mathrm\{RP\}$, admit no FPRAS even on this restricted domain\.
