# Measuring the Influence of Emerging Technologies on Service Delivery among University Libraries in South\-East, Nigeria

**Autores:** Patience Odenigbo; Augonus Nnamdi Uhegbu; Samuel Kelechukwu Ibenne; Augustine Uzoma Madu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examined the influence of emerging technologies on service delivery among university libraries in South\-East Nigeria\. Specifically, it assessed the influence of artificial intelligence, cloud computing, radio\-frequency identification \(RFID\), virtual and augmented reality \(VR/AR\), and blockchain technology on library service delivery\. A quantitative correlational research design was adopted, and data were collected from librarians in 13 university libraries in the south\-east, Nigeria using a structured questionnaire\. The demographic data and research objective data were analysed a descriptive statistics while the hypotheses were analysed using multiple regression analysis to determine the influence of the emerging technologies on service delivery\. The findings revealed that artificial intelligence \(β = \.224, t = 4\.026, p &lt; \.001\), cloud computing \(β = \.248, t = 4\.290, p &lt; \.001\), RFID \(β = \.289, t = 4\.620, p &lt; \.001\), and VR/AR \(β = \.173, t = 2\.723, p = \.007\) had statistically significant positive influences on library service delivery\. RFID recorded the strongest influence, followed by cloud computing, artificial intelligence, and VR/AR\. In contrast, blockchain technology had no statistically significant influence on service delivery \(β = \.011, t = \.180, p = \.857\)\. The study concludes that emerging technologies exert varying influences on university library service delivery, with RFID, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, and VR/AR demonstrating significant positive influences\. The study recommends that university libraries prioritise emerging technologies with demonstrated positive influences on service delivery, and blockchain technology with specific demonstrated service needs and empirical evidence
