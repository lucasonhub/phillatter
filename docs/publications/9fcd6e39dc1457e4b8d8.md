# Financial toxicity profiles and associated factors among lung cancer patients receiving immunotherapy: a latent profile analysis

**Autores:** Jing Nie; Peijuan Hu; Wei Qian; Xiaoyan Lu; Yulong Tan

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objectives To identify latent profiles of patient\-reported financial toxicity and factors associated with profile membership among lung cancer patients receiving immunotherapy\. Methods A total of 331 lung cancer patients receiving immunotherapy were recruited by convenience sampling\. Financial toxicity, perceived social support, and psychological resilience were assessed using the Comprehensive Score for Financial Toxicity \(COST\-PROM\), Perceived Social Support Scale \(PSSS\), and Connor\-Davidson Resilience Scale \(CD\-RISC\), respectively\. Latent profile analysis \(LPA\) based on the 11 COST\-PROM items was used to identify financial\-toxicity profiles\. Factors associated with profile membership were examined using the Mplus three\-step R3STEP procedure, which accounts for classification uncertainty\. A conventional multinomial logistic regression based on most\-likely class assignment was used as a sensitivity analysis\. Additional analyses examined the robustness of the LPA solution, an R3STEP model including all available covariates, collinearity, and possible nonlinear effects of PSSS and CD\-RISC\. Results Three profiles were identified: Severe Financial Toxicity \(28\.7%\), Moderate Financial Toxicity \(39\.9%\), and Mild Financial Toxicity \(31\.4%\)\. In R3STEP analyses, lower monthly income, out\-of\-pocket payment, and non\-working status were associated with higher odds of Severe Financial Toxicity profile membership \( p &lt; 0\.05\), whereas higher PSSS and CD\-RISC scores were associated with lower odds \( p &lt; 0\.05\)\. The modal\-assignment sensitivity analysis yielded materially consistent findings\. The three classes differed mainly in overall severity, with broadly parallel item profiles\. Average posterior probabilities were 0\.989, 0\.982, and 0\.992 for the Severe, Moderate, and Mild classes, respectively\. Both alternative LPA models retained three classes, and the additional multivariable R3STEP model produced similar results\. Conclusion Patient\-reported financial toxicity was heterogeneous among lung cancer patients receiving immunotherapy\. Socioeconomic vulnerability and psychosocial characteristics were associated with profile membership\. Routine financial\-distress screening and financial navigation may be considered for patients with greater financial toxicity, while psychosocial intervention strategies require prospective evaluation before their effectiveness can be inferred\. The classes mainly reflected different levels of financial toxicity rather than distinct response patterns\.
