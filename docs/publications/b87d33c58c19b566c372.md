# Hard Instances on Demand

**Autores:** Jamie Fravel; Martin Koutecký; Pavlo Tsitsei

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

The conclusions of an experimental study of algorithms are only as good as the instances they are run on\. Benchmark libraries for well\-studied problems took decades to curate, while evaluations of new problems often fall back on random instances, which are known to be misleading: they may say more about the random generator than about the algorithmic question at hand\. We treat instance generation as an optimization problem: parameterize the instance space, take the solver&\#x27;s running time as the objective, and let a black\-box optimizer search for hard instances\. We develop a widely applicable workflow built on the Nevergrad optimization platform and demonstrate it on four NP\-hard problems: satisfiability, traveling salesperson, Hamiltonian cycle, and non\-permutation flow\-shop scheduling\. The search needs only the solver, an instance encoder, and a compute budget\. Our evolved instances beat the hardest instance of a budget\-matched random draw in all four case studies, by an order of magnitude or more in three\. They are often competitive with long\-curated benchmark sets: our SAT champion is harder \(for a three\-solver portfolio\) than 78% of the 498 SAT Competition formulas in its size class; our 20\-city TSP champions take several times longer than the hardest measured classical TSPLIB instance \(229 cities\); and our flow\-shop champion takes 238\.6 seconds of CP Optimizer time to solve to proven optimality \(versus 4\.5 seconds for the hardest of 100,000 random draws\) while denying CP\-SAT an optimality proof\. The optimizer even recovers known structure from running time alone: it locates a SAT clause/variable ratio near the phase transition and finds structurally different champions for the two scheduling engines\.
