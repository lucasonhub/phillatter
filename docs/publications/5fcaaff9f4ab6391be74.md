# Glacier change at the ISM\-MLW transition: multi\-decadal evidence of spatial heterogeneity from the Sara Umga watershed, western Himalaya

**Autores:** Soumik Das; Soumik Das; Suresh Das; Suresh Das; Sandip Tanu Mandal; Elora Chakraborty; Prachi Rajput; Ishita Manna; Sanjay Deswal; Milap Chand Sharma

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study presents a multi\-decadal assessment of glacier change in the Sara Umga Watershed \(SUW\), between the Parvati and Chandra basins\. The region receives a majority of its annual precipitation from Indian Summer Monsoon \(ISM\) and a comparatively lower share contributed by winter Mid Latitude Westerlies \(MLW\)\. Using multi\-temporal satellite data, we quantify changes in glacier length, area, surface velocity, elevation, debris cover, ice cliff dynamics, and high\-faltitude lakes, supplemented by long\-term climate trend analysis\. A total of 54 glaciers \(&gt;0\.05 km 2 \) were mapped, covering 128\.38 ± 2\.98 km 2 in 2024, of which 24\.84 ± 1\.15 km 2 comprised debris\-covered ice\. Between 1994 and 2024, total glacier area declined by 4\.54 ± 0\.04 km 2 \(0\.15 ± 0\.001 km 2 yr −1 \), while debris cover expanded to 24\.84 ± 1\.15 km 2 \(0\.37 ± 0\.09 km 2 yr −1 \)\. Frontal retreat rates for the five major glaciers ranged from ∼12 to 26\.2 m yr −1 \. The largest glacier, Sara Umga \(GL01\), exhibited a mean surface velocity of ∼23\.4 m yr −1 \. Elevation\-change analysis reveals pronounced spatial heterogeneity in glacier thinning, with maximum surface lowering concentrated in the lower, debris\-covered ablation zone \(3900–4400 m a\.s\.l\.; −1\.22 ± 0\.95 to −1\.67 ± 0\.39 m yr −1 , locally exceeding −3\.0 m yr −1 \), diminishing thinning at mid\-elevations, and near\-equilibrium conditions across the accumulation area of GL01 \(−0\.07 ± 0\.54 to 0\.06 ± 0\.33 m yr −1 \)\. Zones of higher thinning and flow attenuation coincide with dense ice\-cliff and lake development, indicating strong melt\-dynamics coupling\. Climate analysis \(1901–2024\) shows significant winter warming \(0\.01 °C yr −1 \) and declining Mid\-Latitude Westerly \(MLW\) dominated winter precipitation \(−0\.160 mm yr −1 \), alongside increasing pre\- \(0\.354 mm yr −1 \) and post\-monsoon \(0\.070 mm yr −1 \) precipitation\. These findings demonstrate that changes in debris\-covered Himalayan glaciers cannot be inferred from frontal retreat alone\. Instead, coupled changes in thickness, velocity, surface morphology, and climate trends govern long\-term glacier response and hence should be studied alongside\.
