# L2 grit and enjoyment as the crucial links between ideal L2 self and willingness to communicate: evidence from mediation and latent profile analyses

**Autores:** Lirui Ma; Hong Shi

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The present study explored the associations among ideal L2 self \(ILS\), L2 grit, foreign language enjoyment \(FLE\), and willingness to communicate \(WTC\) among Chinese EFL learners\. It examined the mediating roles of L2 grit and FLE in the link between ILS and WTC, and identified latent learner profiles using L2 grit and FLE as profile\-defining indicators\. A sample of 249 Chinese EFL learners completed self\-report questionnaires\. Data were analyzed via structural equation modeling \(SEM\) and latent profile analysis \(LPA\) followed by BCH distal outcome analysis\. The SEM results showed that ILS was positively related to L2 grit, FLE, and WTC\. Both L2 grit and FLE exerted significant mediating effects on the association between ILS and WTC\. LPA yielded three distinct profiles: low grit–FLE, moderate grit–FLE, and high grit–FLE\. BCH tests revealed significant pairwise between\-profile differences in WTC and its subdimensions\. WTC exhibited a clear hierarchical gradient across profiles, with the high grit–FLE profile showing the highest WTC, followed by the moderate grit–FLE profile, and the low grit–FLE profile reporting the lowest WTC\. The study contributes to positive psychology research in SLA by integrating variable\-centered and person\-centered perspectives and offers pedagogical implications for fostering learners’ communicative engagement in EFL education contexts\.
