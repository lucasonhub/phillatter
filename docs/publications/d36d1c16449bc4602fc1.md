# A Preliminary Assessment of Literacy Standards for Diﬀerentiated Learning Against Standard Reading Score Evaluations

**Autores:** Margaretann Connell

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Current state and local school district accountability systems often use reading scores as the primary measure of student competency\. However, the 2026 priorities of the U\.S\. Department of Education and the U\.S\. Department of Labor \(ED\-DOL\) increasingly emphasize skilled trades, career pathways, workforce readiness, and artificial\-intelligence literacy\. For students who score lower on reading assessments, could evaluations of career and technical education \(CTE\) and AI/technology skills serve as alternative indicators of student competency and valid measures of state proficiency? This study proposes the implementation of a multi\-tiered evaluation standard that assesses literacy through both pedagogical learning measures and emerging innovation standards\. These evaluative tiers would be incorporated into K–12 curricula to support postsecondary career preparation and ED\-DOL workforce requirements\. Current state evaluation methods often apply rigid competency measures that rely heavily on reading scores as indicators of overall district performance and student ability\. A student’s reading assessment performance can disproportionately shape perceptions of that student’s academic success, career potential, and long\-term learning trajectory within the U\.S\. school system\. Because emerging workforce requirements demand varying combinations of academic, technical, and vocational skills, it is imperative to evaluate the full scope of each student’s abilities\. A broader evaluation method could also help educators identify appropriate learning supports that improve students’ literacy levels while preparing them for advanced workforce demands\. The proposed multi\-tiered literacy assessment would include the following classifications of student capability: • Students demonstrating high digital proficiency \(HDP\) with lower performance on reading assessments • High\-reading\-achieving students with lower levels of digital proficiency \(HRA\-LDP\) • Vocationally proficient students with lower performance on reading assessments • Students demonstrating foundational workforce skill development \(FWSD\), lower academic achievement, and limited digital proficiency Students may also be classified within targeted subcategories based on high\-to\-low levels of digital, reading, and vocational proficiency\. These classifications should be evaluated as preliminary and supplemental indicators of student competency to determine persistence rates in literacy development and broader learning outcomes\. The goal of this study is to address the misalignment created when reading\-score assessments serve as the primary measure of literacy and student competency for U\.S\. K–12 students\. The study will examine whether differentiated instructional categories should be included when determining literacy requirements and overall student literacy rates\. It will also evaluate the learning expectations established by the 2026 ED\-DOL workforce priorities, including skilled trades, AI accessibility, and the realistic capabilities of the current U\.S\. student population\. When determining literacy, educators should consider abilities such as high digital proficiency, vocational competence, and foundational workforce skills to develop a more complete evaluation of student capability\. Across states, school districts often use assessment results to support budget decisions and establish district credentialing standards\. By expanding the scope of literacy evaluation, states may gain a more accurate understanding of student strengths and weaknesses, as well as the professional\-development needs of teaching staff\.
