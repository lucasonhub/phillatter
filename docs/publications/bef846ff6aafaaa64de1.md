# Lung Organ Failure Score for Predicting In\-Hospital Mortality after Blunt Thoracic Trauma in Indonesia: A Retrospective Validation Study

**Autores:** Charita Ulfah Widyawan; Arie Hasiholan Lumban Tobing; Theodorus

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Blunt thoracic trauma carries substantial in\-hospital mortality, yet Indonesian referral hospitals lack locally validated tools for early risk stratification\. The Lung Organ Failure Score \(LOFS\) was derived to predict pulmonary organ failure, not death\. Objective: To evaluate how well LOFS discriminates and classifies in\-hospital death after blunt thoracic trauma\. Methods: Retrospective cohort of adults admitted with blunt thoracic trauma to Dr\. Mohammad Hoesin General Hospital, Palembang, Indonesia \(January 2023–January 2025\)\. LOFS was calculated from admission records\. We estimated the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve \(AUC\) and classification accuracy with Wilson 95% confidence intervals \(CIs\)\. Because patient\-level cross\-classifications were unavailable, a sensitivity analysis enumerated every joint table consistent with the observed data and fitted Firth\-penalised models across them\. Results: Of 32 patients, 7 died \(21\.9%; 95% CI 11\.0–38\.8\)\. Mean LOFS was 21\.00 ± 5\.29 in non\-survivors and 14\.16 ± 3\.92 in survivors \(difference 6\.84, 95% CI 3\.15–10\.53; p&lt;0\.001\)\. The AUC was 0\.840 \(95% CI 0\.685–0\.995\)\. LOFS ≥18 had sensitivity 85\.7% \(48\.7–97\.4\), specificity 80\.0% \(60\.9–91\.1\) and negative predictive value 95\.2% \(77\.3–99\.2\), identical to severe thoracic injury \(Abbreviated Injury Scale thorax ≥4\) alone\. In every admissible dataset, LOFS ≥18 remained associated with death after adjustment for severe head injury \(adjusted odds ratio 12\.3–64\.4\) or sex \(14\.4–143\)\. Its contribution beyond severe thoracic injury could not be identified\. Conclusion: LOFS discriminated in\-hospital death well but imprecisely, and at ≥18 it classified patients no better than severe thoracic injury alone\. Validation including at least 100 deaths is required before clinical use\.
