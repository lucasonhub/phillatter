# Machine learning\-enabled parameter space exploration for drilling fluid filtration

**Autores:** Mohammad S\. Alosail; Paula A\. Gago; Zhixi Chen; Sheik S\. Rahman

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Drilling\-fluid filtration is governed by coupled effects of particle size distribution \(PSD\), solids concentration, and particle\-retention conditions, making systematic exploration by direct particle\-scale simulation computationally demanding\. This study integrates computational fluid dynamics–discrete element method simulations with a machine\-learning surrogate to enable rapid interrogation of filtration behaviour while restricting interpretation to data\-supported regions\. The surrogate predicts stabilised filtrate flux from numerical retention cutoff, volume\-weighted particle\-size\-distribution descriptors, and particle volume concentration\. Predictive performance was examined using both random and input\-space grouped validation, together with a k\-nearest\-neighbour support criterion for identifying poorly supported regions\. The more demanding grouped evaluation yielded a pooled $$\\:\{R\}^\{2\}=0\.955$$ , demonstrating useful predictive performance when compact regions of the input space were withheld\. Parameter\-response analysis showed that particle volume concentration produced the strongest and most consistent response, while numerical retention cutoff and volume\-weighted PSD mean both produced substantial responses; the response associated with PSD standard deviation was comparatively weaker and more condition\-dependent\. A targeted same\-framework assessment produced a mean relative prediction error of 5\.76% at selected supported conditions\. The novelty of the framework lies in combining physics\-based filtration simulation, support\-aware surrogate interrogation, condition\-dependent parameter\-response analysis, and targeted numerical assessment within a single workflow\. The resulting approach provides a computationally efficient screening tool for prioritising supported candidate conditions for subsequent numerical or experimental confirmation\.
