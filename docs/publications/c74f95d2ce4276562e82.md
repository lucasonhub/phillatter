# Analysis on deformation and failure of multi\-stage high slopes in open\-pit mines based on strength reduction method

**Autores:** Wei Sun; Zhenhua Tang; Ling Ye; Xiao Fan; Jibing Tang; Huangsong Pan

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Multi\-stage high slopes in open\-pit mines containing weak diabase dikes exhibit complex progressive stepwise sliding failure under the combined effects of excavation disturbance and rainfall, yet their staged instability evolution laws remain insufficiently clarified in existing research\. Taking the No\.1 high slope of the Maoniup Rare Earth Mine in western Sichuan as the research object, this study systematically reveals the deformation\-failure mechanism controlled by diabase weak interlayers through field geological investigation, in\-situ shear tests and FLAC3D strength reduction numerical simulation\. Field surveys show that the slope develops numerous tensile cracks on multi\-level platforms and local slumping between platforms, with fractured, water\-sensitive diabase dikes serving as the dominant internal weak structural planes triggering instability; Monitoring data show a progressive\-failure pattern propagating from the 3,056 m platform toward the 3,080 m platform; in\-situ shear test results confirm that diabase possesses extremely low saturated shear strength, which deteriorates sharply after rainwater softening\. A three\-dimensional numerical model matching actual stratigraphy and platform topography is established, and the Mohr–Coulomb strength reduction method is adopted to reproduce the whole progressive failure process under continuous strength degradation\. The simulation results indicate that deformation initiates at diabase outcrops on the upper 3,056 m platform, and shear strain bands and plastic failure zones gradually extend downward along diabase belts to form interconnected through\-going slip surfaces across multi\-stage platforms, which well reproduces the field stepwise sliding characteristics\. Numerical calculations using saturated geomechanical parameters under rainfall conditions yield a slope safety factor of 1\.15, and the resultant deformation\-failure pattern is highly consistent with simulation results obtained with strength reduction factors ranging from 1\.75 to 2\.0, verifying the reliability of the strength reduction method for predicting progressive slope failure when accurate rainfall\-induced rock\-soil mechanical parameters are unavailable\. Furthermore, six groups of models with varying diabase dike dip angles and dip directions are constructed to quantitatively analyze the control effect of diabase occurrence on slope stability\. The results demonstrate that diabase dikes subparallel to slope platforms deliver the highest slope safety reserve \(safety factor = 1\.4\), while anti\-dipping diabase dikes with a 45° dip angle generate the largest sliding volume and the highest landslide risk\. This research clarifies the staged progressive failure evolution law of open\-pit multi\-stage high slopes with diabase weak interlayers, and provides theoretical references and technical support for stability evaluation, excavation optimization and geohazard prevention of analogous high\-altitude rare earth mine high slopes\.
