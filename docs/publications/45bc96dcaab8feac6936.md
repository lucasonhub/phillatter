# What if curiosity begins exactly where numerical representation fails?

**Autores:** FEDERICO NICOLAS BAEZ RIZZO

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

What if curiosity begins exactly where numerical representation fails? I&\#x27;ve been thinking about something that connects floating\-point arithmetic, epistemology, and the limits of artificial intelligence\. Not because I believe intelligence can be reduced to numerical precision, but because I suspect we are overlooking something fundamental about how information disappears\. 1\. The Gap — ULP \(Unit in the Last Place\) A floating\-point number can be represented as: x = \(−1\)ˢ · \(1 \+ m · 2⁻ᵖ\) · 2⁽ᵉ⁻ᵇⁱᵃˢ⁾ Between any representable number x and its immediate successor, nextafter\(x\), there are uncountably many real numbers\. The machine cannot represent them individually\. Yet they exist\. This makes me wonder whether curiosity can be understood as the capacity to recognize that reality contains distinctions our current representations cannot preserve\. 2\. Absorption — When Information Becomes Invisible In floating\-point arithmetic: x \+ ε = x A sufficiently small perturbation disappears under rounding\. In round\-to\-nearest arithmetic, this generally happens when ε falls below half an ULP\. The operation completes successfully\. The result is numerically valid\. And something has been lost\. I see a disturbing parallel with language models\. A subtle intention enters a system, passes through multiple transformations, and eventually becomes indistinguishable from something the model already knows how to express\. The output may be coherent, statistically plausible, even technically correct\. But was the original intention preserved? Or was it absorbed? 3\. Intention at the Edge of Precision The exponent and most significant bits determine the numerical scale and dominant value\. The least significant bits preserve distinctions that can disappear under subsequent operations\. I find this an interesting analogy for cognition\. Established knowledge dominates representation\. Familiar patterns become attractors\. Meanwhile, hesitation, ambiguity, contradiction, and emerging ideas are often the first things compressed away\. I am not claiming that intention literally resides in the tail of a binary mantissa\. I&\#x27;m asking whether our cognitive architectures have an equivalent of numerical absorption — and whether we can design systems capable of detecting what their own representations discard\. Because preserving information is not necessarily the same as preserving meaning\. And preserving meaning is not necessarily the same as preserving intention\. This is one of the questions driving my work on CORTEX\-9: moving beyond systems that merely produce plausible answers toward architectures that can track transformations, detect representational loss, and distinguish what was inferred from what was actually preserved\. Perhaps the next meaningful advance in AI will not come exclusively from increasing the number of parameters\. Perhaps part of it will come from teaching systems to recognize the boundaries of their own representational precision\. A system that cannot perceive what it has lost cannot reliably claim to have understood what it received\. And that, to me, is where curiosity becomes an architectural problem rather than just a philosophical concept\. — Federico Nicolas Baez Rizzo \#ArtificialIntelligence \#NumericalComputing \#CognitiveArchitecture \#Cortex9 \#Epistemology
