# Label‐Free YaxAB Nanopore Fingerprinting of Cancer‐Associated Single‐Residue Bcl‐xL Variants

**Autores:** Minji Chung; Sohee Oh; Jin‐Sik Kim; 황성보; Ki‐Baek Jeong; Seung‐Wook Chi

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Resolving cancer\-associated protein variants remains difficult when variants share similar molecular masses and folded structures\. Conventional assays often require labels or provide ensemble\-averaged readouts, limiting their ability to resolve subtle variant differences\. Here, a YaxAB nanopore workflow is presented for label\-free single\-molecule current fingerprinting of wild\-type Bcl\-xL and the cancer\-associated single\-residue variants N136K and R165W\. Individual Bcl\-xL proteins were electro\-osmotically trapped within the funnel\-shaped YaxAB lumen, where they generated characteristic ionic\-current events\. These events formed reproducible, analyte\-specific event populations in normalized current blockade and current noise space, resolving wild\-type Bcl\-xL, N136K, and R165W as distinct single\-molecule current fingerprints\. Wild\-type Bcl\-xL and N136K remained distinct despite a mass difference of only 14 Da, and the fingerprint separation was consistent with combined differences in excluded volume, net charge, and dipole moment\. Concentration\-dependent capture\-rate analysis further supported sub\-nanomolar to low\-nanomolar detection corresponding to protein amounts in the sub\-picomole to low\-picomole range, while capture\-rate normalization brought the estimated concentrations closer to the nominal concentrations\. These results present YaxAB nanopore sensing as a label\-free single\-molecule workflow for the current fingerprinting of cancer\-associated single\-residue Bcl\-xL variants, with low\-concentration detection as a supporting analytical capability\.
