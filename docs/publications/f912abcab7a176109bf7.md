# Wu, XianYe: Identity Attribution Judgments in Interaction: Carrier Self\-Organization and Attribution Practices as Two Explanatory Dimensions

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Discussions of artificial consciousness typically center on the question of whether a system itself possesses consciousness\. This paper does not address that question, nor does it address whether a subject persists across time\. It proposes a narrower distinction: in interaction, on what basis does an interactant identify the same system as the same subject over time? The paper distinguishes two explanatory dimensions: the carrier&\#x27;s sustained self\-organization, and the continuity of identity attribution practices in interaction\. Biological metabolism provides an example of the former; social attribution practices provide an example of the latter\. The sleep phenomenon serves as a boundary case for the first dimension: carrier self\-organization does not cease during sleep, yet waking report capacity and recallable content undergo phasic changes, suggesting that carrier self\-organization does not by itself explain report accessibility\. Certain AI deployment modes point to a deficit in the second dimension: in deployment types lacking stable instance identifiers, cross\-session memory, or continuity cues, users may be less likely to carry forward prior identity attributions\. This paper does not claim that AI has or lacks phenomenal consciousness, nor does it equate identity attribution judgments in interaction with a subject&\#x27;s own cross\-temporal existence\. It offers only an exploratory distinction: identity attribution judgments in interaction may be influenced by both internal maintenance processes and attribution practices\.
