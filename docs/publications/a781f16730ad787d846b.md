  to build an integrated toolkit for defense SMEs. Future work can examine the generative

  environments. The method offers a replicable template for integrating emerging technologies
  into highly regulated and risk-averse operations, helping small firms remain competitive
  while safeguarding national security.

# Operationalizing AI Governance for Defense SMEs: A Modular, Generative\-AI\-Assisted Framework for NIST SP 800\-171 and CMMC Compliance

**Autores:** Rizwan Virani

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This praxis investigates how artificial intelligence \(AI\) governance can be integrated into the cybersecurity compliance workflows of United States defense small and mid\-sized enterprises \(SMEs\)\. These firms are navigating the transition to the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification \(CMMC\) 2\.0 program and the foundational requirements of National Institute of Standards and Technology \(NIST\) Special Publication \(SP\) 800\-171\. Existing AI risk guidance, such as the NIST AI Risk Management Framework \(AI RMF\), does not provide implementation\-focused direction suited to the resource constraints of smaller firms\. This praxis therefore describes a control mapping method consistent with the overlay logic of NIST&\#x27;s Control Overlays for Securing AI Systems \(COSAIS\) concept paper\. It uses that method to develop the Modular AI Governance Framework \(MAGF\), a &quot;comply once, satisfy many&quot; governance framework and toolkit\. My research uses a convergent mixed\-methods design guided by five research questions\. Four ask how practitioners map AI risk categories to control families, which families they identify most often, how feasible they judge lightweight governance artifacts to be, and what barriers and enablers shape implementation\. The fifth asks to what extent the resulting framework is practical, scalable, and audit\-aligned\. In the first phase, 38 practitioners mapped six AI risk categories onto the 14 NIST SP 800\-171 control families through a structured survey\. They prioritized &quot;auditability gaps&quot; in data integrity and model transparency, yet many reported lacking the specialized staff to document these areas manually\. Qualitative analysis of their open\-ended responses produced eight empirically grounded design requirements, including control family anchoring, traceability\-first evidence, change control for probabilistic systems, and a dual\-purpose evidence pattern\. Inspired by operational &quot;playbooks,&quot; the MAGF defines governance tasks as structured modules with goals, actions, and lightweight evidence artifacts\. The modules model the work already required in defense cybersecurity and span the AI lifecycle from initiation and data collection to production deployment and monitoring\. A prompt library provides engineered prompts for generative AI chatbot tools like Google&\#x27;s Gemini and Microsoft&\#x27;s Copilot to support human\-reviewed drafting of artifacts such as bias assessment records and model/prompt change logs\. No output of that prompt library was evaluated in this study\. I evaluated the framework through a structured expert review rather than a measured field deployment\. A panel of 13 qualified reviewers, six of whom identified primarily as assessors, rated the framework and its artifacts against defined criteria\. The results were encouraging but bounded\. Reviewers judged the framework relevant, clear, and valuable as assessment evidence\. Their reservations centered on the feasibility of the most measurement\-intensive artifacts for the smallest firms\. Only 30\.8% of reviewers were favorable on scalability, which marks the boundary of what this study establishes\. An illustrative estimate anchored to published federal compliance cost figures and peer\-reviewed evidence on generative writing productivity suggests a meaningful reduction in recurring drafting effort while human review remains constant\. The estimate is not a measurement, and it does not assert that any requirement or practice is satisfied\. It also shows that careful coordination is needed to keep documentation bloat and fatigue from eroding the benefit and slowing certification\. This praxis contributes a method for compliance\-aligned and resource\-optimized AI governance design and applies it to build an integrated toolkit for defense SMEs\. Future work can examine the generative AI assistance in greater detail and apply the framework to other emerging regulatory environments\. The method offers a replicable template for integrating emerging technologies into highly regulated and risk\-averse operations, helping small firms remain competitive while safeguarding national security\.
