# If Not us Then Who: Using Photovoice to Explore Black Women Teachers’ Othermothering and Fictive Kinship

**Autores:** Christina Wright Fields; Myra Bellamy; Lynnette Mawhinney; Laura Krystal Porterfield; Carol R\. Rinke

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Black teachers in the United States exhibit remarkable resilience, often are expected to do a lot with a little, while combatting inequitable practices and educational politics while striving to meet the diverse needs of their students\. This paper draws together complementary concepts of othermothering and fictive kinship to reimagine the roles of Black teachers in educational spaces\. Othermothering refers to the act of accepting responsibility for a child not one’s own \(James, 1993\), while fictive kinship encompasses creating familial\-like bonds within the community \(Fordham, 1996\)\. Using photovoice methodology, this study explored one Black woman teacher’s experiences, focusing on how she cultivated, nurtured, and sustained meaningful relationships with her students \(Beauboeuf\-Lafontant, 2002\)\. The findings underscore themes of interconnectedness, othermothering, and fictive kinship, highlighting the importance of advocacy, responsibility, and empathy toward students\. Through visual counterstorytelling \(Author 5 et al\., 2025\), the teacher shared photographs that illuminated how she engaged in othermothering and fictive kinship to meet her students’ academic, personal, and socio\-emotional needs\. By sharing her experiences visually, this Black woman teacher not only gained a deeper understanding of her journey but also contributed to a broader dialogue about the intersection of identity and teaching practices in educational systems\.
