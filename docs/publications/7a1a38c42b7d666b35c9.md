# Evaluation of Different Methods of Haemoglobin Estimation Among Malaria‐Infected Patients

**Autores:** Mina Ofosu; Jessica Ntiamoah; Patricia Sam Barko; Prince Elikem Torli

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: Haemoglobin \(Hb\) estimation is essential for assessing anaemia, particularly in malaria\-endemic settings\. Automated Full Blood Count \(FBC\) analysers, cyanmethemoglobin spectrophotometry, and portable Hb meters are commonly used, but differences in analytical performance may affect measurements\. METHODS: This cross\-sectional study compared Hb measurements obtained using an automated FBC analyser, the cyanmethemoglobin method, and a portable Hb meter among 100 malaria\-infected patients aged 16\-65 years at Kumasi South Hospital, Ghana\. Measurements were compared using Pearson&\#x27;s correlation coefficient, intraclass correlation coefficient \(ICC\), Lin&\#x27;s concordance correlation coefficient \(CCC\), Bland\-Altman analysis, percentage error, and Passing\-Bablok regression\. RESULTS: The three methods showed very strong correlations \(r = 0\.955\-0\.992, p &lt; 0\.001\), excellent ICC \(single measures = 0\.969; average measures = 0\.990\), and high concordance \(CCC = 0\.95\-0\.99\)\. Mean biases were \-0\.06 g/dL for FBC analyser versus portable Hb meter, \-0\.25 g/dL for FBC analyser versus cyanmethemoglobin, and \-0\.20 g/dL for portable Hb meter versus cyanmethemoglobin\. The corresponding 95% limits of agreement were \-0\.59 to 0\.47, \-1\.48 to 0\.98, and \-1\.35 to 0\.95 g/dL, respectively\. Passing\-Bablok slopes were 1\.00 for all comparisons\. The FBC analyser and portable Hb meter showed the closest agreement\. CONCLUSION: The three methods demonstrated good agreement and broadly comparable Hb measurements\. The findings support the practical use of portable Hb meters where point\-of\-care testing is required, while recognising that agreement does not establish diagnostic accuracy or analytical validation\.
