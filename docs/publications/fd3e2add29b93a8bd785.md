# Plant extract mediated liquid phase exfoliation of MoS2: a sustainable approach to micro and nanostructured material preparation

**Autores:** Hariprasad Ramachandran; Sharmila Nageswaran

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Molybdenum disulfide \(MoS 2 \), a two\-dimensional transition metal dichalcogenide with distinctive layer\-dependent optical, electrical, and catalytic properties, has attracted significant attention for applications ranging from flexible electronics to energy storage and biosensing\. However, conventional synthesis and exfoliation methods for MoS 2 often rely on harsh chemicals, high energy input, and generate toxic derivatives, posing significant health and ecological concerns\. Addressing these limitations, this study presents an eco\-friendly alternative \- the green synthesis of plant extract\-mediated MoS 2 micro\- and nanostructures using Cissus quadrangularis \(CQ\), Ocimum sanctum \(OS\), and Lawsonia inermis \(LI\) as natural exfoliating and stabilizing agents\. Characterization techniques, including X\-ray diffraction, Raman spectroscopy, Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy, UV\-Visible spectroscopy, dynamic light scattering, SEM, and conductivity studies, validated the successful synthesis of MoS 2 materials with distinct morphological, optical, and electrical properties\. XRD confirmed the hexagonal phase of MoS 2 , with peak broadening observed in plant\-mediated samples consistent with reduced crystallite size and/or lattice strain\. Raman spectroscopy and FTIR results revealed the presence of functional groups and spectral features consistent with few\-layer character\. Dynamic light scattering and UV\-Visible spectroscopy studies revealed changes in hydrodynamic size and band structure based on the synthesis route\. SEM images showed structural variances ranging from stacked layers to flower\-like assemblies, while EDX confirmed the presence of Mo and S in all samples\. Electrical measurements suggested semiconducting behaviour in all samples, with conductivity modulated by synthesis conditions\. The plant\-mediated exfoliation method presents a viable, eco\-friendly alternative to conventional approaches, with potential relevance to flexible electronics and catalysis; the solvent\-exfoliated control \(MoS 2 –NMP\) showed higher conductivity than the plant\-mediated samples, an important finding for optimizing electrical performance in future device\-oriented studies\.
