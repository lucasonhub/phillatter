# Machine Learning Detection of Peritoneal Entry from Vibroacoustic Signals During Veress Needle Insertion

**Autores:** Moritz Spiller; Mohammad Rezapourian; Axel Boese; Alfredo Illanes Manríquez; Thomas Neumuth; Nazila Esmaeili

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background/Objectives: Establishing access is a hazardous step of laparoscopic surgery, and the widely used Veress needle offers only subjective tactile and auditory cues for peritoneal entry\. Proximal vibroacoustic \(VA\) sensing captures needle–tissue interaction non\-invasively and is known to contain a characteristic peritoneal puncture signature, which could help surgeons to precisely identify peritoneal entry\. However, the VA signature has been evaluated only by retrospective, video\-anchored and manual signal analysis\. We investigate whether peritoneal entry can instead be detected automatically\. Methods: Cavity entry is framed as a binary classification of VA signal segments, and four model families are benchmarked under a single unified protocol: a one\-dimensional convolutional neural network \(1D CNN\), a multilayer perceptron, a bidirectional long short\-term memory network \(LSTM\), and a support vector machine \(SVM\) with a radial basis function kernel\. All models were trained, selected, and threshold\-tuned on a synthetic abdominal phantom \(996 segments\) and tested once on an independent porcine cadaver dataset \(154 cavity, 462 non\-cavity signals\) so that every test result measures generalisation across a phantom\-to\-porcine domain shift\. Results: The 1D CNN performed the best \(accuracy 0\.869, AUROC 0\.904, macro F1 0\.833, cavity recall 0\.818\), closely followed by the SVM \(AUROC 0\.900, AUPRC 0\.878\)\. Conclusions: These results establish the feasibility of automatically detecting peritoneal entry across a phantom\-to\-porcine domain shift and extend the descriptive cadaveric characterisation of the puncture signature toward the automated machine learning\-based detection of peritoneal entry in laparoscopic access with a Veress needle\.
