# X\-AiDO: An expert AI\-driven framework coupled with multi\-optimization for gas adsorption prediction in activated carbons

**Autores:** Helder R O Rocha; Jimmy Romanos; Sara Abou Dargham; Roy Roukos; Jair A\.l\. Silva; H\. J\. Wörtche

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rational design of porous carbonaceous adsorbents remains pivotal for clean energy transitions, encompassing hydrogen storage, carbon dioxide capture, methane upgrading, and nitrogen separation\. Conventional single\-network machine learning models often impose shared parameters across divergent gas\-solid adsorption mechanisms, introducing structural bias\. Here, we present X\-AiDO, an expert artificial intelligence\-driven framework coupling a Mixture\-of\-Experts \(MoE\) topology with an Adaptive Reference Multi\-Objective Whale Optimization Algorithm \(AR\-MOWOA\)\. The architecture integrates independent gradient\-boosted decision tree experts with continuous soft gating across an experimentally verified database of 2275 records spanning 142 unique activated carbons\. Model generalization was systematically evaluated across two distinct cross\-validation strategies implemented with strictly fold\-isolated Multivariate Imputation by Chained Equations \(MICE\)\. Under grouped evaluation on previously unseen adsorbent matrices \(Protocol B\), the model delivers a Root Mean Squared Error of 3\.36 ± 1\.48 wt% \( MAE = 2\.01 ± 0\.86 wt% \) and R 2 = 0\.9407 ± 0\.0507 \( 3\.32 ± 1\.46 wt% , MAE = 1\.97 ± 0\.83 wt% , R 2 = 0\.9407 ± 0\.0528 for Hard MoE\), significantly outperforming monolithic baselines \( RMSE = 4\.70 ± 3\.35 wt% , MAE = 2\.96 ± 2\.09 wt% , R 2 = 0\.8593 ± 0\.1514 \)\. For inverse design, materials are defined by continuous physicochemical descriptors within experimental boundaries, avoiding discrete identity constraints\. Rigorous multi\-objective benchmarking against NSGA\-II across all four gas systems under identical 10,000\-evaluation budgets confirms competitive convergence and reference\-directed hypervolume performance \(up to 0\.5432 for CO 2 \)\. Pareto knee\-point solutions with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals identify optimal materials yielding 10\.22 wt% for H 2 , 44\.06 wt% for CO 2 , 9\.02 wt% for CH 4 , and 6\.71 wt% for N 2 \.
