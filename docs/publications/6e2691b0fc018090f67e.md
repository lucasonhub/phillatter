# Liquid Crystal as Solid Electrolytes: Selected Developments and Perspectives

**Autores:** Manasa Chandramouli; Vrushabendra Basavanna; Srikantamurthy Ningaiah

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The liquid crystalline state arises from orientation\-dependent, non\-covalent interactions among molecules in condensed phases, producing materials that combine structural order with molecular mobility\. Because this ordered arrangement depends on a delicate balance of intermolecular forces, liquid crystals \(LCs\) are highly responsive to external stimuli, making them attractive for sensing and electrochemical applications\. This review critically examines recent advances in liquid crystal\-based solid electrolytes, with emphasis on materials design strategies, ionic transport mechanisms, and electrochemical performance\. The discussion is organized into key thematic sections covering \(i\) fundamental intermolecular interactions governing liquid crystalline behavior, \(ii\) classes of ionic liquid\-crystalline systems, including zwitterionic and mesoionic mesogens, and \(iii\) liquid crystalline materials composed of organic cations paired with organic or inorganic anions interacting through electrostatic and other non\-covalent forces\. Representative studies from recent literature are analyzed to highlight structure–property relationships and performance trends in solid\-state devices such as lithium batteries, fuel cells, and dye\-sensitized solar cells\. By integrating material design principles with functional performance analysis, this review provides a focused perspective on the potential and limitations of liquid crystalline solid electrolytes for next\-generation all\-solid\-state technologies\.
