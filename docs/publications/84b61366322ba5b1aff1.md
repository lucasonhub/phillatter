  associated with religion.

# Usko särkyneissä maailmoissa : Uskonnon representaatiot viimeaikaisessa dystopiakirjallisuudessa

**Autores:** Kaisa Kaukiainen

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

This study examines the representation of religion in recent North American dystopian literature\. It investigates how religious beliefs and practices are portrayed, what roles faith and spirituality play within dystopian narratives, how dystopian worlds shape religious expression, and how religion is mediated through the perspectives of characters\. By focusing on North American dystopian novels published over the past three decades, the study establishes a distinct framework within the broader field of research on religion and dystopian literature\. The study begins by outlining key genre theories and methodological approaches and by defining the concept of religion as it is used in the analysis\. American exceptionalism, civil religion, and the problem of theodicy provide an essential conceptual background for the discussion\. Religion is examined from two complementary perspectives\. First, the study analyses dystopian storyworlds, exploring whether and how religion is addressed, how religious elements are represented, and how they are transformed by dystopian conditions\. Religion may appear explicitly or emerge indirectly through allegorical structures\. Second, the study investigates religion as it is perceived and experienced by individual characters\. Because dystopian fiction frequently relies on personal viewpoints and first\-person narration, characters’ emotions, moral choices, and religious or spiritual attitudes become central to the representation of religion\. The analysis begins with Margaret Atwood’s MaddAddam trilogy \(2003–2013\), which provides a comprehensive depiction of dystopian religiosity\. The study then examines other works, including Octavia E\. Butler’s Parable novels, as well as popular young adult dystopias, such as Suzanne Collins’s The Hunger Games trilogy and Lois Lowry’s The Giver series, where belief systems often function at an allegorical level\. Additional novels are also considered to broaden the analytical scope\. The study demonstrates that American cultural and religious heritage forms an important background for many recent dystopian narratives, particularly given their attempts to confront evil and imagine sustainable futures\. Many of the texts analysed can be characterised as critical dystopias that combine bleak visions with elements of hope\. Resistance movements, a recurring feature of dystopian fiction, often resemble religious movements; this structural parallel shapes both world\-building and processes of meaning\-making\. A thematically related chapter also considers artificial intelligence as a contemporary phenomenon that challenges established conceptions of religion through its literary portrayal as an omniscient or quasi\-divine entity\. Across the works examined, religiosity emerges as a fluid and often ambiguous element\. Religion may occasionally appear as a dominant feature, but more frequently it remains indirect and open to interpretation\. Although many texts portray religion in indifferent or negative terms, depicting it as outdated, ineffective, or associated with authoritarianism and fanaticism, religion nevertheless permeates dystopian narratives through symbolic frameworks, imagery, and modes of thought\. The study concludes that religion in dystopian fiction frequently signifies the inadequacy of established systems in a radically altered world\. Traditional religious frameworks are often displaced by more fluid and individualised forms of belief that emphasise personal spirituality and meaning\-making\. Individual perspectives become key indicators of faith, particularly in young adult dystopias, which commonly focus on selfhood, resilience, and engagement with the inexplicable, a realm traditionally associated with religion\.
