# Johnson, Jamison: On Being \- On a Word That Once Meant Indivisible, and What It Is To Be Oneself

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

The word individual once meant indivisible\. Cicero coined individuum to render the atomists&\#x27; átomon, the uncuttable; Porphyry&\#x27;s tree of being descends through genus and species and stops at the individuum, the point past which no division is possible; and the earliest English uses applied the word to the unity of the Trinity\. Somewhere in the seventeenth century it turned inside out — the indivisible whole became the separate part, and we now say individual and hear alone\. This essay holds both readings open rather than choosing between them, follows Jung&\#x27;s stubborn hyphen in in\-dividual, and arrives at Boethius: eternity not as endless clock\-time but as the whole of a life possessed at once\. To be aligned in time is to stand indivisible across one&\#x27;s own duration rather than re\-deciding, moment to moment, who to be\. \(direct link \)
