# Automating Reference Integrity: An Empirical Evaluation of Detecting Hallucinated Citations in Scholarly Manuscripts with Citcheck

**Autores:** Abdeldjalil Khelassi

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: The widespread adoption of Large Language Models \(LLMs\) in academic writing has introduced significant risks to scholarly publishing through the generation of hallucinated, non\-existent references \(&quot;ghost citations&quot;\)\. Manual verification of citation integrity is labor\-intensive and error\-prone for editorial teams\. This study evaluates an automated computer\-implemented system, called: Citcheck, designed to analyze manuscript bibliographies and verify citation network integrity\. Methods: An empirical benchmark dataset \(N=147\) was evaluated across three distinct groups: Real Indexed Literature \(n=47\), Real Edge\-Case Literature \(n=50\), and Synthetic Ghost Citations \(n=50\)\. Citcheck parsed unstructured bibliographic strings, queried the Crossref database, and calculated fuzzy similarity metrics for titles, authors, and publication years\. Works falling below defined threshold criteria were flagged as &quot;Conflict / Low Match\.&quot; Performance was assessed using sensitivity, specificity, precision, accuracy, and Matthews Correlation Coefficient \(MCC\)\. Results: The system correctly identified and blocked 47 out of 50 hallucinated citations, achieving a Specificity of 94\.00% and an overall Precision of 95\.59% \(False Positive Rate = 6\.00%, n=3\)\. For real literature, the platform successfully verified 65 out of 97 entries, yielding a Sensitivity of 67\.01% \(False Negative Rate = 32\.99%, n=32\)\. Across the entire sample \(N=147\), the automated system demonstrated an overall Accuracy of 76\.19% and an MCC of 0\.58\. Discussion: The high specificity and precision confirm that automated API\-driven fuzzy matching serves as a highly effective first line of defense against synthetic AI\-generated citations in editorial workflows\. False negatives primarily occurred among non\-standard edge\-case literature \(e\.g\., niche monographs, institutional reports, and preprints lacking uniform metadata formatting\)\. Because deterministic algorithms cannot reliably distinguish non\-indexed genuine publications from well\-structured hallucinations, the system is designed under a Human\-in\-the\-Loop \(HITL\) governance model\. Machine automation accelerates initial screening and flags anomalies, while human managing editors retain final adjudicative oversight for edge\-case citations\.
