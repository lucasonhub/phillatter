# Complexity\-Aware Geographic Fairness in Crop Yield Prediction Using Diversity\-Weighted Learning

**Autores:** Vipin Bihari Yadav; Aditya Khamparia

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Machine\-learning models for crop yield prediction are usually judged by overall accuracy alone, which hides large differences in prediction quality between regions\. This study shows that a standard Random Forest with an aggregate R² of 0\.935 can be misleading: state\-level MAE ranges from 0\.168 t/ha to 2\.505 t/ha, a 14\.9\-fold gap that the overall score conceals\. Two independent structural factors underlie this imbalance: the number of records per state \(data\-volume imbalance\) and the agricultural complexity of each state, both of which can be observed in any census or statistical record\. To address both at once, a Complexity\-Aware Geographic Fairness \(CAGF\) framework is presented that combines a Crop Diversity Index based on Shannon entropy with inverse\-frequency sample re\-weighting\. Four architectures \(Random Forest, XGBoost, SVR and MLP\) are evaluated under three training regimes \(unweighted baseline, inverse\-frequency weighting and CAGF\) using expanding\-window temporal cross\-validation, and TreeSHAP attribution shows that CAGF directs model attention toward crop\-specific regional patterns\. For XGBoost, CAGF gives the best overall accuracy among the three regimes \(R² = 0\.918; MAE = 1\.299 t/ha\) together with a lower geographic Gini of prediction error than the unweighted baseline \(GGPE = 0\.289 versus 0\.293\), so fairness improves without any loss of accuracy\.
