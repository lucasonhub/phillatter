# Current state and future directions of the discipline of rural medicine in Aotearoa New Zealand: a qualitative study

**Autores:** Katharina Blattner; Lynne Clay; Rory Miller; Garry Nixon; Tim Stokes

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

INTRODUCTION: Rural medicine is shaped by geographic distance from resources and specialist care, requiring broad generalist practice\. International evidence supports rural\-targeted postgraduate training, clinical\-academic integration, and a dedicated evidence base\. Progress in Aotearoa New Zealand \(NZ\) has been constrained by underinvestment, workforce shortages, and persistent inequities\. AIM: This study aimed to explore postgraduate rural medicine stakeholder perspectives on the current status of rural medicine as a speciality in NZ and what is needed to strengthen it\. METHODS: We conducted a qualitative study using semi\-structured interviews\. Thematic analysis was undertaken using a framework\-guided rapid analysis approach\. Interviews explored key patterns relating to clinical practice, postgraduate training, academic capacity, and system\-level influences\. RESULTS: Interviews with 31 participants \(including trainees, clinicians, educators, academics, and institutional leaders\) identified two themes\. The first described postgraduate rural medicine development to date, including the importance of rural\-targeted training, emerging career pathways, and ongoing invisibility\. The second highlighted future priorities, including a rural\-specific general practice pathway, stronger rural leadership, and greater collaboration and institutional investment\. DISCUSSION: Strengthening rural\-specific postgraduate pathways, together with stronger support for rural educators and researchers, is essential for improving visibility and fostering clinical\-academic career development\. A coordinated, nationwide strategy is also needed to strengthen the academic standing of rural medicine, ensure its long\-term sustainability, and advance rural health equity in NZ\.
