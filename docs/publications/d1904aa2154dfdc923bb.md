# Immunotechnology\-based toolbox for care of chronic wounds

**Autores:** Munishwar Nath Gupta; Premlata Ambre

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Wound healing is a complex natural process with overlapping sequences of inflammatory, proliferation, and remodeling stages\. All stages involve immune cells and their secreted molecules, including macrophages, neutrophils, and cytokines\. This process becomes dysregulated in chronic wounds, such as diabetic ulcers and malignant \(fungating\) wounds, where healing halts before it can be completed\. Both generic immunomodulation and targeted immunoengineering approaches constitute a powerful toolbox to reset healing processes\. This brief review first provides background on the involvement of immune system components contribute to wound healing and their dysregulation during impaired wound healing\. It then discusses the current state of knowledge on immunomodulation and immunoengineering as applied to chronic wound care\. The immunomodulation approach uses stem cells or extracellular vesicles to regulate inflammation and prevent cellular senescence so that tissue regeneration takes place\. Immunoengineering approaches cover the use of DNA nanoparticles to aid tissue regeneration, and aptamer\-antibody conjugates to control inflammation\. Finally, theranostic devices which provide real\-time sensing and delivery of necessary proteins and drugs to reset wound healing have been discussed\.
