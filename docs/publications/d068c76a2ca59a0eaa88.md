# Pan\-genome analysis reveals conservation and diversification of the SWEET gene family in wheat

**Autores:** Yanlin Yang; Liuliu Wu; Junyan Sun; Jie Li; Shuhan Liu; Mingming Tang; Zongli Chu; Liping Dong; Liping Dong; Shumei Li; Lei Dong; Lei Dong

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

SWEET \(Sugars Will Eventually be Exported Transporter\) proteins mediate sugar transport and play important roles in plant growth, development, source\-sink allocation, and responses to environmental stimuli\. However, the conservation and diversification of the SWEET gene family across the wheat pan\-genome remain poorly understood\. Here, we performed a systematic pan\-genome analysis of the SWEET gene family across 19 hexaploid wheat \( Triticum aestivum L\.\) accessions\. A total of 1,594 SWEET genes were identified, with 77–90 members in individual genomes\. Gene copies belonging to core, soft\-core, dispensable, and private homologous groups accounted for 52\.6%, 30\.9%, 14\.6%, and 1\.9%, respectively, based on presence/absence variation \(PAV\), revealing a predominantly conserved genomic framework accompanied by a smaller accession\-variable component\. Phylogenetic, motif, chromosomal and synteny analyses further revealed extensive evolutionary and genomic conservation accompanied by localized diversification among family members\. Extensive homeologous and inter\-accession collinearity was observed, and most pairwise ortholog comparisons exhibited Ka/Ks ratios below 1, supporting predominant purifying selection on conserved SWEET coding sequences\. Promoter analysis identified diverse putative cis\-regulatory elements related to environmental, hormonal, and developmental regulation, while transcriptome profiling showed heterogeneous tissue\- and stress\-associated expression patterns among TaSWEET genes\. qRT\-PCR analysis further suggested distinct temporal responses of selected TaSWEET genes under cold and polyethylene glycol \(PEG\)\-induced osmotic stress\. TaSWEET47 overexpression significantly increased grain width, thousand\-grain weight, and mature\-grain starch content in two independent transgenic lines without significantly affecting grain length\. Collectively, these results reveal gene\-content, evolutionary, regulatory, and transcriptional diversification within a broadly conserved SWEET family framework\.
