# Molecular docking and validation of azoreductases for bioremediation of high concentrations of azo and anthraquinone dyes\- an integrated in silico\-in vitro approach

**Autores:** Sakthishabarish Kumar; Goverdhini Sekar; Sweetha Rajamuthu; Vidhya Shree Vijaykumar; Sreenidhi Yuvaraja; Kannabiran Krishnan

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

High concentrations of synthetic dyes in textile effluents remain a major environmental challenge due to the limited availability of effective treatment strategies\. This study integrated in silico – in vitro approaches to identify and validate efficient azoreductases for textile dye bioremediation\. Ten reactive dyes were docked with reference crystallographic azoreductase structures, AzoP \(PDB ID: 2v9c\) and AzoK \(PDB ID: 7n2w\) using AutoDock Vina\. Based on favourable binding energies and strong interactions, four dyes–Acid Red 131 \(AR131\), Reactive Red 195 \(RR195\), Reactive Blue 19 \(RB19, anthraquinone dye\), and Reactive Blue 160 \(RB160\) were screened\. AzoK exhibited strong binding affinity towards RB19, RR195, and AR131, whereas AzoP showed high binding affinity for RR195, AR131, and RB160\. Under minimal nutrient conditions, partially purified enzymatic treatment achieved decolorization efficiencies of 96\.4%, 95\.6%, and 93\.4% for AR131, RB19, and RR195, respectively, at 1,000 mg/L, while RB160 exhibited 88\.3% decolorization at 2,000 mg/L\. These results further demonstrated the broad substrate specificity of azoreductase in decolorization of both azo and anthraquinone dyes\. FTIR suggested structural degradation through azo bond cleavage and transformation of major functional groups\. Enzymatic treatment reduced phytotoxicity by 80\.1%, enhancing root elongation of Zea mays \(AR131\) by 20\.7% and shoot elongation of Vigna radiata \(RB19\) by 43\.9%\. Overall, azoreductases extracted from Pseudomonas aeruginosa VITKSS5 \(AzoP\) and Klebsiella pneumoniae VITKSS6 \(AzoK\) demonstrated a sustainable and effective solution for decolorization, degradation, and detoxification of highly concentrated azo and anthraquinone dye pollutants\.
