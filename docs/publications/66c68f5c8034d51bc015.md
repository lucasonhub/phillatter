# Experimentos Mentales y Racionalismo Modal: una evaluación crítica del enfoque de Chalmers

**Autores:** Luis Alejandro Murillo Lara

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

“Thought experiments” are common in many scientific disciplines and can have significant theoretical consequences\. However, we know they can fail, which leads to the question of how to establish that a thought experiment legitimately achieves its result\. Given the modal dimension of thought experiments, one plausible option is to address this question within the framework of a particular modal epistemology\. However, David Chalmers’ modal epistemology, incompatible with the currently most widely accepted view of thought experiments, could pose a significant obstacle to developing this option\. Seeking to determine the viability of this option, in this manuscript I analyze and evaluate this form of modal epistemology, particularly whether it advances our understanding of modal thinking\. My discussion will focus on notions crucial to Chalmers, such as “primary conceivability,” “ideal conceivability,” and “positive conceivability\.” I will begin by presenting what is arguably the most widely accepted conception of thought experiments, and then move on to discuss Chalmers’ approach\. Finally, I will consider some theoretical alternatives in the field of modal epistemology; the compatibility of one of them with the most accepted view on thought experimentas will allow us to conclude that the path is clear to develop the aforementioned option\.
