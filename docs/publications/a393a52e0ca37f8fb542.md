# Deer disturbance influences avian acoustic activity across functional groups in temperate forests

**Autores:** C\. Brock; Simone Ciuti; Jonathan Willans; Virginia Morera‐Pujol

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Cervid overpopulation is increasingly altering temperate forest ecosystems worldwide, when browsing pressure exceeds ecosystem resilience, with consequences that extend beyond individual species to the structure and functioning of entire communities\. While the impacts of deer \( Cervidae \) on specific bird species are well studied, there remains a critical gap in understanding how deer influence avian communities across a spectrum of functional traits\. Addressing this gap is essential for predicting community\-level responses to environmental disturbances and informing targeted conservation strategies\. Here, we examine the effects of sika deer \( Cervus nippon \) on avian communities in Irish temperate forests by combining camera trap and passive acoustic monitoring with traditional forest surveys, such as tree and understory vegetation surveys, and avian functional trait data\. We assessed relationships between site\-level deer encounter rate, derived from camera trap detections as a proxy for relative deer disturbance, and \(i\) overall avian vocal activity using the mid\-frequency cover \(MFC\) acoustic index in a generalized additive mixed model \(GAMM\), and \(ii\) functional responses using generalized linear mixed models \(GLMMs\)\. Our results show that site\-level deer encounter rates are associated with overall avian vocal activity in a pattern which aligns with the broader ecological context of the intermediate disturbance hypothesis \(IDH\), with avian acoustic activity highest at low\-to\-intermediate deer encounter rates and declining at higher encounter rates\. The strongest declines in acoustic activity at higher deer encounter rates were observed among near\-ground and tree\-nesting species, as well as species associated with anthropogenic, forest/woodland, and shrubland habitats, insectivores, omnivores, and vertebrate feeders, and generalist, insessorial \(branch\-perching\), and terrestrial species\. These findings demonstrate that high levels of deer disturbance can reshape avian community composition across functional traits, highlighting the need for management strategies that recognize how increasing deer pressure influences avian functional groups in temperate forest ecosystems\.
