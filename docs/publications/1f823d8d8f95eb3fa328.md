# Human\-Ecosystem Coupling Mechanisms and Data\-Driven Spatial Optimization in Urban Green Infrastructure

**Autores:** Ye Wen

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

High\-density urban construction continuously alters land cover, hydrothermal cycles, and habitat patterns, significantly impacting residents&\#x27; well\-being through heat exposure, air quality variations, activity opportunities, and nature contact\. Urban green infrastructure, relying on vegetation, water bodies, and soil processes to buffer built\-up environmental stressors, serves as a crucial link between ecological resilience, public health, and spatial governance\. From a socio\-ecological coupling perspective, this paper outlines the complex interaction chain between built\-up environmental pressures, ecological process responses, resident exposure, and governance feedback, systematically analyzing the synergy, trade\-offs, and equity differences of multifunctional services\. Based on this framework, it discusses the advanced integration of remote sensing, street view imagery, spatial machine learning, multi\-objective optimization, and urban digital twins in state identification, priority area determination, scheme generation, and dynamic feedback\. Existing evidence suggests that green infrastructure performance is jointly shaped by ecological structure, urban morphology, population activity, and governance conditions\. Ultimately, spatial optimization for high\-density cities requires a fundamental shift from static green volume assessment to dynamic decision\-making that comprehensively combines ecological processes, actual exposure, equitable allocation, and long\-term feedback mechanisms to ensure sustainable urban development and enhanced community well\-being across diverse socio\-economic contexts\. Furthermore, addressing these challenges necessitates interdisciplinary collaboration to bridge the gap between theoretical models and practical urban planning implementations\.
