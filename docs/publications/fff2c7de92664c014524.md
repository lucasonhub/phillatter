# Zhang, Sulin: Categorical Exemption: How the Logic of Species Discrimination Recurs in Human Inequality

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Accounts of human inequality usually emphasize historical, economic, and cultural structures and say little about how such structures resemble the routine downgrading of nonhuman animals\. This paper develops a structural account of that resemblance without claiming that species discrimination historically or psychologically precedes oppression among humans\. It introduces the concept of categorical moral exemption: once a being is assigned to a morally downgraded category such as &quot;pest,&quot; &quot;vermin,&quot; or &quot;parasite,&quot; standard deliberative procedures, including case\-by\-case judgment, proportionality, and the arousal of sympathy, are procedurally short\-circuited, and harm can be inflicted at low psychological cost\. Drawing on moral philosophy, the social psychology of dehumanization, and ecological criticism, the paper identifies a four\-step exemption schema \(categorical downgrading, harm minimization, procedural bypass, low\-cost infliction\) and traces its recurrence across the species boundary, in pest control and animal industry, and the group boundary, in witch\-hunting, colonial animalization, and genocidal propaganda\. The mosquito is read not as a harmless victim but as a limiting case: because killing a disease vector can be defensible, it exposes with unusual clarity how a &quot;killable&quot; category, once established, becomes portable to human targets for whom no such defense exists\. The account is positioned against social dominance theory, dehumanization research, and moral disengagement theory, and its scope is deliberately bounded; it addresses contempt\-based rather than envy\-based prejudice\. The paper concludes that universalist ethics remains incomplete while protection depends on prior boundary work that places some lives outside deliberation, and that reform requires attending to this exempting procedure rather than to an alleged predatory instinct\.
