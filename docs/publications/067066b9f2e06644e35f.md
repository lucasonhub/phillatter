# A Multi\-Stage Physics\-Verified Data Fusion and Bayesian\-Optimized Machine Learning Framework for Multiclass Prediction of Ice Jam Floods and Release Waves: A Case Study of the Chaudière River, Québec

**Autores:** Amir Hossein Salimi; Tadros R\. Ghobrial; Hossein Bonakdari

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate ice\-period river monitoring is essential for characterizing ice jam floods \(IJFs\) and associated jam\-release waves \(JAVEs\)\. However, physics\-based and machine\-learning \(ML\) approaches remain constrained by data scarcity, uncertainty, and incomplete observations\. This study develops a physics\-verified data fusion and preprocessing workflow combined with Bayesian\-optimized ML classification \(PV\-DFBML\) for six IJF/JAVE states at Beauceville on the Chaudière River\. The workflow utilizes a three\-stage data refinement pipeline\. First, categorical fusion of remote sensing \(Sentinel\-1/2, Landsat\-8, and MODIS\) and meteorological data isolates physically relevant ice regimes\. This is followed by spatiotemporal validation and gap\-filling of water level data to preserve longitudinal hydraulic consistency, alongside multi\-step anomaly screening \(DBSCAN and CUSUM\) cross\-checked against upstream–downstream consistency to isolate sensor noise\. Among five Bayesian\-optimized classifiers, B\-RF achieved the strongest performance under the common 5\-fold validation framework, with BACC = 0\.76, MCC = 0\.89, and minority\-class F1 = 0\.83\. Full\-winter input records provided the strongest classification skill, while physics\-verified preprocessing improved minority\-event detection\. Random undersampling modestly increased BACC and minority F1 to 0\.79 and 0\.84, respectively, while reducing MCC to 0\.85, indicating a trade\-off in imbalance treatment\. More stringent leave\-one\-winter\-out validation yielded mean BACC = 0\.68 ± 0\.10, MCC = 0\.61 ± 0\.13, and minority F1 = 0\.65 ± 0\.09, demonstrating greater difficulty in generalizing to unseen winters\. Transfer to Saint\-Joseph\-de\-Beauce without site\-specific retraining further reduced BACC to 0\.67, MCC to 0\.71, and minority\-class F1\-score to 0\.67\. Overall, the results demonstrate the importance of physically consistent data refinement while highlighting limitations associated with rare\-event representation and temporal and spatial generalization\.
