# Designing a pluriversal foresight epistemology and schematic, an essay

**Autores:** Raphaële Bidault\-Waddington

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This article emerges from a reflection on how current global transitions and paradigm shift \(the Anthropocene, Artificial Intelligence revolution and post\-colonial era being some of its markers\) impact conventional western epistemologies; could it be possible, in the more open field of futures research, to explore an innovative pluralistic epistemological position? An academic research project on futurescaping , which includes an experiment on the future of museums, i\.e\. organizations deeply impacted by these transitions, becomes a context to engage an investigation shared in this article—following a presentation at the WFSF 26th Conference 2025, Stellenbosch University, Cape Town\. The method consists in revisiting and continuing the post\-structural and decolonial approach of foresight epistemology developed by Inayatullah \[ 1 \] in his seminal essay Deconstructing Reconstructing the Future published in 1990, which already highlighted the limits of western epistemologies\. Each of the three reviewed epistemology categories \(predictive\-positivist, cultural\-hermeneutic, and critical\-post\-structural\) is confronted with the current critical challenges mentioned before and enriched by new theoretical inputs to bridge and weave western and non\-western knowledge towards the design of what could be a pluriversal epistemology\. Its conceptual architecture takes the shape of a foundational schematic opening space to scape and constructed a plurality of perspectives on five levels of reality: Onto \(ontologies\), Micro \(social uses\), Meso \(territories\), Macro \(planet\), and Cosmo \(worldviews\)\. Behind the deconstruction of western, modernist, and post\-modernist philosophies of knowledge and the reconstruction of a relevant and equitable pluralism, appears a rising and important political epistemology discussion, scholars of all disciplines should pay more attention to\.
