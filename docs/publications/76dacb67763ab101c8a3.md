# Viewing as Possession: Section 15 of POCSO after Just Rights for Children Alliance

**Autores:** Baby Mariyam; Mohd Ali

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Section 15 of the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012 was rewritten in 2019 to create three separate offences relating to the storage and possession of sexual material involving children\. In January 2024 the Madras High Court quashed a prosecution on the reasoning that merely downloading and watching such material in private was not an offence\. In Just Rights for Children Alliance v\. S\. Harish, decided in September 2024, the Supreme Court reversed that decision\. It held that possession includes constructive possession, that watching such material online without downloading it can amount to possession, and that an intention to transmit may be inferred from a failure to delete, destroy or report it\. The Court also asked Parliament to replace the term “child pornography” with “child sexual exploitative and abuse material”\. This paper examines the decision doctrinally\. It is argued that the ruling correctly closes a gap the High Court had opened, but that two consequences need attention\. First, reading intention from omission, together with the statutory presumption of a culpable mental state in Section 30, places a heavy burden on the accused, which is defensible only if the reporting route is clear and accessible\. Second, the decision does not settle how Section 15 applies to synthetic imagery created with artificial intelligence or to adolescents who share images of themselves\.
