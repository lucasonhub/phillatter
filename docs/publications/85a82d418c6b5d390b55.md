# A Confirmatory Investigation into the Cognitive Effects of Background Music: Evidence from Sudoku\-Based Experiments

**Autores:** Yutong Ding

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The growing presence of background music in learning environments highlights the need to examine its influences on cognitive performance under varying task demands\. Based on Zajonc&\#x27;s arousal theory, this study investigates how background music and task difficulty jointly influence cognitive performance and evaluates the relevance of this theory in student learning contexts\. Accordingly, a 2×2 mixed experimental design was conducted with forty high school AP Psychology students, incorporating two environmental conditions, background music and silence, and two task difficulty levels, simple and complex Sudoku tasks\. Participants solved low\-load 4×4 Sudoku puzzles and high\-load 9×9 Sudoku puzzles under both conditions, with completion time and errors recorded as performance measures\. The results indicate that background music enhances simple\-task performance by reducing completion time \(M = 55\.50 vs\. 78\.35\) and errors \(0\.15 vs\. 0\.25\)\. Nevertheless, it impairs complex\-task performance by increasing completion time \(M = 542\.45 vs\. 410\.95\) and errors \(M = 7\.30 vs\. 3\.65\)\. Thus, the effects of background music depend on task demands\. In Zajonc&\#x27;s arousal theory, music\-induced arousal can enhance simple task performance by strengthening dominant responses, while excessive arousal may interfere with the controlled processing required for complex tasks\.
