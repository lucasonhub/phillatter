# Amornbunchornvej, Chainarong: Disagreement without Representational Deficit: Attention Dynamics over a Shared Evaluative Basis

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

When two people disagree for years despite understanding each other, the standard diagnosis is representational: they must lack shared resources for representing one another&\#x27;s considerations\. The same diagnosis is applied within a single person whose evaluations stay organized around one consideration across situations that should pull them apart\. This paper argues that a large class of both cases requires no representational deficit\. Given a shared evaluative basis, disagreement and fixation decompose into three separable components that the representational diagnosis conflates: differences in attention profile \(what is weighted, relatively\), differences in engagement \(how much the domain matters at all\), and bounded relative plasticity \(why neither moves quickly\)\. A minimal formal model makes the decomposition precise\. A persistence bound, derived from a single dynamical axiom, then shows that long\-lived disagreement and fixation are exactly what bounded plasticity predicts under fully shared representation \-\-\- so persistence is not evidence of representational mismatch\. Each of the model&\#x27;s structural assumptions is drawn from the formal literature on dimensional attention in category learning, where it has been independently proposed and, in part, fitted; the transfer from stimulus dimensions to evaluative considerations is defended as an explicit, falsifiable hypothesis\. The account complements a recent cognitive\-geometric theory of interpretation: where that theory locates communication failure in the null space of an interpretation map, this model characterizes the disagreements that survive when nothing is annihilated\.
