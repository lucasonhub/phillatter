# Artificial Intelligence Sovereignty and Technological Dependency

**Autores:** Hamad Ali M\. A\. Al Sowaidi

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This research investigates the deepest and most decisive layer of digital sovereignty: Artificial Intelligence, focusing on foundational models, computational power, and hardware infrastructure\. By analyzing the State of Qatar’s strategic transition from a technological consumer to a producer—manifested in the national AI model “Fanar” and the establishment of the “QAI” company—the study explores the boundaries of strategic independence for small states\. While Qatar has successfully secured a genuine sovereign asset through developing massive, culturally contextualized Arabic datasets that address structural linguistic gaps, critical analysis reveals a profound dependency on foreign foundational models and cloud architectures\. The research further delves into the geopolitics of semiconductors, illustrating how hardware monopolies and export controls act as a rigid ceiling to absolute sovereignty, increasingly weaponized within the US\-China technological rivalry\. Consequently, the study reconceptualizes digital sovereignty for small states not as absolute autarky, but as a dynamic spectrum of “managed dependency\.” To navigate this unequal global technological order, the state of Qatar must employ strategic multilateral partnerships, leverage open\-source architectures, and capitalize on their sovereign wealth to mitigate vulnerabilities\. Ultimately, a small state’s digital sovereignty is measured by its capacity to manage these dependencies, protect its socio\-cultural specificity, and maximize its geopolitical margin of maneuver within entrenched structural constraints\.
