# A Fuzzy Knowledge\-Inference Framework for AI\-Adoption Risk Assessment in Automotive SMEs

**Autores:** S\. Davidson; Loveleen Gaur; Shavi Bansal; Varsha Arya; Ramakrishnan Raman

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Small and medium\-sized enterprises in the automotive supply base face mounting pressure to adopt artificial intelligence, yet they are least able to absorb the consequences of misscoped adoption\. Assessing this risk means reasoning under vagueness: Data maturity, workforce skill, financial capacity, and above all safety and regulatory readiness are graded, interdependent judgments that crisp, compensatory instruments discard\. This paper proposes the fuzzy knowledge inference framework, a two\-stage rough–fuzzy framework\. Eight automotive\-specific risk factors are represented as linguistic variables over an information system; rough\-set analysis establishes their nonredundancy; and a hierarchical fuzzy inference process—anchored, compliance\-weighted, and noncompensatory—yields an interpretable risk index\. Using no primary human data, it is well calibrated at its anchors, monotone in favorability, and rank\-convergent with two independent instruments, while escalating the profiles a compensatory model would understate\. Its contribution to the literature on AI\-adoption risk assessment for automotive SMEs is the noncompensatory treatment of safety and regulatory readiness\.
