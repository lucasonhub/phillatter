# A Comparative Analysis of Tuned and Untuned Machine Learning Models for Cybersecurity Intrusion Detection

**Autores:** Kun Wang; Jinlei Gu

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study benchmarks tuned and untuned machine learning models for intrusion detection across a 63\-class attack taxonomy using the Cybersecurity Attack and Defense Dataset \($n=14\{,\}133$\)\. A leakage\-safe pipeline is designed, fitting all encoders and scalers exclusively on training data\. Nine models are evaluated: five classical methods \(Random Forest, Histogram\-based Gradient Boosting, Extra Trees, SGD\-based SVM, and Logistic Regression\), two boosting frameworks \(XGBoost and LightGBM\), and two hybrids \(soft\-voting ensemble and two\-level stacking with a LightGBM meta\-learner\)\. All models are systematically tuned via stratified randomized search and assessed on twelve metrics\. XGBoost performs best, achieving a weighted $F\_1$ of 0\.8062, accuracy of 0\.8094, MCC of 0\.8030, and Cohen&\#x27;s $\\kappa$ of 0\.8028, outperforming Random Forest \($F\_1=0\.7062$\), LightGBM \($F\_1=0\.7925$\), linear classifiers, and hybrid models\. LightGBM shows the largest tuning gain \($\+0\.755$ $F\_1$\)\. The proposed protocol offers a reproducible, bias\-controlled benchmark for high\-cardinality intrusion detection\.
