# The relationship between gut microbiota‐friendly diet and the prevalence and severity of asthma in children

**Autores:** Li‐Juan Tan; Xifeng Tang; Yijun Shan; Yuqian Ren; Ting Sun; 周一平; Jialei Fu; 张玉彩; Yun Cui

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OBJECTIVES: Dietary factors may impact asthma indirectly by modulating the composition and activity of the gut microbiota\. This study aimed to explore the potential relationship between gut microbiota\-friendly diet and both the prevalence and severity of asthma in children\. METHODS: We selected a total of 2256 children from the 2021\-2023 National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey\. The gut microbiota\-friendly diet was assessed using the Diet\-Gut Microbiome Index \(DI\-GM\), which incorporates 14 specific food constituents\. To calculate the DI\-GM, two non\-consecutive 24\-h dietary recalls data were used, from which the relevant food constituents were derived\. A higher DI\-GM score indicates greater alignment with a gut microbiota\-friendly diet\. RESULTS: Among the 2256 U\.S\. children, the weighted prevalence of asthma is 13\.36%\. In fully adjusted models, DI\-GM was not significantly associated with asthma prevalence overall or within any subgroups\. However, higher DI\-GM \(T2\) was significantly associated with a lower risk of the mild asthma phenotype in the 5\-16 age group\. In contrast, no significant associations were observed between DI\-GM and asthma severity in the other age groups\. Subgroup analysis showed a higher DI\-GM score was significantly correlated with a lower risk for asthma in subgroups stratified by obesity status\. CONCLUSIONS: Higher DI\-GM scores were significantly inversely associated with asthma severity risk in children aged 5\-16 years and in obese individuals\. These cross\-sectional findings support the need for longitudinal studies to determine whether dietary modulation may affect asthma severity via microbiota\-dependent mechanisms\.
