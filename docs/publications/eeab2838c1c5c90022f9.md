# 11 million days of longitudinal Fitbit data reveal novel future health insights

**Autores:** Evelynne S\. Fulda; Bennett J\. Waxse; Slavina B\. Goleva; Tam C\. Tran; Henry J\. Taylor; Caitlin P\. Bailey; Dana L\. Wolff‐Hughes; Huan Mo; Chenjie Zeng; Jacob M\. Keaton; Tracey M\. Ferrara; Anya Topiwala; Aiden Doherty; Joshua C\. Denny

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Wearable devices offer a scalable, objective measurement of physical activity, but most studies reduce these data to a single metric over 7\-days\. Here, we compare wearable\-derived phenotyping approaches to understand their impact on activity\-disease associations, analyzing 11 million days of Fitbit data from 29,303 All of Us Research Program participants\. We derive four daily activity metrics \(step count, peak 1\-min cadence, peak 30\-min cadence, and heart rate per step\) across five time\-windows \(1\-day, 1\-week, 1\-month, 6\-months, 1\-year\), then run phenome\-wide analyses across &gt; 700 incident and &gt;1,400 prevalent disease outcomes\. Though all phenotypes are highly correlated, longer measurement windows yield stronger, more stable associations: 1\-year step count was associated with 389 prevalent and 61 incident outcomes \(versus 271 and 33 for 1\-day step count\) after Bonferroni\-correction, including novel links to chronic pain syndrome and SARS\-CoV\-2\. Differences between prevalent and incident associations suggest that activity metrics may act as both early markers of disease and risk factors\. These findings highlight how large\-scale, longitudinal wearable data can advance understanding of health and disease\.
