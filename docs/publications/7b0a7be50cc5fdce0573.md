# Role of Institutional Policy Literacy in Strengthening Psychological Safety, Reducing Fear of Retaliation, and Promoting Bystander Intervention, Formal Reporting and Academic Progress

**Autores:** Ureeda Akhtar; Eraj Sohail; Baroosh Ahsan; Iffat Fatimah; Shanzay Harris; Aiman Fatima; Fatima Mudassir; Anaab Tariq

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Online harassment in Pakistani universities extends beyond social media into academic group chats\. Yet few researchers examine how policy knowledge shapes student responses\. This quantitative study investigated how understanding campus rules impacts psychological safety, retaliation fear, bystander action and formal reporting among university students\. Most students faced high exposure to digital misconduct inside class networks\. However, undergraduates knew very little about institutional policies\. This knowledge gap stayed wide through the first three years\. Policy awareness improved only during the final year of study\. Fear of peer backlash was remarkably high across campus\. Male and female students shared the exact same concerns\. When fear of retaliation increased, psychological safety dropped drastically\. Hostile digital environments clearly silence student voices and disrupt learning\. Crucially, knowing campus guidelines directly boosted willingness to intervene or report misconduct\. These results prove that static guidelines alone cannot ensure safety\. Higher education leaders must move beyond hosting rules on university websites\. Administration must provide interactive policy training during early student orientation\. Finally, safe, anonymous reporting systems remain essential to protect reporters from peer backlash\.
