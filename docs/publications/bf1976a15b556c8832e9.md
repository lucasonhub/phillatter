# Halve\-Then\-Greedy: Adaptive Sampling for Posterior Success with Near\-Optimal Instance Dependence

**Autores:** Yinan Li; Kwang\-Sung Jun; Kapilan Balagopalan; Tuan Nguyen; Yao Zhao; Abbas Zaidi; Houssam Nassif

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

We study fixed\-budget adaptive experimentation aimed at maximizing the probability of obtaining sufficient posterior evidence that a recommended design&\#x27;s mean performance exceeds a target\. Unlike best\-arm identification, this objective does not require finding the best design\. For Gaussian bandits with a Gaussian prior, we propose \\emph\{Halve\-Then\-Greedy\} \(HTG\), which combines a large initial balanced allocation, successive halving, and posterior\-greedy sampling\. We prove instance\-dependent, finite\-budget exponential bounds on posterior\-failure probability that capture both separation from unfavorable arms and the presence of multiple favorable arms\. On one\-positive\-arm families, we establish an asymptotic sample\-complexity lower bound that HTG matches up to a logarithmic factor\. We further analyze uniform sampling and sequential halving, revealing regimes where they require more samples than HTG by linear and logarithmic factors, respectively\. Simulations illustrate HTG&\#x27;s sample\-efficiency advantages and their dependence on instance structure\.
