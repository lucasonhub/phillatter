## Descrição do feed

This paper develops a structural constructivist model of electoral behavior by applying the Core Emotion Framework \(CEF\) to political campaigns\. Rather than treating emotions as fixed categories, the CEF defines them as “dynamic configurations of ten functional operators that process, modulate, and transform internal interoceptive signals and external environmental data” \(quoted from the document\)\. Using the tripartite architecture of the Head, Heart, and Gut centers, the analysis models how campaigns transform raw public dissatisfaction—what Aristotle described as a collective “sense of injustice” regarding the distribution of power and honor—into coordinated political action\. The Head Center governs cognitive appraisal through sequential activation of Sensing, Calculating, and Deciding, enabling campaigns to detect voter discontent, analyze demographic pathways, and commit to platform direction\. The Heart Center structures relational dynamics through Constricting, Expanding, and Achieving, regulating boundary defense, coalition building, and competitive performance\. The Gut Center operationalizes strategy via Arranging, Boosting, Appreciating, and Accepting, coordinating logistics, sustaining mobilization energy, preventing burnout, and recalibrating after setbacks\. The paper further maps electoral microstates—such as Political Anxiety, Campaign Momentum, and Electoral Readiness—to formal operator sequences, and identifies pathological operator fusions \(e\.g\., Calculating–Constricting, Achieving–Boosting\) that produce strategic paralysis or burnout\. Finally, it outlines detangling protocols that use somatic inferential adjustments to restore systemic clarity and action\. Together, these findings position the CEF as a unified psychodynamic architecture for modeling political strategy, organizational resilience, and the emotional mechanics of electoral behavior\.
