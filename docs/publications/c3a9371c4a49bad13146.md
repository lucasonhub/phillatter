# Landscape buffers as microscale streetscape design factors for enhancing perceived walkability

**Autores:** Coryn Brinks; Xiaowei Li; Jun–Hyun Kim; Zeenat Kotval\-K

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Walking, as an active mode of transportation, has been highlighted for its benefits to physical and mental health, environmental sustainability, and social connectivity\. However, walkability studies have often emphasized neighborhood\-scale walkability indicators over microscale streetscape design\. This study examined associations between landscape buffer typologies, surrounding context and perceived walkability through a two\-phase visual evaluation\. Phase I presented 28 simulated streetscape images comprising seven landscape buffer scenarios across two urban and two suburban settings\. The dataset contained 95 survey records; the main model analyzed 8,768 ratings from 74 respondents\. The seven scenarios, namely concrete, lawn, shrub, biodiverse planting, lawn with trees, shrub, shrub with street trees, and biodiverse planting with street trees, were displayed in a fixed, non\-randomized order\. Participants rated five dimensions of perceived walkability: overall satisfaction, safety from crime, aesthetic pleasure, safety from cars, and comfort\. Phase I showed significant main effects of landscape buffer scenario, setting, and perception dimension on perceived walkability ratings, as well as significant interaction effects among these factors \(all p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Vegetated scenarios with street trees generally received the highest ratings\. Urban scenes were rated more positively, whereas suburban scenes showed larger differences between concrete and planted\-buffer conditions\. Phase II provided an exploratory comparison between seven returning participants’ two\-dimensional \(2D\) ratings and their subsequent immersive ratings\. The overall media test was not significant; preliminary context\- and dimension\-specific differences require caution, given the small sample and fixed media order\. Landscape buffers incorporating street trees were associated with more favorable perceived walkability ratings, with larger differences relative to the concrete condition in suburban scenes\. These patterns support further evaluation of planted buffers as context\-sensitive streetscape interventions, although fixed presentation order limits causal interpretation\.
