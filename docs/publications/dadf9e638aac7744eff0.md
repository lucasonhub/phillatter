# Borda, Ann ; Winkel, Ken &amp; Coghlan, Simon: A Plurality of Intelligence

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Against the backdrop of the 75th anniversary of the original Turing Test devised by mathematician and computer scientist Alan Turing in 1950, this reflection considers a reconceptualization of that famous “imitation game”\. Researchers have long noted limitations of the Test, which focuses on a computing machine’s ability to imitate our intelligence and to effectively fool people that the machine is a human\. Crucially, the design of the Test to measure intelligence against the standard “human” kind remains fraught and risks excluding diverse intelligences, not only the of human variety, but also the many nonhuman intelligences found in Nature\. In the accelerated use of Artificial Intelligence \(AI\) to imitate standard human intelligence and human\-centric values, we are at risk of losing a deeper understanding of nonhuman intelligences existing in Nature\. In a not\-so\-distant future, Nature might, for example, become largely defined by an AI with anthropocentric ideals\. We thus propose the idea of a Turing Test for Nature that can provide an ethical basis and metric to critically determine whether AI systems accurately represent nonhuman intelligences and values found in Nature\. Such a Test would also highlight not only more\-than\-intelligences, but the positioning of ourselves in the complex co\-existence with Nature on which human flourishing depends\.
