# Change\-point analysis of Altman Z″\-score dynamics: structural regime detection in a capital\-intensive emerging market firm

**Autores:** Wilfredo Bazán\-Ramírez; Javier Antonio Prudencio\-Vidal; Amador Grover Mejia Osorio; Edgar Gutiérrez\-Gómez; Ybar Gustavo Palomino\-Malpartida; Aldo Bazán\-Ramírez; Miguel Ángel Bello\-Bernal

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study proposes an integrated quantitative framework combining the Altman Z″\-score with Binary Segmentation change\-point detection—using a least squares cost function—to examine corporate financial distress as a dynamic regime\-shifting process amenable to structural analysis, addressing the gap in longitudinal distress monitoring for capital\-intensive firms in emerging markets\. The framework formally minimizes a piecewise least squares cost function over a financial time series of 56 consecutive quarterly observations \(2011Q1–2024Q4\), identifying endogenous breakpoints that delimit regimes internally homogeneous in mean level of financial deterioration\. Because these breakpoints are selected endogenously, standard Chow test critical values are complemented by a supremum\-Wald test accounting for the search over candidate break locations, which confirms the presence of at least one genuine structural break \(sup\-Wald = 38\.61, exceeding the 1% asymptotic critical value\)\. The framework is applied to Telefónica del Perú S\. A\. A\.—a publicly listed capital\-intensive telecommunications operator that filed a request to enter insolvency restructuring proceedings in February 2025—providing an empirical case with a formally observable ex post validation event\. Five endogenously detected breakpoints—located at 2013Q2, 2014Q3, 2018Q2, 2020Q4, and 2022Q1—delimit six structurally differentiated regimes, with mean Z″\-score values evolving from 1\.58 \(phase 1: moderate vulnerability\) to a transitory peak of 2\.42 \(phase 2: transitory stability peak\), followed by a progressive decline to 0\.60 \(phase 3: transition to distress\), −0\.29 \(phase 4: structural distress onset\), −1\.40 \(phase 5: accelerated deterioration\), to −2\.52 \(phase 6: severe terminal distress\)\. Under the primary market\-value specification, persistent financial distress signals were observable from 2015Q2—a horizon that emerges naturally from the longitudinal depth of the analysis and exceeds the anticipatory windows typically documented in prior retrospective studies on corporate financial distress\. These findings demonstrate that integrating change\-point detection algorithms with accounting\-based financial indicators substantially enriches the dynamic interpretation of insolvency risk trajectories beyond static classification approaches\. A sensitivity analysis comparing market\-value and book\-value specifications of the model confirms that the identified structural breakpoints are robust to this methodological choice, although the resulting Z″\-score levels and distress\-zone classifications are specific to the market\-value specification adopted as primary in this study, addressing a gap not systematically tested in prior applied literature\. The proposed framework offers a replicable mathematical approach for longitudinal monitoring applicable to comparable capital\-intensive firms in emerging markets\.
