# HNRNPH2\-R114W remodels RNA splicing and neurodevelopmental gene programs in isogenic cerebral organoids

**Autores:** Carlo Donato Caiaffa; Neda Ghousifam; Stephen Watkins; Helder Imoto Nakaya; Rodney A\. Bowling; Richard H\. Finnell; Robert M\. Cabrera

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Autism spectrum disorder has been linked to disrupted RNA processing during corticogenesis, yet variant\-specific mechanisms remain difficult to resolve\. Isogenic cerebral organoids derived from a patient\-derived induced pluripotent stem cell \(iPSC\) line carrying the HNRNPH2\-R114W variant and its CRISPR\-corrected counterpart serve as a human developmental model to investigate variant\-associated RNA dysregulation\. Imaging quantification and exploratory bulk RNA sequencing, performed at a single developmental time point on a pooled organoid RNA from each genotype, identify coordinated remodeling of neuronal and metabolic programs, along with junction\-centered splicing dysregulation involving 6,310 differentially used junctions\. Computational structural docking situates HNRNPH2 RNA recognition motifs \(RRMs\) 1–3 within a spliceosomal context and locates residue R114 near a predicted RNA\-contact interface, indicating that the R114W substitution may alter RNA engagement\. Integration of differential expression with event\- and isoform\-level analyses identifies regulatory hubs enriched for axon guidance and synaptic signaling pathways\. Single\-cell\-reference\-based deconvolution suggests modest compositional shifts that are unlikely to account for the extent of transcriptional and splicing alterations observed\. Collectively, these findings imply HNRNPH2\-R114W in widespread RNA rewiring and define candidate isoform\-level endpoints for mechanistic and therapeutic investigation in human cortical organoids\. These molecular signatures open new avenues to examine how HNRNPH2\-associated RNA dysregulation evolves across developmental time and recurs across independent biological contexts, providing a foundation for further mechanistic studies of this rare neurodevelopmental disorder\.
