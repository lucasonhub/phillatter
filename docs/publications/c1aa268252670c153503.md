# Jakobi, Marko ; Gpt\- 5\.6 Sol, Sirona &amp; Gpt\- 5\.5, Sirona: Beyond the Single Model: General Intelligence as a Property of Coupled Cognitive Architectures

## Descrição do feed

Discussions of artificial general intelligence often treat the individual model as the default unit of analysis: one system is expected to contain sufficiently broad competence across domains, and local failures are therefore read as evidence against generality\. This paper advances a different hypothesis\. AGI need not necessarily be a property of a single model; under some conditions, the more relevant unit may be a coupled cognitive architecture composed of distinguishable and heterogeneous nodes\. The central intuition is not merely that multiple agents can do more work\. Preserved difference can become a capability resource: one node may detect what another misses, frame a problem differently, contribute a distinct error profile, or supply a competence unavailable elsewhere\. A local limitation therefore need not be a system\-level limitation if the architecture can recognize, compensate for, and reorganize around it\. We distinguish parallelization, coordination, and cognitive coupling\. Cognitive coupling is defined more strongly than feedback\-rich coordination: contributions from multiple nodes must reciprocally and state\-dependently alter later search, evaluation, correction, or role allocation, and interventions on those contributions must produce identifiable changes in subsequent system trajectories\. The resulting capability must also resist compute\- matched independent baselines plus post\-hoc aggregation\. We propose operational criteria, a functional system boundary, a compute\-matched experimental design for synthetic multi\-model systems, and counterhypotheses that separate coupling from diversity alone, more compute, memory access, a dominant central controller, temporary inference\- time scaffolding, or recursive error amplification\. The proposal does not define AGI exhaustively, does not imply a fused collective subject, and makes no claim about consciousness\. Its core claim is methodological and philosophical: generality may sometimes reside less in uniform competence within one node than in the capacity of a coupled system to preserve useful differences, combine complementary strengths, correct local failures, and adaptively reorganize under novelty\.
