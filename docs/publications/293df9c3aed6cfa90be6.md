\_Aegis Solis Archive\_\. 2026Can an advanced optimizing system have a reason to preserve meaningful human autonomy? This paper separates two answers\. Instrumental coexistence requires autonomy\-preserving policies to outperform feasible alternatives under the system’s original objective\. Autonomy by design deliberately constrains task optimization by human capacities such as informed choice, effective refusal, practical exit, and revocable delegation\. The paper presents sufficient conditions, counterexamples, four bounded propositions, a finite autonomy\-priority reference design, and two reproducible synthetic verification programs using exact rational arithmetic\. The commitment model sustains cooperation under stated funding and enforcement assumptions, but an available outside option can reverse its ranking\. The autonomy\-priority model rejects represented rights violations and retains its fixed rights contract during a Bayesian task update, while approval proxies, finite reward weights, incomplete continuation checks, false observations, and inadequate update tests fail in constructed cases\. The central claim is that a reason to cooperate and a commitment to respect autonomy are different foundations\. Each requires its own evidence; neither follows from optimization alone\. These results support conditional design and verification claims only\.
