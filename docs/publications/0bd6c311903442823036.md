# Topology\-mediated deformation and failure mechanisms in heterogeneous cellular SiCp/Al composites revealed by mesoscale finite element analysis

**Autores:** Haipeng Chen; Zhong Zheng; Bo Yuan; Sainan Ma; Likun Wang; Bowen Gong; Wenting Ouyang; Dongming Chen; Cheng Liu; Xiang Gao

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction The spatial heterogeneity of reinforcement distribution strongly influences the mechanical response of particle\-reinforced metal matrix composites \(PRMMCs\)\. However, the respective roles of local reinforcement concentration and network connectivity in deformation and failure remain unclear\. Methods In this study, two\-dimensional mesoscale finite element models of SiCp/6061Al composites with identical global SiC volume fraction but distinct cellular architectures were developed\. By varying local SiC volume fraction and reinforcement\-rich region connectivity, the effects of heterogeneous particle distributions on stress transfer, strain localization, crack initiation and propagation were investigated\. Results The results demonstrate that moderate reinforcement localization enhances tensile strength by improving stress transfer efficiency, whereas excessive localization accompanied by network discontinuity deteriorates macroscopic load\-bearing capability\. Furthermore, local SiC concentration primarily determines damage initiation through intensified deformation incompatibility, while reinforcement network connectivity governs subsequent crack propagation behavior\. A continuous particle\-rich framework promotes efficient load transfer but accelerates crack transmission after damage initiation, whereas a disconnected network enhances crack deflection but reduces strengthening efficiency\. Discussion These findings establish a topology\-based design principle for heterogeneous SiCp/Al composites, emphasizing that reinforcement connectivity is essential for achieving a favorable balance between strength and damage tolerance\.
