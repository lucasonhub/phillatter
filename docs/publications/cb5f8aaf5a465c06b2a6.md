# The Shift from Google Search to ChatGPT: How Generative AI Is Changing Students’ Information\-Seeking Behaviour

**Autores:** Prof\. Vishwas Kenchi; Prof\. Vishwas Kenchi; Rutuja More; Sarika Kore; Jay Shinde

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid spread of generative artificial intelligence \(AI\) tools such as ChatGPT is changing how students search for, understand, and verify academic information, a role long held by search engines such as Google\. This study examines students’ self\-reported information\-seeking behaviour with Google Search and ChatGPT, covering frequency of use, purpose, first\-source preference, reasons for adoption, trust, verification practice, and perceived change\. A descriptive, quantitative survey was conducted using a 25\-item structured Google Forms questionnaire administered to college and university students through convenience sampling \(N = 200\)\. Data were analysed using frequency and percentage analysis\. Of the respondents, 73\.5% use ChatGPT/AI at least once a day, and 61\.2% name ChatGPT/AI as their first source for academic answers, compared with 30\.6% for Google Search\. Understanding concepts, faster answers, and easier explanations were the main reasons for use\. Trust was conditional: 59\.2% verify ChatGPT answers always or often, 59\.2% have encountered incorrect or misleading output at least sometimes, and Google Search was the most common verification source \(42\.9%\)\. In total, 93\.9% reported at least some change in how they find information\. The findings point to a hybrid, AI\-assisted pattern rather than a replacement of search engines; however, they rest on self\-reported data from a non\-probability sample and cannot be generalised\.
