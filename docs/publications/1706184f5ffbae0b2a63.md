# Multiphysics of serpentinite dehydration

**Autores:** Austin Tanner Arias

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Water plays a fundamental role in Earth&\#x27;s geodynamic processes\. Subduction zones cycle water between the surface and interior through the burial of hydrated lithosphere and its subsequent metamorphic dehydration\. Serpentinites are particularly important because they store substantial quantities of structurally bound water and release fluids during progressive dehydration\. These fluids influence subduction dynamics, arc magmatism, element cycling, and seismicity\. However, the mechanisms by which fluids escape from rocks that are typically impermeable at subduction\-zone depths remain poorly understood\. This thesis investigates the coupled evolution of permeability and mechanical properties during serpentinite dehydration, focusing on antigorite and brucite breakdown and the formation of olivine\-bearing dehydration veins\. First, X\-ray microtomography and finite element simulations of Stokes flow were used to quantify the permeability of porous olivine aggregates \(POAs\) formed within dehydration veins\. Representative volume element analyses show that permeability converges at scales several times larger than the mean grain size, reaching approximately 10^\-14–10^\-13 m^2 at porosities near 30%\. This represents an increase of up to nine orders of magnitude relative to intact serpentinite\. Permeability is nearly isotropic and follows grain\-size\-dependent Kozeny–Carman scaling, demonstrating that dehydration simultaneously generates fluids and highly permeable drainage pathways\. Second, finite element homogenization was used to investigate the effective poroelastic and mechanical properties of these aggregates\. Despite their high porosity, POAs retain a mechanically connected, load\-bearing olivine framework\. Numerical compaction models indicate that these veins compact more slowly than surrounding serpentinite, potentially preserving permeability and fluid conduits over extended timescales\. These findings demonstrate that microstructural heterogeneity governs both fluid transport and mechanical behavior\. Third, natural dehydration vein networks in the Erro\-Tobbio meta\-serpentinites of the Voltri Massif, Italy, were investigated using X\-ray tomography, drone\-based imaging, electron microscopy, thermodynamic modeling, and generative machine learning\. The reconstructed networks exhibit highly connected drainage structures capable of sustaining efficient flow at bulk porosities below 1%, with permeabilities reaching approximately 10^\-15 m^2\. Mineralogical and chemical evidence indicates that fluid transport and reaction progress were localized along pre\-existing brucite\- and magnetite\-rich domains, highlighting the role of geochemical heterogeneity in organizing fluid pathways\. Finally, microstructural and crystallographic analyses of folded olivine veins reveal the coexistence of brittle fracturing, crystal\-plastic deformation, and pressure\-solution creep during low\-temperature deformation\. These mechanically strong veins concentrate stress and undergo hardening, brittle failure, and viscous relaxation\. Their coupled brittle\-viscous behavior may contribute to the rheological conditions associated with slow slip and tremor along subduction interfaces\. Overall, this thesis demonstrates that serpentinite dehydration is a self\-organizing process that generates interconnected, permeable pathways while producing mechanically robust structures\. Across scales from micrometers to outcrops, dehydration\-driven microstructural evolution influences fluid migration, compaction, deformation, and potentially seismic behavior, providing new insights into the coupled transport of water and stress within Earth&\#x27;s interior\.
