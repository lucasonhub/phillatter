# A cross\-temporal individual\-level framework for identifying, associating, and counting multiple mounting events for dairy cow in estrus monitoring

**Autores:** Seri Azi; Haowen Pan; Xiangfeng Kong; Yuhan Wu; Chunhui Geng; Zhiwen Song; Huaibo Song; Zuojing Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In vision\-based estrus monitoring, mounting behavior serves as a key indicator\. However, reliance on a single mounting event may cause false positives because non\-estrous cows may also exhibit mounting behavior\. To address this issue, a cross\-temporal individual\-level framework was proposed to identify, associate, and count multiple mounting events of the same cow over time\. The framework combines an improved You Only Look Once Version 11\-nano \(YOLO11n\) model with an improved baseline variant of the HiT lightweight hierarchical vision transformer tracking model \(HiT\-Base\)\. The artificial intelligence contribution comprises improved detection and tracking\. For detection, Normalized Wasserstein Distance loss was integrated with Complete Intersection over Union loss to improve small\-target detection, and a lightweight Golden Cudgel Block \(GCBlock\) was designed to enhance feature representation under complex farm conditions\. For tracking, a plug\-and\-play detail\-enhanced convolution \(DEConv\) module was designed to strengthen boundary and fine\-grained features, improving target discrimination and tracking stability\. The engineering application contribution lies in reducing reliance on isolated observations and providing more reliable behavioral evidence through cross\-temporal association and counting of multiple mounting events\. Experimental results showed that the proposed detection model achieved 96\.42% average precision and 94\.74% balanced F\-measure \(F1\-score\) for cow in estrus, with an inference speed of 265\.96 frames per second\. The proposed tracking model achieved an area under the curve of 70\.76%, a normalized precision of 88\.73%, and an average overlap of 70\.74%\. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework for real\-time estrus monitoring and its potential for precision dairy farming\.
