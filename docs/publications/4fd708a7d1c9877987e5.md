# Cost\-driven design and optimization of steel support structures for offshore energy platforms: a review of CAPEX cost models

**Autores:** Alireza Asgari Motlagh; Timothy J\. McCarthy; Aziz Ahmed

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Steel support structures for offshore energy platforms require large volumes of steel and complex manufacturing, making them one of the most expensive elements of any offshore project\. Capital expenditure \(CAPEX\), which includes material procurement, manufacturing, transportation, and installation, typically makes up around 70% of the total lifecycle cost of these structures\. Although many studies have proposed cost models to support design and optimization, the approaches used vary considerably in terms of scope, methodology, and level of detail\. This paper reviews existing CAPEX cost models for fixed and floating steel offshore structures, covering jackets, monopiles, spar buoys, semi\-submersibles, and tension leg platforms, based on peer\-reviewed work published mainly between 2000 and 2026\. Moreover, this study offers a new quantitative framework that combines cost model completeness, modelling detail, and structural complexity to compare studies\. The findings show that material costs appear in 98% of studies, with 66\.7% relying on material cost alone\. Manufacturing costs have been included in 29% of studies, even though they can account for more than 30% of total production costs in welded steel structures\. Transportation and installation costs have received less attention, appearing in 16% and 20% of studies, respectively\. Only 14\.1% of the reviewed studies include all four CAPEX components together\. Finally, the key gaps identified across the reviewed literature are discussed, along with recommendations for future work\.
