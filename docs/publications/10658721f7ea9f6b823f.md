# Analysis of influencing factors of atmospheric pollution on pulmonary tuberculosis incidence in China from 2014 to 2020

**Autores:** Haibo Shi; Xinyu Guo; Yangzong Ciren

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective To investigate the spatiotemporal heterogeneity in the associations between ambient air pollutants \(PM 2\.5, SO 2 , NO 2 , CO, O 3 \) and pulmonary tuberculosis \(TB\) incidence in China from 2014 to 2020, and to provide evidence for region\-specific prevention and control strategies\. Methods Based on panel data from 31 provincial\-level regions, spatial autocorrelation analysis, spatiotemporal scanning, and a Geographically and Temporally Weighted Regression \(GTWR\) model were employed\. An exposure\-window robustness analysis using 1\- to 5\-year moving averages was further conducted\. Results The overall TB incidence declined, yet high\-burden clustering remained significant in Xinjiang, Xizang, and Qinghai \(RR = 3\.56\)\. The GTWR model showed good fit \( R 2 = 0\.889\)\. PM 2\.5 coefficients exhibited an “east\-negative, west\-positive” gradient; SO 2 , NO 2 , and CO showed synergistic positive associations in Northeast China; O 3 was negatively associated in most provinces and attenuated as the exposure window lengthened, suggesting predominantly short\-term associations; and GDP coefficients reversed from positive in the east to negative in the west\. The moving\-average analysis confirmed that these spatial patterns were temporally robust\. Conclusion The associations between air pollution and pulmonary TB exhibit marked spatiotemporal non\-stationarity, supporting region\-specific prevention and control strategies prioritizing high\-burden western regions\. As an ecological study, this investigation identifies associations rather than causal relationships\.
