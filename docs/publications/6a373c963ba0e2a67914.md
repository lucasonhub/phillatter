# Youth and emotional vulnerability predict chatbot appeal

**Autores:** Dara Greenwood; Jingyi Shang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The present survey study \(Prolific sample, N = 361\) aimed to clarify relationships among age and socioemotional well\-being as they pertain to chatbot use and perception by sampling across two age cohorts: emerging adults \(18–24 years\) and middle\-aged adults \(48–54 years\)\. We assessed loneliness and attachment anxiety in concert with AI chatbot use frequency, perceptions of chatbots as comforting, and as easier to interact with than humans\. Results show that the younger cohort reported significantly more frequent chatbot use and increased perceived comfort from that use, compared to the older cohort\. The younger cohort also reported significantly greater loneliness and attachment anxiety\. Results of regression analyses show that attachment anxiety and loneliness each positively predicted perceptions of chatbot ease\. The latter association was qualified by a marginal interaction with age: loneliness significantly predicted increased chatbot ease for the younger cohort only\. Findings underscore the need to continue probing the appeal of chatbots for vulnerable populations and the potential impact those interactions may have on well\-being\.
