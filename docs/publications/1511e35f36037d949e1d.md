# Reduced contrast volume in coronary CT angiography: effects on coronary attenuation

**Autores:** Perihan Adel Ali El Naqeeb; Medhat Wahba Shafaa; Ayman Mohamed Ibrahim; Ahmed Hamdy; Ahmed Shehata Ismail

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Background Coronary CT angiography \(CCTA\) requires intravenous iodinated contrast to achieve diagnostic intraluminal opacification\. Because reducing the contrast volume administered per examination is an exposure\-reduction strategy of practical interest, institutions have explored lower\-volume protocols; whether attenuation can be preserved above the diagnostic threshold under real\-world, non\-randomized implementation is not well documented outside research settings\. Methods This retrospective, single\-centre, non\-concurrent before\-and\-after protocol\-implementation study was conducted at Radiology Center \(January 2023–December 2024\)\. From 200 CCTA examinations initially identified from radiology reports as potentially eligible, 106 were excluded \(44 body weight &gt;120 kg; 12 beta\-blocker administered; 27 coronary calcification/calcium scoring without significant stenosis; 23 motion artefact\), leaving 94 CT\-angiographically normal examinations: 51 under the standard \(pre\-reduction\) protocol and 43 under the reduced\-volume \(post\-reduction\) protocol\. All examinations used a third\-generation dual\-source CT scanner \(Siemens SOMATOM Force\) with automated tube\-voltage selection \(CARE kV\) and tube\-current modulation \(CARE Dose4D\), retrospective ECG gating, and a 32\-cm scan length\. Omnipaque 350 mgI/mL was administered by power injector with bolus tracking \(ascending aorta ROI, 90 HU trigger, 5 s post\-trigger delay\)\. Intraluminal attenuation was measured by a single reader in the left main trunk \(LMT\) and proximal right coronary artery \(RCA\); no inter\- or intra\-observer reproducibility assessment was performed\. Distributions were assessed with the Shapiro–Wilk test; normally distributed variables were compared with the independent\-samples \(Welch\) t\-test and non\-normally distributed variables with the Mann–Whitney U test\. A multivariable linear model examined coronary attenuation as a function of protocol epoch, kVp, body weight, heart rate, and iodine delivery rate\. Results Contrast volume fell from 63\.4 ± 7\.3 mL to 49\.0 ± 8\.0 mL \(median 60 \[IQR 60–65\] vs 45 \[45–52\.5\] mL\), a 22\.8% reduction \(Mann–Whitney p &lt; 0\.001\), with a corresponding 22\.8% reduction in iodine mass \(22\.20 ± 2\.56 vs 17\.13 ± 2\.79 gI\)\. Iodine delivery rate was essentially unchanged \(1\.69 ± 0\.14 vs 1\.69 ± 0\.14 gI/s; p = 0\.91\)\. Mean LMT attenuation decreased from 520\.4 ± 93\.6 HU to 451\.9 ± 90\.7 HU \(− 13\.2%; t\-test p &lt; 0\.001\) and mean RCA attenuation from 500\.2 ± 107\.1 HU to 418\.1 ± 89\.3 HU \(− 16\.4%; Mann–Whitney p &lt; 0\.001\)\. At the per\-patient level, 41/43 post\-reduction examinations \(95\.3%; exact 95% CI 84\.2–99\.4%\) reached ≥ 300 HU at the LMT and 39/43 \(90\.7%; 95% CI 77\.9–97\.4%\) at the RCA; 4/43 examinations, therefore, fell below the SCCT diagnostic threshold at the RCA and 2/43 at the LMT\. In the multivariable model, protocol epoch remained independently associated with lower attenuation after adjustment for kVp, weight, heart rate, and iodine delivery rate \(LMT: β = − 75\.7 HU, 95% CI − 111\.6 to − 39\.8, p &lt; 0\.001; RCA: β = − 87\.8 HU, 95% CI − 128\.0 to − 47\.7, p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Mean DLP was 1158\.2 ± 637\.4 mGy·cm \(pre\) versus 1072\.1 ± 530\.6 mGy·cm \(post\), a non\-significant 7\.4% difference \(Mann–Whitney p = 0\.71\); the reduction in DLP standard deviation \(16\.8%\) corresponds to an estimated 30\.7% reduction in variance, which was not statistically significant \(Levene’s test p = 0\.24\)\. No renal function or image\-quality metric was measured\. Conclusion A reduced\-volume contrast protocol lowered administered contrast volume and total iodine mass by 22\.8% while iodine delivery rate was maintained\. Mean coronary attenuation remained above 300 HU in both groups, but a per\-patient minority of post\-reduction examinations fell below this threshold, particularly at the RCA\. Radiation\-dose differences were a co\-temporal secondary observation and are not attributable to the contrast protocol\. Renal outcomes were not assessed, and the findings should not be interpreted as evidence of nephroprotection or as a formal non\-inferiority result\.
