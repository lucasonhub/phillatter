# Type I Error in Single\-Case Experimental Designs

**Autores:** Katie Wolfe; John M\. Ferron

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Type I errors, or false positives, occur when a researcher erroneously concludes that the independent variable caused a change in the dependent variable\. Type I errors can result in resources being misused in both research and practice\. Although Type I errors are most often associated with group design paradigms, researchers using single\-case experimental designs are also susceptible to making Type I errors\. In this article, we describe the research on Type I errors in single\-case experimental designs across analytic methods, including visual analysis, machine learning, effect estimation, and randomization approaches\. We also suggest ways that each analytic method can be used to mitigate Type I errors and highlight aspects of Type I error in single\-case experimental designs that are understudied and in need of additional research\.
