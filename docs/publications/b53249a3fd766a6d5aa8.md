# Immunohistochemical and morphometric assessment of carotid body remodeling under conditions of chronic hypoxia and hypertension

**Autores:** Elena Sergeevna Ganina; Julia Grigoryeva; V\.N\. Sevryukova; Galina Nikolaevna Suvorova; Alexander V\. Bormotov; Ваньков Владимир Александрович

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The comorbidity of arterial hypertension and hemodynamically significant carotid artery stenosis presents a serious clinical challenge\. The carotid body is a peripheral chemoreceptor that plays an important role in blood pressure regulation, but its structural changes in this comorbid condition remain poorly understood\. The aim of this study was to evaluate changes in the histoarchitecture of the carotid body in patients with the combined pathology of arterial hypertension and hemodynamically significant common carotid artery stenosis using the morphometric and immunohistochemical markers PGR9\.5 and S\-100\. Research methodology: Ten carotid body preparations from healthy controls and ten preparations from patients with verified hypertension and hemodynamically significant common carotid artery stenosis were studied\. Immunohistochemical staining with PGR9\.5 and S\-100 markers was used, followed by morphometric analysis in ImageJ\. Statistical analysis included descriptive statistics, Shapiro\-Wilk, Mann\-Whitney, and Student&\#x27;s t\-tests, as well as Spearman&\#x27;s correlation analysis\. The results of the research: In the group with hypertension and hemodynamically significant stenosis, a significant reduction in the number of supporting cells was observed\. Against this background, compensatory hypertrophy of chief cells was observed, manifested by an increase in their overall area, nucleus, and nuclear\-cytoplasmic ratio\. Correlation analysis in the comorbid group demonstrated the disappearance of significant correlations for PGR9\.5 and S\-100 previously observed in the control group, as well as the emergence of new pathological associations, indicating a profound disruption of tissue regulation\. Conclusions: The obtained data suggest a new pathophysiological mechanism for carotid body dysfunction in hypertension and common carotid artery stenosis, based on the disconnection of functional elements within the organ&\#x27;s parenchyma\. Disruption of cooperation between cell populations creates the basis for persistent chemoreceptor hyperactivation, which contributes to elevated blood pressure and the progression of hypertension\.
