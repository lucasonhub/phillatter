# Bouchard, Michael: Measuring the Grammar of a Mind: A Pre\-Registered, Single\-Subject Study of Epistemic Structure in Longitudinal Human–AI Interaction

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Proactive AI assistance depends on one input above all others: an accurate predictive model of the individual user\. The field&\#x27;s own ablation studies show this, and its benchmarks concede that such models are currently trained on synthetic users, because no suitable longitudinal record of a real person exists in the research commons\. The extended mind thesis faces a parallel gap\. Decades of experimental work show that cognition can extend into external processes, yet no study has measured the persistent structure of one real, long\-lived human and tool coupling\. This paper supplies both missing objects from a single source: the author&\#x27;s complete conversational record with AI assistants, comprising 13,682 timestamped user turns across three and a half years, together with versioned self\-consolidation documents and essays\. Two findings result: a confirmed structural measurement, and a security discovery that follows from it\. Study 1 establishes that this conduct decomposes into a thirteen\-move taxonomy of recurring epistemic moves whose sequences carry structure far beyond chance \(permutation z ≈ 188\), a signal that survives removal of self\-transitions and replicates on 255 subsequently recovered conversations \(transition\-structure correlation r = 0\.985\), with an effect size of Cramér&\#x27;s V ≈ 0\.17\. Study 2 imposed a fully pre\-registered confirmatory protocol: dual labeling of every turn on a workflow facet and a reasoning facet, criteria and thresholds frozen before labeling, a human verification gate whose measured reliability \(58% subject agreement\) is reported with the results, and a sealed 20% hold\-out used exactly once\. Two registered predictions from the development split, a reasoning rate of 39\.3% and a workflow\-to\-reasoning mutual\-information lift of 0\.137, were confirmed on the sealed hold\-out at 40\.7% and 0\.157 respectively, with both directional couplings significant out\-of\-sample at p &lt; 10⁻²⁰\. This is, to the author&\#x27;s knowledge, the first pre\-registered prediction about the structure of one&\#x27;s own mind confirmed on sealed data\. The second finding is a security discovery, developed in its own section: the measured individual baseline that enables anticipation also enables impostor detection\. Commercial behavioral\-security tools construct baselines from aggregate data across fleets of users, which is precisely why an attacker operating inside stolen credentials can pass by behaving like a plausible generic user\. An individual baseline detects what the aggregate baseline structurally cannot, and the modest effect size is no obstacle: an impostor&\#x27;s behavior is drawn from a different distribution altogether, and distinguishing two distributions is a far easier problem than forecasting within one\. The model of a person doubles as the definition of that person&\#x27;s absence\.
