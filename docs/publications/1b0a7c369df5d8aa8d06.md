# Beyond Robust Mapping: The Explanatory Selection Problem in Physical Computation

**Autores:** Mehmet Ali Sarı

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Anderson and Piccinini’s Robust Mapping Account \(RMA\) explains when a physical system implements a computation and how that computation can explain its computational behaviour\. Their appeal to the explanandum can also identify a uniquely explanatory computation\. This article asks what grounds remain necessary when that explanation is compared with another legitimate account of the same phenomenon\. It calls this the Explanatory Selection Problem and proposes three requirements for addressing it: explanandum sensitivity, comparative relevance, and responsiveness to the epistemic demands of inquiry\. Two schematic cases clarify the problem’s scope\. Neural damage may alter or destroy implementation; a digital computer may, by stipulation, continue to robustly implement the same computation while its execution slows\. These cases show how explanatory demands change, without establishing competition between accounts of a fixed explanandum\. The argument accepts RMA’s constitutive explanation of computational behaviour at the relevant granularity and identifies what must still be justified wherever a comparison remains open\.
