# Enhancing operational efficiency in resource\-constrained apparel SMEs through simulation\-based process standardisation

**Autores:** Cristopher Antonio Muñoz Ibáñez; Rogelio Zarza\-Díaz; Juana Isabel Méndez; Mariel Alfaro\-Ponce

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examines Discrete Event Simulation as a strategic tool for operational optimisation in small and medium\-sized textile enterprises, focusing on a women&\#x27;s boxer shorts production line\. It addresses common inefficiencies in resource\-constrained, high\-variability environments: task fragmentation, unbalanced workloads, and excessive cycle\-time variability\. A validated simulation model was built in Arena® software using time and motion studies, probability distribution fitting, and field data\. Two improvement scenarios were tested: \(1\) adding auxiliary labour at bottleneck stations, and \(2\) standardising operating procedures to reduce both mean processing time and cycle\-time variability of critical operations\. Scenario 1 yielded negligible throughput gains, whereas Scenario 2 increased average shift production by 31\.7% over the simulated baseline of 398 pieces/shift \(\+26\.5% versus the observed 414 pieces/shift\), while reducing variability and improving stability\. Statistical significance was confirmed via ANOVA \(30 replications per scenario, p &lt; 0\.05\) with Tukey&\#x27;s test, and model validity by deviation within ±4% of real performance\. The standardisation strategy is also expected to yield sustainability benefits, raising good\-quality output from existing capacity and reducing waste, though these effects are inferred, not directly measured, and left to future work\. Overall, results highlight simulation&\#x27;s value for improving efficiency and competitiveness in modular textile manufacturing without major capital investment\.
