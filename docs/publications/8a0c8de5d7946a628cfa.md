# Precision Exercise Support for University Students: A Scoping Review of Assessment, Prescription, Monitoring, Feedback, and Adaptation

**Autores:** Wenshan Du; Yingsoon Goh

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: To map how exercise and physical\-education research with university students has implemented assessment, subgroup targeting, individualized prescription, monitoring, feedback, and response\-based adjustment, and to identify what is still required for a reproducible closed\-loop precision\-support system\. Methods: This scoping review followed JBI guidance and the PRISMA extension for scoping reviews\. PubMed, Scopus, and CINAHL Ultimate were searched on August 21, 2026, for English\-language studies, supplemented by an existing 28\-report full\-text seed set\. Eligible studies involved university students and evaluated an exercise, physical\-education, fitness\-testing, monitoring, or feedback component relevant to tailoring exercise support\. A five\-level framework was applied: standardized provision \(level 0\), subgroup targeting \(level 1\), assessment\- or feedback\-informed adaptation \(level 2\), individual prescription \(level 3\), and response\-adaptive closed loop \(level 4\)\. Results: The searches and seed set identified 403 records; 358 unique records were screened, 48 reports were assessed in full text, and 30 studies entered the evidence map\. Fifteen studies used standardized provision, three targeted a predefined subgroup, six implemented assessment, monitoring, or personalized feedback without a complete individual prescription, and six reported individual exercise prescriptions\. No study fully specified a level\-4 closed loop with prospective monitoring variables, decision thresholds, and reproducible rules for changing frequency, intensity, time, or type\. Exercise programs improved selected fitness, body\-composition, physical\-activity, and mental\-health outcomes, but effects were outcome\-specific and heterogeneous\. Wearables and mobile platforms made monitoring scalable, yet fixed goals, multicomponent designs, and limited use of monitored data for treatment decisions constrained personalization\. Assessment feedback could also reduce competence and intrinsic motivation when interpreted negatively\. Conclusions: The literature has progressed beyond uniform exercise delivery, but digital monitoring should not be equated with precision\. A defensible university model requires an auditable chain from multidimensional assessment to stratification, FITT prescription, process monitoring, interpretable feedback, and threshold\-based adjustment, alongside evaluation of safety, equity, adherence, teacher workload, and operational efficiency\.
