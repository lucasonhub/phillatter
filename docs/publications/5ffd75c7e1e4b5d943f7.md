# Technology and Artificial Intelligence Law in India: A Critical Analysis of Legal Challenges, Regulatory Framework and Emerging Jurisprudence

**Autores:** K Jagadish

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Technology has become an essential part of modern society, influencing almost every aspect of human life, including communication, commerce, healthcare, education, banking, governance and the administration of justice\. Among the most significant technological developments of the twenty\-first century is Artificial Intelligence \(AI\)\. Artificial Intelligence refers to computer systems capable of performing tasks that traditionally require human intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, prediction, decision\-making, language processing and image recognition\. The rapid development of AI presents considerable opportunities for economic and social development, but it also creates complex legal and ethical challenges\. Questions relating to privacy, data protection, discrimination, algorithmic bias, intellectual property rights, cybercrime, intermediary liability, consumer protection, employment, surveillance, deepfakes, autonomous systems and accountability have become increasingly important\. India does not presently have a single comprehensive legislation exclusively regulating Artificial Intelligence\. Instead, the legal framework is distributed among constitutional principles, the Information Technology Act, 2000, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, intellectual property legislation, criminal law, consumer protection law, contract law and sector\-specific regulations\. Government policy initiatives, including the National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence and the Responsible AI framework developed by NITI Aayog, have also contributed to India&\#x27;s emerging AI governance framework\. The Supreme Court of India has developed important constitutional principles concerning privacy, freedom of speech, informational autonomy and technological governance\. Decisions such as Justice K\.S\. Puttaswamy \(Retd\.\) v Union of India, Shreya Singhal v Union of India, Anuradha Bhasin v Union of India and Justice K\.S\. Puttaswamy \(Retd\.\) v Union of India \(Aadhaar\) provide a constitutional foundation for addressing several AI\-related concerns\. However, specifically AI\-generated content, AI authorship, algorithmic decision\-making and liability for autonomous systems remain comparatively underdeveloped areas of Indian jurisprudence\. This paper critically examines the existing Indian legal framework governing technology and Artificial Intelligence\. It analyses constitutional rights, data protection, cyber law, intellectual property, intermediary liability, AI\-generated content, deepfakes, algorithmic discrimination and emerging jurisprudence\. It further compares selected international approaches and proposes a balanced Indian regulatory model based upon innovation, transparency, accountability, human oversight and protection of fundamental rights\. Keywords: Artificial Intelligence, Technology Law, AI Governance, Data Protection, Privacy, Algorithmic Bias, Cyber Law, Deepfakes, Intellectual Property, Digital Personal Data, Constitutional Law, Intermediary Liability\.
