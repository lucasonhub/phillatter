# People change their decisions when an AI system contradicts them, given a counterfactual explanation

**Autores:** Xinyue Dai; Mark T\. Keane; L\. Shalloo; E\. Ruelle; Ruth M\. J\. Byrne

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

We tested whether people change their decisions when an AI system proposes they do so, and the effects of a counterfactual explanation\. In eight experiments with 2,369 participants, they made a decision and an AI system recommended they make the opposite one\. Participants did not tend to switch their decisions when an AI system proposed they do so, compared to a human expert \(Experiment 1\), regardless of how it was described \(Experiment 1b\)\. Their reluctance occurred whether or not they committed to a decision first \(Experiments 2a and 2b\)\. They switched to the AI system’s decision more when it provided a counterfactual explanation than none \(Experiment 3\); and compared to a causal or evidence explanation \(Experiment 4\)\. Explanations did not increase switches to a decision proposed by a human expert \(Experiments 5a and 5b\)\. The results occurred whether the AI system proposed a riskier or a safer decision\. People are reluctant to adopt a decision proposed by an AI system when it is contrary to their preferred one; counterfactual explanations increase their willingness to do so, because they enable them to consider alternatives\. The data are available at https://osf\.io/frhk9/\.
