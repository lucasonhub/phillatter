# The AI collaboration paradox: A job demands\-resources perspective on auditor performance and burnout

**Autores:** Anabella Jessica Sutanto; Christoveron Mories Efendi; Agustinus Winoto

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid development of Artificial Intelligence \(AI\) has been successfully transforming organizational practices across many industries, including auditing\. Through the lens of the Job Demands\-Resources \(JD\-R\) framework, AI can be viewed as a collaboration paradox, since it works simultaneously as a job demand that generates technostress and as a job resource that facilitates employee\-AI collaboration\. Therefore, the purpose of this study is to examine the collaboration paradox by exploring the effect of AI as both a job demand and a job resource on auditor burnout and performance\. Quantitative research with a primary, cross\-sectional, and non\-experimental design was conducted in this study\. Data were obtained via Google Forms from 167 external auditors working at Public Accounting Firms \(PAFs\) in Indonesia who had prior hands\-on experience using AI in the audit process and were then analyzed using SmartPLS 4\.0\. The results indicate that technostress and employee\-AI collaboration positively affect burnout, while burnout negatively affects performance\. In contrast, employee\-AI collaboration positively affects performance, while technostress has no direct effect\. Additionally, burnout successfully mediates the relationship between technostress and performance but fails to mediate the relationship between employee\-AI collaboration and performance\. These findings reveal the paradoxical role of AI in the auditing context\. As a job demand, AI generates technostress that indirectly affects auditor performance by increasing burnout\. As a job resource, AI facilitates employee\-AI collaboration, which enhances auditor performance but also increases burnout\. Therefore, the findings indicate that the successful integration of AI into audit practice depends not only on the technical capabilities of AI tools but also on how effectively PAFs manage auditors&\#x27; physical and emotional well\-being\.
