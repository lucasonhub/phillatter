# Niami, Morteza: When the Past Cannot Be Narrated Experience, Trace, Memory, and the Emergence of Narrative

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

The philosophical study of memory often begins from the assumption that a past event is either remembered or forgotten\. This article proposes a more differentiated account of the relation between past experience and its subsequent articulation\. A person may have lived through events, retain effects of those events, and remain affected by them while lacking a narratively organized access to the corresponding past\. The article develops a four\-level conceptual distinction among experience, trace, memory, and narrative\. Experience designates the occurrence of something in lived time\. A trace designates a modification or remainder produced by that occurrence\. Memory designates the recognition or retrieval of something as belonging to the past\. Narrative designates the temporal and semantic organization through which remembered elements become intelligibly related\. These distinctions establish a further difference between the passage of time and the narratability of time\. Chronological succession can occur without producing an accessible autobiographical sequence\. The article then examines the figure of the empty diary as a phenomenological model of a past whose events remain inaccessible to narrative organization\. The central paradox emerges when the recognition of an inability to narrate becomes itself narratable\. The statement “I have nothing to narrate” can therefore initiate a second\-order relation to the past: “I have a past that I cannot narrate\.” This transition transforms narrative absence into an object of reflection and establishes the possibility that narrative can begin through the recognition of its own deficiency\. The central contribution of this article is the introduction of narrative absence as a distinct phenomenological category that cannot be reduced to either forgetting or remembering\. By distinguishing four analytically separable levels—experience, trace, memory, and narrative—the article establishes a conceptual space between the mere persistence of the past and its narrative articulation\. The key theoretical innovation is the identification of a reflexive structure: the recognition of an inability to narrate can itself become the beginning of a narrative, transforming narrative absence into a narrative object\. Keywords: memory, narrative, temporality, autobiographical memory, phenomenology, lived time, narrative identity, trace, recollection, narrative absence, past \(direct link \)
