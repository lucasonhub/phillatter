# The Trust Paradox of AI\-Generated Content Transparency: Impulse Buying from the Perspective of Generative Agents

**Autores:** Xi Cheng

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

As generative artificial intelligence becomes increasingly embedded in digital e\-commerce environments, the transparency labeling of AI\-generated content has emerged as a crucial institutional variable in modern platform governance\. Drawing upon the established information–trust–behavior theoretical logic, this study conducted a large\-scale AI\-agent simulation, comprising 900 distinct purchase decisions executed by large language model agents, to systematically test how disclosure labeling affects simulated consumer trust and simulated impulse buying intention\. Furthermore, the research investigated whether trust acts as a mediating mechanism and whether consumer involvement moderates these relationships\. Rigorous model\-rationality validation confirmed that the agent decisions consistently conformed to their assigned behavioral personalities and rational economic logic\. The empirical results demonstrated that disclosure labeling significantly suppressed both simulated trust and impulse buying intention\. Additionally, simulated trust positively predicted impulse intention, revealing a negative indirect association within the disclosure–impulse behavioral link\. Notably, consumer involvement moderated the trust–impulse path during the primary simulation phase, although this moderating effect was not replicated in the cross\-model robustness check\. Ultimately, these comprehensive findings reveal the critical boundary conditions of the institutional logic surrounding the exchange of transparency for trust\. The study provides robust, simulation\-based empirical evidence for the development of AI\-content labeling policies and offers valuable insights for optimizing platform information governance strategies in the digital economy\.
