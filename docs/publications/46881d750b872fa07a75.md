# Stilwell, Phil: ✶ Mental Irreductionism \- Experience, Representation, and the Mistaken Regress from Description to Ontology

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

An argument for anti\-physicalist mental irreductionism begins from a genuine feature of consciousness: a description of an experience is not the experience, and entertaining that description produces a further experience\. Because the further state can also be described and experienced, the sequence has no fixed endpoint\. The argument then concludes that consciousness always escapes physical realization\. This paper argues that the conclusion does not follow\. The sequence changes its target at each step: it moves from an experience to a representation of that experience and then to a new state about the representation\. A physicalist can treat every member of the sequence as a distinct physically realized event\. The regress is therefore compatible with both anti\-physicalist mental irreductionism and recursively representational physicalism unless a further premise connects representational nonidentity with nonphysical realization\. The strongest objections reveal important limits\. First\-person acquaintance may provide knowledge that a public description does not; phenomenal concepts may present experience in a distinctive way; and a complete theory must explain why physical processes have a felt character at all\. Those are serious challenges developed by the knowledge\-argument and explanatory\-gap literature\. They are not established merely by the regress\. The warranted conclusion is narrow but useful: representational open\-endedness supplies no independent reason to prefer mental irreductionism over physical realization\. Physicalism is not thereby proven, and the explanatory burden concerning consciousness remains\.
