# Determinants of osteoarticular involvement in brucellosis: a multicenter retrospective case–control study

**Autores:** Yakup Gezer; Muhammet Rıdvan Tayşi; Arzu Tarakçı; Esra Sağlam Kandemir; Tuğba Demircioğlu; Hanife Uzar

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective Osteoarticular involvement is the most common focal complication of brucellosis and usually requires prolonged treatment\. However, data regarding the factors associated with osteoarticular involvement remain limited\. This multicenter retrospective study aimed to identify the clinical and laboratory factors associated with osteoarticular involvement in patients with brucellosis\. Methods This multicenter retrospective case\-control study included adult patients diagnosed with brucellosis at three centers between October 2020 and September 2025\. Patients with osteoarticular involvement were defined as cases, whereas patients without osteoarticular involvement served as controls\. Demographic, clinical, laboratory, and radiological data were retrospectively collected\. Factors associated with osteoarticular involvement were investigated using univariate and multivariable logistic regression analyses\. Results A total of 804 patients with brucellosis were included in the study\. The median age was 45 years, and 516 \(64\.2%\) were male\. Osteoarticular involvement was identified in 217 \(27\.0%\) patients\. In the multivariable logistic regression analysis, symptom duration \(OR: 1\.102; 95% CI: 1\.068–1\.136; p&lt;0\.001\) and erythrocyte sedimentation rate \(ESR\) \(OR: 1\.025; 95% CI: 1\.016–1\.034; p&lt;0\.001\) were independently associated with osteoarticular involvement\. Receiver operating characteristic \(ROC\) analysis identified optimal cut\-off values of 3\.5 weeks for symptom duration and 22\.5 mm/hour for ESR in predicting osteoarticular involvement\. Conclusion Symptom duration and ESR were independently associated with osteoarticular involvement in patients with brucellosis\. These readily available clinical and laboratory parameters may facilitate the early identification of patients at high risk for osteoarticular involvement and assist in planning appropriate diagnostic evaluations\.
