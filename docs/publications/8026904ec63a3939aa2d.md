# MacDonald, Calum: Completely Measured: Integrated Information Theory and Identity

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Suppose any experience is completely measurable\. What follows? Drawing on Integrated Information Theory and such works as Frege’s analysis of identity, I argue that complete measurements of experience function as criteria of identity for experiences\. It follows that wherever complete measurements are equal, the experiences they measure are identical—even should the experiences be specified by different substrates; even should those substrates be separated by vast distances of space and time\. We can reasonably conclude that any experience which has ever occurred might once again exist in the universe\. No moment is necessarily ‘lost forever’\. And death is not what we had thought it to be\. Further, while it has been claimed by some and attributed to others that we cannot know whether we are fundamentally diachronic or synchronic entities, Integrated Information Theory reveals an approach to a clarifying view: by expressing the essential properties of phenomenal existence, we find an identity for the essence of experience\. Thus we find firmer ground for claiming that what we are, essentially, subsists across moments\. The implications of the justification of this judgment, however, are radical: we find that what we are, essentially, is phenomenal existence itself\. These findings may resolve such challenges of identity as the interchange of bodies, fission or fusion, and teletransportation\.
