# A scalable process aware routing architecture for resilient wireless communication in industrial and control networks

**Autores:** Abed Saif Alghawli; Ali Raza; Altahir Saad Ahmed; Suzan Hassan Bakhit; Muhammad Farman

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Industrial carbon\-capture plants depend on timely and reliable communication among distributed sensors, controllers, and process models\. Existing studies commonly examine fault recovery, energy\-aware routing, reinforcement learning, or digital\-twin monitoring as separate problems, leaving limited evidence on their coordinated use in one reproducible industrial communication framework\. This study presents a process\-aware MeshStar wireless sensor network \(WSN\) that combines low\-overhead star clusters with redundant mesh links among cluster routers\. A tabular Q\-learning controller selects recovery and traffic\-management actions from network reliability, latency, congestion, residual energy, link availability, and thermal\-process criticality\. The framework is evaluated through OMNeT\+\+/INET network simulation coupled with a MATLAB/Simulink carbon\-capture process model\. Thirty independent seeds are used for each method and operating scenario, with comparisons against static Dijkstra, RPL\-MRHOF, energy\-aware RPL, conventional Q\-routing, and deep Q\-network routing\. Under the revised evaluation protocol, the proposed method achieved a 95th\-percentile latency of 84\.6 ms, a packet delivery ratio \(PDR\) of 98\.32%, and a route\-recovery time of 3\.74 s, corresponding to improvements of 12\.6%, 0\.78 percentage points, and 16\.1%, respectively, over the strongest baseline\. Scalability, security stress, process stability, and scenario\-based economic results are reported separately\. Because the validation is software\-dominant, the findings demonstrate comparative feasibility rather than field\-deployment readiness\.
