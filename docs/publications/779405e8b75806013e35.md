# Data\-driven insight into CO2 electroreduction: Ensemble machine learning for CO Faraday efficiency prediction in single\-atom catalysts

**Autores:** Dhanveer  Singh Rawat; Amit Pratap Singh; Arjun Singh Rawat

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study presents an ensemble machine\-learning \(ML\) framework to predict the Faraday efficiency of CO formation \(FE CO \) for electrocatalytic CO 2 reduction \(CO 2 RR\) on single\-atom catalysts \(SACs\)\. A literature\-derived dataset of 795 experimental entries from 42 primary studies was compiled, representing 24 carrier classes, 12 dopant atoms, and H\-cell, flow\-cell, and membrane\-electrode\-assembly \(MEA\) configurations\. Eighteen regressors from nine algorithm families were systematically evaluated\. A stacking ensemble combining gradient boosting decision tree, random forest, extremely randomized trees, support vector regression, k \-nearest\-neighbor regression, and CatBoost base learners with a kernel\-based meta\-regressor achieved the highest record\-level performance, with a test R 2 of 0\.9106 and an RMSE of 8\.41 percentage points\. Repeated five\-fold cross\-validation confirmed robust within\-domain performance across random partitions \(mean R 2 = 0\.882 ± 0\.006 \)\. Permutation importance, SHAP, partial\-dependence analysis, and descriptor ablation identified dopant atom, applied potential, catalyst identity, and carrier as the dominant model\-associated descriptors\. Screening recovered established associations with Ni\-based SACs, with graphene showing the highest mean model\-predicted FE CO among the represented carrier classes\. A discrete metal\-potential response surface identified a Ni\-associated region with a mean predicted FE CO of approximately 85% near \-0\.77 V versus RHE and a maximum of approximately 99% near \-0\.97 V\. More stringent catalyst\-, publication\-, and Ni\-restricted grouped validation showed weaker transfer to unseen systems\. Thus, record\-level performance primarily reflects interpolation within the represented experimental domain, while screening identifies experimentally grounded, dataset\-conditioned trends for further validation\.
