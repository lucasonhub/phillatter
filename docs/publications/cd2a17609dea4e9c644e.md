# Performance and safety of large language models versus two early\-career urologists in urological oncology: a source\-masked vignette benchmark with repeated\-query assessment of LLM response stability

**Autores:** Ahmet Tufekci; EYÜP KAPLAN; Özlem Başgut; Görkem Durna; Süleyman Sağır; Mehmet Sakıp Erturhan

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Background Aggregate large language model \(LLM\) performance may obscure consequential errors and variability across repeated queries\. We compared the clinical performance, safety, and repeated\-query stability of three consumer\-facing LLMs with two fixed individual early\-career urologist comparators in urological oncology\. Methods One hundred synthetic vignettes spanning five urological cancers and five decision categories were answered by GPT\-5\.2 Instant, Claude Sonnet 4\.5, Gemini 3 Pro Preview, and two early\-career urologists using the same standardized prompt without external resources\. Two source\-masked urologists with uro\-oncology expertise independently scored all 500 initial responses against a prespecified 2025 European Association of Urology \(EAU\)\-anchored framework\. Benchmark\-defined primary composite success required scores ≥ 2 in guideline concordance, clinical safety, decision completeness, and risk/stage accuracy, with no prespecified disqualifying clinical error\. Safety\-critical errors and exact three\-run stability were assessed separately\. Results Primary composite success was achieved in 70/100 \(70\.0%\) GPT\-5\.2, 64/100 \(64\.0%\) Claude, and 60/100 \(60\.0%\) Gemini responses, versus 85/100 \(85\.0%\) and 89/100 \(89\.0%\) for Urologists A and B \(Cochran’s Q = 34\.23; P &lt; 0\.001\)\. Five of six prespecified human–LLM contrasts remained significant after Holm adjustment; Urologist A versus GPT\-5\.2 did not \(difference, 15 percentage points; 95% CI, 3–27; adjusted P = 0\.095\)\. Safety\-critical errors occurred in 25\.0%, 25\.0%, and 26\.0% of LLM responses versus 2\.0% and 1\.0% of urologist responses \(Cochran’s Q = 51\.34; P &lt; 0\.001\); all six human–LLM contrasts remained significant\. Across 60 vignette–platform combinations, exact three\-run stability was 45\.0% for primary classification, 48\.3% for safety\-critical status, and 28\.3% for the full four\-domain score profile\. Stable classifications included both consistently successful and consistently unsuccessful outputs and both always\-safe and always\-unsafe outputs\. Conclusions The consumer\-facing LLMs evaluated during the study period often produced acceptable responses, but safety\-critical errors and limited repeatability remained clinically relevant\. Aggregate performance did not fully capture response reliability\. These findings support clinician\-supervised rather than autonomous use and support incorporating safety\-gated endpoints and repeated\-query stability alongside conventional performance measures\.
