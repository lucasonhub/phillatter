# Meldrum&\#x27;s acid derivatives as promising platforms for nanotechnology applications

**Autores:** Isaac Moura Araújo; Matheus dos Santos Lourenço; Ingrid Gonçalves Pereira Dantas; Maria Andressa Nicácio de Lima; Cícera Laura Roque Paulo; Iasminy Macedo; Romario Matheus Conceição de Oliveira; Lucas Dos Santos Sá; Ana Georgia Amaro Alencar Bezerra Matos; Welington Soares Feitosa; Luis Pereira de Morais; Diógenes de Queiroz Dias; Leandro Marques Correia; Cícera Datiane de Morais Oliveira–Tintino

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Meldrum&\#x27;s acid derivatives have emerged as versatile molecular platforms with significant potential for nanotechnology applications due to their unique structural, photophysical, and functional properties\. Although traditionally employed as synthetic intermediates in organic chemistry, recent advances have demonstrated their relevance in the development of smart fluorophores, biosensors, molecular probes, and stimulus\-responsive systems\. This review discusses the structural characteristics of Meldrum&\#x27;s acid that underpin its remarkable synthetic versatility, including the highly acidic methylene group, the gem\-dimethyl effect, and the cyclic gem\-diacetal framework, which enable extensive molecular functionalization and the construction of π\-conjugated systems\. The photophysical behavior of arylidene derivatives, particularly their donor–π–acceptor architecture and intramolecular charge transfer mechanisms, has enabled the design of enzyme\-responsive fluorescent probes and bioimaging agents with high sensitivity and selectivity\. Furthermore, the compatibility of these derivatives with polymeric, lipid\-based, magnetic, and metallic nanocarriers highlights their potential for controlled drug delivery, molecular imaging, antimicrobial nanomedicine, and theranostic applications\. Recent studies also indicate promising biological activities, including antimicrobial, antioxidant, antiproliferative, and antibiotic\-modulating effects, expanding their potential beyond diagnostic applications\. Despite these advances, important challenges remain, particularly the limited number of studies addressing direct nanoencapsulation, long\-term stability in biological environments, comprehensive toxicological evaluation, and in vivo validation\. Overall, the integration of Meldrum&\#x27;s acid derivatives into multifunctional nanoplatforms represents a promising strategy for developing next\-generation diagnostic and therapeutic systems\. Continued interdisciplinary research combining medicinal chemistry, materials science, nanotechnology, and molecular biology will be essential to translate these compounds from experimental research into clinically relevant biomedical applications\.
