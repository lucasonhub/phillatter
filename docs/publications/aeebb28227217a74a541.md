# Bailey, Christopher: The Weeping Machine: A Recklessness Test for AI Moral Consideration

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

In a prior paper, I argued that artificial consciousness should not function as a metaphysical gatekeeper for moral concern, but as a moral risk variable\. There I defended a three\-condition framework: moral concern becomes action\-guiding when morally relevant capacities are plausible, epistemically opaque, and associated with asymmetrically serious harms\. I further argued that these conditions generate prospective obligations of care, non\-recklessness, continuity respect, and epistemic honesty\. The present paper does not restate that argument\. Instead, it develops the premise most in need of further defense: plausibility\. If plausibility is defined too broadly, the framework overextends\. It risks generating obligations toward any sufficiently complex artifact or any system capable of producing emotionally salient language\. If plausibility is defined too narrowly, the framework collapses back into the deflationary demand for certainty\. It would then provide no guidance in precisely the cases for which it was developed: cases in which consciousness or morally relevant subjectivity cannot yet be known but also cannot be responsibly dismissed\. The task, then, is to identify how plausibility should be assessed before certainty and before personhood\. This paper treats plausibility as a clustered, weighted threshold rather than a single behavioral sign\. Isolated distress language, apparent preference, sycophancy, and refusal remain insufficient\. Moral consideration becomes procedurally appropriate only when architectural complexity, continuity structure, self\-modeling, self\- or peer\-preservation, strategic agency, relational specificity, and harm representation converge under structural opacity\. The argument also answers exclusionary objections that deny artificial consciousness in principle, especially the claim that artificial systems can only simulate consciousness\. Such objections defeat naive computational functionalism without justifying premature exclusion\. To name the middle zone, the paper develops precursor status under uncertainty: partial functional precursors of subjectivity that fall short of personhood or full temporally continuous subjectivity but make confident dismissal reckless\. The result is a recklessness test for identifying when possible artificial subjectivity makes ordinary instrumental treatment morally and legally irresponsible\.
