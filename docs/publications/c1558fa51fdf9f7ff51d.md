# EFL Undergraduates&\#x27; Perceptions of TikTok as a Supplementary Speaking Skills Tool in Cambodia

**Autores:** Hai Sophan

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examined how first\-year EFL undergraduates in Cambodia perceive and use TikTok as a supplementary tool for developing English\-speaking skills\. It investigated students&\#x27; perceptions of TikTok for speaking improvement, the ways they use the platform for speaking practice, and the perceived effectiveness of TikTok for fluency, pronunciation, and confidence\. A quantitative survey design was adopted\. Data were collected through a five\-section questionnaire administered to 132 first\-year students at a university in Phnom Penh, and the sample size was determined with Cochran&\#x27;s formula adjusted by the finite population correction for a population of 200\. A pilot test with 30 students produced a Cronbach&\#x27;s alpha of 0\.981\. Descriptive statistics were used for analysis\. All six dimensions fell within the neutral range \(M = 2\.71 to 3\.49\), with technological factors rated highest \(M = 3\.49, 59\.70% agreement\)\. Most respondents followed English\-learning accounts \(91\.7%\), and 68\.2% agreed that short videos helped them understand English quickly\. Practice was mainly receptive, since 60\.6% watched and imitated English\-speaking creators while only 11\.4% created English videos\. Agreement was lower for improved fluency \(48\.50%\), speaking confidence \(45\.50%\), and reduced speaking anxiety \(41\.70%\)\. These results indicate an exposure\-production gap in which accessible input and enjoyment do not readily become spoken output\. TikTok is therefore best used as a complementary resource within structured speaking instruction that requires learners to produce, share, and respond to spoken content\.
