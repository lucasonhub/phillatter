# Specialty\-aware benchmark design, split integrity, and baseline evaluation for Korean medical question answering

**Autores:** Mi\-Ae Yang; Kang\-Su Ha

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Korean medical question answering requires evaluation resources that reflect local terminology, clinical specialty structure, and essential\-care priorities\. We characterized the AI Hub Specialized Medical Knowledge and Essential Medical Knowledge datasets, audited the independence of their distributed validation labels, and implemented a package\-specific baseline evaluation\. The resources contained 222,073,009 source\-data tokens and 34,487 labeled question\-answer pairs across 17 labeled domains\. Domain concentration was pronounced \(Herfindahl\-Hirschman Index 0\.178; effective domains 5\.62\), and 78\.7% of items were multiple\-choice\. Question\-type distributions differed between datasets \(χ²\(2\) = 165\.82, p &lt; 0\.001; Cramér’s V = 0\.069\)\. Among 3448 distributed validation records, 3333 \(96\.7%\) shared qa\_id values with the model\-training files, with median normalized question similarity 0\.885, indicating that these files were unsuitable as independent tests of the accompanying fine\-tuned models\. On an AI Hub\-supplied 2494\-row KorMedMCQA\-derived evaluation file containing 2489 unique normalized questions, essential\-care and specialized\-care Qwen2\.5\-14B models achieved 64\.35% and 64\.03% accuracy, respectively; the paired difference was not significant \(exact McNemar p = 0\.440\)\. The framework is technically implementable, but valid specialty\-level comparison requires a separately constructed, decontaminated test set and prespecified clinical error review\.
