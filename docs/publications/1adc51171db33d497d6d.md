# Clinical and regulatory evaluation of digital therapeutics and biomarkers in rheumatology research and care

**Autores:** Cinja Nadana Koller

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Background: Access to specialized rheumatology care is limited and increasing healthcare demands challenge the management of chronic rheumatic diseases\. Digital health solutions, including digital therapeutics \(DTx\) and digital biomarkers, offer promising opportunities to support remote care and research, particularly within decentralized clinical trials \(DCTs\), which conduct study activities remotely\. Objective: This thesis aimed to evaluate DTx and digital biomarkers in clinical research and rheumatology care, with a special focus on decentralized approaches, by investigating the opportunities and challenges associated with their integration, including regulatory, methodological, and implementation aspects, and by evaluating a novel digital biomarker for joint swelling\. Methods: A scoping and narrative review were conducted examining methodological, regulatory and implementation aspects of DCTs, DTx and digital biomarkers\. A hybrid prospective clinical study in patients with chronic musculoskeletal pain investigated the impact of onboarding strategies on user adoption and engagement with a digital health application\. Cross\-sectional and longitudinal multicenter clinical studies in patients with inflammatory arthritis were conducted to evaluate the Finger Fold Index, a machine learning\-based digital biomarker for proximal interphalangeal \(PIP\) joint swelling, derived from smartphone images\. Finally, a study protocol was designed within an EU funded consortium for a hybrid randomized clinical trial \(Pix\-Risk\) aimed at evaluating a remote monitoring platform\. Results: The reviews highlighted substantial regulatory and methodological complexities, limited harmonized guidance, and DTx adoption and sustained engagement as key challenges for DCTs and remote care implementation\. In\-person onboarding to the digital health application improved initial use but did not impact long\-term engagement\. Within the multicenter study, feasibility of self\-collected hand images was established\. The digital biomarker demonstrated significant associations with PIP joint swelling but showed limitations in processing of low\-quality images, diagnostic performance and longitudinal responsiveness\. Collectively, these findings informed the development of the Pix\-Risk protocol, which was submitted to regulatory authorities and the local ethics committee\. Discussion: Although DTx and digital biomarkers have considerable potential to and support rheumatology care, their widespread implementation remains limited by regulatory challenges, patient engagement barriers, and the need for further validation of the digital biomarker\. Future work should focus on strengthening regulatory pathways, optimizing engagement strategies, and generating evidence through prospective studies, such as Pix\-Risk, to establish clinical validity before regulatory qualification and routine implementation in decentralized rheumatology research and care\.
