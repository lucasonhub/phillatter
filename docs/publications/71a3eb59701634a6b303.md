# Selective contestation of the liberal international order: institutional adaptation strategies in Colombia, Brazil, and Mexico \(2018–2026\)

**Autores:** Carlos Hernán Gonzalez\-Parias; Gustavo Adolfo Londoño Ossa; José Alexander Velásquez Ochoa; Jorge Andrés Acosta Strobel; José Luis Castilla Cabezudo

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This article examines how Colombia, Brazil, and Mexico developed differentiated strategies of selective contestation toward the liberal international order between 2018 and 2026, and it argues that the three countries maintained their multilateral membership while contesting specific norms, procedures, distributions, and agendas\. The article redefines selective contestation as a bounded, observable category—continued participation, an identifiable institutional object, and a change\-oriented purpose—and distinguishes it from rhetorical criticism, foreign\-policy autonomy, non\-compliance, and exit\. It applies that framework in a structured, focused comparison combining dictionary\-based coding of official national statements to the UN General Assembly \(2018–2025\) with the coding of observable institutional behavior to a cut\-off of 31 January 2026\. Three patterns emerge\. Brazil paired Bolsonaro’s rhetorical and normative contestation with institutional pragmatism, then shifted under Lula III toward procedural and distributive contestation from within, through its 2024 G20 and 2025 BRICS presidencies and COP30 in Belém\. Colombia moved from Duque’s performative Western alignment to Petro’s selective thematic reorientation, culminating in its 2026–2027 Security Council term\. Mexico sustains a non\-interventionist doctrine under Sheinbaum while expanding its multilateral presence\. The comparison supports, without demonstrating, an explanation that locates selective contestation at the intersection of systemic incentives, ideologically mediated domestic preferences, and path\-dependent institutional repertoires\.
