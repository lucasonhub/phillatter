# A single dose of Dengvaxia is associated with transient reduction in dengue infection risk among dengue\-naïve children

**Autores:** Maria Abad Fernandez; Jedas Veronica Daag; Lindsay Dahora Hein; Kristal An Agrupis; Maria Vinna Crisostomo; Michelle C\. Ylade; Jacqueline L\. Deen; Laura White; Lakshmanane Premkumar; Ana Lena Lopez; Aravinda M\. de Silva

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Dengvaxia ® induces antibody responses in dengue virus \(DENV\)\-naïve children, but the durability of these responses and their impact on subsequent wild\-type \(WT\) DENV infection remain unclear\. Although previous studies have primarily evaluated vaccine efficacy against dengue disease, the impact of Dengvaxia ® on susceptibility to WT DENV infection and subsequent immune responses remains less well defined\. We evaluated vaccine\-induced immunity and susceptibility to WT DENV infection in a longitudinal cohort of 186 seronegative children, including 114 who received a single dose of Dengvaxia ® and 72 who remained unvaccinated, followed for 40 months\. Samples collected at baseline and two follow\-up time points \(20 months \[T1\] and 40 months \[T2\]\) were analyzed using yellow fever virus \(YFV\) non\-structural protein 1 \(NS1\) antibody assays, which is expressed by Dengvaxia ® but not by WT DENV, enabling discrimination between vaccine\-associated and WT infection\-induced responses\. At T1, 61% of vaccinated children were responders\. Additional low\-level YFV NS1 antibody responses were detected in some children originally classified as vaccine non\-responders, suggesting that the predefined binary classification may not capture the full range of vaccine\-associated antibody responses\. Low\-level YFV NS1 responses remained detectable through T2\. During the first follow\-up period \(baseline\-T1\), vaccination was associated with a reduced risk of WT DENV infection \(vaccine effectiveness \[VE\]=45%\)\. During the second period \(T1\-T2\), no difference in infection risk was detected between groups\. Following breakthrough infection during the first follow\-up period, vaccinated children developed antibody responses with greater magnitude and broader serotype breadth than unvaccinated children with primary infection, consistent with vaccine\-associated immune priming following WT DENV infection\. In contrast, during the second period \(T1\-T2\), differences in antibody magnitude and breadth following infection were no longer detected between groups\. Together, these findings suggest that a single dose of Dengvaxia ® in DENV\-seronegative children was associated with a reduced risk of WT DENV infection and with differences in subsequent antibody responses during the first follow\-up period, but these associations were no longer observed during the second period\.
