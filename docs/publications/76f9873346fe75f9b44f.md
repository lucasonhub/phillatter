# Enteropathy in inborn and acquired errors of immunity: histological patterns and clinical correlates

**Autores:** Meera Thangarajah; Samar Ojaimi; Alisa Kane; Sanjay Swaminathan; Ming Wei Lin; Alberto Pinzón‐Charry; Tiarni Templeton; Peter B\. McNaughton; Thanh\-Thao Adriana Le; Constance Helen Katelaris; Julian J\. Bosco; Lucinda J\. Berglund

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Enteropathy is a significant and under\-recognised complication of inborn errors of immunity \(IEI\), frequently misclassified as inflammatory bowel disease \(IBD\) or coeliac disease due to overlapping and non\-specific histopathological findings\. Diagnostic criteria remain poorly defined, contributing to substantial diagnostic delay and associated morbidity\. Methods We conducted a structured literature review and established the AT\-SEA \(Adult Tertiary Severe Enteropathy Audit\) working group, a collaborative network of Australian clinical immunologists operating under the Clinical Immunogenomics Research Consortium Australasia \(CIRCA\), to characterise the clinicopathological features of IEI\-associated enteropathy across a multicentre cohort\. Results Twenty\-eight patients were identified\. Moderate or severe disease was present in 89%, with malabsorption in 71% and protein\-losing enteropathy in 43%\. Across 48 biopsy reports, the most frequent histopathological patterns were coeliac\-like changes \(including villous atrophy, increased intraepithelial lymphocytes and crypt hyperplasia\), IBD\-like changes, reduced or absent plasma cells, graft versus host disease \(GVHD\)\-like changes or increased apoptotic bodies and intraepithelial neutrophilia\. Seventy\-seven percent of patients demonstrated two or more concurrent patterns\. Increasing histological complexity correlated significantly with clinician\-assessed disease severity \(Kruskal\-Wallis p=0\.042\)\. Discussion IEI\-associated enteropathy is characterised by overlapping and evolving histopathological patterns rather than discrete subtypes\. The co\-occurrence of multiple patterns should prompt consideration of an underlying IEI\.
