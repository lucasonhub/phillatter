# Joint determination of factors influencing adoption of climate smart agricultural practices in modern rice production in Southwest Bangladesh: a multivariate probit approach

**Autores:** Basanta Kumar Barmon; Ehsanur Rauf Prince; Sanzidur Rahman

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Climate change and environmental degradation are increasingly threatening rice production in the coastal regions of the developing world, where the goals of raising productivity and building resilience became a joint priority\. Climate smart agriculture offers a portfolio of practices that serve both goals, yet knowledge on how farmers in saline rice systems combine these practices, and why adoption remains often partial, is still limited\. This study jointly examines the factors associated with the adoption of five common climate smart practices \(i\.e\., crop diversification, agroforestry, integrated soil fertility management, small scale irrigation, and integrated pest management\) among modern rice farmers in southwest Bangladesh, using a multivariate probit model\. The analysis draws on 493 farm households randomly selected from complete household lists in three purposively selected villages, one in each of three districts spanning the region’s salinity gradient\. Results show that adoption decisions are interdependent, and most households maintain a portfolio of three or four practices\. Soil improving practices complement one another, whereas small scale irrigation substitutes for diversification and soil and pest management\. Education, farm size, livestock ownership, and climatic exposure are associated with specific individual practices, while access to credit, extension services, and market information are associated with adoption across the portfolio\. The findings highlight the important roles of institutional access and climate\-related factors in the adoption of climate smart agricultural practices, particularly advisory services, credit access, and support for investment\-intensive practices\.
