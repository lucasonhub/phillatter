# Criticality in neural systems is controlled: a mean\-field analysis of excitation\-inhibition balance

**Autores:** Maximino Aldana

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract The hypothesis that the brain operates at criticality has been widely supported by the observation of neuronal avalanches with approximate power\-law distributions in cortical tissue\. However, the identification of the corresponding control parameters remains unclear, and the notion of self\-organized criticality is often invoked to bypass this problem\. Here, we address a complementary notion of criticality, namely a continuous symmetry\-breaking transition of the global order parameter, rather than the scaling statistics of neuronal avalanches\. We analyze a noisy McCulloch\-\-Pitts network within a mean\-field approximation and derive an explicit dynamical map for the average activity\. For fixed connectivity, the model incorporates two key control parameters: the noise level and the asymmetry of synaptic weights, which can be interpreted as the balance between excitation and inhibition\. We show that critical behavior does not occur at a single point but along a critical manifold in parameter space\. A finite\-size analysis shows that the numerical pseudocritical point converges toward the mean\-field prediction as the network size increases\. Additional simulations demonstrate that the order\-\-disorder transition persists in small\-world and scale\-free networks and when Dale&\#x27;s law is imposed, although network topology and correlations among outgoing synaptic signs can substantially shift and broaden the transition\. Our results suggest that neural criticality is not a parameter\-free property, but can be regulated by physiologically accessible mechanisms\. This provides a simple and physically interpretable framework for understanding how experimental manipulations drive neural systems away from criticality and clarifies the role of the excitation\-\-inhibition balance as a control parameter\.
