# Mapping Road Safety Inequity in India Using Artificial Intelligence Where Fatalities Cluster, Where AI Road\-Safety Technology Is Deployed, and What Should Be Prioritised

**Autores:** Samarth Mathur

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

India records more road deaths than any other country in the world, with 172,890 in 2023, but the public data behind that number is rarely studied to ask whether the danger is spread evenly and whether new artificial\-intelligence road\-safety technology is reaching the worst impacted areas\. This paper uses official Ministry of Road Transport and Highways \(MoRTH\) figures for the country, for every state and union territory, and for fifty cities, together with a list of few of India’s AI road\-safety deployments, to map where road deaths cluster and to compare that with where the technology has already been installed\. Then, it builds a simple, literature\-based model of how many lives the proven tools could theoretically save\. The work is quantitative, with fifteen figures and six tables\. The main results are as follows\. The absolute and per\-person data are almost unrelated, with a correlation of only 0\.28, so Uttar Pradesh has the most deaths but only an average rate per person, while Tamil Nadu, Chhattisgarh, and Telangana have the highest rates per person\. AI systems have mostly been implemented in states that already had strong administration rather than to the worst impacted ones, which matches the World Bank’s split between high\-capacity and low\-capacity states\. A preventable\-fraction model estimates that AI\-suited vehicle and enforcement measures could prevent roughly 26,000 deaths a year at moderate coverage, in a range of 11,900 to 49,500, with automated speed enforcement the largest single measure at 17,600\. Even the high estimate is below the national target of halving deaths by 2030, so road engineering and emergency care must take over the burden of covering the rest\. Most of the preventable deaths fall in the worst impacted states that currently have no major deployment, which shows the real cost of the deployment gap\.
