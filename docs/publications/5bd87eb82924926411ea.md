# Natural killer cell\-derived extracellular vesicles in cancer: mediators of intercellular communication and tumor immune signaling

**Autores:** Jun Liu; Yancong Yang; Wei Zhang; Jianwu Liu; Yanyan Yu; Ye Song; Xiaofei Liao; Qiuhua Jiang; Hua Guo; Qianliang Huang

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Natural killer \(NK\) cells are essential components of innate immunity and play a pivotal role in tumor surveillance through direct cytotoxicity and immune regulation\. In recent years, NK\-cell\-derived extracellular vesicles \(NK\-EVs\) have emerged as a promising cell\-free therapeutic modality that preserves key antitumor functions of parental NK cells while overcoming several limitations associated with cell\-based immunotherapy\. As biologically derived nanovesicles, NK\-EVs combine nanoscale delivery properties with a cell\-free formulation format and generally low immunogenicity\. However, although selected preclinical studies suggest tumor\-interacting or target\-cell\-recognition properties, a broadly applicable intrinsic tumor\-targeting capacity—and, in particular, intrinsic blood–brain barrier \(BBB\) penetration—has not been established for native NK\-EVs\. This review systematically summarizes the biological characteristics, biogenesis pathways, and molecular cargo of NK\-EVs, with particular emphasis on their antitumor mechanisms\. Current evidence supports perforin/granzyme\-mediated cytotoxicity and Fas/Fas ligand \(FasL\) signaling as the most established mechanisms of NK\-EVs\-mediated tumor killing, whereas miRNA\-mediated regulation and tumor microenvironment remodeling represent emerging functional pathways requiring further validation\. By contrast, tumor necrosis factor\-related apoptosis\-inducing ligand \(TRAIL\)–death receptor 4/5 \(DR4/DR5\) signaling remains putative because direct NK\-EVs\-specific causal evidence is limited\. Functionally investigated miRNAs and tumor\-microenvironment effects represent context\-dependent or emerging pathways whose generalizability across NK\-cell sources and preparation conditions requires further validation\. Importantly, recent advances in engineering strategies—including parental\-cell priming, cargo loading, antigen\-recognition engineering, tissue/barrier\-targeting modification, biomimetic nanovesicle construction, and formulation\-based stabilization\. These approaches can alter cargo composition, recipient\-cell recognition, or tissue\-specific delivery through distinct mechanisms\. Improvements achieved by engineered composite platforms should therefore not be conflated with intrinsic properties of native NK\-EVs\. Nevertheless, major translational challenges remain, including scalable manufacturing, purification and characterization standardization, mechanism\-linked potency testing, biodistribution uncertainty, batch comparability, and product\-specific regulatory considerations\. Overall, NK\-EVs should be viewed not only as potential cell\-free therapeutic tools but also as specialized mediators of tumor–immune communication that integrate cytotoxic protein transfer, receptor\-mediated signaling, RNA\-based regulation, and microenvironmental crosstalk\.
