# From Transfer Probes to Expert Grouping: Evidence from LoRA Post\-Training

**Autores:** Xueyang Wu

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

A platform that post\-trains one base model for many business lines often has more lines than experts it can maintain, so lines must share experts, yet lines seldom come with a taxonomy saying which should share\. We ask whether transfer probes \(train on one source, measure paired changes on all sources\) can discover a grouping when the standard one is unknown\. To test probes against a reference, we use seven public sources that do have a label tree \(five mathematics subfields and two kinds of programming, standing in for business lines\) and treat the tree&\#x27;s grouping as a label reference that the evaluator holds and the method does not\. With four LoRA experts under fixed per\-source exposure and per\-expert budgets, we compare a purely probe\-derived grouping and an uninformed uniform default \(whose tied routing serves every line from one expert\) against this reference\. Trained once for one interval from a shared warm\-up state and routed by a rule fixed by each allocation, neither reached the prespecified practical gain of 2 percentage points, and both had lower overall utility than the reference \($\-2\.43$ and $\-2\.54$ pp; 95\\% intervals below zero\)\. In a post hoc comparison, the probe\-derived grouping was indistinguishable from the uniform default \($\+0\.11$ pp, $\[\-1\.25,\+1\.54\]$\)\. In fixed\-budget LoRA post\-training, a grouping built from initial\-state single\-source probes thus did not deliver the advantage predicted from the probes once carried to a later training state, a finite budget, and specified routing\.
