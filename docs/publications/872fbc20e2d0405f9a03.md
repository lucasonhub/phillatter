# Physical Artificial Intelligence and Differentiable Physics: Sim\-to\-Real Transfer, Whole\-Body Humanoid Locomotion, and Tactile Manipulation

**Autores:** Jangid Krishna; Nikhil Kumar Lakhera; Mukesh Mishra; Vishal Shrivastava; Dr\. Ashok Kajla

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract—Physical Artificial Intelligence \(Physical AI\) represents a transformative frontier in autonomous cyber\-physical robotics, shifting machine intelligence from disembodied algorithmic computation into dynamical systems that perceive, reason, and actuate directly within the physical world\. While pure reinforcement learning \(RL\) and kinematic motion planners excel in controlled, isolated environments, deploying high\-degree\-of\-freedom \(DoF\) bipedal humanoids and dexterous anthropomorphic hands in real\-world unstructured settings introduces severe physical and computational bottlenecks: non\-smooth contact mechanics, extreme sim\-to\-real transfer gaps, high\-dimensional non\-convex hybrid dynamics, and closed\-loop motor bandwidth constraints\. Differentiable Physics engines—which compute exact analytical state and contact gradients through forward simulation—alongside massively parallel GPU\-accelerated domain randomization, establish a foundational computational methodology to bridge this chasm\. This paper delivers an exhaustive, mathematically formal technical investigation and empirical benchmark of Physical AI for whole\-body humanoid locomotion and dexterous tactile manipulation\. We formalize the non\-linear hybrid dynamical equations of underactuated bipedal contact, derive gradient backpropagation through Linear Complementarity Problem \(LCP\) friction cones, and model domain randomization over non\-stationary physical distribution manifolds\. Furthermore, we construct an empirical robotic evaluation testbed comprising full\-scale bipedal humanoid platforms \(Unitree H1, Fourier GR\-1\) and high\-density optical tactile arrays \(GelSight Svelte\) evaluated across 800 challenging trials spanning uneven terrain locomotion, dynamic external impulse rejection, and blind in\-hand object re\-orientation\. Our empirical findings demonstrate that differentiable physics\-guided policy search achieves an 88\.6% zero\-shot sim\-to\-real locomotion transfer success rate, reducing training sample complexity by 4\.2x over model\-free PPO while bounding tracking error under 120 N external lateral impulses\. Concurrently, high\-frequency optical tactile feedback raises blind in\-hand manipulation success by 54\.2% over vision\-only baselines\. Finally, we formulate open challenges in non\-rigid soft body contact differentiability, whole\-body tactile\-semantic foundation models, and real\-time safety barrier certificates\. Index Terms—Physical AI, Differentiable Physics, Sim\-to\-Real Transfer, Humanoid Locomotion, Whole\-Body Control \(WBC\), Tactile Manipulation, Linear Complementarity Problem \(LCP\), Domain Randomization\.
