# Articulation Upper Bounds for Language Models

**Autores:** Alexander Spangher; Daniel E\. Ho; Diyi Yang; Noah Goodman; Sanmi Koyejo

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Language captures only a subset of the perceptions, intuitions and intents that form meaning between speakers\. What, then, are the limits of language? We take steps towards measuring this in LLMs as follows: for a set of concepts, we measure how well each concept can be transmitted between two LLMs as their capabilities scale\. This requires novel measurement tools: \(1\) optimal messages: we introduce a prompt optimization algorithm that beats existing algorithms by up to 14% on benchmarks and accurately estimates prompt upper bounds\. \(2\) A transmission measure: the sender is asked to label text; the receiver then tries to recover the sender&\#x27;s prompt, and recovery is measured; we find this correlates strongly with human label error\. With these measures, we use observational scaling laws to study how transmissibility of a concept, conveyed with optimal messages, increases as transmitter and receiver capacity grows: does it asymptote below or above a threshold? Measuring 1\.2k concepts in eight domains, we find concepts that \(a\) can be fully articulated; these concepts usually depend on local patterns, e\.g\. &quot;joke ends with surprise&quot;; \(b\) might be articulated by future, stronger models; these rely on whole\-text structural patterns, e\.g\. &quot;story&\#x27;s worldbuilding coherence&quot;; \(c\) concepts that face articulability limits; these concepts&\#x27; meaning, we find, is tied to identity and social\-group awareness, e\.g\. &quot;comic voice&quot;\. Interesting, these same concepts are not all challenging for humans: humans have moderate\-to\-high levels of agreement in 76% of concepts that LLMs struggle with; articulation limits, we find, are executor relative\. Finally, we characterize the nature of improvement in each case: while recoverable concepts can be articulated through definitions and explanations, irrecoverable concepts cannot be transmitted, even with examples, and might require novel learning mechanisms\.
