# ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE GOVERNANCE READINESS AND RESPONSIBLE ADOPTION AMONG HEALTH ADMINISTRATION AND HEALTH INFORMATION PROFESSIONALS: A MULTICENTER CROSS\-SECTIONAL STUDY

**Autores:** Fawaz Mofareh N Gohal, Abdullah Ai thamari, Riyadh\.T\. AlRabeeah and Moudhi Hashim Alabdulrahman

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Health systems are adopting artificial intelligence \(AI\) faster than many organizations are developing the policies, workforce capability, data controls, and monitoring processes needed to govern it safely\. Health administration and health information professionals are central to procurement, data stewardship, workflow design, quality oversight, and implementation, yet their readiness for responsible AI adoption remains underexamined\. To propose and test an analysis framework for assessing AI governance readiness and responsible adoption among health administration and health information/informatics professionals across multiple Saudi healthcare organizations\. A multicenter cross\-sectional survey design was specified for six coded healthcare institutions across three Saudi regions\. The proposed 31\-item instrument covers governance readiness, data and technical readiness, workforce AI capability, responsible AI climate, perceived value, and responsible adoption\. Because field data have not yet been collected, a synthetic dataset of 432 complete records was generated solely to test scoring, reliability, tables, figures, and regression procedures\. Analyses included frequencies, means with 95% confidence intervals \(CIs\), Cronbach alpha, group comparisons, Spearman correlations, and multivariable linear regression with center fixed effects and HC3 robust standard errors\. In the sample, 241 \(55\.8%\) records represented health administration professionals and 191 \(44\.2%\) represented health information/informatics professionals\. Proposed high governance readiness was observed in 119 \(27\.5%\) records\. Mean scores ranged from 2\.97 \(SD 0\.75\) for governance readiness to 3\.45 \(SD 0\.80\) for perceived value; Cronbach alpha ranged from 0\.80 to 0\.85\. Health information/informatics records had higher workforce capability \(3\.46 vs 3\.20; p=0\.001\), responsible adoption \(3\.45 vs 3\.28; p=0\.031\), and overall readiness \(3\.18 vs 3\.07; p=0\.037\)\. In the adjusted model, responsible AI climate \(B=0\.204 per SD, p&lt;0\.001\), workforce capability \(B=0\.167, p&lt;0\.001\), perceived value \(B=0\.159, p&lt;0\.001\), governance readiness \(B=0\.133, p=0\.001\), data and technical readiness \(B=0\.079, p=0\.020\), and prior AI use \(B=0\.159, p=0\.019\) were positively associated with responsible adoption\. Adjusted R2 was 0\.425\. The prototype demonstrates a coherent way to measure and analyze AI governance readiness in the healthcare administrative and information workforce\. The simulated pattern suggests a potentially important gap between perceived AI value and operational governance capacity, but no prevalence or association should be interpreted as an empirical finding\. Real\-world content validation, pilot testing, ethics approval, and multicenter data collection are required before scientific submission\.
