# EXPERIMENTAL INVESTIGATION OF WINGTIP\-VORTEX DECAY OVER A NACA 63\-209 AEROFOIL WITH WINGLET AND MORPHING DEVICES

**Autores:** MUYIWA O\. ATOYEBI; Olumide Adewole Towoju; Olawale Sayid Ismail; Ademola Adebukola Dare

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

An aerofoil’s performance is frequently constrained by excessive drag and disruptions produced during takeoff\. By lowering the Diffusion Length \(DL\) and Decay Time \(DT\) of the aerofoil’s related wake vortex, Add\-on Devices \(ADs\) such tip fences and tip sails have been investigated to overcome these constraints\. However, the literature’s earlier data on DL and DT was not enough\. This work aimed to assess the performance of an aerofoil fitted with Winglet, and Morphing Wingtip Add\-on Devices\. An aerofoil NACA 63\-209 with two ADs was employed in the experiment, while Aerofoil Without Add\-on \(AWA\) served as the control\. Whitcomb winglet \(Ww\) and Combined Winglet and Morphing \(CWM\) were the ADs under investigation\. The experiment was conducted under standard fluid flow take\-off circumstances with an aerofoil Angle of Attack \(AOA\) of 1°, 2°, 3°, and 4° with a Reynolds number of 8,997,222\. The measured values of pressure and velocity were used to determine the values of DL and DT\. At an AOA of 4°, the experimental DLs for Ww, CWM, and AWA were 16335\.00±0\.67, 9284\.60±0\.67, and 17028\.80±0\.67 m, respectively\. Similarly, at an AOA of 4°, the experimental DTs for Ww, CWM, and AWA were 225±0\.67, 130±0\.67, and 232\.00±0\.67 s, respectively\. At every AOA analysed, the aerofoil with CWM had the lowest DL and DT, indicating that it has the least tendency to create excessive drag and disturb its neighbours\. The aerofoil equipped with the Combined Winglet and Morphing \(CWM\) add\-on device performed best in alleviating wake vortex effect during takeoff\.
