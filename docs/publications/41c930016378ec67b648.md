# Violence against men in intimate partnerships in Dédougou, Burkina Faso: reported experiences, forms, and the culture of silence

**Autores:** Yacouba TENGUERI

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Domestic violence research has largely focused on women as victims, leaving male victimisation under\-recognised\. This study examines the prevalence, forms, and consequences of domestic violence against men in Dédougou, Burkina Faso\. Methods A mixed\-methods design combined a KoboToolbox survey of 598 men with a current or past intimate relationship with 26 semi\-structured interviews with male victims, women, religious and customary leaders, law enforcement, the judiciary, and social services \(June–July 2025\)\. The survey used non\-probability convenience sampling, so the sample is not statistically representative of men in Dédougou or in Burkina Faso\. Results 64\.55% of surveyed participants reported experiencing at least one form of violence, most often verbal \(49\.50%\), psychological \(24\.58%\), economic \(21\.74%\), sexual \(11\.54%\), or physical \(9\.20%\)\. The principal factors reported by respondents were economic difficulties \(59\.20%\) and jealousy or infidelity \(52\.51%\)\. Only 0\.78% of those reporting violence sought help from law enforcement, while 43\.26% stayed silent\. Consequences included anxiety \(45\.65%\), low self\-esteem \(31\.27%\), and social isolation \(25\.59%\)\. Conclusion Domestic violence against men is common but under\-recognised in Burkina Faso\. Findings call for inclusive policies, formal recognition, support services, and awareness campaigns, without diminishing the gravity of violence against women\.
