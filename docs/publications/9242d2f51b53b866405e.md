# MASTERING THE MATH MINDSET: NAVIGATING THE GAP BETWEEN WHAT TEACHERS KNOW AND WHAT THEY TEACH

**Autores:** Nurideen Yakubu Gbegini; Mohammed Yikehere Marifa; Bismark Basigi; Patrick Alade; Johnson Kwadwo Ankomah; Daniel Kudjo Adiku; Collins Oteng; Dennis Offei Kwakye

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In Ghana, Senior High School mathematics curriculum standards strongly advocate for inquiry\-based and problem\-centered pedagogies\. However, empirical evidence regarding how teachers’ pedagogical knowledge translates into classroom practice remains scarce, particularly in marginalized administrative regions\. This study investigated the self\-reported knowledge levels, classroom instructional practices, and the theory\-practice alignment amongst public SHS mathematics teachers in the Wa Municipality of the Upper West Region, Ghana\. Adopting a quantitative descriptive survey design, data were collected from N = 76 mathematics teachers using a self\-developed structured questionnaire\. Descriptive statistics \(means and standard deviations\) and inferential statistics \(paired\-samples t\-test\) were utilized for data analysis\. Findings revealed high overall self\-reported knowledge \(M = 3\.27, SD = 0\.72\) and implementation levels \(M = 3\.23, SD = 0\.70\)\. Teachers reported highest competence in assessing learner reasoning processes \(M = 3\.49\) and selecting real\-world problems \(M = 3\.34\) but demonstrated lower scores in theoretical underpinnings \(M = 3\.11\) and providing extension activities \(M = 3\.17\)\. The paired\-samples t\-test indicated no statistically significant difference between teachers’ perceived knowledge and instructional practices, t\(70\) = 0\.34, p = \.738, d = 0\.06, revealing strong self\-efficacy alignment\. Continuous professional development should focus on strengthening foundational theoretical frameworks and diagnostic formative assessment strategies\.
