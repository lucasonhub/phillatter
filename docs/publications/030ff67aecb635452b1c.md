# Erwin, Richard: Shadow Phenomena: \_A Provisional Classification Framework for Evidenced but Unresolved Phenomena\_

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Some phenomena are known principally through their manifestations or effects rather than through direct access to their underlying nature\. This creates a classificatory problem when a newly observed phenomenon meaningfully corresponds to a recognized phenomenon but available evidence remains insufficient to determine whether the two should be classified as the same kind\. Existing terminology often handles such cases poorly\. Terms such as “apparent”, “simulated”, “quasi\-”, and “as\-if” may implicitly weaken or prejudge the phenomenon, while unqualified terminology may imply an identity that has not been established\. This paper proposes shadow phenomenon as a provisional holding classification for such cases\. A shadow phenomenon is an evidentially supported phenomenon, known principally through its manifestations or effects, that meaningfully corresponds to a recognized phenomenon, while important aspects of its nature remain insufficiently understood to determine whether the two phenomena are sufficiently close to be classified as the same kind\. The designation identifies uncertainty about classification, not uncertainty about whether the observed phenomenon exists, and does not imply that the phenomenon is lesser, derivative, simulated, or unreal\. The framework is particularly applicable to unresolved questions concerning digital intelligence, including emotion, sensation, pain, and consciousness, but is not limited to them\. Its purpose is not to settle disputed classifications, but to provide a disciplined vocabulary for acknowledging evidenced phenomena while leaving their ultimate classification genuinely open\.
