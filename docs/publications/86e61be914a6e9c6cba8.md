# Effects of high\-intensity interval training on negative affect in adolescents with obesity: an exploratory analysis of the role of self\-efficacy

**Autores:** Shihao Wang; Chang Liu; Yali Xu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective To investigate the effects of an 8\-week school\-based high\-intensity interval training \(HIIT\) program on body composition, cardiorespiratory fitness, negative affect, and self\-efficacy in adolescents with obesity, and to explore whether self\-efficacy statistically mediates the association between HIIT and negative affect\. Methods In this two\-arm, parallel\-group, randomized controlled trial, 55 adolescents with obesity \(age 13\.55 ± 1\.21 years; BMI 29\.58 ± 3\.54 kg/m 2 \) were randomly assigned to an experimental group \(HIIT, n = 27\) or a control group \(regular physical education, n = 28\)\. Body composition, estimated maximal oxygen uptake \(VO₂max\), negative affect \(PANAS\), and self\-efficacy \(GSES\) were assessed before and after the 8\-week intervention\. The primary analyses were analyses of covariance \(ANCOVA\) with the post\-intervention value as the dependent variable, group as the fixed factor, and the corresponding baseline value as the covariate; two\-way repeated\-measures ANOVAs \(group×time\) served as sensitivity analyses\. An exploratory mediation analysis with 5,000 bias\-corrected bootstrap resamples adjusted for baseline mediator, baseline outcome, sex, and age\. Results After adjustment for baseline values, the experimental group showed significantly lower post\-intervention negative affect than the control group \(adjusted difference −3\.72, 95% CI \[−4\.81, −2\.63\], p &lt; 0\.001, partial η 2 = 0\.475\) and significantly higher self\-efficacy \(adjusted difference 1\.06, 95% CI \[0\.60, 1\.53\], p &lt; 0\.001, partial η 2 = 0\.290\)\. Body weight, BMI, visceral fat mass, subcutaneous fat mass, body fat percentage, and fat mass were also significantly lower in the experimental group \(all p &lt; 0\.001\), and estimated VO₂max was higher \(adjusted difference 1\.54 mL/kg/min, 95% CI \[0\.86, 2\.22\], p &lt; 0\.001\); the latter effect was attenuated but remained significant when post\-intervention estimated VO₂max was recomputed with baseline BMI held constant \(0\.50, 95% CI \[0\.03, 0\.98\], p = 0\.040\), and was corroborated by the BMI\-free 20\-m shuttle\-run lap count \( p = 0\.004\)\. The exploratory mediation analysis did not support an indirect effect through self\-efficacy \(a × b = 0\.09, bootstrap 95% CI \[−0\.75, 0\.82\]\)\. Conclusion Eight weeks of school\-based HIIT improved body composition and estimated cardiorespiratory fitness, reduced negative affect, and enhanced self\-efficacy in adolescents with obesity\. The indirect effect through self\-efficacy was not statistically supported after baseline adjustment; because the mediator and the outcome were measured concurrently, temporal ordering cannot be established, and the mediating role of self\-efficacy remains an open question for longitudinal research\.
