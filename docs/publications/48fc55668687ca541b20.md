# Naipal Singh, Sirohi: Is Our World Really Colored? Part 1\. A Philosophical Inquiry into Color, Consciousness, and Reality\.

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Color is one of the most familiar features of human experience, yet it remains one of the most philosophically challenging\. Contemporary physics describes visible light as electromagnetic radiation of different wavelengths, while neuroscience explains that the retina converts these wavelengths into electrochemical signals that are processed by the brain\. This scientific framework successfully explains how visual information is transmitted and processed\. However, it leaves an important philosophical question unresolved: if the external world contains only wavelengths and neural processing consists only of electrochemical activity, where does the subjective experience of redness, blueness, or any other color originate? This question represents a particular expression of the Hard Problem of Consciousness\. While neuroscience explains the mechanisms of visual processing, it does not yet fully explain why or how these physical processes are accompanied by qualitative subjective experience \(qualia\)\. This paper examines that explanatory gap and argues that the relationship between physical information and conscious experience remains philosophically open\. The paper does not challenge the empirical findings of modern science\. Rather, it explores whether conscious perception should be understood merely as an internal representation constructed by the brain or as a mode of access to aspects of reality that are not yet completely described by current physical theories\. To investigate this question, the paper develops a comparative philosophical dialogue between contemporary consciousness studies and Advaita Vedānta\. The objective is not to equate these two traditions nor to claim that one validates the other\. Instead, the paper argues that both raise a common epistemological concern: whether immediate perceptual experience can be identified with ultimate reality\. By placing modern philosophy of mind and Advaita Vedānta into constructive dialogue, this study seeks to clarify both the possibilities and the limits of current explanations of conscious experience\.
