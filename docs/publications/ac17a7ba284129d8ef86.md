# Dynamic reconfigurable cylindrical vector beam demultiplexing using a multilevel optimized vortex\-Dammann grating

**Autores:** Juncheng Fang; Ying Li; Ting Lei; Xiao\-Cong Yuan

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Mode\-division multiplexing boosts fiber capacity density, but static demultiplexers limit dynamic reconfigurability\. We propose a dynamically reconfigurable cylindrical vector beam \(CVB\) demultiplexing device using a liquid\-crystal variable retarder \(LCVR\) and vortex\-Dammann gratings\. CVBs are decomposed into left\-handed circularly polarized \(LCP\) and right\-handed circularly polarized \(RCP\) orbital angular momentum \(OAM\) components; the gratings with a Fresnel lens phase profile selectively demultiplex them\. The LCVR enables reconfigurable focusing of the LCP and RCP components of the same\-order CVB to distinct positions\. A cosine annealing algorithm converts the Dammann grating phase from binary to ternary \(0,π/2,π\), improving diffraction efficiency by ∼14% points while preserving uniformity across diffraction orders\. Experiments show a 40 ms dynamic response, reconfigurable demultiplexing of CVB orders from −4 to \+4, and crosstalk below −11 dB\. In the communication demo, dynamic multiplexing of CVBs with orders m=−1 and m=\+4, each carrying a 10 Gbps on\-off keying \(OOK\) signal, yields bit error rates below the forward error correction threshold\. This reconfigurable CVB optical interconnect technology shows strong potential for data centers, high\-performance computing, and artificial intelligence clusters\.
