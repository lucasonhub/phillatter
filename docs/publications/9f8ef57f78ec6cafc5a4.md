# Boether, Olivier: Barrett’s Error: The Theory of Constructed Emotion and the Foreclosure of the Philosophical Self

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Lisa Feldman Barrett’s theory of constructed emotion builds the human being from the body upward: interoceptive prediction, concept\-driven categorization, and the regulation of the body budget \(allostasis\) together construct emotion, perception, and the self\. This article argues that the theory, however powerful as an account of how experience is assembled, forecloses the philosophical self \(Φ\) — the normative, meaning\-making, value\-determining faculty\. Two results are proven\. The Categorization Gap Theorem shows that no sequence of predictive categorizations and free\-energy minimizations entails any conclusion about worth, because the descriptive is conservative over the normative \(Hume’s Law; Moore’s open question\); even the “value” optimized in the free\-energy framework denotes expected reward, not axiological worth\. The Minimal Completion Theorem shows that the philosophical self is the necessary, sufficient, minimal, and unique repair of this incompleteness\. The paper distinguishes Barrett’s foreclosure from Damasio’s: where Damasio omits Φ by silence, Barrett forecloses it by absorption, importing the vocabulary of Φ — concept, meaning, construction — and redefining each as a psychological \(Ψ\) operation\. The constructed self is thereby shown to be Damasio’s autobiographical self generalized to the whole person, and the philosophical self is identified as the faculty that judges and re\-authors what the brain has built\. The correction is consilient, not eliminative: it restores value\-determination to the center of human becoming while preserving Barrett’s constructionist neuroscience\.
