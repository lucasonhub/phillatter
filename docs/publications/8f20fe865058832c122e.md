# Topology\-aware and constraint\-guided deep reinforcement learning for climate\-resilient control of urban stormwater systems

**Autores:** Kaiyu Jing; Mohammed Abubakor Baljon; Ghanshyam G\. Tejani

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Urban stormwater systems are increasingly challenged by extreme precipitation events, leading to hydraulic surcharge when system capacity is exceeded\. Traditional rule\-based control \(RBC\) is reactive, whereas standard deep reinforcement learning \(DRL\) does not explicitly exploit drainage‐network topology or ensure physically consistent control\. This study introduces Constraint\-guided Graph\-PPO \(PIG‐PPO\), a topology‐aware reinforcement learning framework designed to improve stormwater control under extreme precipitation conditions\. Methods PIG‐PPO integrates a graph convolutional network \(GCN\) state encoder with proximal policy optimization \(PPO\)\. A soft mass\-balance residual penalty is incorporated into the reward function to encourage physically consistent control\. The framework was trained on 20 moderate design storms and evaluated on 30 held‐out extreme storms and 20 long‐duration monsoon‐like events\. Performance was assessed across 10 independent PPO seeds, with comparisons against RBC and a topology‐agnostic MLP‐PPO ablation\. Few‐shot adaptation, cross‐topology validation using a second PyStorms scenario after retraining, and parameter‐count analysis were also conducted\. Results Across 10 independent PPO seeds, PIG‐PPO reduced mean overflow by 31\.2% relative to RBC and reduced the control‐effort metric by 78\.3% relative to the MLP‐PPO ablation, with Holm‐adjusted p‐values below 0\.001\. Few‐shot adaptation reduced overflow during monsoon\-like events by 58\.8% relative to zero‐shot transfer\. Parameter‐count analysis demonstrated a 106\.69\-fold encoder‐size advantage over a flat MLP at 1,000 nodes\. However, this result reflects parameter efficiency rather than direct validation at city scale\. Discussion The results indicate that topology‐aware state encoding and constraint\-guided reward shaping can improve stormwater control performance and support adaptation to previously unseen precipitation regimes\. Cross‐topology evaluation provides additional evidence of the framework’s applicability beyond the initial network configuration\. Nevertheless, the climate‐resilience implications are limited to performance under synthetic extreme precipitation regimes designed to emulate intensifying rainfall patterns associated with climate change; direct validation against observational climate\-projection datasets was not conducted\.
