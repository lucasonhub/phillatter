# Impact of supportive virtual characters on presence and pain perception

**Autores:** Isabel Neumann; Paul Pauli; Ivo Käthner

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction The benefit of social virtual characters is of particular interest in the context of digital health interventions\. The present study aimed to gain a deeper understanding of the social influence of virtual characters in the context of social pain modulation\. To this end, we investigated how virtual characters varying in perceived agency and perceived empathy impact the social influence on users in the context of social pain modulation\. Methods In this mixed design study, healthy participants \( N = 98\) were immersed in a virtual laboratory and underwent three within conditions\. In two of these conditions, a virtual character supported the participants verbally immediately before heat pain stimulation\. In one of these support conditions, the virtual character was presented as empathic, in another as unempathetic\. The third within condition served as control condition\. In this condition, no virtual human was present, and no social support was provided\. The perceived agency of the virtual characters served as between\-subjects factor\. Participants in the avatar group were led to believe that another participant controlled the virtual characters\. Participants in the agent group were told that they interacted with a computer\. However, in both conditions, virtual characters were computer\-controlled\. Pain ratings, presence ratings, and psychophysiological measurements were recorded\. Results Participants reported a higher feeling of presence and social presence in conditions with virtual characters compared to the control condition\. The avatar group showed higher skin conductance levels than the agent group\. Virtual verbal support did not affect pain responses\. Conclusion The current study contributes to insights into the use of virtual characters in the context of social pain modulation\. However, future research is needed to unravel the direct effects of virtual social support on pain perception\.
