# Institutional policies for responsible generative AI use in Saudi higher education: a policy review and gap analysis of national\-to\-institutional implementation

**Autores:** Fouad Ahmed Atallah

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Saudi Arabia has established a comprehensive national\-level governance landscape for generative artificial intelligence through the Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority \(SDAIA\), including the AI Ethics Principles \(2023\), Generative AI Guidelines \(2024–2025\), the AI Adoption Framework, and the Saudi Academic Framework for AI Qualifications, alongside broader strategic directions under Vision 2030\. This study employed systematic documentary analysis combined with gap analysis to examine eight national\-level policy and strategic documents, focusing on governance principles, operational mechanisms, and their relevance to Saudi higher education\. The analysis reveals three critical findings: \(1\) national frameworks articulate detailed governance principles, organizational responsibilities, and operational guidance for responsible AI use, although the regulatory status and degree of prescriptiveness vary across documents; \(2\) national documents reference Islamic values and cultural alignment but generally lack explicit integration of Maqasid al\-Shariah principles into governance language or operational mechanisms; and \(3\) an institutional audit of 17 purposively selected Saudi universities identified publicly accessible formal AI governance policies or guidelines at six institutions, indicating substantial variation in the publicly documented institutional translation of national AI governance frameworks\. The findings suggest that translating national principles into operational institutional frameworks may benefit from three key components: higher\-education\-specific policy translation mechanisms, clearly defined accountability structures with appropriate monitoring indicators, and explicit consideration of Islamic ethical dimensions\. Documentary analysis combined with institutional policy auditing and gap analysis provides a systematic approach to identifying discontinuities between national governance frameworks and publicly documented institutional policy development\. The findings contribute to understanding AI governance challenges in Saudi higher education and highlight the importance of institutional\-level policy architecture for connecting national governance principles with educational practice\. The research also has implications for other Arab and Muslim\-majority higher education systems seeking to align emerging global AI governance standards with local Islamic and cultural values\.
