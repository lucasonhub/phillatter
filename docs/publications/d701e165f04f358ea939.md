# Performance of smooth cayenne pineapple under tropical conditions: unravelling effects of postharvest duration and ripening stage

**Autores:** Kalolaini Koto Colaitiniyara; Shamal Shasang Kumar; Owais Ali Wani; Savenaca Cuquma; Mohammadi Begum Jeelani; Nouf Abdulrahman Alqahtani; Qasem Al\-mdallal

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Optimizing harvest maturity and postharvest handling is important for maintaining pineapple quality and reducing losses under tropical storage conditions\. However, limited information is available on how ripening stage at harvest interacts with postharvest storage duration to influence physical and quality characteristics of Smooth Cayenne pineapple under ambient conditions in Fiji\. Methods This study evaluated the effects of four ripening stages and postharvest storage duration on fruit weight, crown weight, fruit dimensions, and °Brix of Ananas comosus \(Smooth Cayenne\) pineapple stored under ambient conditions at 27 ± 2 °C and 85 ± 5% RH\. Forty fruits were assigned to four ripening stages: S1 \(25%\), S2 \(50%\), S3 \(75%\), and S4 \(100%\), with 10 fruits per stage\. The same fruits were repeatedly evaluated from Day 0 to Day 14\. Although the estimated shelf life of some treatments ended by Day 5, observations were continued to document the progression of quality deterioration during extended ambient storage\. Results and Discussion At S1, total fruit weight and fruit weight reached their highest recorded means on Day 6 \(2\.42 and 2\.10 kg, respectively\), while °Brix varied across storage days, with relatively higher values observed on Days 5 and 9\. At S2, the highest fruit weights occurred early in storage, whereas °Brix increased during the later observation period and reached 17\.00°Brix on Day 12\. At S3, fruit weight and dimensions were greatest around Day 3 and subsequently declined, while °Brix remained relatively stable\. At S4, fruit weight, dimensions, crown weight, and °Brix showed comparatively minor fluctuations during storage\. Postharvest shelf life decreased with advancing ripening stage, with S1 fruits showing the longest storability and S2, S3, and S4 fruits exhibiting shorter storage periods under ambient conditions\. Earlier\-harvested fruits provided greater storage flexibility, whereas more advanced ripening stages were associated with higher initial soluble\-solids concentrations but reduced postharvest storability\. These findings demonstrate the importance of matching harvest maturity with intended marketing and storage conditions to maintain pineapple quality and minimize postharvest losses\. Future research should evaluate larger fruit populations across multiple seasons and locations and incorporate additional quality indicators, including titratable acidity, firmness, weight loss, microbial deterioration, and physiological disorders, together with improved storage technologies for extending the postharvest life of pineapple under tropical conditions\.
