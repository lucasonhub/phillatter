# How generative AI becomes embedded in design learning: a 14\-instructional\-day intensive longitudinal study of reciprocal dynamics between AI use and self\-efficacy

**Autores:** Yang Li; Yi Yang; Silu Bao

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Generative AI is increasingly embedded in design education, yet little is known about how students’ daily engagement with generative AI is dynamically associated with their learning\-related capability beliefs across successive instructional days\. Drawing on self\-efficacy theory, this study examined the reciprocal next\-instructional\-day associations between daily generative AI use in design learning and daily design\-learning self\-efficacy, and further investigated whether baseline hassles were associated with individual differences in these dynamics\. A 14\-instructional\-day diary study was conducted with 260 Chinese undergraduate design students, yielding 3,558 observed person\-day assessments out of 3,640 possible assessments\. Daily generative AI use and design\-learning self\-efficacy were assessed throughout the diary period, whereas hassles were measured at baseline\. The data were analyzed using bivariate dynamic structural equation modeling within a Bayesian estimation framework\. The results showed significant autoregressive effects for daily generative AI use \( β = 0\.198, 95% CrI \[0\.154, 0\.237\]\) and daily design\-learning self\-efficacy \( β = 0\.138, 95% CrI \[0\.100, 0\.175\]\)\. At the within\-person level, higher\-than\-usual generative AI use on one instructional day positively predicted design\-learning self\-efficacy on the subsequent instructional day \( β = 0\.043, 95% CrI \[0\.006, 0\.077\]\), while higher\-than\-usual design\-learning self\-efficacy also positively predicted generative AI use on the subsequent instructional day \( β = 0\.059, 95% CrI \[0\.023, 0\.095\]\)\. At the between\-person level, greater baseline hassles were associated with higher average generative AI use \( β = 0\.135\), lower average design\-learning self\-efficacy \( β = −0\.254\), and a stronger cross\-lagged association from generative AI use to subsequent self\-efficacy \( β = 0\.458\)\. Gender did not significantly predict the main random effects\. These findings indicate modest reciprocal next\-instructional\-day associations between generative AI use and design\-learning self\-efficacy, suggesting that the two may reinforce one another across instructional days\. The stronger association from generative AI use to subsequent self\-efficacy among students reporting greater baseline hassles further suggests that contextual demands may shape how generative AI becomes integrated into students’ day\-to\-day design learning\.
