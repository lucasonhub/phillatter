# Garden soil‐based microbial fuel cell equipped with an amplification system coupled with a secondary PV ‐assisted module: Utilization as eco‐sustainable electrical power supply for carbon monoxide gas sensor

**Autores:** Mustapha Abdeldjabar Charef; Mohamed Benghernit; Mostéfa Kameche; Moncef Ati; Toufik Sahraoui; Christophe Innocent

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Autonomous environmental telemetry requires continuous, maintenance‐free power sources capable of driving commercial sensing electronics\. This study reports the development of a self‐powered bio‐electrochemical sensing platform utilizing a garden soil‐based microbial fuel cell \(s‐MFC\) coupled with a hybrid power management system \(PMS\) to drive a commercial off‐the‐shelf \(COTS\) carbon monoxide \(CO\) gas sensor\. Operating under a sub‐volt thermodynamic limit, the raw s‐MFC generated a steady‐state open‐circuit voltage of 0\.5 V, governed by the extracellular electron transfer of indigenous electroactive bacteria\. To overcome this voltage bottleneck and satisfy the 3\.0 V nominal operating threshold of the commercial sensor, an ultra‐low‐power DC/DC boost converter was engineered\. The PMS incorporates a high‐efficiency switching network driven by an operational amplifier and an NE555 timer configured in an astable multivibrator topology, synergistically integrated with a miniature photovoltaic \(PV\) solar module to sustain the active control loop and mitigate internal power overhead\. Continuous 15‐day galvanostatic testing demonstrated that the hybrid PMS successfully achieved a 6‐fold voltage amplification, delivering a regulated 3\.0 V rail to the gas‐diffusion sensor while yielding a 70% increase in overall energy harvesting efficiency\. These findings validate the scalability of soil bio‐electrochemical systems combined with hybrid solid‐state power conditioning to achieve long‐term energetic autonomy for terrestrial micro‐telemetry networks\.
