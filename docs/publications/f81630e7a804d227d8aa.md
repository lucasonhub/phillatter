# Frequency\-Step Cache: Separating Synchronization and Repair for Efficient Autoregressive Video Diffusion

**Autores:** Kerry Xu; Param Misra; Zachary Stenglein; Athri Karunamurthy; Fanjiang Ye; Yuke Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Autoregressive \(AR\) video generation enables long\-horizon synthesis but requires repeated forward passes through large diffusion transformers\. Training\-free caching reduces this cost by reusing intermediate computation, yet existing controllers often couple two decisions: when to synchronize the cache exactly and how much computation to repair between synchronizations\. We introduce Frequency\-Step Cache \(FSC\), which separates these roles\. FSC schedules exact full\-computation anchors on a dense\-to\-sparse trajectory, while a lightweight latent\-space risk based on frequency\-resolved drift and token age controls inter\-anchor repair through whole\-step replay, selective token recomputation, or full repair\. On SkyReels\-V2, whole\-step reuse provides most of the acceleration, while selective token computation is most effective as fidelity repair\. A controlled ablation shows that using one reactive signal for both synchronization and repair reduces fidelity at matched latency, with the same separation advantage observed on FramePack and Matrix\-Game\. Across multiple autoregressive video backbones, FSC improves speed\-quality trade\-offs\. On SkyReels\-V2, FSC\-Speed reaches 2\.36x speedup versus MotionCache&\#x27;s 2\.275x while improving SSIM by 0\.019 and PSNR by 0\.5 dB\. At MotionCache\-matched speed, FSC\-Quality improves SSIM by 0\.031 and PSNR by 1\.0 dB and lowers LPIPS by 0\.024\. Without retuning, FSC\-Quality transfers to SkyReels\-V2 14B at 2\.12x speedup versus 1\.79x for MotionCache\.
