# Task\-specific theta enhancement and domain\-general alpha/beta suppression as oscillatory signatures of cognitive flexibility

**Autores:** Mareike Hülsemann; Christoph Löffler; Anna‐Lena Schubert

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Cognitive flexibility\-the ability to adapt to changing situational or task demands\-is a fundamental aspect of human cognition and a potential source of individual differences in higher\-order cognitive abilities\. To further understand the neural mechanisms underlying this ability, we examined event\-related spectral perturbations \(ERSP\) in three cued switching tasks \(parity/magnitude, global/local, number/letter\) in a sample of 148 adults\. Employing a data\-driven approach that combined mass\-univariate time\-frequency analysis, cluster\-based permutation testing, and latent change structural equation modeling, we identified two robust neural signatures: a transient frontal theta increase and a sustained alpha/beta suppression\. Both effects were more pronounced in switch compared to repeat trials\. Frontal theta reflected task\-specific control processes, whereas parietal alpha/beta indexed domain\-general attentional processes that persisted from cue onset through target processing\-consistent with two\-stage models of task switching\. Notably, flexibility\-related alpha/beta dynamics did not correlate with measures of intelligence or working memory capacity\. However, stronger cue\-elicited alpha/beta suppression correlated with reduced non\-decision\-time switch costs, indicating more effective task preparation, underscoring that alpha/beta suppression captures a neurally coherent but behaviorally distinct dimension of cognitive flexibility\. These findings provide evidence that alpha/beta suppression constitutes a reliable, cross\-task generalizable neural signature of attentional reconfiguration during task switching, while highlighting the multi\-component nature of individual differences in cognitive flexibility\.
