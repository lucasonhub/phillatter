# Mahi, Sk Sabbir: The Great Cessation: A Policy and Philosophical Imperative for Halting AI Advancement

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper provides a transformation that seems too radical but eminent at the same time to overcome the sky\-high capabilities that we saw in our decade of artificial intelligence and thereby collapse the stability grid itself—the Great Suspension, or forbidden to do any further work on AI\. It criticizes the current AI ethical framework as inactive and vacant in meeting the sociopolitical risks of unbounded technical innova\- tion\. The paper also analyzes how AI is used for a particular power, thus creating a society where inequal\- ity is more clearly felt, mass surveillance and algorithmic oppression\. In the conclusion, by referencing of Karl Marx, Michel Foucault and Hannah Arendt, it suggests that AI serves universal goals by helping so\- called good rulers and international corporations this is under the cover of digital colonialism and obstruct\- ing democracy\. This paper argues that the current AI governance has no accountability: its non\-binding eth\- ical guidelines only help us to avoid effective regulation\. The Great Suspension—an immediate halt to all A\.I\. development\-\- perhaps excepting only where these technologies already exist and can be brought into use for the benefit of underdeveloped countries\. This entrains AI to restore its humanitarian purpose: that technology is to serve human society more than the big tech companies and their instruments of control\. This breaking point is proposed on the grounds that political prudence should dictate a balancing effect as well in society—bringing about co\-governance between human beings and &quot;things&quot; post\-technology\.
