# ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AND EARLY RECOGNITION OF CLINICAL DETERIORATION: NEW PERSPECTIVES FOR NURSING PRACTICE IN PATIENT SAFETY

**Autores:** Victor Lucas Dutra de Morais; Vinícius Luan Dutra de Morais; Allana Beatriz Silva Medeiros; Golda Meyr Ângelo de Lima Barbosa; Shirliane Romana Da Silva Barros; Raiane Patrícia Nascimento Guedes da Silva; Débora Ilana Silva de Oliveira

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Clinical deterioration in hospitalized patients may be preceded by progressive physiological changes that, when recognized in a timely manner, enable healthcare interventions before severe adverse events occur\. In this context, artificial intelligence \(AI\) has been investigated as a tool capable of analyzing large volumes of clinical data and identifying patterns associated with worsening health status\. This study aimed to analyze the evidence regarding the use of artificial intelligence for the early recognition of clinical deterioration, emphasizing its implications for nursing practice and patient safety\. A narrative literature review was conducted based on scientific studies addressing artificial intelligence, machine learning, early warning systems, and clinical deterioration in hospitalized patients\. The literature indicates that predictive models may increase the ability to identify patients at risk of deterioration, support care prioritization, and facilitate more timely clinical responses\. However, limitations related to algorithm heterogeneity, data quality and representativeness, external validation, interpretability, bias, privacy, and integration of alerts into clinical workflows were also identified\. For nursing, AI represents a complementary resource to clinical assessment and may strengthen surveillance and decision\-making when integrated with professional judgment and structured care processes\. It is concluded that the responsible incorporation of these technologies requires professional education, critical evaluation of AI systems, and preservation of the central role of human care\.
