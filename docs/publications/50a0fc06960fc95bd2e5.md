# Effect of Ti3C2Tx MXene content on the structural properties and optical band gap of PVA/PEG nanocomposite thin films

**Autores:** Joyanta Sonowal; Ankur Tanti; Profulla Chandra Kalita

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In this work, Ti 3 C 2 T x MXene was synthesized by selective chemical etching of the Ti 3 AlC 2 MAX phase and incorporated into a poly \(vinyl alcohol\) \(PVA\)/poly \(ethylene glycol\) \(PEG\) matrix to fabricate nanocomposite thin films with Ti 3 C 2 T x MXene loadings of 0\.5–1\.5 wt% by using a simple drop\-casting technique\. The structural, morphological, compositional, and optical characteristics of pure PVA/PEG and Ti 3 C 2 T x MXene/PVA/PEG nanocomposite thin films were systematically investigated using X\-ray diffraction \(XRD\), field\-emission scanning electron microscopy \(FESEM\), energy\-dispersive X\-ray spectroscopy \(EDX\), Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy \(FTIR\), X\-ray photoelectron spectroscopy \(XPS\), and UV–Vis spectroscopy\. Fourier transform infrared \(FTIR\) analysis revealed enhanced interfacial interactions between the polymer chains and the surface functional groups of Ti 3 C 2 T x MXenes\. XRD and field\-emission scanning electron microscopy \(FESEM\) results confirmed the successful formation of MXene and its incorporation into the polymer matrix\. The crystallinity index showed a non\-monotonic trend with Ti 3 C 2 T x MXene loading, reaching a maximum at 0\.5 wt% Ti 3 C 2 T x MXene with crystallinity indices of 48\.01% and 49\.87% as determined by XRD and FTIR, respectively, due to the heterogeneous nucleation effect of MXene nanosheets at low filler loading\. UV–Vis spectroscopy showed a gradual red shift of the absorption edge as the Ti 3 C 2 T x MXene concentration increased\. Both the direct and indirect optical band gap energies decreased from 5\.49 to 4\.98 eV and from 5\.17 to 3\.28 eV, respectively, as the Ti 3 C 2 T x MXene content increased from 0\.0 to 1\.5 wt%\. These findings suggest that Ti 3 C 2 T x MXene/PVA/PEG nanocomposite thin films are promising candidates for future investigation in optoelectronic, sensing, and energy\-related applications\.
