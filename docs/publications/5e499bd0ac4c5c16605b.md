# An Empirical Study on the Impact of Moral Interventions on Addictive Behavior and Psychological States in Mobile Applications

**Autores:** Jiuyuan Zhu; Yixin Yu; Yao Chen; Xuesong Li

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

INTRODUCTION: With the widespread use of intelligent mobile devices, addictive design in mobile applications has increasingly influenced users&\#x27; behavioral patterns and psychological well\-being\.OBJECTIVES: This study examines the effects of addictive interface design and the feasibility of ethical intervention through a three\-stage conceptual pathway of “addictive mechanisms–moral intervention–user response\.”METHODS: A five\-day controlled experiment with 72 participants compared a standard interface containing addictive mechanisms with an otherwise equivalent interface incorporating duration reminders, excessive\-reading prompts, and exit guidance\. Behavioral logs, GAD\-7 and short\-form SAS scores, a post\-experiment acceptance questionnaire, and semi\-structured interviews with three domain experts were used for triangulation\.RESULTS: After Holm–Bonferroni correction, the intervention group showed significantly shorter usage duration, lower scrolling frequency, and lower anxiety and dependency scores than the control group; the corresponding effects were moderate to large\. The intervention received a mean acceptance rating of 4\.2 out of 5\.CONCLUSION: Mild, non\-coercive interventions can reduce immersive use while preserving user acceptance, providing empirical and practical guidance for ethically responsible mobile interface design\.
