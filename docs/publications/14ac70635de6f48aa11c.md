# Robotic airship for measurement and validation of a CERN detector stray magnetic field

**Autores:** Francesco Mazzei; Paolo Francesco Scaramuzzino; Luca Bernardi; Fabio Curti; Corrado Gargiulo

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The detector caverns of the Large Hadron Collider \(LHC\) at the European Organization for Nuclear Research \(CERN\) are harsh environments for robotic operation: confined, without satellite navigation, close to fragile hardware, and permeated by intense stray magnetic fields that can disturb onboard electronics, actuators, and sensors\. Characterising these fields is a prerequisite for deploying robots in the caverns, and is also relevant to detector operation, since anomalous fringe fields can affect the surrounding infrastructure and particle tracking\. Yet conventional approaches based on fixed sensors, mechanical systems, or manual surveys provide only sparse spatial coverage\. This paper presents a robotic airship that combines a three\-axis Hall\-effect sensor with ArUco marker\-based visual localisation to measure the three\-dimensional stray field in the ALICE \(A Large Ion Collider Experiment\) detector cavern\. Two campaigns were conducted with the magnets at nominal current: static measurements at twenty known positions, isolating sensor accuracy from localisation uncertainty, and dynamic flights over the same instrumented region\. The measurements were compared with an electromagnetic simulation through a dedicated uncertainty\-propagation framework\. The simulation reproduces the transverse field components well \(R 2 = 0\.86–0\.98\), whereas the vertical component shows a lower agreement \(R 2 = 0\.75–0\.79\) and a systematic positive bias of 0\.65–1\.12 mT, more consistent with simplifications in the electromagnetic model than with measurement uncertainty\. These results demonstrate the feasibility of aerial robotic platforms for stray field characterisation in large experimental facilities and lay the groundwork for a fully autonomous survey system\.
