# A Multiphysics Model of Magnetically Enhanced ATP Hydrolysis in Ferromagnetic Nanosystems

**Autores:** Paata J\. Kervalishvili

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study develops a multiphysics framework for evaluating whether alternating magnetic fields \(AMF\) applied to Fe₃O₄ and MnFe₂O₄ nanoparticles can modify ATP hydrolysis through thermal, mechanical, and ion\-mediated pathways\. Rather than assigning a single phenomenological magnetic activation\-energy reduction to all effects, the model starts from Arrhenius/Eyring kinetics and derives the observed rate change as the sum of a temperature\-dependent contribution and explicitly defined non\-thermal perturbations\. The thermal term is constrained by the measured temperature history and nanoparticle specific absorption rate \(SAR\); the mechanical term is related to magnetic torque, hydrodynamic damping, and experimentally measurable particle motion; and the catalytic term is expressed through independently measurable Fe²⁺/Mn²⁺ concentrations and dose\-response parameters\. The available experimental observations reported in the original manuscript—2\.5–3\-fold enhancement of non\-enzymatic hydrolysis under nanoparticle/AMF conditions, approximately 40% enhancement of Na⁺/K⁺\-ATPase activity under stated isothermal conditions, and Raman/FTIR changes in phosphate\-associated bands—are retained, but their interpretation is deliberately narrowed\. In particular, the present data support rate enhancement under specified conditions and are consistent with multiple mechanisms; they do not, by themselves, establish a unique mechanical mechanism, nanoscale temperature gradients, or synergistic coupling\. A mechanism\-separation experimental matrix is therefore defined, together with quantitative spectroscopic observables and statistical requirements\.
