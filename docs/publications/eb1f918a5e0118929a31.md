# AI\-Driven Object Detection for Projectile Localization on Trauma X\-rays: An Analytical Study of Diagnostic Accuracy and Forensic Utility

**Autores:** Balamanikandan S; Vijayakumar Nair; Thumma Amar; Srimukesh A

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Trauma radiographs may reveal retained bullets or metallic fragments, and early localization can influence further imaging, surgical planning, safety assessment, and forensic documentation\. Methods: This feasibility study used a fully synthetic, computer\-generated tabular dataset of 400 case\-level records divided into 240 training\-design records, 60 validation\-design records, and a 100\-record held\-out test set\. The 400 records were not pixel\-level radiographs: no DICOM, PNG, or other X\-ray images were generated for model training, and no YOLOv9 neural network was trained\. Instead, ground\-truth labels, confidence scores, simulated detector outputs, a simulated time\-constrained radiologist first pass, object\-level matches, and processing times were produced by a fixed\-seed probabilistic simulation \(seed 9173\) to demonstrate an evaluation framework\. The test set comprised 70 projectile\-positive and 30 projectile\-negative records\. Results: The simulated AI output yielded 65 true positives, 5 false negatives, 3 false positives, and 27 true negatives, corresponding to 92\.0% accuracy, 92\.9% sensitivity, 90\.0% specificity, 95\.6% precision, and a 94\.2% F1 score; ROC\-AUC was 0\.972\. The simulated radiologist first pass was correct in 83\.0% of test records\. In paired correctness analysis, AI alone was correct in 12 records and the radiologist alone in 3 \(exact McNemar p=0\.035\)\. Of 145 synthetic annotated fragments, 136 were matched, with mean IoU 0\.828, mAP@0\.50=0\.937, and mAP@0\.50:0\.95=0\.593\. Conclusion: These values are simulation outputs and do not constitute evidence that YOLOv9 detects projectiles on patient radiographs\. The study demonstrates a reproducible analysis plan that requires validation on authentic multicentre radiographs, independent annotation, external testing, calibration, human\-factors evaluation, and forensic governance before any clinical use\.
