# FROM AIOPS TO AGENTOPS: SERVICE\-LEVEL OBJECTIVES AND ERROR\-BUDGET\-GOVERNED AUTONOMY FOR PRODUCTION AI AGENTS

**Autores:** Adedamola Abiodun Solanke

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Large language model \(LLM\) agents now run production workflows, including IT and network operations, yet they are usually monitored with signals designed for deterministic services: availability, latency, and error rate\. An agent can satisfy all three while completing the wrong task\. This paper extends AIOps\-style service management to agentic systems\. It contributes a layered set of service\-level indicators \(SLIs\) covering platform health, step\-level tool use, verified task outcomes, and guardrail integrity; a taxonomy of seven agent failure classes mapped to the signals that expose them; and an error\-budget policy that governs an agent&\#x27;s autonomy as well as its release cadence\. A Monte Carlo simulation of a production operations agent evaluates the approach across 2,100 injected incidents\. Conventional platform monitoring detected only platform failures, firing in at most 1\.7% of runs for the other six classes\. Burn\-rate alerting on verified task outcomes detected every class in 89\.7% to 100% of runs within 24 hours, with no false alarms, but took a median of 3\.5 to 10\.6 hours under standard thresholds\. A change\-aware rule, active for six hours after a deployment or model change, cut median detection of change\-induced regressions to 44 to 63 minutes at a cost of about one additional page per month\. The results support layered, outcome\-based service\-level objectives as the operational basis for production agents\.
