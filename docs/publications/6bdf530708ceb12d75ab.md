# A Hybrid Deep\-Learning and Impedance\-Based Scheme for Fault Classification and Location in Transmission Lines: A Physics\-Validated, Leakage\-Free Evaluation

**Autores:** Abdulla Habbib; Omar Ibrahim; Mohammed Majeed

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Deep\-learning fault classifiers and locators for transmission lines are often evaluated on unvalidated simulations, with possible data leakage and without comparison against the impedance\-based methods used in practice\. This study builds an exact time\-domain simulator of a 400 kV, 200 km two\-source line, whose fault currents agree with symmetrical\-component calculations within 2%, and generates 22,600 independent scenarios covering ten fault types, fault resistance, location, inception angle, loading and source strength\. Single\-ended 25 ms windows sampled at 4 kHz were used to train one\-dimensional convolutional neural networks \(CNNs\), which were compared with feature\-based classifiers and with the reactance and Takagi locators\. The CNN classified faults with 99\.91% accuracy in 0\.4 ms per window, but feature\-based classifiers were equally accurate, and all learned classifiers failed for fault resistances beyond the training range until that range was included in training, which in turn increased false alarms under strong noise\. CNN regression located faults with a mean absolute error of 5\.81 km, better than other learned models but far worse than the Takagi method on the third post\-fault cycle\. A hybrid scheme, in which the CNN supplies the fault type and the Takagi method the distance, reduced the error to 1\.40 km, located 93\.8% of faults within 2% of the line length, and remained accurate for weak sources, noise and high fault resistance\. Deep learning is thus best used for fast classification, and physics\-based methods for precise location\.
