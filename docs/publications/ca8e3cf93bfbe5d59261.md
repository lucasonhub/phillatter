# Cybercrime in India under the New Criminal Laws: Emerging Challenges in Investigation, Digital Evidence and Criminal Justice

**Autores:** Srinivas Peraboina; Pittala Sanjay Kumar

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid growth of digital technology has changed the way people communicate, conduct business, access financial services and interact with government institutions\. At the same time, this transformation has created new opportunities for criminal activity\. Cybercrime today includes online financial fraud, identity theft, unauthorised access to computer systems, cyberstalking, impersonation, data theft, social media offences and technologically facilitated forms of traditional crime\. The investigation and prosecution of such offences present difficulties that differ from those associated with conventional crimes because electronic evidence can be easily altered, deleted, transferred and stored across multiple jurisdictions\. The introduction of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 represents a significant transformation of India&\#x27;s criminal justice framework\. This paper examines the implications of the new criminal laws for cybercrime investigation, digital evidence and criminal justice administration\. It also considers the continuing importance of the Information Technology Act, 2000 and the constitutional protection of privacy and due process\. The paper adopts a doctrinal and analytical methodology based on legislation, judicial decisions and authoritative legal materials\. It argues that legislative reform by itself cannot adequately address the complexity of cybercrime\. Effective enforcement requires trained investigators, modern forensic infrastructure, rapid preservation of electronic evidence, coordination between investigative agencies and service providers, and appropriate safeguards for individual rights\. The paper further examines emerging challenges associated with artificial intelligence, deepfakes, digital impersonation and sophisticated online fraud\. It concludes that India&\#x27;s new criminal laws provide an important foundation for dealing with cybercrime, but their success will ultimately depend upon effective implementation, technological capacity and a careful balance between criminal enforcement and constitutional liberties\.
