# Machine learning\-based long\-term land\-use dynamics and forest recovery in Aweta sub\-basin \(1972\-2025\), Southern Ethiopia

**Autores:** Wendafiraw Abdisa Gemmechis; Abiyot Legesse Kura; Berhanu Keno Terfa; Mohammed Ahimed Mahammed

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Tropical forests are vital for ecosystem health, providing habitat, regulating water, and preserving soil\. However, rapid land use/cover \(LUC\) changes, particularly forest loss, threaten their stability\. Long\-term LUC monitoring is difficult due to limited high\-resolution data and inadequate analytical methods, especially in tropical areas facing significant forest loss\. In response to these concerns, the study quantified LUC dynamics on Ethiopia’s Aweta sub\-basin for over 53 years\. Landsat imagery and two machine learning models were used: the Statistical Machine Intelligence and Learning Engine \(Smile\) Random Forest and Smile Classification and Regression Tree \(CART\) classifiers, both implemented in Google Earth Engine \(GEE\)\. To comprehensively assess these dynamics, the study evaluated rates, gross and net gains and losses, and the persistence of forest loss across five\-time intervals: 1972–1986, 1986–2000, 2000–2014, 2014–2025, and 1972–2025\. The results show: \(i\) The Smile Random Forest algorithm outperformed Smile CART in LUC classification accuracy\. \(ii\) Agricultural land expanded steadily, but forests, wetlands, shrublands, grasslands, and water bodies declined around initial study periods\. \(iii\) The greatest forest loss rate, \-11% per year \(\-31,182\.16 hectares/year\), \-1\.52% per year \(\-7859\.3 hectares/year\) occurred in the first and last intervals, respectively\. By the second and third intervals, the annual forest loss rate dropped to 0\.39% per year \(474\.9 hectares/year\) and 1\.9% per year \(2,739\.2 hectares/year\), respectively indicating the highest forest recovery\. \(v\) From 1972 to 2025, forest cover decreased at an average rate of \-2\.64% per year\. \(vi\) Net change in forest cover showed forest recovery in the second and third periods \(44993\.6 hectares\) and losses in the first and fourth periods \(\-592010\.54 hectares\)\. This study primarily supports environmental resource management by enabling more accurate detection and monitoring of LUC patterns\. The superior performance of the Smile Random Forest model facilitates ongoing LUC assessment, highlighting the positive impact of conservation initiatives and afforestation on forest cover\.
