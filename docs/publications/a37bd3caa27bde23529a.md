# Deep learning\-driven optical fiber sensor for 2D vector flow velocity detection

**Autores:** Haiqing Li; Boxue Sun; Xiaonan Bian; Yujia Wang; Xianping Fu

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Flow velocity, direction, and temperature are key parameters for characterizing underwater flow fields\. However, traditional optical fiber sensors suffer from low integration, poor sensitivity, and severe cross\-sensitivities\. Inspired by the lateral line receptors of fish, we propose a biomimetic cilium\-based multimode fiber speckle sensor with multi\-head decoding for multi\-parameter sensing\. The sensor employs a cylindrical cilium structure, embedded in a PDMS, to convert external fluid disturbances into modal coupling and phase shifts within the multimode fiber, resulting in local speckle displacement and micro\-texture variations for optical encoding of flow direction and velocity\. Temperature variations induce near\-common\-mode phase drifts that manifest as smooth global changes in the speckle field, producing identifiable temperature features\. A shared backbone multi\-head deep network jointly decodes the three parameters to mitigate cross\-sensitivity and task interference\. In the velocity\-direction dataset, covering a tested flow\-velocity range of 0\.167–0\.5 m/s, the proposed method achieves a flow\-direction accuracy of 93\.62% and a flow\-velocity MAE of 0\.0137 m/s\. Across the tested temperature range of 5\-40°C, it achieves a temperature MAE of 0\.5412°C\. The results validate a compact laboratory scale fiber sensor architecture for controlled multi\-parameter optical sensing and demonstrate its potential for future underwater monitoring applications\.
