# Self\-efficacy, future expectation, and perceived stress in high school student\-athletes: a cross\-sectional study

**Autores:** Ufuk DİLEKÇİ; Suriye Tan; Lamia Selçuk; Çetin Tan; Malik DÜNDAR; Mustafa Karadağ; Yunus ŞAHİNLER; Selin Baikoğlu; Ismail Belli; Faik Öz; Levent Ceylan

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Adolescence is a period in which beliefs about personal capability and expectations for the future develop alongside increasing academic and, for student\-athletes, sporting demands\. This study examined the associations among self\-efficacy, future expectation, and perceived stress in high school student\-athletes\. Methods This cross\-sectional study included 425 high school student\-athletes who completed measures of self\-efficacy, future expectation, and perceived stress\. Confirmatory factor analyses and multivariable regression models were used to examine domain\- and component\-level associations\. Results Self\-efficacy dimensions were positively associated with several future\-expectation domains, with social self\-efficacy showing the most consistent pattern after FDR correction\. Emotional self\-efficacy was negatively associated with Perceived Distress, whereas no self\-efficacy dimension remained significantly associated with Perceived Coping after FDR correction\. Future\-expectation domains were not significantly associated with either perceived\-stress component in the adjusted models after FDR correction\. Female gender and higher grade level were associated with greater Perceived Distress\. Conclusion Self\-efficacy showed domain\-specific associations with future expectation and perceived stress in high school student\-athletes\. Emotional self\-efficacy was associated with lower Perceived Distress, whereas future\-expectation domains showed no significant adjusted associations with either perceived\-stress component after FDR correction\. Given the cross\-sectional design, these findings should be interpreted as contemporaneous associations rather than causal effects\.
