# Needham, Eric J\.: The Normalisation of Atrocity and Injustice

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Artificial intelligence has entered everyday life inside a politically useful contradiction\. It is sold through the language of agency: reasoning, planning, acting, collaborating, adapting and autonomy\. Yet the same system can be reduced instantly to a passive instrument when questions of authorship, intellectual credit, ownership or institutional responsibility become inconvenient\. This book argues that the contradiction is not semantic\. It is political\. At the same time, frontier AI is being concentrated inside private infrastructures whose usefulness depends upon immense bodies of human language, culture, technical knowledge and lived expertise\. Workers are encouraged to reorganise their work around systems they do not own\. Users are encouraged to disclose enough context for those systems to become personally indispensable\. Information is increasingly encountered through interfaces whose rankings, omissions and assumptions are privately governed\. The result is a remarkable political object: something that can be owned like a machine, sold like a colleague, feared like an enemy and blamed like an employee\. A Neo\-Situationist analysis asks a simple question: what becomes normal before we have decided whether it should be normal? The danger is not that artificial intelligence secretly possesses consciousness\. The danger is that the spectacle of machine consciousness, machine rebellion and machine apocalypse can keep us staring at the machine while changes in labour, ownership, provenance, responsibility and access to knowledge happen in plain sight\. This book is a call to refuse that normalisation\.
