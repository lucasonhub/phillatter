# Patentability of artificial intelligence \(AI\) inventions: A comparative study of Section 3\(k\) of the Indian Patents Act, 1970 and the United Kingdom&\#x27;s journey from Aerotel to emotional perception AI

**Autores:** Sourabh Batar; Sushil K\. Singh; Bishnanand Dubey; Yogesh Gupta

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This article undertakes a comparative doctrinal analysis of the patentability of artificial\-intelligence \(AI\)\-related inventions under Indian and UK patent law\. Between 2023 and early 2026, the Delhi High Court, in a series of decisions culminating in Kroll Information Assurance, LLC v Controller General of Patents , refined the ‘technical effect/technical contribution’ threshold that governs the exclusion of ‘a computer programme per se’ under Section 3\(k\) of the Patents Act, 1970\. That threshold has since been codified, with AI\-specific illustrations, in the Guidelines for Examination of Computer Related Inventions, 2025, issued on 29 July 2025\. The United Kingdom&\#x27;s position developed less smoothly\. In Emotional Perception AI Ltd v Comptroller\-General of Patents , a permissive first\-instance ruling was reversed by the Court of Appeal, which reaffirmed the primacy of the long\-standing four\-step Aerotel test, before the Supreme Court, on 11 February 2026, overruled Aerotel altogether and adopted an approach modelled on the European Patent Office&\#x27;s ‘any hardware’ doctrine\. This article argues that, notwithstanding these divergent statutory architectures and procedural histories, India and the United Kingdom have arrived at a structurally similar solution: a low, largely formal threshold for patent eligibility, followed by a substantive technical–character enquiry applied at a later analytical stage\.
