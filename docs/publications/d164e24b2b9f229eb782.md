# A Real\-time Multimodal Driver Monitoring System with Context\-dependent Rule\-based Fusion for the Detection of Distraction, Drowsiness, and Sudden Medical Conditions

**Autores:** Hasan Abdulhadi Kazum; Jumana Waleed; Abbas M\. Al\-Bakry

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Driver distraction, fatigue, and acute medical conditions are among the main contributors to road traffic crashes\.Most existing driver monitoring systems rely on a single sensing modality or address a single driver state, and few translate a detected state into a graduated safety response\.This paper presents a real\-time multimodal driver monitoring system that combines infrared camera\-based behavioral analysis, wearable physiological sensing, and OBD\-II vehicle\-speed information through a context\-dependent rule\-based decision\-level combination\.The visual subsystem integrates a YOLOv8n driver\-behavior detector, trained for this work on 3,971 annotated in\-cabin images spanning nine distraction\-related classes, with MediaPipe\-based facial, ocular, and hand landmark analysis\.Temporal confirmation, physiological corroboration, and speed\-dependent escalation are applied to produce stable event\-level driver\-state decisions and to suppress transient false alarms\.The system is implemented as an embedded prototype on a Raspberry Pi 5 and evaluated on data from ten participants, comprising 273 annotated unsafe\-driving events and ten normal\-driving segments totalling 51\.1 minutes\.Event\-based evaluation yields a precision of 95\.41%, a recall of 98\.90%, and an F1\-score of 97\.12%, with a reconstructed mean event\-detection delay of 0\.568 s; 98% of matched events occurred within one 1\.0\-s logging interval of the annotated event onset\.No confirmed safety activation occurred during the normal\-driving segments, while 290 transient detections that did not meet the false\-alarm criteria were logged and are reported separately\.The decision parameters are fixed rather than learned, and the results were obtained on a small scripted dataset without a head\-to\-head baseline comparison; they should therefore be interpreted as prototype\-level validation of an integrated embedded architecture and its evaluation protocol rather than as evidence of general real\-world performance\.
