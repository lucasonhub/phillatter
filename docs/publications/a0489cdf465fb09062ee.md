# Zhao, Xiangbin: Why Do Organisms Move One Way Rather Than Another? — The Probability Group\-Form of On Self\-Manifestation

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

The fundamental role of probability is not to represent “how large a possibility is,” but to organize already\-formed Laws into directions of future continuation\. Only once there are directions can Intention have anything to be directed toward\. The Probability Group\-Form of On Self\-Manifestation asks a question prior to numerical probability: how does a real system form multiple directions of future continuation from its own history, and how does its own structure bear the relative\-generation relation among those directions? Every actual physical execution settles into one actual occurrence; multiple outcomes do not stand there waiting to be sampled\. Probability therefore does not begin with “possibility,” but with Laws formed through history\. Through prolonged interaction between system and environment, different paths undergo disturbance, feedback, reward and punishment, forming sedimentation, familiar paths and stable continuation structures\. Paths capable of bearing later continuation obtain Law qualification and become probability dimensions\. Probability then organizes multiple such Law\-bearing directions within one and the same continuation question, while the organization itself bears the relative\-generation relation among the dimensions\. A numerical probability is only a read\-out of this structure once it has been recognized and represented\. It follows that observing probability is not the same as finding Probability, and outcome\-bearing is not rate\-bearing \(Outcome\-bearing ≠ Rate\-bearing\)\. A stable 70:30 distribution shows only that outcomes occur in a 70:30 ratio; it does not identify what structure inside the system generates and bears that ratio\. Probability research thereby moves from “What is the probability?” to “What physical organization makes this system possess this Probability?”—from statistical description to generative mechanism\. The Probability Group\-Form further distinguishes Probability, ProbabilityAct and ProbabilityDyn: the holding of Probability, its current action, and its cross\-time dynamics\. It treats qualification, inversion, determination, limit, source, dynamics, construction, reachability and stability separately\. Zero rate, equal rates, complete determinism, or a numerical rate that is currently unreadable do not by themselves eliminate Probability\. The real boundary is structural: whether multiple Law\-bearing continuation directions still exist, whether they belong to one and the same continuation question, and whether their relative generation is still borne by the organization itself\. New probability dimensions may form, old dimensions may exit, and both rate relations and rate\-bearing structures may change\. Not only probability values, but future directions themselves can therefore be generated and rewritten by history\. This makes probability a mechanistic problem shared by physics, neuroscience, cognitive science and artificial intelligence\. Physics must distinguish the unity of each actual execution from the multi\-directional structure formed across history; neuroscience must distinguish measuring a probability distribution from finding the neural organization that generates it; cognitive science thereby gains an intermediate layer between experiential history and directed behavior; and artificial intelligence must distinguish “computing probability” from “forming future directions through its own history\.” Its most important consequence is that Probability provides a genuine directional structure for Intention\. A Law answers: “How does this already\-formed path replay?” Probability answers: “What already\-formed paths are there, and how are they generated relative to one another?” Only after multiple real directions have formed does a new question become physically meaningful: toward which dimension? Probability provides the dimensions toward which continuation can proceed; Intention then selects, biases and directs continuation among them\. Thus: Environmental action → actual occurrence → sedimentation → Law → probability dimension → Probability organization → selection and bias → Intention → action\. History sediments into Law; Probability organizes Laws into directions; Intention makes the system proceed along one of them\. The Probability Group\-Form therefore ultimately asks not merely, “What is the probability of an outcome?” but a more fundamental question: What physical organization makes a system genuinely possess directions toward its own future?
