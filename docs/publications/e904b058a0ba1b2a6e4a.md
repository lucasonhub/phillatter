# Fractional exhaled nitric oxide in non\-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis: an exploratory prospective observational study

**Autores:** Eugerta Dilka; Eva Muja; Artan Simaku; Perlat Kapisyzi

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Fractional exhaled nitric oxide \(FeNO\) is a non\-invasive biomarker of type 2 airway inflammation, but its role in non\-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis remains uncertain\. This study explored the associations of FeNO with peripheral blood eosinophils, pulmonary function, high\-resolution computed tomography \(HRCT\)\-defined morphology, and short\-term post\-treatment change in adults with non\-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis\. We conducted an exploratory prospective observational study including 152 adults with clinically and HRCT\-confirmed non\-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis admitted to a national tertiary pulmonary center\. FeNO was measured using a standardized single\-flow technique\. Pulmonary function testing, blood eosinophil assessment, and HRCT evaluation were performed\. Descriptive statistics, between\-group comparisons, correlation analyses, and exploratory multivariable linear regression were used\. A subgroup underwent repeat FeNO assessment after antibiotic treatment\. Mean FeNO was 12\.1±7\.9 ppb\. FeNO correlated positively with blood eosinophil percentage \(r=0\.41, p&lt;0\.001\) and MEF50% predicted \(r=0\.48, p&lt;0\.001\) and inversely with total lung capacity % predicted \(r=−0\.39, p&lt;0\.001\)\. FeNO distributions differed across ventilatory patterns \(overall Kruskal\-Wallis p=0\.030\) and HRCT morphology groups \(overall Kruskal–Wallis p=0\.040\); the exploratory cylindrical versus cystic/saccular comparison yielded p=0\.023 \(unadjusted\)\. In exploratory multivariable regression, blood eosinophil percentage remained associated with FeNO \(β=1\.12, 95% confidence interval: 0\.29\-1\.95; p=0\.009\)\. Among 55 reassessed patients, FeNO decreased from 12\.1±6\.8 to 7\.0±3\.1 ppb after antibiotic treatment \(p=0\.010\)\. FeNO was generally low in adults with non\-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis but showed exploratory associations with eosinophilic inflammation, selected pulmonary function parameters, HRCT morphology, and short\-term post\-treatment change\. Larger longitudinal studies with standardized severity classification, microbiological characterization, and predefined outcomes are needed before FeNO can be recommended for routine clinical use\.
