# Psychological Autopsy in Adolescent Hanging\- A Cross\-Sectional Study to Find out the Background of Adolescent Suicide

**Autores:** Manivel Subramani

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Adolescent suicide is a growing public health concern worldwide, with hanging being one of the most commonly employed methods\. Despite its increasing incidence, limited information is available regarding the psychosocial circumstances preceding adolescent suicidal hanging, particularly in India\. Psychological autopsy is a valuable investigative tool for reconstructing the biological, psychological, social, and environmental factors associated with suicide\. Aim: To identify the socio\-demographic characteristics and psychosocial risk factors associated with suicidal hanging among adolescents using the psychological autopsy approach\. Materials and Methods: A cross\-sectional observational study was conducted in the Department of Forensic Medicine and Toxicology, AIIMS, New Delhi, India, from October 2021 to April 2022\. Fifty\-nine consecutive cases of suicidal hanging among adolescents aged 10–19 years were included\. Following written informed consent, psychological autopsies were performed through face\-to\-face interviews with close biological relatives using a semi\-structured questionnaire\. Information obtained from police records, crime scene investigations, and medico\-legal autopsies was integrated to reconstruct the circumstances surrounding each death\. Data were analysed using descriptive statistics\. Results: Late adolescents \(17–19 years\) constituted the majority of victims \(62\.7%\), with females predominating \(59\.3%\)\. School dropout was observed in 32\.2% of cases, while 22\.0% belonged to families with financial difficulties\. Depressed mood \(15\.3%\), loss of interest in daily activities \(20\.3%\), previous self\-harming behaviour \(15\.3%\), suicidal ideation \(11\.9%\), suicide notes \(11\.9%\), and substance use \(17\.0%\) were identified in a proportion of victims\. A family history of suicide was present in 8\.5% of cases\. Menstrual\-related emotional distress \(27\.1%\) and romantic relationship problems \(8\.5%\) were the most frequently identified psychosocial stressors among female adolescents\. Most suicides occurred at home \(88\.1%\), with cloth serving as the most common ligature material \(74\.6%\) and ceiling fans as the most frequent suspension point \(67\.8%\)\. In 74\.6% of cases, no definite precipitating stressor could be identified\. Conclusion: Adolescent suicidal hanging is a multifactorial phenomenon influenced by interacting psychological, familial, social, and environmental factors\. Psychological autopsy provides valuable insights into these antecedent circumstances and facilitates the identification of modifiable risk factors that may inform targeted suicide prevention strategies, improve medico\-legal investigations, and strengthen adolescent mental health interventions\.
