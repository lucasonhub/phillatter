# Designing Engineering Curricula as Socio\-Technical Systems: A Practice\-Informed ST\+EHoM Model\.

**Autores:** Nikita Hari; Abel Nyamapfene; Janet Hanson

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Electrical and electronic engineering graduates increasingly need to work with complex socio\-technical systems involving technical, human, organisational, environmental and ethical dimensions\. This creates a curriculum design challenge: programmes must develop technical depth while also helping students recognise interdependencies, frame problems, work with uncertainty, iterate solutions and adapt to changing constraints\. This paper develops and illustrates a curriculum design process that combines systems thinking with Engineering Habits of Mind \(EHoM\)\. It argues that systems thinking can function both as a curriculum design logic and as an integrative engineering habit, while EHoM provides a language for translating this logic into learning outcomes, pedagogy, assessment and reflection\. The paper adopts a practice\-informed conceptual design case approach, drawing on the design and early implementation of an undergraduate engineering degree apprenticeship curriculum in a new higher education provider in England\. The case is used to examine how the ST\+EHoM model supported curriculum coherence, academic\-workplace integration, assessment alignment and adaptive redesign, while also surfacing tensions around accreditation, staff capacity, student expectations and the balance between technical depth and disposition\-oriented learning\. The paper contributes a practical, conceptually grounded model for designing engineering curricula that prepare students to think and act as engineers within complex socio\-technical systems\.
