# Prior\-Informed Spatially Adaptive Network \(PISA\-Net\) for multi\-parameter water quality retrieval in Poyang Lake using Sentinel\-2 imagery

**Autores:** Lin Wu; Yihang Duan; Yu Zhang; Yabo Huang; Duojia Yang; Wenfu Wu; Ning Li

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate retrieval of water\-quality parameters from remote\-sensing imagery is essential for monitoring large inland lakes, yet pronounced spatial heterogeneity and limited transferability across varying environmental conditions remain major challenges\. This study proposes a Prior\-Informed Spatially Adaptive Network \(PISA\-Net\) for multi\-parameter water\-quality retrieval from Sentinel\-2 imagery in Poyang Lake\. PISA\-Net integrates a CNN branch for nonlinear spectral–spatial feature learning with a knowledge\-guided branch based on empirical optical descriptors and coordinate\-derived geographic priors\. A geographic soft\-assignment mechanism constructs the spatial prior vector, which conditions CNN features through Feature\-wise Linear Modulation \(FiLM\), while a stop gradient consistency loss \(SG\-CL\) promotes agreement between the two prediction pathways with controlled gradient propagation\. Chl\-a, TP, and CODMn were modeled independently and evaluated using 5\-fold spatial\-block cross\-validation against six representative empirical, statistical, machine\-learning, and deep\-learning baselines\. PISA\-Net achieved R 2 values of 0\.6113, 0\.5112, and 0\.5054 for Chl\-a, TP, and CODMn, respectively, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline, 2D\-CNN, while reducing RMSE by approximately 29\.6%, 21\.4%, and 19\.0%\. An additional April 2018 cross\-season experiment revealed substantial degradation under direct zero\-shot transfer, whereas few\-shot adaptation markedly reduced prediction errors, indicating useful cross\-season adaptability after limited local calibration\. Overall, PISA\-Net provides a prior\-informed spectral–spatial framework for water\-quality retrieval under spatially heterogeneous conditions, while broader multi\-season validation remains necessary to establish comprehensive seasonal generalizability\.
