# The underestimated emission reduction effect of green credit: a systematic study based on peer effects

**Autores:** Bo Wen; Dayi He; Ping Lei

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Against the backdrop of global efforts to address climate change and advance the low\-carbon transition, green credit is widely regarded as a financial instrument that directs financial resources toward low\-carbon activities\. However, most existing studies focus on the direct effects of green credit on individual firms while overlooking interactions among firms\. This study systematically examines the peer effects of green credit on corporate carbon reduction at the industry and city levels and explores the underlying mechanisms\. To this end, a theoretical model is developed to explain how green credit induces peer effects on corporate carbon reduction\. The theoretical predictions are then tested using a sample of Chinese A\-share listed firms\. The results show that greater access to green credit among peer firms significantly reduces the carbon emission intensity of focal firms, indicating that green credit generates carbon\-reduction peer effects at both the industry and city levels\. Market competition and learning and imitation are important mechanisms through which these peer effects operate\. The carbon\-reduction peer effects of green credit are more pronounced among firms in industries not subject to green\-credit restrictions and among those located in Green Finance Reform and Innovation Pilot Zones\. This study advances theoretical modeling and empirical research on green finance and corporate peer effects and provides useful evidence for policymakers seeking to reassess the emission\-reduction effects of green credit policies\.
