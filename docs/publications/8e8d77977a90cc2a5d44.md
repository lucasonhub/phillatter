# Neutrophil heterogeneity under IL\-17A deficiency in experimental periodontitis

**Autores:** Zhongyuan Tang; Renjie Hu; Luying Zhu; Jing Hu; Fan Xiao; Lijian Jin; Liwei Lu; Yanqi Yang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Periodontitis is a dysbiotic inflammatory disease characterized by progressive periodontal destruction, in which neutrophils play a central but functionally diverse role\. IL\-17A is closely linked to neutrophil\-mediated host responses; however, how IL\-17A deficiency reshapes neutrophil heterogeneity during periodontitis remains unclear\. Here, we established a Porphyromonas gingivalis –induced experimental periodontitis model in wild\-type and IL\-17A knockout mice and integrated micro\-CT, histology, 16S rRNA sequencing, and single\-cell RNA sequencing to define the consequences of IL\-17A loss in the periodontal microenvironment\. IL\-17A deficiency exacerbated alveolar bone loss and osteoclast accumulation and was associated with altered oral microbial composition consistent with dysbiosis\. Single\-cell profiling revealed marked remodeling of the immune landscape, with impaired neutrophil accumulation and altered neutrophil subset composition in IL\-17A\-deficient periodontal tissues\. Neutrophils from IL\-17A knockout mice showed reduced inflammatory chemokine/cytokine programs and a shift toward antimicrobial granule\-associated, extracellular matrix\-responsive, and TGF\-β/THBS\-linked transcriptional states\. Trajectory, transcriptional regulatory, and cell–cell communication analyses further demonstrated that IL\-17A deficiency disrupted neutrophil state transitions and attenuated neutrophil\-centered CCL, CXCL, TNF, SELPG, and ICAM signaling while enhancing matrix\-associated communication pathways\. Together, these findings identify IL\-17A deficiency as a driver of neutrophil reprogramming and aggravated periodontal destruction and indicate an association with microbial dysbiosis, providing single\-cell\-level insight into the IL\-17A–neutrophil axis in dysbiotic periodontitis\.
