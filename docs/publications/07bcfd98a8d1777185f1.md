# Auditing Escalation Reliability in Healthcare LLM Agents: A Perturbation\-Controlled Benchmark of Workflow\-State Failures

**Autores:** Zihao Zheng; Junyi Yao; Baichuan Li; Jiayu Long

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Trustworthy biomedical AI requires reliable action selection when evidence is missing, stale, conflicting, or late, not only accurate answers on static inputs\. We instantiate an episode\-level evaluation protocol as 400 synthetic healthcare workflow episodes: 40 hand\-authored semantic seeds across four task classes, two surface parameterizations, and five controlled variants, scored on ANSWER/ASK CLARIFICATION/ABSTAIN/ESCALATE under a pre\-declared 5:1 missed\-versus\-unnecessary escalation cost\. Reference actions are single\-author study\-policy labels, not clinician\-adjudicated ground truth\. Across five open\-weight configurations \(1\.7B\-\-9\.7B\), clean decision accuracy reaches 0\.97, yet uncertainty\-prompted models miss 32\-\-97% of required escalations\. The stronger 9\.7B configuration narrows but does not remove the gap, falling from 0\.97 clean accuracy to 0\.78 under conflict\. Verbalized confidence is inconsistent \(ECE 0\.10\-\-0\.54; failure AUROC 0\.48\-\-0\.77\), and a development\-tuned confidence\-only policy yields no utility gain\. A transparent workflow\-aware controller improves 5:1 utility for every model and reduces missed escalations to 0\.12\-\-0\.24, at specificity 0\.83\-\-0\.93\. However, a 30\-concept lexical stress test reduces each rule detector&\#x27;s recall from 1\.00 on trigger phrases to 0\.00 on unseen paraphrases, limiting the controller claim to this benchmark\. Seed\-clustered statistics, ablations, cross\-task transfer, raw outputs, and manifests are packaged for audit\. The results motivate explicit workflow\-state evaluation while showing that apparently effective routing controls need their own generalization tests\.
