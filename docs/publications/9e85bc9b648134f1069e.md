# Quantum Machine Learning \(QML\) and Parameterized Quantum Circuits for High\-Dimensional Optimization: Barren Plateaus, Quantum Kernels, and NISQ Benchmarks

**Autores:** Harshit Tinker; Divyajeet Swami; Vishal Shrivastava; Vibhakar Pathak; Deepmala Sharma; Shikha Gupta

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract—Quantum Machine Learning \(QML\) operating on Noisy Intermediate\-Scale Quantum \(NISQ\) hardware represents an emerging computational paradigm to address high\-dimensional optimization problems that challenge classical Von Neumann supercomputing architectures\. By mapping complex feature vectors into exponentially large 2^N Hilbert state spaces via Parameterized Quantum Circuits \(PQCs\) and Variational Quantum Algorithms \(VQAs\), QML targets computational advantages in combinatorial optimization, high\-dimensional classification, and quantum\-enhanced molecular modeling\. However, the theoretical promise of NISQ\-era variational computing is severely constrained by three fundamental bottlenecks: \(1\) Barren Plateaus, characterized by the exponential concentration of measure and vanishing variance of cost function gradients; \(2\) Hardware Decoherece Noise and readout error rates that corrupt quantum state fidelity; and \(3\) Classical\-to\-Quantum State Preparation bottlenecks that can erase theoretical computational speedups\. This paper delivers an exhaustive, mathematically formal technical investigation and empirical benchmark of QML architectures for high\-dimensional optimization\. We formalize the Haar\-distributed geometric mechanics of barren plateaus under 2\-design unitary assumptions, evaluate barren plateau mitigation through shallow local cost operators and layer\-by\-layer parameter initialization, and contrast Variational Quantum Eigensolvers \(VQE\) with Quantum Kernel Support Vector Machines \(Q\-SVM\)\. Furthermore, we construct an empirical benchmarking testbed executing across both state\-vector simulators and superconducting quantum backends \(IBM Quantum Heron / Eagle architectures\), evaluating 127\-qubit Hamiltonian optimization and high\-dimensional financial portfolio allocation\. Our findings demonstrate that while global cost functions suffer exponential gradient decay Var\[grad\] \~ O\(2^\-N\) across circuit depth d &gt;= 4, local Hamiltonian decomposition maintains trainable variance, achieving a 98\.2% ground\-state fidelity under zero\-noise extrapolation \(ZNE\) error mitigation\. Finally, we formulate open challenges in quantum error mitigation scaling, non\-perturbative barren plateau escapes, and quantum data loading efficiency\. Index Terms—Quantum Machine Learning \(QML\), Parameterized Quantum Circuits \(PQCs\), Variational Quantum Eigensolver \(VQE\), Barren Plateaus, Quantum Kernel Methods, NISQ Computing, Error Mitigation, Hilbert Space\.
