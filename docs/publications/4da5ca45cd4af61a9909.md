# Improving Molecular Formula Assignment of Nitrogen\- and Sulfur\-Rich DOM in Ultrahigh\-Resolution Mass Spectrometry: Validation of Oligopeptides

**Autores:** Peter Herzsprung; Oliver Jens Lechtenfeld; Wolf von Tümpling; Norbert Kamjunke; Shuxian Gao

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Molecular formula \(MF\) assignment in ultrahigh\-resolution mass spectrometry \(UHRMS\) analyses of dissolved organic matter \(DOM\) containing multiple non\-oxygen heteroatoms \(e\.g\., nitrogen and sulfur\) is highly environmentally relevant yet remains challenging\. Recent studies of DOM composition in aquaculture systems with protein\-rich surface sediments have reported numerous CHNO \(N1−8\), CHOS \(S1−3\), and even CHNOS MFs containing multiple N and S atoms, suggesting the presence of oligopeptide\-like molecules\. Due to insufficient mass resolution, multiple formula assignments \(MultiAs\) caused by interchangeable replacement pairs, within a mass error threshold of 1 ppm, complicate the validation of MF assignments\. While N5S2 formulas are known as likely false\-positive assignments of 13C1 isotopologue peaks, the multiple N\-containing MFs in MultiAs of potential oligopeptide origins involving replacement pairs with N6 ↔ S1 and S2 ↔ N4 could not be validated properly\. Here, we introduce a new chemically informed validation approach based on the elemental composition and stoichiometry of typical peptides with atomic ratios 0\.2 &lt; O/C &lt; 0\.65 and 1\.5 &lt; H/C &lt; 2\. Amino acid combinations matched about 36% N\-rich MF \(n = 960\) among N6 ↔ S1 related MultiAs \(N7 ↔ N1S1, N8 ↔ N2S1··· N8S2 ↔ N2S3\) and 30% N\-rich MF \(n = 1040\) among S2 ↔ N4 related MultiAs \(N1S2 ↔ N5, N2S2 ↔ N6···N4S3 ↔ N8S1\), supporting the true assignments of N\-rich MFs based on oligopeptide stoichiometry\. Overall, the chemical complexity of heteroatom\-rich DOM warrants advances in MF assignment for UHR mass spectra by expanding the analytical window to include multiple\-heteroatom formulas, which would especially benefit protein\-rich samples from aquaculture systems or wastewater\.
