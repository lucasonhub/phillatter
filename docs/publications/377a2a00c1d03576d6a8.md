# Phytochemical\-capped silver nanoparticles and cationic polymeric nanoparticles form antibacterial hybrid nanoassemblies

**Autores:** HUMAIRA KHAN; Maged Abdelaziz; Nourina Nasim; Jungmi Park; Simona Piccolella; Vincent M\. Rotello; Severina Pacifico

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Hybrid antibacterial nanomaterials that combine metallic nanoparticles with membrane\-active polymers represent a promising strategy to improve antimicrobial efficacy while reducing the amount of silver required for bacterial inhibition\. Herein, we report the development of sustainable hybrid nanoassemblies by integrating phytochemical‐stabilized silver nanoparticles \(AgNPs\), green‐synthesized using Camellia japonica flower extracts, with cationic poly \(oxanorbornene\-imide\) \(PONI‐C11‐TMA\) polymeric nanoparticles\. The obtained nanoparticles were characterized by UV‐Vis spectroscopy, ATR‐FTIR spectroscopy, transmission electron microscopy \(TEM\), dynamic light scattering \(DLS\), and zeta potential measurements\. Cationic PONI‐C11‐TMA nanoparticles were synthesized and combined with AgNPs to generate hybrid PNP‐Ag nanoassemblies through electrostatic interactions\. The antibacterial activity of AgNPs, PONI‐C11‐TMA nanoparticles, and hybrid nanoassemblies was evaluated against multidrug‐resistant Gram‐negative bacterial strains, including Acinetobacter baumannii and Escherichia coli, by determining minimum inhibitory concentrations \(MICs\) and fractional inhibitory concentration \(FIC\) indexes\. Cytocompatibility was assessed using NIH‐3T3 fibroblast cells\. Comprehensive UHPLC‐HRMS/MS and HPLC‐DAD analyses demonstrated that the flower extract is enriched in anthocyanins, flavan‐3‐ols, and other polyphenols, which serve as both reducing and stabilizing agents during nanoparticle formation\. The resulting AgNPs were predominantly spherical, with diameters of 20–30 nm, and exhibited excellent colloidal stability, as confirmed by UV‐Vis spectroscopy, transmission electron microscopy, and dynamic light scattering\. Electrostatic assembly with positively charged PONI‐C11‐TMA nanoparticles generated stable hybrid nanoassemblies, as demonstrated by increases in hydrodynamic diameter and partial surface charge neutralization\. Individually, AgNPs and polymeric nanoparticles inhibited the growth of multidrug‐resistant Acinetobacter baumannii and Escherichia coli at minimum inhibitory concentrations \(MICs\) of 10 and 2\.5 μg mL‐1, respectively, while maintaining high cytocompatibility toward NIH‐3T3 fibroblasts\. The hybrid nanoassemblies further reduced the effective antimicrobial concentrations, producing 2‐4‐fold MIC reductions against A\. baumannii and 4‐8‐fold reductions against E\. coli , with an additive interaction \(fractional inhibitory concentration index = 0\.75\)\. These findings demonstrate that phytochemical surface engineering of green‐synthesized AgNPs enables their integration with membrane‐active polymeric nanoparticles into stable hybrid nanoassemblies, providing an effective and sustainable platform for antibacterial nanomaterials with improved performance against Gram‐negative pathogens\.
