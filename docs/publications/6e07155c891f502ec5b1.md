# A Neural Network\-Based Approach for Trust\-Guided Anomaly Detection in IoT Networks

**Autores:** 

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid growth of heterogeneous Internet of Things \(IoT\) networks has expanded the attack surface and exposed the limitations of static, signature\-based intrusion detection\.This study proposes and evaluates a trust\-guided anomaly\-detection framework in which a label\-free behavioral trust score is computed for each network flow and appended to conventional traffic features\.The score combines connection state, missed bytes, packet and byte balance, flow duration, source and destination reputation, and interaction confidence\.Experiments were conducted on the TON\_IoT network dataset containing 211,043 flows \(50,000 normal and 161,043 attack records\)\.Four binary detectors were evaluated on identical splits: a trustagnostic Random Forest \(RF\), a trust\-guided RF, an LSTM autoencoder, and a trust\-guided LSTM autoencoder; a separate trust\-guided RF performed ten\-class attack classification\.On the stratified test split, the trust\-guided RF achieved 99\.93% accuracy, an F1 score of 0\.9995, and a 0\.24% false\-positive rate, compared with 99\.87%, 0\.9992, and 0\.33%, respectively, for the baseline RF\.This corresponds to a 28% relative reduction in false positives\.The LSTM autoencoders achieved F1 scores near 0\.81 but produced false\-positive rates above 0\.92\.A grouped source\-IP validation yielded an F1 score of 0\.9627 with the full feature set; performance declined substantially after removing port, DNS, and HTTP status features, revealing dataset\-specific dependence\.The findings show that behavior\-derived trust features can improve supervised IoT anomaly detection, while also demonstrating the need for grouped and cross\-dataset validation before deployment\.
