# Two Methodological Pitfalls in Matched\-Parameter Quantum–Classical Benchmarking for Paraphrase Identification: Class\-Imbalance Collapse and Readout\-Offset Asymmetry

**Autores:** Nazmus Salehin Sammo; Sariya Akhter Lura; Md Jannatul Rakib Joy; Raza Nowrozy; Savitri Bevinakoppa

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Quantum machine learning is commonly benchmarked by matching a quantum head’s parameter count to that of a classical baseline\. We show that this control is insufficient\. We compare a 32\-parameter parameterized quantum circuit \(PQC\) head against a nominally matched 31\-parameter multi\-layer perceptron \(MLP\) on three paraphrase tasks—the Microsoft Research Paraphrase Corpus \(MRPC\), Paraphrase Adversaries from Word Scrambling \(PAWS\), and Quora Question Pairs \(QQP\)—and document two pitfalls that inflate an apparent quantum deficit\. First, under the tested protocol, unweighted binary cross\-entropy on imbalanced data leads to near\-all\-positive prediction, producing an illusory equivalence\. Second, the surviving gap traces to a readout that sits offset from the decision threshold at initialization \(⟨Z0⟩=\+0\.922\), and no circuit parameter acts as a threshold shift that could recenter it\. A pre\-specified calibration experiment removes 61% to 76% of the gap, and a training\-free probe detects the condition in one forward pass\. Threshold\-free metrics then changed our own conclusions\. On PAWS, both heads rank barely above chance \(area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, AUC, at most 0\.508\), so the difference there is threshold placement\. Only QQP retains a residual, and a post hoc control doubling the qubit count narrows it by 42% without removing it \(paired p=0\.0009\)\. This pattern is consistent with a capacity or optimization limitation, but does not distinguish between them\. Four of five pre\-specified predictions failed\. Parameter roles and readout offsets must be audited, not only counts\.
