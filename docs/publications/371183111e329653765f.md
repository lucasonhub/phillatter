# Geometry Instructional Design Model Enhancing Comprehension Using Aligned Digital Tools, Guided Practice, and Reflection

**Autores:** Thomas Jerome Yeboah

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Geometry remains a foundational yet challenging domain in mathematics education, largely due to students’ difficulties with spatial reasoning, abstraction, and conceptual transfer\. This review paper examines the Geometry Instructional Design Model \(GIDM\) as an integrated framework for enhancing learner comprehension through the deliberate alignment of digital instructional tools, guided practice, and reflective learning strategies\. Drawing on contemporary research in instructional design, cognitive load theory, and technology\-enhanced learning, the study synthesizes evidence on how structured geometry instruction can improve conceptual understanding, procedural fluency, and long\-term retention\. The review critically evaluates the role of dynamic geometry software, interactive visualizations, and adaptive digital platforms in supporting multiple representations and reducing extraneous cognitive load\. Additionally, it explores guided practice mechanisms—including scaffolded problem\-solving, formative feedback, and collaborative tasks—that facilitate knowledge consolidation and error correction\. Reflective components such as metacognitive prompts, learning journals, and self\-assessment tools are analyzed for their contributions to deeper conceptual integration and learner autonomy\. By consolidating findings across empirical studies and theoretical models, the paper proposes a cohesive instructional design pathway that links technological affordances with pedagogical intent\. The review further identifies implementation challenges, including teacher readiness, digital equity, and curriculum alignment, while highlighting emerging opportunities for artificial intelligence–driven personalization and learning analytics\. Overall, this paper positions the Geometry Instructional Design Model as a robust, scalable approach for improving geometry learning outcomes across diverse educational contexts\.
