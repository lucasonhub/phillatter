# Course reassignment and learning engagement: the intervention strategy of AI\-attributed reassignment

**Autores:** Han Li; Yuner Zhu; Yifan Tang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Course reassignment is a common practice in university registration systems, yet reassigned students often exhibit lower learning engagement than those in self\-selected courses, and the underlying psychological mechanism remains unclear\. This study examined the impact of course reassignment on learning engagement, the mediating role of self\-efficacy, and the buffering effect of AI\-attributed course reassignment\. Methods Two scenario\-based online experiments were conducted with university students randomly assigned to conditions\. Experiment 1 \( N = 393\) used a single\-factor between\-subjects design comparing reassignment vs\. self\-selection\. Experiment 2 \( N = 293\) added an AI\-attributed reassignment condition in which an AI engine matched students&\#x27; interests, traits, and abilities with the most suitable available courses \(participants in the AI condition read a simulated AI\-generated learning analysis report\.\)\. Participants completed a learning task and reported learning engagement and self\-efficacy\. Mediation analyses were conducted using bootstrapping\. Results Experiment 1 showed that course reassignment was associated with lower learning engagement compared with self\-selection, and this effect was mediated by self\-efficacy \(indirect effect B = 0\.198, SE = 0\.051, 95%CI \[0\.096, 0\.299\]\)\. Experiment 2 demonstrated that AI\-attributed reassignment attenuated this negative effect\. Learning engagement in the AI\-attributed condition did not differ from self\-selection and was significantly higher than in random reassignment\. Moreover, the indirect effect through self\-efficacy became non\-significant \(B = 0\.140, SE = 0\.113, 95% CI \[−0\.077, 0\.362\]\)\. Discussion These findings identify self\-efficacy as a key psychological mechanism through which course reassignment impairs learning engagement, and demonstrate that AI\-attributed reassignment can restore both self\-efficacy and engagement by signaling person\-course fit\. This study extends self\-efficacy theory to the course selection context and offers a feasible, technology\-driven intervention for mitigating the negative consequences of course reassignment in higher education\.
