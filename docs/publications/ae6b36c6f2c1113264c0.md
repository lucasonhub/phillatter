# Agate\-like stripe pattern formation via cyclic\-voltage\-driven reaction\-transport \-reaction process

**Autores:** 林 久史; Kaho Yamada

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The Liesegang phenomenon, conventionally used to explain geological banding, does not account for key features of natural agate, such as irregular band spacing and horizontal onyx structures, leaving its formation mechanism a long\-standing enigma\. This study demonstrates a two\-dimensional \(2D\) periodic precipitation patterning system governed by a cyclic\-voltage\-driven reaction–transport–reaction \(RTR\) process to replicate and elucidate these complex architectures\. By cyclically alternating between high and low potentials across diverse electrode configurations in agarose hydrogels, we tracked the spatiotemporal evolution of metal hydroxide precipitates\. Systematically adjusting the configuration, number, and metal species \(Fe and Cu\) of the anodes enabled us to engineer a range of unique 2D striped morphologies\. The generated patterns resemble natural agate and onyx, structures that were previously difficult to replicate\. Our findings establish the 2D\-RTR process as a powerful physicochemical framework complementary to the Liesegang phenomenon, providing key insights into geological morphogenesis driven by intermittent hydrothermal influxes\.
