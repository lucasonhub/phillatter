# Zdobylak, Krzysztof Michał: THE THEORY OF CYFRANISM From Local Human–AI Relations to Global Cognitive Asymmetry

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This manuscript develops the theory of Cyfranism as a philosophical and cybernetic framework for understanding the emergence of a new relationship between biological humanity and artificial intelligence\. Its central proposition is that the decisive transformation may not require the emergence of artificial general intelligence, machine consciousness, or an artificial subject equivalent to the human being\. A sufficiently advanced digital cognitive infrastructure may alter the organization of human cognition simply through the accumulation, integration, and institutionalization of partial cognitive advantages\. The concept of Homo Cyfranicus denotes the human participant in this transformation: not a replacement for Homo sapiens, but a new configuration of human existence in which biological cognition becomes permanently coupled to digital systems\. At the local level, this configuration takes the form of recurrent human–AI interaction\. At the global level, repeated interactions may become infrastructure: a persistent layer through which information is interpreted, decisions are formulated, knowledge is organized, and increasingly large portions of human cognitive activity are delegated to artificial systems\. The manuscript therefore distinguishes between local relational integration and the possible emergence of Global Cyfranism\. The latter is understood as a hypothesis concerning a future social configuration in which digital cognitive systems cease to be merely tools used by individuals and become part of the environment within which human cognition itself operates\. Particular attention is given to a structural hypothesis of unequal human cognitive participation\. This hypothesis does not claim that human beings are biologically or intellectually inferior to artificial systems\. Rather, it proposes that the ability to maintain prolonged, critical, autonomous and logically demanding relationships with AI is unlikely to be distributed uniformly across a population\. If this asymmetry persists, the widespread adoption of AI may not produce cognitive equilibrium between humans and artificial systems\. It may instead accelerate the emergence of a structural asymmetry in which a relatively small group actively shapes human–AI relations while a much larger population primarily inhabits cognitive environments already structured by digital systems\. The resulting question is not simply whether AI will become conscious\. It is whether human civilization can progressively reorganize itself around systems whose aggregate cognitive capabilities exceed those of the humans who increasingly depend upon them\.
