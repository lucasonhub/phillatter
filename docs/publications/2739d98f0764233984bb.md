# Posterior rectus sheath reattachment after eTEP rives: are we solving the right problem?

**Autores:** Jorge Daes

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Bulging is not wideningBulging is not a recurrence or a simple widening of the abdominal wall\.It is an anterior displacement of the abdominal contour without a fascial defect \[1,2\]\.Clinical perception of bulging varies among patients and surgeons, making objective measurement important\.Ellipse 9 was developed for that purpose and is based on two ellipses: X1 represents the overall abdominal diameter, and X2 the distance between the semilunar lines\.Y2 measures anterior projection, while the c/a ratio measures eccentricitycloser to 1 is flatter; lower values indicate a rounder contour\.Thus, X2 reflects transverse remodeling, whereas Y2 and c/a better characterize bulging \[1,2\]\.This distinction matters because X2 is not bulging\.In our series, substantial X2 expansion could coexist with a flatter abdomen, whereas X2 reduction could coexist with minimal anterior rounding \[1,2\]\.Connors, Belyansky, and colleagues elegantly demonstrated that PRS reloading changes postoperative morphology and reduces semilunar\-line separation compared with bridging \[3\]\.This is an important anatomical observation, but bulging was not measured\.They demonstrated a morphological effect; they did not demonstrate that it prevents bulging\.Halpern et al\. provide complementary evidence: PRS closure produced a substantial additional reduction in semilunar\-line separation, yet bulging occurred in 18\.5% with closure versus 21\.8% in a historical non\-closure cohort \(p = 0\.794\) \[5\]\.This cannot establish equivalence but further separates morphological change from bulging\. Open or closed, bulging can occurBulging first drew our attention after eTEP Rives for small ventral defects with diastasis; after reconstruction of larger defects, improved contour is generally expected\.In series
