# Using Generative Artificial Intelligence to Craft Patient\-Facing Report Summaries: Opportunities for Enhancing Resident Training

**Autores:** Geoffrey Michael Currie; Tracy Yarbrough; Jagruti Shah; K Elizabeth Hawk; Sarah Lonsdale; Min Gyu Noh; Lamia Saiyara Choudhury; Ray Rui Zhang; Anthony Fu Chen; Eric Rohren

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

IntroductionEffective patient communication is an essential skill for radiology and nuclear medicine specialists, yet it remains underemphasized in residency training\. With increasing patient access to electronic medical records, the ability to translate technical reports into patient\-facing summaries is critical\. Generative AI may offer a dual benefit of improving workflow by producing patient\-appropriate summaries and serving as a training tool to help residents hone their communication skills\. MethodsSeventy\-six de\-identified nuclear medicine imaging procedure reports \(covering general nuclear medicine, PET/CT \(positron emission tomography\-computed tomography\), and nuclear cardiology\) were used to generate 608 patient\-facing summaries: four versions of each report were generated using ChatGPT models \(o1\-mini, 4o, 4o\-mini, 4 legacy\) and four versions were written by residents\. Summaries adhered to a four\-sentence maximum and targeted a sixth\-grade reading level\. GPT\-4o was used to critique and rank AI\-generated summaries, and the two best\-performing AI versions were compared with resident summaries\. Blinded evaluations by three experienced nuclear medicine physicians assessed summaries on readability, accuracy, appropriateness, and overall fitness for purpose\. Likelihood Ratio Chi\-Square tests assessed differences in performance\. ResultsGPT o1\-mini was ranked as the best summary in 86% of initial AI comparisons and 100% of final comparative assessments with resident summaries\. GPT 4o and o1\-mini outperformed residents across multiple domains including clarity, health literacy, and tone\. The best human summaries were consistently outperformed by AI in both physician and AI assessments, though discrepancies in ranking priorities \(e\.g\., tolerance for jargon vs\. inaccuracy\) were noted and AI more often produced summaries containing misinterpretations of data in the original reports\. Resident summaries showed wide variation in tone, technicality, and patient appropriateness\. Overall, 65% of all summaries \(AI and human\) were deemed fit\-for\-purpose\. ConclusionGenerative AI models can produce consistently appropriate and readable patient\-facing summaries of imaging procedure reports and may serve as effective tools to develop resident communication skills\. Generative AI may therefore model best practices and provide timely feedback, offering structured opportunities to refine written communication\. Future work should explore its integration into learner\-tailored educational constructs using frameworks such as Bloom’s taxonomy, to enhance resident training and effectiveness of feedback mechanisms\.
