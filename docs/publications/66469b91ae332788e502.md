# Molecular shape evolution of cyanobacterial circadian clock protein KaiC

**Autores:** Yoshihiko Furuike; Shuji Akiyama

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

A primitive form of clock protein KaiC has diverged into autonomous or passive time\-measuring system\. Here we traced molecular shape evolution of ancestral KaiCs using X\-ray solution scattering and structure prediction techniques to date this diversification\. The results show that the oldest KaiC emerged approximately 3\.5–2\.8 billion years ago as a moderately expanded and asymmetric double\-ring hexamer, and subsequently evolved over approximately 1 billion years into a compact and symmetric form essential for the self\-sustained rhythmicity in extant cyanobacteria\. In parallel, the oldest KaiC branched into an oligomer composed of two hexamers after approximately 0\.5 billion years\. It appears that this prototypical dodecamer gained a higher enzymatic activity during the next approximately 0\.4 billion years and was inherited by non\-cyanobacterial lineages as the passive timer capable of responding rapidly to environmental cues\. Consequently, geological fluctuations over approximately 1 billion years since the earliest KaiC appeared led KaiCs into the two distinct evolutionary pathways\.
