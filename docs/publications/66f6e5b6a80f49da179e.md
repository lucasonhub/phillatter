# CT habitat and peritumoral radiomics for predicting progression after repeat TACE in hepatocellular carcinoma: a multicenter study

**Autores:** Fei Wang; Yue Hu; Zhongxing Shi; Xinyu He; Yuhang Sui; Wen Fu\-kai; Jia\-Shan Zhan; Ning\-Jing Yang; Han Xue\-Jing; Jiahui Wang; Jia\-Yi Yang; Wen Lv; Hong\-Fei Liu; Liming Cui

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background This study developed and validated an machine\-learning\-based radiomics model integrating tumor habitat radiomics, peritumoral radiomics, and clinical features to predict progression\-free survival \(PFS\) following repeat transarterial chemoembolization \(TACE\) in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma \(HCC\)\. Methods This retrospective multicenter study included 270 patients with HCC\. Patients from Center 1 were assigned to training \(n = 94\) and internal validation \(n = 41\) cohorts, while Centers 2 \(n = 82\) and 3 \(n = 53\) served as external validation cohorts\. Tumor habitats and 5\-mm peritumoral regions were generated from contrast\-enhanced CT images\. Radiomics features were selected using univariate Cox and LASSO\-Cox regression\. A fusion model and HPC risk score were constructed by integrating selected radiomics signatures and clinical variables\. Performance was assessed using time\-dependent ROC, C\-index, calibration, decision curve analysis, and Kaplan–Meier analyses\. Results The optimal habitat cluster number was K = 2\. Eleven habitat features, three peritumoral features, and alpha\-fetoprotein were retained in the fusion model\. The model achieved AUCs of 0\.923, 0\.921, and 0\.897 for 3\-, 6\-, and 9\-month progression\-free survival in the training cohort, and 0\.932, 0\.863, and 0\.916 in the internal validation cohort, respectively; all external AUCs exceeded 0\.82\. The fusion model showed higher C\-index values than the clinical model across cohorts\. High\-risk patients had significantly shorter PFS than low\-risk patients \(all P &lt; 0\.001\)\. Calibration and decision curve analyses demonstrated good agreement and clinical utility\. Conclusion The CT\-based fusion model and HPC risk score effectively predicted progression risk in HCC\.
