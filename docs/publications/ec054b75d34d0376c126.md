# Boundary\-aware geometric Mamba network for breast tumor segmentation in 3D MRI

**Autores:** ZheJun Kuang; Yuxuan Zhu; Dawen Sun; Jian Zhao

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Accurate breast tumor segmentation in Dynamic Contrast\-Enhanced Magnetic Resonance Imaging \(DCE\-MRI\) is important for reliable diagnosis and treatment planning\. However, complex tumor morphology, diverse spatial structures, and indistinct boundaries remain challenging for existing segmentation methods\. Convolutional neural network\-based methods have limited ability to model long\-range dependencies, while Transformer\-based methods often introduce substantial computational overhead\. Although Mamba\-based approaches provide an efficient alternative, conventional scanning strategies may suffer from directional bias and insufficiently capture three\-dimensional structural variations\. Methods We propose a Boundary\-Aware Geometric Mamba Network \(BAGMNet\) for three\-dimensional breast tumor segmentation\. BAGMNet establishes a structural representation learning framework that combines geometric modeling, multi\-directional state\-space propagation, and boundary\-aware feature refinement\. The Geometric Mamba Encoder constructs complementary spatial representations through multiple geometric transformations, while the Multi\-directional Selective State Space module enhances three\-dimensional dependency modeling and alleviates the directional bias of conventional Mamba scanning\. The Gated State\-space Fusion and Adaptive Skip Recalibration modules further integrate local structural details with global contextual information and enhance boundary\-related representations during feature propagation\. Results Extensive experiments were conducted on the ISPY1 and Yunnan breast MRI datasets\. BAGMNet achieved Dice scores of 70\.34% and 79\.14%, with average surface distances of 9\.79 mm and 8\.96 mm on the ISPY1 and Yunnan datasets, respectively\. Compared with representative convolutional neural network\-, Transformer\-, and Mamba\-based methods, BAGMNet achieved competitive performance in both regional segmentation accuracy and boundary localization\.
