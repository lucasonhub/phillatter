# Measurement\-level clustering of ligamentum flavum thickness identifies distinct phenotypes associated with lumbar lordosis in adult spinal deformity: a proof\-of\-concept study

**Autores:** Dylan Glaser; Ahmad Kareem Almekkawi; Ashlesha S Bhojane; Ali Ebada; Christopher Carron; Salah G\. Aoun; Andrew L\. Abumoussa; Carlos Antonio Bagley

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Study design Retrospective cohort study\. Objectives To identify distinct ligamentum flavum \(LF\) thickness phenotypes in adult spinal deformity \(ASD\) using measurement\-level clustering and to characterize their association with traditional spinopelvic parameters in a proof\-of\-concept analysis\. Methods LF thickness was measured on preoperative MRI in 44 consecutive ASD patients \(2018–2023\), yielding 1,250 measurements; complete clinical and radiographic data were available in 40\. K\-means clustering \( k = 3\) was performed at the measurement level, treating thickness regimes as exploratory and unsupervised\. Patients were assigned to a dominant phenotype, and all inferential comparisons were performed at the patient level\. Between\-phenotype differences in spinopelvic parameters were assessed with independent\-samples t \-tests and Mann–Whitney U \-tests, with Hedges g effect sizes\. Preliminary clinical and radiological outcomes were reported descriptively\. Results Three measurement\-level thickness regimes were identified: Low LF \(2\.21 ± 0\.51 mm, n = 619 measurements\), Medium LF \(3\.92 ± 0\.56 mm, n = 499\), and High LF \(6\.11 ± 0\.90 mm, n = 132\)\. High\-cluster measurements were distributed across 32 patients, but only one patient had High LF as a dominant phenotype and was excluded from comparisons \(43 patients analyzed; 25 Low, 18 Medium\)\. Lumbar lordosis differed significantly between Low and Medium phenotypes \(Low: −44\.6 ± 12\.0° vs\. Medium: −32\.5 ± 8\.9°; p = 0\.003; Hedges g = −1\.10, 95% CI −1\.81 to −0\.39\), a large effect that was robust to nonparametric testing\. Cluster purity was higher in Low LF \(61\.9% vs\. 53\.3%, p = 0\.027; g = 0\.70\)\. Principal component analysis of standard parameters showed that mean LF thickness was moderately correlated with the dominant pelvic\-morphology component \( r = −0\.44, p = 0\.008\), indicating that LF thickness is partially related to, rather than independent of, traditional parameters\. Intra\-rater reliability, assessed by blinded re\-measurement of 15 patients, was moderate \[ICC\(2,1\) = 0\.632, 95% CI 0\.242–0\.855; Cohen kappa for phenotype = 0\.524\], and sensitivity analysis showed that the phenotype\-lordosis association does not survive substitution of the re\-read values \( p = 0\.005 to p = 0\.245\)\. In contrast, the continuous rank correlation does \(rho = \+ 0\.496, p = 0\.0015\)\. Conclusions Measurement\-level clustering identifies distinct LF thickness phenotypes in ASD that are associated with lumbar lordosis\. These exploratory findings establish a methodological foundation and motivate larger, outcome\-based studies with formal measurement\-reliability assessment to determine clinical utility\.
