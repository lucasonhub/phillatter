# Xerogenic medications and clinical markers of salivary hypofunction

**Autores:** Yeon‐Hee Lee; Seonggwang Jeon; Dohoon Kim; Sunyoung Kim; Tae\-Seok Kim; Sang\-woo Lee; Yung‐Kyun Noh

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OBJECTIVES: To determine whether xerogenic medication\-class exposure and chair\-side salivary markers contribute to risk stratification of objective salivary hypofunction across a broad clinical age spectrum, using conventional regression and explainable machine\-learning approaches\. MATERIALS AND METHODS: This retrospective cross\-sectional study included 850 patients \(mean age, 60\.8 ± 15\.6 years; 657 \[77\.3%\] female\) who underwent salivary flow assessment and oral clinical evaluation at Kyung Hee University Dental Hospital\. Patients were categorized into four age groups: &lt;40, 40\-64, 65\-79, and ≥ 80 years\. Unstimulated salivary flow rate \(UFR\), stimulated salivary flow rate \(SFR\), and flow\-rate\-defined hyposalivation were analyzed using age\-group comparisons, multivariable regression models, incremental gradient boosting models, and SHapley Additive exPlanations \(SHAP\)\-based model interpretation\. RESULTS: Mean UFR was significantly lower in older age groups, ranging from 0\.57 ± 0\.30 mL/min in patients aged &lt; 40 years to 0\.36 ± 0\.24 mL/min in those aged ≥ 80 years \(p &lt; 0\.001\), whereas age\-group differences in SFR were less pronounced\. SFR\-defined hyposalivation was most frequent in the ≥ 80\-year group \(17\.3%; p = 0\.007\)\. In adjusted models, the ≥ 80\-year group had lower UFR than the &lt; 40\-year group \(β = \-0\.194; 95% CI, \- 0\.272 to \- 0\.115; p &lt; 0\.001\) and higher odds of SFR\-defined hyposalivation \(OR = 5\.47; 95% CI, 1\.44\-20\.78; p = 0\.013\)\. Female sex was associated with lower UFR \(β = \-0\.055; p = 0\.009\) and SFR \(β = \-0\.369; p &lt; 0\.001\)\. High\-confidence xerogenic medication\-class count was independently associated with SFR\-defined hyposalivation \(OR = 1\.63; 95% CI, 1\.12\-2\.36; p = 0\.010\)\. In incremental machine\-learning models, adding salivary pH and buffer capacity produced the largest improvement in discrimination\. AUROC increased from 0\.468 to 0\.723 for UFR\-defined hyposalivation \(ΔAUROC = 0\.255; DeLong p &lt; 0\.001\) and from 0\.608 to 0\.725 for SFR\-defined hyposalivation \(ΔAUROC = 0\.116; DeLong p = 0\.005\)\. SHAP analysis ranked salivary buffer capacity as the leading contributor for both outcomes, with salivary pH also among the top predictors\. CONCLUSIONS: Objective salivary hypofunction was not fully explained by age, systemic disease burden, or medication burden alone\. Instead, chair\-side salivary functional markers, particularly salivary pH and buffer capacity, captured clinically relevant information about current salivary status\. These findings support a practical, layered approach to identifying patients who may require objective salivary flow assessment\. CLINICAL RELEVANCE: Chair\-side salivary pH and buffer capacity may help clinicians identify patients who warrant objective salivary flow assessment, particularly when considered together with age, sex, and xerogenic medication\-class exposure\.
