# Fine\-Tuning Strategy, Not Just Data: A Statistically Rigorous Audit of Gender and Stereotypical Bias in Embedding Models for HR Recommendation Systems

**Autores:** Ekta Yadav

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Embedding models underpin modern job\-candidate matching: resumes, job descriptions, and queries are encoded as dense vectors, and recommendations come from nearest\-neighbor retrieval over that space\. Any social bias in the embeddings therefore feeds directly into HR recommendations\. We audit six multilingual sentence\-embedding models \(LaBSE\- and E5\-family, both public and custom fine\-tuned\) using Bias\-in\-Bios \(occupational gender bias\) and StereoSet \(stereotypes across profession, race, gender, and religion\)\. We treat statistical validity as a first\-class concern: we identify a non\-independence error in a naive significance test, where pooling non\-independent pairwise similarities inflated a trivial 0\.0046 mean difference to $p \\approx 0$, and replace it with a profession\-level paired Wilcoxon signed\-rank test, individual\-resampling bootstrap confidence intervals, and permutation tests for StereoSet\. Under the corrected methodology, knowledge\-distillation \(KD\) fine\-tuning of E5\-Base significantly increases measured bias on Bias\-in\-Bios \($Δ= \-0\.177$, $p &lt; 10^\{\-8\}$; more negative means more bias\), although this measure is sensitive to global embedding geometry\. The KD result is corroborated by pro\-stereotype patterns in the StereoSet profession category \(uncorrected $p &lt; 0\.05$\)\. Binary\-classification fine\-tuning of the same base model shows no statistically distinguishable change \($p = 0\.218$\)\. Fine\-tuning strategy is thus an actionable design lever for practitioners building HR recommenders\. We also report which benchmark categories are underpowered, and note that two domain\-adapted models could not be included in significance testing due to a software\-access constraint, which limits our conclusions\.
