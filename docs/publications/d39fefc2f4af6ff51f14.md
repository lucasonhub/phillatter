# Smartphone\-Based Virtual Reality Perimetry Quadrant\-Level Validation Against the Humphrey Field Analyzer and Pediatric Feasibility: A Prospective, Single\-Center Study

**Autores:** Süleyman Demir; Ata Baytaroğlu; Serife Nur Ciftci; Suzan Doğruya; Mesut Saka

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: To evaluate quadrant\-wise diagnostic agreement between the Humphrey Field Analyzer \(HFA\) and a low\-cost smartphone\-based virtual reality \(VR\) perimeter through 2 protocol\-matched substudies: \(1\) adults with pathological visual field defects comparing HFA SITA\-Fast \(24\-2 or 30\-2\) with a VR 72\-point full\-field module, and \(2\) healthy children comparing HFA 24\-2 SITA\-Fast with a reduced 24\-point VR module\. Methods: This prospective, single\-center, method\-comparison study analyzed adults and children separately\. Forty adults \(80 eyes; age, 21\-61 years\) and 20 healthy children \(40 eyes; age, 6\-15 years\) underwent paired testing within 4 weeks\. In adults, per\-quadrant mean sensitivity \(MS\) was computed, with defects flagged at MS &lt;15 dB; eye\-level patterns were derived from a 4\-quadrant binary code\. The pediatric module sampled the superior hemifield only; endpoints included feasibility, tolerability, specificity, and Bland\-Altman agreement\. Results: In adults, VR perimetry correlated moderately to strongly with HFA \(r = 0\.69\-0\.90\)\. Bland\-Altman mean deviation \(MD\) bias was \+2\.59 dB \(95% CI, 2\.10\-3\.08 dB\), with limits of agreement from −1\.78 to \+6\.96 dB\. Per\-quadrant intraclass correlation coefficients ranged from 0\.70 to 0\.86; defect\-classification κ was 0\.22 to 0\.57, with 90% to 100% specificity and 25% to 54% sensitivity\. Mean quadrant concordance was 3\.2 of 4; 80\.0% of eyes achieved ≥3 of 4 quadrants\. Eye\-level pattern match was 41\.2%\. A worse\-eye sensitivity analysis confirmed these findings \(MD bias, \+3\.47 dB; κ, 0\.22\-0\.49\)\. In children, completion was 100%, mean VR test duration was shorter than HFA \(3\.8 vs 4\.5 minutes\), and superior\-hemifield specificity was 100%, with a \+8\.3\-dB VR\-vs\-HFA calibration offset\. Conclusions: The adult module demonstrates acceptable correlation with HFA but has a systematic positive MD bias and fair\-to\-moderate defect agreement, supporting a screening role rather than stand\-alone diagnosis\. The pediatric module is feasible and well tolerated with no false\-positives in healthy eyes, warranting validation in pathological samples\.
