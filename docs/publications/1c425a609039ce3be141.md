# Taming Nonconvexity with Epigraph Lifting

**Autores:** Clara Lage; Hok Shing Wong; Matthias Ehrhardt

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

We consider inverse problems regularized by a trained neural network with \\emph\{convex activations\} and \\emph\{unrestricted weights\}\. The resulting optimization problem is generally nonconvex, and gradient\-based methods may converge to poor local minima\. We introduce a \\emph\{penalized epigraph lifting\} of the network and show that the lifted problem admits a \\emph\{difference\-of\-convex \(DC\) decomposition\}\. For Lipschitz continuous activations, we establish \\emph\{finite exactness\}: the original objective is recovered exactly at a finite and explicitly computable penalty threshold $\\barρ$\. Building on this structure, we propose \\emph\{H\-DC\} \(Homotopic DC\), a homotopic scheme that solves a sequence of DC subproblems with increasing $ρ$, warm\-starting each level from the previous solution\. Numerical experiments on synthetic problems, a manifold denoiser regularizer, and image inpainting with a learned Mumford\-\-Shah regularizer show that H\-DC consistently escapes poor local minima and reaches solutions with lower objective values than gradient\-based methods from the same initialization\.
