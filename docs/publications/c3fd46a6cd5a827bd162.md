# SymbolFusion uses relation constraints in neurosymbolic graph learning to model literary structure and emotion trajectories

**Autores:** Yuhang Du

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Literary texts contain recurring imagery, typed symbolic relations, and long\-range emotion shifts that sequence models do not represent explicitly\. This paper presents SymbolFusion, a relation\-constrained neuro\-symbolic graph framework that predicts imagery and symbolic candidates from raw text, validates three literary relation types through an auditable node\-relation compatibility function, and propagates emotion only across valid graph paths\. No gold symbolic or affective annotation is supplied during validation or testing\. The 3,120\-passage corpus is divided by literary work into 2,184 training, 468 validation, and 468 test passages, with an additional author\-disjoint evaluation\. Across ten seeds, SymbolFusion obtains 91\.3% symbolic graph agreement, an emotion consistency score of 0\.880, a symbolic\-emotion alignment score of 0\.820, and a transition\-point F1 of 0\.842\. Against the strongest constraint\-based baseline, the SGA gain is 2\.2% points \(95% bootstrap CI 1\.5–2\.9; p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Controlled comparisons among unconstrained attention, soft penalties, hard masks, and permuted relation types isolate the effect of literary compatibility rules\. Standard metrics, repeated latency measurements, expert\-rating correlations, reproducibility settings, and error analysis delimit the method as an inspectable computational aid rather than a replacement for human literary interpretation\.
