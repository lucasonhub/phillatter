# Human–AI Task Redistribution in Higher Education

**Autores:** Viktor Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Generative artificial intelligence is redistributing academic work among humans, AI, and human–AI collaboration, raising questions about which tasks learners should continue performing independently\. This mixed\-methods study examined task allocation using survey data from 317 university students representing 974 recurring academic tasks and interviews with 24 faculty members across 24 disciplines\. Student data showed substantial movement from human\-only performance toward joint human–AI and AI\-delegated work\. Task redistribution was significantly associated with perceived skill demands and learning opportunities\. Faculty strongly supported preserving independent practice for foundational, embodied, and high\-stakes activities while permitting AI for appropriate peripheral, advanced, and efficiency\-oriented tasks\. Integrating both datasets produced a Human–AI Task Allocation Model organized around a Training Zone–Performance Zone principle\. The findings suggest that appropriate AI delegation should depend on learners’ developmental stage, task purpose, and retained capacity for verification, judgment, and accountability\.
