# Numerical Modeling and Experimental Characterization of an R454B Heat Pump Battery Thermal Management System for Electric Public Transportation

**Autores:** Dilek Özlem Esen; Bayram Haşıl; Sief Addeen Yacoub Shabaneh

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract A heat pump\-based Battery Thermal Management System utilizing the low\-GWP R454B refrigerant \(GWP ≈ 466\) was investigated numerically and experimentally\. Numerically, a Simcenter AMESim electro\-thermal model evaluated a 175S2P Nickel Manganese Cobalt \(NMC\) battery architecture, identifying a nominal peak thermal load of 3852 W and a cycle\-averaged heat generation of 1264 W under a 1C operating profile\. Experimentally, the thermohydraulic performance of series, parallel, and symmetrical 2S\-2P coolant routing topologies was evaluated using resistance\-based thermal simulators under a continuous 8 kW cumulative bounding load \(2 kW per simulator\) at 37\.5 °C \(summer\) and 7 °C \(winter\) ambient conditions\. Summer cooling durations ranged from 114 to 135 seconds \(0\.0639–0\.0762 kWh\), while winter cooling required 50 to 84 seconds \(0\.0339–0\.0535 kWh\)\. Parallel heating required 222 seconds \(0\.0782 kWh\) in summer and 279 seconds \(0\.1755 kWh\) in winter\. While both the parallel and 2S\-2P configurations achieved statistically equivalent system\-level temperature uniformity \(ΔT≈ 1\.5 \- 1\.7 °C\), the symmetrical 2S\-2P hybrid topology proved to be the optimal architecture due to its balance of rapid thermal response and minimal compressor energy consumption\. By achieving system\-level temperature uniformity statistically equivalent to the parallel configuration while requiring the lowest compressor energy and shortest cooling duration, the 2S\-2P topology offers an effective and balanced solution for dynamic multipack cooling requirements\.
