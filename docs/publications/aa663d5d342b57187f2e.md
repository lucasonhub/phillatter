# A Theoretical Framework for Non\-Invasive Neurosurgery Utilizing Biosonic and Bioneurosonic Resonance Properties of the Human Voice

**Autores:** abdelhadi Abouelfida

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper establishes a novel neuroscientific paradigm that defines the human brain not merely as a passive acoustic receptor, but as an active generator of synchronized biosonic \(mechanical/fluid\-conducted\) and bioneurosonic \(electrophysiological\) vibrations\. We present the foundational mathematical equations governing how the brain constructs intrinsic &quot;sound units&quot; \(\\\(U\_\{s\}\\\)\) via non\-linear phase\-coupling and carrier wave modulation across neural networks\. Furthermore, we introduce a theoretical framework for non\-invasive surgical intervention utilizing focused external human vocal frequencies \(\\\(V\_\{ext\}\\\)\)\. By calculating the dynamic phase delays \(Δ t\) required to induce precise constructive or destructive wave interference at targeted cerebral focal points, we outline how a trained operator can achieve localized tissue ablation, neuromodulation, or localized seizure suppression\. This work bridges the gap between acoustic physics, computational neuroscience, and non\-invasive neurosurgical therapies\.
