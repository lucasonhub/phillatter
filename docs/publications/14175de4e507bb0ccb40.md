# Influence of intrinsic factors on at\-sea behavior of late chick\-rearing emperor penguins \(Aptenodytes forsteri\) at Cape Crozier, Antarctica

**Autores:** Parker Forman; David R\. Thompson; Kimberly T\. Goetz; Markus Horning; Matthew H\. Pinkerton; Birgitte I\. McDonald

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Intrinsic differences among individuals can shape how marine predators forage in heterogeneous environments\. For emperor penguins at Cape Crozier, the southernmost breeding colony in the Ross Sea and a potential climate refuge, the influence of sex, size, and body condition on at\-sea behavior during late chick\-rearing is unknown\. Using data from eight emperor penguins, we identified two behavioral groups through cluster analysis; both traveled comparable distances from the colony but differed in dive depth, dive rate, and the use of the seafloor\. Group membership was associated with sex but not defined by it, as behavioral grouping and sex explained different aspects of trip behavior\. A Hidden Markov Model \(HMM\) further showed that a simpler parameter set based on horizontal movement \(step length, turning angle\) effectively classified key at\-sea behaviors \(Rest, Foraging, and Transit states\), and revealed that adding dive depth as a parameter did not improve foraging state discrimination\. Penguin dive patterns suggest sex\-based differences: females had higher stroke rates and dived more frequently but shallower, while males dived less frequently but deeper\. Dive depth showed a nonlinear relationship with body condition, with individuals in intermediate condition reaching the greatest depths, while body mass had no detectable effect\. Together, our results provide an initial description of variation in late chick\-rearing foraging behavior at Cape Crozier\. The findings suggest that emperor penguins exhibit different foraging strategies influenced by sex and physical condition, which produces variation in dive behavior and use of bathymetric features\. Such differences could affect how individuals exploit a dynamic marine environment and provide a foundation for understanding the factors that shape emperor penguin foraging behavior\.
