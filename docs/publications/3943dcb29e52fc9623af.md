# Evaluating OpenEvidence Across Pediatric Subspecialties: A Guideline\-Anchored Expert Review

**Autores:** R Brandon Hunter; Lauren M\. Hess; Douglas Wells; Duy M Dinh; Hani Abou Hatab; Mikael Guzman Karlsson; Sutapa Khatua; Denver Niles; Bradford Nguyen; Shawki Qasim; Marla BK Sammer; Ila Singh; Sanjiv Mehta; Andy Tsai; Rajkumar Venkatramani; Jennifer Benjamin

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OBJECTIVE: To evaluate the quality, safety, and citation characteristics of OpenEvidence across pediatric subspecialties\. METHODS: Guideline\-anchored clinical questions spanning high, moderate, and low evidence certainty across 6 pediatric subspecialties were submitted to OpenEvidence\. Expert reviewer pairs independently scored responses using the validated Quality Analysis of Medical Artificial Intelligence \(QAMAI\) instrument \(6\-30 range, higher scores indicate better quality\) and a modified Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality harm assessment \(0\-7 range, higher scores indicate more harm\), followed by consensus reconciliation\. Citation analysis examined overlap with guideline references, claim substantiation, and pediatric specificity\. Response consistency was assessed across 5 repeated queries\. RESULTS: Seventeen questions were evaluated\. The median QAMAI score was 27/30 \(IQR, 24\-29; &quot;very good&quot; quality\)\. Potential harm was low \(median 0/7, range 0\-3\), though 6 of 17 responses carried nonzero harm potential\. OpenEvidence cited the primary guideline in 15 of 17 questions, yet only 17\.6% of responses showed strong citation overlap \(≥75% shared references\) with the corresponding guidelines\. OpenEvidence&\#x27;s citations reliably supported the claims they accompanied \(88\.2% with high substantiation\), and 70\.8% of reviewer assessments rated the evidence base as comparable in quality to that of the corresponding guidelines\. In total, 35\.3% of responses cited studies lacking pediatric specificity\. Responses were clinically consistent across repeated queries \(77\.9% had no meaningful or only minor variation\); no contradictory recommendations were observed\. CONCLUSIONS: OpenEvidence produced high\-quality responses with low expert\-rated potential for harm across multiple pediatric subspecialties\. The pediatric specificity of cited evidence warrants attention, reflecting the broader challenge of applying artificial intelligence tools that are trained on adult\-predominant literature to pediatric populations\.
