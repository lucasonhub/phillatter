# A Quasi‐n‐i‐p Architecture Realized by Sequential Lamination Delivers 20\.58% Efficiency in Inverted Organic Solar Cells

**Autores:** Silu Huang; Guangquan Zhang; Yongjoon Cho; Sein Chung; Zhenmin Zhao; Jiancheng Zhong; Xiaoge Huang; Wuning Wei; Lixing Tan; Anhai Liang; Yufei Wang; Guangye Zhang; Kilwon Cho; Zhipeng Kan

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Inverted organic solar cells \(OSCs\) are promising for their enhanced operational stability; however, their power conversion efficiencies \(PCEs\) have lagged those of conventional devices, largely due to interfacial losses at the active layer/metal\-oxide interface\. Here, we develop a sequential lamination strategy to construct a quasi\-n\-i\-p active layer comprising an acceptor\-rich layer, a bulk heterojunction \(BHJ\) core, and a donor\-rich layer\. This vertically engineered architecture establishes cascade energy\-level alignment and preserves interfacial integrity, thereby facilitating efficient charge extraction and suppressing recombination losses\. As a result, the optimal device achieves a PCE of 20\.58%, with a certified efficiency of 20\.07%, placing it among the highest reported for single\-junction inverted OSCs\. This architecture was successfully fabricated with different donor\-rich layers, BHJ cores, and acceptor\-rich layers, demonstrating broader material applicability across multiple material combinations\. Interfacial adhesion and vertical compositional control improve electrical continuity, enhance charge generation and transport, and reduce trap\-assisted and bimolecular recombination, leading to higher open\-circuit voltage, short\-circuit current density, and fill factor\. This work provides a scalable route to active\-layer engineering in inverted OSCs and offers a general strategy for high\-performance organic photovoltaic fabrication\.
