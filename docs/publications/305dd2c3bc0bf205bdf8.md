# Decoding molecular heterogeneity in lupus nephritis: integrating single\-cell and spatial multi\-omics for endotype\-driven precision therapeutics

**Autores:** Jun Zhou; Wei Xiao; Hui Jian

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Lupus nephritis \(LN\) develops in 40–60% of patients with systemic lupus erythematosus \(SLE\) and remains the leading cause of renal morbidity and progression to end\-stage renal disease \(ESRD\)\. While the upstream immunological events initiating LN—including loss of self\-tolerance, pathogenic autoantibody production, glomerular immune complex deposition, and type I interferon \(IFN\) activation—are broadly shared, accumulating evidence demonstrates that these convergent triggers give rise to molecularly heterogeneous tissue injury programs at the renal level\. This fundamental divergence provides a mechanistic explanation for the inconsistent therapeutic responses observed with standardized immunosuppressive protocols\. Advances in single\-cell transcriptomics, spatial gene expression mapping, CITE\-seq, and cross\-platform multi\-omic integration have deconvolved this heterogeneity into at least five recognizable tissue\-level injury programs: \(i\) an IFN\-γ\-polarized macrophage–tubular epithelial cell co\-activation circuit, \(ii\) a Tfh–B cell ectopic germinal center pathway, \(iii\) a neutrophil extracellular trap \(NET\)\-driven inflammatory cascade, \(iv\) a podocyte\-autonomous injury program, and \(v\) a TREM2\+ macrophage\-orchestrated fibrotic program\. Concurrently, multiplexed urinary biomarker platforms are under development as non\-invasive surrogates for tissue\-level pathogenic activity\. This review synthesizes current understanding of disease mechanisms, molecular profiling evidence, biomarker candidates, and mechanism\-directed therapies corresponding to each tissue injury program\. We map existing and investigational agents—including anifrolumab, obinutuzumab, voclosporin, CAR\-T cell therapy, and complement inhibitors—to their mechanistic targets and delineate the design requirements for prospective biomarker\-stratified trials essential to validating molecularly guided treatment selection\.
