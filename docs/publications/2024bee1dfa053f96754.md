# Balancing Temperature Stability and Dielectric Loss in Microwave Ceramics via Embedded Polar Nanoregions

**Autores:** Zhihan Zhang; Hongyu Yang; Yihang Li; Linzhuang Xing; Dongyan Zhang; Zhimin Li; Yue Hao

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

ABSTRACT The deployment of 5G/6G communication systems demands microwave dielectric ceramics that simultaneously possess a near‐zero temperature coefficient of resonant frequency and an ultra‐low dielectric loss \(tan δ \), which represents a long‑standing challenge that demands effective optimization strategies\. Herein, the fundamental limitation is substantially alleviated by a strategy integrating interpretable machine learning with entropy engineering, enabling the reverse design of a medium‐entropy perovskite microwave ceramic composition \(Ca 0\.7 Sm 0\.27 Nd 0\.03 \)\[Ti 0\.686 \(Mg 1/3 Nb 2/3 \) 0\.014 Al 0\.3 \]O 3 \. This ceramic material delivers a desired combination perfomance of τ f = −0\.18 ppm/°C and tan δ = 1\.19 × 10 −4 at 4\.6 GHz\. Multiscale characterization reveals that entropy‐induced chemical disorder creates isolated polar nanoregions that pin charged defects, transforming them from a source of relaxation loss into static structural units, which is different from the conventional defect–loss correlation mechanism\. The optimized ceramic is implemented in a dielectric resonator antenna, which demonstrates superior performance: a radiation efficiency of 96\.2% and a gain of 6\.06 dBi\. The findings establish a generalizable framework for navigating complex compositional spaces and resolving conflicting properties in perovskite materials\.
