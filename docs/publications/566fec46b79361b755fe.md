# Ethics and Use of Artificial Intelligence for Service Delivery in Nigerian Academic Libraries

**Autores:** Goodluck Okwudiri Allison; Chidiebere Evans\-Otuza

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial Intelligence \(AI\) in library offers a transformative opportunity to enhance service delivery amidst persistent staffing and funding deficits\. However, this technological shift creates tension regarding ethical governance, data privacy, and cultural representation\. This study investigated the ethical dimensions and operational usage of AI for service delivery in Nigerian academic libraries, with policy frameworks, algorithmic justice, data privacy, and technological equity\. The study adopted a descriptive survey research design\. The population comprised 8,323 certified librarians in Nigeria, from which a sample size of 368 was selected using a convenience/purposive sampling technique\. Data collection was executed using a validated self\-structured 5\-point Likert scale questionnaire administered during the EDO 2025 Nigerian Library Association \(NLA\) Conference\. A total of 318 valid copies were retrieved, yielding an 86\.4% response rate\. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, including frequency counts, percentages, mean, and standard deviation\. The results revealed that while librarians demonstrate a high overall level of ethical awareness and a desire for structural policy frameworks \(M = 3\.64\), their specific practical awareness regarding daily data privacy and intellectual property implications remains high \(M = 3\.79\)\. Result showed that current library professional ethics are moderate \(M=3\.36\) to regulate AI adoption\. Furthermore, respondents reported a moderately critical level of algorithmic bias moderate \(M=3\.10\), strongly indicating that current Western\-centric AI systems marginalize local research, regional cultural contexts, and indigenous Nigerian languages\. Concerns surrounding data privacy, cloud breaches, and third\-party commercial data\-mining also scored moderate \(M=3\.18\), exposing the vulnerabilities of libraries operating with sub\-baseline digital infrastructures\. The study concluded that forcing AI adoption without context\-responsive ethical governance exacerbates algorithmic injustice and risks compromising user trust\. It is recommended that stakeholders co\-design localized AI deployment policies, conduct rigorous pre\-deployment algorithmic audits, and establish human\-AI collaborative frameworks for patron confidentiality against aggressive data mining\.
