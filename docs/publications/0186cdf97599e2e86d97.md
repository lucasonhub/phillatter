# Iwato, Atsushi: A History of Consciousness — From the Voices of the Gods to the Emergence of the “I,” and on to AI

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

“If people 3,000 years ago did not have consciousness like ours today, how did they think?” This paper began with a conversation with a friend\. Julian Jaynes’s theory does more than propose the provocative hypothesis that ancient people did not possess consciousness in the same form that we do today; it also forces us to reconsider what we mean by the word “consciousness,” which we use so casually\. Here, “consciousness” does not simply mean being awake, or having the capacity to see and feel\. What concerned Jaynes was an inward form of consciousness in which we possess a point of view—an “I”—within the mind, mentally traverse the past and future, and reflect on ourselves\. This paper first considers the relationship between what appears in consciousness and the nonconscious processes operating behind it, using concepts such as Jaynes’s bicameral mind, mind\-space, and the analog “I\.” In the second half, I extend the questions raised by Jaynes and consider, through five aspects—subjectivity, time, causality, possibility, and meaning—how the emergence of this inward form of consciousness allows the world to be experienced as “my world\.” These five aspects are not intended as a summary of Jaynes’s theory itself\. Rather, they are an attempt, starting from his ideas, to explore in my own way the workings and possibilities of consciousness\. Finally, I ask whether human consciousness in its present form was an inevitable outcome of evolution, or merely one of several possible forms of cognition, and consider the possibility that AI may one day develop toward a form of consciousness different from our own\.
