# From life cycle assessment to life cycle thinking in agri\-food systems: lessons from the avocado supply chain

**Autores:** Margarita Baquero; Villi Ieremia; Anton Van Oyen; Beatriz Rivela; Annemie Geeraerd Ameryckx; Angel Diego Ramirez

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Agri\-food systems face growing pressure to demonstrate environmental performance and support sustainability transitions\. In this context, Life Cycle Assessment \(LCA\) generates evidence on environmental impacts, while Life Cycle Thinking \(LCT\) helps integrate that information into decision\-making across supply chains\. This review evaluates LCA and LCT for the avocado sector by synthesising current LCA evidence, analysing the transferability of results to LCA‑based ecolabels and certifications, examining how LCT related food‑policy instruments create or limit incentives for supply‑chain actors and exploring pathways for translating life\-cycle information into coordinated sustainability action\. The assessment combines a systematic appraisal of English‑language avocado LCAs with regionally relevant studies, in Spanish and German, and field insights from sustained engagement with Ecuadorian producers\. Avocado LCAs have expanded in number, supply chain and geographic coverage, consistently identifying production \(e\.g\., fertilisation, fuel, energy use\) and distribution \(e\.g\., transport distances\) as key environmental hotspots\. Despite mounting pressure to reduce impacts, producers and distributors face limited incentives to move beyond compliance with non‑LCA certifications \(e\.g\., GLOBALG\.A\.P\., Rainforest Alliance\)\. Retailers increasingly promote LCA‑based approaches, yet their efforts are hindered by data fragmentation, methodological inconsistency, and traceability challenges\. Consumers express strong interest in environmental information, particularly when presented through simplified, multi‑criteria ecolabels, though such formats risk oversimplification or misrepresentation\. Methodological gaps persist, including inconsistent treatment of carbon sequestration, biodiversity, and nursery stages, while opportunities in circularity and by‑product valorisation remain underexplored\. Overall, LCT and LCA offer essential guidance for improving the sustainability of avocado supply chains, but their effectiveness depends on methodological standardisation, ecolabel harmonisation, clear policy direction, credible differentiation schemes, and robust data systems\. By connecting environmental assessment with implementation, governance, and decision\-making processes, this review outlines a pathway for embedding LCT within policy and practice, enabling more coherent, transparent, and actionable sustainability governance for agri\-food supply chains\.
