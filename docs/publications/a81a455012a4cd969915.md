# Beyond Answer Voting: Fine\-Grained Correction of LLM Reasoning through Graph Alignment across Sampled Traces

**Autores:** Haotian Liu; Yingjian Chen; Xiaohang Xu; Lang Huang; Yusuke Iwasawa; Yutaka Matsuo; Irene Li

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Large language models increasingly tackle tasks that require multi\-step reasoning, where errors in intermediate claims can affect the final answer\. Self\-consistency \(SC\) improves reasoning by voting over sampled final answers, but its vote leaves intermediate disagreements unresolved\. To address this problem, we introduce DDRG \(Disagreement\-Driven Verification Guided by Reasoning Graphs\), a framework for selective correction from a frozen bank of reasoning traces\. Our insight is that graph alignment can expose claim\-level disagreements and provide correction evidence without expanding the sampled trace bank\. DDRG builds source\-grounded claim graphs and sends localized conflicts to an answer\-blind verifier\. A verifier\-supported refutation that reaches the majority answer&\#x27;s supporting path triggers one focused repair; otherwise, DDRG returns the exact SC vote\. Experimental results across five benchmarks show that native zero\-shot DDRG improves macro\-average accuracy over direct answering by 8\.65 and 15\.83 percentage points for Qwen2\.5\-7B and Llama3\.1\-8B\. It also exceeds CoT, the evaluated GoT and GoV question\-answering adaptations, and SC on this macro metric\. DDRG links local verification to selective correction without enlarging the initial candidate bank\.
