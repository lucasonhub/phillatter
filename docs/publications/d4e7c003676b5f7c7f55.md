# Anticipatory Anxiety and Psychological Well\-Being in Middle\-Aged Adults with Claustrophobia

**Autores:** Reena Kumari Jain

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Claustrophobia can produce anxiety before exposure to confined environments, but its relationship with psychological well\-being remains insufficiently explored\. This study examined anticipatory anxiety and psychological well\-being among middle\-aged Indian adults with claustrophobia and compared individuals with predominant fears of restriction and suffocation\. A cross\-sectional survey included 12 adults aged 40 to 60 years who reported a diagnosis of claustrophobia\. Participants completed the Claustrophobia Questionnaire and Ryff&\#x27;s 42\-item Scales of Psychological Well\-Being\. Moderate anticipatory anxiety was reported by 58\.3% of participants, while 83\.3% demonstrated moderate psychological well\-being\. Fear of restriction predominated in 66\.7% of participants\. Anticipatory anxiety was not significantly associated with overall psychological well\-being but showed significant negative correlations with self\-acceptance \(s = \-\.79\) and personal growth \(rs = \-\.67\)\. Differences between predominant fear groups were not statistically significant\. These preliminary findings suggest that anticipatory anxiety may be associated with selected dimensions of psychological well\-being\. Larger studies using dedicated measures of anticipatory anxiety are needed to confirm these relationships and examine their clinical implications\.
