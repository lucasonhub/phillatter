# Helminthic therapy and hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer \(Lynch syndrome\): what the data really say

**Autores:** Jacques Le Marois

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Lynch syndrome, historically called HNPCC \(hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer\), is an inherited genetic predisposition syndrome that raises the risk of developing colorectal cancer and certain other cancers\. A carrier may nevertheless never develop cancer\. It is mainly associated with a pathogenic germline variant in one of the four DNA mismatch repair genes \(MLH1, MSH2, MSH6 or PMS2\)\. Certain deletions of the EPCAM gene can also silence the expression of MSH2 and cause Lynch syndrome\. It is inherited in an autosomal dominant manner: a carrier parent has a one in two chance of passing the mutation to each child\. According to Wikipedia, carriers make up about 0\.35% of the population \(1 in 279\) and about 3% of colorectal cancers\. The risk of developing colorectal or endometrial cancer varies considerably with the gene concerned, sex and age\. It cannot be summarized by a single estimate applicable to all carriers of Lynch syndrome\. Other cancers are associated: stomach, ovary, small bowel, pancreas, urinary and biliary tracts, brain, skin\. Surveillance relies notably on regular colonoscopy, starting at age 20 to 25 for MLH1 and MSH2, and at age 35 for MSH6 and PMS2\. This article explores, with the help of an AI, the effect that therapeutic helminths could have on this risk of colorectal cancer\. Please read its content as hypotheses to explore and verify, not as established conclusions\. Any correction is welcome: publications\[a\]lemarois\.com\. This article is also available in French\.
