# A Review of AI\-Based Women Safety and Emergency Response Systems

**Autores:** Achal M\.  Pande; Vaishnavi J\.  Katare; Ashutosh R\.  Parde; Gagan S\.  Dave; Om S\.  Wadurkar; Prof\.  S\.  S\.  Mamarde

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Women&\#x27;s safety applications have increasingly adopted mobile sensing, Global Positioning System \(GPS\), cloud services, and artificial intelligence to support emergency communication and risk awareness\. However, existing systems differ considerably in their methods of detecting emergencies and responding to them\. Some applications primarily provide manual SOS functions and location sharing, while others introduce voice activation, contextual risk assessment, offline communication, predictive analysis, or evidence collection\. This paper reviews recent technology\-based women safety and emergency response systems, with particular attention to their emergency triggers, use of artificial intelligence, location services, evidence collection, communication mechanisms, and limitations\. The reviewed literature covers mobile applications, predictive safety systems, contextual monitoring, and cloud\-supported emergency platforms\. The analysis shows that current solutions have improved the speed and accessibility of emergency assistance, but several systems remain focused on individual functions rather than integrated risk detection and response\. Important gaps remain in continuous behaviour analysis, anomaly detection, automatic triggering, reliable operation under limited connectivity, evidence management, and reduction of false alarms\. Based on these observations, the paper discusses a proposed research direction for an integrated AI\-based women safety platform designed to combine predictive risk assessment, multiple emergency triggers, location tracking, evidence collection, and automated emergency response\.
