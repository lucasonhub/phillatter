# Dried blood spot IP\-10 as a biomarker of tuberculosis severity and treatment responses: results from a phase IIb trial of adjunctive acetylsalicylic acid and ibuprofen

**Autores:** Iselin Høgbakk Edlund; Noelia Alonso‐Rodríguez; Chiara Sopegno; Judith Farrés; Nino Gogichadze; Lilibeth Arias; Kaori L\. Fonseca; Kennedy Otwombe; Ziyaad Waja; Tumelo Moloantoa; Natasha Pillay; Thabiso Seiphetlo; Neil Martinson; Cristina Vilaplana; Anne Ma Dyrhol\-Riise

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Host\-directed therapies may improve tuberculosis \(TB\) outcomes by modulating immune responses and attenuating hyperinflammation, yet accessible biomarkers remain limited\. We hypothesized that interferon\-γ–inducible protein 10 \(IP\-10/CXCL10\) measured in dried blood spot \(DBS\), a low\-threshold and field\-adapted sampling method, has potential as a biomarker for treatment response in pulmonary TB\. Accordingly, we assessed IP\-10 levels in patients receiving adjunctive acetylsalicylic acid \(ASA\) or ibuprofen \(IBU\) alongside TB standard therapy\. Methods IP\-10 levels were quantified longitudinally in DBS from 120 pulmonary TB patients enrolled in the SMA\-TB clinical trial \(NCT04575519\) at two sites in South Africa\. Samples collected at baseline, week 2, week 8, and week 24 were analyzed by the enzyme\-linked immunosorbent assay \(ELISA\)\. Associations with disease severity and treatment response were assessed, and the predictive value of IP\-10 for microbiological, clinical, and radiological outcomes was evaluated\. Results IP\-10 levels correlated with TB disease severity, with higher concentrations observed in patients with elevated clinical and radiological scores and positive sputum acid\-fast\-bacilli staining\. This association was reflected in treatment response, as patients with good clinical outcomes exhibited an earlier decline in IP\-10 \(week 8, p=0\.0005\), whereas those with poor outcomes showed a delayed decline \(week 24, p&lt;0\.0001\)\. Overall, IP\-10 levels decreased during treatment irrespective of intervention or HIV status, with no significant differences between treatment arms at week 24\. Notably, IP\-10 levels at week 2 predicted rapid sputum culture conversion in the IBU arm, as well as in HIV\-negative patients in the ASA arm \(AUC: ASA 0\.79; IBU 0\.76\)\. Conclusion IP\-10 levels measured in DBS are associated with disease severity and treatment response and may serve as a biomarker for therapy monitoring and early prediction of microbiological outcomes in TB, irrespective of adjunctive host\-directed therapies\. These findings require validation in larger patient cohorts before the method can be implemented in clinical practice\.
