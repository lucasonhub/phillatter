## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026What would happen if Brassierian operations ceased to be locally concentrated philosophical competencies and became population\-scale capacities? This paper treats that question not as a thought experiment about universal agreement with Ray Brassier, but as a counterfactual simulation problem\. “Brassierian operations” are operationalized minimally as capacities for revising commitments under impersonal constraints, resisting the identification of intelligibility with human phenomenological privilege, distinguishing causal patterning from conceptual articulation, and permitting inherited conceptual resources to become obsolete\. The simulation then varies population penetration, network topology, revision speed, institutional coupling, external\-memory persistence, resource constraints, and rates of environmental reconfiguration\. The central result is non\-monotonic\. Increasing the distribution of rational\-revision capacities can initially improve error correction and reduce dependence on inherited conceptual fixations, but universalization does not simply produce a maximally rational population\. Beyond specific configurations, the same operations can destabilize the social, institutional, and mnemonic infrastructures required for their own reproduction\. Rational revision therefore exhibits fragility transfer: robustness gained at one operational layer can relocate vulnerability to another\. At population scale, philosophical penetration can consequently modify the conditions under which philosophy itself remains a non\-redundant practice\. The paper distinguishes philosophical doctrine from the operational shapes through which doctrines propagate, transform institutions, reorganize observation and inference, and alter subsequent possibility spaces\. This permits a stronger counterfactual: if Brassierian operations became sufficiently distributed across humans, machines, institutions, and external memory, their successful penetration could eliminate the need for specifically “Brassierian,” philosophical, or even human mediation\. The limiting case is therefore not universal Brassierianism but self\-obsolescence: successful philosophical penetration produces configurations in which the philosophy that initiated the transformation ceases to constitute a necessary passage point\. \(direct link \)
