# SubTerrAInNet: A Multimodal CNN\-GNN Framework for Collapse Susceptibility Prediction in Underground Mining Tunnels

**Autores:** 

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The collapse of underground mine workings remains a severe safety problem in artisanal and small\-scale mining, where engineered ground support, geotechnical instrumentation, and systematic rock\-mass characterization are often unavailable\. Motivated by the 12 August 2026 Kassa mine\-pit collapse in Barkin Ladi, Plateau State, this study presents SubTerrAInNet, a multimodal deep learning framework that integrates high\-resolution tunnel imagery with low\-cost geological and structural observations for segment\-level collapse\-susceptibility classification\. The visual stream uses a transfer\-learned Residual Network\-50 \(ResNet\-50\) convolutional neural network \(CNN\) to extract image signatures associated with cracking, seepage staining, weathering, and spalling, while a graph neural network \(GNN\) encodes tunnel geometry, overburden, joint orientation, water condition, crack density, and inter\-segment connectivity\. The dataset comprises 8,500 RGB images linked to 264 annotated tunnel segments from 15 abandoned workings in the Jos Plateau\. Partitioning was performed group\-wise at the physical\-site level: each mine site was assigned exclusively to training, validation, or hold\-out testing, so that no tunnel segment or image from a given site occurred in more than one partition\. On the reported hold\-out test set, SubTerrAInNet achieved 91\.82% accuracy, an F1\-score of 0\.88, and an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve \(AUC\-ROC\) of 0\.95\. This represents a 2\.82\-percentage\-point accuracy gain over the best conventional baseline, Extremely Randomized Trees \(89\.0%\), and a 1\.62\-point gain over the strongest reported deep learning baseline, long short\-term memory with attention \(LSTM\-Attention; 90\.2%\)\. Ablation results further show that multimodal fusion outperforms CNN\-only and GNN\-only variants\. The final evaluation also considers collapse\-class sensitivity, specificity, precision, precision\- recall area under the curve \(PR\-AUC\), probability calibration, and bootstrap confidence intervals\. Gradient\-weighted class activation mapping \(Grad\-CAM\) is used to relate image\-driven predictions to visible degradation precursors\. SubTerrAInNet therefore provides a resource\-aware framework for camera\-assisted hazard screening, although prospective external validation, calibrated uncertainty estimation, and engineering review remain necessary before operational deployment\.
