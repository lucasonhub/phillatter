# Artificial Intelligence in Eye Disease: A Systematic Review of Screening, Diagnosis, Prognosis, Treatment\-Response Prediction, and Clinical Decision Support

**Autores:** Manal M Alharbi; Emtenan M\. Alharbi; Alanoud S Alsalamah; Hanan Asiri; Najla Beati; Renad AlAtawi; Amr A\. Arafat

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background/Objectives: Artificial intelligence \(AI\) is expanding eye care beyond image\-based detection toward prognosis, treatment\-response prediction, monitoring, triage, and clinician\-facing decision support\. We evaluated AI across eye\-disease screening, diagnosis, prognosis, treatment response, and decision support, emphasizing validation, explainability, and clinical readiness\. Methods: This PROSPERO\-registered review followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta\-Analyses \(PRISMA\) 2020 statement\. Six databases plus supplementary routes identified English\-language literature published from 1 January 2015 through 10 August 2026\. Included studies underwent design\-mapped Joanna Briggs Institute \(JBI\)/adapted\-JBI item\-level appraisal; heterogeneity precluded meta\-analysis\. Results: The evidence base comprised 270 studies\. Diabetic retinopathy/diabetic macular oedema was the largest clinical area \(82\), followed by retinal/multi\-disease AI \(52\), glaucoma \(46\), and age\-related macular degeneration \(AMD\)/macular disease \(29\); convolutional neural network \(CNN\)/convolutional deep learning was the largest primary method group \(136\)\. External validation was confirmed in 130 studies, including 95 with a clinical/geographic/prospective/real\-world external\-validation component \(86 clinical\-only and nine mixed clinical \+ technical\); 49 had a strict direct clinical comparator and 90 used explainability\. Pediatric/pediatric\-relevant evidence accounted for 30 studies, while explicit optometry/optometrist involvement appeared in only three\. Conclusions: Ophthalmic AI spans diagnosis through clinical management, but translation remains limited by heterogeneous outcomes, incomplete independent validation, few direct comparators, and sparse evaluation of safety, workflow effects, and patient outcomes\.
