# THE INTEGRATIVE NEURO\-FASCIAL SYSTEM IN MUSCULOSKELETAL REHABILITATION: CONCEPTUALIZING THE KOVALENKO SYSTEM WITHIN THE BIOPSYCHOSOCIAL PARADIGM

**Autores:** Olga V\. Puchyna; Ольга Анатоліївна Черепєхіна; Vasyl Mazin; Анастасія Володимирівна Турубарова; Valerіі Bulanov; Mariia Hrechanyk

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction\. Musculoskeletal disorders remain a leading cause of functional limitations, reduced quality of life, and disability worldwide, with increasing prevalence driven by aging, chronic stress, and lifestyle changes\. In the context of prolonged crisis conditions, including war, rehabilitation requires a multidimensional approach integrating physical and psychoemotional factors\. Despite the growing recognition of the biopsychosocial model, fragmentation between physical and psychological interventions persists in rehabilitation practice\. Aim\. To conceptualize and theoretically substantiate the Integrative Neuro\-Fascial System\-Kovalenko \(INFS\-K\) as a comprehensive framework for musculoskeletal rehabilitation within the biopsychosocial paradigm\. Materials and methods\. The study employed a conceptual\-methodological approach based on interdisciplinary synthesis of contemporary theoretical models, including the biopsychosocial framework and neuroplasticity\. Analytical generalization of clinical practice and theoretical modeling were used to develop a structured conceptual framework\. Results\. The INFS\-K was conceptualized as a multilevel integrative system combining psychoemotional, behavioral\-motor, and neurophysiological components of rehabilitation\. Psychoemotional regulation is defined as an integrated functional element associated with patient engagement, movement quality, and motor pattern formation\. The system includes six interrelated components: psychoemotional stabilization, motivational readiness, body awareness, motor activation, neuroplastic adaptation, and functional integration, forming a dynamic hierarchical structure that reflects the internal logic of the rehabilitation process\. Conclusions\. The proposed INFS\-K extends existing rehabilitation models by shifting from additive to structurally integrated approaches, where psychoemotional processes are embedded within the core architecture of recovery\. The system contributes to the theoretical development of rehabilitation science by offering a unified conceptual framework aligned with contemporary understandings of neuroplasticity and patient\-centered care\.
