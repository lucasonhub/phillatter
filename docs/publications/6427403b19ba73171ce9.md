# Consequences of tilt of the magnetic field in magnetically enhanced capacitively coupled plasma reactors

**Autores:** Mark J\. Kushner

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Magnetically enhanced reactive ion etching \(MERIE\) reactors are capacitively coupled plasmas \(CCPs\) used in microelectronic fabrication with a static magnetic field that is typically parallel to the wafer\. The purpose of the magnetic field is to increase plasma density and ion fluxes, while affording some control to the ion energy and angular distribution \(IEAD\) incident onto the wafer\. The uniformity of the plasma produced reactive fluxes onto the wafer are sensitive to the orientation of the magnetic field with respect to the wafer\. For example, fluxes are sensitive to the tilt of the magnetic field with respect to the surface of the wafer, as opposed to being strictly parallel to the wafer\. There is some likelihood that this angle may change from before to after preventative maintenance of a commercial MERIE reactor\. In this paper, we discuss results from a computational investigation of the consequences of tilt in the magnetic field in MERIE reactors on the uniformity of ion fluxes and IEADs delivered to the wafer\. This investigation was performed for CCPs sustained in Ar and Ar/Cl2 mixtures at tens of mTorr pressure with magnetic fields up to 150 G\. We found that tilt of the magnetic field of as small as 1 o produces significant side\-to\-side non\-uniformity in ion fluxes delivered across a 20 cm wafer\. There is less sensitivity of the IEAD to the tilt angle\. The dynamics of electric field reversals, which are common in MERIE reactors, are sensitive to the tilt angle, with the electric field reversals traversing diago\-nally across the reactor as the tilt angle increases\. The role of digital twins in insuring consistent performance of plasma reactors is discussed\.
