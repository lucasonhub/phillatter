# IMPACT OF EXTENDED REALITY USAGE FOR OFFICE TASKS: A LONGITUDINAL STUDY

**Autores:** Ashwini Srinivasaprasad

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Headsets now let workers switch between virtual reality \(VR\) and augmented reality \(AR\), but the costs of such switching had not been measured in continuous work, across sessions, or against posture and muscle load\. Thirty\-six participants typed while standing in three sessions a median of about one week apart\. Each session had eight counterbalanced blocks: VR with a mid\- air keyboard and AR with a physical keyboard, each immersive \(virtual scene\) or passthrough \(video of the room\)\. Typing, spine motion, muscle activity, workload and 2\-back scores were analyzed with mixed\-effects, recovery\-curve and machine\-learning models\. Input modality dominated performance\. VR typing was 65\.5% to 69\.0% slower than AR passthrough typing and less accurate, and VR was rated higher in workload than AR\. Muscle load redistributed, with forearm extensor activity higher in AR and upper trapezius activity higher in VR\. Observational posture scores missed this shift, but muscle and motion features identified held\- out participants&\#x27; blocks by modality with 73\.7% balanced accuracy \(chance 50%\)\. Blocks after compound transitions, which changed view and keyboard together, were 12\.0% slower than after view\-only and 7\.5% slower than after input\-only transitions\. After every type of switch, typing returned to 95% of its steady\-state speed within 45 seconds of typing time, about a minute of clock time, so the slower pace after compound transitions outlasted that recovery\. Typing became 9\.2% faster each session, and VR typing improved at more than twice the AR rate\. Practice shrank the slowdown in each session&\#x27;s first block but not the slowdown after each switch\. Early measures predicted later typing: Session 1 typing predicted Session 3 speed, and more Session 1 neck flexion, flexion variability, drift and movement variability predicted slower Session 3 typing\. Neck drift, forward neck flexion building within a block, also predicted next\-block typing 1\.4 words per minute slower per standard deviation, beyond current speed\. Within\-session gains were confined to mid\-air typing: 12\.6% per 10 minutes in Session 1, 5\.2% in Session 2 and no change in Session 3\. On the 2\-back memory test, which asks whether each item matches the one two items earlier, telling matches from non\-matches improved from before to after Session 1 and declined in later sessions\. 16Designers should keep physical input for sustained typing, change view and keyboard one at a time, allow a minute after each switch before precision work, support the arms in mid\-air work, and evaluate each input modality over several sessions\.
