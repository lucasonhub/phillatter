# Presencia, distribución e infección de triatominos con Trypanosoma cruzi en el estado de Campeche, México

**Autores:** Paulino Tamay\-Segovia; Yessenia Montes\-Vergara; Selene Blum\-Domínguez; Alberto Antonio‐Campos; José Miguel Padilla\-Valdez; Nancy Rivas; Ricardo Alejandre‐Aguilar

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction\. Campeche is a Mexican state with an ideal habitat for triatomines, the vectors of Trypanosoma cruzi, the parasite that causes Chagas disease\. While different entomological indices and the geographic distribution of these triatomines have been reported in the state, data remain limited to a few localities\. Objective\. Evaluate the occurrence, distribution, and natural infection of triatomines in Campeche, Mexico\.Materials and methods\. Triatomines were collected from 1996 to 2023 in 32 localities of Campeche within intradomicile, peridomicile, and sylvatic areas\. The percentage of natural infections was calculated, and associations between triatomine species and locality, as well as between triatomine instars and infection status, were analyzed\. Results\. A total of 532 triatomines were collected: Triatoma dimidiata was the most abundant and widely distributed species in the state, whereas only a single specimen each of Triatoma nitida and Eratyrus cuspidatus was found\. Across all three collection areas, adults were more frequently found than nymphs\. Furthermore, most females were captured in intradomicile areas, and more females exhibited a higher proportion of Trypanosoma cruzi infection compared to males\. The overall natural infection rate for the entire region was 18\.8%\. The only significant statistical association was between triatomine developmental instars and Trypanosoma cruzi infection \(χ2 = 80\.396, p &lt; 0\.0001\)\.Conclusion\. This work highlights the importance of continuously monitoring areas around dwellings to prevent potential infestation by non\-domiciliated bugs and crucially to reduce the risk of Trypanosoma cruzi infection among the local population\.
