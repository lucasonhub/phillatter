# Neural Dimensional Analysis: Foundations of Dimensionless and Scale\-Consistent Computation

**Autores:** Bum Jun Kim; Makoto Kawano; Yusuke Iwasawa; Yutaka Matsuo

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Modern Transformers rely on many scale\-control mechanisms, including normalization, residual scaling, attention temperatures, loss weighting, and adaptive updates\. To explain how these mechanisms maintain scale consistency, we introduce Neural Dimensional Analysis \(NDA\), a unified framework based on dimensional consistency\. We define neural units that specify how tensors rescale and which quantities can be added, just as physical units do in physics\. Our rules match residual units, nondimensionalize attention and gate inputs, combine losses across units, and convert gradient units into parameter units\. We implement NDA through dimensionless Unit Violation Indices \(UVI\) for diagnosing scale mismatches within Transformers, a unit\-consistent multi\-loss objective, and the Gauge\-Canonical Transformer \(GCT\) with normalized coordinates and orthogonalized residual additions\. In language\-model experiments spanning 51M to 1B parameters, GCT remains stable during training across a broad sixteenfold learning\-rate range, outperforming Pre\-LN, LLaMA\-style, and QK\-Norm in 124M validation loss at tuned learning rates\. GCT&\#x27;s normalized residual stream also shows improved robustness to quantization\. At 1B parameters, calibrated per\-tensor int8 quantization increases GCT&\#x27;s validation loss by only 0\.009 nats, compared with up to 5\.51 nats for the baselines\. Vision and physics\-informed neural network experiments further confirm NDA&\#x27;s benefits beyond language modeling\. These results establish dimensional consistency as a neural network design principle\.
