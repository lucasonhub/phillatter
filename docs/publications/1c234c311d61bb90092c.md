# Artificial Intelligence in Pharmacovigilance: Current Applications and Future Perspectives

**Autores:** Hiten Thakkar, Mansi Vasava, Vankar Padamdas, Pathak Shailly, Dr\. Ria Memoria\*

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Pharmacovigilance \(PV\) is the science and practice of detecting, assessing, understanding, and preventing adverse drug reactions \(ADRs\) and other medication\-related problems\. The increasing volume of healthcare data generated through electronic health records, spontaneous reporting systems, biomedical literature, social media platforms, and realworld evidence databases has created significant challenges for traditional pharmacovigilance methods\. Artificial Intelligence \(AI\), including machine learning \(ML\), deep learning \(DL\), natural language processing \(NLP\), and large language models \(LLMs\), has emerged as a transformative technology capable of improving drug safety surveillance\. AI\-based approaches facilitate automated signal detection, adverse event identification, duplicate report detection, causality assessment, and pharmacovigilance workflow optimization\. Recent studies demonstrate that machine\-learning models often outperform conventional statistical methods in identifying safety signals while reducing manual workload\. Despite these advances, challenges related to data quality, algorithmic bias, interpretability, regulatory acceptance, and ethical concerns remain barriers to widespread implementation\. This review discusses the current applications of AI in pharmacovigilance, evaluates existing evidence, highlights limitations, and explores future perspectives for integrating AI into routine drug safety monitoring\.
