# BaniRostam, Touraj: From Observable Behaviour to Developing Intelligence

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

What does it mean to explain intelligence scientifically? Twentieth\-century psychology offered differently oriented responses to this question\. Behaviourism prioritised the objective description and prediction of observable behaviour; the Cognitive Revolution redirected explanation towards internal representations and information\-processing mechanisms; and Piagetian constructivism examined how cognitive organisation emerges and changes through development\. Rather than treating these traditions as isolated theories or successive stages in which one displaced another, this paper reconstructs them as articulating three complementary explanatory demands\. It therefore proposes a Performance–Mechanism–Development framework: performance concerns what a system can reliably do; mechanism concerns the organised processes that generate its behaviour; and development concerns the learning history, interaction, adaptation, and structural transformation through which its capacities emerge\. In concise terms, performance constrains explanation, mechanism accounts for the production of behaviour, and development accounts for the formation of cognitive organisation\. Applied to contemporary artificial intelligence, this framework reveals the limits of predominantly performance\-centred evaluation\. Although mechanistic interpretability and research on continual and developmental learning increasingly address mechanism and development, neither high performance nor access to internal model states constitutes a complete explanation of intelligence\. At the same time, artificial systems need not reproduce human or Piagetian development\. A more adequate scientific account must distinguish what a system does, how it does it, and how it became capable of doing it\. \(direct link \)
