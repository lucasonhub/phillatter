# Revisiting spin Hamiltonian parameters in a Kitaev material via Bayesian optimization of magnetization curves

**Autores:** Takahiro Misawa; Ryo Tamura; Kazuyoshi Yoshimi; Youhei Yamaji

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Determining the spin Hamiltonian of a magnetic compound is crucial for understanding its magnetic properties\. A standard approach is to derive model parameters from $ab$ $initio$ calculations based on the crystal structure\. However, the resulting Hamiltonian can depend sensitively on methodological details of the $ab$ $initio$ procedure\. This issue is particularly evident in $α$\-RuCl$\_3$, a candidate Kitaev material\. Here, we present an alternative, data\-driven approach to determine the spin Hamiltonian parameters of $α$\-RuCl$\_3$ by Bayesian optimization of experimental magnetization curves along the $b$\- and $c$\-axis directions\. We optimize five parameters, namely the Kitaev interaction $K$, off\-diagonal interactions $Γ$ and $Γ&\#x27;$, the Heisenberg interaction $J$, and the $c$\-axis $g$\-factor $g\_c$\. The parameter set that minimizes the cost function is $\(K,Γ,Γ&\#x27;,J,g\_c\)=\(\-6\.0,\\,7\.5,\\,\-0\.3,\\,\-1\.75,\\,2\.3\)$, where the exchange couplings are in meV\. We find that the cost function is insensitive to the absolute value of the Kitaev coupling $K$\. Thus, the magnetization data alone do not determine its energy scale\. The cost function also depends only weakly on $Γ&\#x27;$ and $J$, while the optimization favors a large positive $Γ$\. By computing the static spin structure factor, magnetic susceptibility, and specific heat, we show that these quantities favor the large\-$Γ$ scenario over the small\-$g\_c$ scenario and that the parameter set that minimizes the cost function yields good agreement with experiment\. The combination of Bayesian optimization and accurate low\-energy solvers provides an effective approach for determining parameters of spin Hamiltonians\. This methodology opens a systematic route to determining spin Hamiltonians in quantum magnets from experimental data\.
