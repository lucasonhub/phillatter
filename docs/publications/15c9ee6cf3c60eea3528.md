# Ismayilov, Oruj: THE MOON, THE HOUR, AND THE SIGN: Grammar and Narrative Structure in the Opening of Sūrat al\-Qamar

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper examines the opening verses of Sūrat al\-Qamar \(Q 54:1–2\) — &quot;The Hour has drawn near, and the moon has split\. And if they see a sign, they turn away and say: &\#x27;a continuing magic&\#x27;&quot; — not in order to adjudicate whether the splitting of the moon occurred as a historical event, but in order to separate two questions that the exegetical tradition has frequently run together: the temporal reference of the verb inshaqqa \(does it report a past event, or convey the certainty of a future one?\), and the compositional function of 54:1–2 within the architecture of the sūra as a whole\. This paper does not propose a third temporal interpretation standing alongside the historical\-past and future\-eschatological readings\. Rather, it argues that a verse&\#x27;s temporal reference and its compositional function are distinct questions, and that attending to the latter can proceed without settling the former\. Working across three evidentiary planes — the grammatical value of inshaqqa, the syntax of the conditional clause in verse 2, and the sūra&\#x27;s threefold narrative architecture — the paper shows that the wording of 54:1 does not by itself settle its temporal reference, and that reading 54:1–2 alongside the sūra&\#x27;s closing verses \(54:43–46\), which place a possible this\-worldly defeat of the Quraysh \(54:45\) alongside an explicit reference to the Hour as the time of their ultimate reckoning \(54:46\), suggests a compositional link between the opening and closing that merits attention independently of how 54:1&\#x27;s temporal reference is ultimately resolved\. The paper engages directly with a significant textual objection to a generalizing reading of verse 2 — Muḥammad Ḥusayn Ṭabāṭabāʾī&\#x27;s argument that the verse requires the &quot;sign&quot; in question to be a specific, witnessed event — and shows, via Ibn ʿĀshūr&\#x27;s reading of the same verse, that the grammatical evidence is not univocal\. Throughout, the paper deliberately brackets the question of the historical reliability of the ḥadīth reports describing the event, restricting itself to what can be established from the Qurʾanic text and its immediate exegetical tradition\. \(direct link \)
