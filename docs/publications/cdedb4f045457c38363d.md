# Stokes, Margaret: The World Before the Word

**Autores:**

## Descrição do feed

What makes a claim answerable to anything beyond its own wording? This paper argues that answerability depends on a world that persists independently of the claim, together with criteria governing what is being identified, the interval over which the claim holds, and the continuing attribution of its source and basis\. Meaningful language does not require every referent to exist\. It requires a domain against which claims can be assessed, including claims whose standing is negative or unresolved\. Directions to a river and the disputed classification of a one\-antlered roe deer expose the difference between describing something and establishing what may follow from that description\. The argument then examines predictive world models, including Yann LeCun’s JEPA programme\. Accurate prediction, object tracking and retained memory do not by themselves establish which identity criterion governs, whether an earlier claim remains authoritative, or whether a proposed action is admissible\. The paper develops an account of persistent standing: what has been established, under which criteria, on what basis, and with what unresolved conditions\. Its central claim concerns the order of dependence wherever prediction is used to justify action\. Unsettled identity or authority cannot acquire settled standing merely because a predicted continuation is plausible\. The mention is free\. The act is not\. \(direct link \)
