# Admission METS\-IR in relation to severe acute pancreatitis among patients with hypertriglyceridemic acute pancreatitis: a retrospective cohort study

**Autores:** Ziyi ZHANG; Changwei Peng; Hong Peng; Zhu Yuanxia; Chenghu Huang; Li Rong

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Hypertriglyceridemic acute pancreatitis \(HTG\-AP\) is accompanied by significant metabolic abnormalities, but few studies have investigated whether a composite measure of this metabolic burden is associated with severe acute pancreatitis \(SAP\)\. The metabolic score for insulin resistance \(METS\-IR\), derived from fasting glucose, high\-density lipoprotein cholesterol, triglycerides, and body mass index, can be calculated from routinely available clinical data without requiring an insulin assay\. However, its association with SAP in HTG\-AP remains poorly characterized\. Objective This study investigates the association between admission METS\-IR and SAP in patients with HTG\-AP and assesses the robustness of this association through subgroup and sensitivity analyses\. Methods We retrospectively assembled a single\-center cohort of 456 adults admitted with HTG\-AP between January 2019 and July 2025\. Logistic regression evaluated METS\-IR as a continuous exposure and in quartiles\. Restricted cubic splines, subgroup analyses, and sensitivity analyses were performed\. Exploratory ROC analyses compared apparent discrimination with TyG, TyG\-BMI, and BISAP\. Results SAP occurred in 78 of 456 patients \(17\.1%\)\. Median METS\-IR was 69\.99 \(IQR, 61\.92–83\.67\) in the SAP group and 58\.18 \(IQR, 51\.47–66\.46\) in the non\-SAP group\. In Model 3, each one\-point increase in METS\-IR was associated with higher odds of SAP \(OR, 1\.06; 95% CI, 1\.04–1\.08\)\. After additional adjustment for TG, TG/HDL\-C, and TyG, the estimate was attenuated \(OR, 1\.03; 95% CI, 0\.997–1\.064; P = 0\.073\)\. Apparent AUCs were 0\.730 for METS\-IR, 0\.720 for TyG, and 0\.758 for BISAP; neither paired comparison showed a statistically significant difference\. In the three\-category analysis, the METS\-IR association was stronger for SAP vs\. MAP than for MSAP vs\. MAP\. Conclusion Higher admission METS\-IR was independently associated with higher odds of SAP in patients with HTG\-AP\. METS\-IR may provide a readily available measure of metabolic burden for early clinical assessment of patients with HTG\-AP, although its clinical utility requires confirmation in prospective multicenter studies\.
