# Molecule\-aware machine learning for predicting absorption and emission of benzothiadiazole derivatives

**Autores:** Duc Toan Truong; Phuong Chu Thi Hong; Phuong Nam Le Pham; Thuat Thanh Trinh

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Predicting the absorption and emission wavelengths of 2,1,3\-benzothiadiazole \(BTD\) derivatives across diverse solvents from molecular structure alone remains challenging, and conventional random\-split validation can obscure whether models truly extrapolate to new chemical structures or merely interpolate solvent effects for known ones\. By enforcing a molecule\-aware train–test split, in which all solvent measurements of any given chemical structure are held out together, we show that champion\-model $$R^2$$ falls by approximately 27–29 percentage points relative to random splitting, revealing that high random\-split accuracy primarily reflects reliable prediction of solvent\-induced spectral shifts rather than generalisation to unseen BTD structures\. Within this framework, replacing binary with count\-based Morgan fingerprints raises single\-split $$R^2$$ from 0\.747 to 0\.941 for absorption and from 0\.797 to 0\.916 for emission, an improvement rooted in the ability of count fingerprints to encode the recurrence frequency of donor substructures, a key determinant of intramolecular charge\-transfer character\. A systematically optimised deep neural network reaches $$R^2 = 0\.954$$ for absorption on its best of twenty random initialisations \(mean $$0\.83 \\pm 0\.12$$ \), the highest value reported for this dataset, yet the molecule\-aware benchmark confirms that this performance does not translate to structure\-level extrapolation\. Comparative evaluation of graph neural networks, multi\-task learning, extended descriptor sets, and ensemble methods confirms that none outperforms the count\-fingerprint approach at this dataset size\. Feature\-importance analysis identifies tertiary amine substitution as the dominant structural driver and solvent polarity as the strongest single feature for emission \(ranked first or second in all four model/encoding combinations examined, versus 8th–13th for absorption\), an asymmetry consistent with the stronger solvatochromism of relaxed charge\-transfer states\. These results offer practical guidance for computational screening: the models are well suited to solvent selection for known dyes, whereas the design of genuinely new structures demands expanded structural diversity in the training set\.
