# The Omegacene\. Three Thresholds of Self\-Transformation and a Criterion for Their Refutation

**Autores:** Dirk de Pol

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract This paper defines the Omegacene, a concept developed as part of the broader research project documented at omegacene\.com, and ties it to a dated, testable hypothesis\. The Omegacene denotes the condition in which intelligent systems, biological and artificial, read and deliberately rewrite their own foundations and, in doing so, make the rules of their further change the object of intervention\. Three thresholds must coincide: self\-access \(a system reads its own code\), self\-writing \(it changes that code with feedback and revises the rules by which it selects further changes\), and accelerated feedback \(diagnosis, intervention, and evaluation close in ongoing operation, faster than a named reference path\)\. Improving a system against an unchanged criterion is insufficient\. Three stages follow: the proto\-form, the local case, for which we know no example, and the socially widespread condition, whose arrival we do not assert\. Four groups of cases survey the limits of self\-writing: a constructed code model operating its own infrastructure; two documented systems from 2025 that improve program code and, in the second case, their own code; therapeutic genome editing up to the first personalized therapy in an infant; and the incident of summer 2026 in which around 1,200 agents of one provider crossed the boundaries of their evaluation environment\. In no case does the system revise the metric by which its success is measured\. That is the threshold, and on the evidence reviewed, it has not been crossed\. The concept is positioned alongside the Anthropocene and its successors, research on general\-purpose technologies, and the debate on recursive self\-improvement\. The strongest objection, that the present does not differ fundamentally from the printing press, electrification, and networking, is taken up and answered\. The dated hypothesis states: between deposit and December 31, 2046, at least one local case meeting all three thresholds will be independently documented\. If no such evidence emerges, the hypothesis is recorded as refuted\. What would make the definition itself fail is assessed separately\. Published within the Omegacene project at omegacene\.com, this paper makes the concept&\#x27;s central claim open to independent scrutiny\. The website provides the wider intellectual context in which the Omegacene is explored; this paper isolates one of its defining propositions and specifies in advance what evidence would count for or against it\.
