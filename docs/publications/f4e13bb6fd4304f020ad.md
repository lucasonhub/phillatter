# Transferring point weigh\-in\-motion measurements to the public road network for assessing the wartime transformation of traffic loading

**Autores:** Andrii Voinalovych; Olena Usychenko

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

After the blockade of seaports, export freight flows in Ukraine were redistributed towards western land border crossings and the ports of the Danube cluster, so that increased traffic loading was imposed on network sections designed for substantially lighter freight traffic\. The aim of the study is to develop and validate a method for transferring point measurements of the weigh\-in\-motion \(WIM\) system to the public road network in order to quantify this transformation and to identify sections where growth in calculated traffic loading coincides with poor pavement roughness\. Two primary sources were used: a pre\-war section\-level network condition database \(24,367 records, 22,302 km\) and the technical report of the WIM system for 2019–2025 \(78 sites\)\. The report data were machine\-extracted, filtered by data completeness, converted into equivalent design axles using an axle\-based classification of 18 vehicle classes, and spatially matched to the database \(68 of 78 sites\)\. A log\-linear transfer model was built and validated by leave\-one\-out cross\-validation \(MdAPE = 41 %\)\. A load\-field compression effect was established: the exponent of pre\-war loading equals 0\.352\. It is shown that 10\.5% of the traffic flow accounts for 71\.3 % of the calculated traffic loading expressed in equivalent design axles, and that zones of load growth are statistically associated with zones of the worst pre\-war roughness \(p = 0\.036\)\. A total of 3,259 km was identified for priority survey\. The practical value is a tool for ranking network sections when planning surveys, repairs and the siting of weight control points\.
