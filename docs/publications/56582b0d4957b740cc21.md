# Whether, or under what conditions? A configurational approach to AI\-supported antimicrobial stewardship in sub\-Saharan Africa

**Autores:** Abdulrasheed Ibrahim

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Sub\-Saharan Africa carries a disproportionate share of the global burden of antimicrobial resistance\. Western sub\-Saharan Africa has the highest death rate attributable to resistance of any world region, at 27\.3 per 100,000\. \[1\] Yet the region has some of the thinnest data and diagnostic infrastructure on which modern stewardship depends\. \[2\] This is the setting into which artificial intelligence is now being introduced\. Machine learning tools are being developed to predict resistance, guide empirical therapy, and flag inappropriate prescriptions, promising data\-driven stewardship in exactly the settings where the need is greatest\. \[3\] The prevailing question about these tools is whether they improve stewardship\. That question asks for an average effect\. Averaged across hospitals with varying infrastructure, artificial intelligence&\#x27;s effect on stewardship may look small, and be misread as evidence that it is not ready for African hospitals\. More likely, its effectiveness depends on data and infrastructure that some hospitals have and others do not, so judging it by an average becomes a question of equity\. This calls for a different question\. The success of AI\-supported stewardship in sub\-Saharan Africa will not be settled at the level of the technology; it will turn on particular combinations of enabling conditions\. It should therefore be studied configurationally, asking under what combinations of infrastructural, epidemiological, and human conditions it works, rather than evaluating it for an average effect\.An artificial intelligence tool is not a single intervention, but a bundle of preconditions that must hold together for the tool to help\. These preconditions span three dimensions, and each is unevenly met across sub\-Saharan Africa\. The author received no specific funding for this work\.
