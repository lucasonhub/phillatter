# Data\-driven, integrated control of Streptococcus suis substantially reduces mortality and antimicrobial use in a commercial swine herd

**Autores:** László Gombos; László Búza; Veronika Szűr; Tamás Révész; László Varga

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Streptococcus suis is a major cause of meningitis, septicemia, and arthritis in swine, and its control is complicated by antimicrobial resistance and the absence of broadly protective vaccines\. We evaluated a data\-driven, integrated control program in a 3\-year prospective, single\-site quality\-improvement study using an interrupted time\-series design in a PRRSV\-free, 1,000\-sow herd comprising approximately 126,000 liveborn piglets\. The program combined clinical, pathological, and bacteriological surveillance, including capsular polysaccharide \( cps \) serotyping, antimicrobial susceptibility testing with performance and antimicrobial\-use monitoring\. Interventions included sow vaccination with a herd\-specific autogenous bacterin \(serotypes 2 and 1/2\), strengthened internal biosecurity, structured colostrum management supported by Brix refractometry, and antimicrobial stewardship\. Data were captured using an in\-house animal\-health monitoring and decision\-support platform\. Within 6 months of program implementation, S\. suis \-associated mortality had markedly declined, and pathological lesions detected at necropsy decreased by approximately 50%\. Within 1 year, use of Category B antimicrobials, including ceftiofur and enrofloxacin, was eliminated\. By the end of year 2, metaphylactic amoxicillin use had been discontinued, and florfenicol metaphylaxis had decreased by nearly 25%\. Total antimicrobial use, expressed as active antimicrobial ingredient, in suckling and weaned piglets decreased by 98\.2% \(from 615\.8 to 11\.1 kg\), S\. suis \-associated mortality declined by approximately 90%, post\-weaning mortality decreased from 2% to &lt; 1%, and mean weaning weight increased by 10% \(from 6\.8 to 7\.5 kg\)\. These findings demonstrate a strong temporal association between implementation of the integrated herd\-health program and substantial reductions in S\. suis \-associated disease and antimicrobial use, including elimination of Category B antimicrobials\. Because multiple interventions were implemented concurrently and no contemporaneous control herd was available, the observed improvements cannot be attributed to any single program component\.
