# C\. elegans GPCR kinase GRK\-1 maintains behavioral persistence in a dynamic thermal environment

**Autores:** Tzu‐Ting Huang; Shunji Nakano; Maria Lukova; Hironori J\. Matsuyama; Leona C\. Cesar; Yuki Tsukada; Ikue Mori

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

How do animals maintain behavioral states as sensory inputs vary with movement and location? Here, we uncover a neuronal mechanism that allows the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans to move isothermally on a temperature gradient for several minutes at temperatures associated with its food experience\. We find that prolonged isothermal tracking along the preferred temperature requires GRK\-1, the C\. elegans homolog of human GPCR kinase GRK5\. Loss of grk\-1 reduces isothermal tracking durations, and these defects can be rescued by simultaneous grk\-1 expression in RIM and AVA interneurons\. Furthermore, silencing RIM and AVA restores the short isothermal tracking defects in the absence of grk\-1, while leaving wild\-type isothermal tracking unchanged\. These data suggest that wild\-type RIM and AVA are in a suppressed neuronal state during isothermal tracking\. Also, imposing a normally suppressed neuronal state in RIM and AVA during isothermal tracking can functionally compensate for the loss of grk\-1\. Together, our findings reveal a previously unknown molecule\-to\-circuit function of GRK\-1/GRK5 in RIM and AVA interneurons that maintains a minute\-scale behavioral state on a temperature gradient\.
