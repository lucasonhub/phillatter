# The experiences of neurodivergent midwives in Aotearoa New Zealand: An appreciative inquiry

**Autores:** Amy Ria Taylor; Suzanne Miller; Annabel Fakhri

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: As societal awareness of neurodivergence increases and diagnostic pathways become more accurate, health professionals are among those who are seeking and receiving diagnoses\. Previous research has highlighted the need for neuro\-affirming workplace accommodations and inclusive social conditions to enable neurodivergent \(ND\) healthcare professionals to thrive in their work contexts\. To date, little is known about the experiences of ND midwives \(kahu pōkai\) within the Aotearoa New Zealand \(Aotearoa\) workforce, including what accommodations would assist them to thrive\. Aims: To explore the experiences of ND kahu pōkai in Aotearoa, identify the strengths they feel they bring to the profession and explore ideas for neuro\-affirming accommodations in healthcare settings\. Method: Appreciative inquiry methodology was utilised to generate strengths\-based data\. Person\-orientated research ethics minimised barriers to engagement for the ND participants\. Semi\-structured interviews with 15 ND kahu pōkai were followed by a four\-day, online, asynchronous focus group\. Reflexive thematic analysis was undertaken to recognise patterns within the data\. Findings: Five themes were identified: 1\. ND traits facilitate strong therapeutic relationships with whānau; 2\. Spiky skill profiles; 3\. A journey: finding a workplace; 4\. Fostering ND midwifery culture and leadership; and 5\. Expanding our idea of what a midwife can be\. Participants experienced their neurodivergence as an asset to their clinical practice, particularly in building meaningful connections with whānau\. They also highlighted challenges stemming from their spiky skill profiles – areas of both high and low ability – which often clashed with workplace expectations\. This mismatch created a gap between expectations and reality, largely driven by ableist barriers embedded in healthcare workplace cultures and systems\. To address these challenges, participants explored workplace accommodations tailored specifically to the needs of kahu pōkai\. Conclusion: This research provided novel insights into the experiences and strengths of one group of ND kahu pōkai working in Aotearoa\. It is by increasing ND visibility that constructive challenge to neuro\-normative assumptions within midwifery workplace settings is enabled\.
