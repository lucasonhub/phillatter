# Can think\-aloud sessions enhance critical thinking in medical students? evidence from third\-year MBBS students

**Autores:** Numera Yousuf; Jaweria Sikander; Syeda Kauser Ali; Mehreen Fatima

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: The objective of this study was to evaluate the effect of think\-aloud methodology on critical thinking scores among third\-year MBBS students\. Methodology: A quasi\-experimental pre\-test and post\-test design was conducted at Jinnah Sindh Medical University, Karachi\. Third\-year MBBS students who consented and completed both assessments were included\. Critical thinking was measured using the Critical Reasoning Test Battery 2 \(CRTB2\) both before and after intervention\. Five structured think\-aloud sessions were conducted over four months, incorporating CRTB2\-style and discipline\-specific questions in forensic medicine\. Students verbalized and justified their reasoning through probing questioning and peer discussion, the answers were noted followed by immediate feedback\. Pre\- and post\-intervention CRTB2 scores were compared using paired\-samples t\-tests\. P\-value of 0\.05 was considered significant\. Results: A total of 174 students were included in the final analysis\. Mean CRTB2 total scores increased from 5\.03 to 5\.77 after the intervention\. Significant improvements were observed in verbal reasoning \(p &lt; 0\.001, Cohen’s d = 0\.53\) and numerical reasoning \(p &lt; 0\.001, Cohen’s d = 0\.32\)\. Overall CRTB2 scores showed a statistically significant increase \(p &lt; 0\.001, Cohen’s d = 0\.56\)\. The majority of students demonstrated improvement in verbal \(55%\), numerical \(48%\), and total scores \(61%\)\. Conclusions: Think\-aloud methodology significantly improved critical thinking scores among medical students\. It also provided valuable insights into students’ reasoning processes, enabling the identification and correction of misconceptions and faulty reasoning patterns\.
