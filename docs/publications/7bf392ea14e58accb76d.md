# Kander, Ilan: The TME Loop and Epistemic Correction: From Experience to Valid Knowledge

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This article develops the Theoretical\-Mechanistic\-Experiential \(TME\) framework to argue that a feedback loop in the human mind has epistemic implications for acquiring valid knowledge\. Without such a loop, the acquisition of valid knowledge is less scalable and less reliably corrigible; understanding may remain partial, unstable, or mistaken\. The epistemic loop, in which disturbance, mismatch, or possibility becomes available as lived significance, is further detailed in the TME correction sequence and visually summarized in Figure 1\. The central claim is that the evolution of the TME epistemic loop enabled human beings to convert cortical capacity and symbolic language into corrigible knowledge\. Cortex, language, and diverse forms of expression are necessary enabling conditions for processing, communication, and cultural transmission, but they remain insufficient without the TME loop\. The loop first enables stabilized understanding: feedback\-relevant contents become lived, salient, holdable, comparable, and correctable\. Such understanding moves toward valid knowledge only through further testing, external verification, and correction across cultural, rather than merely biological, timescales\. To maintain conceptual rigor, the framework establishes clearly distinct, non\-overlapping epistemic roles for consciousness, cognition, experience, salience, attention, ordinary awareness, reflective delay, secondary segmentation, witnessing, and discriminative intelligence\. The goal is to dismantle epistemic noise caused by tautological definitions collapsing into one another and to ensure a strict segregation between the functional model and its biological instantiation\. Consciousness is defined as the real\-time experiential field\. Cognition is defined as the set of interpretive operations whose outcomes enter, shape, or become available within that field\. By framing consciousness as a no\-hysteresis workspace without its own dedicated storage, the hard problem is bordered as a precise ontological limit\. This allows easy\-problem functions to be described as mechanistic feedback processes\. Ultimately, consciousness is positioned as a necessary link in the mind\-specific feedback loop\. It functions as the real\-time experiential field in which feedback\-relevant content becomes salient and available for conscious regulatory processing when automatic regulation is insufficient\. The framework therefore remains detached from theories of consciousness except where their functions overlap with the mind\-specific feedback loop\.
