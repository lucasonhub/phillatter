# Improving low\-temperature drying of municipal sewage sludge with wood\-derived biochar: Mechanistic analysis and implications for sludge treatment

**Autores:** Erik Mihelič; Olivija Plohl; Marjana Simonič

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Municipal sewage sludge \(MSS\) drying is a critical, but energy\-intensive step in sludge treatment, affecting handling, transport, storage, and downstream valorization\. This study investigates wood\-derived biochar \(BC\) as a structural additive to intensify the low\-temperature convective drying of MSS under natural convection and develop a reproducible Python workflow that converts mass–time records from isothermal drying experiments into physically interpretable drying parameters\. The approach was applied to MSS and MSS/BC blends \(1:1, 2:1, and 3:1 mass ratios\) dried as thin layers at 25–40 °C\. The workflow estimates the equilibrium moisture content \( EMC \), identifies the transition between the first drying period and the diffusion\-controlled period, and calculates the drying rate, molar flux, heat flux, and interfacial heat and mass transfer coefficients\. The EMC decreased with increasing the temperature and BC content, ranging from about 0\.026 to 0\.064 g g⁻¹ depending on the composition and temperature\. For MSS, the drying rate increased strongly with the temperature, from about 0\.044 g m⁻² s⁻¹ at 25 °C to about 0\.184 g m⁻² s⁻¹ at 40 °C, with latent heat flux values up to about 451 W m⁻²\. The estimated effective diffusivities ranged from 3\.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ to 2\.9 × 10⁻⁹ m² s⁻¹ under natural convection\. A master curve analysis showed a strong overlap for MSS across 25–40 °C, whereas the BC blends exhibited partial divergence during the first drying period\. Overall, the workflow provides a transferable tool for converting drying experiments into engineering descriptors that support the process comparison, scale\-up, and optimization of MSS drying systems\.
