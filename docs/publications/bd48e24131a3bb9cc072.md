# The teaching\-research nexus under pressure: sustaining ideals in uneven and changing times across disciplines

**Autores:** Cecilie Hansen; Kjetil Egelandsdal; Marit Ulvik

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Professors in higher education are commonly expected to combine teaching and research, yet the relationship between these activities remains contested\. This article explores how professors make sense of the teaching\-research nexus in their everyday academic work\. Drawing on semi\-structured interviews with 16 professors in philosophy, mathematics, law and medicine at two Norwegian research\-intensive universities, we explore professors&\#x27; meaning\-making with particular attention to the dialectical process between academic experiences and institutional structures\. Framed by mundane phenomenology, we find the nexus to be a core element of the academic role and central to how professors understand academic work\. The combination of teaching and research is sustained through professors&\#x27; understanding of the role as interconnected engagements within their disciplinary fields\. Research deepens teaching, and teaching can generate reflection, questions and, at times, new research ideas\. At the same time, organisational arrangements, such as course structures, workload models, funding mechanisms and research\-only positions, increasingly separate teaching and research in everyday practice\. The article contributes to research on the teaching\-research nexus by shifting attention from whether teaching and research are connected and from the possible effects of combining these activities, to how the connection as a social phenomenon is sustained through professors&\#x27; meaning\-making, but also increasingly challenged through organisational conditions\.
