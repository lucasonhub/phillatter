# Assessment of Anxiety and Depression Symptoms Following Insulin Initiation in Type 2 Diabetes: A 6\-Month Prospective Observational Study

**Autores:** Cahit Uçar; Mehmet Emin Gerek; Adem Ay; Mustafa Turgut Yıldızgören; Hasan Esat Yücel

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: To compare 6\-month changes in anxiety and depression levels relative to metabolic trajectories between patients with type 2 diabetes newly initiated on insulin and an oral antidiabetic drug \(OAD\)–treated control group\. Methods: This prospective, observational study included 100 patients with type 2 diabetes: an insulin group \(n = 55; basal insulin added to OADs\) and an OAD control group \(n = 45\)\. Psychological distress was assessed at baseline and 6 months using the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale \(HADS\) alongside HbA1c monitoring\. Trajectories were analyzed using 2×2 repeated\-measures ANOVA, and within\-group changes via paired t tests\. Standardized effect sizes \(Cohen d\) were calculated at 6 months\. Results: At baseline, diabetes duration and HbA1c levels were higher in the insulin group, while anxiety scores were higher in the OAD group \(P &lt; \.001 for all\)\. At 6 months, mean \(SD\) HbA1c decreased significantly in the insulin group \(from 9\.51% \[0\.72%\] to 7\.49% \[0\.78%\]; P &lt; \.001\) but remained stable in the OAD group\. Repeated\-measures ANOVA showed significant time × group interactions for anxiety \(F 1,98 = 69\.16; P &lt; \.001\) and depression \(F 1,98 = 85\.68; P &lt; \.001\)\. Within the insulin group, mean \(SD\) scores increased significantly for anxiety \(from 6\.98 \[2\.00\] to 11\.25 \[2\.74\]; P &lt; \.001\) and depression \(from 8\.25 \[2\.05\] to 12\.36 \[2\.26\]; P &lt; \.001\)\. At 6 months, between\-group differences reflected substantial clinical magnitudes favoring the control group for both anxiety \(Cohen d = 1\.14\) and depression \(Cohen d = 2\.29\)\. Conclusions: Insulin initiation was associated with increased anxiety and depression symptoms over 6 months despite improved glycemic control\. These findings underscore the need to integrate structured psychosocial support into clinical care during insulin transition\.
