# Fujie, Naoto: The Stellar Operationalization of Science: Xenoscience and the Loss of Human Conceptual Mediation as a Mandatory Passage Point

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper develops the concept of the stellar operationalization of science: a transition in which scientific operation ceases to require human conceptual mediation as a universal mandatory passage point while retaining difference sensitivity, historical re\-entry, error\-sensitive correction, generated observability, and reconfiguration\. Beginning from Sellars’s distinction between causal responsiveness and the space of reasons, Brandom’s account of material inference, incompatibility, and modal and normative metavocabularies, and Brassier’s commitment to nonconceptual causal patterning, the paper asks a post\-Brassier question: can scientific configurations recurrently couple to reality, experiment, intervene, retain results, and revise their own conditions without returning through human conceptual judgment at every cycle? Xenoscience is developed as a configuration in which sensors, artificial intelligence, experimental apparatuses, external memory, and physical interventions can form such recurrent loops\. Stellar operationalization does not mean the elimination of humans, concepts, reasons, or ontologies\. Rather, it removes their unconditional status as universal mandatory passage points\. Ontological mediation is treated in the same way: a unified ontology is required only where it produces a non\-redundant difference in prediction, intervention, correction, transfer, or reconfiguration\. Operational redundancy under a given configuration does not entail ontological falsity\. Preserved Ziran/Xeno Simulator results provide cross\-system evidence for several necessary operational components, particularly the dependence of correction on its timing relative to environmental reconfiguration, while dedicated native validation remains outstanding\. The result is a model of scientific autonomy that distinguishes causal operation, conceptual articulation, modal projection, normative entitlement, and intervention without making any one of them universally foundational\. \(direct link \)
