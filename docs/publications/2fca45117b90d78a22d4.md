# DEEP DISCRIMINATIVE AND GENERATIVE MODELS FOR ENCODING AND DECODING CORTICAL ACTIVITY

**Autores:** Bilal Ahmed

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Many questions about how the brain represents and processes information remain open\. This dissertation uses machine learning to study these questions\. The work focuses on auditory cortex, inverse problems, and speech synthesis from neural activity\. First, speech\-recognition networks are used as encoding models of spiking activity in squirrel\-monkey auditory cortex\. Their intermediate representations predict responses to English speech and monkey vocalizations at fine temporal resolution\. The results show a relationship between task\-optimized network features and cortical responses, while also showing that network depth does not map directly to a single cortical area\. Second, Restart Posterior Sampling \(RePS\) is developed for solving linear and nonlinear inverse problems with pretrained diffusion models\. RePS combines measurement\-conditioned ordinary differential equation steps with stochastic restarts\. This avoids backpropagation through the diffusion score network and gives an efficient approximate posterior sampler for image restoration\. Third, an invertible neural\-network decoder is used to synthesize speech from approximately half an hour of paired intracranial neural and audio recordings\. The decoder predicts mel spectrograms and uses a pretrained vocoder to generate waveforms\. Experiments across four datasets show improved speaker similarity and acoustic reconstruction, although reliable intelligibility remains difficult\. Together, these studies show how learned representations, generative priors, and structured decoder architectures can make better use of limited data\. Future work will use encoding models and generative audio priors to design stimuli for auditory cortex and to study speech decoding from motor\-cortex activity\.
