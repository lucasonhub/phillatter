# From conventional campus grid to optimized HRES microgrid: a grid\-constrained techno\-economic and environmental assessment of renewable energy integration at Shivaji University, India

**Autores:** Amit Chandrakant Kamble; Himmat Tukaram Jadhav

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Higher\-education campuses are well suited to staged decarbonization because they combine concentrated electrical demand, substantial rooftop area, diverse load flexibility, and long\-term institutional ownership of energy assets\. This study develops a Campus Microgrid Renewable Integration and Energy\-Saving Assessment Framework \(CMR\-ESAF\) for planning the transition of Shivaji University, India, from grid\-dependent operation to an optimized hybrid renewable energy system \(HRES\) microgrid\. Unlike a greenfield sizing study, the framework explicitly separates three transition states: S0, a grid\-only reference; S1, the existing campus with 930 kW of rooftop photovoltaic \(PV\) capacity; and S2, an optimized HRES incorporating PV expansion, supplementary wind generation, battery energy storage \(BESS\), demand\-side management \(DSM\), and energy\-management\-system \(EMS\) dispatch under grid and campus\-network constraints\. The S2 planning problem is solved using an adapted NSGA\-III many\-objective framework with normalized constraint handling, dynamic feasibility relaxation, reference\-vector association, dual archives, and fuzzy best\-compromise selection\. Under the stated annual hourly simulation assumptions, S0 requires 3650 MWh/yr of grid electricity, emits 2993 tCO $$\_2$$ 2 /yr, and reaches a maximum demand of 1080 kVA\. S1 lowers grid import to 2430 MWh/yr, corresponding to a 33\.4% reduction in grid dependency and 1000\.4 tCO $$\_2$$ 2 /yr of avoided emissions\. The selected S2 configuration comprises 1580 kW total PV, 150 kW wind DG, a 500 kW/1500 kWh BESS, and 130 kW of DSM participation\. It reduces grid import to 1175 MWh/yr and maximum demand to 695 kVA, yielding a 67\.8% grid\-dependency reduction, 35\.6% peak\-demand mitigation, 72\.3% renewable fraction, and 65\.8% renewable self\-sufficiency\. The modeled annual utility cost decreases from INR 413\.6 to 145\.7 lakh/yr; the corresponding planning estimate gives a levelized campus electricity cost \(LCEC\) of INR 4\.85/kWh and a simple payback period of 6\.4 years\. The principal contribution is a replicable transition framework that distinguishes the value already delivered by installed PV from the incremental benefit of coordinated HRES, storage, DSM, and EMS investment\.
