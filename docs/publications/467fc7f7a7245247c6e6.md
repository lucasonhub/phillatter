# A multi task deep learning framework for automated health condition prediction from retinal fundus images using hybrid attention and explainable AI

**Autores:** Ravi Kumar Tenali; Anitha Potnuri

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Although retinal fundus imaging is a non\-invasive tool to assess systemic and fundus pathology, current deep learning algorithms are mostly single\-task, lack clinical interpretability and are unable to handle acquisition variation between different domains\. These constraints limit their clinical utility: when a patient comes to the clinic for fundus screening, they could also be diabetic, have glaucoma, AMD, and increased cardiovascular risk, with each disease having retinal vascular biomarkers that require a patient to be tested for in the clinic, which must be managed within a framework of common, interpretable, and generalizable models rather than a pipeline of independent models\. To solve these problems, we suggest a multi\-task deep learning network that simultaneously classifies DR severity \(five\-class\), glaucoma likelihood \(binary\), AMD stage \(four\-class\) and cardiovascular risk \(binary\) from a single retinal fundus image using a single forward pass\. It adopts an EfficientNet\-B4 convolutional backbone pretrained on the ImageNet dataset, a six\-layer multi\-head self\-attention Transformer encoder \(d\_model = 512, h = 8\), a novel dual\-branch spatial\-channel attention module that builds on, but significantly expands, the CBAM module, and four task\-specific output heads that are optimized using the homoscedastic uncertainty\-weighted multi\-task loss following the formulation of Kendall et al\. Per\-task anatomical explanations are produced by back propagating the CNN backbone via Grad\-CAM in the anatomical space\. The proposed model was evaluated on EyePACS, APTOS 2019, Messidor\-2 and RIM\-ONE v3 and achieved an accuracy of 94\.7%, AUC = 0\.978, sensitivity = 93\.2% and specificity = 95\.8% while being statistically better than all single task models \(DeLong test, p &lt; 0\.01, Bonferroni corrected\)\. Under the reported experimental setting, the zero\-shot cross\-dataset evaluation achieves AUC ≥ 0\.951, which is promising for its performance across datasets\. Grad\-CAM saliency maps are anatomically similar to established clinical grading criteria for each disease\. The four task\-specific heads share a single architecture and produce four outputs in one forward pass, but no individual patient in the training corpora had verified ground\-truth labels for all four conditions simultaneously; the model is trained on partially labelled, multi\-source data via a masked multi\-task loss, and the cardiovascular output is an AVR\-derived image\-based surrogate rather than a validated clinical cardiovascular diagnosis\. This proposed framework could be considered for an additional potential step toward the future when it comes to multi\-disease fundus screening with an interpretable single\-model solution, if it is assessed and approved at an independent multi\-centre level and by regulatory bodies\.
