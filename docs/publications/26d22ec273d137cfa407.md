# Serial Cross‐Sectional Mapping of Bone Marrow Microenvironment Remodelling During Fracture Repair Using Multiparametric MRI and Histopathology

**Autores:** Rui Zhou; Guochen Li; Yinglin Zhou; Caihong Yao; Liuyi Deng; Xianchun Zeng

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Restoring bone marrow microenvironment \(BMM\) homeostasis is critical for fracture healing, yet non\-invasively tracking physiological events like apoptosis, angiogenesis and matrix remodelling remains challenging\. This study evaluated multiparametric 7\.0T MRI \(T2 mapping and proton density\-weighted imaging \[PDWI\]\) for in vivo tracking of BMM spatiotemporal remodelling during natural and pharmacologically accelerated bone repair\. Utilizing a rat tibial drill\-hole injury model \(N = 36\), we compared a natural healing control with a Huoxue Qiangji Decoction \(HQD\) intervention group\. Serial high\-resolution sagittal MRIs were acquired at days 7, 14 and 21, with signal intensity ratios \(SIR\) normalized to ipsilateral intact marrow using ITK\-SNAP\. Distance correlation \(dCor\) analysis explored non\-linear dependencies between MRI metrics and histological features \(c\-cas3, Masson&\#x27;s trichrome and day\-14 CD31/Ki67 expression\)\. Results showed distinct MRI signal trajectories, with earlier homeostatic stabilization in the accelerated healing group\. Spatiotemporal mapping revealed closely mirrored non\-linear dependencies between MRI and histology\. Specifically, T2 mapping SIR mirrored early\-stage apoptosis \(dCor = 0\.527\) and mid\-term cellular proliferation \(Ki67, dCor = 0\.732\)\. Concurrently, PDWI reflected dynamic trends of mid\-term angiogenesis \(CD31, dCor = 0\.637\) and late\-stage collagen deposition \(dCor = 0\.480\)\. In conclusion, normalized multiparametric MRI provides complementary macroscopic insights into BMM histological progression, highlighting its potential as an objective, non\-invasive translational tool for monitoring musculoskeletal healing\.
