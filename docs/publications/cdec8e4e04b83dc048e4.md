# Evaluating Urban Agriculture Methods: A Grey Relational Analysis Approach

**Autores:** Sathiyaraj Chinnasamy; M Ramachandran; Libiya Saravanan

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

populations expand, the demand for fresh, nutritious food increases, often outpacing traditional supply chains\. Urban agriculture encompasses various practices, from community gardens and rooftop farms to vertical farming and aquaponics, transforming urban spaces into productive landscapes\. This innovative approach not only enhances food security and promotes sustainability but also fosters community engagement and social equity\. By integrating food production into urban planning, cities can pave the way for a more resilient and sustainable food system that meets the needs of their residents\. The significance of researching critical issues such as food security, sustainability, and social equity in urban environments\. As cities face increasing populations and diminishing resources, urban agriculture offers innovative solutions for local food production, reducing reliance on distant supply chains\. It promotes community engagement and fosters resilience by empowering residents to grow their own food\. Furthermore, understanding the economic, environmental, and health benefits of urban agriculture can inform policy decisions, guiding investments in infrastructure and resources ultimately contributing to the well\-being of urban populations\. The methodology for researching urban agriculture involves a mixed\-methods approach, combining qualitative and quantitative techniques\. Surveys and interviews can be conducted with urban farmers, community members, and policymakers to gather insights on motivations, challenges, and benefits associated with urban agriculture\. Additionally, case studies of successful urban farming initiatives in various cities can provide context and best practices\. Geographic Information System \(GIS\) mapping can analyze spatial distribution and accessibility of urban agriculture sites\. Rooftop gardens, Vertical Farming, Community Gardens, Aquaponics, Urban Orchards\. Environmental Impact, Economic Viability, Social Benefits, Scalability &amp; Feasibility\. Vertical Farming is getting top of the table and Urban Orchards is getting last place of the table\.
