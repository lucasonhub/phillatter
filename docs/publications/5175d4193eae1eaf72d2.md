# Endovascular versus open repair for aortoiliac occlusive disease – nationwide in\-hospital outcomes by sex

**Autores:** Jonas Salm; Tanja Böhme; Elias Noory; Lucas Bacmeister; Constantin von zur Mühlen; Dirk Westermann; Thomas Zeller; Klaus Kaier

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Summary: Background: Evidence comparing endovascular therapy and open surgery for aorto\-iliac occlusive disease remains limited, particularly for early complications, resource use, and sex\-specific outcomes\. Patients and methods: We conducted a retrospective nationwide cohort study using the 2023 German inpatient dataset\. Adult hospitalisations with major endovascular or open aorto\-iliac reconstructive procedures and arterial occlusive disease were included\. Hospitalisations with aneurysm, dissection, or combined procedure codes were excluded\. Outcomes included in\-hospital mortality, myocardial infarction, ischaemic stroke, major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events \(MACCE\), complications, length of stay, and reimbursement\. Adjusted estimates were obtained employing a doubly robust estimator using machine learning algorithms and were stratified by sex\. Results: Among 2,710 hospitalisations, 1,923 \(71\.0%\) underwent open surgery and 787 \(29\.0%\) endovascular therapy; 41\.3% involved women\. Endovascular therapy was associated with lower adjusted risks of in\-hospital mortality \(RR \.44, 95% CI \.31–\.64\), MACCE \(RR \.47, 95% CI \.34–\.64\), acute kidney injury \(RR \.38, 95% CI \.29–\.50\), major bleeding \(RR \.36, 95% CI \.25–\.51\), prolonged ventilation \(RR \.28, 95% CI \.18–\.43\), and SAEs \(RR \.46, 95% CI \.33–\.65\)\. Length of stay was shorter after endovascular therapy \(−8\.57 days, 95% CI –9\.63 to −7\.52\), while reimbursement did not differ significantly\. Sex\-stratified estimates were more pronounced in men but exploratory without interaction testing\. Conclusions: Endovascular therapy was associated with lower in\-hospital morbidity and mortality and shorter hospital stay than open surgery, with similar reimbursement\. Case\-based data lacked longitudinal follow\-up and detailed clinical, anatomical and treatment\-selection information, limiting causal and patient\-level interpretation\.
