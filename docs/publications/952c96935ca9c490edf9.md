  the in-situ formation of a robust interfacial adhesive layer during the printing
  process between the ink and fabric surface. The incorporation of various functional
  fillers, including silver, liquid metal, carbon nanotubes, and piezoelectric materials,
  imparts the ink with exceptional versatility, enabling tunable electrical properties.
  This formulation is compatible with multiple textile substrates and printing techniques,
  resulting in printed e-textiles that demonstrate remarkable durability under repeated
  bending, stretching, washing, and wear. This work establishes a solid foundation
  for the subsequent development of high-performance pressure-sensing textiles. Building
  upon this material platform, a skin-inspired, structure-gradient fiber mat (SGFM),
  which can serve as the sensing layer for self-powered pressure sensors, is then
  developed via template-assisted layer-by-layer electrospinning and electrode printing
  techniques. Mimicking the gradient mechanical structure of the human skin, SGFM
  demonstrates outstanding dielectric property and mechanical resilience that outperforms
  the one only with homogeneous fiber-mat design. It endows the pressure-sensing textile
  with high sensitivity and broad pressure detection range (0.068 kPa⁻¹ from 0-53
  kPa, and 0.013 kPa⁻¹ from 53-660 kPa). The resulting pressure sensing textile showcases
  excellent resistance to wear and washing, and can be integrated into a wearable
  rehabilitation monitoring system, highlighting its practical potential for rehabilitation
  assistance and sports training. Furthermore, the sensing capability of the pressure-sensing
  textile was extended to multi-axis force detection by printing the multi-channel
  strain-sensing array with a pressure-sensing unit within the multilayered fiber-mat
  matrix. Such an integrated pressure-sensing textile can sensitively detect the magnitude
  and direction of the pressure forces, and transmit to the terminal when coupled
  with a data-acquisition circuit and machine-learning algorithms. As a proof of concept,
  such pressure-sensing textile is adhered to a fingertip to perform real-time tactile
  sensing, enabling the real-time recording of delicate finger motions such as object
  manipulation and writing. In summary, this work advances high-performance, flexible
  and multi-dimensional pressure-sensing textiles through material innovation, structural
  engineering, and device integration. The resulting fabrics deliver outstanding sensitivity,
  broad sensing range, multi-dimensional detection, and long-term durability, thereby
  paving the way for next-generation smart textiles. Moreover, the fabrication and
  integration strategies investigated in this work are believed to be readily adaptable
  to other textile-based wearable electronics, including power sources, computing
  components, and displays. Collectively, the findings of this thesis are poised to
  make a meaningful impact on the continued evolution of flexible electronics and
  smart textile systems.

# Development of multi\-dimensional pressure sensing textiles

**Autores:** Jinxing Jiang

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Flexible and wearable electronics have emerged as a transformative technology platform, enabling a new generation of smart devices for applications across a wide spectrum of fields, including healthcare monitoring, sports training, aerospace, defense, and industrial protection\. Among the various functional components in these systems, pressure sensors play a vital role, enabling accurate detection and quantification of mechanical forces\. This capacity is essential for realizing functionalities such as physiological monitoring, motion analysis, and human\-machine interaction\. To achieve seamless integration with wearable gears, electronic textiles \(e\-textiles\) have attracted increasing attention\. By embedding electronic components directly into textile substrates, e\-textiles offer softness, breathability, and lightweight properties, providing superior comfort and wearability compared to conventional film\-based electronics\. In the context of pressure sensors, e\-textiles typically employ flexible sensing components that are fabricated by printing electronic functional materials onto textile substrates\. This approach not only preserves the desirable mechanical properties of textiles but also enables large\-area, scalable production\. However, the inherent mechanical mismatch between textiles and functional electronic materials can lead to interfacial delamination and subsequently the device failure under repeated or complex deformations\. Additionally, the limited selection of materials and structural design strategies available for textile\-based sensing layers restrict their sensitivity, sensing range, and ability to detect multi\-dimensional force detection\. As a result, simultaneously achieving high mechanical robustness, exceptional sensitivity, broad sensing range, and reliable multi\-axis detection in pressure\-sensing textiles remains a critical challenge\. This thesis systematically addresses these challenges through the rational design and fabrication of advanced pressure\-sensing textiles, integrating innovative functional ink formulations, engineered sensing architectures, and robust device integration strategies\. The development of novel inks enables highly conductive and durable electrodes, while optimized sensing architectures significantly improve sensitivity and expand the sensing range\. Integration strategies further advance pressure\-sensing textiles from single\-axis to multi\-dimensional force detection\. Together, these innovations result in e\-textiles with outstanding mechanical robustness, high sensitivity, broad sensing range, and reliable multi\-axis force detection capability, paving the way for applications in intelligent healthcare and interactive wearables\. Firstly, a cohesive functional ink formulation is developed to achieve strong adhesion and mechanical stability on various textile substrates\. By rationally tailoring the ink’s solvent system, such a cohesive ink facilitates the in\-situ formation of a robust interfacial adhesive layer during the printing process between the ink and fabric surface\. The incorporation of various functional fillers, including silver, liquid metal, carbon nanotubes, and piezoelectric materials, imparts the ink with exceptional versatility, enabling tunable electrical properties\. This formulation is compatible with multiple textile substrates and printing techniques, resulting in printed e\-textiles that demonstrate remarkable durability under repeated bending, stretching, washing, and wear\. This work establishes a solid foundation for the subsequent development of high\-performance pressure\-sensing textiles\. Building upon this material platform, a skin\-inspired, structure\-gradient fiber mat \(SGFM\), which can serve as the sensing layer for self\-powered pressure sensors, is then developed via template\-assisted layer\-by\-layer electrospinning and electrode printing techniques\. Mimicking the gradient mechanical structure of the human skin, SGFM demonstrates outstanding dielectric property and mechanical resilience that outperforms the one only with homogeneous fiber\-mat design\. It endows the pressure\-sensing textile with high sensitivity and broad pressure detection range \(0\.068 kPa⁻¹ from 0\-53 kPa, and 0\.013 kPa⁻¹ from 53\-660 kPa\)\. The resulting pressure sensing textile showcases excellent resistance to wear and washing, and can be integrated into a wearable rehabilitation monitoring system, highlighting its practical potential for rehabilitation assistance and sports training\. Furthermore, the sensing capability of the pressure\-sensing textile was extended to multi\-axis force detection by printing the multi\-channel strain\-sensing array with a pressure\-sensing unit within the multilayered fiber\-mat matrix\. Such an integrated pressure\-sensing textile can sensitively detect the magnitude and direction of the pressure forces, and transmit to the terminal when coupled with a data\-acquisition circuit and machine\-learning algorithms\. As a proof of concept, such pressure\-sensing textile is adhered to a fingertip to perform real\-time tactile sensing, enabling the real\-time recording of delicate finger motions such as object manipulation and writing\. In summary, this work advances high\-performance, flexible and multi\-dimensional pressure\-sensing textiles through material innovation, structural engineering, and device integration\. The resulting fabrics deliver outstanding sensitivity, broad sensing range, multi\-dimensional detection, and long\-term durability, thereby paving the way for next\-generation smart textiles\. Moreover, the fabrication and integration strategies investigated in this work are believed to be readily adaptable to other textile\-based wearable electronics, including power sources, computing components, and displays\. Collectively, the findings of this thesis are poised to make a meaningful impact on the continued evolution of flexible electronics and smart textile systems\.
