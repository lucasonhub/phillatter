# Measurement\-based Vehicle to Vehicle \(V2V\) Pathloss Modeling and Leakage Aware Machine Learning Prediction in Indonesian Urban and Suburban Environments

**Autores:** Elfira Nureza Ardina; Rustam Asnawi; Eko Marpanaji; Ari Endang Jayati; Nor Azlan Othman

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Reliable short\-range Vehicle\-to\-Vehicle \(V2V\) links require propagation models that reflect local road geometry, low antenna heights, and trial\-to\-trial variation\.This study characterizes nominal large\-scale path loss at 920 MHz over 2\.15\-99\.44m on urban and suburban routes in Central Java, Indonesia\.The analysis uses 224 spatially aggregated observations obtained from 18 measurement trials\.To prevent target leakage, Received Signal Strength Indicator \(RSSI\) is used only to construct the link\-budget response, while RSSI, Signal\-to\-Noise Ratio \(SNR\), measured\-path\-loss columns, and empirical\-model outputs are excluded from the predictors\.Ordinary Least Squares \(OLS\), Random Forest, Gradient Boosting Regression Trees \(GBRT\), and histogram gradient boosting are evaluated using identical predictors, preprocessing, and leave\-one\-measurement\-trial\-out folds\.Their mean Root Mean Square Errors are 11\.306, 11\.205, 11\.779, and 12\.420 dB, respectively, while the corresponding mean Mean Absolute Errors are 9\.439, 9\.351, 9\.773, and 10\.436 dB\.Random Forest has marginally lower trial\-mean errors, whereas OLS has the best pooled R\-squared value \(0\.428\)\.Overlapping confidence intervals do not demonstrate predictive superiority for any nonlinear model\.The contribution is therefore methodological: a leakage\-aware, fair, and reproducible framework for evaluating cross\-trial V2V path\-loss generalization under controlled experimental conditions\.
