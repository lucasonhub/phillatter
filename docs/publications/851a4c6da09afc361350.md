# CONSTRUCTING PROFESSIONAL IDENTITIES IN AI\-ENHANCED LANGUAGE CLASSROOM: A STUDY OF PAKISTANI ENGLISH LANGUAGE TEACHERS’ PERSPECTIVES AND PRACTICES

**Autores:** Muhammad Atiq\-ur\-Rehman; Muhammad Sabboor Hussain

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The main aim of the study is to investigate the construction of English Language Teachers’ \(ELTs\) professional identity in the Artificial Intelligence \(AI\) era in the Pakistani Punjab context\. The qualitative analysis is embedded in semi\-structured interviews\. Multilayered sampling techniques were used, and data were collected from ELTs working in public and private educational institutions and analyzed using the Grounded Theory Approach \(GTA\)\. Initially, purposive sampling was used to identify appropriate samples, and those samples referred additional samples until saturation was attained on the thirteenth sample\. The investigation was theoretically informed by Gee’s \(2000\) distinction between I\-identity and A\-identity\. Findings created five interrelated categories: Institutionally Framed Professionalism, Agentive Professional Self, Constrained Autonomy, Negotiation Professional Identity, and Hybrid Identity Construction\. Results highlight that examination\-driven systems, curricular mandates, and administrative checks and balances powerfully form ELTs’ identities\. They are sustained simultaneously by contemplative practice, adaptive strategies, and professional achievement\. Autonomy was developed as conditional rather than absolute, leading teachers to negotiate between organizational compliance and personal pedagogical obligation\. The study demonstrates that ELT identity in Punjab is dynamic, context\-sensitive, and permanently negotiated\. By integrating hierarchical and agentive dimensions of identity, this research contributes to a nuanced understanding of teacher professionalism within examination\- focused educational systems\.
