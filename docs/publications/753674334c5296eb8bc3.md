# Representation learning for low\-data protein fitness prediction using sequence–structure embeddings and graph neural networks

**Autores:** Chanoknun Phunnasorn; Narupat Hongdilokkul; Wasakorn Laesanklang; Charin Modchang; Tipaluck Krityakierne

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate prediction of mutational fitness effects remains challenging in low\-data protein engineering settings, where experimentally characterized variants are limited\. Although recent protein language models and structure\-aware neural networks have demonstrated strong performance on large\-scale datasets, the relative importance of structural information, pre\-trained embeddings, and optimization strategies under data\-constrained conditions remains insufficiently understood\. In this study, we investigate a graph\-based framework that integrates AlphaFold2\-derived protein structures, Masked Inverse Folding with Sequence Transfer \(MIF\-ST\) embeddings, and graph neural networks for protein fitness prediction using a dataset of 152 TEM \\\(\\beta \\\) \-lactamase variants\. Through controlled ablation experiments, we systematically evaluate the effects of structural quality, feature richness, pre\-trained embeddings, and automated hyperparameter optimization across both random train–test splits and mutation\-specific splits requiring generalization to unseen mutations\. Under standard random splits, simpler feature representations perform competitively because training and test samples follow similar distributions\. In contrast, under mutation\-specific distribution shift, models incorporating structural information and pre\-trained sequence–structure embeddings achieve considerably stronger predictive performance and improved generalization\. Among the evaluated configurations, the full framework combining AlphaFold2, MIF\-ST, and Optuna achieved the highest overall performance across the evaluation datasets\. These findings demonstrate that the effectiveness of structure\-aware representations depends strongly on the evaluation regime and highlight the importance of mutation\-specific generalization when designing data\-efficient protein fitness prediction frameworks\.
