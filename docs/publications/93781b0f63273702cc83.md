# Rootstock\-mediated abiotic stress tolerance in fruit crops: physiological mechanisms, long\-distance hormonal and molecular signalling, and implications for precision rootstock breeding

**Autores:** M\. K\. Jayavigneshwaran; Madhusmita Dishri; Utpal Kumar Das; S\. Poonguzhali

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Perennial fruit crops face intensifying drought, salinity, temperature extremes, flooding and nutrient limitation under climate change\. Rootstocks alter below\-ground stress tolerance without changing the fruit\-determining scion cultivar\. Previous reviews have addressed rootstock breeding, stress physiology, rootstock diversity and long\-distance signalling from different perspectives, but an integrated cross\-crop synthesis linking causal rootstock–scion mechanisms with precision breeding criteria remains limited\. Objectives The review is restricted to rootstock\-mediated abiotic stress tolerance and its physiological and molecular basis, excluding biotic resistance to sustain mechanistic depth\. It dissects the four signalling subsystems coupling rootstock to scion, reconciles conflicting evidence, and derives testable selection criteria\. Methods Peer\-reviewed studies \(2000–2026, emphasis 2021–2026\) from Web of Science, Scopus, ScienceDirect and PubMed were prioritized for causal evidence: reciprocal grafting onto biosynthetic or transport mutants, direct flux and conductance measurement, isotope tracing and map\-based cloning\. Key findings Tolerance emerges from hydraulic regulation, ion homeostasis, hormonal and mobile signalling, and graft\-union integrity\. In the experimentally separated systems reviewed, transport has emerged as an important limiting step in several cases, although root abscisic acid biosynthesis also contributes to rootstock\-conferred drought tolerance; in the systems tested, measured aquaporin activity has shown stronger correspondence with hydraulic performance than transcript abundance\. Hormone ratios \(cytokinin:ACC, ACC: ABA\) were informative in the systems tested and merit systematic evaluation, whereas leaf abscisic acid must be interpreted against plant water status\. Core toolkits are conserved but deployed divergently by lineage, so markers validated in one crop may not be directly transferable to other crops without further validation\. Four contradictions \(aquaporin transcript versus activity, root\-sourced abscisic acid, sodium\- versus chloride\-driven toxicity and the proposed dwarfing–drought trade\-off\) are reframed as testable breeding targets\. Conclusion Climate\-resilient rootstock development would benefit from greater emphasis on flux\-, ratio\- and activity\-based phenotyping alongside concentration\-, single\-hormone\- and transcript\-based criteria, embedded in ideotype\-driven pipelines, with greater research attention to under\-resourced tropical and subtropical crops\.
