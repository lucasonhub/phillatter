# Perioperative nutrition\-related laboratory profiles predict outcomes after digestive procedures in patients with gastrointestinal cancer

**Autores:** MengHui Zhang; Liangliang Hui; Jialing Zhang; Chengran Wang; Fan Xu; Guoquan Tao; Honggang Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Routine laboratory measurements may capture nutritional, inflammatory, and organ\-function vulnerability in patients with gastrointestinal cancer\. We developed nested logistic regression models using INSPIRE v1\.4\.2 and evaluated a portable model in MIMIC\-IV v3\.1\. The original INSPIRE cohort comprised 11,471 operations among 10,728 patients, with 893 deaths within 365 days\. Patients were allocated to development and held\-out test sets\. For 365\-day mortality, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve \(AUROC\) was 0\.783 \(95% CI 0\.752–0\.811\) for clinical variables, 0\.843 \(95% CI 0\.820–0\.868\) after adding laboratory variables, and 0\.856 \(95% CI 0\.834–0\.879\) for the full perioperative model\. Patient\-level full\-pipeline bootstrap analysis yielded an optimism\-corrected AUROC of 0\.837\. External AUROC was 0\.676 \(95% CI 0\.663–0\.688\) for 365\-day mortality and 0\.724 \(95% CI 0\.692–0\.754\) for recorded hospital death after harmonizing CRP units\. Calibration drift was substantial, and hospital\-death discrimination was sensitive to laboratory timing\. Corrected operation\-linked risk tertiles showed graded mortality and lower albumin and hemoglobin levels in higher\-risk groups\. Perioperative nutrition\-related laboratory profiles provided additional discrimination for 365\-day mortality beyond clinical variables, although limited external performance and calibration drift warrant further validation before clinical use\.
