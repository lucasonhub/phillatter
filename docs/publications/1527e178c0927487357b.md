# From a Large\-Scale Teacher to Efficient Multimodal Students for Search Relevance Prediction

**Autores:** Tomasz Gniazdowski; Mohammadreza Momenifar; Nadia Vase; Shahram Khadivi; Wiktor Walentynowicz; Nicola Ueffing; Jan Pawlowski; Piotr Szymanski; Abraham Bagherjeiran

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Relevance prediction between user queries and items is a fundamental component of e\-commerce search systems\. Traditionally, high\-quality relevance labels are obtained through human judgment, which is reliable but expensive, slow, and difficult to scale\. Recent advances in large language models \(LLMs\) enable automated relevance judgment at scale, achieving performance comparable to human annotators\. However, such systems often rely on large commercial LLMs with complex prompts, resulting in high inference costs and limited throughput\. In this work, we propose a framework that bridges this gap by distilling a high\-performing LLM\-based relevance judgment system into efficient multimodal student models\. We first curate a small, high\-quality human\-annotated dataset to serve as ground truth\. Using this dataset, we develop a reliable LLM\-as\-a\-judge pipeline through careful prompt engineering and iterative refinement\. This pipeline is then used to generate large\-scale relevance labels, which serve as training data for compact multimodal models\. Our distilled models achieve performance close to the teacher system while significantly improving latency, cost, and scalability\. This approach enables practical deployment of high\-quality relevance prediction systems in production environments\.
