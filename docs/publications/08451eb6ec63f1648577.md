# Cervical Dystonia Treatment with Injections Supplemented by Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation: The D‐ TWIST Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial

**Autores:** Jessica Frey; Aparna Wagle Shukla; Janine Lobo Lopes; Lauren Fanty; Manahil Wajid; Adolfo Ramirez‐Zamora; Irene A\. Malaty; Jackson N\. Cagle; Coralie de Hemptinne; David M\. Scarisbrick; Patrick D\. Worhunsky; Jun Yu

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: For some patients with cervical dystonia \(CD\), botulinum toxin \(BoNT\) injections last less than 12 weeks\. OBJECTIVES: To determine if a novel, accelerated repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation \(rTMS\) protocol is a safe and feasible adjunctive treatment with BoNT for CD\. METHODS: Patients with CD reporting BoNT benefit lasting ≤9 weeks were enrolled in a randomized double\-blind, sham\-controlled, crossover study\. The Toronto Western Spasmodic Torticollis Rating Scale \(TWSTRS\), mood, cognitive, and gait measures were collected before, after, and 2\-weeks following rTMS/SHAM\. RESULTS: In a homogenous subset of patients \(N = 4/5\), TWSTRS scores were lower 2\-weeks following rTMS, but evidence of an effect was potentially limited by sample size\. There was little evidence of rTMS effects on mood, cognition, or gait\. CONCLUSIONS: Accelerated rTMS for CD was safe, feasible, and well\-tolerated; larger randomized studies are needed to determine whether accelerated rTMS may be an effective treatment bridge for dystonia symptoms\.
