# Prospective Worst\-Attack Reliability Floors for Flow\-Based Machine\-Learning Intrusion Detection under Forward\-Time Shift

**Autores:** S\. M\. Bahauddin Bakhtiar

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Flow\-based machine\-learning intrusion detectors are commonly assessed using aggregate scores, which can conceal weak attack\-family recall\. This study tests a source\-calibrated minimum\-recall floor on a short forward\-time UNSW\-NB15 capture as a proof of methodology\. Four raw files were deduplicated to 2,059,414 flows and divided into group\-disjoint source partitions and a later same\-environment test\. Logistic regression, random forest, and XGBoost five\-seed ensembles were screened using source data only\. The contribution integrates family\-recall calibration, simultaneous lower confidence bounds, frozen retention criteria, and cryptographic prediction sealing before future\-label evaluation\. On 1,034,783 future primary flows, the locked XGBoost ensemble achieved 95\.9% accuracy, 68\.1% balanced accuracy, a Macro F1 of 0\.599, and an MCC of 0\.773\. Its minimum simultaneous recall bound was 13\.3%, narrowly exceeding the frozen 12\.6% floor, while worst\-family point recall was 22\.0%\. Analysis, Backdoor, and Reconnaissance did not retain their family\-specific bounds\. Post\-review checks preserved global retention at 5,000 and 10,000 bootstrap replicates, but alternative temporal\-block groupings did not consistently yield a future bound above the original floor\. Feature diagnostics showed both missingness and observed\-value changes without establishing causality\. XGBoost remained best among the three fixed configurations\. Global floor retention therefore supports a limited protocol\-conditional finding; it does not establish acceptable detection performance, uniform family stability, or contemporary deployment readiness\.
