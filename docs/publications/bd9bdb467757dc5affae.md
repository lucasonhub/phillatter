# Chromatic Illusion: Kubrickian Technique of Narrative Depth

**Autores:** Arya B Jayakumar; Sajitha M A

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This article examines color as a formal narrative system in Stanley Kubrick’s cinema\. Using neoformalist close analysis of selected sequences from 2001: A Space Odyssey \(1968\), The Shining \(1980\), and Eyes Wide Shut \(1999\), it identifies three related chromatic operations: construction, warning, and connectivity\. “Chromatic illusion” describes how organized variations in hue, saturation, light, and spatial placement shape spectators’ expectations beyond dialogue and plot\. The Stargate sequence and Room 237 construct contrasting spatial experiences; HAL’s red eye and the blue\-lit bedroom anticipate narrative tension; and recurring red and blue connect dispersed scenes\. Rather than attributing fixed meanings to individual hues, the study shows how chromatic patterns operate within each film’s formal design, suggesting a framework for comparative analysis of cinematic color and narrative perception\.
