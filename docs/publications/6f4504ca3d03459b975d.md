# Interpretable surrogate modeling for load\-aware thermal risk assessment of bolted switchgear busbar joints using electro\-thermal simulation

**Autores:** Kuineng Chen; Huan Liu; Fenglu Li

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Thermal\-risk assessment of bolted busbar joints is complicated by the coupled effects of electrical loading, contact condition, and heat dissipation\. This study develops an interpretable forward\-surrogate framework based on three\-dimensional electro\-thermal finite\-element simulation\. Physics enters through finite\-element generation of the training data, physically defined input variables, and post hoc consistency checks, rather than through constraints embedded in the learning algorithm\. Five prescribed inputs were considered: load current, contact resistance, effective contact ratio, ambient temperature, and convective heat\-transfer coefficient\. The effective\-contact heating power was checked against P ≈ I 2 R c , with a maximum relative deviation of 0\.173% across 400 simulated cases; 382 cases were retained within the intended applicability domain\. Four candidate models were compared using 5 × 10 repeated cross\-validation\. The XGBoost regressor achieved R 2 = 0\.990 ± 0\.005 , R M S E = 1\.877 ± 0\.542 , and M A E = 1\.042 ± 0\.198 K , with performance comparable to random forest\. For four\-class simulation\-oriented thermal\-risk classification, XGBoost achieved an accuracy of 0\.938 ± 0\.026 and a macro F1\-score of 0\.941 ± 0\.033 \. SHapley Additive exPlanations \(SHAP\) analysis identified load current and prescribed contact resistance as the dominant contributors, while convection, ambient temperature, and effective contact ratio produced smaller, condition\-dependent effects\. The lower global importance of effective contact ratio reflects the area\-averaged temperature\-rise target and does not imply that current constriction is physically unimportant\. Across three threshold schemes, all out\-of\-fold errors were between adjacent classes\. The framework is geometry\-specific and has not been experimentally validated\. It predicts internal effective\-contact temperature rise; relating this quantity to infrared surface measurements requires further validation\.
