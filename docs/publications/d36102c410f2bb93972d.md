  benefitted significantly from advances in Artificial Intelligence (AI). Foundation
  models have revolutionized medical image analysis by enabling the transfer of knowledge
  from large-scale pre-training on their source domain, mostly comprising of natural
  images, to specialized, data-scarce medical tasks through a process termed as fine-tuning.
  This is of critical importance in medical image analysis where data privacy laws
  and high acquisition costs limit the availability of large, annotated datasets,
  thus, prohibitting successful training of foundation models. However, the direct
  application of these models to the ethically sensitive domain of healthcare faces
  significant challenges related to trustworthiness. Models trained on imbalanced
  real-world data can inadvertently encode and amplify biases leading to unfair outcomes.
  Similarly, generative models pose risks to patient privacy through memorising the
  training data. Furthermore, the lack of standardized, comprehensive evaluation protocols
  for these models makes it difficult to assess their true clinical value. This thesis
  presents a cohesive body of work that systematically addresses these critical challenges
  by developing novel methodologies and efficient fine-tuning protocols to improve
  the trustworthiness and utility of AI in medical imaging. This thesis, therefore,
  focuses on both discriminative and generative tasks in medical image analysis, with
  a specific emphasis on efficient fine-tuning protocols under the umbrella of Parameter-Efficient
  Fine-Tuning (PEFT). We show that PEFT is a powerful tool not only for reducing computational
  costs but also for enhancing model trustworthiness. We investigate three key aspects
  of trustworthiness: model fairness and bias, which concerns the disparate performance
  of models across different patient subgroups, training data memorization, which
  involves the risk of generative models inadvertently reproducing specific training
  examples and exposing sensitive patient information, and the need for standardized,
  comprehensive benchmarking and evaluation, that moves beyond narrow metrics to assess
  a model''s true clinical value. Our research addresses these challenges through
  a series of several interconnected studies. We first conduct a systematic evaluation
  of various PEFT methods to establish a foundational understanding of their efficacy
  in medical imaging. Following this, we introduce bi-level optimization frameworks,
  where an inner loop fits parameters and an outer loop uses validation performance
  to select the PEFT configuration, to target fairness and privacy risks. Finally,
  we propose a rigorous new benchmark to enable a holistic assessment of generative
  models'' fidelity, privacy, and clinical utility, moving beyond fragmented and inconsistent
  evaluation practices. First, we address the absence of a structured benchmark for
  Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) in the medical image analysis domain (Chapter
  2). Through an extensive evaluation of 17 distinct PEFT methods across both convolutional
  and transformer-based networks, we demonstrate PEFT''s significant potential in
  medical imaging, particularly in low-data scenarios. Our experiments cover the prevalant
  categories of tasks, including discriminative (image classification) and generative
  (text-to-image generation), revealing performance gains of up to 22% over traditional
  full fine-tuning. This study established a foundational benchmark that not only
  fills a critical gap in the literature but also provides the community with valuable
  insights into the most effective PEFT methods for different types of medical tasks
  and data regimes. Building on this, we tackle the critical issue of algorithmic
  bias by introducing FairTune (Chapter 3), a novel bi-level optimization framework
  that leverages PEFT to enhance fairness in downstream medical tasks. Conventional
  fairness methods often fail to address the “fairness generalization gap” that arises
  in high-capacity deep models, which can appear fair on training data but show biased
  performance on unseen data. FairTune overcomes this limitation by automatically
  searching for the optimal configuration of which PEFT parameters to fine-tune that
  specifically maximizes fairness on external data, moving beyond heuristic parameter
  selection strategies. Our empirical findings across diverse medical imaging benchmarks,
  including dermatology and radiology, consistently demonstrate that FairTune reliably
  improves fairness over the standard fine-tuning baseline. Next, we address the significant
  privacy risk posed by training data memorization in generative diffusion models,
  an especially sensitive concern in medical contexts where patient information must
  be protected. We build on the previous study, FairTune (single-objective optimisation),
  and propose a bi-level multi-objective optimisation framework, MemControl (Chapter
  4), that automates the selection of PEFT parameters to achieve a superior trade-off
  between generation quality and memorization mitigation. Our work demonstrates that
  regulating model capacity through PEFT is a highly effective training-time intervention
  that can mitigate memorization. MemControl, by fine-tuning as few as 0.019% of the
  model''s parameters, outperforms existing mitigation strategies while generating
  high-quality medical images. We also show that the discovered parameter subsets
  are transferable to non-medical domains, providing a versatile and effective solution
  for generative tasks in sensitive fields. Finally, we propose CheXGenBench (Chapter
  5), a rigorous and multifaceted evaluation framework that unifies the assessment
  of synthetic chest radiograph generation across fidelity, privacy risks, and clinical
  utility. CheXGenBench overcomes the limitations of fragmented and inconsistent evaluation
  protocols by systematically analyzing 11 leading text-to-image models with over
  20 quantitative metrics, including conditional analysis for individual pathologies.
  Our findings reveal that current generative models struggle with long-tailed data
  distributions, and even models with poor fidelity can pose high patient re-identification
  risks. We further identify a texture-topology gap: synthetic radiographs can preserve
  macroscopic anatomy while losing fine-grained texture, explaining their differing
  utility across classification, segmentation, and report generation. In conclusion,
  this thesis provides a suite of novel methodologies and benchmarks that advance
  the field of medical AI by making foundation model fine-tuning more efficient and
  by developing rigorous, multi-faceted frameworks for building and evaluating trustworthy
  and clinically relevant models. In addition to the methodological advancements,
  we open-source a suite of state-of-the-art generative models and the SynthCheX-75K
  dataset. These artefacts have collectively garnered more than 10,000 downloads,
  underscoring the practical utility of this thesis in advancing responsible AI development
  in healthcare.'

# Efficient fine\-tuning of foundation models for trustworthy medical image analysis

**Autores:** Raman Dutt

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

The domain of biomedicine, specifically biomedical image analysis, has benefitted significantly from advances in Artificial Intelligence \(AI\)\. Foundation models have revolutionized medical image analysis by enabling the transfer of knowledge from large\-scale pre\-training on their source domain, mostly comprising of natural images, to specialized, data\-scarce medical tasks through a process termed as fine\-tuning\. This is of critical importance in medical image analysis where data privacy laws and high acquisition costs limit the availability of large, annotated datasets, thus, prohibitting successful training of foundation models\. However, the direct application of these models to the ethically sensitive domain of healthcare faces significant challenges related to trustworthiness\. Models trained on imbalanced real\-world data can inadvertently encode and amplify biases leading to unfair outcomes\. Similarly, generative models pose risks to patient privacy through memorising the training data\. Furthermore, the lack of standardized, comprehensive evaluation protocols for these models makes it difficult to assess their true clinical value\. This thesis presents a cohesive body of work that systematically addresses these critical challenges by developing novel methodologies and efficient fine\-tuning protocols to improve the trustworthiness and utility of AI in medical imaging\. This thesis, therefore, focuses on both discriminative and generative tasks in medical image analysis, with a specific emphasis on efficient fine\-tuning protocols under the umbrella of Parameter\-Efficient Fine\-Tuning \(PEFT\)\. We show that PEFT is a powerful tool not only for reducing computational costs but also for enhancing model trustworthiness\. We investigate three key aspects of trustworthiness: model fairness and bias, which concerns the disparate performance of models across different patient subgroups, training data memorization, which involves the risk of generative models inadvertently reproducing specific training examples and exposing sensitive patient information, and the need for standardized, comprehensive benchmarking and evaluation, that moves beyond narrow metrics to assess a model&\#x27;s true clinical value\. Our research addresses these challenges through a series of several interconnected studies\. We first conduct a systematic evaluation of various PEFT methods to establish a foundational understanding of their efficacy in medical imaging\. Following this, we introduce bi\-level optimization frameworks, where an inner loop fits parameters and an outer loop uses validation performance to select the PEFT configuration, to target fairness and privacy risks\. Finally, we propose a rigorous new benchmark to enable a holistic assessment of generative models&\#x27; fidelity, privacy, and clinical utility, moving beyond fragmented and inconsistent evaluation practices\. First, we address the absence of a structured benchmark for Parameter\-Efficient Fine\-Tuning \(PEFT\) in the medical image analysis domain \(Chapter 2\)\. Through an extensive evaluation of 17 distinct PEFT methods across both convolutional and transformer\-based networks, we demonstrate PEFT&\#x27;s significant potential in medical imaging, particularly in low\-data scenarios\. Our experiments cover the prevalant categories of tasks, including discriminative \(image classification\) and generative \(text\-to\-image generation\), revealing performance gains of up to 22% over traditional full fine\-tuning\. This study established a foundational benchmark that not only fills a critical gap in the literature but also provides the community with valuable insights into the most effective PEFT methods for different types of medical tasks and data regimes\. Building on this, we tackle the critical issue of algorithmic bias by introducing FairTune \(Chapter 3\), a novel bi\-level optimization framework that leverages PEFT to enhance fairness in downstream medical tasks\. Conventional fairness methods often fail to address the “fairness generalization gap” that arises in high\-capacity deep models, which can appear fair on training data but show biased performance on unseen data\. FairTune overcomes this limitation by automatically searching for the optimal configuration of which PEFT parameters to fine\-tune that specifically maximizes fairness on external data, moving beyond heuristic parameter selection strategies\. Our empirical findings across diverse medical imaging benchmarks, including dermatology and radiology, consistently demonstrate that FairTune reliably improves fairness over the standard fine\-tuning baseline\. Next, we address the significant privacy risk posed by training data memorization in generative diffusion models, an especially sensitive concern in medical contexts where patient information must be protected\. We build on the previous study, FairTune \(single\-objective optimisation\), and propose a bi\-level multi\-objective optimisation framework, MemControl \(Chapter 4\), that automates the selection of PEFT parameters to achieve a superior trade\-off between generation quality and memorization mitigation\. Our work demonstrates that regulating model capacity through PEFT is a highly effective training\-time intervention that can mitigate memorization\. MemControl, by fine\-tuning as few as 0\.019% of the model&\#x27;s parameters, outperforms existing mitigation strategies while generating high\-quality medical images\. We also show that the discovered parameter subsets are transferable to non\-medical domains, providing a versatile and effective solution for generative tasks in sensitive fields\. Finally, we propose CheXGenBench \(Chapter 5\), a rigorous and multifaceted evaluation framework that unifies the assessment of synthetic chest radiograph generation across fidelity, privacy risks, and clinical utility\. CheXGenBench overcomes the limitations of fragmented and inconsistent evaluation protocols by systematically analyzing 11 leading text\-to\-image models with over 20 quantitative metrics, including conditional analysis for individual pathologies\. Our findings reveal that current generative models struggle with long\-tailed data distributions, and even models with poor fidelity can pose high patient re\-identification risks\. We further identify a texture\-topology gap: synthetic radiographs can preserve macroscopic anatomy while losing fine\-grained texture, explaining their differing utility across classification, segmentation, and report generation\. In conclusion, this thesis provides a suite of novel methodologies and benchmarks that advance the field of medical AI by making foundation model fine\-tuning more efficient and by developing rigorous, multi\-faceted frameworks for building and evaluating trustworthy and clinically relevant models\. In addition to the methodological advancements, we open\-source a suite of state\-of\-the\-art generative models and the SynthCheX\-75K dataset\. These artefacts have collectively garnered more than 10,000 downloads, underscoring the practical utility of this thesis in advancing responsible AI development in healthcare\.
