# Increased risk of hepatic outcomes after SARS\-CoV\-2 infection in individuals with pre\-existing chronic liver disease

**Autores:** Jessilin J\. Collins; Roham Hadidchi; Leah Katz; Porsche Lee; Aneeta Channar; Sonya Henry; Tim Q\. Duong

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Viral infections can accelerate chronic liver disease \(CLD\) progression, yet whether SARS\-CoV\-2 infection drives worse long\-term hepatic outcomes in patients with pre\-existing CLD remains unknown\. This retrospective cohort study investigated whether SARS\-CoV\-2 infection was associated with mortality, hepatic decompensation, and abnormal fibrosis\-related laboratory markers in patients with pre\-existing CLD up to 4\.5 years post\-infection\. Adults with pre\-existing CLD in the Montefiore Health System in the Bronx, NY \(February 2020\-August 2024\) were stratified by COVID\-19 hospitalization status and 1:1 propensity\-score matched to uninfected controls, with associations estimated using Cox proportional hazards and logistic regression models\. Among 37,559 adults with pre\-existing CLD, 6,178 tested positive for SARS\-CoV\-2 \(2,182 was hospitalized for COVID\-19\)\. Compared with matched controls, hospitalized COVID\-19 patients showed higher mortality \(HR = 2\.92, 95% CI 2\.00\-4\.27, p &lt; 0\.005\) and higher odds of abnormal Fibrosis 4 score \(FIB4, OR = 1\.37, 1\.21–1\.56\), aspartate aminotransferase \(AST, OR = 1\.60, 1\.04–2\.47\), and platelet count \(PLT, OR = 1\.40, 1\.02–1\.92\) within the first year, with FIB4 remaining abnormal beyond one year \(OR = 1\.20, 1\.06–1\.37, p = 0\.006\)\. In contrast, non\-hospitalized COVID\-19 patients did not show increased mortality but showed lower odds of hepatic decompensation, and lower odds of abnormal aminotransferases within the first year and beyond \( p &lt; 0\.005\)\. These paradoxically favorable associations likely reflect selection bias, as non\-hospitalized COVID\-19 patients represent a healthier subgroup while controls are enriched for sicker patients\. A sensitivity analysis in a matched cohort with FIB4 staging confirmed overall findings\. Severe, hospitalized COVID\-19 is a clinically actionable risk marker in patients with pre\-existing CLD, warranting structured hepatic surveillance, whereas patients with mild infection can be reassured\.
