# Triad of national security management: Controlled artificial intelligence, neural interfaces, and directive personnel allocation

**Autores:** D\. M\. Bortnikov; Mikhail A\. Lebedev; Дмитрий Александрович Репин

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Aim\. To substantiate the content and limits of application of three heterogeneous but interrelated areas of strengthening state capacity in the sphere of national security, specifically controlled artificial intelligence, neural interfaces, and directive personnel allocation, as well as to determine the conditions for their coordinated development without artificial unification into an automated system\. Objective s\. To reveal the concept of controlled artificial intelligence as applied to public administration; to establish the legal and organizational boundaries of delegating analytical and managerial functions to algorithms; to analyze directive personnel allocation as state planning of the training and placement of competencies while preserving the contractual freedom of citizens; to assess the technological maturity of neural interfaces and to identify proven areas of their application; to determine the points of interconnection among the three areas in the tasks of big data processing, predictive modeling, anti\-terrorist prevention, and socio\-economic development\. Methods\. The study employs systemic, institutional, functional, and comparative\-legal approaches, methods of analyzing strategic and regulatory legal documents of Russia, scenario analysis, and qualitative comparison of technological maturity\. The empirical basis consists of materials from the National Anti\-Terrorism Committee and the Federal Security Service of Russia, strategic documents of the Government of Russia, scholarly publications by Russian researchers on data mining, artificial intelligence ethics, and neural interfaces, as well as OECD and UNESCO recommendations and regulatory acts of the European Union\. Results \. It is established that the elements under consideration are at different stages of institutional and technological maturity\. Controlled artificial intelligence is used in analytics, monitoring, forecasting, and the preparation of decision options, provided there is documented human control\. Directive personnel allocation has an existing legal basis in the mechanisms of targeted education, state orders, personnel forecasting, and service rotation, but requires a transition from planning professions to planning competencies and teams\. Neural interfaces have high evidentiary maturity, primarily in medicine and assistive technologies; their application in public administration appears permissible in limited tasks of digital system accessibility, training, and voluntary support for operators\. A differentiated model of state policy is proposed in which the three areas are interconnected at the level of goals and regulations rather than through the unification of data and powers on a single platform\. Conclusions\. The triad can be viewed as a concept of coordinated development of the state’s analytical, personnel, and human\-machine capacity, but not as a ready\-made unified technological framework\. The most mature areas are controlled artificial intelligence and state management of the personnel order\. Neural interfaces require special legal guarantees, ethical expertise, and phased piloting\. The condition for the effectiveness of the triad is the preservation of personal human responsibility, technological sovereignty, the contractual nature of personnel obligations, and the prohibition of covert use of neural data\.
