# Detecting Trade\-Based Money Laundering in the Documents Themselves: A Cross\-Validation Graph with Closed\-Loop Self\-Iteration

**Autores:** Shijie Qu

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Trade\-based money laundering \(TBML\) hides in the documents of trade rather than in the payments that settle it\. Conventional transaction\-monitoring systems examine amounts, frequencies, and velocities, and are therefore blind to the documentary substance of a shipment; machine\-learning systems trained on transaction\-level features inherit the same blindness; and static models of either kind decay as laundering typologies migrate across goods, corridors, and forgery techniques\. This paper describes a detection system, disclosed in U\.S\. Provisional Patent Application No\. 64/170,275, that is organised around the documents themselves\. Heterogeneous trade documents — commercial invoices, bills of lading, customs declarations, packing lists, letters of credit, and associated payment records — are parsed into canonical shipment assertions that retain provenance metadata, and entity mentions across documents are resolved to canonical identities, with alias collisions preserved as signals rather than merged away\. The resolved material is assembled into a heterogeneous entity–document–shipment graph whose edges are of two kinds: documentary edges recording what each document declares, and consistency edges — computationally verifiable assertions linking attributes across documents that purport to describe the same shipment — each carrying a consistency score\. From this graph the system computes features that encode known TBML typologies at the documentary level: unit\-price deviation against benchmark price distributions indexed by HS code, trade corridor, and time window; cross\-document quantity and value reconciliation; circular\-trading cycles; phantom\-shipment document asymmetries; and shipment\-structuring patterns\. A calibrated scoring model emits per\-shipment and per\-entity risk scores with machine\-readable documentary reason codes, exposed through a real\-time scoring API designed for pre\-settlement interception\. Investigation outcomes — suspicious activity report filings, confirmed typologies, and analyst labels — close the loop: confirmed activity reweights the suspicious\-entity subgraph and re\-runs community detection, labelled cases retrain the model, an active\-learning strategy allocates analyst review by uncertainty over graph neighbourhoods, and per\-corridor stationarity testing of benchmark price distributions detects typology drift and triggers benchmark re\-estimation, targeted labelling, and model refresh — all without manual feature re\-engineering\. A further embodiment extends the graph across the full lifecycle of direct\-to\-consumer cross\-border e\-commerce\. The paper reports no evaluation and no measurements; numerical values cited are the design targets and structural parameters stated in the disclosure\.
