# Peptide\-modified nanocellulose from dairy waste for organic compound removal in dairy wastewater

**Autores:** Saeedeh Rastgar; Parichehr Hanachi; Tony Robert Walker

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Dairy effluent with a high organic loading demands efficient and sustainable treatment solutions\. This work aimed to produce a peptide\-modified crystalline nanocellulose \(PCN C \) bio\-adsorbent from dairy sludge and crustacean waste to remove organic compounds from industrial dairy wastewater\. To optimize operational variables for maximum removal of chemical oxygen demand \(COD\), 5\-day biochemical oxygen demand \(BOD₅\) and total organic carbon \(TOC\), response surface methodology \(RSM\) was applied based on a central composite design \(CCD\)\. Operational variables were pH \(3–10\), adsorbent dosage \(100–500 mg L⁻1\), contact time \(15–55 min\), peptide\-to\-cellulose mass ratio \(0\.5:1–3:1\) and temperature \(15–55 °C\)\. The effective functionalization of the peptide and formation of a mesoporous structure with good thermal stability were validated by characterization using FESEM, XRD, FTIR, BET and TGA/DTG\. Under the optimal conditions \(pH 6\.5, PCN C dose of 300 mg L⁻1, contact time of 35 min, peptide\-to\-cellulose mass ratio of 3:1 and temperature of 25 °C\), the maximum removal efficiencies were 97\.80%, 93\.60% and 96\.40% for COD, BOD₅ and TOC, respectively\. The most significant factors were pH and PCN C dose according to analysis of variance \(ANOVA\) \( p &lt; 0\.0001\)\. The quadratic models showed high predictive ability \(R2 = 0\.9519\) and were experimentally validated with a relative error of &lt; 0\.50%\. Furthermore, PCN C showed good reusability, with removal efficiencies of nearly 95% for COD, 92% for BOD₅ and 89% for TOC after five successive adsorption–desorption cycles\. Generally, our results highlight the potential of PCN C as a bio\-based adsorbent for dairy wastewater treatment while promoting waste valorization and circular economy concepts\.
