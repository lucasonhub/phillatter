# Wang, Jiaping: Temporal Integration, Bidirectional Criticality and Emergence of Consciousness Hierarchy English version

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Leading theories of consciousness \(integrated information, global workspace, and predictive processing\) mostly offer static characterizations and do not derive the temporal structure, hierarchical layering, and first\-person character of experience from first\-principle constraints on any finite system\. Starting from three minimal constraints \(bounded resources, non\-equilibrium persistence, and discrete input\), this paper builds a unified framework by eliminative convergence\. To preserve cross\-temporal identity over unlabeled inputs, a system must construct its own causal order and converges on a self\-referential closed loop; with bounded capacity and growing maintenance cost, the framework derives in turn a finite temporal window, active forgetting, a dual\-configuration division of labor, and bidirectional critical transitions, and yields a five\-level spectrum from selfless valence to a first\-person subject\. An information\-level unidirectional constraint makes higher\-order formats irrecoverable from lower\-order ones without loss, so the format gap becomes quantifiable\. Experience receives a structural definition: qualia are the first\-person self\-access process itself rather than an extra substance, dissolving the ontological hard problem while preserving experience\. Presupposing no neural substrate, the framework applies to carbon and silicon alike, yields testable predictions of age\-related temporal decline, anaesthetic emergence lag, and a metabolic working\-memory scaling, and delimits the respective scope of the three major theories\. \(direct link \)
