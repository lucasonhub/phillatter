# Development and Validation of a Blood Test Informed Risk Prediction Model for Lung Cancer—The Copenhagen Lung Cancer Risk Model \(CPH\-Lung\)

**Autores:** Sune Møller Skov\-Jeppesen; Camilla Jannie Kobylecki; Søren Egstrand; Torben Riis Rasmussen; Erik Hugger Jakobsen; Ole Hilberg; Zaigham Saghir; Klaus Fuglsang Kofoed; Stig E\. Bojesen

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose Lung cancer screening reduces lung cancer mortality, but identifying high\-risk individuals remains difficult\. We wanted to develop a blood test–based risk model \(CPH\-Lung\) to improve risk stratification before image\-based screening\. Materials and Methods We included individuals from two prospective general population cohorts to model 6\-year lung cancer risk; the Copenhagen General Population Study \(CGPS\) for training and the Copenhagen City Heart Study \(CCHS\) for validation\. We analyzed clinical and biochemical variables from ever\-smokers \(689 cases and 1,924 controls\) nested within CGPS to train a 6\-year lung cancer risk model\. CPH\-Lung was validated in a cohort of ever\-smokers age ≥50 years in the Copenhagen City Heart Study \(CCHS, 3,195 individuals\) and compared with the validated PLCOm2012 model\. Results There were 689 and 68 events of 6\-year lung cancer in the CGPS and the CCHS\. In the CCHS validation study, the CPH\-Lung versus the PLCOm2012 yielded AUCs of 0\.783 \(95% CI, 0\.731 to 0\.835\) versus 0\.720 \(95% CI, 0\.669 to 0\.770\) for detection of all 6\-year lung cancers \( P = \.0059\)\. The discrimination was similar in the CGPS\. At specificity of 77\.7% \(as would have been achieved using currently applied screening criteria, eg, NELSON or NLST\), sensitivities were 57\.6% versus 39\.2% in women \( P &lt; \.0001\) and 61\.4% versus 54\.7% in men \( P = \.004\) for CPH\-Lung versus PLCOm2012\. The mean age at diagnosis was 68\.2 years \(standard deviation \[SD\], 7\.50\) in the 120 individuals with lung cancer predicted exclusively by CPH\-Lung compared with 76\.5 years \(SD, 8\.04\) in the 37 individuals predicted exclusively by PLCOm2012 \( P &lt; \.0001\)\. Conclusion After inclusion of blood test information, the CPH\-Lung model was more sensitive in the Danish population than PLCOm2012 for prediction of 6\-year lung cancer risk particularly among women and younger individuals\.
