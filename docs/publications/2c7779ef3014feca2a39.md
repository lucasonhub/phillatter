# AI\-Based Traffic flow optimization system using computer vision: research design and prototype

**Autores:** Tarun Sharma; Chitransh Nagar

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

AI\-Based Traffic Flow Optimization System Using Computer Vision: Research Design and Prototype Abstract Urban traffic congestion contributes to increased travel time, fuel consumption, and road\-safety challenges\. Conventional fixed\-time traffic signal systems may not respond efficiently to changing traffic demand\. This project presents the design of an AI\-based traffic flow optimization system using computer vision to support traffic monitoring, vehicle detection, traffic density estimation, vehicle tracking, and adaptive traffic signal recommendations\. Objectives The primary objectives are to: Detect and count vehicles from traffic video footage using computer vision\. Estimate traffic density across different approaches at an intersection\. Analyze traffic conditions to identify congestion and potential incidents\. Develop a density\-aware signal allocation approach that responds to changing traffic demand\. Explore emergency vehicle prioritization and traffic\-performance evaluation\. Methodology The proposed system integrates computer vision, vehicle detection, tracking, traffic density analysis, and signal optimization into a unified monitoring framework\. Traffic observations are processed to estimate vehicle counts and congestion levels\. These estimates can inform signal\-duration recommendations, subject to appropriate operational and safety constraints\. The research workflow also includes dataset inspection, data cleaning, source documentation, and an experimental comparison framework\. Potential evaluation metrics include vehicle\-counting accuracy, average waiting time, queue length, throughput, and fairness across traffic approaches\. Research Contribution The project provides an integrated prototype for investigating computer\-vision\-based traffic management\. Its contribution lies in combining traffic observation, density analysis, adaptive signal recommendations, and research\-oriented data\-quality tools within a single system\. The framework is intended to support further experimentation and reproducible evaluation of intelligent transportation approaches\. Data Sources and Limitations Official road\-safety statistics from published government sources are used to provide historical context for Jaipur and Rajasthan, with geographic scope identified separately\. The demonstration intersection dataset contains synthetic records for testing the data\-processing workflow; it is not a collection of measured Jaipur traffic observations\. The current prototype does not establish experimentally validated reductions in real\-world waiting time, congestion, fuel consumption, or emissions\. Such claims require verified traffic observations, documented experimental conditions, and controlled comparisons against a fixed\-time baseline\. Future Scope Future work includes collecting authorized traffic\-video observations from selected Jaipur intersections, validating vehicle\-detection accuracy against manually annotated samples, evaluating signal\-allocation strategies under comparable demand conditions, and reporting statistical uncertainty and limitations\. Keywords Computer Vision; Intelligent Transportation Systems; Traffic Flow Optimization; Vehicle Detection; Vehicle Tracking; Traffic Density Estimation; Adaptive Traffic Signal Control; Road Safety; Jaipur; Artificial Intelligence\.
