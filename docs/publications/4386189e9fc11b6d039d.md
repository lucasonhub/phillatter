# Usefulness of serum amyloid A to differentiate feline inflammatory lower respiratory tract diseases from cardiac or neoplastic disease: a retrospective study of 22 cats \(2021‐2024\)

**Autores:** E\. Berro; Aline Fastrès; Anaïs Lamoureux

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OBJECTIVE: To evaluate the usefulness of serum amyloid A concentration in differentiating inflammatory lower respiratory tract diseases from cardiac/neoplastic respiratory diseases in cats\. METHODS: A retrospective analysis of medical records of cats presented between 2021 and 2024 to a single referral centre was performed\. Cats in respiratory distress with serum amyloid A assay performed within 48 hours after admission were included and divided into two groups \(inflammatory and cardiac/neoplastic\)\. The inflammatory group included infectious or non\-infectious inflammatory lower respiratory tract diseases, and the cardiac/neoplastic group was subdivided into cardiac and neoplastic subgroups\. Signalment, clinical signs and diagnostic testing results, including serum amyloid A concentration, were recorded and compared between groups \(P &lt; \.05\)\. RESULTS: Twenty\-two cats were included\. Median serum amyloid A concentration was significantly higher in the inflammatory group \(57\.5 μg/mL \(12\.2 to 70\.3\); n = 15\) compared to the cardiac/neoplastic group \(5 μg/mL \(5\); n = 7; P = \.003\)\. There was also a significant difference among the inflammatory, cardiac \(5 μg/mL \(5\); n = 3\) and N \(5 μg/mL \(5\); n = 4\) subgroups \(P = \.01\); however, pairwise comparisons were not statistically significant\. The optimal serum amyloid A concentration cut\-off to differentiate between inflammatory and cardiac/neoplastic groups was 8\.6 μg/mL \(sensitivity of 80% \(95% confidence interval: 59\.5 to 91\.6\) and specificity of 100% \(95% confidence interval: 84\.6 to 100\)\)\. CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: Cardiac and neoplastic diseases were associated with low serum amyloid A concentration\. Serum amyloid A concentration could be a potential diagnostic tool but larger studies are needed to validate the proposed cut\-off value\. Further prospective studies are also needed to establish the usefulness of serum amyloid A concentration in distinguishing between infectious and non\-infectious inflammatory diseases\.
