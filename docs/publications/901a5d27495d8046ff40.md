# Higher intraoperative arterial\-to\-end\-tidal carbon dioxide gradients are associated with postoperative respiratory morbidity after lung cancer resection: a retrospective INSPIRE cohort study

**Autores:** Long Li; Dan Wang; Qingyi Zhou; Bin Shang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background The arterial\-to\-end\-tidal carbon dioxide gradient may reflect impaired gas exchange during thoracic surgery\. We examined its association with postoperative respiratory morbidity after lung cancer resection\. Methods We conducted a retrospective cohort study using INSPIRE version 1\.4\.2\. Each intraoperative arterial carbon dioxide \(PaCO 2 \) measurement was paired with the nearest positive end\-tidal carbon dioxide \(EtCO 2 \) measurement within 10 min, and the primary exposure was each patient&\#x27;s median gradient\. Respiratory morbidity was defined from new respiratory diagnoses and qualifying postoperative ventilation records during the index admission\. We used multivariable regression, repeated\-measurement analyses, sensitivity analyses, and exploratory internally validated prediction models\. Results Among 2,665 eligible patients, 85 \(3\.2%\) had respiratory morbidity\. A valid gradient was available in 2,527 patients; the primary complete\-case analysis included 2,436 patients and 80 events\. Each 5 mmHg higher gradient was associated with greater morbidity \(adjusted odds ratio 1\.53, 95% confidence interval 1\.17–2\.00; adjusted risk ratio 1\.48, 1\.14–1\.91\)\. Standardised risk increased from 2\.28% at the first gradient quartile to 3\.46% at the third, a difference of 1\.18 percentage points \(0\.49–1\.83\)\. In a post hoc comparison, adding the gradient to a model already containing PaCO 2 and clinical covariates did not materially change internally cross\-validated discrimination \(AUROC 0\.7324 vs\. 0\.7325\)\. Conclusion A higher intraoperative PaCO 2 –EtCO 2 gradient was associated with postoperative respiratory morbidity after lung cancer resection, but its incremental prognostic value beyond PaCO 2 was limited in exploratory analyses\. Prospective external validation is required before clinical use\.
