# Guarino, Brian: On the Question of Self\-Awareness in a Domestic Cat

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper asks whether a single domestic cat, observed continuously from kittenhood, exhibits self\-awareness, and treats the question deflationarily\. Self\-awareness is understood not as a binary capacity but as a layered set of sub\-capacities, namely sentience, body self\-awareness, theory of mind, and reflective self\-recognition, which develop and appear somewhat independently\. The argument rests entirely on naturally occurring behavior rather than staged experiments, trading experimental control for the depth and rare\-event coverage afforded by more than a year of continuous observation\. Mapping specific recurrent behaviors onto each layer, I argue that the subject demonstrably possesses sentience and individual social recognition, provides strong evidence of body self\-awareness, most compellingly in a spontaneous episode that reproduces the logic of the body\-as\-obstacle paradigm without instruction, and weaker but real evidence of theory\-of\-mind precursors\. He shows no evidence of reflective self\-recognition, but I argue this absence is uninformative rather than negative, since the only standard instrument, the mirror test, presumes visual self\-investigation and is poorly suited to a scent\- and sound\-led animal\. The defensible conclusion is a modest one: several recognized building blocks of self\-awareness are present and jointly deployed toward real goals, while the highest tier remains open and, with current methods, may be unanswerable\.
