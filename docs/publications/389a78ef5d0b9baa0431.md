# Towards precision medicine in diabetes: pharmacogenomic approaches for MODY and type 2 diabetes with insights from GLIS3 gene variants

**Autores:** Abhisheka Lenin; Jasmin Sajini Rajayan

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Diabetes mellitus encompasses a heterogeneous group of metabolic disorders with a significant genetic basis that current standardized treatment protocols fail to adequately address\. Type 2 diabetes in particular is driven by genetic factors that operate independently of lifestyle, with a substantial proportion of patients in South Asian populations developing the disease despite normal body weight and healthy habits\. India presents a uniquely important context for pharmacogenomic research given its extraordinary population stratification across tribal, linguistic, religious, and geographic groups creating a genetic landscape that Western genomic data cannot adequately represent\. Objective This review evaluates pharmacogenomic evidence across monogenic and polygenic diabetes subtypes, presents Indian clinical case studies demonstrating the consequences of genomic misclassification, and positions GLIS3 as an underexplored regulatory gene with cross\-disease relevance spanning neonatal diabetes, Type 1, and Type 2 diabetes\. It further examines the potential for expanded clinical implementation of genotype\-guided therapy, particularly in genetically diverse populations\. Key insights GLIS3 encodes a zinc finger transcription factor that governs beta\-cell development, insulin gene transcription, and endoplasmic reticulum stress protection by functioning as a master regulator of beta\-cell survival rather than acting through a single pathway\. Its association across multiple diabetes subtypes, combined with emerging evidence of population\-specific variants in Indian cohorts, positions it as a compelling future pharmacogenomic target\. However, no GLIS3\-directed clinical testing or therapy is currently available, and its pharmacogenomic utility remains hypothetical pending prospective validation\. Indian pharmacogenomic data further reveal 76 known variants affecting antidiabetic drug response, with higher frequencies of poor metformin response alleles compared to global population findings with immediate clinical implications for one of the world’s highest diabetes\-burden nations\. Conclusion Realizing the potential of precision medicine in diabetes requires population\-specific genomic research, integration of pharmacogenomic testing into national health schemes such as Ayushman Bharat, development of AI\-driven clinical decision tools, and policy frameworks guided by bodies such as ICMR\. Genotype\-guided therapy represents an emerging clinical priority with the potential to improve outcomes in genetically diverse populations where standardized protocols may be suboptimal\.
