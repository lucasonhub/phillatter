# Mothering at the Edge of Time: Ectogenesis, Gestational Communism, and the Ironic Cyborg in Marge Piercy’s Woman on the Edge of Time

**Autores:** Labanya Dey; Smita Jha

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Every person now alive was gestated inside a body\. Artificial womb technology promises to change that, and the feminist tradition has never agreed on what the change would mean\. This paper reads Marge Piercy’s Woman on the Edge of Time \(1976\) as the century’s most searching attempt to hold that disagreement open rather than resolve it\. Against readings that take the novel as either a straightforward emancipatory utopia or a cautionary tale, I argue that Piercy stages ectogenesis as an ironic cyborg utopia in Donna Haraway’s precise sense: the technology liberates only under conditions of collective ownership, and the same device, transposed into neoliberal biocapitalism, becomes an instrument of domination\. The novel’s brooders do not abolish the mother; they redistribute her, most vividly in the image of men who lactate and wet\-nurse\. Reading this redistribution alongside Shulamith Firestone’s call to overthrow the “sex class,” Herbert Marcuse’s account of technological rationality, and Melinda Cooper’s analysis of life as biocapital, I show that the question the novel poses is not whether machines can gestate but who controls the machine\. Speculative fiction functions here not as prophecy but as method: a mode of feminist fabulation that makes the political condition of a technology visible before the technology arrives\. The paper thus intervenes in current debates over ectogenesis by insisting that its emancipatory potential is entirely conditional, and that the condition is a revolution the present forecloses\.
