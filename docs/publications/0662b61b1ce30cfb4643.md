# The Future of the Law of the Sea: Emerging Challenges and the Niger Delta in a Changing Maritime Order

**Autores:** Oluchi Jane Jide\-Bello

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The international law of the sea is undergoing substantial transformation as climate change, sea level rise, marine biodiversity loss, deep\-seabed activities, marine genetic resources, autonomous shipping, maritime cybersecurity, illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing, maritime insecurity \.247and geopolitical competition increasingly challenge established approaches to ocean governance\. This article examined the capacity of the 1982 United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea \(UNCLOS\) to respond to these emerging challenges, with particular attention to Africa, the Gulf of Guinea and the Niger Delta\. Using a doctrinal methodology, the article analysed international legal instruments, judicial and institutional developments, regional arrangements, Nigerian legislation and scholarly literature\. It argued that UNCLOS remains the foundational framework of the international law of the sea, but its continuing effectiveness depends upon adaptive interpretation, complementary treaty development, technological regulation, environmental protection, institutional cooperation and effective domestic implementation\. The Niger Delta is a demonstration of how international maritime obligations intersect with petroleum pollution, marine ecosystems, fisheries, coastal livelihoods and maritime security\. Recent developments, including the entry into force of the BBNJ Agreement in January 2026, the adoption of the non\-mandatory MASS Code by the International Maritime Organization and recent proceedings before the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea, demonstrate that the maritime legal order is evolving rather than being replaced\. In conclusion, the future of the law of the sea lies in strengthening the UNCLOS system through progressive development and effective implementation, particularly in developing coastal States\.
