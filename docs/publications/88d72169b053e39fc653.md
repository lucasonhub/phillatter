# Random forest\-based soil organic carbon prediction using landsat temporal indices and global covariates in a Peruvian coastal valley

**Autores:** Carlos Carbajal; Carlos Mattos\-Mendoza; Rosario Javier\-Astete; Kevin Ortega\-Quispe; Ruth Gutierrez\-Oriundo; Santiago Altamirano\-Quispe

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study evaluated whether a global modeled soil product adds predictive value to locally calibrated digital soil mapping of topsoil soil organic carbon \(SOC; 0–30 cm\) in an irrigated Peruvian coastal valley\. Random forest models used 135 composite samples collected in 2024–2025, terrain derivatives, temporally weighted Landsat indices, and OpenLandMap\-soildb predictions\. Configurations were compared using internal performance assessment with 200 random 80/20 partitions and 50 repetitions of block spatial cross\-validation \(SpCV\); no independent external validation dataset was used\. The retained model \(elevation, slope, focal valley wetness index, topographic position index, and OpenLandMap\-soildb p84\) showed weak transferable predictive ability within the valley, with SpCV R² = 0\.155 \(95% CI 0\.077–0\.221\), compared with 0\.189 under random partitioning\. Terrain alone achieved SpCV R² = 0\.090; adding p84 increased this by 0\.065, whereas p84 alone performed poorly \(R² = –0\.058\)\. OpenLandMap therefore offered limited supplementary information and could not replace local environmental covariates or field observations\. Predictions were displayed on a 30 m grid, but p84 retained its effective 120 m support and the composite observations represented management areas up to 3 ha\. Quantile regression forest intervals achieved empirical coverage of 0\.88 at the nominal 90% level under both internal resampling schemes, with a median mapped width of 1\.51% SOC\. The area of applicability included 97\.5% of mapped pixels but assessed covariate similarity, not prediction reliability\. The main contribution is an evaluation protocol showing why single\-partition model rankings are unstable; the map supports screening and sampling prioritization, not field\-level decisions\.
