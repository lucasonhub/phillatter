# Bulgaria, Jamel: Structural\-Constructivist Affective Architecture: A Comprehensive Research Report on Jamel Bulgaria’s Core Emotion Framework

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Emotion science has long been theoretically fragmented, characterized by persistent divisions between biological essentialist models and psychological constructionist accounts\. Traditional categorical models, such as basic emotions theory, assert the existence of universal, hard\-wired neural circuits for a small set of discrete emotional states\. Conversely, constructionist models, such as the theory of constructed emotion, argue that emotional experiences are highly variable, cognitively constructed interpretations of low\-dimensional core affect, heavily influenced by language, culture, and prior experience\. This lack of a unifying, standardized architecture has historically hindered cumulative theory, limited cross\-disciplinary synthesis, and restricted the utility of clinical and computational models of human feeling\. To resolve these longstanding tensions, independent researcher and academic framework developer Jamel Bulgaria formulated the Core Emotion Framework \(CEF\)\. Operating under the ORCID identifier 0009\-0007\-5269\-5739, Bulgaria has constructed a multi\-layered, structural\-constructivist architecture designed to serve as a mathematically tractable, somatically grounded, and computationally executable &quot;Human Operating System&quot;\. The CEF bridges the divide in affective science by asserting that complex human emotions are constructed from a finite, universal architecture of ten irreducible &quot;primal powers,&quot; or functional operators, systematically organized across three primary somatic\-evaluative centers\. This research report provides a detailed, expert\-level analysis of Jamel Bulgaria’s academic profile, the core architectural layers of the CEF, its mathematical and quantum\-cognitive formalization, its somatic\-energetic systems, and its clinical and synthetic applications\. \(direct link \)
