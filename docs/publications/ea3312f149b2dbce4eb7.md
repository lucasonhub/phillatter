# Artificial Intelligence\-Enabled Biomarker Discovery in Clinical Biochemistry: Emerging Biomarkers, Machine Learning and Barriers to Clinical Translation

**Autores:** Dr\. K\. Narendra Kumar Reddy; K\. Ravi; Pedavegi\. Mounika; Guntaka\. Charita Sree

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Clinical biochemistry now produces large, multidimensional datasets from automated analysers, laboratory information systems, mass spectrometry and molecular assays\. Artificial intelligence \(AI\), and machine learning \(ML\) in particular, offers tools to combine such measurements into multivariable patterns, to monitor laboratory quality and to prioritise candidate biomarkers\. This review examines how AI is used across the pre\-analytical, analytical and post\-analytical phases of laboratory work and how it is being applied to four groups of emerging biomarkers: pentraxin\-3, extracellular vesicle cargo, circulating microRNAs and adipokines\. It describes the main learning approaches, the discovery pipeline, the failure modes that most often inflate reported accuracy \(overfitting, data leakage, batch effects and confounding\) and a staged validation pathway running from analytical validation to post\-deployment monitoring\. The literature indicates that AI is most defensible where it supports established laboratory processes and combines established tests with emerging markers\. High discrimination in a development cohort does not establish biological validity or clinical usefulness\. Standardised measurement, leakage\-free modelling, calibration, external and prospective validation, transparent reporting and human oversight are the conditions under which AI\-assisted biomarker research can move into routine practice, including in resource\-variable settings such as India\.
