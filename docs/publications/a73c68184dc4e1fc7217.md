# Machine\-learning\-Based modelling of multicomponent forward osmosis processes using novel ternary organic draw solutions

**Autores:** Kudzai Hamish Ruzvidzo; Ashwani Kumar Tiwari; Raminder Kaur; Deshraj Meena; Yogendra Kumar Meena; Manish Jain

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Predicting water flux in forward osmosis \(FO\) systems that use multicomponent organic draw solutions remains a difficult problem This study evaluates whether data\-driven models can predict flux for binary mono propylene glycol propyl ether \(PGPE\) and ternary hydroxypropyl cellulose\-PGPE \(HPC\-PGPE\) and sodium carboxymethyl cellulose\-PGPE \(NaCMC\-PGPE\) draw solutions, using 312 flux measurements from 26 independent bench\-scale FO runs\. As the 12 measurements within each 120\-min run lie on a single flux\-decay trajectory, five partitioning schemes of increasing strictness were compared: point\-wise random, run\-wise random, leave\-one\-run\-out \(LORO\), leave\-one\-operating\-condition\-out \(LOCO\), and leave\-one\-draw\-solution\-out \(LODO\) cross\-validation\. Point\-wise partitioning overstated accuracy relative to run\-wise partitioning, confirming leakage from within\-run autocorrelation\. Under LORO, the best network \(one hidden layer, 16 neurons, 161 parameters\) achieved R² = 0\.56, RMSE = 2\.92 LMH, against a measured experimental repeatability of 1\.42 LMH \(11\.4% RSD\); a nine\-parameter ridge regression performed better still \(R² = 0\.803, RMSE = 1\.95 LMH\), indicating a neural architecture is not justified here\. Withholding an entire operating condition \(LOCO\) or draw\-solution formulation \(LODO\) eliminated this advantage: both models underperformed the trivial mean\-flux baseline \(R² = −0\.04–−1\.09; RMSE of 5\.0–7\.7 LMH\), most severely when the withheld formulation&\#x27;s molecular weight fell outside the training range\.
