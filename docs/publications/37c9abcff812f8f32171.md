# Fostering team learning for educational innovation: An academic development initiative on genAI and assessment

**Autores:** Franziska Zellweger; Tobias Röhl; Sabine Binder; Jacqueline Egli; Mònica Feixas i Condom; David Gavin; Thomas Matti; Luca Moser; Bernadette Spieler; Saskia Waibel; Markus Weil; Tobias Zimmermann

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Drawing on the notion of workplace learning this study examines how academic development can design and facilitate team learning in the academic workplace to support co\-constructive educational innovation\. Based on a qualitative case study at a Swiss University of Teacher Education, it analyses a participatory initiative addressing generative artificial intelligence \(genAI\) in assessment\. The staged process combined SWOT\-based collective sense\-making, a design sprint, selective project funding, and facilitated reflection during project development\. Data consisted of process documentation and collaboratively generated written reflections, which were analysed and refined through iterative feedback and co\-writing\. The findings show how structured formats, shared artefacts, and deliberate facilitation enabled participants to make distributed expertise visible, develop shared problem understandings, form project teams, and move from exchange towards co\-construction and coordinated experimentation\. At the same time, public pitching, funding decisions, time pressures, and institutional constraints created tensions between collaboration and competition, openness and focus, and participation and decision\-making\. The study suggests that academic development can support educational innovation by acting as facilitator, trust\-building broker, and co\-learner\. Its contribution lies in conceptualising participatory innovation processes as workplace\-learning arrangements that connect emerging team\-learning processes with local experimentation and wider institutional conversations, while recognising that organisational learning requires further institutional uptake\.
