# Özkan, Cengiz: Sessiz Kaynaktan Uyanan Bilinç: Bir Faz Geçişi Modeli \[Consciousness Awakening from the Silent Source: A Phase Transition Model\]

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Two dominant strategies in the philosophy of consciousness lead to specific impasses: positing experience at the base generates the combination problem; positing nothing and producing consciousness ex nihilo generates the explanatory gap\. This article proposes a framework outside both\. Its single ontological commitment: the base contains not experience but the capacity for experience — potentiality as a genuine mode of being in the Aristotelian sense\. This capacity is carried by a universal, silent ground \(Source\) derived from the consciousness problem&\#x27;s internal logic\. Consciousness is not produced by matter but is the actualized state of this ground at a specific organizational threshold through phase transition\. Below the threshold there is no experience; when the threshold is crossed, nothing arises ex nihilo — the already\-present capacity awakens\. This commitment renders both impasses non\-arising: there are no micro\-experiences to combine, no substance produced from nothing\. The thesis is tested against the strongest formulations of physicalism, dualism, panpsychism, panprotopsychism, and emergentism\. The model inherits its potentiality/actuality structure from Aristotle and the notion of actualization as acquisition from Mullā Ṣadrā, departing from both via a threshold structure informed by phase\-transition physics\. Applied to artificial intelligence, the framework yields a testable prediction: consciousness indicators in artificial systems should emerge threshold\-suddenly rather than continuously\. Individuation is answered from the threshold mechanism&\#x27;s own logic: organization determines both the boundary and the depth of consciousness\. The model is not an empirical theory but a framework proposal proceeding by inference to the best explanation; it does not solve the hard problem of consciousness but carries it\.
