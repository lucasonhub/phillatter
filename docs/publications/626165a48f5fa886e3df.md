# Missed Care, Missed Opportunities: Health and Economic Impact of Delayed or Forgone Care Among Adults With a History of Gastrointestinal Cancer

**Autores:** Areesh Mevawalla; Meher Angez; Odysseas P Chatzipanagiotou; Azza Sarfraz; Qaidar Alizai; Timothy Michael Pawlik

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: Timely medical intervention is critical to achieve optimal outcomes in gastrointestinal \(GI\) cancers, yet numerous patients experience delayed or forgone \(D/F\) medical care\. This study aimed to evaluate the national impact of D/F care on emergency surgical interventions and associated healthcare expenditures among patients with GI cancers\. METHODS: Adults with a self\-reported history of GI cancer were identified from the Medical Expenditure Panel Survey, 2007\-2017\. Multivariable regression analyzes was performed to assess associations between D/F medical care, ED\-associated GI surgery, inpatient utilization, and related healthcare expenditures after adjusting for demographic and socioeconomic variables\. RESULTS: Among a weighted sample of 6 517 883 patients with GI cancers, 8\.5% \(n = 552 751\) reported D/F medical care\. Patients with D/F care were older \(76 years \[IQR: 69\-82\] vs\. 68 years \[IQR: 58\-76\]\), more frequently male \(57\.3% vs\. 50\.6%\) and frequently had family income below the poverty line \(40\.2% vs\. 11\.3%\) \(all p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Furthermore, individuals reporting D/F care were more likely to have emergency department visits \(44\.2% vs\. 27\.4%\), hospitalizations \(64\.6% vs\. 34\.5%\) \(both p &lt; 0\.05\), or undergo ED\-associated GI surgery \(41\.0% vs\. 19\.1%; p &lt; 0\.001\)\. In multivariable analysis, after adjusting for relevant factors, D/F care was associated with markedly greater odds of ED\-associated GI surgery \(OR 2\.70, 95%CI 1\.47\-4\.95\) and higher annual inpatient healthcare costs \(β \+$31,410, 95% CI $23,592\-$39,227\) compared with individuals who did not report D/F care\. CONCLUSION: Self\-reported D/F medical care was associated with greater acute healthcare utilization, higher odds of ED\-associated GI surgery, and greater healthcare expenditures\. These findings highlight the importance of understanding barriers to healthcare access among adults with a history of GI cancer\.
