# Scale effects on wave forces and flow structures around a perforated semicircular submerged breakwater

**Autores:** Han Wang; 魏红康; Yikang Gao; Dake Chen; Zishun Yao; Tiago Fazeres\-Ferradosa; Yin Cheng; Yali Luo; Dawei Guan

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Perforated semicircular submerged breakwaters can attenuate incident waves while allowing water exchange, making them suitable for coastal protection and marine infrastructure\. Their hydraulic performance is often assessed using physical models based on Froude similarity, for which Reynolds similarity cannot be preserved\. Extrapolating wave loads from reduced models to prototype conditions may therefore introduce bias\. Methods To quantify this effect, a validated numerical wave flume was used to examine six geometric scale ratios \( λ L = 1, 5, 10, 15, 25, and 35\), four perforation rates \( ϵ = 0–15%\), and five wave conditions\. Validation used measurements from physical model experiments and field pressures obtained at prototype scale\. Comparisons considered horizontal force coefficients, free surface elevations, velocity fields, and vorticity fields\. Results Smaller models increasingly overestimated the shoreward peak force\. At λ L = 35 and ϵ = 10%, the mean model to prototype ratio of the shoreward peak force coefficient reached 1\.23\. The differences between model and prototype responses were mainly associated with changes in wave transmission, reverse flow, and vortical motion over and behind the structure as scale decreased\. Discussion These findings improve the interpretation of reduced model results and support more reliable prototype extrapolation of wave loads on perforated submerged structures\.
