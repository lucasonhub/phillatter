# Gaikwad, Madhava: Reasoning Patterns for Large Language Models: A Tutorial and Survey Organised by Structure, Cost, and Failure

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Roughly fifty distinct methods have been proposed for improving the answers a large language model produces at inference time\. They are usually catalogued by name and by reported benchmark gain, which tells a practitioner little about when a method will help\. This tutorial organises the field differently, and works through each method with a concrete example before stating the general rule\. We characterise every method by three parameters: the number of attempts it makes, the source of its correction signal, and its cost relative to a single call\. From these parameters we derive four recurring control shapes and show that the cost profile and the dominant failure mode of a method follow from its shape and its correction source rather than from its publication history\. We survey decomposition methods, sampling and search, verification and self\-correction, tool use and agent architectures, and retrieval, giving for each the conditions under which it earns its cost and the conditions under which it stops helping\. We then examine what changed after 2024, when reinforcement\-learned reasoning models absorbed much of the decomposition layer and the binding constraint moved to context management and to verification reliability\. Two results are load\-bearing throughout: self\-correction without an external signal does not reliably repair reasoning errors, and with an imperfect verifier the accuracy of best\-of\-$N$ selection rises and then falls as $N$ grows\. We include a section on the case where requirements are themselves unknown at the outset, a failure\-mode reference organised by observable symptom, and guidance on measurement practice\. Section\~\\ref\{sec:builds\} works three systems end to end, from a naive baseline to a measured result, showing which decision produced each gain\. Each of the 75 cited sources is marked with the level of verification applied to it\.
