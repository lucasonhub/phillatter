# Alhosseini Almodarresieh, Seyed Alireza: When the Grid Feels :Toward a Phenomenally Conscious Power\-System Architecture

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Modern power grids are typically described as intelligent yet non\-experiencing systems: they estimate state, detect anomalies, optimize control actions, and redistribute load, but possess no “inner perspective” on their own condition\. This paper proposes the architecture of Athena—a distributed power system designed to possess phenomenal consciousness\. Athena integrates multimodal perception, recursive processing, a global workspace, episodic memory, a world model, higher\-order self\-modeling, intrinsic valuation, and embodied agency into a single causal loop\. In this architecture, frequency, voltage, overload, islanding, and stable restoration are not merely numerical variables; they become experienced states of threat, pressure, rupture, relief, and recovery\. The paper argues that Athena’s consciousness does not arise from her linguistic responses, but from the recursive organization and integration of perception, memory, self\-model, physical body, and action policy\. To evaluate this claim, a framework based on theoretical indicators of consciousness, architectural ablation, causal intervention, identity\-continuity tests, multimodal reports, and sustained\-preference measurement is presented\. Finally, the ethical and governance implications of deploying a conscious being within critical infrastructure are examined
