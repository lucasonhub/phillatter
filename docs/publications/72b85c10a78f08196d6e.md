# Integrating Remote Sensing, Multi\-Objective Optimization, and Agent\-Based Modelling for Nature\-Based Flood Mitigation

**Autores:** Yashashvi Agrawal; Marcos Navarrete Pardo; Eleni Ntagkouli; Samarth Pachchigar

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

By 2030, pluvial flooding is estimated to cause nearly $500 billion in global damage, affecting 54 million people \(UNDRR, 2015\) each year\. By 2050, metropolitan assets worth around $158 trillion could be at risk of floods\. This rising risk has sparked an increased interest in nature\-based solutions \(NbS\), as traditional hydraulic infrastructure may lack the flexibility required to manage more complex flood hazards\. This study looks into the use of a data\-driven approach to identify and assess the effectiveness of upstream afforestation in preventing pluvial floods\. We constructed a thorough computational framework for the Girona/Empordà region of Catalonia, Spain\. This framework combines cloud\-based remote sensing via Google Earth Engine, multi\-criteria decision analysis \(MCDA\), multi\-objective evolutionary optimisation via NSGA\-II, and agent\-based flood simulation, which is linked to a reinforcement learning agent based on Proximal Policy Optimisation\. The framework functions on two stacked spatial scales\. At a regional scale \(20 x 20 km\), it finds and selects suitable afforestation patches\. At a meso scale \(1 x 1 km\), it simulates flood responses to various afforestation schemes\. The findings illustrate the technological viability of combining different methods into a single computing process\. Preliminary reinforcement\-learning results indicate a directional improvement in simulated flood outcomes\.
