# Evaluation of DeepSeek with a RAG\-based knowledge base in preoperative assessment for arthroplasty to improve the decision\-making accuracy of junior physicians

**Autores:** Haibo Wu; Mingzhuang Hou; Gongkai Chen; Zhenlin Wen; Changao Yang; Songlin Li; Xiangfei Meng; Peilai Liu; Qunshan Lu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: Patients undergoing elective joint replacement are mostly elderly individuals with multiple chronic diseases\. These patients have a higher risk, so preoperative assessment for joint replacement is vital for surgical safety but remains challenging for junior physicians\. This study aimed to evaluate the decision\-making performance of different versions of DeepSeek \(Chat and R1\) and their knowledge base \(KB\)\-enhanced schemes in perioperative risk assessment and their impact on junior physicians&\#x27; decision\-making quality\. METHODS: A total of 292 anonymized medical records from joint replacement patients were retrospectively analyzed\. A professional knowledge base was constructed using 21 perioperative management guidelines and retrieval\-augmented generation \(RAG\) technology\. The performance of four models \(DeepSeek\-Chat, DeepSeek\-Chat \+ KB, DeepSeek\-R1, and DeepSeek\-R1 \+ KB\) was compared in ASA scoring, RCRI scoring, surgical tolerance judgment, and risk prediction\. These indicators are related to specific decisions in joint replacement surgery, including preoperative optimization before elective TKA/THA, mitigation of infection risk, planning of VTE prophylaxis, and indication for postponing elective arthroplasty, etc\. Senior physicians evaluated model performance using a Likert scale\. A 5 × 5 balanced Latin square crossover design was used to assess the impact of model assistance on junior physicians&\#x27; clinical decision\-making\. RESULTS: No significant difference was found in ASA scoring accuracy \(p &gt; 0\.05\)\. For RCRI scoring and risk stratification, DeepSeek\-R1 models \(79\.1%\-79\.7%\) outperformed Chat models \(56\.5%\-66\.0%, p &lt; 0\.05\)\. In surgical tolerance assessment, DeepSeek\-R1 \+ KB showed relatively better performance, with significantly better predictive precision \(p = 0\.0284\) and a lower missed diagnosis rate \(p = 0\.005\) compared to the Chat models\. The R1 series also showed higher predictive efficiency \(87\.2%\-86\.4% vs\. 63\.6%\-61\.8%, p &lt; 0\.05\)\. The R1 models showed better performance than Chat models in medical comprehension, retrieval accuracy, and reasoning logic\. After adjusting for physician variability and patient case mix, the DeepSeek\-R1 \+ KB showed a statistically significant improvement over independent decision\-making \(OR = 1\.58, p = 0\.004\), as well as the DeepSeek\-R1 and Chat series\. CONCLUSION: The DeepSeek\-R1 model, with chain\-of\-thought reasoning, shows a marginal advantage over the Chat version in preoperative assessment for joint replacement\. As a cost\-effective clinical auxiliary tool, its integration with a professional knowledge base scheme can optimize the decision\-making quality of junior physicians, particularly in terms of providing helpful and comprehensive decision support and providing novel insights for individualized assessment in the context of precision medicine\.
