# Machine Learning Classification and Adaptive Power Control for RGB Underwater Visible Light Communication

**Autores:** Eithar Alammari; M\. A\. Majid

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Underwater visible light communication \(UVLC\) offers high data rates over acoustic and radio\-frequency links but is highly sensitive to bubble\-induced scattering\. This paper presents an RGB wavelength\-division\-multiplexed UVLC system simulated in OptiSystem across clear\-water, intermediate, and bubble\-induced turbidity conditions\. Eye\-diagram features are classified using XGBoost, achieving 98\.75% test accuracy and 96\.18% \(±2\.17%\) repeated cross\-validation accuracy, statistically validated against SVM and Random Forest\. A held\-out intermediate\-turbidity set, unseen during training, is correctly identified by wavelength with 92\.31–70\.65% accuracy across 25–75% turbidity\. An independently computed Mie scattering calculation shows bubble\-induced scattering becomes wavelength\-insensitive at realistic bubble sizes, consistent with the classifier’s confusion pattern\. A systematic grid search over laser bias and modulation current on the blue channel identifies a distinct optimal operating point for clear\-water and bubble\-induced conditions, with bubble\-induced BER reaching 6\.95×10−9 at its optimum, and indicates a sharp, condition\-dependent transition in which settings tolerated under clear water drive the bubble\-induced link toward its estimated reliability limit\. Shannon capacity analysis indicates multi\-gigabit\-per\-second headroom, and comparison with acoustic and radio\-frequency alternatives confirms UVLC’s data\-rate advantage at short range\. The framework connects physical\-layer simulation, classification, and characterized adaptive control within a single validated system\.
