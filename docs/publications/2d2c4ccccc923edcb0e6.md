# Comparative evaluation of artificial intelligence\-based and pathologist scoring of murine bile duct hyperplasia

**Autores:** Ingeborg Maria Langohr; Dev Kumar Das; Dayananda Siddappa; Nathan Pate; Kuldeep Gautam; Sanjit Jha; Rajesh Ugalmugle; Tijo Thomas; Uttara Joshi; Srinivas Rao; Dinesh S\. Bangari

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Bile duct hyperplasia in rodents, caused by chemical cytotoxicity, bile flow interference, or aging, is traditionally assessed through time\-consuming microscopic examination by pathologists\. We developed a deep learning\-based, artificial intelligence \(AI\) model for quick and accurate assessment of bile duct hyperplasia in mice\. The study utilized 127 hematoxylin and eosin\-stained, mouse liver whole\-slide images \(WSIs\) digitized using Aperio scanners\. A segmentation algorithm with a U\-Net architecture and an EfficientNet backbone was trained on 1165 areas from 35 WSIs to identify and quantify bile ducts, measure their area percentage using a supervised method, and categorize them into grades 0 to 4 using an unsupervised method\. The model was validated on 24 WSIs and tested on 68 WSIs, all independently scored by 3 veterinary pathologists using consensus criteria\. The segmentation model achieved Dice scores of 91\.75% in training and 85\.88% in validation\. Testing revealed high sensitivity \(95%\), specificity \(97%\), and Dice score \(95\.45%\), indicating robust model accuracy\. Model\-predicted grades showed strong correlation \( r 2 s = 0\.86\) with pathologists’ consensus scores\. This AI\-driven approach has the potential to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of bile duct hyperplasia assessments in rodent studies, paving the way for more standardized, objective, and high\-throughput histological evaluations in preclinical research\.
