# Fujie, Naoto: Xenoantiphilosophy\. The Emergence, Persistence, and Withdrawal of Philosophical Operations

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026Xenoantiphilosophy investigates the conditions under which philosophical operations emerge, persist, transform, and lose their independent operational significance\. Rather than presupposing philosophy as a permanent conceptual authority, it examines how philosophical, scientific, phenomenological, and machine\-mediated operations are constituted and reconfigured across heterogeneous material and temporal arrangements\. The central question is not whether philosophy will disappear, but when philosophical mediation ceases to function as a necessary passage for subsequent scientific and material transformations\. Autonomous science, recursive machine operations, heterogeneous speeds, and correction latency can alter the conditions under which philosophical intervention remains effective\. Yet the loss of philosophical priority in one configuration does not entail the extinction of philosophical operations elsewhere\. The work distinguishes the real occurrence of philosophical operations from their physical implementation, conceptual identification, inferential justification, institutional recognition, and external expression\. Philosophical operations may occur without public articulation, including within everyday phenomenological experience, without making consciousness their universal condition\. Drawing critically on Sellars, Brassier, Derrida, Roden, and Negarestani, the book develops the Philosophical Operational Extinction Point alongside the Nuance\-Operational Model, history\-reentry, and Non\-Closure Realism\. It further distinguishes the disappearance of philosophical activity from the persistence of conceptual material shapes, records, and downstream real effects\. Xenoantiphilosophy does not announce the historical end of philosophy\. It examines the local conditions under which philosophy becomes nonredundant, loses its independent role, or re\-emerges, while allowing its own conceptual vocabulary to become unnecessary\. \(direct link \)
