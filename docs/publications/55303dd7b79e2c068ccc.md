## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026Why should heterogeneous real operations be divided into representational and non\-representational processes? This paper investigates the conditions under which such distinctions emerge, become operative, and acquire consequences for material configurations, scientific inference, communication, and intervention\. Drawing on Wilfrid Sellars’s account of picturing, Ray Brassier’s naturalistic reconstruction of representation, and Daniel Sacilotto’s materialist theory of representational cognition, the paper examines the explanatory work performed by representational distinctions without granting them universal ontological authority\. It preserves the achievements of structural mapping, Natural Reason, and scientific theory revision while questioning whether representation and non\-representation constitute a necessary binary division of real operations\. The analysis extends to expressive vocabulary, the localization of human conscious representation, communication failures, address\-domain reconfiguration, effective dimensions, and the material consequences of conceptual classifications\. Representational classifications may influence subsequent scientific operations when implemented in diagnostic, corrective, or intervention procedures, even when their ontological status remains unsettled\. The paper integrates PyTorch experiments and NetworkX–SciPy simulations investigating transformations of computational representations, diagnostic policies, intervention costs, and changing network reachability\. The experiments include connection removal and restoration under matched conditions, alongside controls that modify classification labels without modifying execution rules\. The results show that intervention policies and costs can alter subsequent reachability and cumulative losses, while label\-only changes produce no independent effects in the implemented models\. These findings establish limited computational results rather than an ontological proof of representation or an empirical refutation of Sacilotto’s theory\. In particular, the causal effect of classification when dynamically connected to revision rules remains unresolved\. The philosophical argument and computational evidence are therefore evaluated independently\. The paper concludes that the practical efficacy of representational distinctions does not establish a universal boundary between representational and non\-representational reality\. Their formation, implementation, transformation, and withdrawal must be examined through the specific operations and downstream consequences they produce\.
