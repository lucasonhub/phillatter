# Referral Delay and Admission Hypothermia among Outborn Neonates at PHQ Hospital Gilgit

**Autores:** Amara Akram; Zainab Muhammad Hussain; Umair Riaz; Dr\. Hassan Shah; Shazia Batool; Dr\. Muhammad Hamid

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: To assess whether a longer delay between referral advice and hospital arrival was linked with a lower admission temperature and greater severity of hypothermia in outborn neonates\. Methods: This prospective observational study included 240 referred neonates aged 0–28 days who arrived at PHQ Hospital Gilgit between 1 November 2025 and 28 August 2026\. Referral time, arrival time and the first rectal temperature recorded within two hours of arrival were noted\. WHO temperature categories were used\. The relationship between referral delay and admission temperature was examined using Kruskal–Wallis testing, Spearman correlation and multivariable linear regression, with adjustment for gestational age, admission weight, sex and documented thermal protection\. Results: Median referral delay was 5\.85 hours \(interquartile range 4\.20–8\.20\), and mean admission temperature was 36\.02 ± 0\.61°C\. Hypothermia was recorded in 190 neonates \(79\.2%\): 90 had mild and 100 had moderate hypothermia\. Median delay was 4\.2 hours among normothermic neonates, 5\.5 hours among those with mild hypothermia and 8\.2 hours among those with moderate hypothermia \(p&lt;0\.001\)\. Longer delay correlated with lower temperature \(ρ=−0\.63; p&lt;0\.001\)\. Each additional hour was associated with a 0\.120°C lower temperature after adjustment \(95% confidence interval −0\.137 to −0\.103\)\. Documented thermal protection was associated with a 0\.510°C higher temperature \(95% confidence interval 0\.380–0\.640\)\. Conclusion: Longer referral delays were associated with lower early admission temperatures\. These findings highlight the importance of timely transfer and continuous thermal care, although this observational study cannot prove that delay itself caused the fall in temperature\.
