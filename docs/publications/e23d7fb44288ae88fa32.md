# Automatic insect identification, counting, and monitoring techniques: a review

**Autores:** Mariano González García; Juan Carlos Noguera Polania; Rosana González Combarros; José Ignacio López Sánchez

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: Insect pests threaten agricultural productivity, while conventional surveillance is labour\-intensive and excessive pesticide use creates environmental and health risks\. This review evaluated automated insect detection, counting, classification and monitoring technologies, focusing on machine\-learning integration, field transferability, and operational readiness\. RESULTS: A PRISMA 2020\-guided formal search covering publications from 2010 to 2024 identified 313 records relevant to the technological and methodological scope of the review\. After title/abstract screening and full\-text eligibility assessment, 116 publications were retained\. The evidence encompassed camera\- and vision\-based systems; acoustic, infrared, and optoelectronic sensors; electrostatic and physical approaches; non\-lethal photonic monitoring; radar and biosensing technologies; Internet of Things\-enabled platforms; and machine\- and deep\-learning methods\. Camera\- and vision\-based systems were among the most extensively documented and field\-oriented approaches, particularly when integrated with remote image acquisition, communication networks, and automated classification\. Acoustic systems provided complementary options for continuous or low\-power monitoring, whereas radar, biosensing, and other emerging approaches broadened applications toward biodiversity assessment, large\-area surveillance, and early detection\. Reported performance was not directly comparable across publications because metrics, acquisition conditions, and validation levels differed substantially, ranging from laboratory or dataset benchmarks to semi\-field trials, field validations, and operational deployments\. CONCLUSION: No single technology is optimal for all monitoring objectives\. Operational adoption requires standardized task\-specific reporting, independent and sustained field validation, representative datasets, improved transferability across crops and environments, and system\-level assessment of robustness, energy autonomy, connectivity, maintenance, and cost\. Sensor fusion, edge artificial intelligence, and domain\-adaptive or uncertainty\-aware models are promising when they provide measurable decision\-support benefits\. © 2026 The Author\(s\)\.
