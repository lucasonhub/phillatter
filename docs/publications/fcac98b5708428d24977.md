# Age\-stratified survival and competing mortality in adult acute lymphoblastic leukemia and lymphoblastic lymphoma: a population\-based competing\-risk and restricted mean survival analysis

**Autores:** Zhe Li; Jin Yu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Survival differences by age are well recognized in adult acute lymphoblastic leukemia/lymphoma \(ALL/LBL\), but their timing, competing\-mortality structure, and behavior across recent diagnosis periods remain less clear\. Methods This retrospective SEER cohort included adults diagnosed during 2010\-2023, excluding Burkitt leukemia/lymphoma\. We estimated overall survival \(OS\), 3\-year restricted mean survival time \(RMST\), and cumulative incidence of ALL/LBL\-attributable and other/unknown\-cause death\. Cox, Fine\-Gray, spline, and exploratory time\-split models assessed age associations\. Results Among 9,491 patients, 4,618 died: 3,828 deaths were attributed to ALL/LBL and 790 to other or unknown causes\. Three\-year RMST was 1\.89, 2\.10, and 2\.14 years in 2010\-2014, 2015\-2019, and 2020\-2023, respectively; the recent\-period difference was 0\.044 years \(95% CI \-0\.016 to 0\.104; P = 0\.150\)\. Median follow\-up in 2020–2023 was 22 months\. Compared with age 18–39 years, the adjusted overall\-mortality HR for age &gt;=75 years was 6\.258 \(95% CI 5\.627\-6\.960\)\. Because proportional hazards were violated, this HR should be read as a summary association\. The exploratory HR was 8\.201 \(95% CI 7\.224\-9\.311\) during the first year and 3\.496 \(95% CI 2\.885\-4\.236\) thereafter\. At 3 years, cumulative incidence in patients aged &gt;=75 years was 70\.4% for ALL/LBL\-attributable death by registry attribution and 12\.8% for other/unknown\-cause death\. The spline test supported departure from linearity \(P&lt;0\.001\); the age\-by\-period interaction was not significant \(P = 0\.182\), whereas an exploratory age\-by\-subtype interaction was nominally significant \(P = 0\.024, unadjusted for multiplicity\)\. Conclusion Older age was associated with higher mortality, especially in the first year after diagnosis\. ALL/LBL\-attributable death predominated by registry attribution even in the oldest group\. The 3\-year comparison did not establish a survival difference between 2015–2019 and 2020\-2023; limited follow\-up and residual case\-mix differences preclude concluding that progress has stopped\.
