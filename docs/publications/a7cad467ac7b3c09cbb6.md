# Fifteen\-year real\-world pharmacovigilance of multiple sclerosis disease\-modifying therapies: adverse event profiles, drug\-specific safety signals and clinical implications

**Autores:** Sen Lin; Bei Zhang; Yingzhao Zhang; Xinyu Zhang; YiJia Li; Weiqing Zhang; Li He

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Disease\-modifying therapies \(DMTs\) are the cornerstone of remission\-phase management for multiple sclerosis \(MS\), a prevalent autoimmune neuroinflammatory disorder of the central nervous system\. However, their immune\-modulating mechanisms carry inherent risks of systemic and neurological adverse events \(ADEs\), which remain a major barrier to optimal long\-term care\. This study aimed to comprehensively characterize the real\-world safety profiles of all major MS DMT classes and identify clinically actionable safety signals\. Methods We analyzed 15\-year \(2009Q1–2023Q4\) ADE reports for 8 classes of FDA\-approved MS DMTs from the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System \(FAERS\) database\. Four disproportionality algorithms \(ROR, PRR, BCPNN, MGPS\) were used for signal detection\. Time\-to\-onset \(TTO\) analysis with Weibull distribution and multivariate logistic regression were performed to assess temporal risk patterns and patient\-specific risk factors for important medical events \(IMEs\)\. Results A total of 498,847 ADE reports \(1,048,575 events\) were included, with nearly 50% involving IMEs\. The most common ADEs were systemic symptoms, infections, liver injury and alopecia\. Notably, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy \(PML, ROR = 15\.59\) and spontaneous abortion \(ROR = 2\.54\) were identified as high\-risk severe events\. Drug\-specific analysis revealed distinct safety signatures: anti\-CD20 antibodies carried the highest infection risk, natalizumab was strongly associated with PML \(ROR = 45\.62\), and teriflunomide had the highest alopecia risk \(ROR = 12\.73\)\. Approximately 70% of IMEs occurred within 2 years of treatment initiation\. Multivariate analysis identified older age, male sex and longer treatment duration as independent risk factors for IMEs, while obesity was a protective factor\. Conclusions This long\-term FAERS\-based analysis identified potential safety signals associated with major MS DMTs, providing hypothesis\-generating evidence to support clinical vigilance and prioritize further validation, but not to estimate incidence, comparative risk, or causality\.
