# Cott, Nicholas: Empty Occupancy: Answerability and the Ownership of Machine\-Mediated Institutional Commitments

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Institutions increasingly use automated systems to advise, explain, refuse, classify, and decide\. Fluent output does not settle who bears the commitments presented through these roles\. This paper develops a substrate\-neutral account of Answerability through four public relations: attribution continuity, challenge\-responsive reconsideration, repair competence, and standing\-consequence\. It motivates their necessity for institutional discursive occupancy while distinguishing the existence of standing from its successful exercise\. An institution may own an unjustified commitment or obstruct a challenge without thereby ceasing to be responsible\. Empty Occupancy is consequently a locus\-specific diagnosis, not a general inference from automation or failed review\. The paper separates the epistemic value of machine output from commitment ownership and proposes Normative Sponsorship as a standard for warranted institutional adoption\. Sponsorship requires contestable grounds, adequate authority and review capacity, independent rejection, and effective repair; its failure does not cancel existing attribution or liability\. A fully stipulated insurer–interface case yields a determinate paired verdict: the purported independent AI appeals agent is an Empty Occupant, while the insurer remains the institutional commitment bearer\. Separate, bounded illustrations from Moffatt v\. Air Canada and the Robodebt Royal Commission remain evidentially indeterminate on stronger local claims; they do not validate diagnostic markers or establish prevalence\. Institutional Stewardship names the obligation to preserve a binding route of challenge and repair\. The four\-relation sufficiency proposal applies to a role specified independently of those relations and remains open to counterexample\. \(direct link \)
