# The associations between healthful and unhealthful plant\-based dietary patterns and all\-cause mortality among older Chinese adults with data\-adapted early\-stage cardiovascular\-kidney\-metabolic syndrome \(stages 0–2\): a prospective cohort study from the CLHLS

**Autores:** Shipeng Zhang; Enjie Tang; Yanjie Jiang; Ke Zhu; Xiulian Zhuang; Hanyu Wang; Xingyi He; Yan Lu; Qinxiu Zhang; Xueying Li; Jiaqing Song

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Aims To examine the associations of the overall plant\-based diet index \(PDI\), healthful plant\-based diet index \(hPDI\), and unhealthful plant\-based diet index \(uPDI\) with all\-cause mortality in older Chinese adults with data\-adapted early\-stage cardiovascular\-kidney\-metabolic \(CKM\) syndrome \(stages 0–2\)\. Methods and results We analyzed data from 5,872 participants aged ≥65 years from the 2008 wave of the China Longitudinal Healthy Longevity Survey \(CLHLS\) with data\-adapted CKM stages 0–2 and no prevalent cardiovascular disease\. Dietary intake was assessed using a validated food frequency questionnaire\. Mortality was ascertained through March 2019\. Cox proportional hazards models and restricted cubic spline analyses were used\. During a median follow\-up of 108 months, 2,870 deaths occurred\. In multivariable\-adjusted models, each 1\-point increase in hPDI was associated with a 1\.1% lower mortality risk \(HR = 0\.989, 95% CI: 0\.982–0\.995; p &lt; 0\.001\), whereas each 1\-point increase in uPDI was associated with a 1\.2% higher mortality risk \(HR = 1\.012, 95% CI: 1\.006–1\.019; p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Compared with the lowest quartile, the highest hPDI quartile had a 13\.2% lower mortality risk \(HR = 0\.868, 95% CI: 0\.783–0\.961\), and the highest uPDI quartile had a 19\.2% higher risk \(HR = 1\.192, 95% CI: 1\.060–1\.340\)\. Stratified estimates suggested numerically stronger associations in CKM stages 1–2 than in stage 0; however, formal interaction tests did not reach statistical significance \( p = 0\.062 for hPDI and p = 0\.824 for uPDI\), indicating limited evidence of effect modification\. Conclusion Higher adherence to a healthful plant\-based diet was associated with lower all\-cause mortality, whereas higher adherence to an unhealthful plant\-based diet was associated with higher mortality among older Chinese adults with data\-adapted early\-stage CKM syndrome\. The overall PDI was not independently associated with mortality after adjustment, underscoring that plant\-based dietary quality, rather than plant\-based eating per se, is the relevant exposure\.
