# Algorithmic Guidance and Investor Learning: An Empirical Examination of Robo\-Advisors

**Autores:** Dasari Sharon; I\. Mohana Krishna

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Growth in robo\-advisors has broadened retail access to investor\-centric digital financial advice; however, it remains uncertain whether using them makes investors smart or feels smarter\. This study investigates relationships between robo\-adoption, digital financial literacy, financial expertise, and investment results among young and late\-career retail investors in Vijayawada, Andhra Pradesh\. Data were gathered with a structured, self\-administered Likert\-scale questionnaire; 94 effectively responded \(out of 123 distributed\)\. Chi\-square tests, one\-way ANOVA, and linear regressions were computed\. Robo\-adoption was significantly related to gender \(1\) = 4\.79, p =\.029\. Adoption, digital literacy, expertise, and outcomes were significantly different across age, gender, education, income, experience, and occupational groups\. Simple regressions indicated all four hypotheses are supported: adoption predicted digital literacy \( =\.30, p =\.003\) and expertise \( =\.32, p =\.001\), digital literacy predicted expertise \( =\.26, p =\.012\), and expertise predicted perceived investment success \( =\.65, p &lt;\.001, R =\.43\)\. When adopting and learning were tested jointly, only adoption was significant\. Sub\-dimension analyses indicate that the effect of adoption resides mainly in diversification awareness and information\-based decision\-making, while the self\-perceived risktolerance and rate\-of\-return knowledge was slightly lower among more frequent users\. Reliability of the scales for expertise and performance outcomes was weak, and data set contains duplicate respondents, so results should be considered indicative\.
