# The unbearable \(and unsustainable\) social impact of AI\-generated “cultural monism” and the EU digital legislation

**Autores:** Francesca Rotolo

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The increasing use of generative AI \(genAI\) across cultural and \(re\)creative sectors is indisputable in contemporary times\. Within the EU, these systems are classified as “general\-purpose AI” models \(Recital 99 of the Artificial Intelligence Act – AI Act\) and are expected to deliver a wide range of economic and societal benefits, as affirmed in Recital 105 of the Act\. Yet, significant concerns have emerged regarding their potential impact on individuals, civil society, and democracy\. In the cultural domain, genAI technologies are widely employed to generate outputs often perceived as original\. However, scholarly debate has increasingly highlighted the risk that their widespread diffusion may lead to a form of cultural impoverishment – understood as the depletion of cultural pluralism and diversity of artistic expression resulting from the pervasive use of these systems and their underlying architectures, as well as, ultimately, an increasing standardization of the outputs of the algorithmic creative process\. These systematic risks are not merely social or political but also carry significant legal implications\. The potential effects of genAI on freedom of \(artistic\) expression and cultural pluralism may, in fact, infringe upon fundamental rights enshrined in international human rights charters\. Yet, the role of legal scholarship in addressing such risks remains uncertain\. A promising conceptual instrument for tackling this issue is the notion of systemic risk, recently introduced in two key pieces of EU digital legislation: the Digital Services Act \(DSA\) and the mentioned AI Act\. This article examines the advantages and limitations of employing this concept to address the risk of cultural impoverishment\. The analysis focuses primarily on the EU legal framework, with particular attention to the AI Act, while adopting a functional and comparative perspective, given that the principal challenges stem from US\-based AI systems\. It also situates the inquiry within the broader dynamics of the data economy, specifically the logic of knowledge capitalism\. The article begins by exploring the potential impacts of genAI on cultural pluralism and artistic expression, assessing whether these may with time culminate in cultural impoverishment – or even a transition toward cultural monism, marked by the predominance, in all the cultural productions, of a single mainstream culture\. It then analyses whether EU digital regulation is equipped to subsume such phenomena within the legal category of systemic risk\.
