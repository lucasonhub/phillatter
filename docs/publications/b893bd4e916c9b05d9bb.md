# Niami, Morteza: Recursive Differentiation and Finite Continuance Identity, Grounding, Modality, Causation, and Emergence in a Process Metaphysic

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

A persistent difficulty in contemporary metaphysics concerns the relation among persistence, modal possibility, metaphysical dependence, causation, and emergence\. Substantialist theories commonly explain persistence through numerical identity across temporal stages, while process theories emphasize transformation without always providing a sufficiently determinate account of what makes a continuing process the same process through change\. Modal theories face a related problem\. Possibility is frequently characterized through accessibility among worlds or through dispositional structure without an adequate account of how the space of possibilities is itself generated and altered by concrete processes\. This article develops a process\-metaphysical framework in which finite continuance is understood as the preservation and transformation of reconfigurative capacity within an erosive field\. Its central thesis is that persistence, modality, grounding, causation, and emergence can be understood as formally distinct expressions of an underlying structure of organized dependence\. The article engages directly with Lewisian accounts of persistence and causation, Sider&\#x27;s structuralist treatment of metaphysical structure, Vetter&\#x27;s dispositional account of modality, Fine&\#x27;s distinction between essence and grounding, and the Davidsonian and Kimian analyses of events and causal efficacy\. The resulting position treats identity as organized diachronic continuity, modality as structured reconfigurability, grounding as dependence among organizational conditions, causation as temporally asymmetric production within an evolving configuration, and emergence as the formation of higher\-order constraints upon the space of possible subsequent states\. Finite continuance thereby provides a unified metaphysical vocabulary for explaining how something can remain determinate through transformation while retaining a bounded capacity to become otherwise\. Keywords: finite continuance; process metaphysics; persistence; identity; grounding; modality; causation; emergence; reconfiguration; erosion; metaphysical dependence; event individuation \(direct link \)
