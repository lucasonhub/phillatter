# Student outcomes in a student\-led clinic: beyond clinical learning

**Autores:** P\. Broman; S\. Vaughan; P\. Andersen; M\. J\. Cannings; Marrin Haggie; K\. O’Connor; Kesava Kovanur Sampath; S\. Tan; O\. Webster; J\.\-R\. Yap; Sharon Mary Brownie

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Student\-assisted, \-run or \-led health clinics \(SLCs\) are increasingly common worldwide, but student outcomes remain under\-evaluated, with existing work focused narrowly on clinical and/or academic outcomes\. Methods This study presents the findings of an evaluation of the impact of a student\-assisted clinic in New Zealand, on participating students subjective wellbeing, assessed using the Satisfaction with Life Scale \(SWLS\), as well as on a range of programme\-related intermediate outcomes theorised to support wellbeing, including leadership, communication, and clinical capability\. Students undertaking placements at the clinic were surveyed at three time points: at the start, the end, and at six months post\-placement\. Results Participating students maintained levels of self\-reported wellbeing over placement periods while significantly improving leadership, purpose, cultural connection, and clinical capability\. Income and sleep were shown to be key factors related to SWLS scores\. Discussion Student\-related outcomes from SLCs may extend beyond clinical care and academic performance, highlighting the value of a more holistic approach to SLC evaluation\.
