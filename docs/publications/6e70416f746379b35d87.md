# Information adequacy and household transition to low\-carbon energy solutions: a qualitative case study from Uganda

**Autores:** Florence Nakajubi; Joseph Mpeera Ntayi; Hassan Bashir; Livingstone Senyonga; Muyiwa  Samuel Adaramola; Ronnette Atukunda

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Household dependence on traditional biomass remains high in Uganda despite growing policy efforts to promote cleaner household energy alternatives\. This study examines how information adequacy shapes household transitions to low\-carbon energy solutions in Uganda\. Methods A qualitative case\-study design was adopted, involving semi\-structured interviews with 20 participants comprising 15 household heads and 5 policymakers\. Data were analysed using the Gioia methodology\. Results The analysis generated seven first\-order concepts, organised into two second\-order themes, information quantity and information quality, and one aggregate dimension of information adequacy\. Three first\-order concepts captured information quantity, while four captured information quality\. Information quantity broadened households’ awareness and practical knowledge of low\-carbon energy alternatives by providing sufficient information on available technologies, costs, suppliers and use\. Information quality enhanced households’ ability to evaluate these alternatives when information was clear, credible, accessible and contextually relevant, particularly when communicated through local language, practical demonstrations, visual materials and relatable peer experiences\. Discussion The findings further show that information quantity primarily supports the knowledge stage of Diffusion of Innovations theory, whereas information quality strengthens the persuasion stage by enabling households to assess the relevance and feasibility of alternative energy technologies\. The study concludes that household energy\-transition interventions should move beyond increasing information exposure to providing sufficient, understandable and locally relevant information alongside broader measures addressing economic, technological and infrastructural constraints\.
