# Liquid Chromatography/tandem Mass Spectrometry\-based Target Engagement of Lysine\-specific Demethylase 1 Inhibitor TAK\-418 Through Detecting Covalent Adducts in the Brain

**Autores:** Kouta Matsumiya; Shinji Iwasaki; Satoru Matsuda; Yohei Kosugi; Hisao Shimizu; Masaaki Kakehi; Rina Baba; Nobuyuki Amano; Atsushi Nakatani; Haruhide Kimura; Hideki Hirabayashi

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Translating pharmacokinetics \(PK\) and pharmacodynamics \(PD\) from preclinical to clinical is important in successful development of drugs for central nervous system \(CNS\) diseases\. TAK\-418 is a clinical candidate of lysine\-specific demethylase 1 \(LSD1\) inhibitor with a unique mechanism of action; it covalently binds to flavin adenine dinucleotide \(FAD\) to form a compact adduct formylated FAD \(F\-FAD\), thereby minimizing hematological side effect risks\. In this study, we established a liquid chromatography\-tandem mass spectrometry \(LC/MS/MS\)\-based approach for evaluating F\-FAD as a target engagement marker for TAK\-418 to advance PK/PD translation and clinical dose setting\. In rats, LC/MS/MS enabled detection of TAK\-418\-induced F\-FAD formation in the brain with chromatographic separation from native FAD\. F\-FAD levels were positively associated with LSD1 protein abundance across brain regions and increased with increasing TAK\-418 dose\. LSD1 occupancy calculated from F\-FAD concentrations was correlated with the inhibition level of LSD1 enzyme activity in rat hippocampus\. This mechanism\-based approach provides a link between covalent adduct formation, target engagement, and enzyme inhibition in the brain\. In addition, similar point estimates of the half\-maximal effective concentration \(EC 50 \)​ for F\-FAD formation in rat and human hippocampal homogenates support the potential utility of F\-FAD measurements for translational assessment of LSD1 target engagement\. These findings provide useful information for evaluating the translational relationship between TAK\-418 exposure and LSD1 target engagement and may support future PK/PD modeling and clinical dose selection for TAK\-418\. Moreover, our methodology for measuring covalent adducts would be applicable for multiple CNS drug candidates with covalent binding property\.
