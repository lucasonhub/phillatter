# Longitudinal relationships between physical exercise, psychological resilience, and academic engagement in Chinese university students: a random\-intercept cross\-lagged panel study

**Autores:** Yuankai Qiao; Haibin Liu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The relationships among physical exercise, psychological resilience, and academic engagement have predominantly been examined using cross\-sectional designs, which preclude inference about directionality\. This study examined their longitudinal relationships in a two\-wave panel of Chinese university students\. Of 1,024 students who provided valid baseline data \(October 2024\), 876 \(Mage = 20\.34, SD = 1\.67; 58\.3% female\) provided valid follow\-up data 6 months later \(April 2025\) on exercise volume \(PARS\-3\), psychological resilience \(CD\-RISC\-10\), and academic engagement \(UWES\-S\)\. Cross\-lagged panel models \(CLPM\) and random\-intercept cross\-lagged panel models \(RI\-CLPM\) were estimated in Mplus 8\.8, with longitudinal measurement invariance established for all instruments\. In the CLPM, exercise volume at T1 predicted resilience at T2 \(beta = 0\.17, p &lt; 0\.001\) and resilience predicted engagement at T2 \(beta = 0\.14, p = 0\.003\), whereas the reverse paths and the direct exercise\-to\-engagement path were not significant\. In the RI\-CLPM, which separates between\-person from within\-person variance, the within\-person association of exercise volume with subsequent resilience remained significant \(beta = 0\.11, p = 0\.009\) and was unaffected by assumptions about the reliability of the single\-indicator exercise measure, whereas the within\-person resilience\-to\-engagement path was attenuated \(beta = 0\.07, p = 0\.074\)\. Neither gender nor age moderated the relationships\. Exercise volume is thus prospectively associated with subsequent resilience at the within\-person level, whereas the resilience\-engagement link appears to operate primarily between persons\. Because the two\-wave design and the single\-indicator exercise measure require identifying constraints, the within\-person decomposition is model\-implied, and these findings are best interpreted as temporally ordered associations rather than confirmed within\-person causal effects; exercise promotion stands as a candidate strategy for resilience support in university populations, pending experimental confirmation\.
