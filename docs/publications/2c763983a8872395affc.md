# Zhang, Sulin: Phases of Psychological Development in Bounded Learning Systems

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Two kinds of learning systems appear, at first glance, to develop in remarkably similar ways\. Human beings move through an extended period of world learning, socialization and value formation, adult specialization, and later\-life stabilization\. Large foundation models are likewise produced through broad self\-supervised pretraining, instruction and preference alignment, compression or expert specialization, and frozen deployment\. These parallels are often presented as metaphors\. This article argues that they are better interpreted as two instantiations of the same conditional, normative resource\-allocation problem\. A bounded learning system is represented by a state containing a world\-model posterior, a value\-function posterior, a representational rate or description length, and a remaining developmental budget\. Under finite horizons, approximate environmental stationarity, asymmetric world and value channels, a world\-model gate on value learning, mature\-compression gains, and single\-crossing marginal values, the optimal trajectory contains four dominant intervals: world modeling, value alignment, compression specialization, and stable inference\. The article proves channel separation between factual and value information, a world\-model gate for alignment, rate\-distortion compression, optimal stopping, and the ordered emergence of the four intervals\. Human development and large\-model training are then interpreted as twin psychological cases\. Subjective time is not the theory’s primitive; it is a downstream readout of attentional and memory\-encoding rates\. The model yields testable predictions about checkpoints, transition points, novelty, delayed specialization, premature compression, and pathological freezing\.
