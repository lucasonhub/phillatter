# Prognostic Utility and Exploratory Cut\-off Threshold of the First 24\-Hour APACHE II Score for In\-Hospital Mortality After Thoracotomy: A Single\-Center Retrospective Cohort Study

**Autores:** Awan Rochaniawan; Ahmat Umar; Erial Bahar

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Open thoracotomy imposes severe physiological stress, so objective early risk stratification is needed at General Intensive Care Unit \(GICU\) admission\. Objective: To evaluate how well the first 24\-hour Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II \(APACHE II\) score predicts in\-hospital mortality after thoracotomy, to set an exploratory cut\-off, and to examine differences by surgical indication and organ component\. Methods: This retrospective cohort study analyzed 33 consecutive adults admitted directly to the GICU of Dr\. Mohammad Hoesin General Hospital, Palembang, Indonesia, after open thoracotomy \(January–December 2024\)\. Analyses included Mann\-Whitney U, Spearman&\#x27;s ρ, logistic regression, and Receiver Operating Characteristic \(ROC\) analysis with Youden&\#x27;s J index\. Results: In\-hospital mortality was 27\.3% \(9/33\)\. Non\-survivors had higher median APACHE II scores than survivors \(23\.0 \[IQR: 19\.0–27\.0\] vs\. 8\.0 \[IQR: 6\.75–12\.0\]; U = 9\.5, p &lt; 0\.001\)\. The score correlated with mortality \(ρ = 0\.706, p &lt; 0\.001\) and remained significant on multivariable regression \(aOR = 1\.74; 95% CI: 1\.13–2\.69; p = 0\.0127\)\. The AUC was 0\.956 \(95% CI: 0\.891–1\.000\)\. A cut\-off of ≥12\.5 gave 88\.9% sensitivity, 87\.5% specificity, 72\.7% positive predictive value, and 95\.5% negative predictive value\. Mortality was 32\.0% in malignancy and 12\.5% in non\-malignancy cases \(p = 0\.394\)\. Glasgow Coma Scale \(6\.33 vs\. 1\.83 points\), serum creatinine \(3\.44 vs\. 0\.96\), and arterial pH \(1\.89 vs\. 0\.33\) components differed between non\-survivors and survivors \(all p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Conclusion: The initial 24\-hour APACHE II score is a hypothesis\-generating predictor of post\-thoracotomy mortality at a candidate threshold of ≥12\.5\.
