# Data\-driven multi\-criteria evaluation of intelligent cockpit configuration strategies in Chinese battery electric vehicles

**Autores:** Weixin Zhu; Xue Xiong

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction In this study, we examine how documented cockpit equipment differentiates selected Chinese\-brand battery electric vehicles \(BEVs\)\. We compare configuration richness with and without a price criterion and identify recurring equipment arrangements, rather than using specifications as a proxy for tested cockpit performance\. Methods The dataset comprises 36 purposively selected model–trim combinations from 13 brands\. Two CRITIC–TOPSIS models score four configuration criteria, with guide price included as a cost in the second model\. Gower dissimilarity and partitioning around medoids \(PAM\) describe eight mixed\-type attributes\. Sensitivity checks vary weights, aggregation, criteria, coding, and case composition\. Each model also uses 5,000 vehicle resamples and 5,000 brand\-block resamples to assess conditional weighting uncertainty\. Results The head‐up display \(HUD\) level receives the largest weight in the price‐free model \(0\.3557\)\. Baseline rankings correlate at ρ = 0\.964\. Five clusters yield a mean silhouette width of 0\.519, only slightly above 0\.514 for six clusters\. Removing HUD reduces ranking correlations to 0\.678 and 0\.750\. The leading price\-adjusted scores for ZEEKR 001 and XPeng P7\+ differ by 0\.000285; their first\-place frequencies under brand\-block resampling are 50\.30% and 49\.06%\. Discussion The comparison distinguishes equipment patterns within the recorded sample, but its resolution depends on the criteria and their representation\. Broad ranking agreement does not guarantee a stable first\-place choice\. The clusters describe configurations, not market shares, and neither index establishes superior usability, safety, or overall cockpit capability\.
