# Enhancing ω\-transaminase catalysis of bulky substrates guided by binding mechanism

**Autores:** Haidong Teng; Haoran Yu; Zhehang Xu; Lanxin Xiao; Xinghao Yuan; Lirong Yang; Jianmin Wu; Gang Xu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Chiral amines are indispensable building blocks in pharmaceutical synthesis\. \(R\)\-3\-\(\[1,1′\-biphenyl\]\-4\-yl\)\-2\-aminopropan\-1\-ol \(ABP\), a key chiral amine intermediate of the drug Entresto, has remained difficult to access via enzymatic asymmetric synthesis due to the inability of ω\-transaminases to accommodate its bulky ketone precursor\. Here, we engineer an ω\-transaminase from Bacillus megaterium by integrating Funnel metadynamics, PathDetect\-SOM, and path collective variables to reconstruct the free energy surface of substrate binding, pinpointing high\-energy barrier regions of the binding process and the trade\-off between tunnel accessibility and productive binding\. Dynamic cross\-correlation network analysis further identifies distal regulatory sites governing these bottleneck regions\. Iterative experimental optimization yields the 13M variant, exhibiting catalytic activity 106 times that of the wild type with &gt;99% enantiomeric excess, enabling the efficient enzymatic asymmetric synthesis of ABP\. This integrated computational\-experimental framework for resolving the tunnel\-pocket binding trade\-off offers a generalizable strategy for engineering ω\-transaminases toward bulky substrates in green pharmaceutical synthesis\. This study combines enhanced sampling with experiments to reveal how bulky substrates enter and bind to ω\-transaminases\. Distal\-site engineering balanced tunnel access and binding stability, yielding 106\-fold activity and &gt;99% selectivity\.
