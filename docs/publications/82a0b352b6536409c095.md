# From resource orchestration to robustness and resilience: the role of data analytics capability in bulk commodity maritime supply chains

**Autores:** 李漱岩; Jinge Yao; Xina Ji; Mingjie Fang; Qiwei Pang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Bulk commodity maritime supply chains are increasingly exposed to climate\-related disruptions, geopolitical tensions, port congestion, transportation uncertainty, and other external shocks\. However, limited attention has been paid to how firms transform dispersed supply chain resources into capabilities that support both disruption resistance and recovery\. Drawing on resource orchestration theory and organizational information processing theory, this study examines how maritime supply chain resource acquisition, resource bundling, and resource leveraging are associated with data analytics capability, supply chain robustness, and supply chain resilience\. Methods Survey data were collected from 214 Chinese firms substantially involved in the international seaborne import of bulk commodities\. A two\-wave survey design was employed, and the proposed relationships were tested using structural equation modeling\. Results Resource acquisition was positively associated with resource bundling, which in turn was positively associated with resource leveraging\. Resource acquisition, bundling, and leveraging were all positively associated with data analytics capability, with resource acquisition showing the strongest association\. Data analytics capability was positively associated with both maritime supply chain robustness and resilience\. Bootstrap analysis further identified significant indirect relationships between resource orchestration activities and disruption\-response capabilities through interconnected resource orchestration processes and data analytics capability\. Discussion The findings extend resource orchestration research by showing how data analytics capability is associated with the orchestration of heterogeneous and externally dispersed maritime supply chain resources\. By integrating resource orchestration theory with organizational information processing theory, the study clarifies the proposed process linking resource orchestration, analytics capability, and disruption response capabilities\. The findings also provide practical guidance for bulk commodity importers seeking to strengthen robustness and resilience through coordinated resource management and data\-driven decision making\.
