# Clinical, Patient Experience and Service Outcomes of a Leisure Centre–Based NHS Musculoskeletal Physiotherapy Service: A Service Evaluation

**Autores:** George McNamee; Victoria Roberts; Christopher Newton; Eilis O&\#x27;Donnell; Chris Panter; Gladness Manecka; Simon Blay; Gurpreet Singh

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: Musculoskeletal \(MSK\) conditions place substantial burden on individuals and healthcare systems, increasing the demand for accessible, community\-based care\. This service evaluation assessed an NHS MSK physiotherapy pilot delivered in a leisure centre in a socioeconomically deprived area\. METHODS: A retrospective evaluation of routinely collected data was undertaken for patients attending between August 2025 and January 2026\. Adults referred for physiotherapy for MSK conditions received treatment in a community leisure centre\. Outcomes at initial and final appointments included MSK health \(MSK\-HQ\), pain intensity \(NPRS\), physical activity \(MSK\-HQ physical activity item\), and health\-related quality of life \(EQ\-VAS\)\. Linear mixed\-effects models evaluated clinical outcomes\. Patient experience and service utilisation data were analysed descriptively\. RESULTS: Sixty\-four patients \(mean age 50\.6 ± 14\.8 years; 60\.9% female\) were included, predominantly from socioeconomically deprived areas \(mean Index of Multiple Deprivation 2\.4 ± 1\.5\)\. 53\.2% were from ethnic minority backgrounds and 13 languages were represented\. Significant improvements were observed across all outcomes: MSK\-HQ \(\+11\.2\), NPRS \(\-2\.2\), EQ\-VAS \(\+19\.9\), and physical activity \(\+1\.8 days/week\) \(all p ≤ 0\.002\)\. Sensitivity analyses yielded similar results except for physical activity\. Minimal clinically important thresholds were exceeded for MSK\-HQ, NPRS and EQ\-VAS\. Patient satisfaction was high, attendance was 72\.2%, and 17\.2% of patients were referred to community exercise programmes\. CONCLUSIONS: A leisure centre\-based NHS MSK physiotherapy model was associated with improved clinical outcomes, high patient acceptability and promising service utility\. The service engages a heterogeneous population and aligns with NHS priorities for community\-based care\. Further comparative and economic evaluations are warranted to establish effectiveness and inform future service development\.
