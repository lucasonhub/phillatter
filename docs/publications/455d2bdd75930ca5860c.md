# General Connectivity and the Connectivity Capacity Principle \- Carbon, Silicon, Consciousness, and the Emergence of Complexity

**Autores:** Bhopinderpal \(Pal\) Sahota

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper introduces the General Connectivity \(GC\) framework and proposes Connectivity Capacity \(CC\) as a candidate organizing quantity for understanding the emergence of complexity throughout nature\. The work explores the unique role of carbon as a potential connectivity maximizer whose exceptional bonding versatility enables the formation of highly complex information\-bearing structures, including DNA, proteins, neural networks, and biological intelligence\. Carbon is compared with silicon, the dominant substrate of modern computation and artificial intelligence, to investigate whether both may represent successive stages in an ongoing process of increasing connectivity\. A mathematical framework for Connectivity Capacity is developed, incorporating concepts from network theory, information theory, complexity science, and systems evolution\. The paper introduces Connectivity Density, Connectivity Thresholds, and a General Connectivity Principle describing the tendency of complex systems to evolve toward higher levels of connectivity\. The General Connectivity framework further proposes that consciousness constitutes a primary ontological dimension from which connectivity, information, structure, matter, life, and intelligence emerge\. While this aspect remains speculative, the paper explicitly distinguishes established scientific knowledge from novel GC hypotheses and provides falsification criteria intended to support future empirical investigation\. The manuscript aims to stimulate interdisciplinary discussion across physics, cosmology, complexity science, information theory, neuroscience, artificial intelligence, and philosophy of mind by presenting connectivity as a potential unifying principle underlying the emergence of organized complexity in the universe
