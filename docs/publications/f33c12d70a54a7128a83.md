# miRNA\-543 attenuates placental angiogenesis in female fetal growth restriction by targeting ANGPT2 and inhibiting the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway

## Resumo

Background Insufficient placental angiogenesis is a predominant pathological cause of fetal growth restriction \(FGR\)\. Placental capillary development is regulated in a sex\-dependent manner, with female placentas exhibiting greater susceptibility to angiogenic dysfunction than male placentas\. However, the sex\-specific interplay between microRNAs \(miRNAs\) and human placental angiogenesis in FGR pathogenesis remains poorly defined\. Emerging transcriptomic sequencing evidence has implicated miR\-543 as a pivotal regulator of impaired angiogenesis in female FGR placentas\. This study aimed to elucidate the sex\-dependent mechanistic role of miR\-543 in modulating placental angiogenesis in a cell\-specific manner\. Method FGR pregnancies were recruited, and placental tissues were collected after delivery\. The dual\-luciferase reporter assay was used to detect the direct binding site of miR\-543 to ANGPT2\. Next, immunohistochemistry \(IHC\) and immunofluorescence \(IF\) staining were used to detect the expression and localization of ANGPT2\. To verify the cell\-specific gene expression of miR\-543 and ANGPT2, laser capture microdissection \(LCM\) was employed to isolate endothelial cells from human placentas\. The gene expression levels of miR\-543 and ANGPT2 were quantified by qRT\-PCR\. CCK8, wound healing, Transwell, and tube formation assays and flow cytometry were performed to assess the functional effects of miR\-543 and ANGPT2 on the biological function of human umbilical vein endothelial cells \(HUVECs\)\. The PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway was predicted and verified by Western blotting\. Results Significantly downregulated miR\-543 expression was observed in the placentas of female FGR fetuses relative to matched normal controls\. According to target gene prediction and the dual\-luciferase reporter assay, ANGTP2 was identified as a target candidate of miR\-543\. Moreover, immunofluorescence staining and LCM analysis verified the cell\-specific distribution of ANGPT2 in female FGR placentas, partly indicating its key involvement in placental capillary angiogenesis\. Furthermore, dysregulated expression of miR\-543 and ANGPT2 was closely correlated with aberrant proliferation, migration, and apoptosis of HUVECs\. KEGG analysis revealed that the candidate targets of miR\-543 were mainly enriched in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway\. Western blot analysis demonstrated that the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway mediated the ANGPT2\-induced attenuation of placental angiogenesis in female FGR\. Conclusion miR\-543 negatively regulated ANGPT2 expression, leading to impaired endothelial cell function via inactivation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway\. This, in turn, contributed to sex\-specific inhibition of capillary angiogenesis in the placenta of female fetuses\. These findings reveal a novel miRNA\-mediated, sex\-dependent regulatory mechanism underlying the pathogenesis of impaired placental angiogenesis\.
