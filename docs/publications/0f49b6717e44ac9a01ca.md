# Transformer\-based Digital Biomarker Identification for Early Neurological Assessment

**Autores:** Zahraa Naser Shahweli; Areej Hameed Al\-Anbary; Ahmed Saeed Obied

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The objective assessment and differential diagnosis of movement disorders, particularly Parkinson&\#x27;s disease \(PD\) and Essential Tremor \(ET\), remain challenging due to the subjectivity and limited temporal coverage of conventional in\-clinic clinical assessments\.Although consumer\-grade smartphones equipped with inertial sensors provide a practical platform for continuous movement monitoring, existing deep\-learning approaches remain constrained by their dependence on large, expertly annotated clinical datasets and their limited ability to learn generalized temporal representations\.To address these challenges, this study proposes a self\-supervised, patch\-based Transformer framework that identifies clinically consistent candidate digital features from smartphone inertial timeseries data\.The proposed framework employs a two\-stage training strategy in which the Transformer encoder is first pre\-trained using masked autoencoding on millions of unlabeled inertial sensor samples from the crowdsourced mPower dataset to learn robust latent representations of human movement\.The pre\-trained encoder is subsequently fine\-tuned using a subject\-wise protocol on the Mobile Device Tremor Dataset for multi\-class tremor classification\.Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves a multi\-class classification accuracy of 95\.85% and a macro F1\-score of 95\.52%, consistently outperforming fully supervised 1D Temporal Convolutional \(1D\-TCN\) and BiLSTM\-Attention baselines\.Instead of relying on handcrafted features, the proposed framework identified discriminative movement patterns that aligned with established neurophysiological tremor frequency ranges \(4\-6 Hz and 8\-12 Hz\) in an automated, end\-to\-end manner, bridging self\-supervised deep representations with physical diagnostic criteria\.These findings demonstrate the potential of self\-supervised Transformer models to enable scalable, objective, and clinically interpretable decision support for early neurological assessment in mobile sensor environments\.
