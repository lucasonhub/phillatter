# Operation Dark Clark: AI‑Enabled Russian Disinformation in Latin America and the Campaign to Shape Brazilian Politics \(2020–2026\)

**Autores:** Zen Revista

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This thesis reconstructs and critically evaluates Operation Dark Clark, a Russia\-origin influence operation that OpenAI reportedly disrupted and publicly documented on 8 October 2026\. It assesses the operation as the first influence campaign ever classified by the company in Category 5 of the IO Breakout Scale—its highest rating for a disrupted operation—and the most complex false\-front identity it had dismantled in two and a half years of systematic reporting\. The operation combined generative artificial intelligence with classical intelligence tradecraft\. It recruited unwitting employees in Latin America to staff a seemingly legitimate organization called the “Social Research Center” \(SRC\), which was controlled through a fabricated persona named “Mia Clark\.” Her published corpus comprised more than sixty original research articles examining public opinion on BRICS and comparing the Brazilian economy under Presidents Jair Bolsonaro and Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva\. Drawing on an open\-source intelligence \(OSINT\) verification protocol that distinguishes documented and verified facts from attributed claims and editorial speculation, the thesis traces the operation’s activities across Argentina, Bolivia, Ecuador, Peru, Panama, and Brazil\. It reconstructs the operation’s institutional lineage, from Soviet active measures through the Internet Research Agency, PeaceData \(2020\), and the Prigozhin\-linked “Politology”/“La Compañia” network, reportedly now under the control of Russia’s Foreign Intelligence Service \(SVR\)\. The study situates the case within the broader industrialization of AI\-enabled information confrontation, examining the contemporaneous Iranian “Bogus Bylines” operation and the transnational Pravda network\. A dedicated chapter investigates the October 2026 Ukrainian strikes on Yandex data centers and the observed 94% collapse in automated traffic across the Yandex network, cautioning against unwarranted causal interpretations of the relationship between these events\. The thesis argues that Dark Clark represents a doctrinal evolution from platform\-based amplification toward institutional laundering: the introduction of synthetic or co\-opted authority into established media organizations and academic institutions\. It contends that effective democratic resilience requires a multilayered response combining platform transparency, institutional source verification, provenance standards, and robust fact\-checking capacity\. The analysis deliberately refrains from endorsing the unverified assertion circulating in Brazilian political commentary that the operation materially influenced Brazilian electoral outcomes\.
