# Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation for treatment\-resistant depression in Singapore: a retrospective, naturalistic study

**Autores:** Yi Hang Tay; Ern Wei Christabel Thng

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Naturalistic repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation \(rTMS\) outcome data for treatment\-resistant depression remain limited in Southeast Asia\. This retrospective study describes such outcomes from a general hospital in Singapore\. Method 28 outpatients received rTMS \(10Hz high\-frequency or intermittent theta\-burst\) targeting the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex \(F3 scalp position\) between May 2022 and May 2025\. Treatment\-resistant depression was defined as failing ≥2 antidepressant classes for ≥6 weeks each\. The primary outcome was change in the Montgomery\-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale \(MADRS\) score; secondary outcomes included the Quick Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology\-Self Report \(QIDS\-SR16\), the Depression, Anxiety and Stress Scale\-21 \(DASS\-21\), the Quality of Life, Enjoyment and Satisfaction Questionnaire\-Short Form \(Q\-LES\-Q\-SF\), the Clinical Global Impression\-Severity \(CGI\-S\), and the Global Assessment of Functioning \(GAF\)\. Results Despite 85\.7% of patients having failed ≥3 antidepressant classes, the mean MADRS score decreased by 15\.8 \(SD 9\.1\), from 29\.6 \(SD 7\.6\) to 13\.9 \(SD 11\.1, p&lt;0\.001\)\. This corresponded to a 67\.9% response rate and a 57\.1% remission rate\. All secondary measures \(QIDS\-SR16, DASS\-21, Q\-LES\-Q\-SF, CGI\-S, and GAF\) showed significant improvements \(all p&lt;0\.001\)\. Shorter episode duration and higher baseline quality of life were significantly associated with remission\. Conclusion We report favourable response \(67\.9%\) and remission \(57\.1%\) rates within a general hospital cohort in Singapore following standardised rTMS targeting the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex \(approximated by the F3 position\), despite extensive prior antidepressant medication failure\. These findings contribute real\-world data to the limited Southeast Asian literature on standardised rTMS protocols for treatment\-resistant depression in general hospital and similar clinical settings\.
