# Glucagon\-like peptide\-1 receptor agonists and risk for hematologic malignancies: a multicenter propensity score\-matched analysis

**Autores:** Pratik Shah; Adit Singhal; Tsu Hsien Wang; Albert Ko; Yu\-Cheng Chang; Anita Ng; Andrew Shih; Yuan Ping Hsia; Cho\-Hung Chiang; Chiang Cho\-Han

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Glucagon\-like peptide\-1 receptor agonists \(GLP\-1RAs\) have become central to the management of type 2 diabetes mellitus \(T2DM\) and obesity, with indications now extending to cardiovascular risk reduction, renal protection, and liver disease \[ 1 \]\. As utilization of these agents continues to grow, thorough characterization of their long\-term safety profile, including potential effects on malignancy risk, remains a priority \[ 2 \]\. Preclinical evidence indicates that GLP\-1RAs exert anti\-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects, including suppression of NF\-κB signaling and modulation of immune cell function, which could theoretically influence hematopoiesis and lymphomagenesis \[ 2 , 3 \]\. Nevertheless, clinical data evaluating the relationship between GLP\-1RA use and hematologic malignancy risk are sparse\.
