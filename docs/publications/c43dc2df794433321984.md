# Design Documentation Canvas: A Lightweight Design Rationale Framework for Solo Designers

**Autores:** Adina Fayza Gayo; Hadziq Fabroyir

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Design systems have become standard infrastructure in digital product development, yet they often underdocument the rationale behind design decisions\. This gap is amplified in resource\-constrained startups, where solo designers manage multi\-brand, multiplatform products, increasing the risk of knowledge loss during handoffs and onboarding\. Objective: This study designs, implements, and evaluates the Design Documentation Canvas \(DDC\), a lightweight design rationale and traceability framework for solo designers in multi\-brand startup contexts\. Methods: A single\-case study was conducted at an Indonesian insurtech startup\. Data were collected through two focus group discussion \(FGD\) sessions involving nine participants, one individual interview, a six\-point Likert\-scale questionnaire \(n = 10\), and artifact analysis, with the researcher acting as a participant\-observer\. Results: The framework achieved 100% completeness across 17 qualifying design tickets, defined by substantively completed Description Panels \(FSE\-structured fields: Summary, Question/Issue, Final Decision, Evidence, and References\) and Before\-After Panels\. Questionnaire results showed a median and mode of 6 across all items and 100% agreement\-side responses, although these results should be interpreted with caution because of potential ceiling effects and social desirability bias\. Cronbach’s alpha was 0\.853\. AI\-assisted drafting also emerged during implementation as a means of reducing the perceived documentation articulation burden\. Conclusion: The DDC demonstrates operational feasibility and positive stakeholder reception within a single case, providing an adoptable documentation artifact for solo designers in Southeast Asian startup environments\. The findings remain bounded by the single\-case design and small sample and should not be interpreted as generalizable outcomes\.
