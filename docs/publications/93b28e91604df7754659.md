# A hybrid deep Kronecker and BN\-eLU\-AlexNet framework for early prediction of learner outcomes in e\-learning platforms

**Autores:** Chesta Malkani; Neetu Mittal; Subhranil Som

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Early prediction of learner outcomes in online learning environments can support timely academic intervention and learner support\. Methods This study proposes DKBNE\-AlexNet, a hybrid architecture combining a Deep Kronecker Network branch, a feature\-token batch\-normalized eLU AlexNet\-inspired branch, gated residual fusion, and a wide linear residual path\. Using the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset, 24,153 enrolments from 22 module presentations and 22,234 learners were analyzed\. Eighty\-four predictors were constructed from learner characteristics, course design, and engagement during the first 25% of each presentation\. The target was the weighted mean of tutor\-marked and computer\-marked assessment scores\. Models were evaluated using five\-fold cross\-validation grouped by module presentation and repeated three times\. Results DKBNE\-AlexNet achieved an MAE of 11\.424 \(95% group\-bootstrap CI 10\.389\-12\.560\), RMSE of 14\.843, and R 2 of 0\.117\. Random Forest and LightGBM achieved slightly lower MAEs of 10\.916 and 10\.918, respectively, but the presentation\-level difference between DKBNE\-AlexNet and Random Forest was not statistically significant \(Wilcoxon p = 0\.388; Holm\-adjusted p = 1\.000\)\. Distinct learning materials accessed and active learning days were among the strongest predictive contributors\. Discussion DKBNE\-AlexNet provides a competitive and stable framework for early learner\-outcome prediction, while strong tree\-based models remain important baselines\. The framework is best interpreted as an early\-warning learning\-analytics tool rather than a direct measure of teacher effectiveness\.
