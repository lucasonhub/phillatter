# Influence of age and averaging on band visibility in high\-resolution optical coherence tomography

**Autores:** Lukas Goerdt; Rosa Dolz‐Marco; Kenneth R\. Sloan; Liyan Gao; Christine A\. Curcio

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract This study assessed the influence of age on band visibility in spectral\-domain optical coherence tomography \(SD\-OCT\) with &lt; 3 μm axial resolution and evaluated intergrader reliability using a custom review tool incorporating 28 retinal bands\. Seventy\-two eyes of 72 individuals with normal retinas \(&lt; 45 years: n = 16; 45–65 years: n = 30; &gt; 65 years: n = 26\) were imaged using a prototype SD\-OCT device with varying automatic real\-time averaging \(ART\) levels\. Scans were acquired through the foveal center \(ART 9, 25, 100\) and at 5° superior \(ART 9\)\. Band visibility was graded using custom software supported by longitudinal reflectivity profiles at nine locations and analyzed using chi\-square and regression models\. Intergrader reliability varied between larger hyperreflective and thin hyporeflective bands \(inner plexiform layers \(IPL\) 2 and 4; retinal pigment epithelium layers \(RPE\) 1, 3, 5\)\. Higher ART levels improved visibility of the nerve fiber layer, IPL\-3, \-4, and − 5, RPE\-1, and − 5\. RPE sub\-bands were more frequently detected at 5° superior than at the fovea\. Higher age was associated with reduced visibility of the internal limiting membrane, inner nuclear layer and RPE\-5\. These findings suggest a modest age\-related decline in retinal band visibility under high\-resolution conditions, which should be accounted for in clinical trials\.
