# Elevated ApoB/ApoA ratio is associated with increased risk of sudden sensorineural hearing loss

**Autores:** Zhisong Yan; Weilin Chen; Xiaoyang Cao; Xiao Chen

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective To investigate the association between the apolipoprotein B to apolipoprotein A \(ApoB/ApoA\) ratio and sudden sensorineural hearing loss \(SSNHL\) risk in Nantong, China\. Methods This cross\-sectional study included 1211 participants \(674 with normal hearing, 537 with SSNHL\)\. Baseline demographics, comorbidities, and biochemical indicators were compared\. Multivariable logistic regression evaluated the independent association between the ApoB/ApoA ratio and SSNHL risk\. Inverse probability of treatment weighting \(IPTW\) and propensity score matching \(PSM\) were applied for sensitivity analysis\. Stratified and dose\-response analyses were also conducted\. Results The groups differed significantly in age, sex, hypertension, BMI, and various metabolic markers \(all P &lt; 0\.05\)\. In the fully adjusted model, an elevated ApoB/ApoA ratio was significantly associated with increased SSNHL risk \(OR = 9\.34, 95% CI: 3\.93\-22\.21\)\. This positive association remained robust after PSM \(OR = 8\.79, 95% CI: 3\.26\-23\.66\)\. IPTW analysis showed the high\-ratio group \(0\.78\-1\.34\) had a higher SSNHL risk than the low\-ratio group \(0\.13\-0\.58\) \(OR = 2\.28, 95% CI: 1\.79\-2\.90\)\. Dose\-response analysis indicated a significant linear positive correlation between the ratio and SSNHL prevalence, both before and after PSM\. Stratified analyses demonstrated this association remained consistent across age, sex, hypertension, and diabetes subgroups \(all P for interaction &gt; 0\.05\)\. Conclusion A high ApoB/ApoA ratio has a significant linear positive association with increased SSNHL risk\. It serves as an independent clinical biomarker for SSNHL risk assessment, providing a basis for metabolic management\.
