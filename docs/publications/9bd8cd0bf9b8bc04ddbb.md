# Spinella, Jake: The Belief\-Independence of Perception: An Essay on the Mind&\#x27;s Architecture

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

In this dissertation I argue that the mind has an architecture, and that perception and cognition are functionally distinct systems within it\. What makes them distinct is a single property: the belief\-independence of perception\. A mental process is belief\-independent when cognitive states—beliefs, desires, and thoughts—are dispensable to its computational explanation, which is to say that its outputs cannot be semantically traced back to cognitive contents in the way the conclusion of a genuine inference can be traced back to its premises\. In Chapter 1, I make this notion more precise\. I separate out the functional and representational senses in which cognitive states might be dispensable to a process, and I argue that inferential isolation from cognition is the mark of belief\-independence\. In Chapter 2, I ask how a psychological system could come to be isolated in this way, and I argue that informational encapsulation is sufficient for it, though not necessary\. Defending the claim that perception is encapsulated means taking on the cognitive penetration literature\. I go through the cases one at a time and argue that in each, either a purely perceptual explanation of the phenomenon is at least as well supported as the penetration explanation, or the perceptual outputs in question cannot be semantically traced to the cognitive contents said to affect them, so that whatever relation holds between them falls short of the inferential relation that would threaten the architectural distinction\. In Chapters 3 and 4, I argue that belief\-independence explains features of perception, and so is more than a criterion for locating the perception–cognition boundary\. In Chapter 3, I argue that encapsulation makes perception computationally tractable\. On the standard view, encapsulation does this by shrinking a hypothesis space specified in advance\. I argue instead that it works by leaving hypotheses outside that space out of consideration entirely, and that this same feature makes perception more accurate as well as faster\. In Chapter 4, I argue that belief\-independence is also necessary for perception to anchor thought to the world\. Following a line of thought from Russell and Strawson, I argue that successful singular thought requires acquaintance relations of the kind perception provides, and that the FINST\-and\-object\-file structure underlying visual tracking is one such relation\. A perceptual system whose contents were fixed in part by belief would face the same reduplication problems that acquaintance is supposed to solve\. Taken together, the four chapters argue that perception and cognition are distinct stems of the mind&\#x27;s architecture, that the belief\-independence of perception marks the line between them, and that it is because perception is belief\-independent that it can make cognition both tractable and answerable to the world\.
