# Taylorism reloaded?

**Autores:** Sander Junte; Óscar Molina Romo

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The diffusion of artificial intelligence, data analytics and digital technologies in manufacturing has revived debates about the impact of technological change on work and employment relations\. This article examines the implementation of Industry 4\.0 technologies in a Spanish train manufacturing company and analyses their implications for workers and their representatives\. Drawing on Labour Process Theory and industrial relations literature, the study is based on a qualitative case study including interviews with workers, works council members and technical staff\. The findings show that the transition to Industry 4\.0 primarily involved the implementation of a Manufacturing Execution System \(MES\), which digitalised production processes and data collection\. Rather than increasing worker autonomy, the system reinforced Taylorist principles through greater standardisation, monitoring and performance measurement\. The study also finds that worker representatives played a limited role in regulating technological change despite the existence of formal information and consultation rights\. The article argues that technological opacity, institutional constraints and the prioritisation of traditional bargaining issues limit the capacity of worker representatives to influence the governance of workplace technologies\.
