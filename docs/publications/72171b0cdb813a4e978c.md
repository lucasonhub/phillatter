# Construction of high\-precision satellite positioning model and research on fault feedback mechanism based on improved FP\-Growth algorithm

**Autores:** Ying He; Rui Ye; Yang Pu; Wenqi Linghu; Wei Zhang; Yongrui Yu

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

To address accuracy degradation and delayed fault response in satellite positioning under complex environments, this study proposes a positioning model integrating an improved FP\-Growth \(Frequent Pattern Growth\) algorithm with a real\-time fault feedback mechanism\. The model employs a Bidirectional Frequent Pattern Tree \(Bi\-FPT\) to optimize storage and traversal efficiency, incorporates dynamic support thresholds and item utility values to enhance the mining of key positioning features, and establishes a library of association rules linking location states to fault modes, thereby enabling rapid fault diagnosis\. To evaluate the model’s performance, experiments were conducted using a dataset containing one million measured Global Positioning System \(GPS\)/BeiDou data points\. Horizontal and vertical Root Mean Square Error \(RMSE\) served as the metrics for positioning accuracy, alongside statistics on fault recognition rates and response times\. The results demonstrate a horizontal positioning RMSE of 0\.8 m and a vertical positioning RMSE of 1\.2 m, with a positioning accuracy of 89\.7%, a fault recognition rate of 85%, and a system response time of only 0\.8 s\. Relative to the traditional FP\-Growth algorithm, the proposed method reduces horizontal RMSE from 1\.5 m to 0\.8 m, shortens fault response time from 1\.6 s to 0\.8 s, and increases fault recognition rate from 75% to 85%\. Notably, the model maintains high reliability and rapid feedback capabilities even in challenging scenarios such as urban canyons, demonstrating significant value for engineering applications\.
