# Recalibrating Nursing Education: A Call to Action for Moving from Pedagogy to Andragogy

**Autores:** Tim Porter\-O’Grady; Kathy Malloch; Kathy Scott

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

AIM: The time has come for nursing education to fully abandon historic pedagogical models and embrace andragogical principles that honor the adult learner&\#x27;s capacity for self\-direction and ownership of their professional development\. BACKGROUND: Evidence demonstrates that andragogical approaches that emphasize self\-direction, experiential learning, and problem\-centered education better serve adult learners\. Yet formal deep embrace remains uneven across nursing programs globally\. METHOD: This analysis examines evidence distinguishing pedagogical from andragogical approaches in nursing education\. RESULTS: Andragogical approaches incorporating neuroscience principles consistently outperform traditional methods\. Nursing graduates from adult learning\-centered programs demonstrate stronger critical thinking, clinical reasoning, and professional confidence\. Effective strategies include problem\-based learning, peer teaching, simulation experiences, and technology\-enhanced environments that support learner autonomy and collaborative practice\. CONCLUSION: The profession cannot continue preparing nurses using obsolete educational models\. This transformation demands comprehensive curriculum redesign, faculty development in adult learning competencies, and strategic technology integration\.
