# Vidaurre\-Arguello, Mim: Does Zero\-Edit Practice Support a Claim to Human Authorship?

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This article examines whether meaningful human authorship can survive full post\-generation delegation to a generative AI system: a condition in which a human originates a concept, directs and iterates on its generation, evaluates and selects a result, but performs no subsequent modification of the output\. The author’s earlier article, cited in the references, proposed a five\-dimension agency framework for evaluating Zero\-Edit practice\. The present article takes a different approach: it examines the authorship question through five external approaches \(Deng; Anscomb; Fritz; Militsyna; Huang et al\.\), none of which was developed with full post\-generation delegation as its central case\. These approaches are not treated as equivalent tests: Anscomb supplies a theoretical analogy, while the others are assessed according to their distinct analytical aims\. The analysis applies these approaches to the author’s own Zero\- Edit practice as a boundary case, using primary sources rather than secondary summaries\. It distinguishes creative agency, expressive control, and execution; engages two substantial counterarguments through first\-person case evidence; and tests the practice against one of its own weaker instances, allowing that case to count against the thesis where warranted\. The case does not establish authorship under any single framework\. Under Deng’s approach, the conception is thin under Factor One, while Factor Two shows some iteration but does not establish the degree of control exercised over the generative process\. The central finding is therefore that sufficient expressive control remains undemonstrated across these factors\. Huang et al\.’s standard reveals a separate evidentiary finding: without contemporaneous records of prompts, rejected outputs, and iteration, a Zero\-Edit authorship claim cannot be demonstrated through process evidence and rests instead on retrospective self\-report\.
