# Multi\-omics perspectives on mitochondrial vulnerability in the aging liver: biological modules and translational implications

**Autores:** Hanxi Chen; Weiwei Lu; Kainan Wang; Rui Fang; Sunjae Lee; Shi Chen; Zhengtao Liu

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Chronological age alone is an incomplete proxy for the biological heterogeneity of the aging liver\. Mitochondrial vulnerability may provide a mechanistic link between biological aging and the loss of hepatic resilience under metabolic, toxic, and ischemic stress\. Accumulating multi\-omics evidence suggests that hepatic aging is best understood as a set of coordinated pathway\-level disturbances rather than through isolated biomarkers\. Transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic studies converge on five interrelated biological modules: lipid stress, diminished oxidative reserve, proteostatic strain, inflammatory sensitization, and reduced recovery capacity\. These modules are interconnected through impaired mitochondrial metabolic flexibility, redox buffering, organelle crosstalk, and quality\-control mechanisms\. Age\-associated changes may remain partly compensated at baseline but become more apparent during metabolic overload, ischemia–reperfusion injury, cold preservation, or ex vivo machine perfusion\. Building on cross\-species evidence, this Review organizes current findings into a modular framework and examines how mitochondrial vulnerability may connect physiological liver aging with metabolic and ischemic stress\. Transplantation and machine perfusion are discussed as stress\-exposed settings for evaluating candidate tissue\- and fluid\-based readouts\. Although most omics\-derived signals remain insufficiently validated for clinical decision\-making, a mitochondrial\-centered interpretation may help prioritize biologically coherent mechanisms and improve the translational study of aging\-related liver vulnerability\.
