# Injury Type and Recurrence as Predictors of Market Value Decline in Professional Football: A Retrospective Cohort Study of 12,948 Injuries

**Autores:** Agostinho Sousa Pinto; António Abreu; Manuel Pérez\-Cota; Jerónimo Paiva; Laís Gabriele Vieira

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Professional football clubs spend billions on player transfers, yet a single injury can substantially reduce a player’s market value\. While previous research has established that injuries lower value, no study has systematically compared value penalties across specific injury types or quantified the additional cost of injury recurrence\. Methods: A retrospective cohort analysis was conducted using 12,948 injury events from 2,061 professional footballers across top‑five European leagues \(Premier League, La Liga, Serie A, Bundesliga, and Ligue 1\) between 2008 and 2024\. Mixed‑effects linear regression, Random Forest, and non‑parametric hypothesis tests \(Kruskal–Wallis, Mann–Whitney U\) were employed to examine the effects of injury type and recurrence on percentage change in market value, controlling for age, pre‑injury value, and position\. Results: Anterior cruciate ligament \(ACL\) injuries reduced market value by 6\.0%, and Achilles injuries by 15\.5%\. Ankle injuries increased value by 14\.0%, fractures by 11\.0%, concussions by 10\.5%, and knee injuries by 7\.0%\. Hamstring and muscle injuries showed no median change within the 120\-day observation window\. Recurrent injuries incurred an additional 8\.0% penalty beyond first‑time injuries\. Players under 24 gained 39\.0% following injury, while players aged 30 and over lost 31\.0% – a difference of 70\.0 percentage points\. Higher pre‑injury market value and older age were the strongest negative predictors of post‑injury valuation\. Conclusion: ACL and Achilles injuries are uniquely destructive to player market value\. Most other injury types show no lasting penalty\. Recurrence imposes a substantial additional penalty\. Younger players recover far better than older ones\. Clubs, agents, and insurers should incorporate injury type, recurrence history, and age into transfer negotiations, contract design, and player health strategies\. Keywords: Football injuries, market value, ACL, recurrence, sports economics
