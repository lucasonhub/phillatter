# The Hand\-Proximity Effect in Virtual Learning: How Interaction Design Influences Knowledge Acquisition

**Autores:** Xuefei Ji; Huang Yao; Di Chen; Liuyao Feng

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Although virtual simulation platforms are widely used in education, they often lack integration with embodied cognition theory\. The hand\-proximity effect remains underexplored in VR, and how VR design elements mediate it to support higher\-order knowledge acquisition is unclear\. We manipulated interaction mode, text\-hand distance, and virtual hand appearance across three knowledge domains\. Results showed that for factual and conceptual learning, neither interaction mode nor text\-hand distance significantly affected learning performance\. 3D interaction enhanced VR presence but was associated with greater cognitive pressure when combined with far\-hand text \(p=0\.040\); anchoring text near the virtual hand mitigated cognitive pressure and improved readability \(p=0\.005\)\. For procedural learning, realistic human hand models significantly improved learning performance compared to standard cursors \(p=0\.044\)\. These findings empirically validate embodied cognition in VR and yield guidelines: anchor instructional text in peripersonal space for conceptual learning and use realistic human hand avatars for procedural training\.
