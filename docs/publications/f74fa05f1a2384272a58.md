# Camera\-Based AI\-Assisted Scoring for the Balance Error Scoring System: A Cross\-sectional Validity and Agreement Study

**Autores:** Yushi Chen; 家焰 程; Tsz Yuen Frank Wang; Shanshan Zheng; Le Yu; Xiao’ao Xue; Yang Sun; Yinghui Hua

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: The Balance Error Scoring System \(BESS\) is widely used to assess static postural control in sports medicine and rehabilitation\. However, conventional real\-time scoring is observer dependent and may be influenced by rater experience, viewing angle, attention, and the interpretation of transient or overlapping errors\. This study evaluated the criterion\-related validity and agreement of a camera\-based artificial intelligence \(AI\)\-assisted BESS relative to consensus video\-review manual scoring and compared its error magnitude with on\-site manual scoring\. METHODS: This single\-center cross\-sectional study included 80 of 84 recruited participants: 40 healthy controls, 20 with chronic ankle instability \(CAI\), and 20 after anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction \(ACLR\)\. All participants completed the standard six\-condition BESS protocol\. Scores were obtained using AI\-assisted, on\-site manual, and consensus video\-review manual scoring; the latter was prespecified as the pragmatic manual reference standard\. Primary total\-score analyses included Pearson correlation, the intraclass correlation coefficient \(ICC\) with 95% confidence intervals \(CIs\), Bland\-Altman mean difference and 95% limits of agreement \(LoA\), mean absolute error \(MAE\), and root mean square error \(RMSE\)\. Condition\- and group\-specific analyses were exploratory\. RESULTS: For the total BESS score, AI\-assisted scoring versus the reference showed r = 0\.989, ICC = 0\.989 \(95% CI 0\.982\-0\.993\), a mean difference of 0\.188 errors, 95% LoA from \-2\.063 to 2\.438 errors, an MAE of 0\.787 errors, and an RMSE of 1\.157 errors\. On\-site manual scoring versus the reference showed r = 0\.922, ICC = 0\.920 \(95% CI 0\.877\-0\.948\), a mean difference of \-0\.612 errors, 95% LoA from \-6\.680 to 5\.455 errors, an MAE of 2\.212 errors, and an RMSE of 3\.136 errors\. Condition\-specific discrepancies were larger in the single\-leg and tandem\-stance conditions\. Exploratory group comparisons showed higher total BESS scores in individuals with CAI than in healthy controls\. CONCLUSIONS: Camera\-based AI\-assisted BESS scoring showed strong total\-score agreement with the pragmatic manual reference standard and smaller discrepancies than on\-site manual scoring\. This approach may support more standardized BESS assessment; however, external validation and longitudinal reliability testing are required before interchangeability with manual scoring can be considered\.
