# AI\-neologisms in English, German, French, and Russian

**Autores:** Ekaterina Vladimirovna Nevalenaya; T\. V\. Perutskaya

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The article investigates the processes of word\-formation in the field of artificial intelligence \(AI\) terminology across four languages: English, German, French, and Russian\. The relevance of this research stems from the rapid advancement of AI technologies, which generates a vast number of new lexical units requiring systematic description and standardization\. The core problem of the study is identifying both the universal tendencies and language\-specific features in the formation of AI neologisms within structurally diverse languages under the influence of global English dominance\. The aim of the work is to conduct a comparative typological analysis of productive word\-formation models for AI neologisms and to determine the extent of English, as the donor language, on the terminological systems of German, French, and Russian\.The study proves the leading role of English as the primary source of AI terminology\. We established that among the AI neologisms, selected by continuous sampling on forums and in articles on websites devoted to the topic of artificial intelligence, the dominant methods of terminology formation in the English language are semantic transfer and semantic development of common vocabulary, compound words, as well as abbreviation and blending\. The research establishes that German, French, and Russian primarily employ various types of calquing \(loan translation, semantic calques\) to adapt English\-language terms\. However, each language also demonstrates specific ways of coining neologisms \(e\.g\., creating native compounds in German or using suffixation in French\)\. We find that a significant number of English acronyms are preserved in all the analyzed languages without translation, retaining their original graphic form\. The results of the study contribute to the understanding of current trends in the terminology development and can be applied in translation theory and practice, as well as in lexicographic work\.
