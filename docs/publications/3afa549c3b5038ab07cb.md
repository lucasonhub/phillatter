# AI Ensemble Framework for OSINT\-Based Social Media Crime Prevention in Kenya

**Autores:** Alfred Ngala; Shem Mbandu; Anthony Mile

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Criminal networks increasingly exploit social media to organize, recruit, incite, and coordinate unlawful activities, whereas law\-enforcement monitoring in Kenya remains largely manual and English\-centric\. This study aimed to design, implement, and rigorously evaluate an artificial intelligence \(AI\) ensemble framework for open\-source intelligence \(OSINT\) classification of crime\- and threat\-relevant content in Kenya&\#x27;s trilingual \(English, Kiswahili, and Sheng\) and code\-switched digital environment\. A corpus of 6,212 documents was assembled from six sources: Twitter/X, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and two Sheng\-language corpora\. The corpus was weakly labelled using a 214\-term trilingual crime\-and\-threat lexicon\. Label noise was quantified against a manually annotated stratified validation sample \(n = 320\), yielding Cohen&\#x27;s κ = 0\.350, silver\-label precision of 0\.525, and recall of 0\.750\. Combined word\- and character\-level term frequency\-inverse document frequency \(TF\-IDF\) features comprising 25,831 dimensions were used to train a hyperparameter\-tuned, weighted soft\-voting ensemble consisting of multinomial naive Bayes, a calibrated linear support vector machine, random forest, logistic regression, and XGBoost\. The decision threshold was calibrated using cross\-validated out\-of\-fold probabilities\. The optimized ensemble achieved 98\.3% accuracy, 80\.6% precision, 62\.5% recall, 70\.4% F1\-score, 0\.907 ROC\-AUC, 0\.724 PR\-AUC, 0\.702 Matthews correlation coefficient \(MCC\), and a 0\.50% false\-positive rate\. The ensemble outperformed the best single learner and a frozen multilingual transformer baseline \(F1 = 26\.2%\)\. Ablation analysis demonstrated the contribution of the Sheng\-normalization layer, while comparison of class\-imbalance strategies showed that cost\-sensitive learning combined with threshold calibration produced the strongest overall performance compared with SMOTE\-based resampling\. The findings demonstrate that linguistically inclusive classical machine\-learning ensembles can provide an accurate, auditable, and computationally deployable approach to proactive social\-media crime prevention in low\-resource, code\-switched settings\. The study also derives policy implications concerning threshold\-calibrated triage, expert annotation, data\-protection impact assessments, and safeguards against dual\-use misuse\.
