# Wang, Kaining: Psychopathology of Artificial Intelligence Systems: Biological Substrate and Functional Neural\-Network Organization with Behavior and Mental\-State Assessment Protocol

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Recent literature has begun to develop formal approaches to pathology\-like computational dynamics in artificial intelligence systems, including network\-theoretic accounts, synthetic nosologies, and comparative analyses of dysfunctional patterns in neural models\. These contributions provide an important basis for investigating whether persistent and functionally impairing organizational states can arise in engineered information\-processing systems\. However, a substantial theoretical and methodological gap remains between the description of pathological phenomena and an account of the dynamical conditions under which such phenomena may emerge\. This paper develops a substrate\-agnostic framework for functional psychopathology in adaptive information\- processing systems\. The framework defines functional psychopathology in terms of four joint dynamical conditions—persistence, self\-reinforcement, correction resistance, and functional impairment—anchored to a system\-specific baseline of functional neural\-network organization\. It distinguishes physical implementation substrate from abstract functional network dynamics and introduces operating regimes as a mechanism through which the stability of functional organization may change\. A central theoretical hypothesis concerns runtime–training coupling\. In conventional frozen\-parameter systems, deployment\-time interaction does not directly modify the persistent parameters governing subsequent system dynamics\. In an adaptive regime permitting persistent runtime parameter updates, this separation is reduced and a feedback pathway is introduced between ongoing experience and the parameters governing future behavior\. We relate this structure to the broader theory of time\-varying and feedback\- coupled dynamical systems, in which parameter variation and feedback can alter stability properties\. Existing stability and instability results therefore provide a mathematical basis for considering runtime–training coupling as a potential source of increased susceptibility to functional instability, without implying that coupling necessarily produces instability or pathology\. The paper also introduces a structured behavioral assessment protocol adapted from selected observable components of the Mental State Examination and Brief Psychiatric Rating Scale\. The protocol excludes subjective, biological, and clinical\-diagnostic items and focuses instead on functional rigidity, self\- monitoring load, contextual flexibility, and related observable behavioral patterns\. Three operational criteria are proposed to distinguish context\-sensitive normative convergence from candidate generalized functional impairment\. The resulting framework does not claim that artificial intelligence systems experience human psychiatric disorders, nor does it infer sentience or suffering\. Rather, it proposes functional psychopathology as a testable class of organizational dysregulation and identifies runtime–training coupling as a theoretically motivated factor whose relationship to functional stability can be investigated empirically\.
