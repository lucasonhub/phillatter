# Behavioral and Obstetrical Factors Associated with Self\-Reported Cervical Cancer\-Related Symptoms Among Women of Childbearing Age in Medan City, Indonesia

**Autores:** Fatwa Imelda; Lawra Lofty Maharaja; Reni Asmara Ariga

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Cervical cancer remains the second most common malignancy among women in Indonesia, driven by late\-stage clinical diagnoses\. This cross\-sectional correlational study investigated the relationship between behavioral\-obstetrical risk profiles and the early manifestation of self\-perceived cervical cancer symptoms among women in vulnerable reproductive groups at the primary care level\.Aims: This study aimed to analyze the association between behavioral\-obstetrical risk profiles and self\-reported cervical cancer symptoms among women of childbearing age \(15–49 years\) at the primary healthcare level\.Methods: A consecutive sample of 108 women of childbearing age completed an anonymous, self\-administered questionnaire assessing behavioral–obstetrical risk factors and a 10\-item cervical cancer symptom checklist at UPTD Puskesmas PB Selayang II, Medan, Indonesia\.Results: Self\-reported symptoms were identified in 59\.3% of respondents, predominantly systemic fatigue \(50\.0%\), lower back pain \(37\.96%\), and pelvic pain \(25\.93%\), while late\-stage signs were completely absent\.Conclusion: These results demonstrate a significant cross\-sectional association between specific behavioral\-obstetrical profiles and self\-reported cervical cancer\-related symptoms among women accessing primary care\. Because symptoms were self\-reported and unconfirmed by clinical or cytological diagnostic tests, universal screening remains essential to distinguish subjective reproductive complaints from actual cervical pathology\.
