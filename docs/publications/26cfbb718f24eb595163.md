# Reclaiming legacy and envisioning futures in STEM education: Afrofuturism and a scholar\-activist\-community coalition

**Autores:** Michele R\. Williams; Isaiah M\. Stewart; A\. L\. Rouse; Jayden Seay; Lucienne J\. Petit; Melvin Woolfolk; Donovan L\. Flagg; Kobe Jordan; Immanuel Stewart; Terrell R\. Morton

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Amidst growing efforts to dismantle diversity, equity, and inclusion \(DEI\) policies and programs, educational ecosystems continue to position Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics \(STEM\) as meritocratic institutions\. This disposition is proven inaccurate in higher education STEM culture, perpetuating the marginalization of Black people in STEM\. In an effort to enhance Black students’ STEM educational experiences and outcomes, the Re\-Envisioning Culture Network \(REC\-Network\) established a coalition of professors, K–12 educators, artists, community partners, and activists to reimagine undergraduate biology curriculum through an Afrofuturistic lens\. However, in 2025, funding for the REC Network was subject to the enforcement of new United States federal guidelines that now characterize human\-centered research promoting DEI as a violation of anti\-discrimination laws, resulting in the immediate cancellation of more than $1 billion in grant funds\. Methods This critical qualitative case study of the REC Network unearths its strategizing and collective actions to transform dominant STEM power structures in ways that honor historical Black STEM narratives while engaging in liberatory imaginations\. Data sources were gathered from four participant groups tasked with redesigning curriculum content and providing supplemental materials\. Routine meetings were conducted among authors to deductively code lesson plans, identify emergent findings, and produce final themes from the data\. Results Three themes emerged from the analysis\. The theme of Reckoning with Black Marginalization illuminates the systemic process by which Black individuals and their community are persistently othered by those who identify as members of the dominant racial group in U\.S\. American society\. The theme of Reclaiming Black Legacies situates the components of Black life often lost across the dimensions of time, place, and space\. In our analysis of the biology lessons, Afrofuturist principles consistently create opportunities to foster space for speculative imagination and freedom\-dreaming, leveraging imagination as a productive cognitive stance rather than mere distraction; this imaginative work invited students to conceive beyond present constraints and to use storytelling and future\-oriented scenarios as tools for Re\-envisioning Black Possibilities\. Discussion Our findings demonstrate how a scholar\-activist\-community coalition situated within a race\-critical futurity framing can “gate\-break” the prevalent, exclusionary Western and Eurocentric scientific onto\-epistemologies, even among contentious geo\-sociopolitical contexts\.
