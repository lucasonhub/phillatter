# ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE INTEGRATION IN WATER UTILITY ENGINEERING: DISTINGUISHING CONTROL\-SIDE BOTTLENECKS FROM THERMODYNAMIC LIMITS

**Autores:** Kuznetsov D\.

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Reviews of artificial intelligence in water utilities usually treat digital\-twin control of distribution networks and atmospheric water generation as two instances of the same technology sitting at different stages of maturity\. This article argues that framing is a category error, because the two applications are stalled by fundamentally different obstacles\. Distribution\-network control is bottlenecked by what current sensors and computing hardware can observe and process at network scale, a limit that narrows steadily as utilities add instrumentation, refine the control architecture, and accumulate operating experience\. Atmospheric generation is bottlenecked instead by the gap between dew point and dry\-bulb temperature, a fixed psychrometric relationship that sets the energy cost of extracting water from air no matter how intelligently the control layer allocates load between extraction paths\. A structured comparison of both literatures shows that only the control\-side bottleneck responds to the kind of spending most AI integration programs are built to fund; the generation\-side bottleneck moves only if the extraction physics itself changes or the system draws air from different boundary conditions altogether\.
