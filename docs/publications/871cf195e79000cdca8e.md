# An integrated AI\-based software architecture for load forecasting, controlled anomaly detection, and predictive energy management

**Autores:** Karlygash Alibekkyzy; Gulnur Massabayeva; Michal Majka; Мадина Базарова; Daniyar Kaliyev; Талшын Керибаева; Saule Askerbekovna Belginova; Alina Bugubayeva

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The increasing deployment of smart meters and Internet of Things technologies has created a demand for intelligent computational systems capable of jointly forecasting electricity consumption, detecting abnormal behavior, and optimizing load management\. This study proposes an integrated artificial intelligence\-based framework for automated energy metering and consumption management in smart\-grid environments\. The framework combines a Long Short\-Term Memory network for short\-term load forecasting, an autoencoder\-based model for anomaly detection, and Model Predictive Control for demand\-side optimization\. The system architecture includes data acquisition, communication, preprocessing, predictive analytics, anomaly monitoring, and decision\-support layers\. The primary empirical evaluation used 4,380 measured hourly observations \(approximately 6 months\) from a single administrative building\. A separately flagged synthetic temporal extension was retained only as an auxiliary dataset and was excluded from all primary performance metrics\. Across five independent model initializations, the final LSTM achieved a MAPE of 3\.423 ± 0\.555%, an RMSE of 0\.336 ± 0\.048 kW, and an R 2 of 0\.973 ± 0\.007 on the 633\-sequence independent measured test subset\. The synthetic extension was maintained as a separately identified auxiliary component and was excluded from all primary training, validation, and independent test metrics\. Using a validation\-selected reconstruction\-error threshold, the autoencoder detected 12 of 30 controlled anomalies in the independent test subset, yielding 100\.00% precision, 40\.00% recall, and an F1\-score of 57\.14%\. Under the same controlled protocol, One\-Class SVM achieved higher recall \(53\.33%\) and F1\-score \(69\.57%\), indicating that the autoencoder should be interpreted as an architectural component rather than as the best\-performing anomaly detector for the present dataset\. A historical proof\-of\-concept MPC simulation, retained for architectural illustration, reported a 15% reduction in simulated peak demand and a 12% reduction in estimated electricity cost; these values are not interpreted as measured outcomes from the administrative\-building experiment\. Controlled anomaly injection was used to establish reproducible validation and test labels for the anomaly\-detection component\. The results demonstrate that the joint integration of forecasting, anomaly detection, and predictive control provides a computationally coherent framework for supporting energy\-management efficiency, operational reliability, and data\-driven decision\-making in intelligent energy systems\.
