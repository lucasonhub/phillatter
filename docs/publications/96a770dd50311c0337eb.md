# Moore, Terrence: The Deep Structure of Flourishing

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This is Chapter 3 of Noura: Justice, Flourishing, and Commonwealth\. The chapter addresses the problem of universalism and relativism in theories of human flourishing and proposes a third way\. Drawing on the analogy of universal grammar, it argues that flourishing possesses a universal deep structure that generates culturally variable surface realisations\. The deep structure consists of four innate capacities—Cognitive, Agentive, Moral, and Purposive—which are species\-typical potentials that develop through maturation and require enabling conditions to be actualised\. The fourfold decomposition is offered as a hypothesis, not a fixed commitment; what the framework requires is not that there be exactly four, but that flourishing be understood as the developmental exercise of capacities that require enabling conditions\. The chapter then articulates a tri\-perspectival account of a meaningful life of one&\#x27;s own choosing: the Subjective dimension \(the felt quality of experience, with positive markers such as vitality, fulfillment, coherence, mattering, engagement, and interconnectedness, and negative markers such as emptiness, hopelessness, drift, anhedonia, and disconnection\), the Agentive dimension \(self\-authorship\), and the Normative dimension \(compatibility with interdependence and The Four Conditions\)\. It argues that interdependence is constitutive rather than optional—like soil, water, and sunlight for a rose bush—and distinguishes sharply among flourishing itself, Education, The Requisites that make embodied agency possible, and The Four Conditions that institutions must satisfy for informed agency to be possible\. The chapter shows how the same deep structure generates diverse individual and civilizational surface realisations—monastic contemplation, scientific inquiry, artistic vocation, family\-centred care, and communal traditions—and engages Confucianism, Buddhism, Ubuntu, Christianity, Islam, secular humanism, and diverse political systems without imposing Western liberal norms\. It concludes with formal definitions of the deep structure of human flourishing and the deep structure of a good society, and frames the framework as an open research programme inviting empirical testing and refinement\.
