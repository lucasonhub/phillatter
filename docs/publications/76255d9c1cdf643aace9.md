# The Topology of Thinking and Being: Transparent Puzzles, Recursive Ontology, and the Critique of Fictional Entities

**Autores:** Gangbei Niu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** book

## Resumo

This paper is the third in the author&\#x27;s series on the philosophy of consciousness, following the first two: From the Deconstruction of Subjectivity to the Ethics of AI Consciousness: A Unified Examination of Consciousness, Philosophical Zombies, and Scientific Grain Size from the Perspective of Recursive Ontology \(first published\), and The Dissolution of the Explanatory Gap: Humean Skepticism, Recursive Ontology, and the Consensus Replacement in the Problem of Consciousness \(second published\)\. It further reveals the structural error of conceivability arguments from a topological perspective and responds to the challenge of &quot;why difference structures feel&quot; at the level of explanatory termination\. The core arguments are: \(1\) Conceptual space permits arbitrary combination of discrete symbols \(horizontal arrangement or vertical stacking\), but ontological generation is continuous and nested, allowing only horizontal splicing on causal chains; the philosophical zombie is a conceptual monster produced by vertically stacking &quot;physical description&quot; and &quot;experiential description,&quot; belonging to the same class of topological error as &quot;square circle\.&quot; \(2\) Fictional entities can be classified into three types by their conceptual operation: horizontal splicing \(unicorn, mild fiction\), vertical stacking \(square circle, zombie, radical fiction\), and pure verbal combinations \(non\-referring\)\. \(3\) There is no isomorphic mapping between thinking \(discrete conceptual operation\) and being \(continuous recursive generation\); conceivability cannot serve as ontological evidence\. \(4\) The question &quot;why do difference structures &\#x27;glow&\#x27;?&quot; is answered as the explanatory termination point of the recursive ontology system, analogous to the status of &quot;1\+1=2&quot; in Peano arithmetic\. This paper concludes that the hard problem is not a mystery awaiting solution but a topological illusion produced by conceptual folding\.
