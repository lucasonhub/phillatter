# Do patients perceive peripheral nerve blocks as optional interventions for elective surgery? An evaluation of informed consent at a UK specialist orthopaedic centre

**Autores:** Richard Snooks; Samiha Ismail; Rucha Vora; Lorna Starsmore; Brian Herath; Alexander Sell; Boyne Bellew

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Shared decision\-making is the legal and ethical standard for informed consent in the United Kingdom following Montgomery v Lanarkshire \(2015\)\. Procedure\-specific evidence and guidelines support peripheral nerve block \(PNB\) for many orthopaedic operations, but valid consent still requires that patients recognise PNB as a distinct elective intervention they may decline\. Whether patients perceive PNB in this way is not well characterised\. Methods Single\-centre prospective service evaluation conducted between June and July 2024 at a specialist orthopaedic hospital\. Consecutive adults offered a PNB as part of the anaesthetic plan were surveyed on postoperative day 1 using a standardised 19\-item bedside questionnaire\. Domains of interest included recognition of the block as optional, spontaneous recall of the technique\(s\) received, engagement with pre\-operative information, uptake, and satisfaction; no single measure was designated a priori as primary\. The project was approved by the institutional Clinical Effectiveness Committee as a service evaluation\. Results Sixty\-three of 68 eligible patients participated \(92\.6%\)\. Although 59/63 \(93\.7%\) recalled being offered a PNB, only 25/63 \(39\.7%\) perceived it as optional; 26/63 \(41\.3%\) believed it was part of the anaesthetic and 12/63 \(19\.0%\) were unsure\. Fifty\-one \(81\.0%\) accepted a PNB\. No participant spontaneously recalled receiving a PNB, whereas 15/16 \(93\.8%\) of those who received neuraxial anaesthesia recalled it \( p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Receipt of written information was not associated with recognition of optionality \( p = 1\.00\)\. Prior exposure to regional anaesthesia was strongly associated with recognition of optionality \[20/32 \(62\.5%\) vs\. 5/19 \(26\.3%\); OR: 4\.67, 95% CI: 1\.34–16\.24; p = 0\.009\]\. Satisfaction was uniformly high \(82\.5% very satisfied; none dissatisfied\)\. Conclusions In this specialist orthopaedic single\-centre service evaluation, fewer than half of patients recognised the offered PNB as a distinct elective intervention despite an explicit verbal offer and high satisfaction\. Procedural compliance with consent did not guarantee patient recognition of choice\. The local perioperative consent pathway has been modified to include an explicit optionality statement and a mandatory teach\-back step, aligned with the Association of Anaesthetists&\#x27; newly\-issued 2026 guidance requiring active confirmation of understanding\. Prospective re\-measurement is planned\.
