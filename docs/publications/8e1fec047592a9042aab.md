# Context\-aware optimization of follow\-up intervals for type 2 diabetes care using Markov decision processes

**Autores:** Parisa Lotfibagha; Kristen Miller; William J\. Gallagher; Elizabeth B\. Selden; Müge Capan

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Chronic disease management relies on regular patient\-provider interactions to monitor progression and control\. For type 2 diabetes \(T2D\), current guidelines prescribe fixed intervals between primary care visits, overlooking heterogeneity in clinical trajectories and patient characteristics\. This study formulates subpopulation\-specific follow\-up planning as a context\-specific Markov decision process \(MDP\) to optimize follow\-up intervals using electronic health records from 17,499 patients across 10 primary care clinics\. Contexts were identified through \(i\) principal component analysis for dimensionality reduction of health\-trajectory variables and \(ii\) clustering patients using the resulting components and patient\-level features\. The MDP used a factorized transition model with state components including hospitalization, observed HbA1c, measurement recency, and HbA1c trend and was solved using approximate dynamic programming\. Policies were evaluated against benchmarks using fitted Q evaluation \(FQE\)\. Two contexts emerged, representing lower\- and higher\-risk subpopulations\. The framework recommended shorter intervals as HbA1c increased, shifting to 3\-month follow\-up for moderately elevated values and 1\-month follow\-up for very high values\. The high\-risk subpopulation shifted to shorter intervals at lower HbA1c levels than the low\-risk subpopulation\. Across the full cohort and both subpopulations, the derived policies outperformed benchmarks\. These findings indicate that the framework has the potential to improve subpopulation\-specific follow\-up planning in primary care\.
