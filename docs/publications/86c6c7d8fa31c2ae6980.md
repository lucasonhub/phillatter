# Factors influencing resin production in maritime pine \(Pinus pinaster Ait\.\): climate, stand structure and resin tapping methods

**Autores:** Dalila Araújo Lopes; André S\. A\. Sandim; Carlos A\. M\. G\. Fernandes; José L\. P\. C\. Louzada; Nalin Seixas; Inês S\.L\. Farias; Armando J\. D\. Silvestre; Maria E\. C\. M\. Silva

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction The growing need to replace fossil\-based raw materials with renewable resources has increased interest in the valorization of bio\-based forest products within the framework of the bioeconomy and circular economy\. In this context, natural resin from maritime pine \( Pinus pinaster Ait\.\) represents a renewable raw material with the potential to replace fossil\-derived materials in a wide range of applications\. However, optimizing resin production requires an integrated understanding of the operational, silvicultural, dendrometric, and climatic factors that influence the resin producing capacity of P\. pinaster trees\. Methods This study was conducted in three maritime pine stands located in Tresminas, Nazaré and Isna, Portugal, during 2024 and 2025\. Different resin tapping methods, bio\-based and sulfuric acid\-based stimulant pastes, and management practices were evaluated\. Resin production was compared among treatments and sites using ANOVA and Tukey test, while associations with dendrometric and climatic variables were assessed using Spearman’s correlation\. Results The results showed that the traditional resin tapping method yielded significantly higher resin production than the closed method\. Stimulant paste performance varied according to the tapping method: the traditional paste \(sulfuric acid\-based\) resulted in the highest resin production under the traditional method, whereas Cunningham paste \(sulfuric acidbased \+ ethephon\) was the most productive under the closed method\. In this method, the bio\-based pastes performed comparably to the traditional paste\. The frequency of wound reopening significantly affected production only in Nazaré, where a 21\-day interval resulted in higher yields\. At the less productive sites, no significant differences were observed, suggesting that longer intervals may reduce operational costs without compromising production\. Prescribed burning had no significant effect on production, and tapping trees with a DBH below 20 cm was not economically viable in terms of resin yield\. Conclusion Despite its lower resin production, the closed method offers relevant qualitative and operational advantages, including lower impurity content in the resin, reduced turpentine losses, shorter operating time, and improved working conditions\. The effect of tapping frequency on resin production depends on the climatic conditions at each site\. Optimizing the production factors of resin tapping activities allows for increased productivity and operational efficiency, promoting more sustainable management of forest resources\.
