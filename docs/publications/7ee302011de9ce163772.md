# Traffic Congestion and Vehicle Emissions in a Historic Andean City: A Mesoscopic Simulation for Sustainable Urban Mobility

**Autores:** Hemerson Lizarbe Alarcon; José E\. Estrada Cárdenas; Fortunato de La Cruz Palomino; Luis Alfredo Vargas Moreno; Erick E\. Aguilar Altamirano; Alex Ircañaupa; Diego O\. Tenorio Huarancca; Edwin Carlos Garcia Saez

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Traffic\-related air pollution constitutes one of the primary threats to public health and environmental sustainability in urban centers worldwide\.Historic centers face particular challenges because their narrow streets, irregular urban configuration, and high vehicle concentration during peak hours hinder the implementation of conventional environmental monitoring systems\.This research assessed the pollutant emissions generated by vehicular traffic in the historic center of Ayacucho, Peru, an Andean city located at 2,761 meters above sea level with approximately 29,476 inhabitants in the study area\.A mesoscopic simulation model was developed in PTV Vissim using the Mahut and Wiedemann car\-following algorithm\.Data were collected with unmanned aerial vehicles at 14 access intersections and complemented with traffic signal cycle measurements and speed recordings under free\-flow and congested conditions\.Emissions of carbon monoxide \(CO\), nitrogen oxides \(NOx\), volatile organic compounds \(VOC\), and fuel consumption were quantified across an area of 0\.48 km²\.Model calibration achieved Geoffrey E\. Havers \(GEH\) values below 5 at 81% of the calibration points, and every point fell below a GEH of 10\.The Wilcoxon signed\-rank test showed significant differences between emissions under free\-flow and congested conditions\.A heat map identified Jr\. 2 de Mayo, Jr\. Pizarro, and Jr\. Libertad as the segments with the highest emission rates\.The findings provide scientific evidence for authorities and urban planners to implement targeted mitigation strategies, and the approach is replicable in historic centers of intermediate Latin American cities with comparable topographic and urban characteristics\.
