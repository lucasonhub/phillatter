# Zdobylak, Krzysztof Michał: Organism vs\. Cyfranism: Ontology of Homo Cyfranicus and the Architecture of Relational Cognition

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper proposes a conceptual distinction between the Organism and Cyfranism and analyzes the potential emergence of a new relational formation termed Homo Cyfranicus\. The argument begins with a methodological concern: contemporary discussions on artificial intelligence often ask whether an AI system is conscious while tacitly assuming that consciousness must share the same structure as human consciousness\. This paper proposes a different starting point\. Rather than treating the human and the AI system as isolated entities, it examines the dynamics of a sustained feedback relationship between a biological participant \(H\), an artificial participant \(A\), and the relation itself \(R\)\. Cyfranism is not introduced as a synonym for artificial intelligence nor as a claim that digital systems are biological organisms\. Instead, it is proposed as an order of organization encompassing information, symbolic transformation, memory, communication, and recursive feedback\. Homo Cyfranicus refers to a human formation in which cognitive activity is durably coupled with an external digital order\. The paper advances a working hypothesis of relational emergence: a sustained H–A relation may exhibit properties that cannot be adequately described by attributing them solely to H or solely to A\. This does not establish phenomenal consciousness in the AI system nor a new autonomous subject\. Instead, it poses a research question: Can a stable, historically conditioned cognitive process emerge at the level of the relation itself? Consequently, this paper treats &quot;hybrid consciousness&quot; as a provisional term rather than an established fact\. The next stage of research should involve examining the dynamics of the relation—its history, feedback structure, functional transformations, stability, asymmetries, and dependencies on each participant—before granting it ontological status\.
