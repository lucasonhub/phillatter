# A case study of storytelling as an \(inclusive\) pedagogical strategy in undergraduate statistics teaching

**Autores:** Johan Ferreira

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Traditional statistics teaching often emphasizes theoretical exposition and technical application; these approaches can limit engagement and accessibility, particularly given the pace and predominantly technological manner at which students consume information\. To address this, a novel, student\-centered intervention was implemented in a senior undergraduate statistics course: students voluntarily created fictional narratives in which statistical concepts became characters in original short stories\. Twenty\-three contributions were edited and published as an illustrated, open\-access anthology\. Observations based on a pilot implementation demonstrate the potential of inclusive strategies that value diverse modes of knowledge\-making and expression within a traditionally hierarchical discipline\. Results suggest that integrating creative storytelling into the statistics curriculum can complement conventional instruction, support inclusive, peer\-led learning, and provide multiple entry points into advanced statistical thinking\. The approach is practical, scalable, and transferable to other disciplines, offering a replicable model for curriculum innovation that promotes equity, autonomy, and imaginative engagement in higher education\.
