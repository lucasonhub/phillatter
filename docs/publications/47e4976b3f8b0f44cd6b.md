# Physics\-guided interpretable machine learning for explicit resistance equation discovery in concrete penetration

**Autores:** Changchang Tu; Taiping Guo; Yuhang Mei; Wenhui Zhao; Tian Jin; Chuiqi Zhong; Qiran Sun; Yuxin Sun

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Classical Forrestal penetration calculations are attractive because they are explicit, but their empirical strength\-only resistance term is too restrictive for heterogeneous concrete\-penetration data\. This study derives a closed\-form resistance equation from a governed literature dataset, reducing 497 raw records from 41 source blocks to 443 useable records from 37 sources through duplicate removal, governed density completion, deep\-penetration filtering, and descriptor\-only anomaly screening\. Forrestal inversion maps each measured penetration depth to an operator\-consistent effective resistance target described by mechanics\-informed dimensionless descriptors\. The artificial\-intelligence contribution is a constrained equation\-discovery workflow in which a categorical gradient\-boosting model learns the nonlinear resistance surface, Shapley additive explanations restrict the analytical term family, and limited\-memory Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno optimization with bound constraints identifies equation coefficients under a forward penetration\-depth objective\. The engineering contribution is a 16\-term explicit resistance law that replaces the empirical resistance relation inside the Forrestal operator while retaining its deterministic calculation form\. On the 89\-record held\-out set, the equation gives a coefficient of determination of 0\.9543 and a mean absolute percentage error of 14\.88%, compared with 0\.9206 and 24\.44% for classical Forrestal and 0\.9551 and 13\.95% for the nonlinear resistance\-reference model; its mean prediction\-to\-measurement ratio is 0\.984\. Repeated random, source\-grouped, strength\-stratified, out\-of\-distribution, input\-uncertainty, and density\-robustness tests bound the useable domain\. The 80–120 MPa interval remains cautionary because of weaker held\-out accuracy and limited cross\-source support\.
