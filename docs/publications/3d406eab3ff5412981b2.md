# Hydroclimatic variability and future projections of extreme precipitation and soil moisture in Senegal

**Autores:** TINE Moussa; Lamine MBAYE Mamadou; Assane Ndiaye; BOP Mamadou

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study analyses the spatiotemporal variability of extreme precipitation indices and soil moisture in Senegal by combining historical data with projections from the CMIP6 multi\-model ensemble under the SSP1\-2\.6 and SSP5\-8\.5 scenarios\.The methodology relies on the Modified Mann\-Kendall \(MMK\) test, Innovative Trend Analysis \(ITA\), and K\-means clustering\.Historical analysis reveals a recovery in rainfall since the 1990s, driven by a rapid intensification of extreme precipitation events rather than an extension of the rainy season\.Drought indices, including the Standardized Precipitation Index \(SPI\) and Standardized Soil Moisture Index \(SSI\), highlight a spatial paradox in which southern Senegal has experienced a persistent degradation of its water regime, while the north has shown relative stability despite the return of rainfall\.Regarding soil moisture dynamics from 1981 to 2022, the SSI\-12 and Soil Moisture Percentile \(SMP\) indicate a progressive northward intensification of areas with high soil moisture over the past two decades\.However, this trend is characterised by decadal\-scale spatial variability and localized drought signatures in central\-western Senegal\.Future projections indicate a generalized intensification of climate extremes, with pronounced spatial asymmetry\.Under the sustainable SSP1\-2\.6 scenario, early increases in extreme precipitation are concentrated in the south, reaching up to \+15\.8% for RX1day and \+16\.7% for R99p, before shifting towards the semi\-arid northern zones in the medium term, where R99p increases by up to \+16%\.Under the high\-emissions SSP5\-8\.5 scenario, this spatial shift accelerates in the near term towards the north, with critical increases in RX5day \(\+16\.1%\) and R95p \(\+22\.1%\)\.By 2100, the SSP5\-8\.5 scenario projects the largest increases in extreme precipitation in northeastern Senegal, with R99p reaching \+31\.5%, highlighting an increasingly asymmetric spatial response to climate change amid increasingly scarce rainfall events\.The divergence between the two scenarios indicates that higher radiative forcing does not necessarily result in increased annual rainfall\.Instead, it widens the gap towards substantially more intense extreme precipitation events, with RX1day increasing by up to \+21\.8% by 2100, particularly in eastern and southeastern Senegal\.
