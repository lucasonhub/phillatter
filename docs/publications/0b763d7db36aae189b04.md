# Reimagining the African School in the Post\-COVID\-19 Era: Education, Technology, Culture and Curriculum Change

**Autores:** Wycliffe Osabwa

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The COVID\-19 pandemic disrupted education systems globally and exposed significant vulnerabilities in Africa’s predominantly face\-to\-face model of schooling\. This paper analyses the implications of the pandemic for the future of education in Africa, with particular attention to technology, culture, communalism, curriculum, and the changing conception of the school\. It argues that the disruption of conventional schooling revealed not only infrastructural and technological limitations but also deeply embedded cultural assumptions concerning where, how, and by whom learning should occur\. In many African contexts, formal education has historically been associated with physical school environments, while the home has largely been regarded as a space for domestic, social, and economic activities rather than formal learning\. Consequently, attempts to shift learning from schools to homes during the pandemic encountered technological, socioeconomic, pedagogical, and cultural barriers\. The paper nevertheless contends that these challenges should not be interpreted as evidence that African learners are inherently resistant to technology or online education\. Rather, they demonstrate the need for deliberate preparation, equitable infrastructure, digital literacy, appropriate curriculum design, teacher retooling, and culturally responsive technological integration\. Drawing on African communalist orientations, the paper argues that completely virtual education may inadequately address the relational and collaborative dimensions of learning valued in many African societies\. A blended model that combines face\-to\-face interaction with online learning is therefore proposed as a more contextually appropriate direction\. The paper further advocates an essentialist orientation to curriculum, emphasising foundational knowledge, digital literacy, adaptability, lifelong learning, and competencies relevant to uncertain futures\. Ultimately, COVID\-19 is presented as a catalyst for reimagining the African school rather than as the sole cause of educational transformation\. The emerging school should be technologically enabled, culturally responsive, globally connected, inclusive, adaptable, and capable of preserving the human relationships that remain central to meaningful education\. Indeed, the paper aims to foment a conversation around education technology, contributing to the ongoing global discussions on the place of technology in education and the phenomenon of digital dehumanisation\.
