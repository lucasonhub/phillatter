# Case Report: Dental management of an 8\-year\-old child with alternating hemiplegia of childhood under general anesthesia: clinical challenges and multidisciplinary considerations

**Autores:** Asha Byju Thomas; Sandra Maria Enchackal; Shailesh Ramdas Shenoy; Manjula Sudhakar Rao; Sundeep Hegde K

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Alternating Hemiplegia of Childhood \(AHC\) is an ultra\-rare neurodevelopmental disorder caused by pathogenic ATP1A3 variants, characterized by recurrent hemiplegic episodes, dystonia, autonomic dysfunction, and intellectual disability\. The dental environment represents a recognized trigger for paroxysmal episodes, rendering routine outpatient care unfeasible in affected children\. Case description An 8\-year\-old male with a confirmed de novo ATP1A3 variant presented with extensive dental disease including a grossly decayed tooth 26, multiple carious lesions, retained root stumps, and generalized poor oral hygiene\. Comprehensive dental rehabilitation was performed under general anesthesia following multidisciplinary fitness clearance\. Clinical management and outcome Total intravenous anesthesia with propofol and dexmedetomidine was employed, with deliberate avoidance of succinylcholine and volatile agents; rocuronium and prophylactic levetiracetam were used perioperatively\. Extractions, restorations, and scaling were completed without intraoperative or postoperative hemiplegic, seizure, or autonomic events\. The patient recovered uneventfully following 24\-hour PICU observation\. Conclusions In this child with genetically confirmed AHC, an individualised, multidisciplinary total\-intravenous\-anaesthesia protocol allowed comprehensive dental rehabilitation to be completed without hemiplegic, seizure, or autonomic complications\. This single case suggests that such a protocol can be safe in AHC, although the disease burden observed underscores the need for earlier preventive dental engagement and caregiver education in this population\.
