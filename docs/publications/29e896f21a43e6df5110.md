# Urinary oxalate excretion in astronauts during long‐duration spaceflight: an exploratory analysis

**Autores:** Juliana Villanueva\-Congote; Stephanie Smith; Nathaniel F\. Hansen; Raeann M\. Dalton; Evelyn James; Jay C\. Buckey; Daniel Arthur Wollin; David W\. Sobel

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OBJECTIVES: To explore the relationship between diet, mission phase, urinary calcium, and urinary oxalate during long\-duration spaceflight\. SUBJECTS AND METHODS: Dietary intake of calcium and oxalate and 24\-h urine parameters from 12 astronauts during long\-duration spaceflight while consuming controlled diets were analysed\. Mixed\-effects models assessed differences in urinary calcium and oxalate levels, adjusting for daily intake of calcium and oxalate, and for in\-flight status relative to pre\-flight status\. RESULTS: From pre\-flight to in\-flight, median urinary calcium increased from 178\.9 to 270\.3 mg/day \(P &lt; 0\.01\) and urinary oxalate from 37\.9 to 42\.3 mg/day \(P &lt; 0\.05\)\. Post\-flight, urinary calcium decreased to 185\.8 mg/day \(P &lt; 0\.01\) and urinary oxalate to 31\.5 mg/day \(P &lt; 0\.01\), relative to in\-flight values\. Dietary oxalate remained similar pre\-flight and in\-flight \(382\.0 vs 339\.4 mg/day; P ≥ 0\.05\) but decreased post\-flight to 241\.6 mg/day \(P &lt; 0\.01\)\. Adjusted in\-flight urinary oxalate was 10\.71 mg/day higher than pre\-flight and increased by 0\.050 mg/day per 1\-mg/day increase in dietary oxalate \(95% confidence interval 0\.036\-0\.064; P &lt; 0\.001\); dietary calcium was not associated with urinary oxalate \(P ≥ 0\.05\)\. CONCLUSIONS: Hyperoxaluria was observed during long\-duration spaceflights and persisted throughout the in\-flight phase under controlled dietary conditions\. Although high dietary oxalate was the strongest predictor, evidence of inter\-individual susceptibility was observed, with some astronauts exhibiting elevated oxalate pre\-flight levels, which increased further during flight\. Assessment of oxalate content in current astronaut food systems, mitigation of dietary oxalate load through timed calcium intake, and further investigation of microgravity\-related effects on oxalate absorption and metabolism are required\.
