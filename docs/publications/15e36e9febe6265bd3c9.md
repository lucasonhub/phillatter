# Phenotyping of the post\-traumatic immune response and therapeutic strategies in a refined murine two\-hit trauma model

**Autores:** Christina Sophia Schwenk

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Trauma and its consequences are one of the leading causes of death among young patients\. The reasons for mortality vary depending on when they occur\. Especially in the later stages of care \(days to weeks after the trauma\), death is often caused by immunological complications\. The two\-hit hypothesis offers an explanation for this\. It classifies the initial trauma as the “first hit” for the immune system, triggering a systemic immune response\. Hereby, the systemic inflammatory response \(SIRS\) and its compensatory mechanism \(CARS\) stand in a fragile balance\. This is significantly influenced by regulatory T cells \(CD4\+ Tregs\) and the interplay between immune cells and platelets\. This delicate balance can be destabilized by “second hits” occurring during trauma therapy \(e\.g\. surgery, reperfusion events, or ICU stay\)\. This promotes complications, with sepsis being one of the most dangerous in this context\. Sepsis is a complex syndrome that includes different immunological phenotypes\. It is currently defined as life\-threatening multiple organ failure based on an infection\-triggered, misdirected systemic immune response\. The treatment of immunological trauma consequences is presently \(2026\) based on a symptomatic approach, as despite extensive research, no causal treatment options have yet been successfully applied and approved in humans\. For this reason, there is still a great need for research to gain a better understanding of the immunological consequences of trauma and thus be able to test targeted causal immunomodulatory treatment options\. This study aimed to model the post\-traumatic immune response, including the development of sepsis, by using a murine two\-hit trauma model, to further investigate it and to examine immunomodulators as potential treatment options\. The animal model consisted of a standardized 3rd\-degree scalding of 20 % of the body surface, followed by sepsis induction via Cecal Ligation and Puncture \(CLP\)\. Due to its classification as a severely stressful animal experiment, the model was optimized in accordance with the 3R principles to minimize stress on the mice\. As promising immunomodulators, the fibrinogen\-splitting enzyme ancrod and the versatile compound curcumin, combined with piperine \(C\+P\), were tested\. It was demonstrated that C\+P significantly improves survival in the two\-hit model\. No corresponding advantage was observed for ancrod compared to the untreated control group\. The comprehensive immunomodulatory properties of C\+P therapy may explain the improved survival rate\. It was shown that C\+P, among other things, influences various T lymphocytes and platelets\. CD4\+ Tregs appeared to be primed for further activation, while pro\-inflammatory signals were reduced by downregulation of CD8\+ T cell and platelet activity\. In addition, the release of predominantly anti\-inflammatory cytokines was increased \(e\.g\. IL\-10\), while some pro\-inflammatory cytokines were found to be diminished \(e\.g\. IL\-6\)\. Under the influence of C\+P, end\-organ damage was also reduced\. Overall, the impression is that C\+P may be able to stabilize the post\-traumatic immune response and thus possibly contribute to maintaining the balance between SIRS and CARS\. With the refined protocol in hand, the two\-hit burn/CLP model was successfully applied to provide initial insight into the therapeutic immunomodulatory potential of C\+P in trauma treatment\. The exact mechanisms and causal relationships remain unclear and shall therefore become the subject of further research\.
