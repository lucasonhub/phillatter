# Flavonoids: Mechanisms and Therapeutic Potential in Multitarget Intervention forChronic Neuroinflammation

**Autores:** Ziying Zhang; Tian Yang; Shuxiang Li; Mengyao Lyu; Xi Cui; Changwu Yue; Xingkun Ou; Yuhong Lyu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Inflammation is a defensive response of the body to external stimuli\. However, excessive or dysregulated inflammatory responses contribute to various chronic diseases, with neurodegenerative disorders accounting for approximately 30% of the global disease burden\. Flavonoids are natural polyphenolic compounds widely distributed in plants\. Unlike conventional anti\-inflammatory drugs, which tend to induce drug resistance and cause severe side effects, these natural dietary components exhibit low toxicity, offering a safer alternative for the intervention of chronic inflammatory diseases, with particularly notable advantages in long\-term therapeutic applications\. Accumulating evidence indicates that they exert synergistic anti\-inflammatory effects through multiple targets and pathways\. In neuroinflammatory diseases, the interventional effects of these natural compounds are particularly prominent: nobiletin inhibits β\-Amyloid \(Aβ\) aggregation via the Toll\-like receptor 4/nuclear factor\-κB \(TLR4/NF\-κB\) pathway to ameliorate Alzheimer’s disease \(AD\) pathology; baicalin alleviates neuroinflammation through the Nuclear factor erythroid 2\-related factor 2\-NOD\-like receptor thermal protein domain associated protein 3 \(Nrf2\-NLRP3\) axis to protect dopaminergic neurons in Parkinson’s disease \(PD\) ; luteolin reduces mutant huntingtin protein aggregation to mitigate the progression of Huntington’s disease \(HD\) ; quercetin activates the Sestrin2/adenosine monophosphate\-activated protein kinase/sirtuin 1 \(Sestrin2/AMPK/SIRT1\) pathway to delay motor neuron degeneration in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis \(ALS\) \. Additionally, this review further analyzes the key challenges in the clinical translation of flavonoids, including insufficient blood\-brain barrier penetration, limited bioavailability in vivo, and limitations of experimental models\. To address these challenges, this review focuses on food science\-oriented strategies, emphasizing two core directions: the construction of nanodelivery systems and molecular structural modification\. while integrating advanced approaches such as humanized models, aiming to provide a theoretical basis and translational pathway for the clinical application of flavonoids in neuroinflammatory intervention\.
