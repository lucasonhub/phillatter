# Chronic disease count and fall\-related characteristics among community\-dwelling older adults: a cross\-sectional study of recurrent falls and fall timing

**Autores:** Yu Chen; Junliang Zhou; Yuanyuan Cai; Jierong Li

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Chronic conditions and falls commonly co\-occur in older populations\. This study described fall occurrence, recurrent falls, and participant\-level fall timing across chronic disease count groups in a single\-city community sample\. Methods This cross\-sectional survey included 610 community\-dwelling adults aged ≥60 years in Neijiang, Sichuan, China\. Self\-reported falls during the preceding 12 months were classified by occurrence and frequency; recurrent falls were defined as ≥2 falls\. One participant\-level fall\-timing category was analyzed per faller\. Chronic disease count was an unweighted sum of seven disease domains\. Crude and minimally adjusted logistic regression described count associations\. Disease\-specific crude associations, post\-LASSO models, and an equal\-duration morning\-versus\-afternoon comparison were exploratory analyses\. Results Overall, 303 participants \(49\.7%\) reported falls and 96 \(15\.7%\) reported recurrent falls\. Fall occurrence was non\-monotonic across count groups \(52\.9%, 42\.2%, 47\.8%, 56\.6%, and 68\.6% for 0, 1, 2, 3, and ≥4 diseases\); corresponding recurrent\-fall proportions were 9\.6%, 12\.9%, 16\.5%, 19\.7%, and 37\.3%\. The minimally adjusted OR for recurrent falls in the ≥4 group versus 0 was 4\.85 \(95% CI 2\.06–11\.46\)\. The five\-category timing distribution did not show clear evidence of an overall difference \(Pearson p = 0\.195\)\. The morning/non\-morning contrast had p = 0\.024, and the equal\-duration comparison included 203 fallers \(108 morning and 95 afternoon; p = 0\.011\)\. Disease\-specific estimates varied across diseases and outcomes\. Post\-LASSO findings remained exploratory\. Conclusion Fall\-related characteristics differed across chronic disease count groups in this Neijiang sample, particularly with respect to recurrent falls\. Timing differences depended on the comparison used\. These cross\-sectional findings are hypothesis\-generating and do not establish temporal or causal relationships\. Prospective, multicenter studies are required\.
