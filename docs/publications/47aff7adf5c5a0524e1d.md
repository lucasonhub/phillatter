# SOCIO\-CULTURAL DETERMINANTS OF LEXICAL INNOVATION: AN ETYMOLOGICAL ANALYSIS

**Autores:** Olesia Stoika; S\.M\. Kucheriava

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

With the advent of the computer and the dawn of the digital age, access to information has become easier\. People are now able to easily share experiences and integrate foreign loanwords into their own sociocultural space\. The development of modern languages is inseparable from socio\-cultural processes, which significantly influence lexical change and borrow\- ing\. Every major change in society can be easily traced back to linguistic changes\. Technological progress, globalization, and intercultural communication introduce new concepts into people&\#x27;s everyday lives\. As a result, languages need new ways to describe these changes\. This results in a major issue that linguists and translators face\. It is they who primarily take responsibility of normalizing linguistic units\. However, there is no one system that would make it possible\. That is the reason why it is essential to comprehend the factors that influence the emergence of lexical innovations and the patterns governing their occurrence\. Scholars such as William Labov and James Armstrong provide important insights into language change and the nature of lexical innovation\. David Crystal examines the relationship between language and technological development, par\- ticularly in the context of the Internet and digital communication\. Yu\. Savina and Yu\. Laskava focus on the function\- ing of borrowings and the external and internal factors that influence their emergence\. The works of Einar Haugen and Peter Burke are relevant to the study of language contact and the role of social, cultural, and historical interaction in linguistic change\. Translation\-related aspects of lexical innovation are addressed in the studies of Lawrence Venuti and Jean\-Paul Vinay and Jean Darbelnet, whose works help explain how new lexical units and culture\-specific concepts are transferred and adapted across languages\. This article examines the socio\-cultural determinants that influence the emergence and development of lexical inno\- vations\. The main focus is on such factors as technological progress, economic and trade relations, political and historical events, and cultural exchange\. The study also considers how these external changes interact with internal linguistic needs\. These include the principle of language economy and the naming of new concepts\. The analysis reveals that lexical inno\- vation is closely connected with changes in social practices, technological development, cultural interaction, and historical circumstances, which affect both the formation of new lexical units and the ways they are translated and integrated into another language\. The study employs a qualitative approach based on etymological, comparative, and contextual analysis of selected lexical innovations\. Particular attention is drawn to the role of translation in the spread of the new lexicon\. By examining the etymology and development of new words, the study aims to show how changes in society and culture are reflected in vocabulary and how lexical innovations become part of the language over time\.
