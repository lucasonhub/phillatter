# Rapid Serum IgG Reduction as a Potential Indicator of Short\-Term Clinical Improvement in AChR\-Ab\-Positive Generalized Myasthenia Gravis: A Post Hoc Analysis of a Phase III Batoclimab Trial

**Autores:** Zhijun Li; Qing Ke; 奚剑英; Yaoxian Yue; Yuzhou Guan; Ruisheng Duan; Qun Xue; Haishan Jiang; Fei Xiao; Huan Yang; Ting Chang; Zhangyu Zou; Haifeng Li; Song Tan; Haibing Xiao; Hongyu Zhou; Hua Zhang; Qiang Meng; Wenyu Li; Wei Li; Junhong Guo; Yali Zhang; 李尊波; 涂江龙; Jianquan Shi; Wei Li; Chongbo Zhao; Shuai Zhao; Chen Zong; Hui Deng; Bitao Bu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

INTRODUCTION: Generalized myasthenia gravis \(gMG\) is an autoimmune disorder often associated with anti\-acetylcholine receptor antibodies \(AChR\-Abs\)\. Identifying short\-term predictors or indicators of clinical improvement is crucial for optimizing treatment strategies\. METHODS: This post hoc analysis utilized data from a phase III, multicenter randomized controlled trial enrolling 65 patients with AChR\-Ab\-positive gMG across 27 centers in China\. We evaluated serum immunoglobulin G \(IgG\) as a potential short\-term indicator of clinical improvement in patients treated with batoclimab\. Clinical severity was evaluated using the Myasthenia Gravis Activities of Daily Living \(MG\-ADL\) and Quantitative Myasthenia Gravis \(QMG\) scales\. Spearman correlation analyses, mixed\-effects models, and multivariate analyses were performed to assess relations between biomarker changes and symptom improvement\. RESULTS: Within\-patient Spearman correlation showed significant associations between reductions in IgG or AChR\-Ab levels and symptom improvement \(ΔIgG vs\. MG\-ADL change: r = 0\.372, p &lt; 0\.001; ΔIgG vs\. QMG change: r = 0\.492, p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Mixed\-effects modeling indicated that an 80% reduction in IgG corresponded to a 59% probability of MG\-ADL improvement by ≥ 3 points and a 77% probability of QMG improvement by ≥ 3 points\. High baseline body mass index \(BMI\) was identified as an independent risk factor for slower IgG decline\. CONCLUSION: Batoclimab rapidly reduced IgG and pathogenic AChR\-Ab levels, leading to improved clinical outcomes in patients with gMG\. The reduction rate in total IgG may serve as a valuable short\-term indicator of clinical response in patients with AChR\-Ab\-positive gMG following neonatal fragment crystallizable receptor antagonist therapy\. TRIAL REGISTRATION: ClinicalTrials\.gov Identifier NCT05039190\.
