# ML\-Based Intrusion Detection System with Intrusion Prevention System

**Autores:** Prathysh N; Rajesh H S; Purushothama M; Chandrashekar Y; Syeda Ayesha Unisa

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper presented a hybrid rule\-based and machine\-learning system that intercepts navigation at the browser and blocks phishing, malware, ransomware, and DGA\-style malicious destinations in real time, without requiring network\-level packet capture\. A Random Forest classifier trained on eight lexical and structural URL features reached 95\.83% accuracy and a 95\.52% F1\-score on a held\-out test set, at an inference cost of under 6 ms per request, well within the latency budget of a normal page navigation\. More importantly, a real false\-positive failure was found, measured, and fixed: restricting an entropy rule from the full URL to the hostname alone, combined with removing a spurious pattern from the training corpus, reduced false blocks on ordinary websites from 7 of 10 to 1 of 12 with no loss of true detections\. A live per\-attack\-type accuracy view complements the usual offline benchmark by scoring every trained algorithm against real traffic as it happens, and a per\-class confusion matrix shows the two remaining error types clearly enough to prioritize future work: at the current dataset scale, missed detections \(2 of 34\) matter more than false alarms \(1 of 38\), which argues for growing the malicious\-class training data before anything else\. Taken together, these results suggest that a browser\-level hybrid detector is a practical, lightweight complement to network\-level intrusion detection for the individual users and small deployments that flow\-based systems do not reach\.
