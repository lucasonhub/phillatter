# Morphological characteristics and spatial distribution of shallow landslides in the Qinling\-Daba Mountains, China

**Autores:** Dingchao Qi; Wen Fan; Deng Longsheng; Yufei Song; Jun Li; Yanbo Cao; Yiwei Ji

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Shallow landslides dominate the hazard inventory of the Qinba Mountains, China, yet their regional\-scale characteristics and controlling factors remain poorly understood\. Based on 4,315 historical shallow landslides \(1998–2022\), this study integrates field measurements, GIS\-based spatial analysis, and statistical methods to investigate landslide morphology, spatial distribution, and conditioning factors\. Methodologically, we used variable\-specific correlation analyses \(Spearman, Cramér’s V , Kruskal–Wallis H tests\) and multi\-buffer processing for linear features \(faults, rivers, roads\)\. Results reveal: \(1\) Power law scaling in area\-volume \( V = 2\.27 A 1\.034 \) and size\-frequency distribution \(exponent 2\.27\), confirming self\-organized criticality of the landslide system\. \(2\) Regional differentiation: the Hanjiang River basin shows a gradient from high\-density, small failures upstream to lower\-density, larger failures downstream; the Jialingjiang and the Hanjiang tributary basins are dominated by larger landslides due to competent lithologies and higher relief\. \(3\) Key controlling factors: epimetamorphic rocks, elevation &lt;1,000 m, slope &lt;30°, relief &lt;200 m, annual precipitation 800–1,100 mm, NDVI 0\.5–0\.7, population density &gt;10 persons/km 2 , and distance to roads \(&lt;500 m\), rivers \(&lt;1,800 m\) and faults \(&lt;6,000 m\)\. In general, macro\-topography, regional tectonics, and climate collaboratively define the potential locations of landslides, whereas micro\-topography, lithology, and vegetation cover govern their scale and type; Human activities change the dominant geometry of landslides to a certain extent\. \(4\) The spatial pattern of landslide in continuous variables is analyzed at a 36 km distance threshold, with mean annual precipitation exhibiting the strongest positive spatial autocorrelation \(Moran’s I = 0\.93\), and LISA maps show High\-High clusters of precipitation and coefficient of elevation variation in Ankang, while elevation shows an opposite pattern\. These findings provide a scientific basis for hazard mitigation and land\-use planning in the Qinba Mountains and offer a methodological framework for landslide susceptibility studies in other geologically similar regions\.
