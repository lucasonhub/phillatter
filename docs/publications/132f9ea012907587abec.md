# Trust in voice\-based assistants: a multidimensional conceptualization based on in\-home user interviews

**Autores:** Katharina Frehmann; Marc Ziegele

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Voice\-based assistants \(VBAs\) such as Alexa and Siri are widely used in everyday life and increasingly act as information intermediaries\. Their human\-like voices and behaviors can elicit social responses, while new assistants based on large language models \(LLMs\), such as ChatGPT voice mode, further blur the boundaries between machine and human communicators\. Despite risks associated with misinformation and limited data transparency, adoption remains high\. Trust may be relevant to users’ relationships with conversational AI, but there is little consensus on how it should be conceptualized\. Technical, interpersonal, and media trust frameworks may all be applicable\. This multi\-method qualitative study examined users’ subjective concepts of trust through 22 in\-home interviews complemented by observational tasks involving participants’ own VBAs and ChatGPT voice mode\. Participants drew on interpersonal concepts when defining trust but commonly restricted trust in VBAs to particular tasks and functions\. Technical and media\-related trustworthiness criteria were especially prominent, whereas social responses and anthropomorphic qualities did not necessarily translate into interpersonal trust\. Experience, calibrated expectations, selective use, habitual engagement, and trust were described as closely interrelated\. Although ChatGPT was often evaluated positively, particularly as an information intermediary, its perceived capabilities were not uniformly accompanied by greater trust compared with participants’ familiar VBAs\. Based on these findings, we propose a preliminary conceptual framework of task\-specific hybrid trust in VBAs\.
