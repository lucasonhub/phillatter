# How Long Is the Coast of a Neuron? Resolution\-Dependent Interaction Order and Computational Capacity

**Autores:** Nima Dehghani

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Mandlebrot&\#x27;s coastline paradox reframes measurement as a joint property of an object and its ruler\. Here we ask how observational resolution shapes the apparent structure of neural computation\. We introduce a scale\-indexed decomposition that separates the predictive information captured by direct dependencies from the residual outside that model family\. Exact constructions show that projection can convert higher\-order dependence into pairwise structure\. Projection can increase the direct fraction of surviving information even as total predictive information decreases\. A schematic branch\-to\-soma cascade grounds these effects in local coincidence detection, saturating integration and somatic mixing\. Resolving organized nonlinear subunits improves approximation and raises effective representational rank\. In a nested hierarchy, these gains occur without increasing information about the target\. This distinction separates information availability from access by a restricted readout\. A point\-neuron or direct population model therefore establishes an effective description at a specified scale\. It does not uniquely identify the finer computation that generated the observed relation\. Which resolution is adequate depends on the observable and perturbation of interest\.
