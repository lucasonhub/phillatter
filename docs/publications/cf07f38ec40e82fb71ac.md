# Reconciling Global Data\-Centre Electricity Projections for 2030: A Boundary\-Aware Evidence Map

**Autores:** Aditya Singh

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Published projections of global data\-centre electricity use are frequently compared despite differences in geography, facility and workload coverage, units, and scenario design\. Methods: We constructed a secondary\-source\-seeded, boundary\-aware evidence map of projections for annual global operational electricity use by total data centres in 2030\. Each publication was coded across eight comparability dimensions and assigned a transparent primary\-source verification status\. The primary synthesis treats publications reporting only range endpoints as interval\-censored rather than interpreting their endpoints as complete scenarios\. Publication\-level bootstrap intervals and verification, peer\-review, scenario\-completeness, aggregation, and influence sensitivities were examined\. Results: The corpus contains 28 values from 15 publications; 7 were directly checked in primary or author full text by one reviewer and 8 remain verification incomplete\. The interval\-censored model estimates a median latent publication centre of 912 TWh yr\-1 \(95% publication\-bootstrap interval 712\-1217\) and a geometric standard deviation of 1\.69\. A conventional geometric\-scenario summary gives a median of 945 TWh yr\-1 \(interquartile range 648\-1167\)\. Method, publisher, and vintage contrasts are imprecise and are retained only as exploratory sensitivities\. Conclusions: Boundary harmonisation makes the literature more interpretable but does not identify a probability distribution for realised 2030 demand\. The principal contribution is an auditable comparability taxonomy and minimum reporting standard, not a consensus forecast\.
