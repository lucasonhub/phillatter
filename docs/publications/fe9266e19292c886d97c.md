# Generative Artificial Intelligence, Pandemic Risk, and Biological Weapons: Implications for Clinical Immunology

**Autores:** Thauann Lima Silva Rufino

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The convergence of generative artificial intelligence and the life sciences expands research opportunities, but also raises concerns about its misuse in biological threats\. This article critically reviews the evidence and uncertainties regarding pandemic risk associated with the possible use of AI in biological weapons, with particular attention to clinical immunology\. A narrative and critical review of peer\-reviewed studies, institutional documents, a corporate report, and selected journalistic coverage was conducted\. The reviewed evidence supports preventive concern and structured preparedness, but it does not establish that current AI systems can independently cause a pandemic, nor does it demonstrate generalized immune evasion attributable to AI\. Immunological evidence drawn from SARS\-CoV\-2 and COVID\-19 vaccination is used only as an illustrative framework for interpreting neutralization, cellular memory, mucosal immunity, and clinical protection; it is not treated as evidence that a hypothetical engineered agent would behave similarly\. The analysis emphasizes the distinction between computational capability and biological consequence, separates literature\-supported findings from the author&\#x27;s preparedness proposal, and identifies methodological and evidentiary gaps that require direct empirical study\.
