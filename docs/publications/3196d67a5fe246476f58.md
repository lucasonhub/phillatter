# From Image Creation to Platform Accountability: A Legal and Policy Framework for AI\-Morphed Images on Social Media

**Autores:** Bikash Gortola

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper examines the growing problem of AI\-morphed images and synthetic visual content, with particular attention to non\-consensual intimate imagery, identity misuse, and the responsibilities of social media platforms\. As generative AI makes image manipulation increasingly accessible, controlling individual acts of creation becomes progressively more difficult\. This paper argues that the regulatory focus must extend beyond the creator to the platforms that distribute, amplify, and monetise such content\. It proposes a conceptual legal and policy framework centred on platform\-level accountability, including automated detection, accessible reporting mechanisms, rapid content removal, repeat\-offender sanctions, transparency, and proportionate enforcement\. Particular attention is given to the protection of women against non\-consensual sexualised imagery, while recognising the need to preserve legitimate artistic expression, satire, journalism, and freedom of speech\. The paper also examines the emerging Indian regulatory context and considers how existing intermediary obligations might be implemented and evaluated more effectively\. Its central proposition is that, when the creation of harmful content cannot be comprehensively controlled, the systems through which that content reaches the public must become a principal point of prevention, intervention, and accountability\. Research scope: This is a conceptual legal and policy research paper\. It develops a proposed regulatory framework and research questions for future empirical investigation; it does not present original empirical findings or claim that all jurisdictions apply the same legal standards\. Keywords: Artificial intelligence, AI\-morphed images, deepfakes, platform accountability, social media regulation, intermediary liability, personality rights, non\-consensual intimate imagery, content moderation, digital rights, India\.
