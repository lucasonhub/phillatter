# Designing interactive VR scenarios for speech and language therapy with the assessment framework

**Autores:** Tsvetelin Stefanov; Анна Андреева; Gergana Padareva\-Ilieva; Anna Kostadinova Lekova

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Virtual reality \(VR\) games are becoming increasingly popular for entertainment\. When integrated into speech and language therapy \(SLT\) practice, VR can be an innovative tool that supports therapeutic interventions by offering new opportunities for immersive interaction and greater motivation for individuals with language disorders to participate in therapy\. In this study, we aim to introduce a methodology for designing interactive VR serious\-game scenarios for speech and language therapy, together with an integrated multidisciplinary user\-feedback assessment framework\. The research methodology is based on design research principles and an innovative UX framework to guide the design, evaluation, and improvement process\. The feasibility and usability of the methodology and framework are demonstrated through preliminary interactive sessions involving 10 children and adolescents with language disorders \(5–13 years\) and 10 students undergoing speech and language therapy\. Their role was to evaluate the serious VR games developed in accordance with specific speech and language therapy goals\. The assessment framework consists of a four\-level VR serious game hub statistics module, phonetic and linguistic analysis of children’s and adolescents’ utterances from the serious game sessions, and usability and design questionnaires\. As a result, six VR serious games with integrated large language model \(LLM\)\-based conversational AI were designed for speech and language therapy\. Based on the outcomes, a safety protocol for conducting VR\-based therapeutic activities was proposed\. We used descriptive statistics and Friedman and Wilcoxon post hoc tests with Bonferroni\-adjusted p \-values for pairwise comparisons of questionnaires and speech and language assessment data\. While preliminary, the results of the phonetic and linguistic analyses conducted using Praat indicate that the VR serious games effectively capture and maintain children’s attention, elicit a high level of emotional expressiveness, and motivate them to communicate\. The feedback collected through the participant questionnaires showed that the VR serious games provided a highly positive user experience\. No symptoms of cybersickness or dizziness were observed during the game sessions\. Younger children interacted with the VR environment using controllers, while older individuals preferred interaction with hand gestures\. The proposed solution can be personalized to individual therapeutic needs, enabling children with language disorders to be engaged in therapy, while the conversational AI integrated into VR stimulates not only expressive development but also receptive language development\. To confirm the promising preliminary results, further evaluations with a larger group of children and longitudinal studies are required\.
