# Can Machine Learning Detect and Predict Outcomes in Multiple Sclerosis? A Review of Diagnostic and Prognostic Approaches

**Autores:** Murat Emeç

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Multiple sclerosis \(MS\) diagnosis relies on clinical presentation, magnetic resonance imaging \(MRI\), and the McDonald criteria, whereas long\-term management requires anticipating disability progression and symptom burden\.This narrative review evaluates current evidence on machine learning \(ML\) approaches for \(i\) detecting and classifying MS and \(ii\) predicting disability progression and symptom severity in patients with established MS\.Evidence was synthesized from 25 sources published between 2012 and 2026 \(23 peer\-reviewed journal publications, one arXiv preprint, and one article published in a journal outside the major biomedical indexes\), encompassing MS diagnosis \[MRI, optical coherence tomography \(OCT\), fluid biomarkers, and differential diagnosis\] and prognostic outcome prediction \(disability progression, symptom\-specific severity, and contextspecific changes in disability\)\.MRI remains the predominant data source for ML\-based MS detection: deep learning models applied to structural MRI achieved accuracies of 70\.2\-98\.8%in distinguishing MS from healthy controls, whereas conventional ML models achieved accuracies of 83\.7\-90\.0%\.A broader meta\-review of more than 100 studies reported diagnostic accuracies ranging from 61% to 97%, while differentiation of MS from neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder achieved 74\-88% accuracy, depending on data integration\.For prognostic applications, tree\-based ensemble models generally outperformed simpler regression models: a 231\-patient cohort achieved an R 2 of 0\.97 for two\-year disability score prediction, although this estimate warrants cautious interpretation because of the strong autocorrelation of baseline Expanded Disability Status Scale scores\.A 662\-woman cohort achieved 85\-88% classification accuracy for postpartum changes in disability, whereas a 23\-patient cohort achieved 92\.65% accuracy in predicting restless legs syndrome severity, with sleep quality identified as the leading predictor\.Overall, ML can effectively detect MS\-associated patterns in MRI and is increasingly being applied to OCT and fluid biomarkers\.It can also generate clinically meaningful prognostic predictions after MS diagnosis, although categorical outcome prediction appears to generalize better than continuous regression\.Dataset size, single\-center study designs, and limited external validation remain the principal barriers to clinical deployment across both domains\.Multimodal data integration and prospective multicenter validation therefore represent important priorities for future research\.
