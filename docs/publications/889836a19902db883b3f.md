# Low\-impulse slamming response and residual flexural capacity of seawater\-degraded CARALL fibre\-metal laminate marine panels

**Autores:** Sanan H\. Khan; Mohammad Kamil Khan

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The carbon–aluminium couple behind CARALL fibre\-metal laminates is galvanically active in seawater, and the metal–composite bond suffers first\. This work computes in Abaqus/Explicit what that costs a marine panel, namely a seawater\-degraded \(3/2\) CARALL plating bay under low\-impulse slamming trains, its residual flexural capacity measured after each train, with cohesive interfaces calibrated against in\-house tests of the anodised and the un\-anodised bond\. The trains span 0\.5 to 5% of one 191 Pa s design slam, the damage depends on the delivered impulse alone, and no adequacy at the full design slam is claimed\. The central finding is the failure chronology, resolved frame by frame\. The pulse primes 8\.2% of the degraded un\-anodised interface past a cohesive damage of 0\.917 yet fails none of it, the delamination forms afterwards in free vibration between 1\.05 and 2\.25 ms as the panel swings to peak curvature, and growth arrests at 15\.1% of the critical interface\. Its failed surfaces stay closed to within two micrometres, owing to which the fibre\-direction retention falls only from 0\.97 to 0\.96\. The anodised bond registers zero damage at every one of the 141 stored frames and tolerates at least twice the impulse\. Severe degradation alone removes 2\.7% of capacity before any damage forms, and anodising emerges as a damage\-tolerance measure whose benefit a stiffness survey cannot see\.
