# Reconceptualizing Justice in the Digital Age: A Jurisprudential Study of Digital Courts

**Autores:** Harshit Sharma; Parth Singh Dogra

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper discusses how judicial procedures are changing under the influence of digitalisation, focusing particularly on the creation of digital courts and placing the analysis in a wider jurisprudential context\. It also examines how the application of information and communication technologies such as e\-filing, virtual hearings and digital case management systems has transformed the way justice is delivered in India\. The paper is critical of how such technological developments can improve or jeopardise the constitutional and judicially approved fundamental legal principles, including access to justice, procedural fairness, equality and transparency, as recognised in Anita Kushwaha v\. Pushap Sudan and Swapnil Tripathi v\. Supreme Court of India\. Using an interdisciplinary approach, the paper engages with major theories of jurisprudence, such as legal positivism, natural law, sociological jurisprudence and legal realism, based on the writings of H\.L\.A\. Hart, Lon L\. Fuller, Ronald Dworkin and Roscoe Pound\. It also considers the consequences of new technologies like artificial intelligence in court decisions\. The paper finds that, although digital courts are much more efficient and more accessible, they also provoke challenges of digital exclusion and loss of procedural protections\. It concludes that digital justice can only be legitimate when technological innovation is balanced with constitutional and jurisprudential norms so as to secure an inclusive and fair legal system\.
