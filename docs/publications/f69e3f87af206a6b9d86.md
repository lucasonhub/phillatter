# Empathetic responses by AI legal assistants and user over\-disclosure: evidence from an online survey experiment

**Autores:** 思媛 王

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Generative AI legal assistants rely on users to describe events, evidence, and transaction histories, yet the same accounts may contain private information that is unnecessary for legal guidance\. Empathetic language may affect how selectively users decide what to provide\. Methods A one\-factor between\-subjects online survey experiment on Wenjuanxing randomly assigned 360 participants to rational, mildly empathetic, or highly empathetic AI legal responses \(n = 120 per condition\)\. Participants completed 1\-5 Likert\-type measures of parasocial response, privacy boundary judgment, and over\-disclosure intention, together with related relational measures, and then answered an open\-ended disclosure task\. Results The empathy manipulation was successful, F \(2,357\) = 524\.170, p &lt; 0\.001, and η 2 = 0\.746\. Stronger empathetic responses increased parasocial response \(η 2 = 0\.428\), reduced privacy boundary judgment \(η 2 = 0\.242\), and increased over\-disclosure intention \(η 2 = 0\.406\)\. The coded non\-essential disclosure score rose from 0\.994 in the rational condition to 6\.132 in the highly empathetic condition, F \(2,357\) = 170\.795, p &lt; 0\.001, and η 2 = 0\.489\. Necessary case facts remained stable, F \(2,357\) = 0\.245, p = 0\.783\. Bootstrap analysis yielded an indirect effect through parasocial response of 0\.214, 95% CI \[0\.151, 0\.283\], and a sequential indirect effect through parasocial response and privacy boundary judgment of 0\.063, 95% CI \[0\.033, 0\.098\]\. Discussion Within this consumer\-dispute scenario, stronger empathetic cues reduced users&\#x27; selectivity about which information was appropriate to provide\. Data\-minimization prompts and evidence\-oriented questioning may help retain limited emotional support without encouraging unnecessary disclosure\.
