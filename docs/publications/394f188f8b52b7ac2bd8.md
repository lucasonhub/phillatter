# Failure Analysis, Maintenance Strategies and Models for Enhancing Reliability Performance of Hospital HVAC Systems: A Review

**Autores:** Emmanuel Remmy Lyimo; Christopher T\. Mgonja

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Heating, ventilation, and air\-conditioning \(HVAC\) systems are among the most safety\-critical infrastructure in hospitals\. Beyond thermal comfort, they govern airborne infection control, surgical\-suite sterility, isolation\-room pressurisation, and the environmental stability of pharmacies, laboratories, and intensive\-care units\. Because these systems must operate continuously and to tight tolerances, their reliability is inseparable from patient safety and regulatory compliance\. Yet the majority of healthcare facilities still rely on reactive or fixed\-interval preventive maintenance, which leaves them exposed to unplanned failures, emergency repairs, and compliance drift\. Predictive maintenance \(PdM\)\-condition\-based intervention driven by real\-time sensor data, reliability analytics, and machine learning\-offers a route to intervene before functional failure occurs\. This review synthesises the literature on hospital HVAC reliability, the evolution of maintenance strategies, reliability\-engineering foundations such as reliability\-centred maintenance, failure mode and effects analysis, and remaining\-useful\-life modelling, and enabling technologies including the Internet of Things, digital twins, and data\-driven prognostics\. Building on this synthesis, the paper proposes a conceptual predictive\-maintenance management model that integrates asset criticality analysis, condition monitoring, prognostic decision\-making, and computerised\-maintenance\-management\-system and building\-management\-system workflows into a governance\-aware framework tailored to the healthcare context\. Reported outcomes from adjacent domains, including substantial reductions in maintenance cost and emergency calls, extended mean time between failures, and improved uptime, suggest that a well\-governed predictive\-maintenance model can materially enhance reliability performance\. The review closes by identifying persistent research gaps, including the scarcity of validated hospital\-specific datasets, weak coupling between prognostic outputs and maintenance governance, interpretability limits of opaque models, and cybersecurity and change\-management barriers, and outlines a research agenda for their resolution\.
