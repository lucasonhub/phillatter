# An advanced deep learning framework for mineral grain identification

**Autores:** Julien Maítre; Arnaud L\. Back; Kévin Bouchard; Léo Paul Bédard

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Automatic identification of mineral grains from unconsolidated sediments is a critical step in mineral exploration, environmental assessment, and engineering geology, yet it remains a time\-consuming and error\-prone task when performed manually\. This paper presents an end\-to\-end deep learning framework that addresses both the segmentation and classification of individual mineral grains from high\-resolution photomicrographic mosaics acquired under plane\-polarized Annular Light \(AL\)\. Grain isolation is achieved through the Segment Anything Model 2 \(SAM 2\), combined with a set of geometric filtering rules designed to eliminate incorrect and redundant segments\. Individual grain images are subsequently classified using six pre\-trained architectures \(VGG\-19 with batch normalization, ResNet\-101 and ResNet\-152, DenseNet\-161, ResNeXt\-101\-32x8d, and ViT\-B\-16\) adapted to our task via transfer learning and fine\-tuning\. The dataset comprises 8 654 grain images covering 24 mineral species, representing, to the best of our knowledge, the largest number of mineral classes addressed in a single deep learning study in this field\. Among the evaluated architectures, fine\-tuned ViT\-B\-16 achieves the highest mean grain\-level classification accuracy \(0\.954 ± 0\.006\), under the individual\-grain partitioning protocol, significantly outperforming all five CNN\-based architectures in paired statistical comparisons \(Holm\-adjusted p &lt; 0\.01 \), while achieving a top\-3 accuracy of 0\.981 ± 0\.005\. An independently evaluated mixed\-mineral mosaic containing 170 reference grains was additionally used to assess heterogeneous\-input performance\. Of these grains, 154 were retained for classification\. Conditional classification accuracy on the retained grains was 0\.734 ± 0\.032, while the strict end\-to\-end identification rate relative to all 170 reference grains was 0\.665 ± 0\.029\. These results demonstrate strong classification performance for held\-out grains under the controlled acquisition conditions of the present study, while the lower mixed\-mosaic end\-to\-end performance highlights the additional difficulty of heterogeneous\-input processing\. Broader generalization to independent physical samples remains to be evaluated\.
