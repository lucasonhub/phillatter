# An interventional study on enhancing print awareness in senior kindergarten children through gamified picture book teaching

**Autores:** Jun Zhang; Jiakuo Liu; Yaoyao Xu; Shangqing Yuan; Tie Sun; Deqiang Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Print awareness, encompassing children&\#x27;s understanding of the functional, formal, and rule\-based aspects of written language, is a critical precursor to early literacy\. Despite growing interest in picture\-book interventions, few studies have systematically examined classroom\-based gamified approaches to enhancing print awareness among Chinese kindergarten children\. This study investigated the effects of a six\-week gamified picture book intervention on print awareness within the Chinese logographic writing system\. Fifty senior kindergarten children \(aged 5–6 years\) from two intact classes in an urban kindergarten in China participated in the study\. Using cluster randomization at the classroom level, one class was assigned to the experimental group \( n = 25\) and the other to the control group \( n = 25\)\. Children in the experimental group participated in weekly gamified picture\-book activities, whereas those in the control group received regular picture\-book instruction\. Print awareness was assessed before and after the intervention across three dimensions: Print Function, Print Form, and Print Rules\. Mixed ANOVA revealed significant main effects of Time for all three dimensions, indicating that children in both groups improved from pre\-test to post\-test\. Significant main effects of Group were observed for Print Function, F\(1, 48\) = 4\.68, p = 0\.033, η p 2 = 0\.04, and Print Form, F\(1, 48\) = 4\.32, p = 0\.040, η p 2 = 0\.04, with the experimental group showing higher overall scores than the control group\. No significant group difference was found for Print Rules, and no significant Time × Group interactions were observed for any dimension\. These findings suggest that gamified picture\-book teaching may provide additional support for children&\#x27;s Print Function and Print Form awareness, while the observed improvements over time likely reflect general developmental progress rather than accelerated growth resulting from the intervention\. The study highlights that different dimensions of print awareness may respond differently to classroom instruction and provides practical implications for designing developmentally appropriate, gamified early literacy activities within Chinese kindergarten settings\.
