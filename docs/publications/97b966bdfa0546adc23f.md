# A One Health data portal: linking research community, data, and modelling—a qualitative case study in Canada

**Autores:** Yuliya Bodryzlova; Elda Kokoè Elolo Laison; Tanya Philippsen; Kamal Raj Acharya; Jane Parmley; Bouchra Nasri

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background One Health and emerging infectious disease modelling research requires data from multiple sources and disciplines\. In theory, recent recommendations on data portal and modelling development may help to resolve these issues; in practice, these recommendations may not align with the priorities of data users\.Objective We seek to obtain an in\-depth understanding of the expectations and needs of researchers from the Canadian One Health research network regarding the One Health data portal at the future development stage\.Methods A qualitative case study was conducted using focus groups and a content analysis approach\. Potential participants were invited through the One Health research network OMNI\-RÉUNIS; 14 individuals participated\. Two focus groups were held online, each lasting roughly 120 minutes\.Results The major themes went beyond technical characteristics of the portal and included mostly procedural and human aspects of the data use, such as: \(1\) data access, source credibility, and data quality; \(2\) data stewardship and curation; \(3\) portal’s usability; \(4\) collaboration; and \(5\) ethical issues\. Minor themes focused on: \(1\) a need for contextual variables; \(2\) interoperability, findability, and reusability of the data; and \(3\) support of modelling\.Conclusion Considering users’ needs and expectations prior to data portal design may improve their data utilization experience, thus contributing to One Health knowledge and modelling advancement in Canada\.
