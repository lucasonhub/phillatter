# Invisible burdens: reframing maternal mental health as a core priority in conflict\-affected and low\-resource settings

**Autores:** Ilyas Abdullahi Khalif; Yusuf Abdullahi Hubow; Ahmed Mohamed Omar; Nour Ahmed Dahir; Aniso Mohamed Abdi

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Maternal mental health remains an overlooked dimension of global women&\#x27;s health, particularly in conflict\-affected and low\-resource settings where psychological distress is intensified by poverty, displacement, violence, gender inequality, and fragile health systems\. Although maternal health agendas have achieved progress in reducing mortality, survival\-focused approaches often fail to capture the psychological burdens that shape women&\#x27;s well\-being, caregiving capacity, and intergenerational outcomes\. This Perspective argues that maternal mental health should be reframed as a core component of maternal health rather than a secondary concern\. Drawing on a life\-course and gender\-responsive perspective, we propose a conceptual framework linking conflict and structural inequality with social determinants, maternal psychological outcomes, and consequences for mothers and children\. We highlight the need for integrated health\-system responses that combine mental health screening, psychosocial support, community\-based care, and gender\-responsive policies to achieve equitable and sustainable improvements in women&\#x27;s health\.
