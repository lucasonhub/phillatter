# FRACTAL: A Self\-Similar Multi\-Agent Orchestration Architecture with Formal Communication, Convergence, and Depth Bounds

**Autores:** Ohad Dan; Abhinav Taliyan

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

We present FRACTAL, a self\-similar multi\-agent architecture for recursive goal decomposition, in which the same five\-component organizational pattern \-\- Orchestrator, Ensemble, Knowledge Ledger, Decision Maker, Meta\-Observer \-\- recurs at every level of a decomposition tree\. We make four formal contributions: \(1\) a communication bound showing intra\-node cost is linear in ensemble size and round count \(O\(kr\)\) under a two\-phase blind\-proposal/stress\-test protocol, versus quadratic cost for free\-form debate, with an explicit, stated assumption on fixed evaluator count; \(2\) a two\-mode orchestration criterion separating generative and evaluative phases via a measurable diversity\-saturation threshold; \(3\) a supermajority convergence guarantee with explicit probability bounds at stated agreement levels, and a formal depth bound under an operationalized goal\-complexity measure; \(4\) a Bayesian inter\-layer escalation mechanism unifying hysteresis and contract\-breach signaling under one posterior update\. We illustrate the architecture&\#x27;s execution qualitatively on a worked example and a multi\-layer case study; we do not report quantitative performance claims in this paper, and we explicitly discuss the formal and inductive\-bias limitations of the model, including an unimplemented but architecture\-compatible direction toward bounded self\-improvement\. We use &quot;fractal&quot; as an architectural metaphor for self\-similarity, not a measured mathematical property\. Empirical validation of the architecture&\#x27;s comparative performance is left to future work\.
