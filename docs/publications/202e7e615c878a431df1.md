# ACCURACY OF DUAL\-ENERGY COMPUTED TOMOGRAPHY FOR DETECTING URIC ACID URINARY CALCULI VERSUS CONVENTIONAL COMPUTED TOMOGRAPHY

**Autores:** Gabriel Medeiros Rodrigues; Samuel Bento Guimarães; Anne Katlen Damasceno Uchoa Thome; João Vitor Sanches Cavalcante; Luiz Fernando Filgueiras Rezende; Sílvia Portela Borges

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Noncontrast computed tomography is highly effective for detecting urinary calculi, but conventional single\-energy attenuation values overlap among compositions and cannot consistently identify uric acid\. Dual\-energy computed tomography exploits the energy\-dependent attenuation of materials to classify calculi as uric\-acid or non\-uric\-acid, potentially enabling immediate selection of urinary alkalinization and avoidance of unnecessary intervention\. This structured diagnostic\-accuracy review compares dual\-energy and conventional computed tomography, emphasizing chemical stone analysis as the preferred reference standard, direct comparative evidence, low\-dose performance, and clinically important failure modes\. Early phantom and ex vivo investigations demonstrated near\-complete separation of uric\-acid from non\-uric\-acid stones under favorable conditions\. Prospective and retrospective in vivo studies subsequently reported high specificity, with failures concentrated in very small stones, large body habitus, noisy reduced\-dose acquisitions, mixed composition, and incompletely mapped calculi\. A 2020 systematic review and bivariate meta\-analysis of 21 studies found mean sensitivity of 0\.88 and specificity of 0\.98 for uric\-acid\-dominant stones; performance for any uric\-acid\-containing stone was lower, with sensitivity of 0\.82 and specificity of 0\.97\. Direct studies favored dual\-energy over conventional computed tomography: one prospective in vitro comparison correctly classified 74% versus 52% of stones overall and 93% versus 40% of non\-uric\-acid stones in the binary uric\-acid distinction, while an in vivo comparison reported 90% versus 52% correct composition assessment\. Conventional computed tomography can perform well when mean, maximum, distributional attenuation features and urine pH are combined, but thresholds are scanner\-, protocol\-, size\-, and reconstruction\-dependent; some modern single\-energy algorithms have been validated against vendor dual\-energy classification rather than chemical analysis, which limits claims of equivalence\. Radiation exposure with contemporary low\-dose dual\-energy protocols can approach routine stone computed tomography, although dose reduction increases unclassified small calculi\. Dual\-energy computed tomography is therefore the best\-established noninvasive method for uric\-acid composition assessment when the result will change management, but it should complement rather than replace retrieved\-stone analysis whenever material is available\.
