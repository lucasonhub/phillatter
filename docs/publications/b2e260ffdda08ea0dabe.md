# Human rights to water and sanitation at COP30: a comparative media analysis from a climate\-vulnerable host city

**Autores:** Davi Madureira Victral; Bruna Silveira de Oliveira

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Climate change is placing increasing pressure on urban water and sanitation systems, particularly in cities characterized by persistent socioeconomic inequalities\. As host of the 30th United Nations Climate Change Conference \(COP30\), Belém, Brazil, became a focal point for debates on climate adaptation while simultaneously exposing longstanding deficiencies in essential urban infrastructure\. This study examines how water and sanitation were represented in media coverage of COP30 through the analytical lens of the Human Rights to Water and Sanitation \(HRtWS\)\. A mixed\-methods design combining quantitative descriptive analysis and qualitative thematic content analysis was applied to 143 news articles retrieved from the Google News RSS feed\. Data were collected on 10 March 2026, approximately 4 months after the conclusion of COP30, allowing the complete news cycle associated with the conference to be analyzed\. Articles were manually coded according to a predefined HRtWS framework to identify thematic patterns, media framings, and the visibility of normative rights dimensions\. The findings indicate that media coverage predominantly framed water and sanitation through infrastructure development, urban modernization, and climate adaptation narratives\. Availability and sustainability were the most frequently represented HRtWS dimensions, whereas participation, accountability, and access to information received comparatively limited attention\. The analysis also revealed substantial differences among media categories: institutional and governmental outlets primarily emphasized infrastructure investments and resilience, while investigative and independent media more frequently highlighted environmental injustice and territorial inequalities\. These findings suggest that the HRtWS were only partially incorporated into media narratives surrounding COP30\. Although water and sanitation were visible within the analyzed corpus, they were predominantly portrayed as technical and infrastructural challenges rather than as fundamental human rights closely linked to equity, governance, and climate justice\. By integrating a rights\-based analytical framework with systematic media analysis, this study contributes both methodologically and substantively to research on climate communication, urban sustainability, and environmental justice\.
