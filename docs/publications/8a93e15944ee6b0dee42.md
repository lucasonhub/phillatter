# Ulvan, carrageenan, and fucoidan: comparative immunomodulatory mechanisms and future directions in major algal sulfated polysaccharides

**Autores:** Shuang Zhao; Zhangyi Xia; Jinlin Liu; Meijing Zhang; Lin Maozi; Guangmao Ding; 唐隆晨; Fang Su; Jingyi Sun; Chao Zhao

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Sulfated polysaccharides derived from marine algae have attracted considerable interest in innate immunity research due to their structural diversity and pronounced immunomodulatory activities\. Ulvan, carrageenan, and fucoidan, derived from green, red, and brown algae, respectively, represent three paradigmatic classes of algal sulfated polysaccharides\. This review establishes a four\-tier analytical framework, namely “structural features, receptor recognition, signaling pathways, and immune outcomes”, to systematically compare their mechanistic differences in innate immune modulation\. This framework provides, for the first time, a unified causal chain from molecular structure to immune effector phenotypes\. Ulvan is characterized by coordinated TLR4/cGAS\-STING dual pathways: it drives pro\-inflammatory responses via the TLR4\-MyD88\-NF\-κB axis while inducing type I interferon production through the cGAS\-STING\-TBK1\-IRF3 cascade\. TBK1, as a shared downstream node of both the TLR4\-TRIF and cGAS\-STING pathways, may mediate signal integration and amplification, thereby conferring a distinctive advantage to ulvan in antiviral immunity\. Carrageenan exhibits dual TLR4/NLRP3 activities, with κ/ι/λ subtypes displaying a functional spectrum from localized pro\-inflammation to systemic immunostimulation, determined by their sulfate content and gelation properties\. A critical distinction must be made between high\-molecular\-weight native carrageenan \(&gt;100 kDa\), which is minimally absorbed in the intestine and considered safe, and low\-molecular\-weight degraded carrageenan \(&lt;50 kDa\), which carries inflammatory and toxic risks\. Fucoidan achieves immunomodulatory pleiotropy through dual TLR4/CLR recognition and concurrent engagement of multiple signaling cascades, encompassing NF\-κB\-driven pro\-inflammation, bidirectional NLRP3 inflammasome regulation, and complement C3 activation\. The antiviral effect of fucoidan via the STING pathway, potentially dependent on ROS\-mediated DNA damage or endoplasmic reticulum stress, mechanistically differs from ulvan’s cGAS\-STING activation\. The structural basis driving this pathway selectivity resides in an inverse gradient relationship between sulfate and uronic acid contents across the three polysaccharides: sulfate content ranks as fucoidan &gt; carrageenan &gt; ulvan, while uronic acid content follows the opposite order\. This inverse gradient serves as the first unified structural principle explaining differential receptor preferences and pathway selection among these three distinct classes of algal polysaccharides\. Based on this framework, we propose differentiated polysaccharide design strategies tailored to three therapeutic objectives \(pro\-inflammation, antiviral defense, and immune balance\), offering a roadmap for structure\-guided immunomodulation using algal sulfated polysaccharides\.
