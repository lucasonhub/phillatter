# Admission platelet\-to\-hemoglobin ratio and 1\-year outcomes after percutaneous coronary intervention: a multicenter registry cohort study

**Autores:** Jeong Tae Byoun; Donghyeon Joo; Sungho Jo; Jae Young Cho; Kyeong Ho Yun

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background The platelet\-to\-hemoglobin ratio \(PHR\) is a proposed prognostic marker in cardiovascular disease, but it has not been evaluated against a broad major adverse cardiovascular events \(MACE\) endpoint in a dedicated percutaneous coronary intervention \(PCI\) cohort, and whether it adequately summarizes its two components remains untested\. Methods We analyzed 1,551 patients pooled from two prospective Korean drug\-eluting stent registries \(2016–2022\)\. The PHR was calculated as platelet count \(×10⁹/L\) divided by hemoglobin \(g/L\)\. The primary endpoint was 1\-year MACE, with follow\-up censored at 365 days, a composite of all\-cause death, myocardial infarction, stroke, repeat revascularization, or heart failure hospitalization\. Multivariable Cox models included eight prespecified clinical covariates; the functional form was examined with restricted cubic splines, and the PHR was decomposed using nested models and a log\-scale ratio\-constraint test\. Results MACE occurred in 107 patients \(6\.9%\) within 1 year\. A higher PHR was independently associated with MACE \(adjusted hazard ratio \[HR\] per 1\-standard deviation, 1\.21; 95% confidence interval \[CI\], 1\.05–1\.40; P = 0\.008\), with no evidence of nonlinearity \( P = 0\.700\)\. Adding the PHR improved the clinical model \(likelihood\-ratio P = 0\.013\); the ratio constraint was not rejected \( P = 0\.765\), and a single log\-PHR term retained 98\.7% of the two\-component likelihood\-ratio χ 2 increment over the clinical model in this cohort \(bootstrap 95% CI, 21\.8%–100%\)\. The PHR was associated with all\-cause and cardiovascular death \(both HR, 1\.28\), paralleling hemoglobin\. Repeat revascularization was associated with platelet count \(HR, 1\.39; P = 0\.014\), but not significantly with PHR \(HR, 1\.24; P = 0\.081\)\. Risk was concentrated in patients with both anemia and a platelet count at or above the median \(adjusted HR, 1\.82; 95% CI, 1\.05–3\.15\)\. The gain in discrimination was negligible \( Δ AUC, \+0\.003; 95% CI, −0\.003 to \+0\.012\), and net benefit was virtually unchanged\. Conclusions The admission PHR was independently associated with 1\-year MACE after PCI, with no evidence of nonlinearity, and its log\-scale component effects were compatible with the ratio constraint in this cohort\. Given the negligible gain in discrimination and net benefit, the PHR is an independent prognostic marker rather than a tool for risk reclassification\. Registry registration: Clinical Research Information Service KCT0003392 and KCT0005008\.
