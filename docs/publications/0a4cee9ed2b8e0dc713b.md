# Post\-processing as a design variable in additive manufacturing: A review on thermodynamic, kinetic, and defect\-driven perspectives

**Autores:** Mehmet Erdi Korkmaz; Munish Kumar Gupta; MUSTAFA GÜNAY; Akash Nag; Sergej Hloch

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Additive manufacturing \(AM\) of metallic materials produces components under extreme solidification rates, steep thermal gradients, and cyclic reheating, resulting in non\-equilibrium microstructures, high dislocation densities, metastable phase fractions, chemical segregation, and complex residual stress fields\. These process\-inherited internal states govern subsequent mechanical performance, yet post\-processing is still predominantly treated as a corrective step rather than a mechanistically grounded design variable\. This structured critical review establishes a state\-engineering framework in which post\-processing is interpreted in terms of thermodynamic driving forces, kinetic pathways, and defect evolution mechanisms\. Thermal treatments are analyzed with respect to phase stability, diffusion\-mediated homogenization, recovery–recrystallization kinetics, and stress\-relaxation mechanisms\. Mechanical post\-processing routes are examined as controlled defect\-structure modifiers that introduce strain gradients, dislocation substructures, grain refinement, and compressive residual stress fields governed by plasticity and strain\-hardening mechanisms\. Particular emphasis is placed on process sequencing, highlighting how thermally activated defect annihilation, phase\-transformation pathways, and mechanically induced defect generation interact in a path\-dependent and non\-linear manner\. Fatigue crack initiation, short\-crack propagation, and wear\-induced microstructural evolution are interpreted as time\-dependent responses controlled by the evolution of these internal state variables rather than by static material parameters alone\. By integrating these mechanisms into a structure–property–performance framework, this review establishes a state\-aware basis for designing thermal–mechanical post\-processing pathways to achieve targeted fatigue, wear, and long\-term performance in additively manufactured metals\.
