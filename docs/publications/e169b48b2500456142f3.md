# Bowman probe\-guided lacrimal canalicular repair using a 24\-G intravenous cannula: a retrospective case series

**Autores:** Osman Parça; Nail Işıklı; Emine Şeker Ün

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

AbstractPurpose: The primary goal in traumatic lacrimal canalicular lacerations is to restore canalicular continuity and prevent permanent epiphora\. This study evaluated anatomical and functional outcomes and postoperative complications of Bowman probe\-guided repair using a 24\-gauge intravenous cannula in urgent cases where commercial canalicular stents were unavailable\.Materials and methods: This retrospective single\-center case series included patients undergoing primary canalicular repair with this technique between January 2022 and December 2025\. Patients with incomplete records or follow\-up shorter than 3 months were excluded\. Demographics, injury characteristics, surgical details, cannula retention, follow\-up, success rates, and complications were recorded\. Anatomical success was defined as lacrimal patency on irrigation at final follow\-up; functional success as absence of epiphora \(Munk score 0\)\.Results: Twenty\-one patients \(15 male; mean age 36±16 years\) were included\. The lower canaliculus was most frequently involved\. Fifteen patients underwent repair within 24 hours; 19 underwent concurrent eyelid repair\. Mean cannula retention and follow\-up were 3\.8±1\.0 and 9\.5±2\.7 months, respectively\. Anatomical and functional success were achieved in 18 \(85\.7%\) and 17 \(81\.0%\) patients, respectively\. Complications included corneal abrasion in 3 \(14\.3%\), early cannula loss in 2 \(9\.5%\), and canalicular stenosis in 1 \(4\.8%\)\. No punctal granuloma or wound infection occurred\.Conclusion: Bowman probe\-guided canalicular repair using a 24\-gauge intravenous cannula achieved favorable early\-to\-intermediate anatomical and functional outcomes with manageable complications in this limited series\. In urgent cases without commercial stents, it may be considered a readily available alternative\. These findings should be supported by prospective studies with larger sample sizes\.
