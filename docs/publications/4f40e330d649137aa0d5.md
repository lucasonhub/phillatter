# Digital biomarkers derived from wearable and portable devices in prodromal Parkinson’s disease and at\-risk populations: a scoping review

**Autores:** Ting YU; Qian Wang; Mingxia Pan; Yuanfang Xiong; Shiya Hu; Yiping Deng; Lingyu Kuang; Hanjiao Liu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Prodromal Parkinson’s disease \(pPD\) represents a pre\-diagnostic stage during which subtle and heterogeneous changes may be difficult to detect through conventional clinical assessments\. Digital biomarkers derived from wearable or portable devices offer opportunities for objective and longitudinal assessment\. This scoping review aimed to map the current evidence on digital biomarkers in individuals with formally defined pPD or populations at increased risk of Parkinson’s disease \(PD\), focusing on measurement approaches, analytical methods, types of evidence, and methodological gaps\. Methods A systematic search of PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, IEEE Xplore, Scopus, and ACM Digital Library was conducted from database inception to August 31, 2026\. Studies investigating digital biomarkers derived from wearable or portable devices in individuals with formally defined pPD or populations at increased risk of PD were included\. Study characteristics, digital biomarker domains, measurement and analytical approaches, and types of evidence were synthesized descriptively\. Results Forty\-three studies were included, primarily involving individuals with iRBD or other high\-risk states associated with α \-synucleinopathies, population\-based or broader prodromal\-risk cohorts, genetic high\-risk populations, and individuals with idiopathic hyposmia/anosmia\. Digital biomarkers encompassed motor, speech, sleep/activity, and multidomain features, with motor\-related measures, particularly gait and mobility, being the most extensively investigated\. Machine\-learning approaches were applied across several domains, whereas genuinely multidomain studies remained limited\. Most studies provided cross\-sectional evidence of group differences or discriminative performance, whereas prospective evidence regarding future PD diagnosis or phenoconversion was less extensive\. Considerable heterogeneity was observed in measurement protocols, analytical approaches, and validation strategies\. Conclusion Wearable\- and portable\-device\-derived digital biomarkers represent promising candidate tools for characterizing and monitoring individuals across the prodromal PD risk spectrum\. However, evidence supporting clinical prediction of phenoconversion remains limited by methodological heterogeneity and a lack of sufficient longitudinal and external validation\. Future research should prioritize standardized assessment, large, multicenter longitudinal cohorts, independent external validation, and integration with established clinical and biological markers\.
