# Morphotectonic and topographic correlates of spring occurrence and perennial spring discharge in a forested catchment of the Garhwal Himalaya

**Autores:** Dharm Singh Meena; S\. S\. Rawat; Bhargabnanda Dass; D\. S\. Malik

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Springs sustain domestic water supply across the Indian Himalaya, yet many are declining, and the landscape factors associated with their occurrence and discharge remain poorly quantified at the sub\-catchment scale\. This study examines the association of spring occurrence and dry\-season discharge with elevation, morphotectonic indices and land cover in the Henval River Catchment \(252\.71 km 2 \), Garhwal Himalaya, India\. Field surveys in 2024 mapped 82 springs, of which 74 were perennial, and the dry\-season \(March–April\) discharge of the perennial springs was measured by the volumetric method\. The spring data were integrated with DEM\-derived morphometric parameters, the Stream Length\-gradient \(SL\) index, the Valley Floor Width\-to\-Height ratio \(V f \), hypsometric analysis and Sentinel\-2\-based land\-use/land\-cover \(LULC\) data\. Spring occurrence was assessed with the Frequency Ratio \(FR\) method, and discharge with non\-parametric tests\. Of the 82 mapped springs, 87\.8% were nearest to high\-SL reaches \(SL &gt; 500; FR = 1\.58\), whereas low\-SL reaches \(SL &lt; 200\) were strongly under\-represented \(FR = 0\.08\)\. Of the perennial springs, 66\.2% lay within 200 m of reaches with SL ≥ 200, compared with 45% expected under a random distribution \(χ 2 = 13\.46, p &lt; 0\.001\)\. The 1,400–1800 m zone contained 41\.9% of perennial springs and 59\.8% of total discharge\. Discharge differed among elevation zones \(Kruskal–Wallis H = 19\.86, p = 0\.001\) and showed a weak positive monotonic association with elevation \(Spearman ρ = 0\.314, p = 0\.007\)\. Vf class was not associated with spring occurrence \(FR ≈ 1\.0\) but springs near V\-shaped valleys \(V f &lt; 0\.5\) had higher median discharge than those near transitional valleys \(Mann–Whitney U = 907, p = 0\.018\)\. The hypsometric integral \(0\.44\) indicates a mature landscape with continuing incision\. Springs were over\-represented in forest relative to its areal extent \(FR = 1\.22\)\. These associations indicate that DEM\-derived indices, particularly the SL index, can help prioritise spring surveys and springshed protection in the Lesser Himalayas\.
