# Measuring Youth Mental Health Vulnerability Across Communities: Development and Validation of ThriveAtlas

**Autores:** Christina A\. Mehranbod; Domonique M Reed; Emily Yetton; Aaron Dibner\-Dunlap; Tomas Engelthaler; Peter Smittenaar; Hannah Kemp; Sema K\. Sgaier; Adele Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

CONTEXT: Youth mental health is shaped by interacting individual, relational, and structural factors that vary across communities, yet few tools integrate these drivers into youth\-specific, geographically granular measures for decision\-making\. OBJECTIVE: This study describes the development and preliminary validation of ThriveAtlas, a geographically granular index designed to identify factors that prevent youth from thriving\. DESIGN: ThriveAtlas was developed through a multistep process including a targeted literature review; selection and construction of indicators from public and proprietary data sources; aggregation into thematic domains and a composite index; and preliminary validation using county\-level mental health outcomes\. SETTING: County\-level analysis using school\-based survey data from California \(California Healthy Kids Survey, 2021\-2023\)\. PARTICIPANTS: Ninth\-grade students across 53 California counties\. MAIN OUTCOME MEASURE: County\-level principal components derived from 5 self\-reported mental health indicators; prioritization overlap with the Social Vulnerability Index \(Jaccard statistic\); and univariate explanatory power \(R2\) of ThriveAtlas domains versus Social Vulnerability Index \(SVI\) domains\. RESULTS: The review identified key gaps in existing tools: youth\-focused indices often emphasize general well\-being rather than mental health, while structurally focused indices lack youth specificity or substate granularity\. ThriveAtlas revealed geographic variability in vulnerability, including regional clusters and isolated high\-risk counties\. Validation analyses showed expected patterns in California, where higher vulnerability aligned with greater distress and lower well\-being\. ThriveAtlas and SVI were correlated \(Spearman ρ = 0\.66\) across California counties; however, ThriveAtlas themes better explained county\-level mental health outcomes than SVI domains \(R2 = 0\.41 vs\. 0\.27\), and the 2 tools agreed on only one\-third of high\-priority counties \(Jaccard = 0\.33\)\. CONCLUSION: ThriveAtlas addresses a critical measurement gap by providing a youth\-centered, multidimensional, and geographically granular index of vulnerability\. Early validation supports its utility as a decision\-relevant signal to identify communities with elevated youth mental health challenges and inform targeted intervention strategies\.
