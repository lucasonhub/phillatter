# Effectiveness of Medical French training in developing communicative competence among health professional students in Ghana

**Autores:** Fiawomorm Kofi Fiagbe; Jonas Kwabla Fiadzawoo; Edmond Maliyele Daberou; Dorcas Pearl Slippe; Ernest Nyamekye

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examined the integration of Medical French into the training of health professionals in Ghana in response to the growing need for effective cross\-border healthcare communication within the multilingual ECOWAS region\. Despite the increasing relevance of French proficiency for healthcare delivery, professional mobility, and communication with Francophone patients, limited empirical research has assessed the effectiveness of Medical French instruction in Anglophone health professional education\. Guided by Stufflebeam&\#x27;s CIPP \(Context, Input, Process, Product\) evaluation model, the study adopted a quantitative descriptive design involving 176 students from the Schools of Pharmacy, Nursing, Midwifery, and Allied Sciences in a public university in Ghana\. Data were collected through a structured questionnaire based on the CIPP framework and analyzed using descriptive statistics\. Findings showed strong recognition of the relevance of Medical French for communication with Francophone patients, healthcare practice in border communities, regional collaboration, and international employment opportunities\. However, respondents perceived institutional support and instructional resources as inadequate, limiting practical immersion and authentic communicative experiences\. Although teaching strategies were considered moderately effective, students reported relatively low confidence in their communicative competence in clinical situations\. The study recommends curriculum reforms emphasizing experiential learning, clinical immersion, and cross\-border partnerships to strengthen communicative competence and enhance healthcare delivery within the ECOWAS sub\-region\.
