# How Does AI Literacy Relate to Pre\-Service STEM Teachers’ AI Readiness? Insights from Mediational Analysis

**Autores:** Sunday Ogbu; Emmanuel Ifeanyi Orji; Ugorji Iheanachor Ogbonnaya

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Efforts to achieve widespread applications of AI in education depend, to a large extent, on teachers’ level of AI literacy \(AIL\), AI self\-efficacy \(AISE\), Artificial Intelligence Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge \(AI\-TPACK\), and above all, the teachers’ readiness for AI\-based learning\. This study, therefore, examined AI literacy’s links with pre\-service STEM teachers’ AI readiness \(AIR\), focusing majorly on the mediational roles of AISE and AI\-TPACK\. A quantitative research approach, specifically a cross\-sectional correlational survey, was employed and was led by six null hypotheses\. This study involved 310 final\-year pre\-service STEM teachers who participated using a volunteer sampling approach from five public universities in southeastern Nigeria\. Four adapted structured scales featuring AIL, AIR, AISE, and AI\-TPACK served as the data collection tools\. The scales showed adequate evidence of construct validity and reliability \(Cronbach’s alpha ≥ 0\.79, and AVE ≥ 0\.50\) for all the scales\. “Statistical Package for the Social Sciences \(SPSS\) PROCESS version 5\.0” was used for the data analysis\. The findings indicated that AIL, AISE, and AI\-TPACK have significant direct relationships with AIR\. Additionally, AISE and AI\-TPACK play significant complementary partial mediation roles in the link between AIL and AIR\. The findings highlight the need to redesign Teacher Education Programs \(TEPs\), focusing mainly on hands\-on activities where pre\-service STEM teachers practiceusing specific AI tools to solve STEM problems, such as using machine\-learning algorithms to model climate data in a physics lesson\.
