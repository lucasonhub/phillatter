# Deterministic fixed point inference with tiled parallelism for ASIC implementation of human activity recognition

**Autores:** M\. Madhushankara; H Muralikrishna; Ananthakrishna Thalengala

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Deploying machine learning models on hardware platforms requires careful handling of numerical precision, limited resources, and predictable execution\. In practice, mismatches between floating\-point software models and their register\-transfer level \(RTL\) implementations can lead to inconsistent inference results\. In this proposed work, a hardware–software co\-designed methodology that ensures bit\-accurate and deterministic inference across floating\-point and RTL implementations is proposed\. A fully connected neural network with a 64–64–6 architecture is trained on the UCI human activity recognition \(HAR\) dataset and mapped to a hardware\-friendly fixed\-point representation\. An application specific integrated circuit \(ASIC\)\-oriented RTL design is developed and validated against a cycle\-accurate Python fixed\-point model to ensure consistency between software and hardware execution\. To improve the performance, a tiled parallel multiply–accumulate \(MAC\) architecture with $$\\textit\{PAR\}=8$$ is employed, enabling higher throughput without significantly increasing hardware complexity\. The model achieves $$93\.24\\%$$ accuracy in floating\-point and $$92\.66\\%$$ after fixed\-point and RTL implementation, with a reduction of 0\.58 percentage points following quantization\. The RTL outputs match the fixed\-point model across all evaluated samples, confirming its deterministic behavior\. Synthesis using the TSMC 12 nm technology achieves a compute energy of 30\.33 pJ per inference and a total inference energy of 170\.67 pJ, demonstrating the suitability of the proposed methodology for edge AI applications\.
