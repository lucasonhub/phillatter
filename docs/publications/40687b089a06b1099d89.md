# Pier, Jens: Apperception as Capacity, I as Act: Kant on Self\-Consciousness as the Constitutive Human Faculty

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-19 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Topoi\_\. forthcomingIn recent years, renewed attention has been paid to Kant’s concern with capacities and faculties of the mind\. His critical philosophy is frequently characterized as a “capacity\-first philosophy” or “capacitism\.” A common feature of these characterizations is that they frame this feature as a matter of optional choice or theory design\. This paper also characterizes Kant’s philosophy as capacitism, though I argue that, firstly, he ultimately focuses on but one single capacity—self\-consciousness or apperception\. Secondly, I resist the idea that this focus should fundamentally be seen as a matter of metaphilosophical choice or design\. It is rather an articulation or explication of something non\-optional: on my interpretation, Kant’s capacitism brings out and makes explicit apperception as the starting point and principle of transcendental philosophy\. It occupies this principal position because it is the constitutive human faculty: the faculty in light of which all other faculties must be made sense of\. I further argue that judgment or the “I think” is the paradigmatic act of this capacity, and suggest that ultimately, apperception ought to be regarded as identical to human spontaneity in general\. Kant’s philosophy should thus be characterized as an apperception\-first philosophy\.
