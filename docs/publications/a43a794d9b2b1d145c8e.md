# How do cultural repertoires shape sustainable teacher identity? Confucian striving and Daoist release among Chinese pre\-service teachers

**Autores:** Xuefei Lin; Qi Tan; Bin Xiong

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Teacher identity formation is a dynamic process shaped by professional experience, emotion, wellbeing, and culturally available meanings\. This study examines how pre\-service teachers in China drew on Confucian and Daoist repertoires when reflecting on their developing teacher identities\. Methods Participants were 143 pre\-service teachers at one Chinese normal university\. They wrote prompted reflective narratives about coursework, micro\-teaching, lesson planning, educational observation, and imagined classroom responsibilities\. The analysis combined descriptive keyword mapping with reflexive thematic analysis\. Results Four related processes were identified: Confucian striving as a language of perseverance and self\-cultivation; Daoist release as a resource for non\-contention, recognizing limits, and emotional restoration; situational switching between these repertoires in response to changing demands; and sustainable teacher formation as an orientation toward responsibility with limits\. The study proposes cultural identity regulation as an integrative analytic heuristic for understanding how culturally available meanings connect academic experiences, emotional calibration, and imagined teacher selves\. Discussion The findings suggest that Confucian striving and Daoist release provided participants with culturally familiar ways to interpret effort, pressure, and anticipated responsibility during teacher preparation\. Their value lay in helping participants connect perseverance with self\-cultivation and responsibility, while also recognizing limits, reducing destructive comparison, and restoring emotional balance\. Cultural identity regulation captures this narrated negotiation of professional commitment and personal sustainability in the formation of an imagined teacher self\.
