# Enzymatic optimization for enhancing earthworm peptide yields and biological activities on antioxidant, cytotoxic against HT\-29 and HepG2 cancer cells, and antimigration

**Autores:** Pimphan Wasunan; Mongkol Thirabunyanon

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The purposes of this study were to investigate the antioxidant, cytotoxic against HT\-29 and HepG2 cancer cells, and antimigratory activities of peptide fractions derived from Amynthas arenulus earthworm\. Earthworm proteins were hydrolyzed with alcalase using response surface methodology\. The optimal conditions resulted in a maximum degree of hydrolysis of 61\.25% at an enzyme\-to\-substrate ratio of 1\.5% \(v/v\) and a hydrolysis time of 2 h\. Following simulated gastrointestinal digestion, gastric and intestinal recoveries of the peptides were 56\.83% and 90\.10%, respectively\. The 10–30 kDa peptide fraction exhibited antioxidant activity, with estimated IC 50 values of 3\.18 and 0\.79 mg/mL for DPPH and ABTS assays, respectively\. FRAP activity was also exhibited\. The 10–30 kDa peptide fraction showed antiproliferative activity against HT\-29 and HepG2 cells, with IC 50 values of 0\.634 and 0\.761 mg/mL, respectively, whereas it was not cytotoxic against non\-cancerous cells of L929 fibroblast cells\. This peptide fraction was trigger apoptosis\-associated cellular changes observed using inverted light microscopy, AO/PI and DAPI staining, DNA fragmentation, and scanning electron microscopy\. Relative cell migration values in HT\-29 and HepG2 cells were 67\.34% and 70\.29%, respectively which compared to 100% of control\. As resultants, this peptide fraction might be considered for applied in medical, nutraceutical food, and bioprocessing industries\.
