# Enhanced Diabetes Prediction

**Autores:** Omar Mohammed Amin; Rebin Abdulkareem Hamaamin; Araz Ibrahim Mustafa; Aso Mohammed Aladdin

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Diabetes is a large chronic disease where there is the potential to make early and reliable predictions to aid in timely clinical decisions and screening\. Recently, machine learning techniques have been used for diabetes anomaly detection, but the performance may be influenced by the presence of missing values, class imbalance, and a restricted feature representation in widely\-used benchmark datasets\. In this study, the Tabular Retrieval Network \(TabR\) model\-based framework for diabetes prediction is proposed in Pima Indians Diabetes \(PID\) dataset with 768 patient records and 8 clinical predictor variables and one binary target variable\. The proposed framework handles missing values in biologically unrealistic way by using class\-aware median imputation, introduces domain\-specific feature engineering, retrieves contextually similar patient records, and implements a neural network with Bayesian regularization as a classifier\. Stratified 10\-fold cross validation was used to evaluate the model using accuracy, precision, recall, F1\-score, Matthew’s correlation coefficient \(MCC\) and area under the curve \(AUC\) as performance measures\. The proposed TabR model achieved an accuracy of 82\.04%, precision of 75\.93%, recall of 72\.04%, F1\-score of 73\.74%, MCC of 0\.6035, and AUC of 0\.8541\. These results show promising predictive and discriminative results on the PID data set\. Comparisons with previously reported studies showed that the proposed approach reached the highest accuracy reported compared to some of the existing solutions, however, the comparisons should be treated with caution as each study may have different preprocessing procedures and/or evaluation procedures\. The results indicate that class\-aware pre\-processing and contextual retrieval could be beneficial features for diabetes prediction from tabular clinical data\.
