# Engineering next\-generation CAR\-NK cells for hematological malignancies: genome editing, synthetic biology, and precision immunotherapy

**Autores:** Ahmed Reda Bahr; Youssef Basem; Amani Alhejely; Abdel Rahman Alaa; Saifallah Mabrouk Abozeid; Amira Basem; Mohsen Ali Khormi; Mabrouk A\. Abo‐Zaid

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Chimeric antigen receptor \(CAR\)\-based immunotherapy has transformed cancer treatment by enabling engineered immune cells to selectively recognize and eliminate malignant targets, with CAR\-T cells establishing the first major clinical success in this field\. However, severe associated toxicities, logistical complexities of autologous manufacturing, and high rates of antigen\-loss relapse have accelerated the development of alternative effector platforms such as CAR\-NK cells\. CAR\-NK cells combine antigen\-directed cytotoxicity with the innate antitumor functions of natural killer cells, offering a potentially safer and more readily available off\-the\-shelf therapeutic strategy\. Nevertheless, the clinical efficacy of CAR\-NK therapy is constrained by key biological and translational hurdles: brief in vivo persistence, inadequate trafficking and solid tumor infiltration, severe susceptibility to tumor microenvironment\-mediated immunosuppression, and antigen escape driven by tumor heterogeneity\. These biological constraints are compounded by technical bottlenecks, including cell source variability, limited large\-scale expansion capacity, ex vivo functional exhaustion, and an absence of standardized manufacturing protocols\. To address these multi\-faceted barriers, recent advances in genome engineering, systems biology, and computational modeling provide promising routes forward\. Given that prolonged exposure to immunosuppressive niches induces functional impairment, genome\-editing strategies targeting inhibitory checkpoints and persistence programs are crucial; however, enhancing persistence requires stringent safeguards to mitigate toxicity risks from constitutive signaling or genomic instability\. Thus, engineering next\-generation CAR\-NK cells necessitates balancing extended longevity, potent cytotoxicity, and safety\. Multiomics approaches facilitate deeper characterization of CAR\-NK functional states and tumor–immune interactions, whereas artificial intelligence–guided engineering integrates high\-dimensional biological and manufacturing data to optimize receptor design, predict therapeutic performance, and accelerate translational workflows\. Collectively, these innovations support a paradigm shift toward programmable CAR\-NK cells that can dynamically sense, adapt, and respond to tumor evolution, driving the transition toward autonomous, highly scalable, and precisely controllable cellular immunotherapies in precision oncology\.
