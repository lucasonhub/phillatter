# Challenges affecting revitalization and sustainability in forest\-based land reform projects: lessons from the Thuthukani and Jabulani communities, South Africa

**Autores:** Ratsodo Phillip Tshidzumba; Fridar Bathobile Ntshingila; Paxie Wanangwa Chirwa

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction South African forest plantations are managed as multifunctional landscapes, which are crucial for biodiversity sustainability while providing ecosystem services that positively contribute to the livelihoods of rural communities\. This study examined the challenges affecting the efforts of the Thuthukani and Jabulani community property associations to successfully revitalize and sustain their plantation\. Methods The two communities were purposively selected because they were both beneficiaries of the forest plantations resulting from the successful settlement of land restitution claims\. Descriptive and inferential statistics were used to analyze the household survey data, while content analysis was used to analyze the FGD data\. Results Both communities cited difficulty obtaining resources to combat forest fires; in Jabulani, 46\.7% of respondents reported it was fairly difficult, compared with 40% in Thuthukani\. Furthermore, FGD participants from both communities emphasized the difficulty of securing funding, which prevents them from pursuing efforts to revitalize and sustain their plantation to a functional state\. Due to a lack of financial resources, the community beneficiaries were unable to revitalize and sustainably manage their forested land, sustain the resources, and continue accessing various ecosystem services\. Notably, the other challenge, directly related to the governance structure and legitimacy, was emphasized during the FGDs\. Discussion To ensure the successful revitalization and sustainability of the community beneficiaries&\#x27; forest plantation, it would be essential for the communities to organize themselves and develop clear strategies to establish a mutually beneficial sharing mechanism\. Limitations, including a lack of financial resources for fire management and revitalization, compromise plantation productivity and access to ecosystem services\. These findings underscore the need for a strengthened CPA committee governance structure, equitable benefit\-sharing mechanisms, and sustained institutional support\. Furthermore, enhancing technical capacity and fostering collaborative partnerships are essential to revitalize plantation productivity, ensuring sustainable ecosystem services, and enabling land restitution to contribute meaningfully to livelihoods and rural development\. ensuring sustainable ecosystem services, and enabling land restitution to contribute meaningfully to livelihoods and rural development\.
