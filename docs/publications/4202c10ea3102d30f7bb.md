# Neuromorphic control of a simulated shape\-memory\-alloy\-driven multi\-legged robot using a spiking neural network

**Autores:** Daisuke Miki; Hiroto Takigasaki

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract We present spiking\-neural\-network \(SNN\) reinforcement learning for autonomous locomotion control of a starfish\-inspired multi\-legged soft robot driven by shape memory alloy \(SMA\) actuators\. Such robots are usually driven open loop by pre\-designed periodic signals, because coordinated motion is hard to hand\-design at high degrees of freedom\. We replace the actor of Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient \(TD3\) with an SNN: a Spiking Actor Network\. The membrane potential, an internal SNN state, is reconstructed at update time from a short observation history through the current actor, rather than stored at collection time\. To reduce the controller’s inference cost, we add a regularization term on the squared membrane potentials, encouraging actions from fewer spikes\. In simulation the agent acquired the leg coordination needed to approach a target, and its firing patterns developed a periodicity matched to the SMA response\. The resulting gait gives continuous propulsion without excessive firing\. Goal error matched an ANN\-actor TD3 baseline with smaller spread across seeds, and reconstruction gave the lowest goal error of the membrane\-state treatments\. The regularization lowered the firing rate until the estimated inference energy fell below the ANN’s\.
