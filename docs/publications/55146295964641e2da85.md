# Fujie, Naoto: Do Philosophical Positions Exist as Operational Intervals? Historical Reconstruction and a Progressive Computational Test

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper asks whether philosophical positions such as skepticism, realism, idealism, empiricism, and rationalism should be treated as stable properties of thinkers and doctrines, or reconstructed as historically stabilized families of operational structures and intervals\. Rather than translating philosophical positions directly into computational variables—for example, skepticism into uncertainty, realism into predictive success, or empiricism into sensory weighting—the paper develops a “detect first, name later” methodology\. Pyrrhonian, Cartesian, Humean, and Kantian operational structures are reconstructed independently and fixed prior to machine comparison, while Sellars, Brandom, and Brassier provide important boundary conditions concerning scientific constraint, nonconceptual causal patterning, inference, and normative vocabulary\. A progressive computational program then tests increasingly demanding conditions for any machine–historical comparison, including unlabeled regime differentiation, multiscale temporal robustness, causal bypass, history re\-entry, altered reachability, cross\-medium penetration, endogenous intervention, and frozen\-policy ignition\. Positive controls establish that these properties can be measured independently\. However, the decisive raw\-observation and generic\-objective experiment fails to produce selective configuration\-sensitive ignition: intervention saturates even in the control condition\. Accordingly, the paper does not claim that a machine instantiated skepticism or any other philosophical position\. Instead, it argues that philosophical vocabulary should be introduced only after operational differentiation has been independently established, and should remain withheld when the required non\-redundant structure is absent\.
