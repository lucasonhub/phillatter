In Darrell P\. Rowbottom, Andre Curtis\-Trudel &amp; David L\. Barack, The Role of Artificial Intelligence in Science: Methodological and Epistemological Studies\. Routledge\. forthcomingMachine learning \(ML\) is a major scientific success\. Yet, ML models are notoriously considered black boxes, where this black boxness may refer to details of the ML model itself or details concerning its outcomes\. Hence, there is a flourishing field of &quot;eXplainable Artificial Intelligence&quot; \(XAI\), providing means for rendering several aspects of ML more transparent\. However, given their tremendous success, why would we even want to explain black boxed ML models with XAI? I here suggest that, in order to answer this question, we first need to distinguish between proximate and ultimate aims in using XAI: While the proximate aim may be uniformly to provide instruments for explaining aspects of ML to relevant stakeholders, the ultimate aim varies with the context of deployment\. Furthermore, I argue that in science, the ultimate aim is the understanding of scientific phenomena\. I then sketch three paths along which understanding of phenomena may be gained by means of ML and XAI\. In a coda, I address the possibility of gaining understanding from ML directly, without explanations and XAI\.
