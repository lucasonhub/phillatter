# Physics Constrained Generalization Performance of Machine Learning Paradigms Simulating Unseen Novel Rupture Mechanisms of Complex Fault Geometries

**Autores:** Mohamed Fayez\. Elnagar, Structural Consultant &amp; Certified Forensic Engineering Expert MSc, PMP, MCIArb, MASCE, MACI, M\.AISC, M\.ASTM, M\.FIDIC, PhD

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

The integration of data\-driven artificial intelligence and machine learning into earthquake engineering has opened up new avenues for rapid damage prediction and hazard mapping\. Nevertheless, a profound scientific dilemma persists regarding the physics\-constrained generalization performance of these algorithms when confronting entirely unseen, novel rupture mechanisms originating from complex fault geometries\. Machine learning paradigms, such as deep neural networks, rely completely on historical seismic datasets and synthetic training models to map the relationships between fault ruptures and structural damage responses\. The fundamental flaw in this approach is that earthquakes are inherently rare, high\-consequence events characterized by deep epistemic uncertainty\. When a fault ruptures in a geometrically unprecedented manner—such as a multi\-fault segment rupture combining strike\-slip and dip\-slip motions simultaneously—the resulting ground motion characteristics fall entirely outside the statistical training boundary of the AI system\. Under these conditions, the machine learning models produce highly unreliable, physically impossible predictions, completely miscalculating the structural drift and collapse probabilities of targeted facilities\. Because these algorithms operate as mathematical &quot;black boxes,&quot; they lack the deterministic physics constraints required to enforce conservation of energy and momentum during unexpected wave\-structure interactions\. Resolving this dilemma requires a fundamental overhaul of AI architectures, moving away from purely data\-driven correlations toward deep hybrid models that natively embed the partial differential equations of continuum mechanics within the neural network layers\.
