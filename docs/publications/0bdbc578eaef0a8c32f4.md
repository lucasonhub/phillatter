# Dieu, Patrick\-Olivier: What Must Exist Before Representation?

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Most theories of cognition begin with systems that already perceive, encode, predict, infer, or represent\. This paper asks whether such approaches begin one conceptual step too late\. It isolates a prior question: what minimal constraints must be satisfied for a physical organization of reality to transform its interactions with reality into stable, revisable, and predictively effective representations? The paper does not propose a complete theory of cognition, representation, or scientific knowledge\. Its aim is to identify the minimal unknowns that any future theory of representation emergence must address\. The proposed dependency chain runs from organization of reality to minimal constraints, interaction, representation, stability, fidelity, and theory\. Existing frameworks such as Kantian conditions of experience, naturalized epistemology, enactivism, predictive processing, theories of mental representation, and structural realism are considered as neighboring approaches\. The paper argues that these traditions often begin after representation, organismic coupling, prediction, or semantic content has already been introduced\. By contrast, the present framework asks what must exist before representation itself becomes possible\. Fidelity is treated operationally, not as resemblance to reality in itself, but as the preservation of regularities sufficient for reproducible prediction, action, or intervention\. The intended contribution is modest but foundational: to formulate a research program concerning the minimal constraints under which interaction becomes representationally stabilizable\.
