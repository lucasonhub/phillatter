# A new machine learning based framework for seismic damage ratio prediction of building clusters using satellite remote sensing data

**Autores:** Min Liu; Xiwei Fan; Jiwen An; Jing Wang; Wenyu Nie; Yuanmeng Qi; Fucun Lu; Nuoting Hou; Xuchuan Lin; Hong Chen

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Seismic\-induced building damage prediction is essential for disaster mitigation and risk assessment\. However, traditional methods often require detailed structural parameters that are labor\-intensive to obtain, while post\-earthquake remote sensing is frequently hindered by data latency\. To address these limitations, we propose a novel framework utilizing pre\-event multi\-source satellite data to predict damage, employing building height and construction period as proxies for structural types\. Taking the 2023 Türkiye Mw 7\.8 earthquake as a case study, we modeled the weighted building cluster damage ratio \(WBCDR\) using 26 predictors derived from remotely sensed building key attributes, seismic parameters, topography, and spectral\-textural features\. Random Forest \(RF\) and Support Vector Regression \(SVR\) models were evaluated at spatial scales of 300 m, 500 m, and 1 km\. RF outperformed SVR, with the best mean test performance at 500 m across ten splits \(R2 = 0\.8248, Mean Absolute Error \(MAE\) = 0\.0482, Root Mean Squared Error \(RMSE\) = 0\.0916\)\. Notably, Pseudo\-Spectral Acceleration at 0\.3 s \(PSA03\), Peak Ground Acceleration \(PGA\), and textural Dissimilarity were identified as dominant influencing factors\. The proposed framework enables rapid and effective large\-scale seismic building damage prediction in areas lacking detailed structural information, providing valuable support for seismic risk assessment and emergency response\.
