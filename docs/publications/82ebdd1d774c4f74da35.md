# Risk assessment of water treatment chemical supply disruptions in China using a synthetic water quality dataset and the Best\-Worst Method

**Autores:** Pan Zhao; Yuhui Lei

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Disruptions in the supply of water treatment chemicals can affect water quality, public health, service continuity, and the resilience of water treatment systems\. A systematic method is therefore required to assess mitigation strategies under different disruption conditions\. Methods A data driven multi criteria framework is developed to assess the risks associated with water treatment chemical supply disruptions in China\. A synthetic water quality dataset derived from the China Water Pollution Monitoring Dataset is used to construct indicators for four dimensions: compliance and public health, service continuity, supply vulnerability, and recoverability\. The synthetic dataset is used as a controlled and reproducible source for methodological evaluation rather than as direct empirical evidence from Chinese water utilities\. Criteria weights are determined using the Best Worst Method, and a quantitative scoring model is applied to evaluate mitigation strategies under multiple disruption scenarios\. Results Compliance receives the highest criterion weight of 0\.39\. Among the evaluated mitigation strategies, dual sourcing obtains the highest performance score\. Its score decreases from 0\.74 under lower disruption severity to 0\.63 under higher disruption severity, which indicates relatively stable performance across the tested scenarios\. Supplier prequalification shows moderate performance and improves under severe disruption conditions, whereas safety stock shows the largest reduction in performance\. Sensitivity and robustness analyses indicate that the strategy rankings remain stable under variations in criterion weights and disruption scenarios\. Discussion The results indicate that the proposed framework provides a consistent and reproducible method for comparing mitigation strategies under controlled synthetic data conditions\. The findings also show how multi criteria assessment can integrate water quality, service continuity, supply vulnerability, and recoverability within a common decision model\. Since the analysis is based on synthetic data, the results should be interpreted as methodological evidence rather than direct evidence of the performance of Chinese water utilities\. Further validation with observed utility and chemical supply data is required before operational application\.
