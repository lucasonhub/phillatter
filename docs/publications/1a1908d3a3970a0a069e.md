# Stilwell, Phil: ◼️ The Distortive Invisibility of Counterfactuals: Missing Comparisons, Misplaced Confidence, and Human Decision\-Making

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Human judgment often gives visible outcomes more comparative force than they can support\. Success can be mistaken for superiority, persistence for necessity, and an unfortunate result for a defective decision when credible alternatives receive too little attention\. This book develops a framework for tracing that distortion from the selection of evidence through belief, choice, and subsequent learning\. It distinguishes missing records of actual events from unrealized outcomes, and separates epistemic damage, practical damage, and damage to learning\. A worked formal model shows how evidence compatible with rival explanations can be misread as comparative confirmation, increasing confidence while discouraging the inquiry that could correct it\. The model specifies a possible mechanism rather than an empirically established general law\. Examples from evolution, history, public policy, scientific research, treatment outcomes, inherited convictions, relationships, hiring, and career regret make the structure tangible while preserving the limits of historical and causal reconstruction\. The discussion of Fukushima distinguishes an acknowledged hazard from a possibility given sufficient weight in protective action\. Drawing on causal inference, bounded rationality, decision theory, and research on judgment, the book develops a discipline of explicit baselines, credible alternatives, calibrated confidence, and proportionate inquiry\. Diagrams, a comparison worksheet and worked example, a glossary, and discussion questions help readers examine how missing comparisons affect both what they believe and what they do\.
