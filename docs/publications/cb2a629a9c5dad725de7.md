# Knowledge, attitudes, and information needs regarding norovirus infection among parents of primary school students directly affected by outbreaks: a cross\-sectional study in Hangzhou, China

**Autores:** Xiaojian Duan; Kai Song; Liangliang Huo; Jiayun Wang; Jiayun Wang; Yi Wang; Xiaobin Ren; Jing Wang; Jing Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Norovirus outbreaks are prevalent in primary schools; however, few studies have specifically examined the parents directly impacted by these events\. Methods A cross\-sectional survey was conducted between January 2025 and March 2026\. Parents from seven primary schools affected by norovirus outbreaks in Hangzhou, China, were invited to complete a self\-administered online questionnaire\. Data were collected on sociodemographic characteristics, knowledge of norovirus pathogenesis, attitudes toward prevention and control, and information needs\. Results A total of 322 valid responses were obtained \(response rate 82\.14%\)\. Among respondents, 83\.85% reported awareness of norovirus\. The mean knowledge score regarding norovirus pathogenesis was 6\.43 ± 1\.91 out of 11 \(58\.45% correct\), with only 14\.07% attaining a “good” knowledge level \(≥9 points\)\. Attitude scores were high, with a rate of 88\.22% of the maximum\. Multivariable logistic regression identified educational attainment \(bachelor’s degree or higher: OR = 6\.27, 95% CI: 1\.75–52\.28\), residence type \(detached house: OR = 5\.76, 95% CI: 1\.48–6\.18\), and active information\-seeking \(OR = 24\.60, 95% CI: 11\.28–53\.66\) as factors independently associated with good knowledge level\. Conclusion Parents directly affected by norovirus outbreaks demonstrated inadequate knowledge but held positive attitudes\. Proactive information\-seeking was the strongest predictor of good knowledge\. Targeted health education, particularly through WeChat parent groups, is urgently required to bridge knowledge gaps and foster active learning\.
