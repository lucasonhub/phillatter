# The Role of SOD1 and Oxidative Stress in Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis: An Overview

**Autores:** Carlos Alberto Gómez\-Pérez; Miguel Ángel Ramírez\-García; Petra Yescas

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis \(ALS\) is a rapidly progressing neurodegenerative disorder that selectively affects motor neurons\. The median survival time after diagnosis is 2 to 5 years\. Oxidative stress \(OS\), driven mainly by reactive oxygen species \(ROS\), plays a central role in motor neuron \(MN\) degeneration\. In this narrative review, we analyse the pathogenic role of SOD1 variants and their contribution to OS in ALS\. SOD1 mutations alter the Cu/Zn superoxide dismutase enzyme, thereby promoting oxidative damage and triggering neuroinflammatory cascades via the MAPK, NF\-κB and Nrf2/ARE signalling pathways\. We also discuss emerging proteomic and machine\-learning biomarkers, as well as the current therapeutic landscape\. Riluzole and edaravone are approved treatments, while AMX0035 was approved in 2022 but withdrawn in 2024\. Masitinib and EPI\-589 remain investigational\. In the realm of genetic therapies, the antisense oligonucleotide tofersen received accelerated FDA approval in 2023 for SOD1\-ALS, primarily due to its ability to reduce plasma neurofilament light chain levels\. However, the clinical benefit remains modest\. RNA interference has reached human trials, while CRISPR/Cas9 remains in the preclinical stage\. Both approaches have shown promising results in experimental models\. Further research into ALS pathogenesis is needed to develop treatments that integrate antioxidant, anti\-inflammatory, and gene\-editing strategies\.
