# Niami, Morteza: The Empty Place and Finite Continuance: Reconfigurative Capacity, Singularity, and the Ontological Threshold

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

The concept of the empty place names a critical ontological problem at the intersection of identity, language, singularity, and determination\. Contemporary philosophy has frequently approached threshold spaces through the problem of inclusion and exclusion, particularly in the work of Giorgio Agamben, whose analyses of singularity, potentiality, apparatus, exception, and bare life have reshaped the philosophical understanding of the zone between inside and outside\. This article develops an alternative interpretation through the philosophy of finite continuance\. It argues that the empty place can be understood as a field of predeterminate capacity situated between the emergence of a determination and its complete stabilization\. The empty place therefore possesses a positive ontological function: it preserves the interval through which a finite being can remain capable of future reconfiguration\. This framework shifts philosophical attention from the juridico\-political topology of inclusion and exclusion toward an ontology of erosion, permeability, capacity, and continuance\. The central thesis is that singularity is best understood as a temporary determination whose ontological continuance depends upon the preservation of capacities not exhausted by its present form\. Language participates in the transformation of predeterminate capacities into temporary determinations and remains implicated in their subsequent reconfiguration\. The article further argues that the threshold marks the critical interval at which a finite system can either preserve or lose its capacity for reconfiguration\. Through the concepts of the Threshold of Sufficiency, the Dynamic Mask, Responsible Suspension, and the Political Economy of Erosion, the empty place becomes a condition for the future of agency\. The resulting framework proposes an ontology of singularity centered on finite capacity, temporal reconfiguration, and the preservation of future possibility\. Keywords: finite continuance, empty place, Giorgio Agamben, singularity, predeterminate capacity, reconfiguration, threshold of sufficiency, ontology of erosion \(direct link \)
