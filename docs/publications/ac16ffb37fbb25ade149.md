# Exploring the Potential of Biochar\-Chitosan Biocomposites for Sustainable Wastewater Treatment in Shrimp Aquaculture: A Review of Properties, Applications and Future Directions

**Autores:** 

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

HistoryThe shrimp aquaculture industry faces significant challenges in managing wastewater, which is typically contaminated with high levels of pollutants such as ammonia, phosphates, heavy metals, and organic matter\.Conventional treatment methods often fail to address these complex contaminants effectively\.This review explores biochar\-chitosan biocomposites as a promising solution for wastewater treatment in shrimp aquaculture\.Biochar, a porous carbon material derived from organic biomass, and chitosan, a biopolymer sourced from crustacean shells, work synergistically to remove a wide range of pollutants\.Biochar&\#x27;s high surface area and functional groups, along with chitosan&\#x27;s biodegradability and chelating capacity, enhance the composite&\#x27;s ability to reduce wastewater contamination\.Recent studies show that biochar\-chitosan composites can achieve up to 90% removal efficiency for nitrogen compounds and 85% for heavy metals, demonstrating their effectiveness\.This review covers the synthesis processes, pollutant\-removal mechanisms, and practical applications of these biocomposites in aquaculture systems\.Additionally, it highlights key challenges, including scalability, material regeneration, and environmental impacts\.Future directions include optimizing composite properties, integrating them with other treatment methods, and evaluating economic viability\.Biochar\-chitosan biocomposites offer an innovative, eco\-friendly approach to improving wastewater management in shrimp farming, enhancing sustainability and mitigating environmental degradation\.
