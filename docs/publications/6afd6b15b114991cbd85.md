# Frequency and Co\-Reported Pathologic MRI Findings of Cystic Lesions of the Adult Knee: A Retrospective Study in 4,764 Knees

**Autores:** Leonardo Cavinatto; Elijah C\. Auch; Avianna E\. Arapovic; Muzamil Ahmad; Matthew R Cederman; Noopur Ranganathan; Dhun Chauhan; Ehab S Saleh; Betina B\. Hinckel

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objectives: Cystic lesions of the knee comprise anatomically distinct subtypes that may be reported alongside different degenerative, traumatic, meniscal, and synovial abnormalities\. We sought to determine the frequency of radiologist\-reported cystic lesion subtypes on adult knee magnetic resonance imaging \(MRI\) examinations performed for suspected internal derangement and, among cyst\-positive examinations, to explore subtype\-specific patterns of co\-reported intra\-articular and periarticular MRI findings\. Material and Methods: We conducted a retrospective review of adult knee MRI examinations performed at a single academic institution between January 1, 2022, and January 1, 2023\. MRI reports were reviewed for documented cystic lesions and co\-reported MRI abnormalities using search terms\. Among cyst\-positive examinations, examinations with each cyst subtype were compared with cyst\-positive examinations without that subtype\. Continuous variables were compared using Student’s t\-tests, and categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s chi\-square or Fisher’s exact test, where appropriate\. Unadjusted odds ratios \(ORs\) with 95% confidence intervals \(CIs\) were calculated\. Analyses were exploratory and hypothesis\-generating\. Results: Among 4,764 adult knee MRI examinations, 1,208 \(25\.4%\) contained at least one documented cystic lesion\. Among cyst\-positive examinations, Baker’s cysts were reported in 923 \(76\.4%\), ganglion cysts in 257 \(21\.3%\), parameniscal cysts in 172 \(14\.2%\), and meniscal cysts in 23 \(1\.9%\); these subtypes were not mutually exclusive\. Baker’s cysts were more frequently co\-reported with joint effusion \(OR 1\.48, 95% CI 1\.13\-1\.94\)\. Ganglion cysts were more frequently co\-reported with posterior cruciate ligament pathology \(OR 4\.36, 95% CI 2\.31\-8\.25\)\. Parameniscal cysts were more frequently co\-reported with lateral \(OR 1\.97, 95% CI 1\.42\-2\.73\) and medial meniscal tears \(OR 1\.96, 95% CI 1\.31\-2\.93\) and less frequently with effusion \(OR 0\.51, 95% CI 0\.37\-0\.71\)\. Meniscal cysts were uncommon overall \(n=23\)\. Conclusion: In this single\-institution review, cystic lesions were documented in approximately one\-quarter of adult knee MRI examinations performed for suspected internal derangement, with differing patterns of co\-reported MRI abnormalities across cyst subtypes\. These findings are descriptive and exploratory and should be interpreted in light of the retrospective design and reliance on clinical reports\.
