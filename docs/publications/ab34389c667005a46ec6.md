# Open\-set encrypted traffic recognition via contrastively\-optimized multi\-modal gating and extreme value theory

**Autores:** Kai Liang; Chuanfeng Li; Yuanbo Li; Lei Qian

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The widespread adoption of cryptographic protocols has rendered traditional payload inspection ineffective, necessitating advanced encrypted traffic analysis\. Identifying unseen zero\-day threats within encrypted streams, defined as the open\-set problem, remains challenging due to adversarial feature manipulation, metric distortion induced by traditional cross\-entropy optimization, and the resulting overconfidence of closed\-set classifiers\. In this paper, we propose Contrastively\-Optimized Multi\-Modal Gating with Extreme Value Theory \(COMG\-EVT\), an open\-set encrypted traffic recognition framework designed to establish strict rejection boundaries for unknown attacks\. The framework integrates three core mechanisms: a dynamic GatedFusion module that leverages stable microscopic payload entropy to modulate vulnerable macroscopic topological graphs, mitigating shortcut learning against obfuscation; a two\-stage graph contrastive optimization paradigm that improves representation consistency and class\-aware latent\-space organization to reduce metric distortion; and a calibrated multi\-gate EVT engine that models extreme tail distributions to mathematically bound the open space\. We conduct extensive experiments on two contemporary datasets, including the cipher\-agnostic CipherSpectrum dataset\. The analytical results demonstrate that COMG\-EVT outperforms state\-of\-the\-art open\-set methodologies, effectively minimizing the false positive rate for zero\-day attacks while maintaining high classification accuracy for known traffic categories, thereby exhibiting robust resilience against dynamic open\-space risks\. Not applicable\.
