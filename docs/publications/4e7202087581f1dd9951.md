# RIFT: Token Influence Follows the Path of Least Resistance

**Autores:** Tingan Jin; Shuhang Dong; Haosong Li

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Which context tokens drive a language model&\#x27;s answer? Graph\-based tools such as attention rollout and attention flow answer this from a single forward pass, yet their faithfulness in decoder\-only models is rarely tested against interventions\. We view the layers of a decoder as a causal network whose conductances are attention weights, and read a token&\#x27;s influence on the answer from the resistance between them\. We propose RIFT \(Resistance\-based Information Flow in Transformers\), which scores a token by the series resistance of its path of least resistance to the answer, found exactly by a classic shortest\-path dynamic program\. On synthetic in\-context retrieval tasks and extractive question answering over Wikipedia paragraphs, RIFT predicts the effect of replacing a token with a Spearman correlation of 0\.52\-0\.68; predicting a single replacement, it reaches 77\-96% of the agreement between two different random replacements\. It needs no gradients and no extra forward passes, scoring every token of a prompt in 0\.9\-3\.4 ms on one CPU thread, whereas the replacement experiment needs a forward pass per token\. On 576 held\-out prompts from four models of two families \(Qwen3 and Llama 3\.2\), it outperforms token distance, attention rollout and attention flow in every pre\-specified comparison, by 0\.23 to 0\.88 in mean Spearman correlation, and it is the best or tied\-best of all attention\-based scores we test\. Aggregates that add up all routes \(attention flow, effective resistance and rollout\) instead mostly encode token position: as a simple proposition shows, early tokens have many more parallel routes to the answer\.
