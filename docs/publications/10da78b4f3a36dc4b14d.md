# Redesigning collaborative teaching for human–AI partnership: a phase\-based AI\-constraint design principle in higher education

**Autores:** Felix Höpfl

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Generative AI is now a routine presence in higher\-education classrooms, shifting the operative question from whether to permit its use toward when and how access should be structured within a learning activity\. This paper reports a design\-based research programme examining phase\-based AI constraints, deliberately restricting AI access to specific phases of a collaborative teaching method rather than permitting or prohibiting it wholesale, as a design principle for structuring human and AI contributions within collaborative learning activities\. The wider DBR programme examined AI\-augmented designs across six collaborative teaching methods, administered repeatedly to five student groups over two academic years; the phase\-based AI\-constraint principle reported here as DP1 was developed through, and its evidence anchored in, Think\-Pair\-Share, with the other five methods contributing broader DBR context rather than independent confirmatory evidence\. Its strongest evidence comes from an AI\-free Think phase within Think\-Pair\-Share, replicated across three independent student groups spanning three degree programs, where learners consistently endorsed the AI\-free phase as appropriate; this endorsement pattern was typically the highest\-scoring item on its questionnaire\. This design itself resulted from an earlier design cycle within the same programme: an initial administration permitting AI access during this phase prompted redesign after the instructor&\#x27;s classroom observation indicated that independent reflection was being displaced by AI\-mediated responses\. We treat this endorsement as an empirical finding, not as a direct assessment of the quality or originality of what learners produced, and not as evidence that the phase in fact protects independent cognitive engagement; that stronger claim is a theoretically motivated proposition this study does not test, and we frame DP1 accordingly across three explicit levels: an empirical endorsement finding, a transferable design principle of differentiating AI access by activity phase rather than deciding it once for a whole activity, and an untested theoretical proposition about the cognitive mechanism this differentiation may support\. A complementary, independent stakeholder strand surveyed higher\-education professionals at a practitioner festival session, offering a modest, exploratory social\-validity signal: the phase\-based procedure itself was almost universally endorsed, while personal adoption intention was somewhat more reserved\. An exploratory qualitative coding of practitioners&\#x27; open responses showed only slight inter\-coder reliability \( κ =0\.17\), so we do not use these responses to derive additional design principles, retaining only one response on function\-based AI restriction as an illustrative example\. The study is exploratory and formative, without a no\-AI control condition or random assignment, and we frame its claims as design\-principle evidence within a design\-based research programme rather than as causal effect estimates\. Anchored in independently replicated student endorsement data from Think\-Pair\-Share and supplemented by a practitioner social\-validity signal, phase\-based AI constraints offer higher\-education instructors an actionable, low\-friction design principle, one we expect to be conceptually transferable to other collaborative teaching methods, though this transfer remains to be empirically demonstrated\.
