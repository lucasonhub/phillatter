# OLIY TA’LIM TALABALARIGA HUNARMANDCHILIK VOSITASIDA ESTETIK TARBIYANI RIVOJLANTIRISHNING PEDAGOGIK TIZIMINI TAKOMILLASHTIRISH

**Autores:** FERUZA XUDOYQULOVNA MUKUMOVA

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The study aims to identify the structural components and effective pedagogical conditions of a system that develops university students&\#x27; aesthetic education through traditional Uzbek handicrafts\. The research combines theoretical analysis of pedagogical and methodological literature, comparative analysis of international practices in craft and arts education, pedagogical observation, interviews with teachers, pedagogical modelling and generalisation within a systems approach\. The analysis shows that handicrafts unite theory and practice: while working with embroidery, ceramics, wood carving or ornament painting, students study the historical meaning of patterns, analyse colour harmony, plan compositions and produce a finished item, so that aesthetic knowledge turns into practical experience\. Moreover, the study distinguishes six pedagogical opportunities of craft\-based learning: perception of form, rhythm and proportion; aesthetic evaluation through comparison of samples; creative search beyond copying; acquaintance with national cultural heritage; development of patience, accuracy and responsibility; and self\-expression through the product\. As a result, the authors propose a pedagogical system consisting of five interrelated components: target, content, procedural, technological and outcome\-oriented\. The system relies on project\-based and problem\-based learning, master classes with artisans, museum pedagogy and portfolio\-based reflective assessment, which evaluates sketches, intermediate versions, independent decisions and reflection rather than only the final product\. The study also defines the key pedagogical conditions: an aesthetic learning environment, priority of practical activity, an individual approach to creative solutions, integration of tradition with modern design, and partnership with craftsmen, museums and cultural centres\. Consequently, the sequence &quot;national heritage – aesthetic perception – practical activity – creative search – aesthetic product – reflection&quot; can serve as a working model for higher education institutions\. The benefits of this study lie in providing a coherent framework that teachers can apply when designing courses in applied arts, design and teacher education\.
