# Gut microbiota in patients receiving dialysis: Mechanisms, complications and the road to personalized microbiome‑based care \(Review\)

**Autores:** Chenxia Jin; Yanping Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Gut dysbiosis is increasingly recognized as a potential contributor to poor outcomes in patients receiving dialysis, yet its mechanistic complexity and therapeutic implications remain incompletely understood in clinical practice\.The uremic environment, dietary restrictions, polypharmacy and dialysis modality\-specific factors collectively reshape the gut microbial ecology, promoting the overproduction of deleterious metabolites such as indoxyl sulfate, p\-cresyl sulfate and trimethylamine n\-oxide while depleting protective short\-chain fatty acids\.These alterations compromise intestinal barrier integrity, fuel systemic inflammation through endotoxemia and NOD\-like receptor family pyrin domain containing 3 inflammasome activation and have been associated with cardiovascular disease, immune dysfunction, peritonitis, protein\-energy wasting and cognitive impairment\.although probiotic, prebiotic, synbiotic and dietary interventions show promise, heterogeneous responses across trials underscore the inadequacy of one\-size\-fits\-all strategies\.Multi\-omics profiling has revealed substantial interindividual variability in microbial composition and metabolic capacity, providing a rationale for personalized approaches\.The present review synthesizes current evidence on the mechanisms underlying dysbiosis and complications in patients receiving dialysis and proposes a roadmap toward personalized microbiome\-based care to improve clinical outcomes\.Contents 1\. introduction 2\.
