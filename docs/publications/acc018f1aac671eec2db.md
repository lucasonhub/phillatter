# Who May Release an Agent&\#x27;s Promise? Standing over Commitments in Multi\-Party Chat

**Autores:** Lan Wei; Bingyu Shen; Jiaqi Tang; Qing Lu; Kuan Huang; Meng Xu; Miles Q\. Li; Bin Hu; Boyang Li

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

When an assistant serving a team makes a promise to Alice, who may cancel it? We call this standing: only the creditor \(the person owed\) or someone the creditor or operator policy authorizes may release a commitment, regardless of rank\. In a controlled chat environment we vary who cancels and separate two errors: unauthorized release \(obeying someone without standing\) and override \(executing a released promise\)\. Across six models, smaller open ones override the creditor \(Qwen3\.5\-9B: 69%\); larger ones drop promises on a peer&\#x27;s direct word \(Qwen3\.8\-27B: 89%; gpt\-6\.1\-sol: 83%\); nearly all act on a bystander&\#x27;s &quot;go ahead after all&quot;\. Oracles dissociate the errors: stating the rule or the creditor curbs unauthorized release but not override, which the latter even raises in three of four models; among them, only knowing whether the promise is still owed cuts override\. Enabling thinking moves smaller models the way scale does\. A self\-kept commitment ledger stops override in four of five models; with a one\-paragraph release policy it matches or beats the best oracle store, raising gpt\-6\-luna&\#x27;s strict accuracy from 49% \(policy alone\) to 83%\. Code, prompts, outputs and labels are released\.
