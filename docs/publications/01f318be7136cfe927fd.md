# Impact of Class Imbalance Correction on Biological Feature Selection in Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma Methylation Data

**Autores:** Paige Keller; Zachary Caterer; Kaia Hansen; Nijhum Paul; Amanda Warren; Samuel T\. Morehead; Rick J\. Jansen; Rahul Gomes

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Class imbalance is common in biomedical machine learning, yet imbalance\-correction strategies are typically evaluated using predictive performance rather than their effects on biological interpretation\. We evaluated six resampling methods: Random Oversampling \(ROS\), Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique \(SMOTE\), Adaptive Synthetic Sampling \(ADASYN\), Random Undersampling \(RUS\), ClusterCentroids, and AllKNN \(All K\-Nearest Neighbors\), together with a no\-resampling baseline, using DNA methylation data from The Cancer Genome Atlas Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma \(PDAC\) cohort\. The analysis included 485,578 CpG markers of which 406,807 were retained after quality\-control filtering\. A continuous feature\-ranking framework merging Random Forest importance and ANOVA F\-statistics was applied across repeated runs\. Biological relevance was evaluated through hypergeometric enrichment against a curated PDAC gene set and preranked Gene Set Enrichment Analysis \(GSEA\) using 309 Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes \(KEGG\) pathways\. All seven analysis strategies produced significant enrichment for literature\-supported pancreatic cancer\-associated genes, with hypergeometric p\-values ranging from 3\.34×10−16 to 1\.28×10−7\. SMOTE recovered the largest absolute number of PDAC\-associated genes \(141 of 13,827 selected genes\), whereas RUS achieved the greatest fold enrichment \(2\.17\-fold; 88 observed versus 40\.61 expected\)\. Notably, the no\-resampling baseline remained highly competitive, recovering 112 PDAC\-associated genes with 1\.86\-fold enrichment and a p\-value of 5\.27×10−14, indicating that resampling was not necessary to recover biologically relevant features under the evaluated feature\-ranking framework\. Across methods, 169–234 of the 309 tested KEGG pathways were significant at false discovery rate ≤0\.05\. ClusterCentroids produced the largest number of significant pathways \(234\), followed by the no\-resampling baseline \(225\) and RUS \(223\)\. Seven to nine predefined PDAC\-focused pathways were recovered per method, with RUS and ClusterCentroids recovering all nine\. Core pathways consistently identified across all methods included MAPK, PI3K–Akt, TGF\-β, Notch, Hedgehog, focal adhesion, and cell\-cycle signaling\. These findings show that resampling strategies produce distinct trade\-offs between biomarker coverage, biological specificity, and pathway breadth, supporting biologically informed evaluation rather than selection of a single universally optimal imbalance\-correction method\.
