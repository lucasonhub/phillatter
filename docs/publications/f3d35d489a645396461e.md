# Niami, Morteza \(ed\.\): Dynamic Mask Theory

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper argues that the concept of the dynamic mask can be generalized beyond its technical application in information security into a cross\-domain principle of leakage regulation\. While engineering research employs dynamic masking to regulate information disclosure and preserve confidentiality under conditions of observation, this article develops an analogous philosophical framework in which the dynamic mask regulates subjective disclosure in order to preserve existential continuity\. Rather than claiming conceptual identity between these domains, the paper demonstrates a structural isomorphism: both technical systems and human subjects are open systems exposed to continuous observation, and both require adaptive mechanisms that regulate rather than eliminate outward flows\. On this basis, the paper formulates the General Principle of Leakage Regulation \(GPLR\), according to which sustainable persistence depends upon the adaptive control of leakage rather than complete opacity or unrestricted transparency\. Grounded in the Ontology of Finite Continuity and the Philosophy of Finite Continuance, the GPLR is defended not as a metaphorical generalization but as a taxonomic principle identifying an invariant regulatory structure shared by all observable open systems navigating chronic instability\. This framework contributes to contemporary debates on subjectivity, agency, surveillance, and information ethics by providing a unified conceptual vocabulary for understanding leakage across heterogeneous systems\. Keywords: Dynamic Mask, Information Leakage, Subjective Leakage, Political Economy of Erosion, Responsible Suspension, Structural Isomorphism, Ontology of Erosion, Finite Continuance, Threshold of Sufficiency, Homeorhesis\. \(direct link \)
