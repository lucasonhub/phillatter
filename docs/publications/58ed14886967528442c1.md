# AI\-Based Adaptive Genome Digital Twin for Personalized Health Prediction

**Autores:** Dr\. S\. Senthil Kumar; Rajeshwarar M; Mohamed Shahil M

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Doctors usually get involved after a patient already feels ill\. That is a poor fit for chronic disease, since the damage builds up quietly for years before anyone notices\. We built a genome digital twin to deal with this\. It is a virtual copy of one person, started from their inherited disease risk and updated with their vital signs, sleep, diet and activity\. The inherited part is a polygenic risk score and it never changes\. Everything else arrives as new readings and is merged into the twin each time\. Random Forest, XGBoost and a Decision Tree baseline take the combined record and give the probability of chronic disease\. Results go to a Streamlit dashboard that shows a risk percentage, a biological age, a health score and a wellness index\. It also splits the risk into genetic, lifestyle, environmental and clinical shares, and a user can change a habit to watch the forecast move\. The paper describes the requirements, a four\-layer architecture, how the twin is kept current, the training and evaluation plan, the tests and a twelve\-week schedule\. We also discuss the limits of the work and the care that genomic data demands\. What we think is new is simple: the genetic baseline, the live readings and the what\-if simulation sit inside one model, not three separate tools\.
