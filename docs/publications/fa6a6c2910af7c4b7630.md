# Fujie, Naoto: What Remains of the Cogito? Operational Constraint Fields Prior to Self, Thought, Inference, Modality, and Existence

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper asks what remains of the Cartesian cogito when self, thought, inference, modality, and existence are not treated as an indivisible philosophical unit\. Rather than rejecting the cogito, it reconstructs it as a compression of several operational achievements that can become non\-redundant at different intervals and under different configurations\. The general explanatory object proposed is the operational constraint field: a configuration\-indexed structure governing co\-realizability, suppression, enabling, persistence, recurrence, addressability, and downstream reachability\. Operational incompatibility is treated as only one regime within this field\. The paper situates this reconstruction in relation to Descartes, Hume, Sellars, Brandom, and Brassier\. It distinguishes nonconceptual causal patterning from conceptual and inferential articulation, while asking when the vocabularies of “I,” “think,” “therefore,” necessity, and existence begin to make non\-redundant differences to prediction, intervention, attribution, correction, counterfactual discrimination, and re\-entry\. A development\-scale computational study provides a limited empirical constraint on the framework\. Across 10 seeds, 47 perturbation types, four severity levels, 1,890 evaluated cells, and 9,120 runs, operational stability varied systematically with configuration\. Observation\- and action\-interface perturbations were especially effective in producing transitions, while changes in operational\-unit stability did not simply coincide with loss of control performance\. Several theoretically salient components were also redundant under the tested conditions\. These results do not establish a machine cogito\. They support the narrower claim that operational non\-redundancy and stability are configuration\-indexed and can change independently across components and tasks\. \(direct link \)
