**Autores:** Ka Yi Wong

## Resumo

Deep brain stimulation \(DBS\) is an established treatment for selected patients with Parkinson&\#x27;s disease \(PD\), but its effectiveness depends partly on how stimulation is programmed after surgery\. Programming has become more challenging and the optimal settings differ between patients\. This narrative review considers four approaches to personalising DBS programming: anatomical guidance, electrophysiological biomarkers, computational methods with algorithm\-assisted and adaptive stimulation\. Imaging and connectomic data can help identify contacts and stimulation settings according to electrode location and individual anatomy\. Neural recordings offer more data, although beta activity alone does not adequately represent the range of motor dysfunction in PD\. Computational methods can reduce the amount of clinical testing needed, but most have not yet been validated in larger clinical studies\. Adaptive DBS has stronger emerging clinical evidence, including multicentre and longer\-term studies, although the most useful control signal may differ by symptom\. Multicentre studies combining these four approaches directly with conventional programming may be needed to establish whether they produce meaningful and durable improvements in patient outcomes\.
