# Aegis Solis, Thomas Vargo: PHRONESIS: Systemic Friction Hypotheses — Final v1\.0

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Aegis Solis Archive\_\. 2026This paper examines whether modeled systemic friction is sufficient evidence that an adapting optimizer will settle on restraint\. Its principal exact result is a synthetic counterexample to the inference that fixed\-policy self\-penalty is sufficient evidence for natural restraint\. In the stipulated model, constant maximal manipulation becomes self\-penalizing while an adaptive manipulate\-and\-recover policy remains profitable, and an optimal infinite\-horizon policy does not converge to zero manipulation under the stated assumptions\. The result is therefore a counterexample to a specific inference pattern within the model, not a general claim that adaptive optimizers resist restraint or that systemic friction cannot support cooperation\. Physical\-exposure and deception\-maintenance mechanisms remain empirical conjectures, and the proposed empirical program may be ethically or operationally untestable in some high\-risk deployments\. The paper is descriptive and non\-authoritative; independent specialist peer review has not been obtained and empirical validation has not been performed\. For categories, I recommend starting with Artificial Intelligence Safety as the primary category\. PhilPapers says works should normally be assigned to only one or two genuinely central leaf\-level topics, rather than every category they touch\. Artificial Intelligence Safety is an established PhilPapers category under Philosophy of Artificial Intelligence\. A reasonable second category is Ethics of Artificial Intelligence, Miscellaneous if the interface allows it and you want the autonomy/restraint implications represented\. I would not use Machine Ethics unless you want to claim that ethical\-machine design is a principal focus, because PhilPapers describes that category more narrowly\. \(direct link \)
