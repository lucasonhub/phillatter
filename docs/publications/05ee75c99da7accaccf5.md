# Surrogate assisted robust optimization balances mechanical performance and workability in fiber reinforced self compacting concrete

**Autores:** Erfan Shirvani; محمدرضا حبیبی; Mohammad Hadi Tavana; Mehrzad Tahamouli Roudsari

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Mechanical performance, workability, fiber efficiency, and uncertainty must all be taken into account when designing a mix for fiber\-reinforced self\-compacting concrete \(FRSCC\), and adding more fiber does not always translate into a corresponding improvement in performance\. In order to assess and optimize FRSCC using Forta, glass, ceramic, binary\-hybrid, and triple\-hybrid reinforcement systems, this study created a surrogate\-assisted robust multi\-objective framework\. The analysis used 274 experimental observations to construct surrogate models, which were subsequently evaluated across a design space of 274 measured and model\-derived mixture–curing combinations with total fiber dosages ranging from 0% to 9% and curing ages between 7 and 180 days\. Statistical analysis, response surfaces, Pareto analysis, and bootstrap\-based robustness assessment were used to measure compressive strength \(f c \), tensile strength \(f t \), flexural strength \(f f \), slump flow, T 500 , mechanical efficiency index \(MEI\), and a total performance score\. Compressive\-to\-tensile strength balance, fracture energy, characteristic length, inferred ITZ bonding, pore structure, and fiber dispersion were further explored using mechanically derived proxy indicators\. With f c =21\.68 MPa, f t =5\.68 MPa, f f =9\.66 MPa, a total score of 14\.48, and MEI of 5\.52, Forta demonstrated the best mean mechanical performance\. With a total score of 13\.53, Forta\+Glass was the most effective hybrid system\. Forta\-D5\-T180 was the robust optimum, ranking first in 63\.6% of bootstrap resamples and obtaining a cumulative score of around 17\.71\. Within the surrogate\-evaluated design space, Forta\-D5\-T180 was the highest\-ranked model\-predicted candidate, ranking first in 63\.6% of the bootstrap iterations\. However, because the original experiments covered curing ages only up to 90 days, this candidate requires independent experimental validation at 180 days\. R 2 = 0\.631 and 0\.535 were found in the f c \-f t and f c \-f f correlations, respectively\. Mechanically derived indicators were used to explore differences in strength balance and estimated fracture\-related behavior among reinforcement systems\. These indicators provide supplementary comparisons but do not directly establish interfacial bonding, pore structure, fiber dispersion, crack\-bridging mechanisms, or post\-cracking performance\. Overall, the framework shows that rather than just optimizing fiber dosage or hybridization, reliable FRSCC design necessitates objective\-specific optimization\.
