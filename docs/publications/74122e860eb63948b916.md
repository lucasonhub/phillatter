# CT\-detected inguinal and femoral hernias in women: prevalence and interobserver agreement

**Autores:** Sara Gomez Milanés; Oreanna Quintero Rojas; J\. M\. Barajas Martínez; Fernando Ortiz; Andrés Felipe González Serna

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Inguinal and femoral hernias in women show a reported diagnostic gap, with systematic evidence supported by cohorts composed of approximately 84% men\. Materials and methods We conducted a descriptive, retrospective, cross\-sectional observational study at a reference center in Bogotá during the 2024–2025 period\. We included 494 women, 18 years or older, who had undergone abdominal computed tomography\. Double reading by residents was applied, with 2\-of\-3 majority adjudication between the resident and two independent expert reviewers\. Statistical analysis included 95% Wilson confidence intervals, Cohen&\#x27;s kappa with Landis–Koch interpretation, and sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value, reported in accordance with STROBE guidelines\. Results Resident\-diagnosed prevalence was 88 of 494 \(17\.8%; 95% CI 14\.7–21\.4\)\. Adjudicated prevalence was 83 of 494 \(16\.8%; 95% CI 13\.8–20\.4\)\. Five cases \(1\.0%\) were reclassified as extraperitoneal fat by expert consensus\. Findings included indirect inguinal predominance, bilateral distribution in 26\.1%, a high proportion of occult hernias, and underreporting in the original radiology report\. Resident sensitivity was 100% against both expert reviewers\. Specificity was 84\.4% against Reviewer 1 and 47\.4% against Reviewer 2\. Cohen’s kappa for resident vs\. Reviewer 1 was 0\.878; resident vs\. Reviewer 2 was 0\.326; and Reviewer 1 vs\. Reviewer 2 was 0\.408\. Conclusions The radiological prevalence in women sits above the classical clinical range\. Extraperitoneal fat is an overlooked differential diagnosis in female cohorts and accounts for a large part of interreviewer discordance\. The 2\-of\-3 rule works as a replicable method for diagnostic studies with limited resources\.
