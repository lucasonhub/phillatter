# Johnson, Jamison: The Jay and the Quark \- A Perspectival Dual\-Aspect Monism

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper proposes a perspectival dual\-aspect monism, on which reality is composed of a single class of fundamental events, each admitting two descriptions, complete in their own register but mutually exclusive in any single act of apprehension: an interior one in terms of experience and an exterior one in terms of physical structure\. The fundamental event is the actual occasion of Whitehead’s process metaphysics, and the occasion as undergone from within is named the Jay\. The quark is taken as the exemplar of the exterior description: an entity characterized wholly by its structural and relational properties and never encountered in isolation\. Following Russell’s observation that physics discloses the structure of the world but not its intrinsic nature, the paper argues that the two descriptions are two vantages on one occasion, and that which vantage is available depends on the observer’s relation to what is described\. An orientation toward coherence or away from it, named Intent and related to Whitehead’s subjective aim, carries the position toward a graded panpsychism and, read across a field from an attending Center, yields the quantity the framework calls Legibility\. Written term by term, that reading is a weighted average of the cosines between the reader’s facing and every facing present, a form that gives the Trace, met in a later gathering, a place in the notation\. Confinement is then read at a second scale: an invention is proposed as a bound state of perished occasions whose exterior is the made thing and whose interior is the accumulated Intent that settled each part\. The paper keeps apart the two orders of priority its companion journals require: the occasions are fundamental in what is undergone, and the whole, which those journals argue is one consciousness, is prior in grounding\. Spinoza, whose one substance is known under thought and under extension, is taken as the point where this whole\-first view and the dual\-aspect view first meet\. The paper proposes that, from the whole’s vantage, which Boethius’s definition of eternity and Aquinas’s image of the road seen from a height describe, the occasions are present at once\. The combination problem then gives way to the individuation problem, how one consciousness comes to be many perspectives, which the paper acknowledges as the framework’s principal open question\. \(direct link \)
