## Resumo

The emergence of Generation Z has coincided with major changes in education, communication, technology and social life\. Students of this generation learn in an environment shaped by smartphones, social media, digital platforms, online resources and increasingly, generative artificial intelligence \(AI\)\. These developments have expanded access to information and created opportunities for personalized and innovative learning, but have also introduced challenges such as distraction, information overload, social comparison, digital well\-being concerns and academic pressure\. The present paper examines the educational implications of Generation Z in relation to digital culture, educational innovation and social transformation, while drawing upon an earlier empirical study of anxiety and academic achievement among 400 XII\-grade students in Jamshedpur\. The sample consisted of 200 Arts and 200 Science students preparing for the ISC Board Examination\. Anxiety was examined through Examination, Social, Personal and Family Anxiety, while academic achievement was assessed through pre\-board examination marks\. The findings indicate predominantly moderate anxiety in both groups\. No significant differences were found between Arts and Science students in Examination and Social Anxiety, whereas significant differences were found in Personal and Family Anxiety\. A significant difference was also reported in academic achievement, with Arts students obtaining a higher mean score than Science students\.
