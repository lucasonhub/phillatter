# From Silent Risk to Completed Care: Community\-Based Glaucoma Detection and the Referral Gap Among Adults Aged 40 Years and Older

**Autores:** Deepak Bariyan; Karthik SD

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Glaucoma frequently remains asymptomatic until irreversible visual damage occurs, making community detection and successful referral essential for blindness prevention\. Methods: This prospective community\-based study screened 620 adults aged ≥40 years through Ophthalmology and Community Medicine services\. Demographic and clinical risk factors, intraocular pressure, optic\-disc findings, glaucoma awareness, and healthcare\-access characteristics were assessed\. Glaucoma suspects were referred for comprehensive evaluation, and referral completion within 30 days was recorded\. Multivariable logistic regression identified independent determinants of referral completion\. Results: Of 620 participants, 102 \(16\.5%\) were classified as glaucoma suspects and 78 \(76\.5%\) completed referral\. Glaucoma was newly confirmed in 29 referral completers, representing 4\.7% of the screened population\. Referral completion was lower among rural residents \(66\.7%; p=0\.013\), participants with transportation difficulty \(53\.3%; p&lt;0\.001\), and those perceiving no need for evaluation because they were asymptomatic \(57\.9%; p&lt;0\.001\)\. Transportation difficulty \(aOR=0\.27\) and absent perceived need \(aOR=0\.32\) independently reduced referral completion, whereas glaucoma awareness \(aOR=2\.88\) and family support \(aOR=2\.79\) increased completion\. Conclusion: Community glaucoma screening identified substantial previously unrecognized disease; however, social and access barriers contributed importantly to referral attrition\. Integrating screening with education and patient\-navigation strategies may strengthen continuity of glaucoma care\. Keywords: Community screening, Glaucoma awareness, Glaucoma, Preventable blindness, Referral adherence
