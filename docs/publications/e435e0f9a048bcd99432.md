# ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AND ITS EFFECTS ON STUDENTS’ READING AND WRITING SKILLS AT THE SECONDARY SCHOOL LEVEL

**Autores:** Sana Sarwar; Rashid Minas Wattoo; Ms\. Saba Gul

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Although international scholarship on AI in education had expanded rapidly, empirical evidence concerning its relationship with the reading and writing skills of secondary\-school students in Pakistan had remained limited\. The present study examined the relationship between AI use and the reading and writing performance of male students enrolled in public secondary schools in District Sialkot, Punjab\. A quantitative, cross\-sectional, correlational research design was adopted\. The population comprised male students enrolled in Grades 9 and 10 in government secondary schools, from whom a multistage stratified random sample of 270 students was selected\. Data were collected through an AI Use in Learning Questionnaire, a researcher\-developed reading comprehension test, and an analytical writing performance test\. The study differentiated between productive and substitutive forms of AI use\. Productive use involved explanation seeking, guided practice, idea generation, verification, formative feedback, and reflective revision, whereas substitutive use involved outsourcing comprehension and composition, copying AI\-generated responses, and accepting machine\-generated content without critical evaluation\. Descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, analysis of variance, and hierarchical multiple regression were employed to examine the relationships among the variables\. The study provided a contextually grounded framework for understanding AI\-supported literacy, assessment integrity, digital inequality, teacher mediation, and responsible technology integration in Pakistani secondary education\.
