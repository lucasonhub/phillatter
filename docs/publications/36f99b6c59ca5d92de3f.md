# Beyond Accuracy: A Structured Sensitivity Analysis Framework for CNN\-based Stunting Identification under Realistic Data Constraints

**Autores:** Rostika Flora; Samsuryadi; Mohammad Zulkarnain; Nur Alam Fajar; Yuliarti; Derry Alamsyah; Annisah Biancika Jasmine; Prihatini Dini Novitasari; Dindi Paizer; Danny Kusuma Aerosta

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Stunting remains a major public health challenge, while recent facial\-image studies report near\-perfect CNN performance under conditions that may not reflect real\-world deployment\.This study proposes a sensitivity\-analysis framework characterizing how six factors, within\-subject correlation, pseudo\-capture augmentation, evaluation\-set augmentation, training\-set augmentation, class resampling, and natural class distribution, affect reported performance\.EfficientNet\-B0 pretrained on ImageNet served as baseline across seven dataset\-scheme comparisons and three optimizers using 190 facial images from three Indonesian health centers\.Within\-subject correlation, examined through leakage of transformed derivatives across splits rather than independently captured repeats, was the dominant source of inflation, raising F2\-score by over 22% across all optimizers and establishing subject\-disjoint splitting as a minimum requirement\.Remaining comparisons were each confounded by simultaneous changes in sample size, augmentation, or class distribution: SGD stayed stable under reduced training volume, while Adam and AdamW repeatedly collapsed toward near\-zero specificity, indicating adaptive optimizers depend more on training volume to avoid degenerate behavior\.Under natural imbalance, dynamic thresholding collapsed to zero specificity, whereas Weighted CE with a static threshold preserved non\-zero performance for both classes, yielding F2\-score 65\.37%, sensitivity 74\.93%, specificity 36\.38%\.Extending this to AlexNet, ResNet34, ResNet\-50, and MambaVision\-T showed no single architecture dominating: MambaVision\-T led on sensitivity and F2\-score, AlexNet on accuracy, and ResNet34 on specificity, each trading off F2\-score\.Wide, overlapping confidence intervals indicate these differences are descriptive rather than definitive\.Evaluation protocol and optimizer choice strongly affect reported performance, while architecture choice trades sensitivity against specificity without resolving the low\-specificity limitation\.
