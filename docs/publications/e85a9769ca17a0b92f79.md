# Doctrinal Problems of Applying Copyright to Literary Works Created by Artificial Intelligence

**Autores:** Nurboyeva Durdona

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The article analyses five doctrinal problems in applying copyright to literary works created by artificial intelligence \(AI\)\. First, it shows that the classical theories \(Locke’s labour theory, Hegel’s personality theory, the utilitarian theory and the fair remuneration theory\) come into conflict when applied to AI activity\. Second, it examines the concept of “author” under the legislation of Uzbekistan, Russia and the United States and under the Berne Convention, together with the question of AI subjecthood\. Third, it addresses the originality requirement, fourth, moral rights, and fifth, the idea–expression dichotomy\. The author concludes that all these problems share a single cause: classical doctrines rest on human consciousness, will and personality\. Accordingly, AI should be treated as a tool rather than a subject\. The article also proposes the “objective control criterion doctrine”, which ties the emergence of copyright to objective, verifiable criteria: purposeful direction of AI activity, creative selection among outputs, and determination of the work’s final content and form\.
