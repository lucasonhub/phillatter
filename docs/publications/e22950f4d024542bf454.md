# Changes in precipitation seasonality over the Indochina Peninsula during 1981–2020: observational evidence and attribution of anthropogenic forcing

**Autores:** Yueyue Zhang; Kun Yang; Shihong Du

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Precipitation seasonality is a fundamental characteristic of monsoon climates, with direct implications for regional water resources and ecosystem stability\. Here, we investigate changes in precipitation seasonality over the Indochina Peninsula and assess the responses to different external forcings\. We use multiple observation\-based precipitation datasets and CMIP6 single\-forcing simulations\. Precipitation seasonality is quantified as the difference between precipitation accumulated during the consecutive wettest and driest 6\-month periods identified using a moving\-window approach\. Long\-term observational trends are estimated using ordinary least\-squares regression, while CMIP6 forcing\-response trends are quantified using model\-specific Theil\-Sen slopes and an equally weighted multi\-model mean\. The observation\-based datasets show a strengthening of precipitation seasonality across the region, with trends of \+8\.92% decade −1 in Climate Prediction Center, \+3\.30% decade −1 in CHIRPS, and \+4\.24% decade −1 in GPCP, whereas ERA5 reanalysis exhibits a positive but non\-significant trend\. Analysis of the wet\-season and dry\-season components indicates that the observed amplification is mainly attributable to enhanced wet\-season precipitation rather than changes in dry\-season precipitation\. CMIP6 single\-forcing simulations show that greenhouse gas forcing produces the strongest positive multi\-model mean seasonality index response, with positive trends in four of the five individual models\. In contrast, aerosol forcing produces negative trends in all five models, while natural forcing produces negative trends in four of the five models\. These results support an important role of greenhouse gas forcing in the amplification of precipitation seasonality, while also highlighting inter\-model uncertainty in the magnitude of the simulated responses\. Compared with prior global\-scale findings, the Indochina Peninsula exhibits pronounced seasonal\-contrast amplification, highlighting the sensitivity of this monsoon region to anthropogenic climate change and its potential implications for wet\-season flooding and seasonal water stress\.
