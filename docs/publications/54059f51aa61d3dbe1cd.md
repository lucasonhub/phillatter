# Single\-Water Microsolvation Promotes Proton Sharing in a Neutral Formic Acid–Ammonia Complex

**Autores:** Fan Xie; Luigi Crisci; Wenhao Sun; Federico Lazzari; Vincenzo Barone; Melanie Schnell

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Can single\-water microsolvation promote proton sharing in a neutral acid–base complex? We address this question for formic acid–ammonia by isolating its monohydrated complex with broadband rotational spectroscopy and interpreting the resolved 14N hyperfine structure with calibrated high\-level quantum chemistry\. The observed complex is a single dominant HCOOH–NH3–H2O isomer with effective Cs symmetry, in which NH3 remains the primary proton acceptor, while water closes a cooperative hydrogen\-bond cycle by accepting from NH3 and donating to the carbonyl oxygen\. Rather than simply stabilizing the binary acid–base contact, this water molecule measurably increases its proton\-sharing character\. A resolved A/E tunneling pattern from NH3 internal rotation supplies the missing orientation needed to rotate the experimental 14N quadrupole tensor into the local ammonia frame, yielding an experimental χzz component and a quadrupole\-derived Townes–Dailey ionicity of approximately 26%\. High\-level structures show the same directional response: O–H elongation, H···N contraction, and O···N contraction relative to HCOOH–NH3\. Together, these results show that one water molecule can promote proton sharing in a neutral formic acid–ammonia complex without producing a separated ion pair\.
