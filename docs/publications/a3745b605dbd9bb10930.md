# Perceived reporting\-system function, reporting barriers, and formal near\-miss reporting among operating room nurses in China: a cross\-sectional study

**Autores:** PingPing Li; Lixue Zhang; Qin Cao; Tan Xin; Yanling Fan; Hui Hu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Near\-miss reporting provides an early signal of perioperative system failure, yet formal reporting remains limited in operating rooms\. This study examined correlates of formal near\-miss reporting among operating room \(OR\) nurses at a two\-campus tertiary hospital in China\. Methods In this cross\-sectional study, 2,281 OR nurses returned a structured questionnaire and 2,200 were analyzed, covering self\-reported 12\-month near\-miss observation and reporting, four safety\-culture indices, workload, and professional characteristics\. Exposures were purpose\-built indices rather than validated scales\. Multivariable logistic regression, with a prespecified parsimonious model as the principal specification, examined factors associated with formal reporting among observers \(Model A\) and, as an exploratory aim, near\-miss observation in the full sample \(Model B\)\. Results Of 2,200 OR nurses, 1,551 \(70\.5%\) observed at least one perioperative near\-miss during the preceding 12 months, yet only 478 \(30\.8%\) formally reported an event; the remaining 1,073 \(69\.2%\) made no report, a nurse\-level rather than event\-level measure\. Count discrepancies predominated \(47\.3%\)\. In the principal model, perceived reporting\-system function was the strongest correlate of formal reporting \(aOR: 2\.19 per 1\-SD increase, 95% CI: 1\.84–2\.60\), whereas greater barrier burden was inversely associated \(0\.67, 0\.57–0\.79\); speaking\-up and safety climate, which overlapped substantially, were not independently associated\. The reporting\-system association persisted after removal of the feedback item, which may reflect earlier reporting, and ordinal modeling of reported event volume reproduced both associations\. Model A discrimination was acceptable \(AUC 0\.741\)\. In the full sample, higher time pressure was associated with near\-miss observation \(1\.14, 1\.03–1\.25\), but this exploratory model discriminated poorly \(AUC 0\.600\)\. Conclusion Perioperative near misses were commonly observed but seldom documented\. These cross\-sectional, self\-reported associations cannot establish direction, but they are consistent with formal reporting depending on accessible, easy\-to\-use reporting systems and on reduced hierarchy\-related and relational barriers, rather than on willingness to speak up alone\.
