# A potential role for artificial intelligence in early STEMI diagnosis and reperfusion decision\-making: a retrospective analysis of ChatGPT’s performance in ECG interpretation and treatment guidance

**Autores:** Hüseyin Tezcan; Abdullah Tunçez; Yasin Özen; Muhammed Ulvi Yalçın; Mehmet Murat YİĞİTBAŞI; Kadri Murat Gürses

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Prompt diagnosis and timely reperfusion are essential in ST\-segment elevation myocardial infarction \(STEMI\)\. Large language models, such as ChatGPT, may assist non\-specialist providers by rapidly interpreting electrocardiograms \(ECGs\) and recommending evidence\-based treatment, particularly in high\-volume or resource\-limited settings\. To assess ChatGPT’s accuracy in localizing STEMI \(anterior, inferior, lateral, or true left bundle branch block \[LBBB\]\) on 12\-lead ECGs, and guiding fibrinolysis vs\. primary percutaneous coronary intervention \(PCI\) based on estimated travel times to a tertiary center\. In this retrospective single\-center study, 210 STEMI patients who underwent coronary angiography were included\. For each case, anonymized ECG data and the location where the ECG was recorded were input into ChatGPT\. Using GPS\-based travel estimates to Konya City Hospital, ChatGPT recommended fibrinolysis if transit exceeded 120 min, otherwise PCI\. Diagnostic accuracy was compared with cardiologist\-confirmed STEMI localization, and agreement with real\-life treatment decisions was evaluated via Cohen’s kappa\. ChatGPT correctly identified 192 of 210 STEMIs \(91\.4% accuracy; Cohen’s kappa = 0\.88, 95% CI: 0\.82–0\.93\)\. Of 60 patients &gt; 120 min away, ChatGPT advised fibrinolysis in 57 \(95%\), matching actual fibrinolysis in 49 \(82%\)\. For 150 patients ≤ 120 min away, ChatGPT recommended PCI in 148 \(98\.7%\), aligning with real\-life management in 144 \(96%\)\. Overall concordance with real treatment decisions was 90%\. By integrating ECG data with GPS\-based transit times, ChatGPT achieved high accuracy in STEMI localization and guideline\-concordant reperfusion recommendations\. This approach may help expedite care where cardiology expertise is not immediately available\.
