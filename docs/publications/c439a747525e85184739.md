# Artificial Intelligence as a Catalyst for Enhancing Safety Measures and User Satisfaction in Federal University Libraries in North\-East Nigeria: A Conceptual Framework

**Autores:** Patrick Phanuel Umoriya; Yinasim Pius Musa; Igbinosa Clifford Osayomore

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial Intelligence \(AI\) has emerged as one of the most transformative technologies influencing library operations and information service delivery across the globe\. The increasing complexity of security threats, technological advancement, and users&\#x27; expectations has necessitated the adoption of intelligent systems capable of improving safety management and enhancing user satisfaction in academic libraries\. Federal university libraries in North\-East Nigeria are expected to provide safe, secure, technology\-driven, and user\-centred learning environments that support teaching, learning, research, and innovation\. However, inadequate technological infrastructure, poor funding, limited AI adoption, insufficient digital competencies among library personnel, cybersecurity threats, and weak institutional support continue to hinder effective safety management and quality service delivery\. This paper conceptually examines Artificial Intelligence as a catalyst for enhancing safety measures and user satisfaction in federal university libraries in North\-East Nigeria\. Specifically, the paper discusses the concepts of Artificial Intelligence, safety measures, user satisfaction, and academic libraries; examines relevant theoretical perspectives; identifies challenges affecting AI adoption; and proposes strategies for integrating AI into library safety management\. The paper adopts a conceptual review methodology through the analysis of current scholarly literature\. It concludes that AI technologies such as intelligent surveillance systems, biometric access control, predictive analytics, chatbots, smart sensors, facial recognition technologies, and machine learning applications possess significant potential for improving library safety, operational efficiency, and users&\#x27; satisfaction\. The paper recommends increased investment in AI infrastructure, continuous staff capacity building, supportive institutional policies, and strategic partnerships to facilitate sustainable AI integration in federal university libraries\.
