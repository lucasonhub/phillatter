# Mapping Thermal Transformation in India’s Coastal Megacities: A Spatiotemporal Analysis of Land Surface Temperature and Urban Heat Island Expansion

**Autores:** Amit Kumar Singh

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accelerated land transformation may exacerbate urban heat, however coastal municipalities can benefit from sea\-breeze cooling, elevated humidity, and pronounced diurnal temperature variations\.This research integrates documented remote\-sensing metrics for Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai from 2001/2003 to 2023 to assess urban land\-cover changes, daily cooling patterns, core\-periphery temperature variations, and humid heat\.The Centre for Science and Environment&\#x27;s 2024 evaluation obtained source metrics from MODIS land\-surface temperature and Landsat land\-use/vegetation datasets; this study independently recalculated alterations, rates, and precise small\-sample statistics\.The accumulated proportion rose throughout all urban areas, from an average of 46\.37% in three cities to 68\.57%, but the average green share declined from 28\.33% to 21\.67%\.Chennai had the most rapid transformation, with the built\-up area increasing by 42\.8 percentage points and the green area decreasing by 13\.7 points\.In 2023, Kolkata retained its status as the most developed city with an 80\.1% built\-up area\.Urban centers at night were warmer than their outskirts in Mumbai \(\+0\.4°C\),Kolkata \(\+1\.2°C\), and Chennai \(\+0\.9°C\), despite Mumbai&\#x27;s core being 3\.5°C colder during the day\.Documented daily cooling decreased by 24% in Mumbai and 5% in Chennai; the associated reconstructed cooling values from previous periods were 10\.26°C and 10\.21°C, respectively\.Increased humidity contributed an estimated 5\.0\-6\.6°C to perceived heat stress\.The limited number of three cities rendered paired and exact permutation tests ineffective; the robust descriptive correlation between the ultimate built\-up share and nighttime core heat \(r = 0\.988\) lacked conventional significance\.The results indicate that coastal urban heat is a multifaceted, temporally variable occurrence\.Planning must emphasise nighttime thermal retention, uninterrupted blue\-green infrastructures, cool materials, ventilation pathways, and humidity\-sensitive heat management technologies\.
