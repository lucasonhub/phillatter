# Generating MeSH terms using large language models: From individual models to ensemble strategies

**Autores:** Danielle Hyunsook Lee

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study evaluates how effectively large language models \(LLMs\) can assign Medical Subject Headings \(MeSH\) to biomedical articles, examining individual models and ensemble strategies\. Five LLMs were tested: two biomedical \(MMed\-Llama\-3\-8B and Med42\-v2\-70B\), two general\-purpose \(Mistral\-7B and Llama\-3\-70B\), and one commercial \(Claude\)\. Open\-source models were adapted through Low\-Rank Adaptation fine\-tuning and retrieval\-augmented generation \(RAG\); Claude through five\-shot prompting and RAG\. Four ensemble strategies were applied: majority voting, Bayesian fusion, reliability\-weighted voting, and cascade\. On 1730 target articles, Claude with RAG achieved the highest F1\-score \(58\.4%\) through high recall but lower precision, while fine\-tuned Llama\-70B and Med42\-70B showed the opposite pattern\. The ensemble strategies compensated for this precision–recall trade\-off: majority voting \(F1 = 67\.8%\) and reliability\-weighted voting \(F1 = 67\.6%\) achieved balanced performance, exceeding the best\-performing Claude by more than nine points\. Bayesian fusion achieved the highest recall \(82\.5%\), and cascade achieved the highest precision \(82\.1%\)\. These precision–recall profiles map onto specific stages of the MeSH indexing workflow: Bayesian fusion for candidate generation, cascade for filtering, and majority voting or reliability\-weighted voting for end\-to\-end indexing\. The study therefore gives indexing practitioners a basis for selecting a configuration by task rather than by aggregate accuracy alone\.
