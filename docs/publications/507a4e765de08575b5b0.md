# When Reality Outpaces Cognition: A New Scaling Principle for Reliable Intelligence

**Autores:** Ahsan Adeel; Talha Bin Riaz

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

This paper challenges the understanding capabilities of existing AI paradigms and emerging world models by asking a fundamental question: How quickly can reality change before an intelligent system can no longer reliably perceive, reason, and act? We formulate this as a theory\- and model\-agnostic challenge in which systems must infer reliable actions for increasingly distant future states across a growing observation\-\-reality gap\. We operationalize this challenge through the effective Speed of Coherent Cognition \(\\\(S\_C^\{\\mathrm\{eff\}\}\\\)\), defined as the highest tested Speed of Reality \(\\\(S\_R\\\)\) through which a system continuously satisfies a predefined behavioural reliability criterion from baseline\. As a proof of concept, reinforcement\-learning agents are trained at the baseline \\\(S\_R\\\) and evaluated, without retraining, as \\\(S\_R\\\) is increased by up to \\\(50\\times\\\) while computational resources remain fixed\. We compare Transformer and gated\-MLP baselines with \\\(Co^4\\\), an architecture inspired by cellular mechanisms implicated in conscious processing and awake thought\. Across the tested scenarios, \\\(Co^4\\\) achieves substantially higher \\\(S\_C^\{\\mathrm\{eff\}\}\\\) than the baselines, motivating \\\(S\_C^\{\\mathrm\{eff\}\}\\\) as a new benchmark and the scaling of coherent cognition as a principle for reliable AI\.
