# Clinicopathologic Characteristics, Treatment Patterns, and Survival Outcomes in Malignant Mesothelioma: A Population\-based SEER Study, 2004\-2023

**Autores:** Enes Yeşilbaş; Galip Can Uyar; Kadriye Başkurt; Güner Akgüner; İsmail Dilli; Mustafa Altınbaş

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: Treatment for malignant mesothelioma has changed over two decades\.We described clinicopathologic features, treatment patterns, and survival by primary site, diagnosis era, age group, and histologic subtype\. Material and Methods:This population\-based retrospective cohort study used the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results \(SEER\) 17 registries and included a total of 10,873 patients diagnosed between 2004 and 2023\.Overall survival \(OS\) and cancer\-specific survival \(CSS\) were analyzed with Kaplan\-Meier estimation, log\-rank testing, Cox regression, and Fine\-Gray competing\-risk regression\.Results: Pleural mesothelioma occurred in 8,886 patients, peritoneal mesothelioma in 1,377 patients, and other or unknown sites in 610 patients\.Men comprised 72\.7%; 63\.6% had distant\-stage disease; and 43\.0%lacked a specified subtype\.Chemotherapy was recorded in 48\.5%, surgery in 26\.9%, and radiation in 9\.8%\.Median OS was 9 months and median CSS was 11 months; median OS was 17 months in peritoneal disease and 9 months in pleural disease\.Peritoneal site was associated with lower mortality \[adjusted hazard ratio \(aHR\): 0\.81; 95% confidence interval \(CI\): 0\.76\-0\.88\];diagnosis during 2016\-2023 was also associated with lower mortality \(aHR: 0\.84; 95% CI: 0\.80\-0\.88\)\.Fibrous histology \(aHR: 1\.99; 95% CI: 1\.85\-2\.13\)and age 80 years or older \(aHR: 2\.00; 95% CI: 1\.86\-2\.16\)were associated with higher mortality; all p&lt;0\.001\.Fine\-Gray estimates were directionally consistent \(peritoneal site: subdistribution HR: 0\.87\)\.Conclusion: Mesothelioma survival remained poor overall, but was better among patients diagnosed in 2016\-2023\.Advanced age, fibrous histology, and unspecified subtype were associated with higher adjusted mortality\.More complete subtype ascertainment and reporting would strengthen prognostic interpretation and registry\-based research\.
