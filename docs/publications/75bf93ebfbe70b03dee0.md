# Improving energy efficiency and carbon balance through conservation and precision agriculture with respect to conventional agriculture: a methodological approach

**Autores:** Francisco Márquez‐García; Miguel Angel Repullo\-Ruibérriz de Torres; Gottlieb Basch; Emilio Jesús González\-Sánchez

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Conservation Agriculture \(CA\) is based on three core principles that, at farm scale, imply minimizing soil disturbance through tillage, maintaining a permanent soil cover with residues and diversifying the species in crop rotations\. Precision Agriculture \(PA\) envisages a more efficient use of inputs by reducing the overlaps between machine passes\. The combination of both contributes to a more sustainable agricultural system\. Methods A four\-season study was carried out to assess the energy consumption and carbon balance of a combined CA and PA system\. No\-tillage \(NT\) with residue retention and tractor guidance assistance \(GA\) was compared with conventional tillage \(CT\) in three annual crops: wheat, sunflower and legumes\. To analyse the data obtained, a simple methodological approach was developed to facilitate energy and carbon balance calculations\. Results No significant differences were found in yield between systems\. NT with GA reduced fuel consumption by around 50%, and the energy parameters studied improved compared with CT\. Across crops, energy efficiency relative to CT ranked as follows: sunflower &gt; wheat &gt; legumes\. A similar pattern was observed for carbon use efficiency and carbon use productivity\. Conclusions The results show the suitability of jointly using CA and PA techniques, such as tractor guidance under NT soil management\.
