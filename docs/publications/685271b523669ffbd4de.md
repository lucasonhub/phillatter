# Quantum eddy current wall thickness tomography using nitrogen\-vacancy vector magnetometry for onshore crude oil pipeline integrity assessment

**Autores:** Kuan Fu; Rui Li; Pengchao Chen; Xiaoben Liu; Lili Zuo

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

As core infrastructure for global energy distribution, onshore long\-distance crude oil pipelines face multiple challenges, including wax deposition, structural stress concentration, and H 2 S release\. Traditional non\-destructive testing \(NDT\) techniques, such as eddy current testing and magnetic flux leakage \(MFL\), suffer from limitations including low spatial resolution, limited penetration depth, and directional blind spots, making high\-resolution and omnidirectional defect identification difficult\. Diamond nitrogen\-vacancy \(NV\) centers have shown strong potential for high\-precision vector magnetic sensing\. However, their application in pipeline inspection remains limited by insufficient environmental adaptability, the lack of quantitative wall\-thickness inversion models, and difficulties in large\-scale sensor array integration\. To address these issues, this study develops a hybrid framework combining NV vector magnetometry with quantum eddy\-current tomography\. Directional dependence is substantially reduced through a three\-phase orthogonal excitation field, as verified by controlled comparisons with single\-phase and dual\-phase excitation, while deterministically laser\-written NV centers integrated with metal lenses enable sensor array implementation\. A machine\-learning\-assisted tomographic reconstruction algorithm is further proposed to convert discrete magnetic measurements into continuous wall\-thickness images\. The results show a detection accuracy of 61\.0% for a controlled 1 μm nominal wall\-thickness perturbation and a coherence time of 70\.5 μs under 25 nT noise\. In the matched field evaluation, the proposed system achieved an overall detection rate of 93\.5%, compared with 65\.6% for MFL under the tested conditions, while ultrasonic calibration indicated close agreement between reconstructed and reference thicknesses\. These findings provide preliminary evidence for the feasibility of NV\-based quantitative pipeline inspection under the evaluated conditions\.
