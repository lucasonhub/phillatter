# Embedded AI through algorithm–architecture co\-design: a structured survey, taxonomy, and research directions

**Autores:** Steve Charnot Lengui Lengui; Messaoud Ahmed Ouameur; Miloud Bagaa; Michel Lemaire; Daniel Massicotte

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Embedded AI deployed on highly resource\-constrained devices often operates under milliwatt\-scale power, kilobyte\-range memory, and millisecond\-level latency constraints, although the broader embedded/edge spectrum spans much wider resource ranges\. Traditional separated design flows, where algorithms and hardware platforms evolve independently, can lead to suboptimal implementations that fail to meet these constraints simultaneously\. Algorithm–architecture co\-design has emerged as a systematic approach to address this gap through cross\-layer optimization\. This article provides a structured, taxonomy\-driven survey of co\-design for embedded AI\. We conducted a systematic search across IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, Scopus, and Web of Science \(2015–2026\), screening 1,247 records down to a final mapped corpus of 122 publications\. We organize the literature along four intervention layers: algorithm\-level optimization \(pruning, quantization, neural architecture search\), hardware specialization \(instruction set extensions, dataflow architectures, memory hierarchies\), compiler\-driven transformation \(operator fusion, tiling, scheduling\), and runtime adaptation \(dynamic precision scaling, resource\-aware scheduling\)\. For each layer, we analyze how design choices affect five core dimensions: accuracy, latency, energy consumption, memory footprint, and reliability\. Applying the taxonomy to the complete 122\-publication mapping yields the following corpus\-level statistics: 103 of 122 publications \(84\.4%\) carry the broader cross\-layer flag, whereas 34 of 122 \(27\.9%\) are explicitly coded as having an explicit cross\-layer analysis\. The co\-design classification distribution is HO = 19 \(15\.6%\), HAC = 72 \(59\.0%\), and FSC = 31 \(25\.4%\)\. The taxonomy also reveals a highly uneven distribution of contributions across cells, with a small number of well\-populated cells and a larger set of sparsely populated cells\. These figures are derived directly from the complete S1 mapping and are reported as descriptive corpus statistics rather than as universal measurements\. Three overarching research themes emerge: \(i\) verification\-aware co\-design, \(ii\) agentic and digital\-twin\-driven design automation, and \(iii\) AI\-native hardware substrates\. The taxonomy exposes a persistent gap between algorithm\-level innovation and hardware\-level realization, with cross\-layer co\-design remaining largely ad hoc and rarely validated under shared experimental conditions\. This survey offers a structured reference for researchers and practitioners developing embedded AI systems\.
