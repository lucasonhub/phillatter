# Establishing the reliability of non\-contrast 4D flow in congenital heart disease at 1\.5T

**Autores:** Nicholas K\. Brown; Erin K\. Romberg; Christopher Schmitt; Nelangi M\. Pinto; Waylon Howard; Joshua Daniel Robinson; Brian Soriano; Sujatha Buddhe

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: 4D flow has been shown to correlate well with 2D phase\-contrast \(2D PC\) flow measurements, although most of the current literature is based on post\-contrast imaging\. OBJECTIVE: Given recent interest in non\-contrast techniques, we hypothesized that non\-contrast 4D flow \(NC 4D Flow\) would correlate well with 2D PC and ventricular stroke volumes\. METHODS AND MATERIALS: A prospective cohort of pediatric and young adult patients with suspected/confirmed congenital heart disease \(CHD\) underwent cardiac MRI at 1\.5T with acquisition of NC 4D Flow and 2D PC\. Pearson correlation, linear mixed\-effect correlations, and Bland\-Altman comparisons were performed to compare these methods with secondary analyses of internal consistency and image quality/noise\. RESULTS: In our cohort of 30 patients \(age, 8\-20 years\), NC 4D Flow correlated strongly with 2D PC for net flow \(r=0\.76\), peak velocity \(r=0\.9, P&lt;0\.001\), and regurgitant fraction \(r=0\.75, P&lt;0\.001\)\. By Bland\-Altman comparison, NC 4D Flow was 10\.1% lower for net flows and 18\.9% higher for peak velocity compared to 2D PC\. NC 4D Flow also slightly underestimated ventricular stroke volumes \(18\.1% lower for ascending aorta vs\. left ventricular stroke volume and 13\.9% lower for main pulmonary artery vs\. right ventricular stroke volume\)\. Both datasets were internally consistent; 2D PC had a higher relative signal intensity, but no difference in signal\-to\-noise or contrast\-to\-noise ratios \(SNR/CNR\)\. CONCLUSION: Non\-contrast 4D flow correlates well with 2D PC in CHD with a slight underestimation in flows and overestimation in peak velocities, but good internal consistency and comparable SNR/CNR\.
