# Zhang, Qizheng &amp; Ning, Huansheng: From Passive Delegates to Strategic Existence: A CPST\-Cyberism\-Cybersophy Interpretation of SocialRL

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

When an AI agent negotiates in a user’s name, its strategic capacity can affect task performance as well as the user’s agency, cognitive control, social identity, and relations of responsibility\. Using the fixed SocialRL report as its technical case, this paper combines documentary verification, conceptual analysis, and normative reconstruction\. The analysis proceeds in three stages\. CPST locates the cross\-domain links among information representation, strategy generation, social interaction, and realized consequences\. Cyberism diagnoses problems of authorization, institutional order, cognitive security, identity, and responsibility\. Cybersophy reconstructs the normative boundaries of truthfulness, identity consistency, and value\. Following SocialRL’s reported fabrication of market references, the paper separates benchmark observations, theoretical interpretations, and conditional recommendations for deployment\. It defines strategic existence as a relational and functional concept\. An agent’s strategic capacity can shape how a user acts, is represented, and bears consequences through chains of authorization, attribution, and consequence\. This definition does not attribute consciousness, personhood, or independent moral status to machines\. The paper further proposes fiduciary strategic existence as an analytic and design framework that remains empirically unvalidated\. The framework first restricts action through authorization, truthfulness, and prohibitions, and then optimizes utility, relational quality, and procedural prudence within the permitted set\. The central question is therefore not merely whether an agent can gain an advantage, but in whose name it acts, within which limits, and under whose understanding, control, and responsibility\.
