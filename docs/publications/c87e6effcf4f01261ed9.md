# Institutional student support and psychological well\-being in Chinese vocational colleges: a two\-institution cross\-sectional study

**Autores:** Wang Hui; Huang Huifeng

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Institutional student support is a potentially modifiable feature of the educational environment, but its relationship with distinct forms of positive psychological functioning remains underexamined in vocational higher education\. This two\-institution cross\-sectional study examined associations between perceived quality of institutional student\-support services and the six dimensions of Ryff’s psychological well\-being among 383 students from public vocational colleges in Fujian Province, China\. Students completed an 18\-item Ryff\-based well\-being measure and a contextualized 50\-item service\-quality questionnaire covering accessibility, reliability, responsiveness, assurance, and empathy\. Descriptive analyses addressed students’ well\-being and service\-quality profiles, and six multiple linear regression models examined associations between observed composite scores\. Students reported comparatively stronger positive relations with others and purpose in life, whereas self\-acceptance and personal growth were less favorable\. Support services were generally rated favorably; accessibility was the lowest\-rated service\-quality domain and showed positive adjusted associations with all six well\-being dimensions, whereas the other service\-quality domains showed outcome\-specific patterns\. The models explained 53\.3%–71\.3% of outcome variance\. These large R 2 values should be interpreted cautiously because predictors and outcomes were collected through same\-session self\-report measures and may be affected by common\-method variance or response\-style effects\. The findings identify service access and other service\-quality domains as candidate priorities for institutional assessment, not as evidence that improving accessibility will directly improve well\-being\. Prospective, multi\-source studies with validated measurement models are needed\.
