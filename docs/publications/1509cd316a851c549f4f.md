In Real Time: A Reinvention in British Metaphysics 1883\-1928\. Oxford University Press\. 2026The belief that time brings progress was widespread, taken up by metaphysicians of all kinds: idealists and realists, temporal realists and anti\-realists, eternalists and presentists\. The problem is that Victorian beliefs about progress have a dark side: they come bundled with sexism\. This chapter argues that, when progress is baked into philosophies of time, it surely bakes in patriarchal and sexist assumptions too\. It goes on to argue that Henri Bergson’s theory of time offered tools to overthrow patriarchal accounts, and therein lay part of its appeal for Hilda Oakeley and Karin Costelloe\-Stephen: Bergsonian time has an affinity with their feminism\.The chapter proceeds as follows\. Section 2 returns to Victorian progress\. Building on the groundbreaking work of literature scholar Patricia Murphy, this idea is shown to be rooted in deeply patriarchal, sexist ideas\. Section 3 offers some background on Bergson and feminism more generally, showing that the Frenchman’s stress on change and intuition was generally seen as feminist\. Section 4 explores the specific appeal Bergson’s philosophy of time held for feminist philosophers of time: it offers the means to resist patriarchal accounts of time\. This chapter argues that Oakeley and Costelloe\-Stephen took up precisely the elements of Bergsonian time that were compatible with their feminist politics\. This helps explain the appeal that Bergsonian time held for these philosophers, and opens up a fresh, feminist perspective on the metaphysics of our period\.
