# A causal analytics approach for interpretable supply chain risk and supplier performance evaluation

**Autores:** Ishwar Gupta; Harshitha Konduru; Sergio Correa; Hendro Wicaksono

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Traditional machine learning \(ML\) techniques are widely used to support operational supply chain decisions, but their “black box” nature limits transparency and actionability\. This study addresses this challenge by exploring the effectiveness of causal discovery methods and large language models \(LLMs\) in supply chain management with a focus on the automotive industry\. Six causal discovery algorithms\- Peter–Clark \(PC\), Fast Causal Inference \(FCI\), Greedy Equivalence Search \(GES\), DirectLiNGAM, NOTEARS, and Regression with Subsequent Independence Test \(RESIT\) are benchmarked against an expert\-validated ground\-truth graph constructed from 18 domain experts with a combined 300\+ years of experience, using 12 months of internal supplier Key Performance Indicator \(KPI\) data\. RESIT achieves the closest alignment with expert knowledge, with a False Discovery Rate of 0\.1429, Structural Hamming Distance of 1, precision and recall of 0\.8571 and an F1\-score of 0\.85, outperforming all other algorithms on every metric\. Robustness of the estimated causal effect of Backlog on Escalation is confirmed through three refutation tests\- random common cause, random subset and placebo treatment returning high p\-values of 0\.96, 0\.76, and 0\.98 respectively, indicating insensitivity to spurious confounders and random perturbations\. Counterfactual simulation shows that reducing Backlog by 50%–70% consistently lowers escalation levels across suppliers\. LLMs contribute by extracting causal factors from unstructured external event data, accelerating causal graph construction, and reducing dependence on manual expert input\. LLM\-derived causal graphs are further compared with expert\-validated models to ensure alignment and reliability, and are operationalised through a Weighted Sum Model risk score linking external drivers to internal KPIs for actionable supplier escalation decisions\.
