# the mountain and the ant

**Autores:** Paolo SANTI

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial intelligence is a mountain\. Those who should give it a body are an ant at its foot: applied research, the people who build machines and the people who use them every day\. This essay overturns the usual reading of that disproportion: the mountain does not move, the ant touches the ground\. The thesis is that the future of automation is not held back by a lack of intelligence\. It is held back by the absence of a layer that nobody has an interest in integrating: the machine&\#x27;s autonomic nervous system\. Not the brain that computes, not the arm that executes, but what keeps the whole alive while it works\. It is the layer that senses how the machine is doing, protects it before it breaks, and says what it is about to do, and why\. The essay starts from two languages that do not speak to each other: that of research on intelligence, grown far from matter, and that of the plant, cautious by construction because it rewards repeatability\. It then shows why the missing layer is left in a market no man&\#x27;s land, and tells it through a symbolic scene: a gear motor that changes its voice at 3:47 a\.m\. At the heart of the analysis is Regulation \(EU\) 2023/1230 on machinery, which applies from 20 January 2027\. Read to the end, it draws the outline of that layer\. Of control systems whose behaviour evolves, it asks that they never exceed their assigned limits, that they keep a one\-year trace of safety decisions, and that they always remain correctable\. Of machines that learn, it also asks that they communicate, where appropriate, what they will do and why\. It does not ask for the senses: noticing one&\#x27;s own wear and taking care of it remains the builder&\#x27;s choice, and that is where competition lies\. The essay therefore distinguishes compliance as archive from compliance as physiology\. The text includes: · a comparison between Directive 2006/42/EC and the new regulation, with the European Commission&\#x27;s cost and savings estimates; · a comparative reading of five ways of doing maintenance \(corrective, preventive, predictive, prescriptive, metabolic\) and their effects on spare parts and materials; · an analysis of human work beside the machine that senses; · the question of who owns the experience a machine learns, in light of the Data Act, with a Shapley\-value example of how that value could be shared\. It closes with a concrete invitation to builders of special\-purpose machines\. It is an experiment on a single machine and a single component, with the technical specification of the “first nerve”\. MATERIA VIVA — Il metabolismo dell&\#x27;industria” \(DOI 10\.5281/zenodo\.19758995\), where the architecture is fully developed as the Industrial Nervous System\.
