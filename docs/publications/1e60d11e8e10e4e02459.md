# Urolithin A pretreatment protects against seizures through IGF\-1R Tyr1166/1167 phosphorylation inhibition and enhances the antiseizure effect of valproic acid

**Autores:** Ming Li; Jiabin Zong; Yu Zhang; Minghua Zou; Fu Lu; Songqing Pan; Lily Zhang; Liu Yang; Heyang Qin; Zhouhua Li; Haiyan Wen; Wanbin Huang; 肖哲曼

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Urolithin A \(UA\), a metabolite derived from gut microbiota that originates from ellagitannins found in pomegranate, berries, and walnuts, has emerged as a promising postbiotic exhibiting mitophagy\-inducing and neuroprotective properties\. This study examined the antiseizure effects of UA pretreatment and its potential to enhance the efficacy of valproic acid \(VPA\), a first\-line antiseizure medication, using a pilocarpine\-induced status epilepticus \(SE\) rat model\. UA significantly reduced seizure stage, prolonged latency, shortened duration, and decreased incidence of seizures, while alleviating SE\-induced behavioral and cognitive impairments\. Network pharmacology revealed insulin\-like growth factor 1 receptor \(IGF\-1R\) as a critical target of UA\. Subsequent experiments demonstrated that UA exerts its therapeutic effects by inhibiting the phosphorylation of IGF\-1R\. Importantly, the combination of UA and VPA yielded enhanced antiseizure effects compared to either treatment alone in mitigating seizures and behavioral comorbidities\. These findings suggest that UA may serve as a valuable adjuvant to conventional antiseizure therapy\. Additionally, this study elucidates the antiseizure potential of UA as a pomegranate\-derived postbiotic, providing experimental evidence from an animal model to support a UA\-VPA combination strategy aimed at improving the efficacy of epilepsy treatment\.
