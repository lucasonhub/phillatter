# Nannicelli, Ted: Doxastic Attitudes and the Appreciation of Art across Cultures

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_British Journal of Aesthetics\_ 66\. 2026This article begins with a claim that has come to enjoy widespread acceptance amongst philosophers of art, and it explores some of its unappreciated implications for thinking about the experience \(by which I mostly mean perceptual experience\) and appreciation of artworks of other cultures\. The claim is two\-fold: \(1\) knowledge of an artwork’s original or generative context affects our experience of it, and \(2\) appreciation of an artwork requires knowledge of facts about this context\. Over the last few decades, this view has been defended under the banner of ‘contextualism’ in the philosophy of art\. This article makes a case for an expanded contextualism by arguing that other sorts of broadly cultural aspects of artworks’ generative contexts are among their constitutive properties, affect perceptual experiences of them, and bear upon their appreciation\. In particular, I will argue that various doxastic attitudes, notably religious and spiritual beliefs, that are widely held within the cultural context of an artwork’s creation may constitute essential, relational features of the work and affect how cultural insiders experience a given work\. Furthermore, I argue that the cognitive content that is necessary for the perception of the work’s aesthetic features and its appreciation may extend beyond knowledge \(of the work’s relevant categories, art\-historical context, cultural context, and so forth\)\. In some cases, a particular doxastic attitude may be necessary for apt aesthetic perception and appreciation\. \(direct link \)
