# Perceived readiness for self\-directed e\-learning in undergraduate physiotherapy education: Insights for future curriculum development

**Autores:** Anri Human; M Cochrane\-Boeyens

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background\. Self\-directed learning \(SDL\) is an essential competency for health sciences students, particularly as higher education increasingly integrates blended\- and e\-learning approaches\. In South Africa where students enter tertiary education from diverse socioeconomic and educational backgrounds, the readiness of physiotherapy students to engage in SDL within e\-learning environments remains underexplored\. Objectives\. To investigate the perceived readiness of undergraduate physiotherapy students to participate in self\-directed e\-learning at a South African tertiary institution\. Methods\. A descriptive, cross\-sectional study was conducted among first\- to fourth\-year physiotherapy students \(N=217\)\. Participants voluntarily completed the UniSIM questionnaire, a validated tool assessing 10 SDL readiness domains\. Responses were rated on a 7\-point Likert scale, with descriptive statistics used for analysis\. Results\. A total of 94 students \(43%\) completed the questionnaire\. Most reported readiness in key SDL areas, including motivation and planning \(85\.80%\), comprehension competence \(95\.45%\), time management \(79\.17%\) and online learning proficiency \(72\.27%\)\. However, only 52\.27% felt confident managing tests and examinations, and 71\.82% reported difficulty managing stress\. Despite overall readiness for SDL, 76\.89% preferred guided learning formats, such as lectures\. Conclusion\. Undergraduate physiotherapy students demonstrated overall readiness for self\-directed e\-learning, particularly in areas critical for academic autonomy\. However, stress management emerged as a notable barrier, and the preference for guided instruction suggests a need for structured support\. Future initiatives should incorporate strategies to strengthen emotional resilience and gradually foster SDL competencies across all years of study\.
