# Johnson, Jamison: On Facing \- Alignment, Time, and the Ripples We Leave

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Why are you happy? Asked honestly, the question becomes a chain of reasons that ends where reasons run out and words fail at the same place\. This lyric\-philosophical essay builds from that double edge toward an account of facing: the direction a life is turned\. Drawing on Aristotle&\#x27;s regress of ends, Boethius&\#x27;s definition of eternity and the scholastic \*nunc stans\*, the essay treats the &quot;standing now&quot; as a moment reachable without dying, in which a being is briefly aligned with itself\. It names each such felt moment a Jay and the ripple a life of them leaves behind the Trace, and it marks the one empirical touchstone it leans on, the correlational literature on interpersonal physiological synchrony, as a touchstone and not a proof\. With Hannah Arendt&\#x27;s claim that evil spreads across the surface while only the good has depth, and Augustine&\#x27;s privation account of evil, it argues that good and bad intent are one ground faced toward or away from itself, and it separates two quantities often confused: the final turn a life can make, and the accumulation of how it was spent\. Redemption is real and consequences are conserved\. The essay closes on the priest and the physician as two aligned beings relieving the same fear, and on the claim that the bridge between faith and inquiry is found in a shape of attention rather than in a measurement\. \(direct link \)
