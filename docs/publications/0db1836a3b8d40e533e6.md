# Quechua translation and validity of the revised mental health inventory in Peruvian adolescents

**Autores:** Jonatan Baños\-Chaparro; M\. Ruiz; Rulman Andrei Franco\-Jimenez; Diego Valencia\-Pecho; Paul Ynquillay\-Lima; Tomás Caycho\-Rodríguez

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Adolescent mental health is a growing public health concern, particularly among Indigenous populations, where linguistic and cultural barriers may limit access to appropriate psychological assessment\. Therefore, having brief and culturally adapted instruments in Indigenous languages is essential to improve mental health assessment and research among Quechua\-speaking adolescents\. Objective To translate and culturally adapt the Revised Mental Health Inventory\-5 \(R\-MHI\-5\) from Spanish into Southern Quechua and examine its psychometric properties in Peruvian adolescents\. Methods A total of 2,978 adolescents from Angaraes, Huancavelica, participated in this instrumental, quantitative, cross\-sectional study\. Participants completed the Southern Quechua version of the R\-MHI\-5 and a sociodemographic questionnaire\. Structural equation modeling and item response theory were applied\. Results The Southern Quechua R\-MHI\-5 showed adequate content validity \(Aiken’s V &gt; 0\.70\) and a two\-dimensional structure comprising psychological well\-being and psychological distress, with satisfactory fit indices \(CFI = 0\.99, RMSEA = 0\.03, SRMR = 0\.01\)\. Reliability was adequate for both dimensions \( ω = 0\.72; H = 0\.78\), and measurement invariance by sex was supported\. Item response theory analyses showed satisfactory item functioning\. Item 4 showed the highest discrimination and information for psychological well\-being, whereas item 3 showed the highest discrimination and information for psychological distress\. The scale provided greater information at intermediate levels of well\-being and at intermediate\-to\-high levels of psychological distress\. Conclusion The Southern Quechua version of the R\-MHI\-5 showed adequate psychometric properties supporting its use to assess mental health among Quechua\-speaking adolescents\. Its brief format, two\-dimensional structure, reliability, measurement invariance, and item\-level performance support its application in research and mental health assessment in community settings\.
