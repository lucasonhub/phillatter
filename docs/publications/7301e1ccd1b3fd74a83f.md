# Spatial Modeling of Topsoil Total Carbon and Texture from Spectral Characteristics of Bare Soil Surface Images of Landsat 5/8 Using Machine Learning

**Autores:** P\. A\. Braulov; Pavel M\. Shilov; R\. G\. Gadzhiumarov; V\. K\. Dridiger; E\. V\. Bogdan; V\. O\. Megmerova; Petr Tsymbarovich; A\. E\. Shatunov; Pavel P\. Fil; D\. E\. Mitichkin; Dmitriy S\. Fomin

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

— This study examines the possibility for detecting bare soil surface \(BSS\), using remote sensing data and for applying this approach to model texture and total soil carbon in the topsoil\. The study area comprises agricultural fields within the North Caucasus Federal Agricultural Research Center in the northeastern spurs of the Stavropol Upland\. Landsat 5/8 imagery and spectral indices \(NDVI, NBR2, and BSI\) were used to classify BSS\. Optimal discrimination of BSS from green and non\-photosynthetic vegetation was achieved, using a BSI threshold of &gt;0\.18 \(F1\-score = 0\.98\)\. Spatial modeling of topsoil properties was conducted by comparing five regression models \(Linear Regression, K\-Nearest Neighbors, Random Forest, Extreme Gradient Boosting, and Cubist\)\. A linear regression model demonstrated the highest performance; key predictors included the mean and standard deviation of SWIR2\. The model exhibits strong predictive capability for sand, silt, clay, and total carbon \(adjusted R 2 = 0\.82, 0\.75, 0\.78, and 0\.74, respectively\)\. The topsoil is characterized by the range of clay content from 5 to 30%, of silt content from 15 to 60%, of sand content from 20 to 80%, and of total carbon from 0\.5 to 3%\. Predictive maps of texture and total carbon derived from BSS reveal considerable local heterogeneity, with soils consisting of Chernozems and Phaeozems, influenced by parent rock variability\. The modeling results highlight the high predictive power of the BSS spectral characteristics, even when used as a single predictor for digital soil mapping\.
