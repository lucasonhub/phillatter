# TriAlign UDA for hybrid domain adaptation of frozen histopathology foundation model representations

**Autores:** Merve Özkan; Caner Ozcan

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Foundation models produce strong patch\-level representations in histopathological image analysis; however, domain shifts across different centers, staining protocols, and scanner conditions can limit the direct generalizability of these representations\. In this study, a hybrid feature\-level unsupervised domain adaptation \(UDA\) framework called TriAlign\-UDA, which operates on frozen histopathology foundation model representations, is proposed\. TriAlign\-UDA aims to reduce the mismatch between the labeled source domain and the completely unlabeled target domain without requiring encoder fine\-tuning\. The method combines statistical alignment, prototype\-based semantic preservation, and adversarial adaptation components within a common learning framework based on a bottleneck residual adapter\. In the experiments, NCT\-CRC\-HE\-100K was used as the labeled source domain, TCGA\-COAD/READ as the unlabeled target domain, and CRC\-VAL\-HE\-7K as the independent external evaluation dataset\. In the results obtained over five different seeds, TriAlign\-UDA reached the highest mean accuracy of 0\.9669 ± 0\.0050, balanced accuracy of 0\.9560 ± 0\.0063, and Macro\-F1 of 0\.9515 ± 0\.0067 on CRC\-VAL\-HE\-7K among the evaluated methods, including the recent EUDA and PLADA\-f baselines\. DANN and EUDA produced closely competitive results; however, the differences between TriAlign\-UDA and DANN, EUDA, or PLADA\-f did not reach statistical significance\. These findings indicate that frozen\-feature UDA should be evaluated not only through individual alignment objectives but also through independent external generalization performance\.
