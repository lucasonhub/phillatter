# Needle\-knife lumbosacral therapy for lumbar disc herniation: a single\-center, outcome assessor\-blinded randomized controlled trial

**Autores:** Yiming Zhang; Ruiguo Li; Xiao Zhang; Zhanyou Wang; Ziwei Zhang; Huanhuan Xu; Xiaofan Li

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Lumbar disc herniation \(LDH\) is a common spinal disorder causing significant pain and dysfunction\. Traditional acupuncture is a widely used non\-pharmacological treatment for LDH\. Recently, needle\-knife therapy has emerged as a potential alternative\. Objective This study aims to explore the clinical efficacy of needle\-knife lumbosacral therapy for LDH and compare its advantages with those of conventional acupuncture therapy\. Methods This study adopted an outcome assessor\-blinded randomized controlled trial design\. A total of 74 patients with LDH were enrolled and randomly assigned to the experimental group \( n = 37\), which received needle\-knife lumbosacral therapy, or the control group \( n = 37\), which received conventional acupuncture at the same anatomical treatment sites\. Seventy patients completed the 2\-week treatment and 3\-month follow\-up\. Visual analog scale \(VAS\) and Oswestry Disability Index \(ODI\) were designated as primary repeated outcomes; lumbar flexion mobility \(LFM\) was a secondary repeated outcome, and inflammatory cytokines served as non\-repeated secondary outcomes\. Mixed\-effects linear models were used for repeated measurements, with the group\-by\-time interaction predefined as the primary estimand\. Bonferroni correction was applied for the three repeated outcomes \(VAS, ODI, and three LFM components\), setting an adjusted significance threshold of α = 0\.017\. Unadjusted α = 0\.05 was adopted for inflammatory cytokines\. VAS, ODI, and LFM were evaluated at baseline, post\-treatment, and 3\-month follow\-up; inflammatory markers \(TNF\-α, IL\-6, IL\-1β\) were detected at baseline and post\-treatment only\. Adverse events were monitored throughout the trial\. Patient recruitment was conducted from March 15, 2025, to July 15, 2025, and the final follow\-up was performed on November 18, 2025\. Results Both groups demonstrated within\-group reductions in VAS and ODI scores across all time points\. For the prespecified group\-by\-time interaction analyses under Bonferroni correction \( α = 0\.017\), the needle\-knife group showed significantly larger longitudinal improvements than the acupuncture group for both primary outcomes: VAS interaction \(95% CI: 0\.21–0\.83\), adjusted p = 0\.001; ODI interaction \(95% CI: 1\.89–4\.35\), adjusted p &lt; 0\.0001\. At post\-treatment, the model\-based between\-group differences \(needle\-knife minus acupuncture\) were VAS: −1\.16 \(95% CI: −1\.78 to −0\.54\), adjusted p = 0\.0004; ODI: −3\.78 \(95% CI: −4\.95 to −2\.61\), adjusted p &lt; 0\.0001\. At 3\-month follow\-up, sustained between\-group differences were VAS: −0\.43 \(95% CI: −0\.68 to −0\.18\), adjusted p = 0\.001; ODI: −1\.89 \(95% CI: −2\.56 to −1\.22\), adjusted p &lt; 0\.0001\. For LFM components, all three subindices showed greater numerical improvements in the needle\-knife group, but their group\-by\-time interaction adjusted p \-values exceeded the 0\.017 threshold: lumbar flexion \(95% CI: 0\.62–5\.00\), adjusted p = 0\.034; left lateral flexion \(95% CI: −1\.02 to 2\.42\), adjusted p = 0\.423; right lateral flexion \(95% CI: −1\.10 to 1\.92\), adjusted p = 0\.594\. Among serum inflammatory biomarkers, only IL\-6 showed a statistically significant unadjusted between\-group difference at post\-treatment: IL\-6 difference = −0\.47 ng·L \(95% CI: −0\.89 to −0\.05\), unadjusted p = 0\.03; TNF\-α difference = −0\.18 ng·L \(95% CI: −0\.52 to 0\.16\), unadjusted p = 0\.30; IL\-1β difference = −0\.13 ng·L \(95% CI: −0\.37 to 0\.11\), unadjusted p = 0\.28\. No serious adverse events were reported\. Conclusion Two\-week lumbosacral needle\-knife therapy effectively relieves pain and lumbar dysfunction in LDH patients, with statistically verified sustained superiority over conventional acupuncture for VAS and ODI under the Bonferroni\-adjusted threshold \( α = 0\.017\)\. Although numerical advantages in lumbar mobility were observed in the needle\-knife group, these differences did not reach the prespecified adjusted significance level after multiple testing correction\. As an outcome assessor\-blinded exploratory trial with unmasked participants and therapists, large\-sample and multicenter clinical studies are needed to further verify the conclusions, and future trials should optimize blinding schemes to reduce reporting bias\.
