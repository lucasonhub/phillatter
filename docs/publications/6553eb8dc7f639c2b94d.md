# Evidence for Reduced Somatic T‐Cell Receptor Sequence Diversity Profiles Among Midwestern Amish in an Aging Cohort Study

**Autores:** Lauren A\. Cruz; Shiying Liu; Kristy L\. S\. Miskimen; Jessica N\. Cooke Bailey; Tyler G\. Kinzy; Yeunjoo E\. Song; Reneé A\. Laux; Penelope L\. Miron; Paula K\. Ogrocki; Alan J\. Lerner; Audrey Lynn; Sarada L\. Fuzzell; Sherri D\. Hochstetler; Ioanna Konidari; Jacob L\. McCauley; William K\. Scott; Margaret Ann Pericak‐Vance; Jonathan L\. Haines; Dana C\. Crawford

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: The causes of late onset Alzheimer disease \(LOAD\) are not known but several recent lines of evidence implicate the adaptive immune system\. OBJECTIVE: To characterize the adaptive immune system among Midwestern Amish participating in longitudinal studies of aging\. METHODS: We immunosequenced the T\-cell receptor \(TCR\) beta chain from genomic DNA of 72 participants, including those with clinically diagnosed LOAD \(n = 6\), mild cognitive impairment \(MCI; n = 16\), cognitive impairment but not AD \(n = 3\), and 35 cognitively unaffected\. For a subset of participants, LOAD pathogenesis plasma biomarkers were available\. RESULTS: TCR sequence diversity measured as Simpson&\#x27;s clonality was lower among LOAD\+MCI compared with non\-LOAD, but these differences were not independent of age\. Relatively few clonotypes \(exact nucleotide sequences\) were shared across participants; of those few shared include the Epstein Barr virus associated clonotype\. HLA\-A\*03:01 and several HLA\-DRB1 alleles were under\-represented among LOAD\+MCI participants compared with cognitively unaffected participants, but these associations were no longer significant in adjusted analyses\. Among LOAD\+MCI participants, increased p\-tau181 was nominally associated with decreased TCR sequence diversity, and the nominal association was independent of age; however, this association did not remain statistically significant after strict correction for multiple testing and the limited sample size precluded formal tests of heterogeneity\. RESULTS: In this limited Midwestern Amish sample, the preliminary TCR diversity trends are consistent with the involvement of the adaptive immune system in LOAD\.
