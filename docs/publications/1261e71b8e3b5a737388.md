# Cancer Worry and Supportive Care Needs in Patients with Polycythemia Vera: A Single\-center Cross\-sectional Study

**Autores:** Naime Kaçar Koca; Can Özlü

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Cancer Worry and Supportive Care Needs in Patients with Polycythemia Vera: A Single\-center Cross\-sectional StudyAim: Polycythemia vera \(PV\) is a chronic, lifelong myeloproliferative neoplasm\.Even when hematological targets are controlled, patients often experience significant physical symptoms, emotional strain, and unmet informational needs\.Evidence linking cancer\-related worry to supportive care needs in PV remains limited\.In this study, we examined the association between cancer worry and supportive care needs and explored whether these outcomes differed across demographic groups\.Methods: In this single\-center cross\-sectional study, 70 adult patients with PV completed the eight\-item Cancer Worry Scale \(CWS\) and the 31item Turkish version of Supportive Care Needs Survey \(SCNS\) between March and September 2024\.The primary outcome was the correlation between total CWS and SCNS scores, assessed using Spearman&\#x27;s rank correlation\.Demographic comparisons were treated as exploratory\.Where possible, we report non\-parametric effect sizes; pairwise p values after significant omnibus tests were Bonferroni\-adjusted within the corresponding test\.Results: The study cohort comprised 47 men and 23 women\.The median total scores were 8\.0 \[interquartile range \(IQR\): 8\.0\-12\.8\]for the CWS and 53\.0 \(IQR: 45\.0\-65\.0\)for the SCNS\.Cancer worry was modestly and positively correlated with overall supportive care needs \(Spearman rho=0\.315,95% confidence interval: 0\.086\-0\.512;p=0\.008\)\.Female patients reported significantly higher cancer worry than males \(U=390\.0,p=0\.035; rank\-biserial correlation=0\.278\),whereas male patients reported higher sexuality\-related needs \(U=424\.5, p=0\.032; rank\-biserial correlation=0\.215\)\.Psychological needs varied significantly by income status \(H=6\.621,p=0\.036\); after Bonferroni adjustment, low\-income participants scored higher than middle\-income participants \(adjusted p=0\.034\)\.Additional exploratory analyses also showed differences in physical/daily\-living needs based on education and in sexuality needs based on age\.Conclusion: In this single\-center PV cohort, higher cancer worry was modestly associated with increased supportive care needs\.The demographic subgroup results should be read as exploratory because some categories were very small, multiple outcomes were tested, and clinical covariates were not available\.These findings support prospective work on individualized psychosocial and supportive\-care assessment rather than a definitive screening pathway\.
