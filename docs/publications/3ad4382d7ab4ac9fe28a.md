# How wetland restoration enhanced Red\-crowned Crane habitat quality: evidence from time\-series remote sensing and explainable machine learning

**Autores:** Xinyu Tian; Chunrong Li; Zhizhou Chen; Yadong Liu; Niwen Ying; Jiangming Hong; Yanan Zhang; Yinghai Ke

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Coastal wetlands provide essential habitats for migratory waterbirds\. Although wetland restoration is increasingly implemented, its effectiveness in improving habitat quality and the ecological mechanisms underlying such improvements remain insufficiently understood\. Focusing on Jiangsu Yancheng Wetlands &amp; Rare Birds National Natural Reserve, a key site along the East Asian\-Australasian Flyway, this study evaluated whether and how restoration enhanced wintering habitat quality for the endangered Red\-crowned Crane \( Grus japonensis \)\. Using long\-term satellite observations, crane occurrence records and restoration project data, we developed an integrated indicator framework characterizing vegetation\-water\-soil conditions, spatial structure, and temporal hydrological variability\. These indicators, together with conventional environmental variables, were combined with MaxEnt habitat modelling and explainable machine learning \(XGBoost\-SHAP and GAM\) to quantify restoration effectiveness, identify environmental drivers, and determine ecological thresholds\. Results show the remote sensing indicator framework explained approximately 40% of the spatial variation in crane occurrence, with newly\-developed temporal hydrological and spatial metrics increasing explanatory power by 6–10%\. Following restoration, highly suitable habitats expanded by 65\.1%\. Restoration measures including hydrological regulation, pond\-to\-wetland restoration, and composite restoration produced the largest relative gains in suitable habitat area, increasing it by 34\.6%, 128\.7%, and 436\.8%, respectively\. Restoration enhanced habitat quality by increasing seasonal hydrological variability and habitat spatial heterogeneity, creating shallow\-water and low\-coverage vegetation mosaics, and shifting ecological thresholds toward conditions more favorable for cranes\. These findings show that effective restoration depends not only on expanding wetland area but also on re\-establishing hydrological dynamics and spatial structure\. The framework supports quantitative restoration assessment, evidence\-based planning, and adaptive management for coastal wetland sustainability\.
