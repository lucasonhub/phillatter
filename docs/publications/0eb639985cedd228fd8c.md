# Phage vB\_KpnS\_MK54: lPS\-associated factors involved in phage adsorption and depolymerase\-driven anti\-biofilm effects

**Autores:** Yan Zeng; Zhaomin Wu; Zhuangkan Yan; Yin Wang; Yang ZeXiao; Yixin Huang; Cao SuiZhong; Xueping Yao

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Understanding phage K\. pneumoniae –host interactions is essential for elucidating phage infection mechanisms and developing phage\-derived antibacterial strategies\. This showed that lipopolysaccharide \(LPS\) plays an important role in phage vB\_KpnS\_MK54 adsorption by constructing the isogenic deletion mutants LPKPΔmsbA and LPKPΔlpxB, in which phage adsorption was reduced by 2–3 log compared with the wild\-type strain\. Biofilms are associated with persistent infections and increased tolerance to antimicrobial treatment, posing a significant clinical challenge\. We further characterized the recombinant depolymerase Dep\_Zy, which exhibited potent depolymerase and antibiofilm activities, including depolymerase activity at 0\.1 μg/mL, 55% inhibition of biofilm formation at 10 μg/mL, and 30% degradation of mature biofilms at 1 μg/mL\. Notably, Dep\_Zy potentiated the activity of conventional antibiotics, resulting in a 1–3 log reduction in biofilm\-associated bacterial counts when combined with kanamycin, gentamicin, or streptomycin\. These findings elucidate the molecular basis of phage–host interactions and highlight Dep\_Zy as a promising enzymatic strategy for controlling multidrug\-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae through biofilm disruption and antibiotic potentiation\.
