# Unraveling scale effects in naturalized runoff processes: Insights from interpretable machine learning across the Yellow River Basin

**Autores:** Fenghua You; Shanshui Yuan; Liliang Ren; Chenglong Cao; Xiuqin Fang; Shanhu Jiang; Yi Liu; Xiaoli Yang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Study region: The Yellow River basin \(YRB\), China\. Study focus: Effective water resource management requires a comprehensive understanding of runoff processes across spatial scales and their interconnections\. However, scale effects pose major challenges to deriving a universal spatial scaling law when extrapolating runoff research from fine to broad scales\. Previous studies have mainly focused on relatively small study areas \(&lt;100 km 2 \), which may overlook the heterogeneity of environmental drivers affecting runoff processes\. Furthermore, traditional point\-based, short\-term, or infrequent measurements are insufficient to accurately capture nonlinear characteristics of runoff processes\. To address these issues, we employed interpretable machine learning \(IML\) methods to analyze runoff processes across multiple nested basins \(NBs\) within the Yellow River Basin \(YRB\)\. New hydrological insights for the region: This study evaluates spatial scale effects and their driving factors, with the analysis extended to the river\-basin level \(&gt;100 km²\)\. Our results indicate that the runoff coefficient \(RC\) of most sub\-basins did not follow a single scaling rule\. Instead, RC\-area relationships varied among scale domains, and RC did not consistently increase or decrease with basin area\. In smaller sub\-basins, the spatial distribution of precipitation dominates runoff scale effects, whereas in larger sub\-basins, the spatial distribution of land use governs these effects\.
