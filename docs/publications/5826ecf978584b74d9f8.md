# University Campus Vitality \(UCV\): A Bibliometric Analysis \(2015–2025\)

**Autores:** 杨露露; Putri Haryati Ibrahim; Khalilah Zakariya

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

University campus vitality \(UCV\) has attracted increasing attention across campus planning, built\-environment, sustainability, and student\-oriented research; however, its knowledge structure remains conceptually fragmented, and the relationships among UCV, campus spatial planning \(CSP\), and commercial space \(CS\) have not been systematically synthesised\. This study examines the development, intellectual structure, and thematic evolution of this interdisciplinary field between 2015 and 2025 through a multi\-database bibliometric analysis\. Database\-specific filtering retained 1,161 records \(WOS = 359; Scopus = 354; CNKI = 448\), which were reduced through crossdatabase deduplication and relevance screening to a final integrated corpus of 803 publications\. Bibliometrix and VOSviewer were used to analyse publication patterns, conceptual structures, keyword networks, and thematic evolution, complemented by CiteSpace reference co\-citation and timeline analyses of the WOS subset\. The results show an overall increase in publication activity, with China and the United States as the principal geographical contributors\. The conceptual structure is centered on campus planning and university campus, with interconnected themes involving spatial configuration, public space, sustainability, students, walkability, resilience, landscape, and environmental quality\. Temporal analyses further reveal a persistent planningoriented core accompanied by increasing diversification toward human\-centered, behavioural, and sustainability\-related concerns\. Notably, CS does not emerge as a comparably dominant independent theme, indicating that its explicit integration within the UCV–CSP knowledge structure remains comparatively underdeveloped\. The study therefore clarifies the asymmetrical development of UCV, CSP, and CS and provides a bibliometric basis for future empirical research on the role of commercial space within university campus spatial and functional systems\. Keywords: university campus vitality; campus spatial planning; commercial space
