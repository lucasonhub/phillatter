# Effectiveness of digital health education interventions for chronic disease self\-management in community\-dwelling older adults: an umbrella review and evidence classification

**Autores:** Esra Sezer; Gözde GÜR

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Digital health education interventions are widely promoted to support chronic disease self\-management\. However, existing umbrella reviews pool evidence across all ages and have not assessed community\-dwelling older adults, who face barriers to digital access\. This umbrella review aimed to determine whether current evidence supports the effectiveness of digital health education interventions for chronic disease self\-management in community\-dwelling adults aged 60 years or older\. Methods We conducted an umbrella review of systematic reviews evaluating digital health education interventions for chronic disease self\-management in community\-dwelling adults aged 60 years or older\. Six databases were searched for systematic reviews published between 2015 and 2026\. Meta\-analytic outcomes were re\-estimated using random\-effects models, evidence was graded according to the Ioannidis criteria, methodological quality was assessed with AMSTAR 2, and primary\-study overlap was quantified using the corrected covered area \(CCA\)\. Results Of the 1,272 records identified, 11 met the eligibility criteria, and 10 contributed 44 meta\-analytic outcomes derived from 199 primary randomized controlled trials \(RCTs\) across five chronic disease clusters\. The evidence base showed minimal overlap among primary studies \(overall CCA was 0\.76%\)\. None of the outcomes met the criteria for convincing \(Class I\), highly suggestive \(Class II\), or suggestive \(Class III\) evidence\. Fifteen outcomes \(34\.1%\) were classified as weak \(Class IV\), whereas 29 \(65\.9%\) were non\-significant\. According to AMSTAR 2, only three of the 10 systematic reviews were rated as high confidence, whereas two were rated as low and five as critically low confidence; the 95% prediction interval crossed the null for all 44 outcomes\. Conclusion When the evidence is limited to community\-dwelling older adults, no digital health education intervention for chronic disease self\-management is supported by high\-credibility evidence at the umbrella review level\. This finding most likely reflects the immaturity of the current evidence base—characterized by a limited number of trials, short follow\-up periods, and substantial heterogeneity—rather than a lack of intervention effectiveness\. Well\-powered RCTs with longer follow\-up and higher\-quality systematic reviews specifically focused on older adults are needed to strengthen the evidence base\.
