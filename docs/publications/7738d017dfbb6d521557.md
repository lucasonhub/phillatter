# Explicit Spin Regulation as an Emerging Design Paradigm in Heterogeneous Catalysis

**Autores:** Tingyu Lu; Yuan Huang; Ang Cao; Wei Li; Hui Li

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Conspectus Catalytic activity, selectivity, and durability arise from coupled electronic, structural, interfacial, transport, and spin\-related processes\. Conventional catalyst design has relied largely on electronic effects, strain or stress engineering, active\-site construction, and local reaction environment control\. Although these strategies have enabled major advances, they do not fully capture the spin degrees of freedom associated with open\-shell transition\-metal centers and magnetically responsive catalysts\. Spin state, spin polarization, magnetic moment, spin density, and magnetic ordering can influence adsorbate binding, transition\-state stabilization, charge transfer, and reaction pathways, yet their catalytic consequences are often grouped under the broad label of magnetic enhancement without a clear mechanistic assignment\. In this Account, we develop explicit spin regulation as a framework for magnetism\-informed catalysis\. Magnetic enhancement describes an observed response to a magnetic field, a magnetic component, or a magnetic environment\. Explicit spin regulation, by contrast, requires deliberate modulation of spin\-related variables at or near catalytically active sites and a mechanistic connection to catalytic function\. We organize this framework around two routes\. Catalyst\-integrated spin levers use promoters, interfaces, or interphases to tune the active\-site spin/electronic structure, whereas field\-induced spin levers introduce external fields or catalyst\-localized magnetic perturbations during catalysis\. Although implemented differently, both routes seek to control how the active\-site spin governs adsorption, transition\-state energetics, charge transfer, selectivity, stability, and durability\. Our studies provide representative evidence for this framework\. Promoter\-induced spin modulation stabilizes the N–N dissociation transition state beyond conventional electrostatic promotion, establishing the spin\-promotion effect as a catalyst\-integrated design principle\. In electrocatalysis, external fields, embedded nanomagnets, and magnetic proximity effects provide progressively localized perturbations that can be connected to spin\-sensitive active sites, key intermediates, or local magnetic ordering\. These examples also show why performance enhancement alone is insufficient\. A convincing assignment should connect the imposed perturbation, the active\-site spin/electronic response, the affected intermediate or transition state, and the resulting catalytic consequence\. Magnetic measurements, electron paramagnetic resonance spectroscopy, element\-specific X\-ray spectroscopies, operando methods, kinetic analysis, and spin\-polarized theory provide complementary elements of this evidence loop\. Building on this mechanistic framework, we further translate the active\-site spin into catalyst\-design descriptors\. The local metal\-center spin moment can be correlated with oxygen\-intermediate adsorption and activity trends, enabling descriptor\-guided screening of dual\-atom catalysts\. Spin\-related variables may also inform stability and durability through their connections to phase stability, surface reconstruction, active\-site preservation, dissolution resistance, and long\-term operation\. Generalizing these insights requires paired, standardized, and machine\-readable datasets that connect magnetic perturbations, active\-site structures, spin\-sensitive measurements, catalytic metrics, and durability under comparable conditions\. Artificial intelligence\-assisted literature mining, knowledge graph construction, high\-throughput spin\-polarized calculations, active learning, and magnetism\-aware machine\-learning interatomic potentials may help identify transferable spin descriptors and extend spin\-regulated catalyst design to dynamic interfaces\. The broader aim is to move magnetism\-informed catalysis from phenomenological enhancement toward predictive control through deliberate regulation of the active\-site spin\.
