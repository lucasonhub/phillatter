# From macrofungal polysaccharides to precision microbial immunotherapy: reprogramming the tumor microenvironment through immunometabolic and gene\-regulatory pathways

**Autores:** Kingsley Excel Dunga; Sylvester Chibueze Izah; Ebinyo Rebecca Aseibai; Matthew Chidozie Ogwu; Vivian Ibienebakabobo Promise; Nsikak Godwin Etim; Irene Nneka Ohanusi

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Engineered microbial immunotherapies can localize immune\-modulating signals within solid tumors, where hypoxia, suppressive myeloid cells, deficient antigen presentation, and dysfunctional T\-cell trafficking often limit immunotherapy\. Macrofungal polysaccharides, especially structurally defined β\-glucans, offer mechanistic templates rather than interchangeable drug substances for source\-specific, structure\-controlled immune engineering programs\. Their activity depends on linkage, branching, molecular mass, conformation, solubility, aggregation, and physical presentation\. Critically, the strongest Dectin\-1 and trained\-immunity evidence discussed here uses particulate β\-glucans from Saccharomyces cerevisiae; those findings cannot be assigned directly to macrofungal β\-glucans without material\-specific comparison\. We therefore distinguish four engineering architectures: de novo microbial biosynthesis of a defined β\-glucan, microbial surface display, delivery of a pre\-characterized β\-glucan cargo, and delivery of non\-glycan payloads that mimic the desired downstream immune program\. Because therapeutic bacteria have not yet been shown to reproduce a macrofungal β\-glucan with controlled linkage, branching, conformation, and presentation, near\-term development should prioritize standardized cargo delivery or pathway mimicry\. In pancreatic and colorectal cancer models, yeast whole\-glucan particles have altered myeloid trafficking or generated epigenetically and metabolically trained macrophages linked to CXCL9/CXCL10 production, natural killer\-cell recruitment, conventional type 1 dendritic\-cell accumulation, and stronger CD8 T\-cell responses\. Human evidence remains preliminary and cannot establish β\-glucan\-specific efficacy in uncontrolled combination studies\. This Perspective proposes source\-resolved material characterization, receptor and cell\-state testing, multidimensional pharmacodynamic biomarkers, and microbial safety controls as prerequisites for translating fungal\-inspired immune programs into precision cancer therapeutics\.
