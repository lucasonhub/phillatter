# Body mass index and health\-related lifestyle behaviors among university students in Saudi Arabia: a single center cross\-sectional study

**Autores:** Nora O\. Alafif; Shabana Tharkar; Rinas Alharbi; Hadeel Althobaiti; Rawa Alamry; Reema Al\-harbi; Waad Alfawaz

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Overweight and obesity are important public\-health concerns among young adults\. University students experience changes in dietary, physical\-activity, sleep, and other health behaviors, but evidence from Saudi university populations remains heterogeneous\. Objective To explore associations between BMI and selected dietary, physical\-activity, sleep, and sociodemographic characteristics among university students\. Methods This cross\-sectional study analyzed electronic questionnaire responses collected from January to April 2025 on a sample of 196 students\. Weight and height were self\-reported and BMI was recalculated as weight \(kg\)/height 2 \(m 2 \)\. BMI was categorized as underweight \(&lt;18\.5\), normal \(18\.5 to &lt;24\.9\), overweight \(25 to &lt;30\), or obesity \(≥30 kg/m 2 \)\. Associations were examined using contingency\-table analyses and limited exploratory multivariable linear and logistic regression models\. Results Among 196 analyzed students, 17 \(8\.7%\) were underweight, 106 \(54\.1%\) had normal BMI, 38 \(19\.4%\) were overweight, and 35 \(17\.9%\) had obesity; thus, 73 \(37\.2%\) had overweight or obesity\. Associations of BMI category with lifestyle were not statistically significant\. Electronic\-device use before bed showed a sparse\-cell association \( χ 2 =23\.438, df = 9, Monte Carlo p = 0\.010\) that should be considered exploratory\. In adjusted linear regression, male sex \( β = 5\.26 kg/m 2 , 95% CI 2\.36–8\.16, p &lt; 0\.001\), and students aged &gt;23 years had higher BMI \( β = 2\.86, 95% CI 0\.44–5\.29, p = 0\.021\)\. In adjusted logistic regression, male sex \(OR = 2\.71, 95% CI 1\.19–6\.19, p = 0\.018\) and age &gt;23 years \(OR = 3\.15, 95% CI 1\.17–8\.43, p = 0\.023\) were associated with overweight/obesity\. Conclusion Overweight and obesity were common\. Gender and age showed associations with BMI status, whereas evidence for independent associations of the selected lifestyle measures was limited and imprecise\. The cross\-sectional design and self\-reported measurements preclude causal inference
