# Artificial Intelligence in Higher Education: Psychological Effects on University Teaching

**Autores:** Sergey M\. Grigoriev

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Aim \. To identify GenAI‑specific psychological mechanisms through which LLMs reshape academic teaching work, and to compare effects in content creation versus assessment\. Methodology \. The study combines a transparent integrative review \(2015–2025; Scopus, WoS, PsycINFO, ERIC, RSCI; 56 sources with PRISMA‑style tracking\) with a qualitative pilot \(16 semi‑structured interviews with Russian university instructors; thematic analysis\) using an integrated JD‑R and SDT framework\. Results : GenAI acts both as a resource and a work‑redesign driver, generating AI‑specific demands: epistemic uncertainty, verification workload, trust calibration, accountability / responsibility anxiety, norm acceleration and “silent” intensification, and a role shift toward surveillance in assessment\. A domain asymmetry is found – content work is mainly burdened by hidden cognitive verification, while assessment amplifies emotional and normative risks related to fairness and conflict\. Research implications : a model linking GenAI properties to job demands / resources and basic needs \(autonomy, competence, relatedness\) is proposed; recommendations include formal time for verification, a human‑in‑the‑loop standard for high‑stakes assessment, clearer academic integrity policies, and safeguards against intensification\.
