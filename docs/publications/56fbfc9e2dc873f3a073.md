# Seeing the hidden reservoir: Earth observation and geoinformatics for assessing sand dam impacts in dryland rivers

**Autores:** Andreas Braun; Anna Müller; Nimrod Kibet; Volker Hochschild; Martin Sudmanns

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Sand dams are small water\-harvesting structures that store water within the sandy matrix of ephemeral riverbeds\. They are widely promoted as nature\-based solutions for water security and climate adaptation in drylands\. Nonetheless, it remains difficult to systematically assess their environmental and socio\-ecological impacts for multiple reasons\. This review synthesizes Earth observation and geoinformatics research on sand dams and evaluates how reliably their impacts can be detected from spatial data\. A structured literature search and citation tracking identified 11 relevant studies published between 2008 and 2026\. Evidence on impacts is dominated by vegetation effects: six studies assess vegetation responses, five report localized greening, higher vegetation vitality, or greater drought resilience, while one finds no average greening\. Direct hydrological evidence is less prominent: gravimetric and radar\-based approaches could not fully resolve local storage effects, and long\-term regional responses remain inconclusive\. Geomorphological evidence is limited to one study, while thermal approaches remain largely exploratory\. Across these studies, differences in spatial scale, control\-site selection, dam maturity, and temporal design constrain comparability and attribution\. We develop a methodological taxonomy and propose future research based on time series, Before\-After\-Control\-Impact designs, climatic controls, in\-situ validation, multi\-sensor integration, automated inventories, and data fusion supported by artificial intelligence\.
