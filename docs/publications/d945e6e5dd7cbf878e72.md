# Septal Perforation: Treatment and Management Strategies

**Autores:** Nuray Bayar Muluk

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objectives: This article reviewed the septal perforations and treatment options\. Methods: A comprehensive narrative literature review was conducted utilizing established scientific databases, including PubMed/Medline, Scopus, Web of Science, and Embase, covering articles published between 1980 and 2026\. A structured search strategy was applied using keywords such as “nasal septal perforation,” “septal perforation,” “treatment,” “surgery,” and “septal button\.” Peer\-reviewed clinical studies, systematic reviews, meta\-analyses, and authoritative guidelines were included to synthesize the current evidence regarding the etiology, pathophysiology, and management of nasal septal perforations \(NSPs\)\. Results: Anatomically, an NSP manifests as a full\-thickness defect spanning the osteocartilaginous and mucoperichondrial layers\. The literature highlights a multifactorial etiology, broadly classified into traumatic, infectious, inflammatory, and neoplastic origins\. For conservative management, silicone septal prostheses \(buttons\) can alleviate some of the symptoms associated with NSPs, although patient tolerance varies\. When definitive repair is indicated, a diverse array of reconstructive modalities is available\. Prominent surgical techniques identified include traditional bilateral mucoperichondrial advancement flaps with interpositional grafting, staged inferior turbinate flaps, facial artery musculomucosal flaps, and the utilization of auricular cartilage or acellular dermal matrices\. Conclusion: The operative reconstruction of extensive NSPs continues to present a clinical hurdle\. Rather than a single superior technique, literature suggests comparable closure rates across various methods\. However, the ultimate endpoint of treatment is not merely anatomical closure, but rather patient satisfaction through the alleviation of debilitating symptoms such as recurrent bleeding, crusting, and nasal obstruction\. Ultimately, surgical success depends on a complex interplay of etiology, surgical technique, patient comorbidities, surgeon experience, and the anatomical dimensions of the initial defect\.
