# Sharia Prescribes, Adat Applies: Reconstructing Cognitive Justice in Minangkabau Legal Historiography

**Autores:** Mhd\. Ilham Armi; Ahmad Luthfy Nasri; Syukra Latif; Ayu Ariska; Fitri Rahmawati

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Colonial legal historiography in Minangkabau has long portrayed matrilineal adat and Islamic law as antagonistic systems, casting adat as static custom and Islam as an external imposition\. This article repositions the formula Syarak Mangato, Adaik Mamakai \(SMAM\), rendered here as “Sharia Prescribes, Adat Applies,” as an analytical method for dismantling this binary, rather than treating the formula merely as a cultural description, as much of the existing scholarship has done\. The analysis proceeds in three stages: a genealogical critique of eight colonial legal sources; an analysis of allochronism, examining how twelve Indonesianist studies softened but did not fully dismantle the colonial dualism; and a contemporary socio\-legal analysis of twelve cases spanning household and kinship relations, economic and asset relations, and public governance\. The findings show that SMAM operates as a working legal practice within nagari\-based authorities, which negotiate inheritance disputes through asset categorisation, mobilise ancestral property \(pusako\) as productive economic capital, and assert local social control in the face of institutional hegemony\. The article argues that the integration of adat and Islamic legal norms is better understood as cognitive justice, grounded in the recognition of two epistemologies operating alongside one another, rather than as a compromise between competing systems\. Rather than proposing SMAM as a generalisable model, the article advances it as an empirically grounded comparative approach to examining pluralistic legal orders in postcolonial Southeast Asia\.
