# НЕРІВНІСТЬ УПРОВАДЖЕННЯ ШТУЧНОГО ІНТЕЛЕКТУ ПІДПРИЄМСТВАМИ ЄС: МІЖДЕРЖАВНИЙ І РОЗМІРНИЙ ВИМІРИ

**Autores:** О\.І\. Заяць; Іван Синетар

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial intelligence adoption is expanding across the European Union, while national averages conceal substantial firm\-size differences\. Absolute and relative gaps describe different dimensions of this diffusion\. The paper examines cross\-country and firm\-size inequality in AI adoption across all 27 EU Member States and distinguishes absolute distance from proportional disadvantage\. The empirical base is Eurostat’s 2026 statistical report with 2025 country\-by\-size adoption rates\. The absolute gap is the large\-enterprise rate minus the small\-enterprise rate; the normalised gap divides this difference by their sum\. Spearman rank correlations are primary and Pearson coefficients supplementary\. Because the official all\-enterprise rate is size\-weighted and correlates almost perfectly with small\-enterprise adoption, relative\-gap inference relies mainly on an equal\-weight measure averaging small, medium and large enterprises\. A medium\-enterprise\-only correlation provides a check independent of the normalised\-gap formula\. Coefficients of variation use the population standard deviation for the complete set of 27 Member States\. AI was used by 17\.0% of small and 55\.0% of large EU enterprises\. Small\-enterprise adoption ranged from 4\.1% to 37\.5% across countries \(CV=0\.515\), compared with 20\.8% to 79\.4% among large enterprises \(CV=0\.274\)\. The national adoption rate is moderately positively associated with the absolute gap \(Spearman ρ=0\.477, p=0\.012\)\. The equal\-weight measure is strongly negatively associated with the normalised gap \(ρ=−0\.816, p&lt;0\.001\), and the medium\-enterprise check is even stronger \(ρ=−0\.846, p&lt;0\.001\)\. Among non\-users, only 12\.7% of small enterprises had considered AI adoption, compared with 36\.5% of large enterprises; reported implementation barriers differed much less across size classes\. Cross\-country relative dispersion is substantially greater among small enterprises\. A higher size\-standardised adoption level can coexist with a sizeable absolute large\-small distance while being associated with a markedly smaller proportional disadvantage of small firms\. The results are cross\-sectional associations based on published Eurostat point estimates and do not establish causality\.
