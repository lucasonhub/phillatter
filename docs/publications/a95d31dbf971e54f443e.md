# The Lagrangian Structure of Cost\-Penalized Active Feature Acquisition

**Autores:** Joseph Bingham

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Active Feature Acquisition \(AFA\) sequentially selects features to maximize predictive accuracy under an acquisition budget\. A natural heuristic greedily maximizes Expected Information Gain minus a cost penalty, EIG\(j\|x\_S\)\-lam\*c\_j \(EIG\-Cost\), but its theoretical guarantees are unclear\. We provide an a\-posteriori certificate decomposing the gap to the optimal hard\-budget policy into a budget\-slack term and a greedy\-suboptimality term Del\_lam\. The certificate is computable when the penalized optimum is tractable; on small heterogeneous\-cost instances, we verify it exactly, finding Del\_lam near zero across 4,000 solved cases\. On the large clinical task, Del\_lam is not computable, so the decomposition is diagnostic rather than a numerical guarantee\. Our analysis identifies EIG\-Cost as one\-step greedy optimization of the Lagrangian relaxation of expected\-cost AFA, with lam as its dual multiplier\. For equal costs, we prove Del\_lam &lt;= exp\(\-gam\*I\(Y;X\_\[d\]\)\), where gam is the submodularity ratio, yielding a structural guarantee in the near\-submodular regime\. Supporting results establish an adaptive chain\-rule identity showing that cumulative stepwise EIG optimizes transcript information, and prove that A \-&gt; I\(Y;X\_A\) need not be submodular, even for jointly Gaussian variables\. This rules out a direct Nemhauser\-Wolsey\-Fisher bound; naive Bayes restores submodularity\. We also bound the randomization gap between hard\-budget and expected\-cost optima, analyze sensitivity to dual\-multiplier error under local smoothness, and derive a conditional CVAE generator\-shift bound given a bound on forward predictive KL\. We treat this KL as a diagnostic quantity, not as a transform of training loss\. Experiments cover synthetic Gaussian settings, AFA benchmarks, and clinical differential diagnosis on MIMIC\-IV\. On MIMIC\-IV, EIG\-Cost achieves macro\-F1 0\.180 \+/\- 0\.012 at a $30 budget, using 6\.6% of full\-panel cost\.
