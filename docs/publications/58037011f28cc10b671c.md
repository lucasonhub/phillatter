# A Decentralized Identity\-based Cross\-Domain Data Trading System

**Autores:** Kezhi Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper presents a decentralized identity\-based cross\-domain data trading system designed to address identity inconsistency, service unavailability, transaction non\-atomicity, and insufficient regulatory granularity in blockchain\-enabled data markets\. The proposed architecture introduces a unified decentralized identity layer for users and data assets, a secure cross\-domain DID resolution protocol, a consistency\-preserving cache, and an availability mechanism that combines consistent\-hash\-based scope allocation, redundancy, incentives, malicious\-node detection, and punishment\. On this basis, modular smart contracts implement cross\-domain information verification, identity\-attribute regulation, transaction validation, payment approval, and data transfer, while decentralized custody nodes and parallel transmission improve data delivery robustness\. A prototype was deployed on 20 CentOS 7 servers divided into two blockchain domains, with each server equipped with an eight\-core 2\.4 GHz processor, 8 GB RAM, and 50 GB SSD storage\. Experiments using 100,000 ERC\-1056 DID records show that cache\-free cross\-domain resolution maintains complete consistency, while a 50% cache hit rate causes 14% of first\-resolution results to require secondary resolution; the proposed update and re\-resolution mechanisms restore full consistency and improve the effective hit rate by 72%\. Even when the cache hit rate rises further, the initial inconsistency ratio increases only to 19%\. Without caching, average cross\-domain identity resolution latency is 180 ms, whereas LRU caching at a 70% hit rate reduces it to 82 ms, a 54\.4% decrease\. At a transaction submission rate of 500 tps, submitted transactions wait an average of 2\.3 blocks, and two sequential smart\-contract executions require approximately 5 s\. With a gas limit of 10,000,000, the base blockchain throughput is about 200 tps, while fully cross\-domain trading sustains approximately 90 tps\. Raising the gas limit to 30,000,000 increases throughput from roughly 270 to 360 tps across the tested transaction\-ratio range\. These results demonstrate that the proposed system achieves secure, regulatable, consistent, and practically efficient cross\-domain data trading at scale\. A protocol\-level Monte Carlo analysis further shows that, with eight voting edge nodes and dual\-node cross\-verification, the collusive false\-acceptance upper bound is 0\.522% at a 30% independent malicious\-node probability; a separate consistent\-hash simulation over 2\-16 domains confirms logarithmic lookup growth and decreasing scope remapping as the network scales\.
