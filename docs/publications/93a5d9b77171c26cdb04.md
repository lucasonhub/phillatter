# Beyond Vulnerability: Women’s Water Stewardship and Adaptive Agency in Climate\-Vulnerable Bangladesh

**Autores:** Mumita Tanjeela; Ruma Parvin; Manzuma Ahsan; Bernadette Montanari

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract In Bangladesh, the climate crisis manifests through acute water scarcity, which profoundly disrupts social reproduction, particularly for women\. Using a Feminist Political Ecology \(FPE\) lens, this study explores women’s adaptive agency in daily water management practices at both household and community levels\. The study was conducted in three climate\-vulnerable regions of Bangladesh: Mongla \(cyclone\-prone coastal Bagerhat\), Naogaon \(drought\-prone\), and Cox’s Bazar \(flash flood\- and landslide\-prone\)\. The study employed a qualitative approach incorporating in\-depth interviews with women and focus group discussions which complemented by non\-participant observation\. In addition, key informant interviews were conducted with local government representatives, government officials and NGO staff working on climate change adaptation to understand the institutional setting\. Data were thematically analysed following six steps of the thematic analysis process\. The results show that women play a crucial role as water stewards, using their traditional knowledge, social networks, and collective action to enhance household and community resilience in the face of climate uncertainty\. However, formal governance institutions seldom recognize their contributions\. The study therefore recommends that local water governance and the vital role of women’s traditional knowledge be recognized in policies, and that leadership for a gender\-responsive adaptation framework to climate change be adopted\.
