# A Gated Catchment Hydrology Model \(GCHM\) for Physically\-Interpretable Rainfall\-Evapotranspiration\-Runoff Simulation Across Diverse Hydroclimatic Regimes

**Autores:** M Jawad; HV Gupta; MA Farmani; A Behrangi; GY Niu

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Recent advances in machine learning have improved rainfall\-runoff prediction, yet most data\-driven models remain difficult to interpret and do not explicitly enforce physical conservation laws\. We present the Gated Catchment Hydrology Model \(GCHM\), a mass\-conserving architecture based on the Mass\-Conserving Perceptron \(MCP\)\. GCHM uses five storage states representing canopy interception, snow, soil moisture, groundwater, and streamflow generation\. Constrained gates govern precipitation partitioning, evapotranspiration, infiltration, seepage, runoff, and baseflow while learning context\-dependent process relationships\. We evaluate GCHM across 50 CAMELS catchments spanning snowmelt\-dominated, recent\-rainfall, historical\-rainfall, and arid regimes, using NOAA AORC meteorological forcing and remotely sensed vegetation and land\-surface data\. Performance is assessed using the Jawad\-Kling\-Gupta Efficiency \(JKGE\) metric, which accounts for stationary and non\-stationary hydrologic behavior\. We compare the full architecture with ablated variants and recurrent neural network benchmarks\. GCHM reproduces streamflow dynamics across diverse hydroclimatic regimes, including arid catchments, while maintaining interpretable states and fluxes\. Ablation experiments show that simplified architectures may perform adequately under wet conditions but struggle to represent flow persistence and recession during dry periods, when groundwater storage sustains low flows\. Learned gates recover hydrologically plausible relationships governing snowmelt, runoff, seepage, and evapotranspiration\. GCHM combines competitive predictive performance with mass conservation and interpretability, providing a foundation for hydrologic modeling and counterfactual analysis\.
