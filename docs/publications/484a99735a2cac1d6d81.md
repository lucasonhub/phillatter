# Timing luteal urinary pregnanediol glucuronide assessment using urinary luteinizing hormone: agreement with ultrasound\-determined ovulation

**Autores:** Rene Leiva; Rene Ecochard; Jonathan Yang

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Urinary pregnanediol glucuronide \(PdG\) is a non\-invasive marker of luteal progesterone exposure, but its interpretation depends on timing relative to ovulation\. We evaluated whether urinary luteinizing hormone \(LH\)\-derived reference points could approximate ultrasound\-determined ovulation \(USDO\) for scheduling mid\-luteal PdG assessment\. Methods This retrospective secondary analysis included 283 ultrasound\-confirmed ovulatory cycles from 102 women\. We evaluated the retrospectively identified urinary LH peak and the first urinary LH concentrations ≥10, ≥15, ≥20, ≥25, and ≥30 mIU/mL\. PdG measured 6–9 days after each LH reference was compared with PdG measured on the corresponding USDO\-timed day\. A PdG threshold of ≥15 µg/mL was used for the primary descriptive classification, with sensitivity analyses at ≥5, ≥10, and ≥20 µg/mL\. Ninety\-five percent confidence intervals were estimated using participant\-level cluster bootstrap\. Results First urinary LH ≥15 mIU/mL occurred a median of 0 days from USDO \(IQR −0\.5 to \+1\), with 70\.0% and 80\.7% of events occurring within ±1 and ±2 days, respectively\. At PdG ≥15 µg/mL, overall agreement ranged from 74\.3% to 82\.2% across days \+6 to \+9, with AUC values of 0\.764–0\.835\. Alternative PdG thresholds produced different trade\-offs between positive and negative agreement, while ≥15 µg/mL provided a comparatively balanced descriptive profile\. Conclusions First urinary LH ≥15 mIU/mL is a candidate prospective reference for scheduling day\-specific mid\-luteal urinary PdG assessment\. The present study establishes a candidate timing strategy rather than a clinically actionable decision rule; a single LH\-timed PdG result should not be used alone to diagnose luteal dysfunction or direct treatment\. These findings are hypothesis\-generating; numerical LH and PdG thresholds are assay\-dependent, require prospective platform\-specific validation, and should not be interpreted as diagnostic criteria for luteal phase deficiency unless validated in a clinical trial\.
