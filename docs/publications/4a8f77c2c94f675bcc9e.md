# REGULATORY CHALLENGES AND VALIDATION PROTOCOLS FOR CHIP\-BASED ORGAN MIMICRY

**Autores:** Pradip Karale; Sakshi Ithaped; Seema Gosavi; Nitin Sutar; Amit Narang; Agasti Ware; Nilesh B Chaudhari; Anjali Gavit; Raosaheb S\. Shendge

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The function of tissues and perhaps organs could be replicated utilize organ\-on\-a\-chip technology\. Clone organs, create functional organ\-like components on a fluidic chip platform, and replicate the microbiome of humans\. By getting rid of significant differences between cell cultures and common animal These bioengineered models have advanced toxicity testing &amp; customized medicine\. Organ chip technology may rebuild the human microbiota, recreate the physiology of tissues and organelles, and produce functional organs on multiple levels of chip substrates\. The regulatory acceptance of OoC systems becomes even more important as they find applications into preclinical and clinical research spheres\. Though dispersed, worldwide regulatory systems make standardisation and certification rather difficult\. Along with the global regulatory landscape for OoC platforms, the legislative framework for OoC technologies in important countries namely the United States, the European Union, Japan, China, and developing nations is exhaustively examined in this review stressing the status, verification problems, and future orientations\. The lack of uniformity, unclear classification, and reproducibility concerns create significant obstacles to regulatory approval\. Forward\-thinking initiatives like “Innovative Science and Technology Approaches for New Drugs” \(ISTAND\) Pilot project, which encourages the creation of pharmaceutical research contrivance have been started by the US Food and Drug Administration \(FDA\)\.
