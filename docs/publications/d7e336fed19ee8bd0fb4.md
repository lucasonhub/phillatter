# Cullinane, James: The Cogito Without Origin: Cognitive Continuity and the End of the Metaphysical Subject

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper challenges the Cartesian inference from thinking to selfhood by arguing that the concept of a metaphysical subject rests upon an outdated model of cognition as discrete, self\-originating mental acts\. Drawing upon contemporary neuroscience, evolutionary theory, and process\-oriented accounts of mind, it contends that cognition is temporally continuous, causally inherited, and historically embedded\. Every conscious state emerges from prior neural states shaped by developmental, genetic, and evolutionary conditions\. There is no identifiable mental first cause and no ontologically independent thinker underlying cognitive activity\. The “I” is not an origin point but an indexical marker within an ongoing biological process\. The Cogito succeeds only under the assumption that thought is immediate, unified, and self\-grounding; once cognition is understood as evolutionarily continuous, the inference to a metaphysical self collapses\. What remains is not an enduring subject, but a dynamic, recursively structured organism embedded in causal history\.
