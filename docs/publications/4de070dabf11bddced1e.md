# Statin Exposure and Survival Outcomes in Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis in an Asian Cohort

**Autores:** Anushika Raheja; Kexin Ang; Bernett Teck Kwong Lee; Seyed Ehsan Saffari; Eng‐King Tan; Crystal Jing Jing Yeo

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

INTRODUCTION: Data from Asian cohorts on whether statin therapy affects amyotrophic lateral sclerosis \(ALS\) progression are limited\. We examined whether statin exposure was associated with ALS disease duration or tracheostomy\-free survival in a multiethnic Asian ALS cohort\. METHODS: Data from patients diagnosed with ALS between 2012 and 2025 were retrospectively reviewed\. Patients were categorized as &quot;ever&quot; or &quot;never&quot; statin users for primary analyses, and as &quot;never,&quot; &quot;former,&quot; or &quot;current&quot; users at diagnosis for additional analyses\. Disease duration was defined as time from symptom onset to death, and tracheostomy\-free survival as time from symptom onset to tracheostomy or death without tracheostomy\. Kaplan\-Meier and Cox regression analyses evaluated associations between statin exposure and both outcomes\. Additional analyses examined statin discontinuation after diagnosis using time\-varying exposures\. Sensitivity analyses excluded post\-diagnosis imitators and applied 30\-, 90\-, and 180\-day lags\. RESULTS: Among 103 ALS patients, 27 \(26\.2%\) had ever received statins\. Statin exposure was not significantly associated with disease duration \(hazard ratio \[HR\]: 1\.42, 95% CI: 0\.64\-3\.14, p = 0\.387\) or tracheostomy\-free survival \(HR: 1\.04, 95% CI: 0\.50\-2\.18, p = 0\.913\)\. Post\-diagnosis statin discontinuation was associated with shorter survival in a time\-varying analysis \(HR: 6\.52, 95% CI: 1\.48\-28\.74, p = 0\.013\), although this finding was hypothesis\-generating\. Sensitivity analyses did not show statistically significant associations\. DISCUSSION: In this multiethnic Asian ALS cohort, statin exposure was not significantly associated with disease duration or tracheostomy\-free survival\. These findings add to the evidence that statins do not need to be discontinued due to ALS diagnosis\. Larger studies are needed to define this association\.
