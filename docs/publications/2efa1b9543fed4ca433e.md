# Multi\-Scale Regulation of High\-Temperature and High\-Salinity Water\-Based Drilling Fluids

**Autores:** Wang Zhen; Wen bo; Nie Tiegen; Kou Songlin

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Deep and ultra\-deep well drilling subjects water\-based drilling fluids \(WBDFs\) to multiple simultaneous disturbances: elevated temperature, high salinity, high\-density solids and acid gas contamination\. Conventional studies usually classify materials separately as “polymers, nanomaterials and bio\-based materials”, yet this makes it difficult to explain why the same material behaves so differently in different base muds and evaluation protocols, and equally difficult to handle the trade\-offs among rheology retention, fluid\-loss control, plugging and wellbore stability\. In this work, the 61 full texts provided by the user served as the candidate pool; after title\-and\-abstract screening and full\-text review, 46 were included, of which 33 served as core evidence\. The studies on high\-temperature, high\-salinity WBDFs are reorganised across five scales: molecular structure, particle interface, mesoscopic network, filter\-cake/wellbore barrier, and engineering performance\. The analysis shows that high\-temperature, high\-salinity failure is not a single “polymer degradation” event, but a continuous process comprising clay dehydroxylation and loss of hydrophilicity, electrical double\-layer compression, polymer chain coiling or desorption, particle aggregation, and pore interconnection within the filter cake\. Temperature\- and salt\-tolerant polymers synergistically maintain hydration, adsorption and steric hindrance through sulfonate groups, zwitterions, rigid rings, polyether side chains and hydrophobic association structures; nano\- and organic–inorganic composites rely on dispersion stability and particle\-size grading to build low\-permeability filter cakes; natural polymers must undergo hydrophobisation, grafting, crosslinking or compositing to cross the boundary of harsh conditions; and thermo\-responsive, self\-healing and film\-forming materials are driving the transition of these systems from passive tolerance to on\-demand response\. The main bottleneck in the current evidence is not a shortage of new materials, but the inconsistency of base\-mud composition, aging regimes, salt type, measurement temperature and HTHP conditions, which makes reported performance figures non\-comparable across studies and leaves long\-term cycling, combined contamination and field validation insufficiently addressed\. Future work should focus on standardised multi\-factor evaluation, in\-situ multi\-scale characterisation, data\-driven formulation optimisation, full\-life\-cycle green assessment, and material–system–formation co\-design\.
