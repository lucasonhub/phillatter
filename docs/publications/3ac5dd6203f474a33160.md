# Human\-validated LLM\-assisted assessment of a large\-scale online precision medicine course in diabetes

**Autores:** Lingsong Kong; Adelle Engmann; Chenchen Zhu; Sally L\. Baxter; Amit Dixit; Antony Ross; Alireza Delfarah; Lisa Linda Morselli; Delaram Rezaeikhonakdar; Alison Derbenwick Miller; Joseph Yracheta; Ramesh V\. Nair; Eva M McGhee; Scott Edmiston; Philip S\. Tsao; Arash Alavi; Amir Bahmani

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Precision medicine increasingly relies on multimodal data and artificial intelligence, creating a need for scalable education and assessment\. We developed an online diabetes precision medicine course and conducted an observational single\-group pre–post evaluation of knowledge, confidence, and rubric\-scored open\-ended responses among 435 learners who completed the course\. Because manual scoring of open\-ended responses is time\-consuming and subject to variability and human judgment, we developed a human\-validated, large language model \(LLM\)\-assisted scoring workflow\. Knowledge, confidence, and open\-ended response scores increased by 30\.0 percentage points, 1\.32 points, and 0\.39 points, respectively \(all p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Across 4350 responses evaluated by the LLM\-assisted workflow, only 12 \(0\.3%\) required human review\. In a stratified 300\-response validation sample, the final LLM consensus score achieved 75\.3% exact agreement, 99\.3% adjacent agreement, and 0\.7% major disagreements \(LLM unanimity, 74\.0%; human unanimity, 65\.7%\) with the final human score\. Course completion was associated with improvements in knowledge, confidence, and open\-ended response scores, while the findings also support human\-validated LLM\-assisted assessment as a scalable approach for evaluating open\-ended responses\.
