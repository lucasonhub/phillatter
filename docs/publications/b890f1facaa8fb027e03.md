# Embodied multimodal human\-computer interaction for English language learning: integrating speech, gestures, emotion, and adaptive feedback

**Autores:** Yao Tang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Current English language learning systems are often limited by single\-dimensional learning\-state perception, insufficient modeling of affective\-cognitive characteristics, and delayed or inflexible feedback regulation\. To address these limitations, this study proposes an affective\-cognitive gated cross\-modal transformer \(ACG\-CMT\) for embodied multimodal human\-computer interaction in English language learning\. The framework integrates learners’ speech, gestures, and affective responses as multimodal inputs\. The speech encoder learns low\-level acoustic\-prosodic representations from Mel\-spectral, energy, pitch/prosodic, and temporal patterns rather than receiving rubric\-derived target scores; a spatiotemporal motion encoder captures gesture and posture dynamics; and an affective encoder models facial\-action and vocal\-affect cues\. A cross\-modal transformer captures temporal dependencies and contextual interactions among linguistic output, embodied behavior, and affective fluctuations\. During fusion, an affective\-cognitive gate adjusts modality\-specific contributions using latent affective/cognitive context and pre\-decision task context available at inference time, without access to ground\-truth target labels\. A multi\-task decision module then supports learning\-state recognition, affective\-cognitive load estimation, and feedback\-policy matching\. ACG\-CMT achieves an accuracy of 0\.892, a macro\-F1 of 0\.884, an AUC of 0\.941, and a difficult\-state F1 of 0\.877 for learning\-state recognition; R 2 = 0\.836, RMSE = 0\.084, and MAE = 0\.068 for the operational affective\-cognitive load reference index; and a feedback\-policy matching rate of 0\.861 with an average latency of 438 ms\. These results indicate that the framework can integrate multimodal behavioral information for learner\-state estimation and adaptive feedback, while the internal gating patterns are interpreted as model\-level associations rather than causal psychological mechanisms\.
