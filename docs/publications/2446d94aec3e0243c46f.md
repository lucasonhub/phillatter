# Imitation Holds a Key to Evolution: Localized Repair and Verified Skill Acquisition for Adaptive Agents

**Autores:** Kai Hu; Hua Huang

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

An agent can retain successful trajectories without learning which part of an experience should repair a new discrepancy\. We formulate passive imitation \(PI\) as an architectural loop that localizes a violated contract, extracts a local goal, proposes an effect\-matched skill, tests a small patch, and retains evidence after full\-task verification\. We instantiate this loop as a deterministic symbolic controller and analyze its finite repair\-query complexity under explicit identifiability and noninterference assumptions\. Across ten simulated tool worlds, PI solves all 600 cold held\-out composition tasks with three allowed small trials, compared with 6\.17% for a whole\-trajectory control, while an equally expressive compositional repair control matches PI\. On an unseen tool revision, PI achieves 70\.0% success versus 24\.0% without small trials and 79\.0% with self\-trial enumeration; an exploratory 24\-task adaptation stream subsequently raises first\-submission success to 100%, supporting a limited correction\-and\-retention mechanism rather than a unique incentive advantage or general intelligence\. The CPU\-only implementation, integrity checks, and reproducible results are available at https://github\.com/FriendOfYishan/PI\.
