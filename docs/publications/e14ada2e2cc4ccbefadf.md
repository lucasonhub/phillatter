# Drivers of seniors’ natural burial intentions for sustainable urban land management

**Autores:** Edward Wang; Hung\-Chou Lin; Mei\-Yin Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction This study examines factors influencing Taiwanese adults aged 55 and above in their willingness to choose natural burials, an emerging nature\-based solution \(NbS\) for sustainable urban land management\. Applying the Theory of Planned Behavior alongside social marketing’s 4Ps, environmental awareness, and perceived security derived from attachment to adult children\. Methods A questionnaire survey was administered to older Taiwanese to examine their willingness to choose natural burials\. Results Results indicate that positive attitudes, supportive subjective norms, and strong perceived behavioral control each enhance willingness to adopt natural burials\. Within the 4Ps framework, perceived product relevance, accessible distribution channels, and effective promotional efforts positively influence intentions, whereas price sensitivity exerts a negative effect\. Additionally, heightened environmental awareness and greater perceived security among children further increase openness to eco\-burials\. Discussion These findings suggest that service providers and policymakers in Taiwan should develop affordable, well\-communicated products, ensure convenient access, and incorporate family\-centered messaging to boost adoption among seniors, thereby advancing urban sustainability through nature\-based end\-of\-life choices\.
