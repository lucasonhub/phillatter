# Spikes and Waves

**Autores:** Gwenevere Frank

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Computational demands continue to increase in machine learning and neuroscience, raising concerns about energy, environmental, and equity costs\.Three strategies address this issue: careful usage of Independent component analysis \(ICA\) algorithms, development of hardware for accelerating energy\-efficient spiking neural networks \(SNNs\), and the application of SNNs to real\-world energy\-constrained robotics\. We discuss spikes as the key computational component of SNNs and the biological substrate of EEG, and waves as both the acoustic sensing modality of sonar and the structure of the EEG signal itself\.Chapters 1\-3 discuss ICA, a commonly used blind source separation \(BSS\) technique in EEG processing\. We use a framework of quantitative metrics, mutual information reduction \(MIR\), pairwise mutual information \(PMI\), and source near dipolarity\. The Picard ICA algorithm was benchmarked against Infomax, AMICA, and a series of other BSS techniques, with Picard performing similar to Infomax but worse than the costlier AMICA\. Quantitative metrics for user\-configurable parameters of the AMICA algorithm, typically set by rule of thumb rather than evidence, are derived\. Subsampled data from a 13\-subject, 71\-channel EEG dataset shows decomposition quality following an asymptotic trend with increasing data, suggesting the benefits of additional data may extend beyond commonly cited heuristic thresholds\.Chapter 4 discusses the HiAER\-Spike system, a hierarchical, reprogrammable, event\-driven neuromorphic computing system\. HiAER\-Spike supports up to 160M neurons, 40B synapses running in faster than real biological time simulation\. A co\-designed software\-hardware stack allows for massively parallel, memory\-efficient execution of large SNNs\. HiAER\-Spike is demonstrated on CIFAR\-10, DVS gesture recognition, MNIST, and DVS Pong tasks\.Chapter 5 discusses applying spiking neural networks to object detection in sonar imagery for power\-constrained and battery\-operated autonomous underwater vehicles \(AUVs\)\. A fully spiking object detection network, SpikeYOLO, was benchmarked against convolutional neural network \(CNN\) and transformer\-based detectors on both accuracy and predicted energy usage\. The SNN solution showed competitive accuracy compared to the CNN baseline while using 3\-9x less energy and showing superior performance in certain noisy or low\-data scenarios\.Careful parameter selection, experiment design, hardware\-software co\-design, and architectural choices can greatly reduce the computational and energy burdens of cutting\-edge neuroscience and ML techniques without sacrificing quality\.
