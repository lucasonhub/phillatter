# Escobar L\.\-Dellamary, Luis: Corporeal Mimesis and Convergent Specification: How LLMs Simulate Embodiment

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Large language models exhibit linguistic competences that the embodied cognition tradition predicted would require a body\. Froese \(2026\) formulates the dilemma: ei\- ther these models enact non\-biological sense\-making, or its long\-taken hallmarks do not require it\. His exit, “technologically mediated embodiment,” names the phenomenon without specifying its mechanism\. This paper offers an alternative: LLMs achieve their competence through convergent specification, the fill\-in\-the\-blanks operation ordinary communication itself requires, since meaning\-making never depends on the overtly ex\- pressed alone\. The mechanism is mimetic, not inferential: natural language, as the tes\- timonial enactment of millions of situated, embodied exchanges, constitutes an ecology of convergences whose structure of mutual indication the model exploits by converg\- ing across lexical, syntactic, pragmatic, and narrative layers simultaneously\. Drawing on the philosophy of science of simulation and on non\-representationalist enactivism, the paper distinguishes two routes to world\-involving competence, remissional instal\- lation and the mimetic route\. Under a non\-representationalist ontology, mimesis and embodiment are engagements with the same regularities at different phenomenal res\- olutions, not dual conditions\. The dilemma dissolves: no non\-biological sense\-making is posited, no refutation of embodiment is conceded\. Sense\-making remains with the embodied subjects whose exchanges produced the corpus; what the model achieves is a mimesis, itself enacted and continuous with the intersubjective acts of meaning whose convergences it exploits\.
