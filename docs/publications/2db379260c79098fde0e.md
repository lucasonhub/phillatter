# Ανίχνευση ανωμαλιών κυκλοφορίας δικτύων με μεθόδους μηχανικής μάθησης

**Autores:** Γεώργιος Κόκκινος

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Network intrusion detection is a critical component of modern cybersecurity infrastructure, yet the increasing volume and diversity of network traffic make rule\-based detection systems insufficient against novel and evolving attack patterns\. Machine learning offers a data\-driven alternative, capable of identifying anomalous behaviour from statistical properties of network flows without requiring manually crafted signatures\. This thesis presents a systematic evaluation of four supervised machine learning models \(Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, and a fully connected custom Neural Network\) applied to multi\-class network traffic anomaly detection\. Two publicly available benchmark datasets are used: CIC\-IDS2017, a widely adopted dataset containing over 2\.8 million flow records across 12 traffic classes, and UNSW\-NB15, a structurally different benchmark containing over 2\.5 million records across 10 total categories\. Both datasets exhibit severe class imbalance, with benign traffic accounting for over 83% of samples in each case\. A complete preprocessing and evaluation pipeline was developed in Python, incorporating missing\-value handling, duplicate removal, label encoding, feature scaling, and imbalance mitigation via SMOTE\-based resampling and class weighting\. All models are evaluated under stratified hold\-out protocols using macro\-averaged F1 as the primary metric, supplemented by per\-class precision, recall, confusion matrices, and ROC and precision\-recall curves for the neural network\. Tree\-ensemble models consistently outperform the neural network on macro F1 across both datasets\. On CIC\-IDS2017, XGBoost reaches a macro F1 of 0\.895, compared with 0\.387 for the neural network, while the neural network&\#x27;s weighted F1 is 0\.826 versus XGBoost&\#x27;s 0\.997\. On UNSW\-NB15, performance drops notably on the minority attack classes, with XGBoost leading at a macro F1 of 0\.482\. Comparing the two benchmarks, tree\-ensemble macro F1 falls by about 46%, which highlights how dataset\-specific the decision boundaries are\. The feature\-importance plots suggest that CIC\-IDS2017 is driven by a mix of backward packet\-length and timing features, whereas UNSW\-NB15 places stronger emphasis on inter\-arrival timing alongside window\-size and rate\-based features, which is broadly consistent with network security domain knowledge\.
