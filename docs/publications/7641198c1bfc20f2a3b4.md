# Future\-Ready Public Service: An Integrated Framework for Institutional Adaptability

**Autores:** Tawfiq Bashir Samo

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Public institutions increasingly operate under conditions of technological disruption, workforce transition, fiscal constraint, environmental uncertainty, and complex cross\-boundary problems\. The phrase &\#x27;future\-ready public service&\#x27; is now visible in international and national reform discourse, but existing formulations vary in unit of analysis and emphasis: workforce preparedness, anticipatory governance, broad civil\-service transformation, or organizational renewal\. This article does not claim novelty for the phrase\. Instead, it develops Future\-Ready Public Service \(FRPS\) as an integrated institutional capability construct through a theoretical integrative review of recent literature published primarily between 2021 and 2026\. Five core capabilities are identified: strategic foresight; digital capability; workforce capability; adaptive leadership; and resilience\. The framework argues that future readiness is not adequately represented by isolated or purely additive capability scores: individual capabilities can make direct contributions, but their value also depends on complementarity, the absence of critical bottlenecks, and the institution&\#x27;s ability to mobilize them together as conditions change\. Systems thinking, collaboration, and organizational learning are treated as cross\-cutting enablers, while public value, ethics, accountability, and stewardship define the normative boundary of public\-sector adaptability\. Six theoretical propositions specify the contribution of each capability and the integrative effect of a balanced capability configuration\. The article also develops indicative measurement domains for subsequent empirical validation\. The contribution is therefore the integration, theorization, and operational positioning of future readiness as an institution\-level public\-administration construct rather than the introduction of a new policy label\. Keywords: Future\-ready public service, Institutional adaptability, Strategic foresight, Digital government, Workforce capability, Adaptive leadership, Resilience, Public value
