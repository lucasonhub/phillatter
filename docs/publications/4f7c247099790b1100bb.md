# Dual\-health protocol modelling of Li\-ion battery degradation for UAVs with capacity–resistance coupled metrics in aviation duty cycles

**Autores:** Lalu Gladson Robin; S\. K\. Remmi; T\. Mariprasath; Mengistu Zelalem Zewdu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Batteries must be able to hold enough usable energy, and have enough power to operate, for high power UAVs and electric VTOLs\. But state\-of\-health based on capacity can deem resistance degraded batteries in good condition for use when there is significant voltage sag risk\. This work proposes a dual\-health protocol\-aware model using both the reference\-discharge capacity retention and resistance\-derived power retention to assess and schedule the battery degradation\. Chronological time, current, cumulative discharge capacity and pulse\-resistance measurements were used to reconstruct cycling and reference\-performance data for 22 Sony–Murata VTC6 cells based on different aviation duty protocols\. The analysis resulted in 439 valid capacity checkpoints, 388 impedance checkpoints and 415 paired health states\. A generalized estimating\-equation model was used to quantify the nonlinear cycling and protocol effects and to account for the repeated within\-cell observations\. The leakage\-safe leave\-one\-cell\-out validation was used to assess a random\-forest predictor\. Although there was a strong association between the two degradation measures \(Spearman coefficient of 0\.882\), capacity loss was strongly correlated with resistance growth\. The trajectory of the capacity\-health was down 2\.468% points per 100 mission\-equivalent cycles and was highly curved\. At the last checkpoint observed, 20 out of 21 cells were resistance\-limited for power retention and 1 cell was capacity\-limited\. The median dual\-health scores at 500 cycles varied between 73\.52% for extended cruise operation to 90\.05% for lower voltage charging\. The cell\-wise validation showed a mean absolute error of 0\.800% point, a root\-mean\-square error of 1\.101% point and coefficient of determination of 0\.975\. The dual\-health metric has the benefit of avoiding over\-stating the operational fitness and capacity of a UAV, while providing a conservative state metric for battery eligibility screening, swapping, and risk\-aware UAV mission allocation\.
