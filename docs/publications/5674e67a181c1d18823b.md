# An AI Algorithm for Strategic Financial Decisions of Firms in Risky Imperfect Markets

**Autores:** T\. V\. S\. Ramamohan Rao

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Both the investors and the firms in imperfect markets experience information asymmetry\. As a result, they find obtaining full information before making any decisions to be more expensive relative to expected gains\. In general, individuals provide finances based on the market price of the common stock, dividend payments, and the ease with which they can liquidate common stock\. However, such decisions cannot be explained by a market like mechanism\. Similarly, there is no satisfactory behavioral basis to rationalize the choices of the firms and the investors\. It is possible to develop AI algorithms since a large amount of data is available on the actual decisions made\. The present study postulates a behavioral basis for the observed decisions\. In particular, it will be postulated that the financial manager wishes to generate adequate finances to support the capital decisions of the production manager\. Similarly, the production manager will consider it necessary to invest in capital stock to avoid losing market share to rival firms\. Intuitively, faced with the valuation of many objectives the financial manager attempts to pursue the numerically best objective and utilize the most productive strategy until they find some superior choice\. Empirically it was found that in industries like motor vehicles the demand in the long run would be basically for replacement\. Financial managers tend to concentrate on the short run objectives\. On the other hand, electrical and chemical products do not experience shortage of demand even in the long run\. They focus attention on long run objectives\. The proposed algorithm accounts for such differences\. It was noted that there is a recurrent change in the objectives and strategies of firms\. The numerical magnitudes may themselves be inadequate to describe the behavior\. Far more detailed data on the relative valuation of objectives and patterns of change over time would be necessary to develop satisfactory predictive algorithms\.
