# Towards machine learning\-based digital twinning for autonomous mobile robot control

**Autores:** Sajad Shahsavari

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Digital Twin \(DT\) refers to the technology that enables a synchronized digital replica to mirror the operation of its corresponding physical system\. A representative DT, i\.e\., a comprehensive, high\-fdelity, multi\-physics, multi\-scale, and continuously updated digital representation of a complex physical system, offers signifcant advantages\. These include the ability to optimize system operation at runtime and to test new developments extensively in a controlled digital environment\. In this thesis, we leverage the concept of digital twins to support the control and resource management of autonomous robotic systems\. We demonstrate that a model capable of capturing both the internal characteristics of a robot and the external environmental effects can be effectively utilized for proactive robot control under both long\-term and short\-term operational constraints\. To achieve this, we frst develop a general open\-source DT framework \(MCX\) in which models with different levels of abstraction can be embedded for various robotic subsystems\. We then apply data\-driven and formal models of key mobile\-robot components, such as mechanical dynamics, computational load, and battery behavior, both individually and in combination, to inform actuator control under constraints related to performance, energy consumption, and reliability\. We experimentally demonstrate that separate independent control of mechanical and computational parts does not guarantee an optimal solution for energy management\. Moreover, we show that, by employing even a rather simple DT for proactive control of computational and mechanical parts, robot’s energy management algorithm can operate signifcantly more effciently\. Later, we employ Reinforcement Learning \(RL\) method to learn the robot’s optimal control policy to take into account the energy consumption, mission time and application throughput criteria\. We further consider the degradation of robot’s energy reserve, i\.e\., battery, by proposing a Machine Learning \(ML\) model to quantitatively estimate the long\-term damage on the robot’s battery due to short\-term operational profle\. Experimental results show that exploiting internal modeling of robot behavior for managing long\-term and short\-term operational characteristics signifcantly improves the effciency and robustness of mobile\-robot control across diverse constraint settings\.
