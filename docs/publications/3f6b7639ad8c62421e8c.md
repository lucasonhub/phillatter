# Epidemiological evolution of repetitive strain injuries and work\-related musculoskeletal disorders in Brazil: comparative analysis between 1990 and 2025\.

**Autores:** Paulo Roberto Zétola; Caroline Da Ros Montes D’Oca

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The objective of this study was to analyze the evolution of the epidemiological profile of repetitive strain injuries and work\-related musculoskeletal disorders in Brazil, comparing the industrial and corporate scenario of the 1990s with national and regional data published between 2007 and 2025\. A literature review with comparative analysis of secondary data was conducted, using as historical reference the analysis of 125 repetitive strain injuries cases among workers in the electro\-electronic industry and, as a contemporary counterpart, studies based on data from Brazil&\#x27;s Notifiable Diseases Information System, the Department of Information Technology of the Unified Health System, and recent literature\. The historical sample showed a predominance of women \(73\.6%\) and workers aged 31 to 35 years \(28\.0%\), with initial emphasis on wrist tendinitis \(49\.6%\) and shoulder bursitis \(20\.0%\)\. At the end of follow\-up, chronic pain syndrome was recorded in 65\.6% of cases\. Contemporary data showed a higher case concentration in older age groups, especially between 40 and 49 years in the national study from 2007 to 2021 and between 40 and 59 years in the 2018\-2023 period\. In Paraná, between 2014 and 2023, 5,694 cases were recorded, with female predominance \(62\.5%\) and work shifts longer than six hours in 86\.8% of notifications\. The evolution of cases over time suggests greater clinical and occupational complexity of repetitive strain injuries and work\-related musculoskeletal disorders, underscoring the need for cautious interpretation of indicators and integrated preventive strategies\.
