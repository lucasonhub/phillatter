# Thermal and mechanical modulation of interfacial thermal resistance in GaN/diamond heterostructures based on machine learning potentials

**Autores:** Shengnan Ji; Qing Ai; Bowei Zeng; Jialong Chen; Meng Liu; Xiangyu Liu; Yong Shuai

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Heterogeneous integration of high\-thermal\-conductivity diamond with gallium nitride \(GaN\) represents a compelling paradigm to break through the thermal dissipation bottlenecks in high\-power electronics\. However, the intrinsic acoustic mismatch between GaN and diamond gives rise to a non\-negligible interfacial thermal resistance \(ITR\)\. This ITR severely limits the heat dissipation efficiency\. In this work, a high\-fidelity neuroevolution potential \(NEP\) machine learning interatomic potential is developed\. This potential demonstrates markedly superior predictive accuracy compared with the conventional Tersoff empirical potential\. Driven by this validated framework, non\-equilibrium molecular dynamics \(NEMD\) simulations are carried out across operational temperatures of 300–600 K and external compressive stresses of 0–8 GPa\. These simulations systematically investigate the monotonic and coupled regulation laws governing the GaN/diamond ITR\. NEMD simulations reveal that the ITR at 300 K ranges from 2\.40 to 3\.25 m 2 ·K·GW −1 across distinct interface configurations\. These values are in close quantitative agreement with the theoretical prediction of the diffuse mismatch model \(DMM\)\. Crucially, C\-N\-bonded interfaces exhibit ITR values approximately 20% lower than their C\-Ga\-bonded counterparts\. With increasing temperature, enhanced lattice anharmonicity increases the cross\-interface phonon density of states \(PDOS\) overlap ratio by approximately 3\.65% and raises the total phonon participation contribution \(TPPC\) by 5\.72%\. These combined effects result in a monotonic reduction in ITR\. The modulation of ITR by external compressive stress is even more pronounced\. At 8 GPa, the ITR precipitously drops below 2\.10 m 2 ·K·GW −1 \. This reduction is accompanied by increases in the PDOS overlap ratio and the TPPC of approximately 6\.57% and 10\.54%, respectively\. Simulated HRTEM and HAADF\-STEM characterizations provide direct atomistic\-scale evidence that compressive stress effectively shortens the interfacial bond lengths and reinforces multi\-body interatomic interactions\. The thermo\-mechanical coupled regulation mechanisms uncovered in this study provide essential theoretical guidance and novel design strategies for structural stress engineering and high\-performance interfacial thermal optimization in GaN/diamond electronics\.
