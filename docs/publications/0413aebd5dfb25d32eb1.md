# Road\-class\-aware switching LQR control for real\-time active suspension systems using machine\-learning\-based road identification

**Autores:** Tuğrulhan Akgül; Ali Unluturk

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study presents and experimentally validates a road\-class\-aware switching linear quadratic regulator \(S\-LQR\) for a quarter\-car active suspension system operating under ISO 8608 road classes\. Four road\-dependent LQR gains are designed offline after a statistical comparison of seven meta\-heuristic optimizers, from which Battle Royale Optimization provides the best overall ranking\. Online, an acceleration\-based support vector machine estimates the road class once per second, and a Statistical Decision\-Making Module \(SD\-MM\) forms the switching command from non\-overlapping three\-prediction windows\. A common quadratic Lyapunov function certifies nominal exponential stability of the continuous\-time modes and their exact 1\-kHz sampled\-data realizations under every locally finite switching sequence; thus, the 3\-s SD\-MM interval is not a dwell\-time requirement\. Relative to the manufacturer\-provided fixed\-gain Quanser LQR, the proposed S\-LQR reduces the RMS suspension\-performance measures by 13\.04–66\.83% under variable\-speed roads and by 9\.59–51\.43% under constant\-speed roads\. The ISO 2631\-1 W k \-weighted acceleration RMS decreases from 0\.5143 to 0\.1845 m/s 2 \. Under the primary Delay\-On assessment, the SD\-MM attains 87\.50% accuracy; its stable\-segment accuracy reaches 100%, with no steady false switches\. Ablation, robustness, measurement\-corruption, and timing analyses further define the benefits and limits of the architecture\. The complete S\-LQR task has a mean execution time of 6\.639 μs, a 99th\-percentile time of 15\.4 μs, and no missed deadline across 10 repeated 144\-s real\-time experiments\.
