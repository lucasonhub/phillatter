# Recent Advances in the Molecular Mechanisms of Nutritional Biochemistry in Human Health and Disease

**Autores:** 

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Nutritional biochemistry has evolved from descriptive assessments of nutrient intake to a mechanistic discipline that interrogates how dietary components regulate cellular signaling, gene expression, and metabolic homeostasis\.This review synthesizes recent advances in the molecular actions of macronutrients, micronutrients, and bioactive food constituents on key nutrient\-sensing networks, including AMPK\-mTOR, SIRT1\-PGC\-1α, PPARs, FXR/TGR5, and Nrf2\.Particular emphasis is placed on how these pathways coordinate energy metabolism, redox balance, and immune function, and how their dysregulation contributes to metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, neurodegeneration, and cancer\.We also examine emerging data from metabolomics and microbiome research showing that diet\-derived metabolites such as short\-chain fatty acids, secondary bile acids, and tryptophan indole derivatives act as signaling intermediates at the interface of the gut, liver, immune system, and brain\.In parallel, nutrigenomic and epigenetic studies illustrate how vitamins, methyl donors, amino acids, and phytochemicals remodel chromatin and transcriptional programs that shape individual disease risk\.Finally, we discuss how integrated multi\-omics and systems\-biology approaches are beginning to define molecular phenotypes of dietary response, providing a framework for precision nutrition\.Remaining challenges, including inter\-individual variability, environmental modifiers, and limited large\-scale clinical validation, are highlighted as priorities for future research aimed at translating mechanistic insight into targeted, personalized dietary strategies for chronic disease prevention\.
