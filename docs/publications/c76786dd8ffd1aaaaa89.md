# Safeguarding Fundamental Rights in the Age of Telemarketing: A South African Constitutional Law Perspective

**Autores:** Katlego Arnold Mashego; Tshilidzi Knowles Khangala

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper provides a systematic constitutional analysis of telemarketing regulation in South Africa, arguing that intrusive practices violate the foundational rights to privacy, dignity, freedom of association, and equality\. Using a doctrinal methodology, it examines the jurisprudential evolution of these rights in digital commerce, the efficacy of key statutes such as the Consumer Protection Act \(CPA\) and the Protection of Personal Information Act \(POPIA\), and the normative influence of international law\. The analysis reveals a critical disjuncture between robust constitutional guarantees and their practical enforcement, exacerbated by systemic challenges including inadequate regulatory capacity, technological advancement outpacing legislation, and the disproportionate targeting of vulnerable populations\. The paper concludes by proposing an integrated, rights\-based reform agenda, contending that effective telemarketing regulation is essential not only for protecting fundamental rights and human dignity in the digital age but also for promoting consumer trust, ethical business conduct, and sustainable digital market development\.
