# Beyond the Species Boundary: The Judicial Evolution, Adversarial Arguments and State of the Art of Non\-Human Personhood

**Autores:** Fernando José Sadio\-Ramos

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The traditional divide separating Homo sapiens from other animal species has historically relied on a Cartesian and Roman Law dualism that classifies non\-human animals as property \(&quot;things&quot;\)\. This essay analyzes the paradigm shift from viewing animals as objects to recognizing them as subjects of rights and non\-human persons\. Grounded in the ethical frameworks of sentience and the &quot;subject\-of\-a\-life&quot; concept, this work evaluates the historical impact of the Great Ape Project \(GAP\) and the civil law transition from property status to sentient beings\. Expanding upon these foundations, this study incorporates strategic litigation efforts, such as the habeas corpus petitions led for chimpanzees and elephants, alongside contrasting case studies like the top\-down judicial recognition of the orangutan Sandra in Argentina and the bottom\-up empirical communication demonstrated by the gorilla Koko\. The debate encompasses robust opposition arguments centered on the lack of reciprocal legal duties, contrasted with progressive jurisprudential views advocating for dynamic legal personhood\.
