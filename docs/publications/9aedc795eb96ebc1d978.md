# Bots, Trust, and Generative AI: Framing Communicative Agency in a Small\-Language Media Context

**Autores:** Mariam Kebadze

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This article examines how bots, chatbots, and generative AI are represented as forms of communicative agency in a small\-language media environment\. It draws on a mixed content analysis of 195 digital news items published by 1TV\.ge, Euronews Georgia, Imedi News, and Rustavi 2 between September 15, 2024, and December 31, 2025, a fifteen\-and\-a\-half\-month period spanning parts of two calendar years\. Rather than evaluating AI systems technically or measuring audience responses, the article analyzes how news coverage assigns roles to automated and AI\-based systems through publication patterns, outlet profiles, dominant frames, functional emphases, trust narratives, risk discourse, media literacy elements, and named AI systems\. The corpus was most often organized through a technological or business news frame \(93 items; 47\.7%\)\. Other frames included myth\-nightmare \(36; 18\.5%\), media literacy/fact\-checking \(26; 13\.3%\), global political competition/information war \(24; 12\.3%\), and myth\-idyll \(16; 8\.2%\)\. The comparison shows that Euronews Georgia emphasized international and security\-related coverage, Imedi News foregrounded education and textual interaction, Rustavi 2 emphasized consumer\-facing products and visual applications, and 1TV\.ge displayed a comparatively balanced explanatory profile\. The article argues that bot transformation is better understood as a change in communicative roles than as a simple replacement of older automation by AI chatbots\. In this process, agency, usefulness, trust, risk, and responsibility intersect\. The Georgian\-language case contributes to Human\-Machine Communication, AI\-mediated communication, and media\-framing research by showing how global AI debates are localized outside dominant English\-language media environments\. Coding was conducted by a single researcher; an intracoder stability check on 20 items \(approximately 10% of the corpus\) produced no changes to dominant\-frame assignments\.
