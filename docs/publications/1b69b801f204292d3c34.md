# Social insurance premium reduction and green innovation in enterprises: a study based on social insurance premium reduction reform

**Autores:** Yan Li; Haixia Cao; Zihao Fu; Zhihong Song

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Green innovation is essential for promoting sustainable economic development and mitigating environmental pollution\. Against the backdrop of China’s transition toward high\-quality economic development, this study examines whether reductions in social insurance premiums promote corporate green innovation and explores the underlying mechanisms and heterogeneous effects\. Methods Treating China’s 2019 social insurance premium reduction reform as a quasi\-natural experiment, this study employs propensity score matching combined with difference\-in\-differences \(PSM\-DID\) using panel data on Chinese A\-share listed companies from 2013 to 2024\. Results The findings indicate that social insurance premium reductions significantly promote corporate green innovation, with effects that persist over time\. Mechanism analyses reveal that the policy increases firms’ main business revenue, reduces their debt burden, and stimulates R&amp;D investment, supporting the income effect hypothesis\. However, no significant evidence is found that factor substitution undermines green innovation\. Heterogeneity analyses show stronger positive effects in regions with stringent environmental regulations, labor\-intensive firms, and highly polluting industries, while no significant differences are observed across ownership structures, local fiscal capacities, or levels of marketization\. Further analyses indicate that the policy significantly promotes green utility model patents but has no statistically significant effect on green invention patents\. Discussion These findings suggest that social insurance premium reductions can facilitate corporate green innovation by improving firms’ internal resource allocation\. However, broad\-based cost\-reduction policies may be insufficient to stimulate long\-term, high\-risk green innovation\. The study provides empirical evidence supporting the coordinated design of social insurance policies and targeted green innovation support measures\.
