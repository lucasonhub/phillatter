# Operationalizing doctoral persistence: dissertation chairing as a shadow retention system

**Autores:** Kimberly Jamison

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Dissertation chairs play a consequential role in supporting doctoral persistence, yet less is understood about how their work is situated within doctoral education and the institutional capacity available to support it\. This study examined how experienced dissertation chairs operationalize persistence during candidacy and what their experiences reveal about the organizational conditions shaping this work\. Methods This basic interpretive qualitative study examined the experiences of 20 experienced dissertation chairs representing 12 research\-intensive, predominantly White institutions in the United States through semi\-structured interviews\. Guided by the Intersectional Path\-Goal Mentoring Framework \(IP\-GMF\), data analysis consisted of an iterative process of deductive and inductive coding to examine chairs&\#x27; persistence\-supporting practices and the organizational conditions shaping their work\. Results The analysis generated three key findings: \(1\) operationalizing persistence through individualized, adaptive support; \(2\) navigating organizational conditions that shaped their capacity to support persistence; and \(3\) sustaining persistence through professional formation and the cultivation of candidates&\#x27; scholarly identities\. Across these findings, chairs described exercising adaptive professional judgment, mobilizing institutional resources and relationships, and fostering candidates&\#x27; scholarly identities and professional trajectories toward and beyond dissertation completion\. Discussion The findings conceptualize dissertation chairing as a shadow retention system through which institutions operationalize doctoral persistence by relying on decentralized faculty leadership\. This arrangement relies on chairs&\#x27; professional expertise, institutional knowledge, and relational capital within and beyond their institutions, while revealing an institutionalization gap between the responsibilities entrusted to faculty and the organizational capacity available to sustain their work\. Addressing this gap requires institutions to reconsider how they structure, distribute, recognize, and resource dissertation chairing within faculty workloads, alongside investments in professional preparation and coordinated support\. The study advances an organizational understanding of doctoral persistence by highlighting the need for institutional retention structures at the doctoral level that recognize, resource, and coordinate dissertation chairing as a critical component of persistence support\.
