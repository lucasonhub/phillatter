# Regional Innovation Disparities in Kazakhstan: Input–Output Misalignment in an Official Panel, 2014–2025

**Autores:** E\. O\. Tanassoglo; Dilek Çeti̇n; Myltykbayeva A\. T

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

How are innovation inputs and measured outputs distributed across the regions of a resource\-dependent post\-Soviet economy, and how closely do they correspond? We use a balanced official panel of Kazakhstan’s 17 regions for 2014–2025 \(204 region–year observations\) covering four indicators: R&amp;D expenditure, innovation\-active enterprises, the innovation activity rate and innovative product output\. Granted patents for inventions are available by region for 2021–2025 only; this series is shorter than the panel, so it is used in the 2025 cross\-sectional and descriptive analyses and does not enter the panel or machine learning models\. On the panel, we estimate dispersion measures, panel regression with region\-clustered standard errors and tree\-based machine learning interpreted with SHapley Additive exPlanations; on the 2025 cross\-section, we compute correlations, clustering and spatial association statistics\. Inputs are highly concentrated: Almaty and Astana together accounted for 65\.1% of national R&amp;D expenditure in 2025, and cross\-regional dispersion did not narrow over the period\. In the 2025 cross\-section \(n = 17\), R&amp;D expenditure, innovation\-active enterprises and patent grants are strongly inter\-correlated \(r = 0\.96–0\.97\), but none of the three is significantly correlated with innovative product output\. This input–output misalignment is a pattern whose mechanisms we do not test\. The panel specification has modest explanatory power \(R2 = 0\.188\), which bounds the weight that can be placed on any single coefficient\. Within it the innovation activity rate is the only significant predictor of output \(β = 0\.177, p &lt; 0\.001\), an association that survives exclusion of the three regions affected by the 2022 administrative reform; because that indicator and the output measure are compiled within the same system of enterprise observation, it is reported as a within\-dataset association requiring validation against independently measured indicators\. Under region\-grouped cross\-validation, the machine learning models show no out\-of\-region predictive ability and are reported solely as in\-sample interpretation\. The contribution is a documented diagnosis of input–output misalignment in an understudied regional system, with an explicit account of what such data can and cannot support, and a prospective statement of what institutional readiness for algorithmic decision support in regional innovation policy would require\.
