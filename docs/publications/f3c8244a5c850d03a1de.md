# Intelligence artificielle, cognition humaine et gouvernance environnementale : vers une rationalité écologique augmentée face aux défis climatiques

**Autores:** Salma El Alaoui

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In a context marked by intensifying environmental and climate crises, the transition towards sustainable development models can no longer be approached from a purely technical or economic perspective: it requires an understanding of the cognitive mechanisms that guide human behaviour\. Despite the growing availability of scientific information on ecological risks, a significant gap persists between knowing and acting\. The article asks to what extent human cognitive limits hinder ecological decision\-making and how artificial intelligence can help overcome them without compromising the autonomy of the actors involved\. Using a theoretical and analytical approach, the study draws on Herbert Simon&\#x27;s notion of bounded rationality and Daniel Kahneman&\#x27;s work on cognitive biases to highlight the structural constraints on human judgement, and on Andy Clark&\#x27;s extended mind theory to conceive of artificial intelligence as an extension of cognitive capacities in the processing of environmental data\. Bruno Latour&\#x27;s ecological philosophy further underlines the hybrid nature of an environmental governance in which nature, technology and society are closely intertwined\. The case of Morocco, which faces structural water stress, serves as an illustration\. The findings show that, although artificial intelligence can improve the quality of ecological decisions by facilitating access to and analysis of information, it raises ethical challenges linked to technological dependence and cognitive delegation, and its effectiveness depends on data governance and local institutional appropriation\. The article proposes to redefine ecological rationality as a distributed and augmented process, and concludes that a reflexive framework for these technologies is needed within contemporary environmental policy\.
