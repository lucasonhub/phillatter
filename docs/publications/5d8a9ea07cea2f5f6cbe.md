# Undergraduate Nursing Students’ Experiences and Perceptions of Virtual Reality\-based Clinical Skills Training in Saudi Arabia: A Qualitative Study

**Autores:** Amal A Alfouzan; Ahmed S\. Alsadoun; Fahad Alharbi; Albaraa N Aloudah; Yazan A Alghamdi; Yahia Aldhamri

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Virtual Reality \(VR\) is increasingly used in nursing education to support clinical skills through immersive and interactive learning\. Although evidence of its educational benefits is growing, qualitative research exploring nursing students’ experiences of VR\-based learning in Saudi Arabia remains limited\. Aim: To explore nursing students’ experiences and perceptions of VR\-based clinical skills training at King Saud University, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia\. Materials and Methods: A qualitative study was conducted with 11 undergraduate nursing students who had participated in VR\-based clinical skills training at King Saud University, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia\. Participants were recruited based on predefined eligibility criteria\. Data were collected through individual semistructured interviews via Zoom and analysed using Braun and Clarke’s reflexive thematic analysis\. Results: Four main themes were identified: VR as an immersive and engaging learning environment; building confidence and clinical readiness; comparing VR with traditional clinical skills training; and challenges and future directions for VR\-based nursing education\. Participants reported that VR increased engagement, improved their understanding of clinical procedures, and strengthened confidence, clinical judgement, and readiness for practice\. They valued repeated practice in a safe environment but emphasised that VR could not reproduce the relational and emotional dimensions of real patient care\. Participants therefore preferred a blended approach combining VR with traditional clinical training\. Reported challenges included initial unfamiliarity with the technology, occasional temporary dizziness, and the limited range of clinical scenarios available\. Conclusion: Nursing students perceived VR\-based clinical skills training as a valuable educational approach that supported learning, confidence, and clinical preparedness\. VR may be most beneficial when used as a complementary tool alongside traditional simulation and clinical education rather than as a replacement for direct clinical experience\.
