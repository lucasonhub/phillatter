# Human Presence Verification in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

**Autores:** Tennessee Lantuas

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Human Presence Verification in the Age of Artificial Intelligence A Preliminary Scientific and Philosophical Exploration Dear Members of the International Scientific Community, This video presents a preliminary interdisciplinary reflection on one of the emerging challenges of digital communication: How can we reliably distinguish human participation from autonomous artificial intelligence in online interactions? As conversational AI systems become increasingly sophisticated, the distinction between a human\-authored message, an AI\-assisted communication, and a fully automated response becomes progressively less apparent\. Our research proposal explores the potential of a multimodal human\-presence verification framework combining several technological approaches: \- Real\-time visual presence verification\. \- Smartphone motion sensors, including accelerometers and gyroscopes\. \- Cryptographic device attestation\. \- Time\-sensitive verification challenges\. \- Privacy\-preserving certification mechanisms\. The central hypothesis is that combining independent verification signals could strengthen confidence in the physical presence of a human participant\. However, verifying human presence does not necessarily establish human authorship\. A verified individual may subsequently delegate communication to an autonomous AI agent\. This distinction represents a fundamental scientific and philosophical question\. The mathematical examples presented in this preliminary work are illustrative models rather than experimentally validated findings\. They are intended to support discussion and identify directions for future research\. Further investigation would require controlled experiments, statistical validation, cybersecurity assessments, accessibility evaluations, and rigorous privacy analysis\. Research Ethics and Transparency This project was initiated through human reflection and developed with the assistance of ChatGPT, an artificial intelligence system created by OpenAI\. The human contributor directed the conceptual exploration, identified the research questions, and remains responsible for reviewing and validating the publication\. AI assistance contributed to structuring the scientific discussion, formulating illustrative mathematical models, and developing the explanatory text\. This collaboration is disclosed intentionally because transparency regarding human and artificial contributions is itself central to the research question\. Conclusion The objective of this proposal is not to establish that machines cannot imitate humans, but to investigate how digital systems might provide meaningful, voluntary, and privacy\-respecting evidence of human presence\. As artificial intelligence becomes increasingly capable of communicating on our behalf, the challenge is not merely to identify who is speaking, but to understand who is present, who is responsible, and what constitutes authentic participation\. We invite researchers in artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, digital identity, philosophy, and human–computer interaction to examine, challenge, and further develop these preliminary ideas\.
