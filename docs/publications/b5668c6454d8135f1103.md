# Decoupling Reliability from Timing Stability: An Empirical and Analytical Baseline Study of IEEE 802\.1CB FRER in Time\-sensitive Networking

**Autores:** Ahmed Mahir Mohammed; Emad H\. Al\-Hemiary

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

While IEEE 802\.1CBFrame Replication and Elimination for Reliability \(FRER\) provides reliable, faulttolerant in time\-sensitive networking \(TSN\), it is not proven that an infinitely reliable stream would be the same for timing purposes\.In this work, we performed a detailed empirical and analytical evaluation of FRER using OMNeT\+\+ 6\.2\.0 and INET 4\.4\.0,creating a reproducible baseline and providing a foundation to demonstrate future self\-healing and time\-sensitive networking systems\.Statistical equivalence testing using Two One\-Sided Tests \(TOST\) confirmed that the manual and automatic dynamic configurations were statistically equivalent within the defined tolerance margin \(±𝛿= 5%, p TOST &lt; 0\.00001\)\.The evaluation achieved an effective packet delivery ratio \(PDR\) of 100% \(apparent PDR of 99\.986% due to right\-censored pending frames\), with a mean end\-to\-end latency of 1\.929 ms \(± 0\.491\), network\-level jitter of 1\.398 ms, and packet\-level jitter of 14\.513 ns\.Despite uninterrupted communication and hitless recovery \(&lt;100 μs\), delay percentiles \(𝐿 50 = 1\.513 ms, 𝐿 95 = 4\.766 ms, 𝐿 99 = 6\.082 ms\) revealed a right\-skewed tail distribution, yielding a Timing Stability Index \(TSI\) of 2\.149\.The proposed cross\-connected topology helped show that duplicate elimination has been distributed in intermediate switches and in the destination, which gives more understanding of FRER operations\.Thus, the study demonstrates that perfectly reliable communication does not always ensure deterministic timing and gives an empirical and analytical baseline for the development and evaluation of forthcoming adaptive, self\-healing TSN systems\.
