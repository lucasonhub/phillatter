# University Academic Logos Versus Athletic Logos: Who Is Viewing Matters More Than Which Logo Is Shown

**Autores:** Rand Wergin; Bartlomiej Hanus

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Universities invest heavily in distinct academic and athletic logos, assuming that these visual signals communicate different institutional value propositions to prospective students\. Drawing on signaling theory, this study asks whether viewers’ dispositional traits shape their attitudes toward a university, and whether the type of logo encountered moderates those relationships\. Using a fictitious university and AI\-generated academic and athletic logos, we surveyed 387 respondents, each evaluating one logo, and modeled the data using partial least squares structural equation modeling\. The traits of task/goal orientation, need for learning, and need for social resources each predicted more favorable attitudes toward the institution, with need for social resources the strongest predictor, whereas need for competition did not contribute independently\. Critically, a multigroup analysis found no significant difference in any relationship between the academic\-logo and athletic\-logo conditions, indicating that viewer dispositions, rather than logo type, drove institutional attitudes\. These results qualify the common assumption that academic and athletic logos function as sharply distinct signals and suggest that who is viewing matters more than which logo is shown\.
