# Revealing hydrochemical regimes and health risks in an urban karst spring system

**Autores:** Chunhui Zhang; Xiao Yang; Ruchun Wei; Chuanshun Zhi; Changsuo Li; Xiancang Wu; Jun Wang; Qingwei Su; Qiaoqiao Jiang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Groundwater in urban karst spring systems is increasingly impacted by coupled natural processes and anthropogenic nitrogen loading, yet the linkage between hydrochemical evolution, nitrate\-related health risk, and contaminant transport remains insufficiently resolved\. This study develops an integrated framework for the Baiquan spring system by combining hydrochemical analysis, entropy\-weighted water quality index \(EWQI\), probabilistic health\-risk assessment, numerical transport modelling, and multivariate clustering\. Groundwater is characterized by moderate mineralization, Ca\-dominated cation chemistry, and mixed HCO 3 − –Cl \- anion compositions\. EWQI results indicate generally good to medium water quality, with distinct deterioration hotspots in the central and southwestern sectors\. Deterministic hazard quotients \(HQ\) range from 0\.02 to 1\.93, and Monte Carlo simulations reveal high exceedance probabilities \(0\.80–0\.99\) in these hotspots despite low regional risk\. Process\-based simulations using a coupled MODFLOW–MT3DMS model show that nitrate contamination remains spatially heterogeneous over time, with gradual attenuation of the central hotspot but persistent elevated concentrations in the southwestern sector under the regional EPM representation\. Clustering further distinguishes a dilute recharge\-dominated regime from a mineralized, anthropogenically influenced regime that coincides with water\-quality deterioration and risk hotspots\. The results demonstrate that nitrate vulnerability is jointly controlled by source heterogeneity, groundwater flow organization, and local retention conditions, and highlight the central and southwestern sectors as long\-term priority zones for groundwater management\.
