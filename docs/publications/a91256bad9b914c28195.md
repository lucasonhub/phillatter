# Case Report: Pulmonary artery banding for an older child with end\-stage dilated cardiomyopathy

**Autores:** Jiayuan Huang; Jiayuan Huang; Ling Sun; Changqing Tang; Yue Cao; Xifeng Liang; Yu Chang; Xin Li; Xin Huang; Jie Huang; Jie Huang

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Therapeutic options for children with end\-stage dilated cardiomyopathy are limited\. Pulmonary artery banding has been proposed as a potential bridge or alternative strategy in children with severe left ventricular dysfunction and preserved right ventricular function, but related experience in older children is limited\. We report a 5\.5\-year\-old girl diagnosed with dilated cardiomyopathy who showed progressive left ventricular dilation and persistent systolic dysfunction despite long\-term medical therapy\. Preoperative echocardiography showed left ventricular end\-diastolic diameter \(LVEDD\) of 64\.4 mm, Z\-score of \+9\.7, and left ventricular ejection fraction \(LVEF\) of 25\.0%, while cardiac magnetic resonance imaging \(CMR\) demonstrated relatively preserved right ventricular systolic function\. Pulmonary artery banding was performed without major perioperative complications\. At 4\-month follow\-up, her clinical symptoms improved from NYHA class II to class I; however, echocardiography showed persistent reverse remodeling, with an LVEF of 25\.7% and an LVEDD Z\-score of \+8\.8\. This case suggests that PAB may provide symptomatic benefit in older children with end\-stage DCM but that delayed intervention may limit myocardial recovery\.
