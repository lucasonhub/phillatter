# Temperature\-Field Reconstruction and Heat\-Source Inversion Estimation Method for High\-Voltage Switchgear Based Reduced\-Order PINN Surrogate Model

**Autores:** Shengxue Tang; Xiang Zheng; Er\-Peng Wang; Songyang Han; Yize Cheng

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Localized overheating induced by poor electrical contact, loosened busbar connections, and insulation degradation is a major precursor to failures in high\-voltage switchgear\. To address the difficulty in directly measuring internal heat sources, a fast three\-dimensional temperature\-field reconstruction, candidate heat\-source identification, and heat\-source power inversion method for high\-voltage switchgear is proposed based on a reduced\-order surrogate model integrating Proper Orthogonal Decomposition \(POD\) and physics\-informed neural networks \(PINNs\)\. First, a coupled three\-dimensional thermal–flow coupled computational fluid dynamics\(CFD\) model is established to generate temperature fields under different heat\-source conditions\. POD is then applied to extract the dominant thermal modes and their corresponding low\-dimensional modal coefficients from the high\-dimensional temperature fields\. A PINN\-based reduced\-order surrogate model is subsequently trained to establish the nonlinear mapping from heat\-source parameters to POD modal coefficients, while residuals of the steady\-state heat conduction equation and boundary\-condition constraints are incorporated into the loss function to enforce physical consistency and enhance prediction accuracy\. Based on external temperature measurements, an Improved Grey Wolf Optimizer \(I\-GWO\) is further adopted to identify the active fault heat source from a predefined set of candidate locations and inversely estimate its corresponding heat\-source power\. Numerical simulations and temperature\-rise experiments show that the proposed method accurately reconstructs the three\-dimensional temperature distribution and identifies local high\-temperature regions\. In the temperature\-rise experiment, the relative error of heat\-source power inversion is only 2\.27%, demonstrating that the proposed framework enables rapid and accurate temperature\-field reconstruction and heat\-source inversion\.
