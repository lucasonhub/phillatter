# Ultrassonografia além dos presets

**Autores:** Jamerson Silva De Albuquerque

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

INTRODUCTION: B\-mode/2D ultrasonography is a multiparametric and operator\-dependent imaging modality whose diagnostic quality depends on the integration of physical principles, beam formation, signal processing, and real\-time decision\-making\. Despite the increasing incorporation of automated features, a critical understanding of acquisition and processing parameters remains essential\. OBJECTIVE: To analyze, from a technical and cognitive perspective, the main physical and operational determinants of B\-mode/2D image quality and to discuss their diagnostic implications, practical limitations, and the risks associated with the uncritical use of presets and automated tools\. METHODS: This critical narrative review adopted a technical and conceptual approach and was based on classical and contemporary literature addressing ultrasound image formation, instrumentation, signal processing, safety, and image optimization strategies\. RESULTS: Parameters such as frequency, imaging depth, focal\-zone positioning, acoustic output power, gain, time\-gain compensation, dynamic range, line density, sector width, write zoom, tissue harmonic imaging, spatial compounding, and processing filters directly affect spatial and temporal resolution, tissue contrast, signal\-to\-noise ratio, and artifact expression\. Although presets and automated features may improve workflow efficiency and operational standardization, their application without an adequate understanding of the underlying physical principles and processing mechanisms may alter anatomic representation, obscure clinically relevant information, or contribute to inappropriate interpretation\. CONCLUSION: B\-mode/2D image optimization is an integral component of the ultrasound examination rather than a merely ancillary step\. Given the increasing technological sophistication of ultrasound systems and the expanding use of artificial intelligence–based tools, structured training in applied physics, instrumentation, and optimization strategies is essential to support diagnostic accuracy, patient safety, and the physician&\#x27;s active involvement in image acquisition and interpretation\.
