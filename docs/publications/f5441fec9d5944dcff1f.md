# Surface functionalization of nanostructured lipid carriers for efficient delivery of hydrophobic therapeutics: advances and future outlook

**Autores:** Subhojit Roy; Soumya Ganguly; Shayeri Chatterjee

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Nanostructured lipid carriers \(NLCs\) represent an advanced generation of lipid\-based nanocarriers designed to address the intrinsic challenges of delivering hydrophobic therapeutic agents, such as inadequate aqueous solubility, restricted bioavailability, swift metabolic degradation, and non\-specific tissue distribution\. Unlike traditional solid lipid nanoparticles \(SLNs\), nanostructured lipid carriers \(NLCs\) consist of a combination of solid and liquid lipids that create a somewhat disordered lipid matrix, which improves drug\-loading capacity, reduces drug loss during storage, and facilitates regulated and adjustable drug\-release patterns\. Advancements in fabrication methods, including high\-pressure homogenization, microemulsion templating, solvent evaporation, and ultrasonication procedures, have enabled the scalable, reproducible, and economical manufacture of NLC formulations\. Moreover, sophisticated surface engineering techniques\-such as PEGylation, ligand\-mediated functionalization, charge modulation, and polymeric coating methods\-have significantly enhanced systemic circulation duration, cellular uptake, mucoadhesion, and targeted drug delivery to specific sites\. A thorough physicochemical characterization utilizing techniques such as dynamic light scattering \(DLS\), transmission and scanning electron microscopy \(TEM/SEM\), encapsulation efficiency \(EE%\) assessment, in vitro drug\-release analysis, Fourier\-transform infrared spectroscopy \(FTIR\), and X\-ray photoelectron spectroscopy \(XPS\) is crucial to guarantee the structural integrity, stability, and functional efficacy of NLC systems\. Due to these beneficial features, NLCs have shown significant potential in improving the therapeutic effectiveness of several medications, including anticancer, central nervous system \(CNS\), antifungal, and anti\-inflammatory treatments\. Recent advancements, including stimuli\-responsive NLCs, lipid\-polymer hybrid nanocarriers, artificial intelligence\-assisted formulation optimization, and the integration of genetic and diagnostic payloads, have broadened the potential of NLC\-based nanomedicine\. Despite these advancements, challenges related to long\-term stability, immunogenicity, large\-scale manufacturing, regulatory approval, and clinical translation remain critical considerations that must be addressed to facilitate the successful commercialization and widespread therapeutic application of NLC\-based drug delivery systems\.
