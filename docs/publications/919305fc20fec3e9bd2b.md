# Coelati Rama, Stefano: The Mind as an Information Filter

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Why do living systems represent only a sliver of their environment? This paper argues that the answer is thermodynamic: because information is physical, every bit a system acquires and maintains is paid for in free energy that could otherwise buy persistence\. It introduces the Semantic Free Energy of the Expected Future \(S\-FEEF\), a criterion that selects an organism&\#x27;s perceptual policy by trading the metabolic cost of information against viability\. Building on Kolchinsky and Wolpert&\#x27;s account of semantic information, the paper refounds viability as the non\-equilibrium free energy of the agent&\#x27;s state, its Kullback–Leibler distance from the Boltzmann \(&quot;death&quot;\) equilibrium\. This single move eliminates a free parameter, places cost and benefit in the same physical units, gives the metabolic exchange rate a Landauer floor, and lets the criterion be derived from Millidge et al\.&\#x27;s Free Energy of the Future under four explicitly stated approximations\. An exhaustive computational model yields four results, each stated with its limits: the optimal agent compresses below the point of informational saturation \(&quot;less is more&quot;\); more information can reduce viability, but only for agents with bounded perception–action coupling; the &quot;dark room&quot; is a sharp, computable threshold rather than a paradox; and morphology non\-monotonically gates the value of a mind\. The same marginal\-return logic extends to evolutionary life history \(semelparity, the emergence of senescence, and bet\-hedging\) and to a thermodynamic account of where the perceptual filter comes from in the first place\. The framework is a deliberately small, fully reproducible toy model: it demonstrates mechanisms rather than universal laws, and proposes \(though it does not yet run\) a concrete empirical test\.
