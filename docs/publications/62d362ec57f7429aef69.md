# Unveiling the Lived Experiences of Senior High School Teachers in Adopting Artificial Intelligence\-Enhanced Instruction in Humanities and Social Sciences: A Phenomenological Study

**Autores:** Maria Cristita Bucayu\-Cuerdo

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial intelligence is becoming part of teachers’ instructional work, yet its use in Humanities and Social Sciences raises concerns beyond convenience\. This descriptive phenomenological study explored the experiences of eight Senior High School teachers adopting AI\-enhanced instruction in selected campuses of Las Piñas City National Senior High School\. In\-depth, semistructured interviews were analyzed using Colaizzi’s seven\-step method\. Eleven theme clusters described workload relief, resource access, material construction, activity design, contextual adaptation, professional learning, verification, access and content limitations, teacher agency, learner thinking, and emotional reassurance\. Participants used AI to organize lessons, prepare materials, and develop activities while continuing to verify, revise, contextualize, and sometimes reject outputs\. Difficulties included prompting, inaccurate or conflicting information, limited local context, language concerns, connectivity, subscription costs, and uneven preparation\. Teacherperceived learner interest coexisted with concern about copying, dependence, and reduced independent reasoning\. Teacher responsibility remained central to deciding what was accurate and meaningful for particular lessons and learners\. All eight participants confirmed the final fundamental structure without requesting revision\. The experience involved assistance alongside scrutiny, growing familiarity alongside uncertainty, and technological support alongside teacher agency\. A proposed six\-component framework was derived to inform instructional practice, with teacher agency operating throughout\. The framework remained conceptually developed and untested\. The findings provide a context\-bound account of teacher experience; they do not establish improved learner outcomes or instructional effectiveness\.
