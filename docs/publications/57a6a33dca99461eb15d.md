# Three Essays on Information Disclosure and Risk Exposure in Alternative Investments

**Autores:** Jue Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Information disclosure and risk exposure are central to decision\-making and risk management in financial markets\. Disclosures from alternative investment firms shape the information available to regulators and investors, influencing oversight and capital allocation, while firms’ risk exposure reflects their engagement in financial and social activities\. Hedge funds and Peer\-to\-Peer \(P2P\) marketplace platforms are key components of the alternative investment industry serving institutional and retail investors\. Hedge funds, as major institutional arbitrageurs that reduce market mispricing, managed $4\.3 trillion in assets in the first quarter of 2024\. P2P lending platforms both substitute for and complement traditional financial intermediaries and contribute to financial inclusion\. This dissertation examines how regulatory and voluntary disclosures, as well as exposure to public sentiment, affect risk and performance in alternative investments\. The first chapter, coauthored with William Goetzmann and Bing Liang, studies the 2011 Dodd\-Frank reform and hedge fund transparency\. New Form ADV disclosures improve the prediction of adverse operational events\. Using machine learning, we construct a unidimensional operational risk score from regulatory filings that predicts liquidation, leverage, performance, and fund flows\. Investor sensitivity to operational risk rises after the reform, indicating greater use of disclosed information\. Mandatory disclosure outperforms voluntary vendor data in identifying operational risk and has meaningful economic effects\. The second chapter, coauthored with Bing Liang, investigates whether hedge funds capitalize on fluctuations in ESG sentiment\. Using a global dataset of public ESG perceptions, we construct composite and pillar\-level sentiment indices\. Hedge funds actively time ESG sentiment, generating higher alpha and reducing downside risk\. Timing ability varies across strategies and aligns with sectoral and geographic exposures, indicating that funds incorporate values\-based sentiment into return generation and risk management\. The third chapter, coauthored with Wenyao Hu, examines loan listing practices in the P2P marketplace\. We find that Prosper disproportionately lists higher\-fee loans at the beginning of release periods to maximize revenue\. This strategy raises ethical concerns and highlights the need for greater transparency and regulatory oversight to ensure fair treatment of borrowers\.
