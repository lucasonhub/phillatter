# Closed\-circuit bioelectrochemical operation is associated with a higher relative contribution of deterministic assembly and altered targeted functional\-gene profiles in soil microbiomes

**Autores:** Pengyu Zhao; Shuhui Zhang; Xiaodong Zhao

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Microbial fuel cells \(MFCs\) are increasingly explored for soil remediation, yet the microbial ecological responses associated with conductive\-material interfaces and closed\-circuit operation remain incompletely understood\. Using three experimental setups—a non\-electrode anaerobic control \(AT\), an open\-circuit conductive\-material treatment \(TO\), and a closed\-circuit treatment \(TC\)—we examined bacterial community composition, assembly processes, targeted functional\-gene profiles, and exploratory co\-occurrence patterns\. The TO treatment showed a more highly connected exploratory network and slightly lower diversity within the targeted functional\-gene panel than AT\. The TC treatment showed a higher estimated relative contribution of deterministic processes than TO, although stochastic processes remained dominant in all treatments\. TC was also characterized by several electroactive\-associated taxa, including Geobacteraceae and Anaeromyxobacter , and by higher 16S\-normalized relative abundances of several sulfur\-cycling genes\. These results suggest that the introduction of conductive materials and closed\-circuit operation were associated with different bacterial community and targeted functional\-gene responses\. Because the TO–AT contrast includes both electrodes and carbon fibers, reactor\-level replication was limited, and electrochemical output was not continuously quantified, the findings should be interpreted as exploratory associations rather than direct evidence of current\-driven metabolic specialization\. This study provides testable hypotheses for future bioelectrochemical soil\-remediation experiments with greater reactor replication, electrochemical monitoring, and process\-rate measurements\.
