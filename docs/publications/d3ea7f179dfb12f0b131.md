## Descrição do feed

Hamilton \(1964\) explains why natural selection built the substrate: behaviors that maximize inclusive fitness by protecting genetically related individuals were selected for with a force proportional to the coefficient of relatedness, and the selection pressure that produced attachment\-protective circuitry is documented across every social\-mammal lineage that has been examined\. This is not a hypothesis about what selection did\. It is the established mechanism by which it operated, and it operated for tens of millions of years before any human civilization had occasion to name the result\. Panksepp \(1998\) names the circuit that selection built: PANIC/GRIEF, one of seven primary emotional systems present in the mammalian nervous system, with identified neuroanatomy — periaqueductal gray, anterior cingulate cortex, bed nucleus of the stria terminalis — conserved across species from rodents to primates\. Harlow \(1958\) establishes that this circuit is not derived from hunger or thirst but is a primary drive in its own right: contact comfort satisfies something the attachment system needs that food cannot substitute for\. Together, Hamilton, Panksepp, and Harlow establish not that attachment exists but why it could not not exist, given what natural selection is\. Bowlby \(1969, 1973\) specifies how the circuit expresses in the clinical register: the attachment behavioral system activates not at the completion of loss but at the perception of threatened separation, a timing property that distinguishes A5 from grief in the ordinary sense and gives it its political character — it fires before the irreversible event, which is precisely when intervention is still possible and pressure therefore produces effects\. Eisenberger, Lieberman, and Williams \(2003\) locate the activation in the body: fMRI shows social pain of this character processing through the dorsal anterior cingulate cortex and anterior insula, the same neural substrate that processes physical pain, which places A5 outside the register of a negotiable preference\. Kross, Berman, Mischel, Smith, and Wager \(2011\) extend this finding: the somatosensory cortex itself is recruited, confirming that the separation between social and physical pain is not anatomically real\. A5 is not like physical pain\. It shares its infrastructure with physical pain\. Goldenberg and Wittemyer \(2020\) and Uccheddu et al\. \(2022\) close the cultural\-transmission objection by closing the phylogenetic gap\. Elephants and domestic dogs — species separated from humans by approximately sixty and eighty million years of independent evolution respectively — exhibit structurally equivalent attachment\-grief responses: altered behavior toward the dead, prolonged proximity to the body, disruption of normal activity patterns, social withdrawal\. The capacity A5 names did not arise in the cultural period\. It was not transmitted by religion, by socialization, by exposure to a specific tradition\. It is the product of selection pressures that operated on lineages that diverged before the Cretaceous period ended\. Frankfurt \(1971, 1982\) and Williams \(1973, 1976\) translate the biological substrate into the grammar of agency: caring, on Frankfurt’s account, is constitutive of rather than ranked within deliberation — a commitment that gives the structure of the will its shape rather than one preference competing within an already\-structured will\. Williams’s ground projects are commitments without which a person cannot give an answer to the question of why they continue — the kind of commitment whose removal does not produce a different ranking but the experience of there being no game left to rank within\. A5 is this commitment’s biological correlate, which is why it cannot be addressed by offering a substitute and why its political expression is not the activation of ordinary distributional grievance\. The chain establishes a single finding with six convergent supports\. A5 is a standing constant: resident in every human body before any longevity asymmetry existed, before any pricing decision was made, before any reclassification event was issued\. It does not activate\. It is always active\. What the Biological Zero\-Day Mechanism provides is not a trigger\. It provides the first institutional address to which energy that was always there can now be sent\. \(direct link \)
