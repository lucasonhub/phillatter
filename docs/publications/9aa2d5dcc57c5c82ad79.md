# Search for Anomalous Production of the Higgs Boson at the CMS Experiment and Exascale Computing for the LHC

**Autores:** Aashay Arora

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

This dissertation presents a search for anomalous production of the Higgs boson with the Compact Muon Solenoid \(CMS\) experiment, together with two developments in reconstruction and computing for the High\-Luminosity LHC \(HL\-LHC\)\. The search targets vector boson scattering production of a Higgs boson with two vector bosons\. It aims to constrain the quartic HHVV coupling, quoted relative to its Standard Model value: departures from unity grow the cross section quadratically and boost the final\-state bosons\. The search uses the full Run 2 dataset, 138 inverse femtobarns at a center\-of mass energy of 13 TeV and 283 inverse femtobarns of Run 3 data at 13\.6 TeV, in three channels: an all\-hadronic state with three large\-radius jets and two single\-lepton states\. The dominant multijet and top quark backgrounds are estimated from data with an ABCD method built on two decorrelated classifiers, and the signal is extracted from a simultaneous fit to several orthogonal signal regions per channel\. The combination excludes a coupling of 1\.5, with an observed 95 percent confidence level upper limit of 0\.48 on that rate against an expectation of 0\.64; the Run 3 data alone constrain the coupling between 0\.612 and 1\.387, improving on the published Run 2 result\. The second development addresses charged\-particle tracking at the HL\-LHC using a machine learning approach: the first demonstration in CMS of a transformer\-based model trained with object condensation, built on Line Segment Tracking triplets to reconstruct full track candidates\. The model reduces the fake rate by roughly a factor of three and all but eliminates duplicates, at an efficiency comparable to the algorithmic baseline\. The third concerns moving the resulting data\. The HL\-LHC will produce an unprecedented volume of data, so the wide\-area network is no longer a best\-effort service but a critical resource that must be managed\. This work integrates the Rucio data management system with the SENSE software\-defined networking service, so that bandwidth is allocated by transfer priority and reservations can be reconfigured mid\-transfer without interrupting traffic in flight\. It is demonstrated on a multi\-domain testbed and in benchmarks at 400 Gbps\.
