# Generative AI Literacy and Multidimensional AI Anxiety in Creative Higher Education

**Autores:** Xin Yong; Junjie Gavin Wu; Wei Wei

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Engagement with generative artificial intelligence \(GenAI\) involves practical literacy as well as learning, professional, sociotechnical, and configuration concerns\. This mixed\-methods study examined whether these responses were differentiated among Chinese arts\-university students\. Primary inference used a quality\-screened cross\-sectional sample of 355 questionnaire records from 351 participant\-identifier clusters after excluding 105 of 460 eligible records \(22\.8%\) showing exact whole\-battery straightlining across all 59 structured items; the full eligible sample of 460 records from 455 clusters was retained as a sensitivity analysis\. GenAI literacy was assessed with a 16\-item music\-context\-adapted self\-report instrument covering cognitive \(CL\), behavioral \(BL\), affective \(AL\), and ethical \(EL\) literacy, and AI Anxiety with 21 items spanning Learning Anxiety \(LA\), Job\-Replacement Anxiety \(JR\), Sociotechnical Blindness Anxiety \(ST\), and AI Configuration Anxiety \(AC\)\. Of six prespecified associations, only EL\-JR and EL\-ST met the Holm\-adjusted criterion in the primary sample and remained supported in the full\-sample and prior\-use\-adjusted sensitivity analyses\. CL\-LA and BL\-LA were unsupported in both samples, whereas AL\-LA and AL\-AC were sample\-specification sensitive\. Measurement models showed mixed fit; CL\-BL separation improved after quality screening but remained incomplete\. A secondary 47\-person exact\-identifier matched panel showed higher later\-wave CL, BL, and AL scores, whereas EL and all four AI Anxiety dimensions showed no Holm\-adjusted difference\. These literacy comparisons are exploratory because the administered literacy items were music\-framed while the documented learning activities involved broadcasting scripts and AI\-supported video production\. Independently analyzed qualitative material provided construct\-level consistency with the EL\-ST interpretation and tentative complementarity for EL\-JR, but was not participant\-linked to the quantitative samples\. Overall, the evidence supports a bounded interpretation of differentiated, calibrated rather than anxiety\-free adaptation; it does not establish causal intervention effects or participant\-level mixed\-method mechanisms\.
