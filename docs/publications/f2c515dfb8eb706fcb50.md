# CLINICAL\-FUNCTIONAL EVALUATION OF ORTHOPEDIC TREATMENT OF PATIENTS ON DENTAL IMPLANTS

**Autores:** І\.V\. Yanіshen; K\.Yu\. Andriienko; A\.V\. Pohorila; R\.V\. Kuznetsov; Н\.В\. Горбатовська

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Modern prosthodontics is characterized by the rapid development of digital technologies that encompass virtually all stages of prosthetic treatment, from initial diagnosis to the fabrication and fixation of definitive prosthetic restorations\. Despite the active implementation of digital technologies into clinical practice, the issue of comparative evaluation of conventional and digital impression techniques during implant\-supported prosthetic treatment remains relevant\. In this regard, the comparative clinical and functional assessment of the outcomes of prosthetic rehabilitation on dental implants using conventional and digital impression techniques represents an important scientific and practical task aimed at improving contemporary prosthetic rehabilitation protocols\. Objective\. To increase the effectiveness of prosthetic rehabilitation in patients with partial edentulism through the fabrication of implant\-supported fixed prosthetic restorations\. Materials and Methods\. The study was conducted at the Department of Prosthetic Dentistry and the University Dental Center of Kharkiv National Medical University\. A total of 48 patients aged 28 to 67 years with partial secondary edentulism who underwent prosthetic rehabilitation using dental implants were included in the study\. All patients were divided into two clinical groups according to the implant system used\. Ethical considerations were addressed in accordance with the legislation of Ukraine, Articles 7, 8, and 12 of the Law of Ukraine “On Medicinal Products” \(1996\), the principles of Good Clinical Practice \(International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice, ICH GCP, 2008\), Order of the Ministry of Health of Ukraine No\. 690 dated September 23, 2009, “On Approval of the Procedure for Conducting Clinical Trials of Medicinal Products and Expert Evaluation of Clinical Trial Materials and the Standard Regulations on Ethics Committees,” with amendments and additions, as well as the Declaration of Helsinki of the World Medical Association \(1964, latest revision – 2024\)\. The article is a fragment of the planned research project of the Department of Prosthetic Dentistry of Kharkiv National Medical University entitled “Integration of Modern Achievements in Medicine, Materials Science, and Technologies of Diagnosis, Treatment, and Rehabilitation of Patients in the Clinic of Prosthetic Dentistry” \(State Registration No\. 0125U000939, implementation period: 2025–2027; Principal Investigator – Head of the Department of Prosthetic Dentistry, Doctor of Medical Sciences, Professor I\.V\. Yanishen\)\. All patients were in satisfactory general health and had no absolute contraindications to dental implantation and subsequent prosthetic treatment\. After completion of osseointegration and maturation of peri\-implant tissues, the prosthetic stage of treatment was performed\. Within each clinical group, two impression protocols were applied: a conventional technique using an open\-tray impression and A\-silicone impression material, and a digital technique using an intraoral scanner and scan bodies\. Follow\-up examinations were performed at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after fixation of the prosthetic restorations\. Statistical analysis was carried out using methods of descriptive and inferential statistics with determination of the mean value \(M\), standard deviation \(SD\), Student’s t\-test, and the level of statistical significance at p &lt; 0\.05\. Results A total of 106 dental implants were placed, including 52 Alfabio implants and 54 Megagen implants\. The average number of implants per patient was 2\.21 ± 0\.8\. During follow\-up examinations at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months, no cases of implant loss or impaired osseointegration were recorded, resulting in an implant survival rate of 100%\. The obtained results are consistent with contemporary studies and confirm the high effectiveness of digital technologies in implant\-supported prosthodontics\. The use of intraoral scanners allows optimization of both clinical and laboratory stages of treatment, improves the accuracy of prosthetic restorations, and enhances patients’ quality of life\. Conclusions\. The use of digital intraoral scanning reduces the duration of the clinical impression\-taking stage by 1\.9 times compared with conventional A\-silicone impression materials\. Implant\-supported fixed prosthetic restorations provide a statistically significant improvement in masticatory efficiency, as confirmed by functional assessment at all follow\-up periods\. Following prosthetic rehabilitation, patients demonstrated a substantial improvement in quality\-of\-life indicators according to the treatment satisfaction questionnaire\.
