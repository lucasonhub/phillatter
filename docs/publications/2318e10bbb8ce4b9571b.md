# Breu, Stephan Urs: The J\-Space as an Emergent Organizational Form: From Functional Analysis to a Morphology of Intelligent Organization

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

With the development of the Jacobian Lens, researchers at Anthropic succeeded in systematically reconstructing internal representations in modern language models and making them experimentally accessible across the course of processing\. In doing so, they identified a functionally privileged organizational form termed the J\-Space\. The published experiments show that selected information remains available there across multiple processing steps and serves as a shared internal basis for different parts of subsequent processing\. Targeted interventions and ablations further demonstrate that these representations causally influence the subsequent course of processing\. Later work by Nanda et al\. replicated central functional properties of the J\-Space in an independent Qwen model family\. Together with further, partly heterogeneous findings on related internal structures, this provides the first empirical basis for comparatively investigating organizational forms in modern AI systems\. This article first reconstructs the empirical findings on the J\-Space published to date and then situates them theoretically\. The central comparison is with the functional core of Global Workspace Theory, developed by Bernard Baars and subsequently elaborated in neurocognitive terms especially by Stanislas Dehaene and colleagues\. The analysis reveals extensive correspondence between the empirically demonstrated properties of the J\-Space and the central functional organizational capacities of a Global Workspace\. Given the current state of research, the principal differences concern concrete realization, while particular dynamic properties such as competitive access and ignition\-like threshold formation require further investigation\. The emergence of the J\-Space and the model\-spanning findings now available open a broader perspective\. The functional core of Global Workspace Theory may extend beyond the description of biological cognition and may be understood more generally as a theory of intelligent organizational forms\. This article develops that possibility as a hypothesis and explicitly directs attention away from the question of artificial consciousness and toward the functional organization of flexible intelligence and its potentially substrate\-independent organizational principles\. \(direct link \)
