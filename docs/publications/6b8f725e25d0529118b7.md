# ActionOPD: Distilling Decisions, Not Tokens

**Autores:** Huining Yuan; Xiangmin Yi; Zelai Xu; Zixu Wang; Xinlei Chen; Xiao\-Ping Zhang; Yu Wang; Chao Yu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Distilling capable language\-model agents into smaller models requires aligning the student&\#x27;s behavior with the teacher&\#x27;s\. This raises a fundamental question: at which states and at what granularity should this alignment be performed? Offline imitation provides dense but token\-specific supervision and can suffer from state\-distribution mismatch\. Token\-level on\-policy distillation \(OPD\) addresses this mismatch but typically requires teacher token probabilities and compatible tokenization\. We argue that semantic actions provide a natural unit of supervision for agent distillation\. We introduce ActionOPD, an action\-level on\-policy distillation method motivated by maximizing expected teacher\-action likelihood at student\-encountered states\. At each such state, ActionOPD samples actions from both the student and a black\-box teacher, rewarding student actions according to their semantic agreement with the sampled teacher actions\. Through policy optimization, ActionOPD aligns the student&\#x27;s action\-level behavior with the teacher&\#x27;s decisions without requiring reproduction of teacher\-written reasoning or access to teacher token probabilities\. Training Qwen3\-4B and Qwen3\-8B with two teachers on diverse, curated tool\-use tasks yields average gains of 3\.3\-6\.9 percentage points across four out\-of\-distribution benchmarks\. ActionOPD scales favorably with student size and teacher capability, without requiring the teacher and student to share a model family or tokenizer\. On $τ^2$\-bench, ActionOPD achieves competitive performance against offline distillation and token\-level OPD\. We further explore Self\-ActionOPD with a same\-model teacher and privileged context\. These results establish ActionOPD as a promising approach to agent distillation\.
