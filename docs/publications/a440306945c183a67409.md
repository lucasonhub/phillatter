# Attention Sinks in VLMs Are Borrowed, Not Seen

**Autores:** Dengjia Zhang; Alexander Martin; Pengyu Yan; Xihan Sun; Jiaqi Li; Benjamin Van Durme; Andrew Yates; Reno Kriz; William Walden

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

We study how to detect and mitigate visual attention sinks in vision\-language models \(VLMs\)\. Visual attention sinks are image tokens that absorb a large share of attention over the image regardless of the query or task, and they mislead methods that treat attention maps as a record of where the model \`\`looked\.&\#x27;&\#x27; Prior work has detected sinks through massive activation dimensions and proposed redistributing their attention, but none have addressed what makes a token become a sink\. Comparing six candidate detectors on ten VLMs from four families, we find that only the centered cosine similarity between an image token&\#x27;s key and the key of the language model&\#x27;s text\-sink detects visual sinks\. Thus, a visual sink is an image token whose key aligns with the text\-sink&\#x27;s key\. Moving a sink&\#x27;s key component along this direction to the median of the image keys sends its attention back to the text\-sink \(\\textbf\{Are Borrowed\}\) while leaving model answers unchanged\~\(\\textbf\{Not Seen\}\)\. Building on this, we propose a training\-free, entropy\-triggered re\-look that first cleans the attention map, then crops the most\-attended region of the cleaned map and inserts it into the reasoning trace\. Cleaning adds negligible computation to the forward pass, and the re\-look improves accuracy with a mean gain of $\+4\.3$ points on fine\-detail VQA benchmarks across model families\.
