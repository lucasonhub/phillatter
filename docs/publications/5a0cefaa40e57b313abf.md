  contradictions are developed in detail. The first is internal to a single postulate:
  P5 requires in one clause that the substrate be definite, this whole set of units,
  and supplies in the next the maximization over candidate systems and partitions
  that is to make it so, while P2 requires that the quality and quantity of existence
  be observer-independent. Four lines of evidence establish that the maximization''s
  output depends on the investigator. The search space is the set-partitions of the
  units multiplied by a direction assignment per part, and the procedure defined over
  it terminates for no biological system, so the investigator''s truncation is not
  a shortcut around the theory''s procedure but what returns a substrate at all. The
  measure''s claimed uniqueness is argued from its properties and never from the substrate
  it selects. The theory assigns the partition space, the distinctions, and the grain
  to one principle of maximal existence, and the three searches are nested rather
  than parallel, so they are one dependence. Where the procedure returns more than
  one answer, the theory resolves the tie in the first person plural. The second contradiction
  is between integration (P4) and composition (P6), which requires that consciousness
  be both undecomposable and structured into distinctions and relations, a pair the
  theory holds while stating that "causal distinctions have no inherent structure
  themselves" and reporting Φ-structures whose components it counts to the integer
  and bounds by formula. The third is between definiteness, the first clause of P5,
  and the joint demands of maximal information (P3) and structured composition (P6),
  which together require simultaneous specificity and variability. The available defenses,
  the discovery-property distinction, constitutive composition, and the aspect-distribution
  reading, each preserve the joint commitment by suspending one demand, and the three
  suspend three different demands, so the set is satisfiable at any moment only by
  not being the set the theory states. Two of the three suspensions fall on P5, which
  is the postulate that contradicts itself. Five results follow from the three. The
  observer-dependence of the maximization is the condition methodology names researcher
  degrees of freedom, and it is worse in IIT''s case than in the standard one, because
  the choices reach the object the theory is about rather than the route to testing
  it. The theory records its own non-satisfaction cases: when the resolution of ties
  exhausts, the tied systems "do not comply with the exclusion postulate" and "do
  not qualify as complexes," so a system fails to be conscious because a computation
  returned two values rather than one. The rarity the theory offers as the reason
  ties can be set aside is itself a requirement of micro-level indeterminism, and
  the theory''s worked examples require indeterminism to be absent for Φ to be high,
  with the cortex named as the substrate where both demands fall. The postulates specify
  what obtains and never what operates: where the theory names something at these
  seams it names a principle of maximal or minimal existence, offered as a good explanation
  of why outcomes are what they are, which supplies a reason and not an operator.
  The case of uncertainty, treated structurally rather than phenomenologically, is
  settled by the theory against itself, since the congruence condition on which distinctions
  compose a complex excludes incongruent cause-effect states from the structure, so
  the Φ-structure identified with the experience is not the structure of the state

  IIT 4.0 as stated, and which suspension is in force when a system is called conscious
