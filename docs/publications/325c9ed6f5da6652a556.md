# A two\-stage RAG\-LLM pipeline for roadmapping neuroscience research in operational environments

**Autores:** Cara A\. Spencer

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Neuroscientific research and applications in real world environments are often constrained by limited access, equipment limitations, and high costs\. Consequently, robustly selecting constructs to focus effort on is a high\-impact task\. Method This study introduces a two\-stage large language model \(LLM\) pipeline that generates research roadmaps from existing real\-world data to inform research priorities in the field, given the state of the art\. Stage 1 uses retrieval\-augmented generation on operational logs to identify candidate psychological, social, and neurocognitive constructs and map their co\-occurrence\. Stage 2 evaluates how well these constructs are represented in the literature across four dimensions that commonly inform prioritization: ecological validity, neural correlates, biosensor protocols, and brain computer interface readiness, producing an evidence gap matrix\. Pipeline evaluation combined human review, deterministic computations, and human\-LLM alignment analysis\. Results Applying the pipeline to Mars Desert Research Station logs, Stage 1 identified 19 constructs\. Stage 2 highlighted critical gaps in neuroimaging and measurement protocols, particularly for social domains, thereby identifying potential research priorities and risks\. Discussion This methodology offers a generalizable starting point for synthesizing operational logs into actionable research roadmaps, but the evaluation showed it still required human scrutiny\.
