# Fujie, Naoto: A History of Operational Shapes Conceptualized as “Thought” Operational Intervals, Nature, Kant, Hegel, Posthuman Intelligence, and Xenoscience

**Autores:**

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper reconstructs the history of what has been conceptualized as “thought” without assuming in advance that thought is a uniquely human, mental, linguistic, or rational capacity\. It treats thought instead as a historically variable operational shape whose ignition conditions, temporal intervals, inferential organization, embodiment, and relation to nature have been differently configured across philosophical traditions\. The analysis begins from the problem of how thought is separated from or situated within nature, then examines Kant’s account of judgment, concepts, synthesis, and apperception, and Hegel’s attempt to overcome fixed oppositions between thought and world through conceptual self\-development\. Rather than presenting Kant and Hegel as stages in a teleological history, the paper identifies the operational intervals within which specific forms of thought become possible, stable, and revisable\. It then extends the analysis to posthuman and artificial intelligence, asking whether systems that lack human embodiment, biological continuity, or inherited conceptual architectures can nevertheless generate forms of inference, self\-correction, representation, and world\-discrimination that warrant classification as thought\. Xenoscience provides the final comparative framework: instead of fixing “thought” as a prior category, it tracks the configurations in which thought\-like operations emerge, transform, cease, or become nonredundant\. The broader claim is that the history of thought is better understood as a history of changing operational conditions than as the continuous history of a single faculty, subject, or substance\. \(direct link \)
