# Global Research Trends in Tick\-Borne Diseases of Livestock: A Bibliometric Analysis

**Autores:** 

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Tick\-borne diseases \(TBDs\) are a critical limitation of livestock health and productivity on the international arena as they result in huge economic losses and emerging challenges due to changing environmental conditions\.The objective of the current study was to examine the current trends in the world in terms of the research on tickborne livestock diseases through the use of bibliometric methodology\.A structured search strategy was used to retrieve publications indexed in PubMed database between 2020 and 2026 and 2,405 records were included in the analysis\.The place of bibliometric analysis was conducted with the help of Bibliometrix \(R 4\.5\.2\) and VOSviewer to assess the trends of publications, top contributors, collaboration networks, and theme development\.The findings revealed a more or less fluctuating but generally speaking increasing pattern in scientific production with the highest production expected in 2025\.China, Brazil and the United States were found to be the most prolific nations with some of the most renowned research institutions like Obihiro University of Agriculture and Veterinary Medicine and Universidade Federal de Goiás in the center stage\.Through co\-authorship analysis, it was found that there is a strongly collaborative and interconnected international research network, but there was low participation of lowincome regions\.Thematic mapping and co\-occurrence of keywords gave common areas of research, which were pathogen detection, epidemiology, and host vector interactions, with a rising trend of molecular diagnostics and genomic techniques\.New areas of study associated with climate change and ecological conditions pointed to the changing priorities of research\.The research, in general, indicates a tendency toward multidisciplinary and technology\-related studies of the topic of tick\-borne livestock diseases\.Although much has been done, there are still gaps in the research of the endemic regions and the establishment of sustainable control measures\.The results obtained are useful in understanding the state of research in the global arena and can be used in conducting further research to enhance the management of the disease and livestock well\-being\.
