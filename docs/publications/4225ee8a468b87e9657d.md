# A hierarchical evolutionary deep reinforcement learning algorithm for influence maximization in multilayer hypergraphs

**Autores:** Yanan Zhang; Yufeng Su; Yunfan Zhou; Yao Yue; Zhen Li; Zhi Wu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Multilayer hypergraphs provide a unified representation of influence diffusion in complex networks by coupling relation layers through shared entities and encoding higher\-order group interactions as hyperedges\. Existing influence maximization \(IM\) methods have been extensively studied on ordinary graphs and single\-layer hypergraphs, yet they leave two structural limitations unresolved\. Single\-layer methods cannot represent the propagation of an entity across multiple relation semantics, whereas most multilayer methods still use pairwise edges as their basic diffusion units and therefore omit hyperedge\-induced group interactions\. We address seed selection under the joint effects of multilayer coupling and higher\-order influence by introducing Multi\-Layer Hypergraph Linear Threshold with Cross\-layer Thresholding \(MLH\-LT\-CT\)\. The model combines a dual\-threshold intralayer mechanism for hyperedges and nodes with a δ \-threshold rule that couples entity states across relation layers\. We extend the non\-submodularity result for the single\-layer hypergraph linear threshold \(LT\) model to the multilayer setting and show that cross\-layer thresholding can further modify the marginal\-gain structure induced by intralayer higher\-order coordination\. Consequently, the classical \( 1 − 1 / e \) guarantee for submodular greedy maximization does not directly apply\. To optimize this non\-submodular objective, we propose Hierarchical Influence Via Evolution \(HIVE\), which integrates population\-level global search by differential evolution, adaptive seed\-selection guidance from a deep Q\-network, and topology\-guided local refinement\. We also establish an incremental\-propagation equivalence theorem from the extensivity, idempotence, and monotonicity of the propagation closure\. The theorem establishes equality of the final diffusion state obtained by incremental updates and full recomputation; it is not itself a running\-time guarantee\. Across 11 synthetic and real multilayer hypergraph datasets, HIVE strictly outperformed MA\-IM on eight datasets\. The ablation and sensitivity analyses further assess the contributions of evolutionary search, reinforcement\-learning guidance, and topology\-guided refinement; additional threshold scans characterize the transition between saturated, discriminative, and propagation\-suppressed regimes; the correctness of incremental state reuse is established theoretically\. These results support explicit cross\-layer threshold modeling and hierarchical optimization as a coherent approach to non\-submodular influence maximization in multilayer higher\-order diffusion systems\.
