# EL\-RAKHAWI QUANTUM COLLECTIVE OPTICAL RESONANCE SPECTROSCOPY SYSTEM \(E\-QCORS\) A Femtosecond Near\-Field Optical Architecture for Real\-Time Observation of Quantum Collective Electron Behavior in Two\-Dimensional Materials

**Autores:** mohamed kamal arafa el\-rakhawi

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** book

## Resumo

The El\-Rakhawi Quantum Collective Optical Resonance Spectroscopy System \(E\-QCORS\) is a revolutionary five\-layer femtosecond near\-field optical architecture for real\-time observation of quantum collective electron behavior in two\-dimensional materials\. Combining ultrafast pump\-probe spectroscopy \(10 fs effective temporal resolution via attosecond\-precision delay\-stage scanning\), scanning near\-field optical microscopy \(10 nm spatial resolution\), and dual\-mode detection, E\-QCORS overcomes fundamental limitations of transport measurements, conventional optical spectroscopy, and scanning tunneling microscopy\. The system operates across a wide temperature range \(4K\-300K\) with in\-situ control of magnetic field \(0\-9T\), strain \(0\-2%\), and carrier density\. The core innovation is the Quantum Collective Coherence Index \($\\Xi\_\{QCC\}$\), a strictly bounded dimensionless metric computed from the third\-order nonlinear optical susceptibility using causal transfer entropy decomposition combined with constrained microscopic modeling \(GW\+BSE\)\. This enables unambiguous discrimination between single\-particle and collective quantum behavior while preventing mathematical singularity\. The automated quantum phase classification system identifies superconducting, quantum Hall \(at cryogenic temperatures &lt;10K\-30K\), exciton condensate, spin liquid, and charge density wave phases through machine learning trained on distinctive nonlinear optical signatures with explicit temperature and field constraints\. Real\-time quantum phase mapping with nanoscale resolution accelerates quantum materials discovery by 10\-100x compared to traditional methods\. E\-QCORS provides the first practical pathway to direct, real\-time observation of quantum collective phenomena, enabling rapid development of quantum electronics, discovery of novel quantum materials, and fundamental tests of quantum many\-body theories\.
