# Revisiting Inherited Metabolic Diseases \( IMDs \) Through the Lens of Selective Autophagy

**Autores:** Marianna Maddaluno; Laura Cinque; Viviana Cetrangolo; Chiara De Leonibus

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Selective autophagy is increasingly recognized as a fundamental quality\-control mechanism that complements bulk autophagy by enabling the targeted degradation of specific intracellular cargos, including mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum \(ER\), lipid droplets, glycogen, peroxisomes, ferritin, ribosomes, and protein aggregates\. Cargo selectivity is mediated by selective autophagy receptors \(SARs\), which link damaged or superfluous substrates to the autophagic machinery through LC3/GABARAP interactions, thereby coordinating cargo sequestration\. Recent studies have revealed substantial mechanistic differences between bulk and selective autophagy, highlighting the specialized signaling pathways, receptor networks, and transcriptional programs that regulate selective cargo clearance\. Dysregulation of these pathways has emerged as a key contributor to the pathogenesis of inherited metabolic diseases \(IMDs\), a heterogeneous group of genetic disorders characterized by defective metabolism and multisystem involvement\. This review summarizes the molecular mechanisms underlying major forms of selective autophagy, including mitophagy, pexophagy, glycophagy, lipophagy, ER\-phagy, and ferritinophagy\. It also discusses their relevance across IMD classes, such as mitochondrial disorders, lysosomal storage disorders, and peroxisomal diseases, among many others\. Furthermore, selective autophagy is highlighted as a potential therapeutic target, with modulation of autophagy receptors and signaling pathways offering promising strategies to restore cellular homeostasis and improve disease manifestations in IMDs\.
