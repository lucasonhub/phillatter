# A hybrid scale attention network for uncertainty aware brain tumor MRI classification

**Autores:** Tareque Jamil Josh; Md\. Minhazur Rahman Mim; Md\. Aminur Rahman Joy; Md Assaduzzaman; Md Mahmudul Hasan Moon; Sheak Rashed Haider Noori; Nafiz Fahad; Tze Hui Liew

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate and reliable classification of brain tumors from magnetic resonance imaging \(MRI\) is critical for timely clinical decision\-making\. Existing deep learning methods suffer from three key limitations: overconfident predictions without principled uncertainty estimates, lack of external validation across acquisition protocols, and parameter\-heavy architectures unsuitable for resource\-constrained deployment\. To address these gaps, we propose Hybrid Scale\-Attention Network, a lightweight framework comprising an EfficientNet\-B3 backbone, a depthwise\-separable Adaptive Multi\-Scale Module \(AMSM\), and an Evidential Deep Learning \(EDL\) classification head\. AMSM fuses three parallel dilated convolutional branches at rates \{1, 2, 4\} and an identity branch via learned softmax gating, forming a convex combination of multi\-scale representations at an approximately 6\.6× lower parameter cost than equivalent standard convolutions\. The EDL head enables single\-pass evidential uncertainty estimation based on Dirichlet strength\. Through systematic four\-variant ablation, we find that stacking a Dual Attention Module after AMSM degrades cross\-dataset generalization despite modest in\-domain gains, motivating its exclusion\. On BRISC 2025, the proposed model achieves 99\.20% accuracy and 99\.30% macro\-F1 with only 10\.97 M parameters\. Development\-stage external validation on the PMRAM\-BD cohort yields 97\.41% accuracy without fine\-tuning, indicating strong cross\-dataset performance\. Misclassified samples carry higher mean evidential uncertainty than correctly classified ones, with a misclassification detection AUROC of 0\.9461 at single\-pass inference cost \(16\.8 ms\), offering an efficient alternative to MC\-Dropout\. Grad\-CAM and UA\-GradCAM visualizations alongside MSAD provide radiologist\-interpretable localization and per\-class scale\-branch analysis\. These results demonstrate that the proposed model offers a favorable trade\-off among accuracy, parameter efficiency, and principled uncertainty quantification, supporting potential retrospective clinical investigation\.
