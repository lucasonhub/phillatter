# Welfare effects of behavioral responses to basis risk in remote\-sensing livestock insurance in northern Kenya

**Autores:** Simon Maina; Maryfrances Miller; Gregory L\. Torell; Niall P\. Hanan; Julius Y\. Anchang; Njoki Kahiu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Livestock production in northern Kenya&\#x27;s arid and semi\-arid rangelands is increasingly vulnerable to climate variability\. The Index\-Based Livestock Insurance \(IBLI\) program was introduced to mitigate drought\-related losses by linking payouts to satellitederived Normalized Difference Vegetation Index \(NDVI\) thresholds\. However, IBLI&\#x27;s efficacy may be hampered by basis risk \-the discrepancy between actual losses and insurance payouts\. This study examines how downside basis risk \(losses without indemnity\) is associated with pastoralist welfare and household consumption responses\. Methods Using data from the IBLI program \(2019\-2021\) across eight Kenyan counties \(Garissa, Isiolo, Mandera, Marsabit, Samburu, Tana River, Turkana, and Wajir\), this study employs a two\-stage empirical approach\. First, quantile regression assesses the relationship between precipitation and forage availability\. Second, a modified variance\-of\-error model quantifies the magnitude of basis risk\. Results The analysis reveals persistent downside basis risk across all counties and years, suggesting that households frequently incur losses without receiving insurance compensation\. The findings demonstrate that repeated exposure to downside basis risk is associated with changes in pastoralists&\#x27; consumption, which may ultimately lead to welfare losses\. Discussion While IBLI is designed to stabilize consumption, persistent downside basis risk undermines its welfare benefits and may erode trust in the insurance mechanism\. To strengthen IBLI&\#x27;s potential to deliver sustainable welfare outcomes for pastoralist communities, future policy interventions must enhance index precision, revise subsidy structures, and incorporate behavioral considerations into program design\.
