# Visual Classification of Spirulina Microalgae Maturity Stages Based on Color Using Convolutional Neural Networks

**Autores:** Dicky Hermawan; Hasanul Fahmi Zuhri

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Manual assessment of microalgae maturity is subjective and does not scale well\. This study evaluates whether a convolutional neural network can classify Spirulina microalgae into five visual maturity stages \(dead, unripe, under\-ripe, medium\-ripe, ripe\) using color alone\. From 5,005 images collected over three months at a single facility, byte\-exact duplicates accounted for 41\.9% of the dataset; after removal, 2,909 unique images remained\. To prevent near\-duplicate images from the same session leaking across folds, evaluation used a group\-stratified 5\-fold cross\-validation scheme, with groups defined via perceptual\-hash clustering\. An ablation across four preprocessing configurations found that combining CLAHE with augmentation, the original proposed method, was numerically weakest \(94\.74% ± 2\.15% accuracy versus 97\.43% ± 0\.89% with no preprocessing\), differing significantly from CLAHE alone \(p = 0\.021\) though not from the plain baseline at this sample size \(p = 0\.085\)\. A separate comparison, matching fine\-tuning depth \(last 30 layers\) and preprocessing across a custom CNN, MobileNetV2, and ResNet50, found ResNet50 highest at 99\.52% ± 0\.18% accuracy, significantly ahead of the custom CNN \(97\.06% ± 1\.39%; p = 0\.024\), while MobileNetV2 showed severe fold\-to\-fold instability\. The custom CNN&\#x27;s advantage lay in efficiency: 54\.8 times fewer parameters and roughly 3\.5 times faster inference than ResNet50, for only a small accuracy trade\-off\. A brief, unlabeled field test on six images outside the lab setting showed frequent disagreement among the three models, suggesting limited transfer from controlled to field conditions\. Grad\-CAM maps are presented as an indication of model attention, not evidence of underlying chlorophyll degradation biochemistry
