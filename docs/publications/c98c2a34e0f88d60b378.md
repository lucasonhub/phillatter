# From Pitch to Paycheck: Reconstructing Premier League Salaries with Player\-Grouped Machine Learning Validation

**Autores:** Zicheng Zhong

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Professional football salaries reflect both player performance and the institutional context in which a player competes\. This study evaluated how accurately publicly available demographic and on\-field performance data could reconstruct annual salaries for players in the English top\-flight football league while addressing repeated observations of the same player\. The final dataset contained 1,240 player\-season observations representing 672 unique players across the 2022\-23, 2023\-24, and 2024\-25 seasons\. Annual salary in euros was log10\-transformed, and a fixed set of 17 numerical and three categorical predictors was evaluated with eight regression approaches\. The primary evaluation used five\-fold cross\-validation grouped by player, preventing any player from appearing in both training and validation data within a fold\. Elastic Net produced the strongest grouped performance, with mean R2 = 0\.426 \(SD = 0\.050\), mean absolute error = 0\.225 log10 units \(SD = 0\.017\), and a typical multiplicative error factor of 1\.68\. A secondary temporal evaluation trained on the first two seasons and tested on 2024\-25\. Elastic Net retained R2 = 0\.442 and a typical error factor of 1\.63\. A feature ablation on identical folds showed that club and demographic information carried most of this signal, and that fourteen further match measures added an increment whose paired interval spanned zero\. The findings indicate moderate salary reconstruction but do not support causal claims\. Club context and demographic factors carried more predictive information than any single match\-performance statistic\.
