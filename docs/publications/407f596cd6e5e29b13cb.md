# Generalised Bit\-Vector Abstractions for Formal Verification of Quantum Error\-Detection and Entanglement Circuits over $$\\\{H,X,\{\\text\{C\-NOT\}\}\\\}$$: CSS Constructions, Soundness, and Mutation\-Based Validation

**Autores:** Arun Govindankutty

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Formal verification of quantum circuits becomes increasingly difficult as circuit size increases, since exhaustive reasoning over the underlying Hilbert space is computationally expensive\. In previous work \[1\], we introduced a bit\-vector abstraction that separates the tracking of superposition from basis\-state behaviour, enabling SMT\-based verification of fixed\-size error\-detection and entanglement circuits constructed from H , X , and C\-NOT gates\. This paper extends that framework in five directions\. First, we generalise the correctness properties to parametrised repetition and concatenated phase\-flip codes with up to 101 logical elements\. Second, we verify syndrome extraction for CSS codes specified by arbitrary parity\-check matrices, including the Steane code, the \[\[15, 7, 3\]\] quantum Hamming code, the \[\[15, 1, 3\]\] quantum Reed–Muller code, and rotated surface codes with distances up to 101\. The verification considers arbitrary error patterns, error detection up to a specified weight, and recovery using a classical decoder\. Counterexamples are obtained for errors beyond the correction or detection capability of the respective codes\. Third, we characterise the fragment of $$\\\{H,X,\\text \{C\-NOT\}\\\}$$ circuits for which the abstraction faithfully represents the corresponding Hilbert\-space semantics\. We prove soundness for this fragment, demonstrate the necessity of the stated conditions through counterexamples, validate the characterisation by differential testing against state\-vector simulation, and prove the six lemmas established in the original work\. Fourth, we conduct a mutation study using 13 mutation operators and automatically identify equivalent mutants\. Within the evaluated fault model, the approach detects all 622 non\-equivalent mutants\. Fifth, we implement the approach directly using the Z3 API and verify GHZ circuits with up to 262,144 qubits across three topologies on a 16 GB laptop\. The evaluation includes repeated measurements and a controlled ablation of the encoding pipeline and solver version\. Experiments with random H \+C\-NOT networks show that verification cost increases steeply with circuit depth when outputs depend on a large number of inputs\. The approach is not intended to cover general stabiliser circuits, Y \-type stabilisers, fault\-tolerant protocols, or physical noise\. We identify the extensions required to address these cases\.
