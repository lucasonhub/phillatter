# A Remotely Delivered Exercise\-Based Rehabilitation Program for Patients with Persistent Chemotherapy\-Induced Peripheral Neuropathy \(EX\-CIPN\): A Phase I Feasibility Trial

**Autores:** Eric Antonen; Michelle B\. Nadler; David Michael Langelier; Kristin L\. Campbell; David Flamer; Jang Hyuk Cho; Scott J\. Capozza; Lisa M\. Avery; Kelcey A\. Bland; Scott Leatherdale; Jackie Manthorne; Muna Al\-Khaifi; Jennifer Michelle Jones

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Chemotherapy\-induced peripheral neuropathy \(CIPN\) affects two\-thirds of patients who receive neurotoxic chemotherapy\. In almost half of these patients, CIPN is persistent, with limited treatment options\. Exercise\-based rehabilitation shows promising results in improving CIPN\-related outcomes, but high\-quality research is needed with CIPN symptoms and disability as primary endpoints\. Methods: We conducted a phase I single\-arm, multi\-method pilot study of a 10\-week remotely delivered exercise\-based rehabilitation program \(EX\-CIPN\) for adult \(≥18\-years\) cancer survivors experiencing CIPN symptoms ≥6 months post\-chemotherapy\. Primary outcomes were feasibility \(accrual, retention, and adherence\), acceptability \(interviews and surveys\), and safety \(CTCAE v5\)\. Secondary outcomes included pain, CIPN symptoms, CIPN\-related disability, and physiologic assessments\. Assessments were conducted at baseline \(T1\), post\-program \(T2\), and 3 months post\-program \(T3\)\. Post\-program feedback surveys were completed at T2 and semi\-structured qualitative video interviews were completed between T2 and T3\. Results: In total, 36 participants \(median age: 59, 83% female\) consented to participate in the study\. T1 retention rate \(90%\), adherence rates \(77–96%\), and no reported serious adverse events \(Grade 3 CTCAE v5\) supported study feasibility and safety\. Interviews and surveys supported intervention acceptability\. Retention rates at T2 \(68%\) and T3 \(63%\) did not reach feasibility targets\. CIPN symptoms and CIPN\-related disability showed significant improvement at T3 \(p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Conclusions: Results suggest that EX\-CIPN has encouraging feasibility outcomes and is acceptable and safe\. Preliminary clinical outcome results are promising\. Based on these study results, we can proceed to test this intervention, with a few modifications, in a prospective randomised trial with a CIPN\-related primary outcome\.
