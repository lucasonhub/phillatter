# Regime\-Gated Tail Transfer: Reliable Cross\-Industry Machine Learning for Value\-at\-Risk and Expected Shortfall

**Autores:** Rakshay Pawar

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Pooling financial series gives machine\-learning tail models more extreme observations, but it can also transfer the wrong tail dynamics across assets and regimes\. Regime\-gated tail transfer \(RGTT\) combines Student\-t GARCH, filtered historical simulation, industry\-specific quantile boosting, and pooled cross\-industry quantile boosting\. FZ0\-scored weights depend on nine lagged volatility–breadth states, with shrinkage toward global performance\. A coverage controller \(RGTT\-C\) uses past exceedances and preserves ES ≤ VaR\. Hyperparameters are selected on 1995–1999 and frozen before a 2000–July 2026 test across ten U\.S\. industry portfolios \(66,840 industry\-days\)\. RGTT significantly improves on equal weighting and a state\-agnostic gate\. RGTT\-C attains mean FZ0 −3\.4887 and a 2\.491% breach rate at the 2\.5% target, with significant loss advantages over filtered historical simulation and both quantile\-ML experts\. Its advantage over Student\-t GARCH is not significant at 5% \(two\-sided p = 0\.072\)\. The pooled expert’s mean weight falls from 22\.5% to 13\.5% between low\-volatility/low\-breadth and high\-volatility/high\-breadth states\. The framework makes cross\-industry transfer state dependent while separating expert weighting from an order\-preserving coverage controller\. JEL classification numbers: C22, C45, C53, G17, G32\. Keywords: Expected shortfall, Value\-at\-risk, Forecast combination, Quantile machine learning; negative transfer, Regime adaptation, Market risk\.
