# NAFLD/MASLD, including cirrhosis: Burden from 1990 to 2040, GBD decomposition, CDC WONDER analysis, and machine\-learning attribution

**Autores:** Luna Zhao; Bingjie Li; Xi Zhang; Jinbao Liu; Jingkun Li; Hanwen Zhang; Kexin Cui; Junhao Ma; Fangyi Zhao; 朱庆峰

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Non\-alcoholic fatty liver disease \(NAFLD\)/metabolic dysfunction\-associated steatotic liver disease \(MASLD\), including cirrhosis, is an increasing global health concern, yet long\-term trends, drivers, and subnational disparities remain inadequately characterized\. We assessed the global burden and U\.S\. subnational variations using machine\-learning\-based attribution\. Using Global Burden of Disease 2021 data, we analyzed global incidence, prevalence, disability\-adjusted life years \(DALYs\), temporal trends, decomposition, inequalities, and Bayesian age\-period\-cohort projections through 2040\. For complementary U\.S\. analyses, we used CDC WONDER data, extreme gradient boosting \(XGBoost\)–SHapley Additive exPlanations attribution, and autoregressive integrated moving average/Prophet forecasting\. Globally, incident cases, prevalence, and DALYs approximately doubled from 1990 to 2021\. Age\-standardized incidence and prevalence increased, whereas DALY rates remained stable \(estimated annual percentage change = 0\.04\), with population growth and aging identified as the main drivers\. Absolute inequality, measured by the slope index of inequality \(SII\), widened from 20 to 36 per 100,000\. High\-burden regions included North Africa and the Middle East, Latin America, and Eastern Europe\. In the U\.S\., the South and Midwest had the highest and fastest\-growing mortality rates\. XGBoost identified diabetes prevalence as the dominant state\-level predictor, showing a nonlinear positive association above approximately 10–12%, whereas higher educational attainment showed an inverse association above approximately 90%\. A sharp inflection in mortality occurred in 2020\. Mortality is projected to increase through 2035, particularly in the Southwest and Appalachia\. The absolute burden of NAFLD/MASLD, including cirrhosis, continues to rise despite stable age\-standardized DALY rates\. Global trends and U\.S\. machine\-learning evidence indicate that prevention should shift toward systemic metabolic risk management, with liver fibrosis screening integrated into routine diabetes and obesity care\.
