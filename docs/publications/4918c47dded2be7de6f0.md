# Cytarabine\-based mobilisation yields higher CD34\+ cell counts than cyclophosphamide regardless of prior daratumumab exposure in multiple myeloma

**Autores:** Wojciech Szlasa; Artur Borkowski; Monika Mordak‐Domagała; Jacek Kwiatkowski; Katarzyna Malesa; Mateusz Sawicki; Jarosław Dybko

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Anti\-CD38\-based quadruplets such as daratumumab\-bortezomib\-lenalidomide\-dexamethasone \(D\-VRd\) or daratumumab\-bortezomib\-thalidomide\-dexamethasone \(D\-VTd\), where lenalidomide is not reimbursed, are the internationally recommended first\-line treatment for transplant\-eligible myeloma\. There is ongoing debate about whether anti\-CD38 exposure impairs stem cell collection\. Most studies compare daratumumab\-containing with non\-daratumumab induction whilst keeping the mobilisation strategy constant\. None have compared two mobilisation agents against each other in both daratumumab\-exposed and daratumumab\-naïve patients\. Methods We reviewed 66 patients \(22 received Ara\-C, 44 received CY\) who were mobilised at a single centre in Poland between 2018 and 2024\. No patients received plerixafor\. Results Patients who were mobilised with Ara\-C collected more CD34\+ cells than those who received CY, with a median of 12\.6 \(IQR 9\.1–15\.9\) versus 7\.7 \(4\.9–12\.1\) ×10⁶/kg \(p=0\.005\), with comparable apheresis sessions\. Previous D\-VTd treatment did not reduce the overall yield \(p=0\.43\)\. The benefit of Ara\-C persisted in the D\-VTd subgroup, despite heavy confounding between induction and mobilisation choice\. Discussion This is the first direct comparison of Ara\-C and CY in the daratumumab era\. Ara\-C\-based mobilisation was associated with higher CD34\+ yields than CY in a plerixafor\-free setting\. The apparent advantage in D\-VTd\-treated patients is exploratory and requires prospective confirmation\.
