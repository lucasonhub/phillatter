# Future proofing the academy: the nexus between stakeholder support and academic staff performance

**Autores:** Oladejo Olufemi Michael; Mutereko Sybert; Khalema Nene Ernest; Ganiyu Olayiwola Idris

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Evidence suggests that academic staff in Nigerian public universities are under\-supported in ways that directly undermine the delivery of Sustainable Development Goal 4\. Previous research has established that stakeholder engagement matters for educational quality, but only a few empirical studies have tested the specific institutional mechanisms through which this engagement produces performance outcomes in resource constrained African higher education systems\. To date, studies have not examined how training and development programmes function as an institutional bridge between training and development plans and academic staff performance in this context\. This study set out to investigate the extent to which stakeholder support drives academic staff performance, specifically highlighting how institutional training and development initiatives serve as the crucial bridge for this impact\. Grounded in Stakeholder Theory and Social Exchange Theory, the study tests four hypotheses using a quantitative design\. Data were collected from 197 academic staff across three public universities in South\-West Nigeria, using stratified random sampling\. Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling was conducted using SmartPLS 4\. Analysis showed that stakeholder support had a significant direct effect on academic staff performance \( β = 0\.423, t = 5\.935, p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Training and development programmes fully mediated the relationship between training and development plans and performance \( β = 0\.210, 95% CI \[0\.090, 0\.353\]\), the study&\#x27;s hypothesised mediation pathway \(H4\)\. The results indicate a persistent gap between institutional planning and programme execution\. A further exploratory finding is the fifth hypothesis \(H5\)\. A feedback path from programme execution back to stakeholder support \(TDProg → KSS, β = 0\.511, p &lt; 0\.001\)\. This finding extends Freeman’s \(1984\) instrumental framework\. It adds nuance to Mitchell et al\.’s \(1997\) salience theory by suggesting that visible programme quality can reinforce stakeholder commitment, rather than being solely a precondition for programme delivery\. This pathway has not previously been documented in the African higher education literature and carries direct implications for institutional governance and SDG 4 policy coordination\. The findings of this study carry implications for stakeholder coordination, institutional policy, and the delivery of SDG 4 in resource constrained higher education systems\. Policy documents and strategic plans are necessary but insufficient\. Decisive progress depends on funded, executed, and monitored training programmes backed by coordinated multi\-stakeholder commitment\.
