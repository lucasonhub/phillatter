# Preclinical evaluation of a safety\-aware multimodal robotic pipeline for human–robot interaction in post\-stroke rehabilitation

**Autores:** Bruna Maria Vittoria Guerra; Roberto Soldi; Francesco Lunghi; Stefania Sozzi; LEO RUSSO; Micaela Schmid; Stefano Ramat

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Post\-stroke rehabilitation increasingly requires technologies capable of supporting intensive, measurable, and adaptable interventions while preserving clinical supervision and patient safety\. This study presents a lightweight, safety\-aware, and modular multimodal robotic pipeline for embodied human–robot interaction in post\-stroke rehabilitation, with a first target application in hemispatial inattention\. Methods The system was implemented on the TIAGo mobile manipulator within a ROS 2\-based architecture and integrates therapist\-mediated supervision, speech\-based and graphical interaction, head\-orientation monitoring, adaptive auditory recall, robot\-mediated and screen\-based task delivery, and structured performance logging\. Dialogue management is based on a deterministic finite\-state machine coupled with a rule\-based Natural Language Understanding pipeline, enabling predictable interaction handling, explicit fallback behavior, and therapist escalation for safety\-relevant events\. A preliminary preclinical evaluation was conducted in 16 healthy participants, with a repeated acquisition after 1 month\. The study assessed operational feasibility, multimodal interaction robustness, head\-orientation monitoring stability, task execution, ASR\-related interaction events, usability, acceptance, and 1\-month reproducibility of workflow execution\. Results All participants completed both acquisitions without adverse events or safety\-relevant events requiring interruption of the experimental workflow\. End\-effector recognition accuracy remained high, attention\-recall events were infrequent, screen\-based visual tasks were successfully administered and logged, and usability and acceptance outcomes were favorable, with low anxiety toward the robotic platform\. Discussion These findings support the feasibility of using a therapist\-supervised robotic platform as an integrated tool for structured interaction, head\-orientation monitoring, rehabilitation\-oriented task delivery, and quantitative logging\. The study presents a preclinical evaluation of system architecture, safety\-aware interaction design, and workflow robustness\. Future work is organized in two stages: a dedicated acceptance and robustness protocol in healthy controls to formally test interaction acceptance, speech\-recognition robustness, and safety\-relevant failure handling, followed by a true rehabilitation protocol in post\-stroke patients with hemispatial inattention, including therapist\-supervised exercise adaptation\.
