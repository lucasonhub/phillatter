# When the model defines the message: methodological considerations in experimental lung warm ischemia

**Autores:** Jitte Jennekens; Daan Imholz; Sue A\. Braithwaite; Linda Heer; Arne Neyrinck

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

We read the article by Premachandra et al\. &quot;Resilience of lung grafts to warm ischemia: assessment by ex vivo perfusion using functional and molecular analysis&quot; with great interest \(1\)\. We thank the authors for their comprehensive investigation of the effects of prolonged 2\-hour warm ischemia \(WI\) on lung graft function and biological responses during ex vivo lung perfusion \(EVLP\)\. The authors reported lower initial compliance and increased pulmonary vascular resistance in the WI group compared to control lungs\. These differences resolved over the duration of the EVLP\. At the end of EVLP, no significant differences in molecular and histological markers of injury were observed, except for concentrations of lactate and PECAM, which were higher in the control lungs\.Premachandra et al\. positioned their study within the broader aim to establish the tolerance limit of lungs to WI in a clinically relevant pig model\. While some limitations have been appropriately addressed by the authors, other aspects warrant further consideration and discussion, especially the clinical relevance of their model\. A relevant donation after circulatory death \(DCD\) model should address the critical aspects of the procedure and timeline because these conditions also influence the effect of WI on the lungs\. In clinical practice, extended periods of WI either occur during a prolonged agonal phase or in uncontrolled DCD settings where lungs are mechanically ventilated after failed resuscitation attempts until the &quot;no touch&quot; period \(2,3\)\. We were surprised that only left lungs 53were allocated to the WI group whereas right lungs were allocated to the control group\. 54Consequently, treatment assignment is confounded by anatomical laterality, lung size, and 55 perfusion and ventilation\. Following the Toronto protocol, the authors adjusted perfusion and 56 ventilation using 24% versus 16% of theoretical cardiac output and tidal volumes of 3\.5 versus 2\.5 57 mL/kg for the right and left lung respectively\. This corresponds to a right:left perfusion distribution 58 of 60%/40%\. We propose the alternative explanation that the 65 greater lung mass and potentially greater amount of perfused tissue of the right lungs in the 66 control group may have contributed to differences in absolute marker release\.
