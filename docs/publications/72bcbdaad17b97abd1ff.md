# LA SALA DE RECURSOS Y EL DESARROLLO DE LA AUTONOMÍA EN ESTUDIANTES CON DISCAPACIDAD INTELECTUAL

**Autores:** Renata de Souza Mesquita Gomes

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This article is a narrative literature review that aims to analyze the contributions of the Multifunctional Resource Room \(MRR\) to the development of autonomy in students with intellectual disabilities within the context of Brazilian inclusive education\. It starts from the premise that school inclusion cannot be reduced to a student&\#x27;s enrollment in mainstream schooling, but rather requires the construction of pedagogical, emotional, and institutional conditions that foster full participation and holistic development\. To this end, the article draws on contributions from developmental psychology, educational neuroscience, and inclusive pedagogy, discussing Specialized Educational Support \(SES\) as a mediating, intentional, and planned space that goes beyond traditional remedial teaching centered on content repetition\. Based on the literature review conducted, it is discussed how the MRR can constitute a differentiated pedagogical environment capable of fostering meaningful learning, self\-management, and student protagonism, through strategies such as concrete and manipulative materials, structured visual routines, assistive technologies, and daily living activities, combined with interdisciplinary work aimed at fostering students&\#x27; functional, social, and emotional autonomy\. The role of neuroplasticity and affectivity in consolidating new learning is also highlighted, as well as the importance of psychological intervention integrated with SES in overcoming emotional and behavioral barriers that frequently hinder the schooling process\. The article concludes that the MRR, when articulated with regular classrooms, supported by inclusive public policies, and backed by multiprofessional collaboration, contributes to effective school inclusion centered on the holistic and autonomous development of students with intellectual disabilities, pointing, finally, to the need for future empirical research to validate these contributions in everyday school practice\.
