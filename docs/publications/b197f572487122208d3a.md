# Task\-aware conformal power envelopes for multi\-horizon spacecraft monitoring

**Autores:** Nan Lu; Lehan Cui; Xinian Zhi

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Reliable spacecraft power monitoring requires uncertainty estimates across forecast horizons and consistency between primitive electrical states and derived power\. We present a task\-aware conformal framework that selects joint residual geometry according to downstream power\-envelope efficiency\. A residual iTransformer produced point centres for five primitive electrical states at 1, 5, and 15 min\. Candidate geometries were fitted, selected, and calibrated on chronologically separated complete\-orbit blocks, and the final evaluation comprised 25,278 forecast origins from the chronologically subsequent July 2023 telemetry\. Across three random\-seed runs, the task\-aware selector chose a joint box, whereas a residual\-volume control selected the sample\-covariance ellipsoid\. Orbit\-balanced primitive\-state joint coverage was 0\.892 \(95% complete\-orbit bootstrap confidence interval \[CI\], 0\.883\-0\.899\) at the 90% nominal level and 0\.947 \(95% CI, 0\.943\-0\.952\) at the 95% nominal level\. At 90% and 95%, simultaneous coverage of both power channels across all horizons was 0\.913 and 0\.957\. Relative to residual\-volume selection, the task\-aware method reduced normalized power interval score by 34\.3% and 30\.3%; the paired absolute reductions were 1\.522 \(95% CI, 1\.505\-1\.540\) and 1\.552 \(95% CI, 1\.534\-1\.570\)\. Power envelopes were computed from synchronized primitive current and bus\-voltage sets\. For the selected non\-negative box, zero algebraic gap and full interval\-image containment follow by construction; the empirical analyses quantify coverage and sharpness\. The results demonstrate task\-aware geometry selection for BUPT\-1 multi\-horizon power monitoring\.
