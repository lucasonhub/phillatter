# Postharvest drying of a farmer\-maintained roselle population in Chiapas, Mexico: bioactive compounds and antioxidant capacity

**Autores:** Teresa Avelina Trujillo\-Robles; Cristian Jiménez‐Martínez; Xariss M\. Sánchez Chino; Jorge Martínez\-Herrera; Noé Samuel León\-Martínez; Jorge Castellanos‐Albores; Zendy Evelyn Olivo\-Vidal

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accessible postharvest practices can influence the final chemical attributes of locally maintained crops and may support their continued use in smallholder systems\. This study compared three drying approaches applied to a farmer\-maintained roselle \(Hibiscus sabdariffa L\.\) population cultivated in Villaflores, Chiapas, Mexico; a commercial Sudan\-type sample was included only as an external descriptive reference\. Calyces from 15 local plants were pooled and randomly allocated to oven drying at 40 °C for 33 h to constant mass \(JASI\), direct sun drying on three separate wooden boards for 5 days \(JAST\), or drying on three separate raised beds covered with 50% shade netting for 5 days \(JASCE\)\. Total phenolic content \(TPC\), total monomeric anthocyanins \(TMA\), and four antioxidant responses were measured in aqueous extracts prepared at 98 °C for 20 min\. JASCE had the highest numerical TPC and TMA means among local treatments\. TPC did not differ significantly among local treatments, whereas TMA showed an overall treatment effect; Games–Howell comparison identified a difference between JASCE and JASI, while JAST was intermediate and did not differ significantly from either\. No significant differences were detected for DPPH, ABTS, ferric reducing, or cupric reducing responses\. Because the three JASI portions were dried simultaneously in one oven chamber, comparisons involving JASI were interpreted cautiously\. Shade\-covered raised\-bed drying warrants further evaluation as a low\-energy postharvest option for farmer\-maintained roselle\.
