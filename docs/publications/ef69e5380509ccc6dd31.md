# Urban Readability and Accessibility Analysis for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities: The Case of Üsküdar \(Istanbul\)

**Autores:** Hüseyin Melih Özdemir

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accessibility in urban spaces is closely related not only to physical distances but also to individuals&\#x27; processes of perceiving space and finding their way\. This study aims to examine the effect of the legibility characteristics of the urban road network on accessibility to urban services from the perspective of individuals with mental disabilities\. Within the scope of the study, urban legibility was evaluated using spatial indicators, including the route straightness index, angle change index, intersection complexity index, facade continuity index, and road legibility index\. Based on these indicators, the cognitive path cost, which reflects the perceptual difficulties individuals with intellectual disabilities may encounter in the orientation process within the urban road network, was calculated\. The analyses were performed using ArcGIS software in a geographic information system \(GIS\) environment\. The resulting cognitive path cost surface was used to evaluate accessibility to special education and rehabilitation centers, healthcare facilities, and urban parks and green spaces\. In this context, both physical distance\-based accessibility and cognitive path\-cost\-based perceptual accessibility analyses were conducted for the relevant service areas and compared\. The findings show that, despite the short physical distance in some areas, cognitive accessibility remains limited due to the road network&\#x27;s morphological structure and difficulties with finding directions\. In contrast, it was determined that more readable and easier\-to\-navigate routes can increase accessibility despite longer physical distances on certain axes\. Consequently, the study reveals that evaluating accessibility to urban services solely based on distance criteria is insufficient, and that urban morphology and road network readability must also be considered in accessibility analyses\.
