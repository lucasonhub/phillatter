# Window shading and materiality for psychophysiological stress in hospital waiting areas: a Spatial Frequency perspective through amplitude spectrum slopes in Virtual Reality

**Autores:** Mirna Zordan; Zixian Lei; Junxuan Pei

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Indoor Environmental Quality \(IEQ\) can influence psychophysiological stress in hospital waiting areas; however, specific visual features warrant further investigation\. We introduce amplitude spectrum slope \(− α \) as an image\-based metric that characterizes Spatial Frequency in scene images and participants’ recorded views\. We conducted a VR experiment where 47 healthy young adults viewed conditions combining open/closed blinds and biophilic/artificial materiality\. Outcomes included STAI\-S, EEG\-derived FAA, HRV\-derived RMSSD, and preference ratings\. Open blinds and biophilic materiality showed smaller STAI\-S changes \(b = −0\.301, −0\.444\) and larger RMSSD changes \(b = 2\.877, 2\.665 ms\)\. In separate mixed models adjusted for age, gender, and STAI\-T, each 0\.1 decrease in personal α towards 1 \(range: 1–2\) was associated with lower STAI\-S \(−0\.361\), and higher RMSSD \(\+2\.296 ms\) and FAA \(\+0\.092\)\. Our findings support open blinds and biophilic materiality in fostering reduced stress in healthcare environments, while α provided a spectral measure for visual IEQ assessment\.
