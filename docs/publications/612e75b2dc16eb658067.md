# Legal Evidence–Authority Dissociation in juror judgment: a multistudy reanalysis of memory contamination and AI advice

**Autores:** Xiangran Xie

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Legal decision makers may remember information without using it to update a legal judgment, and may respond differently to the same recommendation depending on its attributed source\. LEAD separates these processes into memory\-entry, judgment\-incorporation, and authority\-deference gates\. Methods We reanalyzed three public, de\-identified datasets: one jury\-deliberation misinformation study and two preregistered judge\-versus\-artificial\-intelligence sentencing studies\. We compared gate\-specific estimates while retaining study\-specific outcomes and raw units\. Results In Study 1, pro\-prosecution misinformation crossed the memory\-entry gate more clearly than the judgment\-incorporation gate\. In Studies 2 and 3, mitigating case facts produced larger judgment\-incorporation effects than authority\-source assignment\. The authority\-source effect was equivalent to zero within a prespecified small\-effect bound in Study 2 \(source Experiment 1\), but remained inconclusive in Study 3 \(source Experiment 2\)\. Discussion LEAD identifies which process changed and supports cross\-gate magnitude comparisons without treating unlike outcomes as identical\. This secondary analysis does not estimate behavior in real courts, but it provides a transparent framework for forensic and legal psychology\.
