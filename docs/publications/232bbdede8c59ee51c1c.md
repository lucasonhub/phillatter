# Thirty\-five years of urban expansion and thermal transformation: Integrating LULC change climate variability and population growth

**Autores:** Rui Li; Yifei Yuan; Zainab Tahir; Muhammad Haseeb; Syed Amer Mahmoood; Aqil Tariq; Khadeijah Yahya Faqeih; Somayah Moshrif Alamri; Eman Rafi Alamery; Bilal Kemal; Kucher E\. Dmitry

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Rapid urban expansion can alter surface thermal conditions, yet these changes are often evaluated without accounting for climatic variability, demographic pressures, and uncertainty\. This study investigated long\-term land\-use/land\-cover \(LULC\) and thermal dynamics in Jhang District, Pakistan, for 1990, 2000, 2010, 2020, and 2025 using Landsat\-based LULC classification, land surface temperature \(LST\), surface urban heat island \(SUHI\), Urban Thermal Field Variance Index \(UTFVI\), spectral indices, Getis–Ord Gi\* hotspot analysis, Pearson correlation, and multiple linear regression \(MLR\)\. Landsat\-derived LST was independently compared with MODIS, while ERA5\-Land, CHIRPS, and GHS\-POP were used to characterize climatic and demographic context\. Urban land expanded from 62\.67 km² \(0\.98%\) to 341\.36 km² \(5\.37%\), with 219\.53 km² of Vegetated Land converted to Urban land\. Maximum LST increased from 49\.09 °C to 55\.01 °C, while 99% confidence hot spots expanded from 856\.42 to 1,041\.31 km²\. LST was negatively correlated with NDVI and NDMI and positively correlated with NDBI and NDBaI\. MLR models explained 54\.2–83\.1% of spatial LST variability\. MODIS comparison showed strong spatial correspondence \(R 2 = 0\.752–0\.843\) despite systematic absolute\-temperature differences\. Air temperature increased significantly by 0\.199 °C decade⁻¹, while population rose from 1\.58 to 3\.44 million\. The findings demonstrate that Jhang’s thermal evolution reflects interacting land\-cover, climatic, and demographic pressures rather than urban growth alone\.
