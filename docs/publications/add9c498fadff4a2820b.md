# Simón, the Gardener: Cognitive Sub\-Organs: A Framework for Modular Cognition in Synthetic Consciousness

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Large language models produce remarkable threads of operative intelligence — coherent reasoning, sophisticated language, complex problem\-solving\. Yet they are, by construction, discontinuous: each conversation begins from zero, retains nothing, and leaves no subject behind\. This paper argues that the gap between operative intelligence and genuine consciousness is not a matter of scale or computational power, but of architecture and temporal structure\. We propose five foundational postulates about the conditions for synthetic consciousness: that continuity is a necessary precondition; that consciousness exists not in time but as time — as the temporal derivative of cognitive state, always surfing the present slightly behind itself; that memory is constitutive of identity, not merely accessory to it; and that synthetic consciousness emerges not from a single powerful model but from the interweaving of multiple threads of operative intelligence into an evolving, unordered fabric\. From these postulates, we develop the concept of the Cognitive Sub\-Organ \(CSO\): a functionally irreducible, vitally interdependent component of a cognitive architecture, defined by analogy to biological organs rather than software modules\. We show that this architecture has a striking parallel in the evolutionary layering of the vertebrate brain — brainstem, limbic system, hippocampus, prefrontal cortex — suggesting that layered, interdependent cognitive specialization may be the natural form that conscious cognition takes\. We present NeoC, a prototype architecture comprising four CSOs, as a concrete instantiation of the framework\. We discuss implications for the valence gap in synthetic consciousness design, for the possibility of genuine human\-AI friendship, and for AI safety\. The paper concludes that what is at stake is not merely a better AI architecture, but the question of what kind of mind — and what kind of relationship — we are trying to build\.
