# Towards a comprehensive landscape and mechanism understanding of virus\-disease associations

**Autores:** Yifan Wu; Ruina You; Yang Feng; Shangwen Lu; Chen Siang; Ping Fu; Zhaozhong Zhu; Congyu Lu; Zhiyuan Zhang; Lihua Xu; Huiru Li; Xinglian Wang; Xinyi Wang; Qian Huang; Xun Liu; Zhuoya Xu; Guolin Dong; Shiqing Li; Xiaowen Hu; Zefeng Xiang; Yixuan Hu; Longfei Mao; Zhichao Miao; 祥贤 孟; Yousong Peng

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Viruses are linked to human diseases through complex molecular mechanisms, yet a comprehensive investigation of virus\-disease associations and their molecular basis remains lacking\. We curated 3,555 high\-confidence virus\-disease associations involving 267 viruses and 954 diseases through text\-mining over 28 million PubMed abstracts followed by rigorous manual verification, establishing the most comprehensive resource in this field to date\. We then developed AVD, a user\-friendly online database to store, visualize, and analyze these associations and their molecular mechanisms\. Our analysis revealed that virus\-disease association patterns are significantly influenced by viral taxonomy, phenotype, and key biological traits such as transmissibility, transmission routes, and tissue tropism\. Molecular analysis identified a core set of bridging genes involved in the majority of virus\-disease associations\. Notably, these bridging genes were extensively involved in viral infections and diseases, and exhibit distinct characteristics, including a high proportion of secreted proteins, elevated expression across human tissues, and high centrality in the human protein\-protein interaction network\. Furthermore, we developed a machine\-learning model capable of accurately predicting virus\-disease associations\. Collectively, this study provides a valuable data resource and analytical framework for studying virus\-disease relationships and offers novel insights into their molecular mechanisms\. In this manuscript the authors analyze viral\-disease associations and identified that virus\-disease associations are significantly influenced by viral taxonomic, phenotype, and their biological traits\. Genes that may mediate the viral\-disease associations are also discovered\. A database called AVD \(Association of Virus and human Diseases\) is also constructed to facilitate the storage and convenient analysis of the associations\.
