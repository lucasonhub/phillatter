# Global burden of type 2 diabetes attributable to SARS\-CoV\-2 infection

**Autores:** Jingjing Li; Guangming Liu; Jianzhou Yang; Haimiao Ma; Xiuli Wang; Ziying Jiang; Yuanhao Liang; Xiaofeng He

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective We aimed to quantify the association between severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 \(SARS\-CoV\-2\) infection and the risk of incident diabetes\. We also aimed to estimate the global burden of incident type 2 diabetes \(T2D\) attributable to SARS\-CoV\-2 infection\. Methods We conducted a systematic review and meta\-analysis of cohort studies identified through PubMed, Embase, Web of Science and CENTRAL from inception to 29 January 2026\. Adjusted relative risks \(RRs\) for incident T2D and type 1 diabetes \(T1D\) following SARS\-CoV\-2 infection were pooled using random\-effects models\. Population attributable fractions \(PAFs\) and attributable T2D cases were estimated by integrating pooled risk estimates with Global Burden of Disease \(GBD\) 2023 data on COVID\-19 prevalence and T2D incidence\. Results Fifteen cohort studies involving approximately 48\.9 million participants were included\. SARS\-CoV\-2 infection was associated with an increased risk of incident T2D \(13 studies; pooled RR, 1\.25 \[95% CI, 1\.16–1\.34\]\), although substantial heterogeneity was observed and the 95% prediction interval \(PI\) included the null \(0\.94–1\.66\)\. In subgroup analysis, the association was confined to mixed\-variant studies \(RR, 1\.29 \[95% CI, 1\.23–1\.36\]; 95% PI, 1\.09–1\.54\), whereas studies evaluating a single variant \(Delta or Omicron\) showed no excess risk \(RR, 1\.00 \[95% CI, 0\.97–1\.03\]\)\. The association with incident T1D did not reach conventional statistical significance \(10 studies; pooled RR, 1\.13 \[95% CI, 0\.99–1\.30\]\)\. Globally, 24\.53 million incident T2D cases occurred in 2023\. The PAF attributable to COVID\-19 of 1\.12% \(95% CI 0\.90, 1\.35\), corresponding to 274,889 \(95% CI 220,360, 331,698\) incident T2D cases attributable to SARS\-CoV\-2 infection\. The largest attributable burdens were observed in India, the USA, and China\. Conclusions SARS\-CoV\-2 infection was associated with a modest increase in pooled T2D risk\. This association was primarily observed in mixed\-variant studies, for which the 95% PI excluded the null\. However, the overall pooled estimated was accompanied by substantial between\-study heterogeneity, and its 95% PI crossing the null, indicating uncertainty in the association across populations and settings\. At the population level, this excess risk translated into an estimated 275,000 incident T2D cases attributable to COVID\-19 worldwide in 2023, highlighting the need for ongoing metabolic surveillance following infection\. Systematic Review Registration https://www\.crd\.york\.ac\.uk/PROSPERO/view/CRD420261307969 , identifier CRD420261307969\.
