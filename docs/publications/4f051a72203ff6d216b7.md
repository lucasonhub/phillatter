# Fostering sustainable engagement with intangible cultural heritage: a flow theory perspective on Chaohua Wind and Percussion Music

**Autores:** Wei Li; Ming Ma; Li Song

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study integrates Flow Theory and Embodied Cognition to examine the transmission of Intangible Cultural Heritage \(ICH\), addressing the psychological distance between contemporary audiences and traditional cultural practices\. A randomized experiment \( N = 150\) compared text\-image, audiovisual, and interactive instrumental\-performance conditions\. The interactive condition produced the highest Flow Experience and Experience Satisfaction, followed by the audiovisual and text\-image conditions; Welch tests and Games\-Howell comparisons confirmed this ordered pattern under unequal variances\. Flow Experience and Experience Satisfaction were strongly associated in the pooled sample, but common\-method and conceptual\-overlap diagnostics indicated that this association contained substantial between\-condition structure alongside residual within\-condition covariation and was not uniform across conditions\. For the two contrasts involving the Text\-Image condition, significant treatment\-by\-Flow interactions required interaction\-aware natural\-effect decompositions: the total natural indirect effects excluded zero, whereas the pure natural indirect effects did not\. The Audiovisual\-versus\-Interactive contrast retained a significant ordinary indirect effect\. These results are consistent with Flow Experience functioning as a condition\-dependent proximal psychological pathway rather than a uniform or causally identified mechanism\. These findings inform the design of brief heritage experiences\. Their implications for sustained participation require longitudinal and field\-based evaluation\.
