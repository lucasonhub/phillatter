# Large language model assistance for report\-based carotid plaque AHA classification: a multi\-reader study

**Autores:** Mengfei Wang; Yixiao Tang; Yulu Yang; Yifan Tu; Zhongzheng Cao; Runjianya Ling; Kaiyue Sun; Jian Ma; Zhicheng Zhang; Ruipeng Zhang; Xiaoer Wei; Dan Wang; Y S Li

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OBJECTIVES: To evaluate whether large language model assistance improves radiologists&\#x27; agreement with a text\-based reference standard in inferring the modified American Heart Association carotid plaque classification from free\-text MRI reports, and whether a lightweight model can perform well under criteria\-guided prompting\. MATERIALS AND METHODS: This retrospective study included 433 patients from three institutions\. In a report\-based reader study, four radiologists classified MRI reports with and without large language model assistance\. In standalone evaluation, a rule\-based baseline and 10 models were compared under naive and criteria\-guided prompting\. The reference standard was expert consensus based on the report text\. RESULTS: Among 433 patients \(mean age, 65\.9 years ± 13\.4; 248 males\), AI assistance improved six\-class agreement with the text\-based reference standard\. At the vessel level, junior radiologists improved from 61\.75% to 83\.12% \(Qwen3\-8B\) and 87\.75% \(DeepSeek\-R1\); senior radiologists from 77\.00% to 87\.50% and 92\.25%, respectively \(all Holm\-corrected p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Patient\-level agreement increased from 41\.0% to 78\.8% \(junior\) and from 59\.8% to 87\.8% \(senior\) with DeepSeek\-R1\. Agreement increased \(quadratic\-weighted κ: 0\.231 without AI vs 0\.775 with DeepSeek\-R1\)\. In standalone evaluation, criteria\-guided prompting improved Qwen3\-8B agreement from 38\.91% to 81\.52% \(95% CI: 78\.87\-84\.06%\), remaining 5\.89 percentage points below DeepSeek\-R1 \(87\.41%; 95% CI: 85\.10\-89\.61%\)\. CONCLUSION: In this report\-based classification setting, large language model assistance improved radiologists&\#x27; agreement with the text\-based reference standard, with larger gains in junior radiologists\. When prompts explicitly encoded AHA criteria, an 8B local candidate model performed well, supporting its potential use as a standardization aid for text\-based workflows\. KEY POINTS: Question Report\-based modified AHA classification of carotid plaques is experience\-dependent and difficult to standardize, as mapping multi\-sequence imaging findings to classification conclusions imposes substantial cognitive load\. Findings Large language model assistance improved agreement with a text\-based reference standard and raised inter\-reader consistency \(weighted κ 0\.231\-0\.775\), narrowing the junior\-senior gap\. Critical relevance statement Embedding explicit AHA criteria in the prompt let an 8B model approach 671B\-model agreement \(38\.91\-81\.52%\), supporting standardized text\-based workflows without fine\-tuning\.
