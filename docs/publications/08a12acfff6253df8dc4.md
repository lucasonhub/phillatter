# Interpretable and Trustworthy AI for Medical Imaging Analysis Beyond Post Hoc Justifications

**Autores:** Longxi Zhou

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Artificial intelligence \(AI\) has demonstrated remarkable potential in medical imag\-ing analysis, but its lack of interpretability and trust remains a central challenge to clini\-cal adoption\. This thesis explores a series of projects that aim to make medical AI in\-terpretable and trustworthy by design rather than through post hoc justifications, focus\-ing on both the reasoning process of the models and their alignment with pathology\. We begin with a study on breast cancer segmentation using dynamic contrast\-enhanced MRI \(DCE\-MRI\), where we leverage the heart as an anatomical anchor\. The heart’s structural and signal stability across patients makes it a reliable internal refer\-ence\. By analyzing correlations between the heart and nearby tissues, we can estimate patient\-level certainty by predicting false positives and false negatives—particularly those caused by image degradations and dataset bias\. This approach transforms ana\-tomical consistency into a built\-in verification tool, illustrating how region\-specific sta\-bility can serve as a source of model certainty and error prediction\. The next project addresses machine\-agnostic segmentation of COVID\-19 lesions, as dataset bias is a major concern for model trustworthiness\. Here, we collected multi\-national data \(from China and Saudi Arabia\) spanning five different hospitals and sev\-en scanning protocols\. We then developed a simulation algorithm based on modeled pathological processes and scanning settings, and a 2\.5D model that minimizes the ef\-fect of dataset bias\. This design improves model trust, as it is highly robust against var\-iations caused by patient populations and scanning conditions\. Building on this, we developed an AI pipeline to detect sub\-visual lesions in COVID\-19 survivors with long\-term respiratory symptoms\. These lesions, invisible to radiologists, were uncovered through a modular, interpretable workflow\. Each module extracted features with clear pathological meaning, and the final predictions were cross\-validated against both clinical outcomes and spatial lung distribution patterns\. This de\-sign convinced physicians that the findings were trustworthy—even though they could not directly observe the features themselves\. Together, these projects outline a unified path toward interpretable and trustworthy AI in medical imaging through embedding verifiability and medical logic into the mod\-el’s structure, training, and workflow design\.
