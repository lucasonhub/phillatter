# Liability at the Authority Boundary: A Framework for Allocating Responsibility Across Distributed and Agentic AI Systems

**Autores:** Deusdedit Ruhangariyo

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial intelligence is increasingly deployed through systems in which authority, execution, oversight, and consequence no longer belong to the same actor\. A model may be developed by one organization, integrated by another, configured by an enterprise, instructed by a human, executed through an AI agent, routed through external tools, and experienced by a person who had no role in any of those decisions\. The resulting liability problem is not adequately described by asking whether a human remained &quot;in the loop&quot; or whether an audit trail exists\. This working paper develops the GAILC Authority Boundary framework\. It defines the Authority Boundary as the point at which consequential decision rights, discretion, intervention responsibility, or consequence\-bearing responsibility passes from one actor, system, organization, or layer to another, and analyses each boundary through five elements: Authority, Transfer, Reliance, Intervention, and Consequence\. The framework identifies two difficult liability cases that conventional control models often leave unresolved\. Intended\-Execution Liability concerns harm produced when a system remains within its authority, follows policy, uses permitted tools, and operates substantially as designed\. No\-Action Liability concerns harm produced or worsened where an actor bears an identifiable duty or assigned responsibility to act, possesses the authority, information, timing, and practical capacity to do so, yet no consequential intervention occurs\. The paper introduces the Authority Envelope, the distinction between authorization and judgment, the Silence\-as\-Authority Problem, the GAILC Allocation Test, Consequence Ownership, Allocation Completeness and Allocation Adequacy, Residual Consequence, and Orphaned Harm\. It proposes a standardized Authority Boundary Record \(ABR\) designed to support verification, contestability, and independent adjudication, together with a boundary failure taxonomy, an irreversibility threshold, an inter\-organizational allocation procedure, and a pre\-deployment test\. Its central proposition is that traceability is evidence, not allocation: an authority and execution chain may be fully reconstructable while accountability and consequence allocation remain incomplete\. The framework is analytical and does not determine legal liability\. It is positioned in relation to agency law, duties concerning failures to act, design\-defect analysis, and scholarship on the problem of many hands, responsibility gaps, and moral crumple zones\. This paper is the third in the GAILC Working Paper series, following WP001 \(The Human Presence Economy\) and WP002 \(The Intervention Test\)\.
