# In vivo monitoring of polymorphonuclear myeloid\-derived suppressor cells in experimental rheumatoid arthritis

**Autores:** Johannes Schwenck; Kerstin Fuchs; mohamed ali jarboui; Sophia Geiger; Marc Neumann; Hasan H\. Öz; Melanie Carevic; Sabrina Hoffmann; Dominik Hartl; Christoph M\. Griessinger; Irene González\-Menéndez; Leticia Quintanilla‐Martínez; Jörg C\. Henes; Gerald Reischl; Andreas Maurer; Bernd Jürgen Pichler; Manfred Kneilling

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Myeloid\-derived suppressor cells \(MDSCs\) regulate immune responses, but their role in autoimmune diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis \(RA\) remains incompletely understood\. Here, we applied non\-invasive positron emission tomography \(PET\) imaging to investigate the migration dynamics of polymorphonuclear \(PMN\)\-MDSCs in experimental RA\. Materials and methods RA was induced in female BALB/c mice via K/BxN serum\. Immune infiltrates in arthritic ankles were characterized by flow cytometry and liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry \(LC–MS\)\-based proteomics\. We radiolabeled bone marrow\-derived \(BM\-PMN\-MDSCs\) and RA ankle\-derived PMN\-MDSCs \(RA\-PMN\-MDSCs\) intracellularly with \[ 64 Cu\]Cu\-PTSM or Ly6G\-specific \[ 64 Cu\]Cu\-NOTA\-1A8\. Radiolabeled PMN\-MDSCs were adoptively transferred into RA mice and tracked longitudinally by PET/MRI\. In a second cohort, \[ 64 Cu\]Cu\-NOTA\-1A8 was injected directly for in vivo imaging of Ly6G \+ myeloid cells over 48 h\. Results RA progression was associated with a marked increase in the number of CD11b \+ Ly6C med Ly6G \+ PMN\-MDSCs in arthritic ankles, whereas the number of CD11b \+ Ly6C high Ly6G − monocytic\-MDSCs remained unchanged\. PET/MRI demonstrated significantly enhanced homing of RA\-PMN\-MDSCs to inflamed ankles compared with that of BM\-PMN\-MDSCs, indicating disease\-associated functional adaptation\. Direct in vivo targeting of MDSCs with \[ 64 Cu\]Cu\-1A8 revealed strongly increased uptake in arthritic ankles at 1 h, 24 h, and 48 h p\.i\. , which was significantly reduced by Ly6G blockade, confirming tracer specificity\. Proteomic analysis revealed enrichment of extracellular matrix organization and integrin signaling pathways in RA\-PMN\-MDSCs, suggesting that a distinct inflammatory signature underlies their migratory behavior\. Conclusion PET/MRI enables non\-invasive monitoring of PMN\-MDSC migration during experimental RA and provides novel insights into the role of these cells in autoimmune inflammation, supporting the development of PMN\-MDSCs as biomarkers and therapeutic targets in autoimmune diseases\.
