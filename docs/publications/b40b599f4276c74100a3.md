# Acceptable Uses of GenAI: Post\-Secondary Students’ Judgements

**Autores:** Rahul Kumar; Robert McGray

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Generative artificial intelligence \(GenAI\) is reshaping academic practice in higher education, yet evidence on how students evaluate its acceptable uses remains limited\. This study surveyed 988 post\-secondary students across 39 countries, recruited through the Prolific online research platform, on the acceptability of GenAI use across 22 academic tasks using a binary Yes/No format\. Using an interpretive framework grounded in normative boundary\-setting, academic integrity values, and academic socialization, the analysis reveals differentiated acceptability patterns: Students endorse GenAI for instrumental and research\-support tasks while maintaining normative boundaries around evaluative and judgement\-intensive activities\. Gender differences are minimal; level of study is the primary source of variation, with graduate students reporting higher acceptability for research\- and professionally oriented uses\. Respondents residing in Canada \(n = 58\) reported significantly lower acceptance of four text\-focused tasks, including academic composition and summarization, than the non\-Canadian cohort\. Findings have implications for institutional policy, pedagogy, and academic integrity frameworks\.
