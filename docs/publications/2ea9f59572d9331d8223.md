# Alhosseini Almodarresieh, Seyed Alireza: PHILOSOPHY YOU CAN FORK :ONTURGY The Philosophical Kernel

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Much of what a person can practically do is decided not by law or intention but by architecture: the platforms, protocols, institutions, and systems that make some actions cheap, others costly, and others unreachable\. Those architectures are built by people\. Those people were shaped by teachers, traditions, books, and, increasingly, by AI systems\. Neither layer is usually declared\. Both encode philosophy\. Onturgy is a philosophical program with two levels\. The first level examines how architectures construct, constrain, distribute, and transform practical possibility, and whether those who live inside them can revise them\. The second level examines the architect: the lineage and cognitive environment that shape what a builder perceives as worth building, now including AI systems that may act as mentors, critics, and collaborators\. The program takes a position it calls bounded constructivism\. Architectures can change which possibilities are reachable and at what cost\. They cannot legislate reality\. Bodies, physics, other people, institutions, and unintended consequences resist\. The paper contributes: 1\. a measurable model of practical possibility, a costed and access\-weighted graph, and of construction’s footprint on it; 2\. a kernel of six dimensions derived from an explicit criterion \(revisability by the affected\), with its exclusions stated; 3\. an audit that detects the gap between a system’s declared and operational philosophy, applied at both levels, including to human–AI cognitive systems; 4\. ONTacture, a method with criteria for when a difference between theories is architecturally real, how failures must be attributed, and how forks are compared; 5\. an operational account of lineage and AI provenance, including a lineage log and a test for when an AI has come to “own” a builder’s possibility space; 6\. kill criteria: observable conditions under which the program’s own claims fail\. The contribution is methodological\. It is stated modestly and is meant to be tested\. Do not build a philosophy of the future\. Build the kernel from which philosophies of the future can be built, and keep it forkable\.
