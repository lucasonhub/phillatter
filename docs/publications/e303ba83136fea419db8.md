# Artificial Intelligence for Sustainable Development Goals \(SDGs\): Building an Inclusive Future

**Autores:** Daisy Dohare

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** book

## Resumo

The foundational quantitative case for artificial intelligence&\#x27;s relevance to sustainable development rests on a single, widely cited finding: a systematic mapping of artificial intelligence&\#x27;s potential effects on all 169 Sustainable Development Goal targets found that the technology could act as an enabler for 134 targets, seventy\-nine per cent, while simultaneously inhibiting 59 targets, thirty\-five per cent, with the two sets overlapping substantially\. This chapter takes that dual finding as its point of departure and argues that a third dimension, distinct from the enabling\-versus\-inhibiting distinction the foundational literature already captures, is required to evaluate whether artificial intelligence is building an inclusive future or merely a more efficient unequal one: whether its benefits reach across social, economic, and geographic strata, or concentrate among populations and regions already comparatively well served\. The chapter develops this as an Accelerator, Inhibitor, and Equaliser framework and applies it to India&\#x27;s rapidly maturing artificial\-intelligence\-for\-development landscape, spanning the Digital Agriculture Mission&\#x27;s farmer\-identity and crop\-survey infrastructure, the Unified Lending Interface and Account Aggregator framework&\#x27;s expansion of formal credit access, and the National Tuberculosis Elimination Programme&\#x27;s deployment of artificial\-intelligence\-assisted chest radiography\. Drawing on recent \(2024\-2026\) India\-specific evidence, including the SDG India Index&\#x27;s composite score improvement from 57 in 2018 to 71 in 2023\-24 alongside a persistent twenty\-two\-point gap between the best\- and worst\-performing states, and on the countervailing evidence of artificial intelligence&\#x27;s own expanding energy footprint, projected by the International Energy Agency to approach 945 terawatt\-hours of data\-centre electricity demand by 2030, the chapter finds an accelerator dimension considerably more developed than either the inhibitor dimension&\#x27;s environmental accounting or the equaliser dimension&\#x27;s distributional evidence\. It concludes that India&\#x27;s SDG\-linked artificial\-intelligence programming has, to date, prioritised the accelerator dimension&\#x27;s demonstrated deployment successes over the equaliser dimension&\#x27;s harder question of whether those successes are narrowing or merely relocating the country&\#x27;s persistent development disparities\.
