# Beyond the Bandwagon: Fear of Missing Out \(FOMO\) as an Affective State in Executives’ Adoption of Emerging Technologies

**Autores:** Alex Mari; Andreina Mandelli; René Algesheimer

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose – Fear of missing out \(FOMO\) is increasingly invoked to explain corporate rushes toward emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence \(AI\), yet it has neither been distinguished from bandwagon and imitation explanations of adoption nor traced from the individual executive to the organizational decision\. This study examines whose loss executives anticipate when they experience FOMO, how FOMO differs from bandwagon pressure, and through which pathways it becomes an organizational adoption decision\.Design/methodology/approach – An inductive study of the adoption of AI\-based voice assistants, such as Amazon Alexa, by global consumer packaged goods firms, drawing on 63 in\-depth interviews \(42 executives from 19 firms and 21 voice\-technology suppliers\) and 115 archival documents\. Explicit coding rules separate FOMO from bandwagon pressure and imitation heuristics\.Findings – FOMO is an anticipatory affective state rather than a decision rule\. It manifests with three referents, namely the executive \(employee\), the team and the firm, which differ in target, relevance and response\. Firm\-referent FOMO produces convergence with peers, whereas employee\- and team\-referent FOMO produce divergence, a rush to be first\. FOMO can arise before any peer has adopted and reaches the organizational decision through four pathways \(downward cascade, upward issue selling, lateral rivalry and external amplification\), typically resulting in a bounded test\-and\-learn commitment\.Originality/value – The study positions FOMO as the affective micro\-foundation of bandwagon behavior, provides a referent\-based typology, and identifies how individual FOMO aggregates into organizational decisions, with practical guidance for bounding FOMO\-driven initiatives\.
