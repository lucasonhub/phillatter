# Myshko, Andrii: METAMONISM IN THE LIGHT OF 20TH\-CENTURY PHILOSOPHY: From the Fragmentation of Thought to the Processual Invariant

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This work offers an introductory overview of Metamonism \(Processual Ontodynamics\) through the lens of key 20th\-century philosophical schools\. The text is structured in three thematic blocks: \(I\) criteria of reality, language, and meaning \(pragmatism, logical positivism, analytic philosophy\); \(II\) subject, perception, and understanding \(phenomenology, existentialism, hermeneutics\); \(III\) social, linguistic, and power structures \(critical theory, structuralism, poststructuralism, neo\-Marxism\)\. It is shown that 20th\-century philosophical thought, despite its diversity, moved toward the rejection of static substance in favor of processuality, difference, and action\. Metamonism offers a unified axiomatic foundation in which these intuitions acquire a rigorous deductive form\. The sole invariant of the system is the Prohibition of Indifference: absolute identity \(I, I\) is excluded from the domain of the actualization operator Dom\(P\) = \{\(D, I\) \| D ≠ I\}\. From this invariant, the entire subsequent dynamics of reality is deduced — from the emergence of space and time to consciousness and complex structures\. The motto of the work — &quot;Ex uno omnia&quot; \(&quot;From one, everything&quot;\) — symbolizes the overcoming of the fragmentation of philosophical knowledge\. The work contains a methodological note and references to published preprints of the series &quot;Metamonism \(Ex uno omnia\)\.&quot; \(direct link \)
