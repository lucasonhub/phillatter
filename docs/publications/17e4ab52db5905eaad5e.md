# Digital Capacity of Labour Markets: Regional Assessment

**Autores:** Diana SHKUROPADSKA; Larysa Lebedeva; Anzhelika Gerasymenko; Kateryna NIKOLAIETS; Iryna Shtunder

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract: This study examines the digital capacity of labour markets amid the global digital transformation and increasing economic volatility\. The aim of the article is to assess the digital capacity of labour markets as one of sustainability criteria across the world&\#x27;s macro\-regions and to identify the structural, institutional, and human capital factors that shape regional disparities of the world\. Digital capacity is conceptualized as a multidimensional construct reflecting economies&\#x27; ability to generate digital jobs, develop digital skills, integrate advanced technologies, and adapt to structural change\. Digital labour market capacity depends upon the ability of an economy to generate digital jobs, provide digital skills, integrate into the digital economy and adapt to technological change\. The methodology is designed to assess the ability of labour markets to adapt in the context of the digital transformation of the economy, as well as to determine their potential for the implementation of innovative technologies and the development of digital skills\. The study proposes a methodological framework based on the Digital Labour Capacity Index \(DLCI\), which combines two components: Digital Workforce &amp; Adoption Capacity and Innovation, Research &amp; Advanced Technologies Capacity\. The methodology defines a list of key indicators, their reference \(base\) level values, as well as an algorithm for calculating the Digital Labour Capacity Index \(DLCI\)\. A comparative regional assessment covering eight macro\-regions reveals substantial disparities in both components and the composite index\. East Asia &amp; Pacific demonstrates high levels of DLCI \(93\.3%\), with strong performance across both components reflecting a mature digital ecosystem with rapid technological adaptability\. North America \(80\.0%\) and Europe \(73\.3%\) show sufficient levels, formed by well\-developed digital skills and innovation infrastructure, though with some internal imbalances\. Latin America and the Caribbean records a medium level \(46\.6%\), with digital progress but weak coherence between human capital and technological usage\. South Asia, Sub\-Saharan Africa, and Central Asia and the South Caucasus each register moderate levels \(25\.0%\), constrained by limited digital competencies, low R&amp;D investment, and weak integration into global innovation networks\. The Middle East &amp; North Africa shows have low level \(13\.3%\), reflecting simultaneously weak workforce and innovation capacity and a fragmented pattern of digital transformation shaped by raw\-material\-oriented economic structures\. The results suggest that labour market digital capacity is a crucial prerequisite for the sustainable development of global regions\. Application of the study methodology reveals pronounced interregional disparities in digital capacity levels\. Regions with strong institutional foundations and sustained technology investment – East Asia &amp; Pacific, North America, and Europe – hold clear leading positions, whereas other regions show considerably weaker capacity for digital transformation of their labour markets\. These disparities point to the need for differentiated policy responses made to regional specifics, with priority given to human capital development, broader access to digital technologies, and stronger support for innovation activity\. The proposed methodology thus offers a practical analytical tool for tracking digital capacity across labour markets, detecting structural imbalances, and informing policy decisions that support sustainable socio\-economic development amid ongoing digitalization\. Keywords: digital capacity, digital skills, sustainable development, innovation capacity, labour market\.
