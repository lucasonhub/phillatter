# Error Correction in Trained TTT Layers

**Autores:** Yuhe Hu; Wen Jia Hu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Test\-time\-training \(TTT\) layers update fast\-weight memory while processing an input sequence\. A gradient\-based update, however, does not by itself establish how the memory revises an existing association\. We study the structure and empirical role of prediction\-derived updates in released TTT implementations\. For dot\-product objectives, we characterize the absence of a prediction\-error residual and establish conditions under which inner\-loop ascent and descent are equivalent after reparameterization\. For the LayerNorm\-based squared\-error objective used by TTT\-Linear and TTT\-MLP, we derive the inner gradient and identify a prediction\-derived component that cancels at the released initialization in the zero\-epsilon limit\. We then measure the full normalized prediction&\#x27;s contribution on three trained checkpoints\. Removing this contribution while retaining the LayerNorm Jacobian gives a mean gradient norm ratio of 0\.25 to 0\.32 across the models\. On 100,303 tokens from War and Peace, the same intervention with local per\-token norm matching raises perplexity by 2\.8\-2\.9% on TTT\-Linear\-760M and 1\.3B, whereas freezing all fast\-weight updates raises it by factors of 4\.2\-4\.5\. Separate synthetic tests illustrate the dependence of plain delta\-rule overwriting on step size\. The results distinguish a prediction\-derived update from its behavioral value and show why implementation analysis and live interventions are both needed to assess test\-time memory\.
