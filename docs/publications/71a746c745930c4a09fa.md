# VZV reactivation, immune remodeling, and dementia risk

**Autores:** Lijun Zhang; Shuang Wu; Yaru Quan; Wenrong Yao

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Varicella\-Zoster Virus \(VZV\) is a highly dermatotropic and neurotropic α\-herpesvirus that causes varicella \(chickenpox\) during primary infection, subsequently establishing lifelong latency, and reactivates to cause herpes zoster when host immunity declines or suppressed\. Recent large\-scale epidemiological studies consistently associate herpes zoster immunization, particularly with the recombinant zoster vaccine \(RZV\), with a significantly reduced risk of incident dementia\. These findings have prompted a rigorous re\-evaluation of the infectious etiology of progressive neurodegeneration\. This review integrates the intersecting molecular mechanisms of VZV latency, localized ganglionic immunosenescence, and systemic inflammaging\. We critically evaluate a synergistic VZV\-HSV\-1 amyloidogenic cascade, while explicitly addressing the biological limitations of extrapolating these transient interactions to human central nervous system \(CNS\) neuropathology\. Furthermore, we propose the hypothesis that AS01B\-induced trained immunity may contribute to neuroprotection\. Therefore, this review provides insight for understanding the systemic consequences of chronic VZV infection and the impact of adjuvant\-mediated immune remodeling in aging populations\.
