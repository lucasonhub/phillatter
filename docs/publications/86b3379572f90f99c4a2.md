# Cross\-Lexicon Emoji Sentiment Divergence for Arabic Sarcasm Detection

**Autores:** Rusul Hussein Hasan; Zinah Rajab Hussein; Mustafa Hussein Zwayyer; Nafea Jameel Khalaf ALHelal; Arkan Raheem Jebur; Duaa Adnan Tawfeek; Rasha Majid Hassoon

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study aims to determine whether the divergence between context\-free and context\-sensitive emoji sentiment scores can serve as a complementary signal for sarcasm detection in Arabic\. This phenomenon has received little systematic attention so far\. To address this aim, we conducted a multi\-scale empirical investigation\. At the lexicon level, we compared 1,031 overlapping emoji from two Arabic sentiment resources: the context\-sensitive Arab\-ESL, derived from 144,196 tweets, and the context\-free CF\-Arab\-ESL, annotated by 53 native speakers across seven Arab regions\. At the corpus level, we examined the divergence pattern in a stratified random sample of 199,999 tweets drawn from the 1,414,523\-tweet TEAD collection\. At the task level, we developed a divergence\-augmented transformer architecture by concatenating eight cross\-lexicon features with the \[CLS\] representation produced by three Arabic BERT encoders: MARBERT, AraBERTv2, and CAMeLBERT\. We evaluated it on the ArSarcasMoji dataset of 24,630 samples across five random seeds\. The results indicate that only 56\.8% of overlapping emoji maintain consistent sentiment labels across the two annotation paradigms \(W = 91,029, p = 2\.44× 10\-74, Cohen&\#x27;s d = 0\.656\)\. The corpus\-level analysis confirms that context\-free scores are systematically more positive than their contextual counterparts \(d = 1\.124, p &lt; 10\-300\)\. At the classification level, the divergence\-augmented MARBERT achieves the highest mean F1\-sarcasm of 0\.5234 ± 0\.015 across five random seeds, compared with 0\.5149 ± 0\.017 for the text\-only baseline, and the augmentation produces a directionally consistent gain across all three encoders\. Ablation analysis shows that continuous divergence scores contribute most of this improvement, whereas binary irony\-risk indicators tend to introduce noise at low prevalence\. The findings establish cross\-lexicon emoji sentiment divergence as a measurable and pervasive feature of Arabic digital discourse, and indicate a modest but directionally consistent benefit when the divergence signal is supplied as a complementary feature for downstream sarcasm detection\. A controlled emoji\-aware comparison further shows that retaining emoji as input tokens allows encoders whose vocabularies cover emoji to recover the dataset construction rule almost perfectly \(F1\-sarcasm up to 0\.998\), which establishes the emoji\-token\-free protocol adopted here, in which no raw emoji tokens reach the text encoder while emoji\-derived lexicon features are retained, as the methodologically sound setting for this corpus\.
