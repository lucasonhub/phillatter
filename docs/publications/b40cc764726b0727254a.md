# β\-Boswellic Acid Suppresses Migration and Invasion and Attenuates TGF\-β1\-Induced EMT\-Related Changes in PC\-3 Prostate Cancer Cells

**Autores:** Kaixin Song; Kunpeng Zang; Zihan Yang; Zhirong Tan; Xiaohui Geng; Jiale Wang; Lixi Zhu; Sijie He; Sian Pan

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

β\-Boswellic acid \(BA\) has potential antitumor activity, but its effects on migration, invasion, and epithelial–mesenchymal transition \(EMT\)\-related phenotypes in prostate cancer cells remain unclear\. In this study, we integrated public transcriptomic data, candidate molecule prediction, machine learning, functional association analysis, and molecular simulations with MTT, wound\-healing, Transwell, qPCR, Western blotting, transcriptomic analysis, and data\-independent acquisition proteomics to evaluate BA effects on PC\-3 cells and explore its potential molecular basis\. We identified 22 candidate BA\-related molecules and prioritised DPP4, MME, and ABL1 for further analysis\. Higher BA concentrations reduced PC\-3 cell viability\. At the same time, BA suppressed wound closure, migration, and invasion and attenuated TGF\-β1\-induced EMT\-related changes, accompanied by increased E\-cadherin and decreased N\-cadherin, Snail, and ZEB1 levels\. DPP4, MME, and ABL1 also exhibited BA\-associated expression changes\. Integrated transcriptomic and proteomic analyses suggested that BA\-associated molecular alterations involved the cell cycle, DNA replication, cell adhesion, cytoskeletal remodelling, and TGF\-β\-related processes\. In conclusion, BA suppresses migration and invasion in PC\-3 cells and attenuates TGF\-β1\-induced EMT\-related changes\. Candidate molecules including DPP4, MME, and ABL1, together with the multi\-omics findings, provide exploratory clues for further investigation of the potential molecular basis of BA activity\.
