# A GeoAI framework integrating YOLO\-based micro mobility detection and GIS–MCDM for heavy\-vehicle blind\-spot warning deployment

**Autores:** Ömer Kaya; Muhammed Yasin Çodur

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Micromobility modes such as e\-scooters, bicycles, and e\-bikes are becoming important components of low\-emission urban transportation systems\. However, their interactions with heavy vehicles pose significant safety risks, particularly in blind spots, at intersections, and during turning movements\. This study proposes an integrated GeoAI\-GIS\-MCDM framework to prioritize the deployment of heavy\-vehicle blind\-spot warning systems in urban areas and evaluate the detection capabilities of camera\-based perception models\. Methods The proposed framework consists of two complementary components: macro\-scale spatial prioritization and micro\-scale object detection\. A GIS\-based spatial decision\-support model was developed using 18 evaluation criteria grouped into three categories: micromobility user exposure potential, heavy\-vehicle operating exposure, and blind\-spot conflict and safety vulnerability\. Criteria weights were determined through assessments by five experts using Fuzzy Simple Weight Calculation \(Fuzzy SIWEC\) and Fuzzy Logarithm Methodology of Additive Weights \(FLMAW\)\. A hybrid weighting approach was then applied to generate a deployment priority map in ArcMap\. Additionally, six YOLO\-based object detection models were trained and evaluated under identical training configurations using a dataset of 6,070 images covering five micromobility\-related classes\. Results Blind\-spot conflict and safety vulnerability emerged as the most influential criterion group, while high\-activity blind\-spot overlap potential received the highest individual hybrid weight\. The resulting spatial priority map identified central and inner urban zones as the most critical candidate areas for warning\-system deployment\. The YOLO models achieved mAP@0\.50 values ranging from 0\.9438 to 0\.9694 and mAP@0\.50:0\.95 values ranging from 0\.8739 to 0\.9079\. YOLO26s achieved the highest mAP@0\.50, whereas YOLO26m obtained the highest mAP@0\.50:0\.95\. Discussion The findings demonstrate the complementary roles of GIS\-based spatial prioritization and YOLO\-based visual perception in supporting micromobility safety management\. By identifying priority deployment locations and assessing object detection performance, the proposed framework provides a decision\-support approach for the targeted implementation of intelligent heavy\-vehicle blind\-spot warning technologies\.
