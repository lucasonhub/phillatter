# Job Seekers&\#x27; Perceptions of the Usability and Accessibility of the Ajira Portal in Tanzania: Evidence from Ilala Municipal Council

**Autores:** Shineni Hemedi Suleiman; Revocatus Kabobe; Furaha Julius

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study explored job seekers’ perceptions of the usability and accessibility of the Ajira Portal in Tanzania, using Ilala Municipal Council as a case study\. The study examined job seekers’ perceptions of the portal’s usability, assessed its accessibility in relation to internet access, digital skills and device compatibility, and explored the major challenges experienced during the recruitment process\. The study was guided by the Technology Acceptance Model \(TAM\) and the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology \(UTAUT\)\. It adopted an interpretivist philosophy, qualitative approach and case study design\. A total of 30 participants were involved, comprising 25 job seekers/unemployed graduates and youth aged 18–35 who participated in five focus group discussions, while five PSRS officers participated in semi\-structured interviews\. Purposive and snowball sampling techniques were used to select participants\. The findings revealed that job seekers generally perceived the Ajira Portal as useful and convenient because it centralised public service vacancies and enabled online applications\. However, difficulties were experienced in registration, profile completion, password recovery, document uploading and application submission\. The study concludes that although the Ajira Portal has improved access to public employment opportunities, its effectiveness depends on improvements in usability, accessibility, system reliability and user support\. The study recommends simplifying application procedures, improving mobile functionality and system capacity, strengthening user guidance, and providing clearer and timely feedback to applicants\.
