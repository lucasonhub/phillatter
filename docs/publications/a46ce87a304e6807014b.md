# Circulating and vascular NAD\+ metabolism remodeling in Tgαq\*44 mice with progressive heart failure

**Autores:** Agata Jędrzejewska; Oliwia Król; Patryk Mucha; Ada Kawecka; Ewa Slominska; Mariola Olkowicz; Ryszard Tomasz Smolenski; Marta Tomczyk

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Heart failure \(HF\) is a complex systemic syndrome in which ongoing myocardial dysfunction and vascular alterations interact to drive disease progression\. Given its pivotal role in cellular energetics and redox balance, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide \(NAD \+ \) has emerged as a key regulator of cardiovascular homeostasis\. We recently demonstrated that myocardial NAD \+ depletion occurs early in Tgαq\*44 mice, a model of chronic HF\. Accordingly, this study sought to characterize systemic and vascular alterations in NAD \+ metabolism throughout the progression of HF by profiling circulating NAD \+ precursors and metabolites, and assessing vascular NAD \+ catabolic pattern\. In female Tgαq\*44 mice aged 4–14 months, whole\-blood NAD \+ and serum nicotinamide \(NAM\) levels progressively declined with increasing HF severity and were strongly associated with deteriorating systolic function\. Reduced circulating NAD \+ availability coincided with enhanced aortic NAD \+ turnover and increased aortic CD38 abundance in end\-stage HF\. In addition, we identified a low\-molecular\-weight VCAM\-1\-immunoreactive band in the aorta, consistent with enhanced vascular activation in Tgαq\*44 mice\. Advanced HF was also associated with perturbations in arginine\-citrulline metabolism, characterized by elevated levels of methylated arginine derivatives that may compromise nitric oxide \(NO\) bioavailability\. This was paralleled by progressive accumulation of 4\-pyridone\-3\-carboxamide ribonucleoside \(4PYR\), an NAD \+ \-derived endothelial toxin, whose levels correlated with all three methylarginines, suggesting convergent pathways of endothelial dysfunction\. Collectively, these findings suggest that enhanced vascular NAD \+ catabolism, associated with increased CD38 abundance, constitutes a previously unrecognized metabolic feature of HF progression that may contribute to systemic NAD \+ dysregulation and endothelial dysfunction\.
