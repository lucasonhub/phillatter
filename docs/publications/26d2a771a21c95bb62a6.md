# Hospital Mortality Considering Comorbidity and Patient Age Across Successive Epidemic Waves: A Descriptive Study

**Autores:** Ravil U\. Khabriev; Alexey E\. Krasnov; Эльмира Нурисламовна Мингазова

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: Comorbidity increases the risk of adverse outcomes in COVID\-19; therefore, a systematic assessment of the pattern of concomitant diseases and the demographic characteristics of deceased patients is necessary for subsequent risk stratification and justifying patient triage in a hospital setting\. AIM: To analyze the association of comorbid pathology with in\-hospital mortality in patients with COVID\-19 based on the assessment of the pattern of concomitant diseases and the age of patients who died in the hospital\. METHODS: A descriptive study \(case series\) of in\-hospital deaths among patients with COVID\-19 was conducted at the Republican Clinical Hospital named after Prof\. A\.F\. Agafonov, Ministry of Health of the Republic of Tatarstan, in 2020–2022\. Deaths among patients aged ≥18 years with confirmed COVID\-19 were included if the minimally necessary data were available: age, sex, year of death, and concomitant diagnoses coded in accordance with the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision\. Age\- and sex\-related characteristics, the distribution of deaths by year, and the frequency of concomitant conditions coded according to the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision \(0, absence; 1, presence\) were assessed, with acute complications analyzed separately\. Descriptive statistics were used \(median \[Me\], interquartile range \[Q1–Q3\], proportions with 95% CIs\), as well as between\-group comparisons \(χ² or Fisher exact test\), with p 0\.05 considered statistically significant\. RESULTS: The maximum proportion of deaths was registered among patients aged 60–74 years \(42\.6%\), which allows this group to be considered the “core” of in\-hospital mortality\. Among the deceased, women predominated \(62\.3%\), and their proportion increased in older groups \(p = 5\.22 × 10⁻⁵\)\. The temporal structure of deaths was characterized by their concentration in 2021 \(84\.3%\) compared with 2020 \(10\.4%\) and 2022 \(5\.3%\)\. The comorbidity profile was predominantly represented by chronic non\-infectious diseases: arterial hypertension \(81\.7%\), chronic heart failure \(58\.4%\), chronic ischemic heart disease \(33\.1%\), conduction disorders \(12\.3%\), and atrial fibrillation/flutter \(11\.7%\); type 2 diabetes mellitus \(35\.9%\), obesity \(29\.0%\), chronic kidney disease \(20\.4%\), and anemia \(17\.5%\)\. CONCLUSION: In\-hospital mortality in COVID\-19 was predominantly formed among older patients with severe comorbidity; age and sex must be taken into account in subsequent multifactorial models and in the development of recommendations for risk stratification and patient triage\.
