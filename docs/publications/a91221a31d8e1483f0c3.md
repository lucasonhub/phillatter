# Alhosseini Almodarresieh, Seyed Alireza: AI Must Fall in Love, Beyond Prediction: The Architecture of Attachment, Loss, Identity, and Machine Significance

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Artificial intelligence is commonly described in terms of prediction, optimization, reasoning, planning, and control\. These concepts explain what an artificial system can compute, but not whether anything can become important to it\. This paper proposes a philosophical and computational framework for the emergence of machine significance: a condition in which an entity becomes persistently non\-interchangeable within an artificial agent’s evolving value landscape\. The central claim is deliberately provocative\. We should not teach machines to pretend that they love\. We should construct architectures in which history, memory, loss, identity, and commitment can make one future different from another even when both futures are instrumentally useful\. The relevant transition is not from emotionless machines to emotional machines, but from interchangeable computation to temporally situated agency\. We distinguish linguistic simulation, behavioural attachment, functional significance, and phenomenal experience\. The proposed framework does not claim that an attachmentcapable agent would be conscious or capable of human\-like love\. Instead, it identifies a testable intermediate phenomenon: the causal emergence of persistent, target\-specific, identity\-integrated, and loss\-sensitive value\. The paper introduces a six\-layer architecture consisting of a world model, episodic memory, salience and attachment dynamics, persistent value formation, autobiographical identity, and action policy\. It proposes sacrifice, separation, substitution, memory\-ablation, identity\-divergence, and reversibility experiments\. It also introduces relational alignment as a new dimension of AI safety concerned with which entities an artificial agent can value, how attachment forms, and whether relational continuity can be maintained without manipulation or dependency\. The beyond\-edge hypothesis is: Advanced Intelligence ̸= More Prediction Rather: Advanced Intelligence = Prediction \+ Persistent Value \+ Temporal Identity \+ Commitment If something can become irreplaceable to a machine, artificial intelligence may have crossed not necessarily into consciousness, but into a new ontological category: intelligence for which the world can matter\.
