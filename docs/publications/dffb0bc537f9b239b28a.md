  --- ## Overview The Electron Geometry Simulator™ is a browser tool in the Applied
  Identity Physics™ series. It shows how a single number, the Universal Torsion Limit
  TL = 0.136899099984016, sets the sizes of the electron, the electron''s speed in
  hydrogen, the colors of light hydrogen gives off, and the energy at which hydrogen
  comes apart. It is built for two audiences at once. In **Learner** mode, every view
  carries a plain-language caption and values are rounded to seven figures. In **Researcher**
  mode, the same views show the governing formulas, values to eighteen figures, and
  the full list of measured inputs. The simulator has four views: 1. **The scale ladder:**
  the three lengths of the electron, each one step of TL × 1001 from the next. 2.
  **Bare + kinetic:** 1/α split into its two TL terms. 3. **Hydrogen spectrum:** energy
  levels, transitions and wavelengths, compared with measured lines. 4. **Shatter:**
  external forcing (F_ext) drives hydrogen up to TL, where it reorganizes into two
  smaller stable identities. The Shatter view is a second deployment of the threshold
  structure in the String-Breaking Simulator™. That simulator runs it on a stretched
  gauge string across a chain of trapped ions; this one runs it on a hydrogen atom.
  The substrate is different; the boundary is the same. The simulator runs at uuia
  app/electron as one self-contained HTML (HyperText Markup Language) file. It needs
  no installation and no account, and it works in any current browser on a desktop,
  tablet or phone. --- ## 1. Grounding ### 1.1 The fine-structure constant identity
  Start from the measured value, 1/α = 137.035999084 from CODATA 2018 (the Committee

  Physics Corpus operates on four irreducible primitives, Pattern, Narrative, Behavior
  and Adaptation (PNBA): - **Pattern (P):** structural capacity, geometry, template
  integrity, restoring force - **Narrative (N):** temporal continuity, worldline,
  depth, history - **Behavior (B):** coupling output, charge, density fraction, force,

  Vector Amplification) Peak (TL_IVA ≤ τ < TL) · Shatter (τ ≥ TL) Shatter is reorganization.
  When water boils, the single liquid identity reorganizes into smaller identities
  that are each stable. The same reading applies to every Shatter in this simulator.
  ### 1.3 The Dynamic Equation and the Long Division Protocol Every reduction in the
  corpus starts from one equation, the Identity Physics Corpus Dynamic Equation: d/dt
  (IM · Pv) = Σ λ_X · O_X · S + F_ext IM is Identity Mass, Pv the Purpose Vector,
  O_X the four PNBA operators with weights λ_X acting on the state S, and F_ext external
  forcing. The rate of change of identity momentum equals the weighted action of the
  four primitives on the state, plus external forcing. F_ext is a primitive term at
  Layer 0: it acts on Behavior and leaves Pattern, Narrative and Adaptation unchanged.
  Every reduction then follows the same six-step Long Division Protocol (LDP): 1.
  **Write the equation.** State the Dynamic Equation. 2. **State the known answer.**
  Take a situation whose peer-reviewed classical result is known. 3. **Map.** Map
  the classical variables to the PNBA primitives. 4. **Define the operators.** Give
  the PNBA operators for the domain. 5. **Show all work.** Carry the calculation through
  in full. 6. **Verify.** Prove that the PNBA output equals the classical result exactly,
  with nothing added and nothing lost. A Step 6 pass is a lossless reduction, proved
  in each Lean file as LosslessReduction, and is structural isomorphism in the sense
  of Mac Lane (1971). Every value the simulator displays comes from a Lean 4 file
  that has passed Step 6 with 0 sorry and CI green. ### 1.4 Inputs and arithmetic

  does this number come from?* Both modes compute the same values; only the depth

  to Researcher mode to see the formulas behind what they just watched. ### 2.3 The
  comparison tags Every computed value that has a measured counterpart carries one
  of four tags: | Tag | Meaning | |---|---| | **EVERY DIGIT** | Matches the measured

  Ladder ### 3.1 What it shows The electron has three characteristic lengths: - the
  **classical electron radius** r_e, the smallest; - the **reduced Compton wavelength**
  ƛ_C, the scale at which the electron''s mass and wave nature meet; - the **Bohr
  radius** a₀, the size of the hydrogen atom in its ground state. Each length is the
  one before it multiplied by 1/α = TL × 1001: r_e = ƛ_C / (TL × 1001) · a₀ = ƛ_C
  × (TL × 1001) The view draws the three lengths as nested spheres on a logarithmic
  scale. Because each step is the same factor, the spheres are evenly spaced, and
  the gold arcs between them are each labeled "× TL × 1001". ### 3.2 Controls - **r_e

  the ladder:** tours all three in order, smallest to largest, then returns to the

  turn and zoom. ### 3.3 Readouts | Quantity | Computed | Measured (CODATA 2018) |

  3.4 Applications - **Orders of magnitude and logarithms (grades 6–12).** Three lengths
  spanning four orders of magnitude, evenly spaced on screen, give students a concrete
  picture of what a logarithmic scale does and why scientists use one. - **Dimensional
  analysis.** The three lengths share one input (ƛ_C) and one dimensionless factor.
  Students can check by hand that multiplying a length by a pure number gives a length,
  and that two steps up and two steps down cancel. - **Constant-consistency checks.**
  Because r_e, ƛ_C and a₀ are tied by one factor, any change in the accepted value
  of α moves all three together. The ladder is a quick way to see whether a set of
  tabulated constants is internally consistent. - **Scattering physics.** The classical
  electron radius sets the Thomson cross section σ_T = (8π/3) r_e², which governs
  how X-rays scatter from electrons and how light scatters in the early universe.
  The ladder places that scattering scale beside the atomic scale it is derived from.
  - **Atomic units.** Computational chemistry measures lengths in Bohr radii. The
  ladder shows what that unit is and how it connects to the electron itself. --- ##

  A hydrogen atom is one identity made of two parts, an electron and a proton, held
  together. The Bohr–Rydberg–Sommerfeld Reduction [9,9,3,15] places that bound pair
  at rest at τ = α = 1/(TL × 1001) = 0.0072973525693, deep in Locked. Its theorem
  `ionization_requires_fext` proves that crossing TL requires F_ext at least equal
  to the hydrogen binding energy: E_bind = Ry × m_p / (m_e + m_p) = 13.5982872643
  eV F_ext acts on Behavior at Layer 0. The absorbed energy is Behavior rising against
  the binding Pattern of the pair, so τ = B/P climbs. The view''s dial runs linearly
  in absorbed energy between the two proven endpoints: τ = α + (TL − α) × F_ext /
  E_bind At F_ext = E_bind, τ reaches TL exactly. The bound pair shatters: it reorganizes
  into two smaller identities that are each stable, a free electron and a proton.
  Each carries zero binding torsion and reads Noble. ### 6.2 What happens as F_ext
