# Multi\-objective process route optimization for aero\-engine casings via graph\-guided large language models under multi\-feature interference constraints

**Autores:** Yubo Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Aero\-engine casings contain densely distributed hole arrays, cavities, bosses, mounting flanges, and datum features whose adjacency relations couple operation sequencing with machine\-tool, fixture, and tolerance constraints\. Process\-route evaluation must account for setup\-related time consumption, propagation of dimensional\-quality risk across tolerance\-coupled features, and dispersion of machine utilization under the same feasible\-route constraints\. This paper presents GGLM\-CAPP, a graph\-guided large language model framework that evaluates and ranks feasible candidate routes generated by constraint\-propagation pruning\. A convolutional\-fully connected branch represents operation arrangement patterns within local setup windows, while a Proximity\-enhanced graph convolution branch represents multi\-hop constraint topology through gated neighborhood aggregation and Laplacian regularization\. Their outputs are fused by residual addition\. A learnable sparse mask then identifies a route\-conditioned focused subgraph\. The retained nodes, relation types, route attributes, and three engineering\-calibrated objectives are converted by deterministic structured serialization into text before tokenization and large language model reasoning\. The three objectives are fixed as setup cost, error propagation risk, and equipment load imbalance, all with lower values preferred\. Their definitions are anchored respectively to setup and changeover time, tolerance\-related dimensional\-quality measurements, and machining\-time\-based equipment utilization\. Segment\-wise prompts read different information from the same process\-knowledge\-graph schema rather than introducing objective\-specific edge types\. Case validation uses one local casing dataset and five mapped benchmark datasets\. The evaluation protocol covers objective calibration, Pareto quality, constraint satisfaction, expert agreement, statistical reliability, and computational cost under a consistent LLM inference configuration\. Cross\-dataset experiments compare GGLM\-CAPP with seven baselines using synchronized HV, IGD, and CSR evaluations, while ablation and convergence analyses examine the contributions and optimization behavior of the main components\. On the local casing dataset, GGLM\-CAPP achieves an HV of 0\.876, an IGD of 0\.112, a CSR of 0\.964, and an LLM\-QAcc of 0\.940 over five independent runs\. Physical calibration yields R^2=0\.94 for setup\-duration validation and R^2=0\.91 for utilization\-dispersion validation, confirming the traceability of the measurement\-informed objectives\.
