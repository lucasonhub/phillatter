# The Dilemma of Multilateral Cooperation and Institutional Innovation Paths in Global Digital Governance

**Autores:** Jian Zhan

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid diffusion of digital technology is profoundly reshaping the global governance landscape\. However, the current global digital governance system faces significant structural difficulties, including the increasing fragmentation of regulatory frameworks, the continued widening of the digital divide, and the persistent inefficiency of existing multilateral mechanisms\. This article systematically analyzes the three core dilemmas currently hindering multilateral cooperation in global digital governance\. Specifically, it examines how the supply of international rules lags behind rapid technological iterations, how strategic competition among major nations squeezes the available space for multilateral negotiation, and how the blurring of rights and responsibilities among multiple stakeholders complicates consensus\-building\. Following this analysis, the study explores feasible pathways for institutional innovation\. The research posits that, marked by the establishment of the World Data Organization, a dual governance model and modular agreement design emphasizing both rules and standards provide new institutional frameworks for breaking through the current deadlock in multilateral cooperation\. Furthermore, the concept of inclusive multilateralism offers crucial directional guidance for constructing a global digital governance architecture that is both highly representative and operationally effective, ultimately fostering sustainable and equitable digital development worldwide across diverse economic and social contexts\. By addressing these critical challenges, this paper contributes to the broader academic discourse on international relations and technology policy, offering actionable recommendations for policymakers\.
