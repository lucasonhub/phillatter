# Bankruptcy Ranking And Probability Quality From Timely Public Filings

**Autores:** Levent Çinko

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Bankruptcy forecasts support credit allocation and ongoing corporate risk monitoring, but their usefulness depends on information timing and the decision they serve\. Ranking performance, probability accuracy, and screening coverage therefore require evaluation within a common forecasting design\. This study examines the predictive information in public annual reports and the incremental contribution of innovation indicators recorded in financial statements\. A report\-based framework aligns predictors with filing dates and links subsequent Chapter 11 petitions to the reporting legal entity through auditable public disclosures\. Frozen gradient\-boosted models are evaluated in subsequent economic periods and compared with financial fundamentals and a logistic benchmark\. Independent calibration and paired firm\-cluster resampling assess probability quality and uncertainty, while explicit alert budgets quantify screening performance\. Timely accounting information retains ranking power across the evaluation periods\. Incremental discrimination varies across periods, with uncertainty intervals spanning zero\. Calibration improves raw probability forecasts; recent period losses remain above a constant reference\. Budget\-constrained screening increases the concentration of failures in review lists, while the rule\-based audit identifies missing inputs among uncovered cases\. The study provides an auditable comparative assessment of these distinct forecasting tasks in practice and shows how evaluation dimensions shape the practical use of public accounting information for risk monitoring\.
