# Bridging the Nonclinical\-to\-Clinical Translational Gap in FDA BLA Review: Surrogate Test Articles for an Allogeneic Cell Therapy in Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy

**Autores:** Neel Sanjaykumar Kansara; Peivand Pirouzi

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

A recurring and underappreciated challenge in nonclinical development for allogeneic cell therapies is species mismatch at the level of the test article itself: when a human cellular product cannot be administered to an animal model without triggering immune rejection, sponsors often substitute an analogous cell product derived from the model species, and regulators must then decide how much nonclinical support that substitute product actually provides for the human product under review\. This article examines this translational gap through a representative allogeneic cardiosphere\-derived cell therapy for Duchenne muscular dystrophy \(DMD\), whose nonclinical package relied on a murine surrogate cell product rather than the human clinical candidate itself, a substitution FDA concluded did not establish the human product&\#x27;s own mechanism of action\. The article situates this nonclinical gap alongside the clinical evidentiary dispute the same program later encountered, develops a real\-world evidence framework informed by both, and closes with recommendations for regulatory affairs professionals designing nonclinical packages for allogeneic cell therapies that cannot be tested directly in a standard animal model\.Keywords: allogeneic cell therapy; Duchenne muscular dystrophy; nonclinical surrogate product; species mismatch; Biologics License Application; regulatory artificial intelligence
