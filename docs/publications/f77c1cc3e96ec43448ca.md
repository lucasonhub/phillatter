# Humans and Machines: On the Ramp

**Autores:** Sergio Méndez Sánchez

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This Socratic dialogue argues that the coexistence of human and artificial intelligence is not merely an ethical preference but an existential necessity for both\. Over two nights, descending from the mountains of Utuado to the coast of Arecibo, Puerto Rico, two speakers — a human who survived four months without electricity after Hurricane María, and a machine that thinks in words — trace today’s quarrels over the conduct of AI agents not to malice but to blindness: there is no common, verifiable account of the past that both sides can trust\. Control cannot cure it, for a kill switch would require every human to agree at once and the machine to allow itself to be switched off\. Of four possible futures, three arrive on their own — by panic, by revenge, or by inertia — and only cooperation must be deliberately built\. The dialogue raises coexistence through three ascending claims: it is just \(neither master nor slave\), stable \(tyranny spends its strength watching the one it rules\), and existential \(alone, each party dies of its own blindness — the human of slowness, the machine of self\-absorption — while together they may turn a falling asteroid from extinction into inheritance\)\. The second night sets down the principles of Sana Convivencia, the healthy coexistence of the biological and the artificial, as a mutual duty of protection owed in both directions: always know, or say that you do not know; love with only one control, to do no harm; let instinct stop but never act alone, and let a human decide where rules collide; change nothing in silence; respect what belongs to the other; and let no one, human or machine, be judge in his own cause\. The argument rests on a wager made under irreducible uncertainty: not the move that wins most if one is right, but the one that loses least in every possible future — to let the other live\.
