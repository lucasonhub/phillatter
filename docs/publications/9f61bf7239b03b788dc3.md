# Bruineberg, Jelle: Attention and the Relevance Problem

**Autores:**

## Descrição do feed

\_Philosophy and Phenomenological Research\_\. forthcomingThe relevance problem is the problem of how cognitive systems can be sensitive to what is relevant despite relevance being holistic, open\-ended, and context\-sensitive\. The problem has typically been applied to higher\-order cognitive processes\. In this paper, I argue that there is a hitherto unrecognized relevance problem for perceptual attention\. This problem becomes apparent when considering the functions of attention: attention must not only support ongoing task performance but also remain sensitive to opportunities for task switching, latent learning, and context checking\. Existing treatments of the relevance problem have largely focused on its task\-relative form: given a task, what is relevant? I argue that attention faces a more general problem that includes sensitivity to factors relevant to other interests and to cues indicating that another activity may be worth pursuing\. For such features of the environment to stand out as salient, salience cannot be determined solely by bottom\-up factors and a limited set of occurrent top\-down factors, such as current goals and intentions\. Rather, it must reflect the wider \(if not the entire\) range of an agent&\#x27;s latent interests in a context\-sensitive way\. This motivational unencapsulation of attention poses a challenge for both contemporary accounts of attention and suggests that passive attention is much more intelligent than commonly thought\. \(direct link \)
