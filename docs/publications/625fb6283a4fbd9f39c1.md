# Multifocal Glioblastoma Managed With Primary Temozolomide as a Strategy to Delay Radiation Therapy: Clinical and Radiological Outcomes

**Autores:** Chun Khai Loh; Helen Wheeler; Adrian Lee; Matthew Wong; Malmaruha Arasaratnam; Nicola Cove; Marina Kastelan; Michael Back

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

AIM: To evaluate the clinical and radiological outcomes of upfront temozolomide \(TMZ\) monotherapy in deferring radiotherapy for multifocal glioblastoma \(GBM\)\. METHODS: We analysed 18 consecutive adult patients with newly diagnosed, multifocal GBM treated with upfront TMZ monotherapy between 2016 and 2024\. The primary endpoint was a 6\-month progression\-free survival \(PFS\)\. Secondary endpoints were overall survival \(OS\) and radiological tumor response\. RESULTS: The median age of the cohort was 69 years\. The median follow\-up was 12 months\. Thirteen \(72\.2%\) patients had O⁶\-methylguanine\-DNA methyltransferase \(MGMT\)\-methylated tumors\. Bi\-hemispheric disease was present in 16 patients \(88\.9%\) at baseline\. Patients received a median of six TMZ cycles\. Disease relapse occurred in 94\.4% \(n = 17\) of cases, with 47\.0% of failures involving distant sites\. The 6\-month PFS was 61% \(95% CI: 42\.3%\-88\.3%\), and the median OS was 12\.2 months \(95% CI: 11\.2\-31\.1\)\. MGMT\-methylated tumors demonstrated a significant volumetric reduction at 3 months compared to unmethylated cases \(median \-34\.2% vs\. \+114\.0%; p = 0\.007\)\. Survival outcomes trended in favour of the methylated cohort \(6\-month PFS 69\.2% vs\. 40\.0%; median OS 12\.3 vs\. 11\.2 months\), though these associations did not reach statistical significance \(p = 0\.480\)\. CONCLUSION: This study provides valuable insights into the use of primary TMZ as an exploratory strategy for short\-term modulation in highly selected patients with extensive multifocal GBM\. Whilst this approach facilitates a strategic delay in large\-volume radiation, the high relapse rate underscores that upfront TMZ serves as a temporary window for deferring neurotoxicity rather than achieving durable disease control\.
