# Revisiting urban pipeline renewal: transparent machine\-learning for failure identification and elucidation

**Autores:** Roya Meydani; Girish Pulinkala; John Leander; Jan Komorowski

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Water utilities have traditionally equated network renewal with the replacement of ageing pipes, a narrow view that, if treated as the sole consideration, constrains strategic planning and overlooks the broader complexity of asset management\. This study aims to identify combinations of variables associated with failure and their value ranges in water piping, providing a better basis for asset management by accommodating uncertainty rather than attempting to eliminate it\. Recognising the importance of model transparency and interpretability, we employ rough set theory to build a machine\-learning rule\-based model that reveals variable interactions directly from data without prior information or assumptions\. Rough sets are data\-driven and generate readable IF\-THEN decision rules, making the model itself the explanation\. We apply this approach to a comprehensive dataset from Stockholm Water and Waste Company containing 36,900 pipe segments, heavily imbalanced at approximately a 1:10 ratio, addressed through undersampling\. Monte Carlo Feature Selection ranked the variables and R\.ROSETTA generated a rule\-based model visualised with VisuNet and VisuArc\. The achieved accuracy was 82% on the balanced hold\-out set, yielding interpretable decision support prioritising holistic understanding of failure processes over failure probability alone\. The proposed approach accounted for multiple failure\-associated variables and their interactions, extending previous findings limited to single\-variable analyses\. Both failure and no\-failure classes are modelled, identifying resilient configurations alongside failure\-prone ones, absent from most existing studies\. Evidence\-based insight informs renewal management, supporting pragmatic decisions while accounting for uncertainty\.
