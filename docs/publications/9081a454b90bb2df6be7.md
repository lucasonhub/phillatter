# AI Behind the Adult, Not in Front of the Child: A Design Framework for Adult\-Mediated AI in Early Childhood

**Autores:** Elif Öztürk

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Artificial intelligence \(AI\) in education has largely been positioned as a child\-facing tutor, conversational agent, or content\-delivery system\. In early childhood, this orientation warrants particular caution because executive functions, self\-regulation, and social\-emotional competencies develop through responsive relationships, play, embodied experience, and co\-regulation with adults\. This theory\-informed conceptual design study addressed three questions: which developmental, pedagogical, and ethical principles should structure AI use in early childhood; how these principles can be translated into a system architecture; and what validation pathway is required before such a system can make defensible claims\. Drawing on four evidence streams \(executive\-function and social\-emotional development, ecological and embodied learning, human\-centered AI ethics, and measurement and implementation science\), the study proposes the Behind\-the\-Adult AI framework, an adult\-mediated design framework initially specified for children aged 4–6 years\. Six design principles were derived and operationalized in a layered architecture: brief, bounded, non\-conversational digital developmental tasks generate behavioral indicators under transparent adaptation rules; a provisional, non\-diagnostic profile organizes longitudinal information; and AI operates only behind the adult, synthesizing observations from center, home, and school into hypotheses for human review\. A five\-stage validation pathway with explicit decision gates separates design claims from evidence claims\. The paper argues that developmentally responsible AI in early childhood should be evaluated not by how convincingly it replaces human interaction but by how effectively it strengthens the adults and environments surrounding the child, and it offers a transferable design heuristic for researchers, developers, and policymakers\.
