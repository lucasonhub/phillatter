# Pre\-service teachers&\#x27; curiosity and soft skills: A multi\-group structural equation modeling approach

**Autores:** Türker Sezer; E\. Özlem Yiğit; Necip Döner

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examines the relationships between curiosity and soft skills levels in pre\-service teachers \(N = 560\) studying in six different teacher training programs, using multigroup structural equation modeling via academic achievement \(grade point average, GPA\)\. Based on Kashdan et al\.&\#x27;s \(2018, 2020\) Revised Five\-Dimensional Curiosity Scale \(5DC\-R\) framework, curiosity was modeled in two latent dimensions: POSMERAK \(Positive Curiosity\) and STRESINT \(Stress Intolerance\)\. Analyses were conducted using Mplus 8\.3 software\. The main structural equation model and multigroup invariance tests were performed using a robust maximum likelihood \(MLR\) estimator with standard errors and a Satorra\-Bentler corrected chi\-square difference test\. Confidence intervals for indirect effects were calculated separately using a maximum likelihood \(MLR\) estimator and a bootstrap method with 5000 resamples\. Measurement invariance was tested in three stages: formal invariance, metric invariance, and scalar invariance\. Then, structural invariance was examined by comparing free and constrained models of structural pathways\. Invariance was achieved at all stages\. The findings revealed a significant difference at two levels\. At the first level, structural pathways are equivalent across all six programs, meaning the functioning of the curiosity\-soft skills process has a universal pattern\. At the second level, latent variable levels are significantly higher in favor of Science and Mathematics teacher candidates\. The results suggest that curiosity\-based developmental interventions in teacher education can be designed within the same framework across all disciplines, but differences in initial positions can be complemented with discipline\-specific support\.
