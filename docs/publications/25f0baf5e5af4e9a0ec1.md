# A spatial GIS–AHP framework for integrating hybrid renewable energy suitability with urban growth planning in arid regions

**Autores:** Mohamed Salah Ezz; Ashraf Mohamed Soliman; Doaa A\. H\. Salem; Hassan Youssef; Mohammed Aldelgawy; Ragab Khalil

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Urban expansion in arid regions drives vital socio\-economic development but places significant pressure on urban systems through land competition and infrastructure strain\. To address these challenges, this study evaluates the spatial suitability and distribution of solar photovoltaic \(PV\), onshore wind, and hybrid solar\-wind energy systems, investigating their spatial interactions with proposed urban expansion zones in Al\-Qassim, Saudi Arabia\. Methods A GIS\-AHP framework was developed using 13 criteria for solar PV and 12 criteria for onshore wind energy\. Spatial datasets were standardized, reclassified, and synthesized via weighted overlay analysis\. Hybrid suitability was determined by integrating the individual solar and wind models, while a post\-model spatial interaction layer was incorporated to evaluate alignment with future urban extensions\. Results The evaluation reveals that 42% of the study area demonstrates high suitability for solar PV, while 35% features moderate to high suitability for onshore wind energy\. The combined “High Suitable for Solar &amp; Wind” hybrid category covers 15\.2% \(10,920 km 2 \) of the region\. Discussion The proposed GIS\-AHP framework provides a practical tool for early\-stage renewable energy site selection\. By integrating spatial interaction analysis with proposed urban growth zones, this approach enables planners to balance future population growth and infrastructure demands with optimal clean energy deployment in arid environments\.
