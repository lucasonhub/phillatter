# Promoting learners’ right to human dignity: perspectives of school management teams in two South African primary schools in the Ehlanzeni District

**Autores:** Ngwenya Lihle Tracia; Makhasane Sekitla Daniel; Mulaudzi Lebohang Victoria

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Human dignity is a foundational constitutional value in South Africa and a core principle underpinning learners’ rights in education\. Despite constitutional and legislative protections, learners’ dignity continues to be undermined in schools through bullying, humiliation, discrimination, and punitive disciplinary practices\. This study explored how School Management Teams \(SMTs\) understand and promote learners’ right to human dignity in two South African primary schools\. Methods Guided by Human Rights Theory \(HRT\), the study adopted an interpretive qualitative case study design\. Data were generated through semi\-structured interviews and document analysis in two public primary schools in the Ehlanzeni District, Mpumalanga Province\. Eight SMT members, comprising principals, deputy heads, and departmental heads, were purposively selected\. The data were analysed thematically, including cross\-case attention to convergence and differences between the two schools\. Results The findings show that SMTs understood learners’ dignity in relation to respect, fairness, inclusion, and recognition of learners’ inherent worth\. Across both schools, dignity was promoted through awareness initiatives, ethical leadership, policy implementation, supportive discipline, anti\-bullying measures, and stakeholder collaboration\. Cross\-case analysis showed differences in emphasis: School 1 participants more often used explicit rights and anti\-bullying language, whereas School 2 participants more often foregrounded belonging, shared values, relational modelling, and home–school continuity\. These efforts were constrained by inconsistent educator practices, limited resources, weak parental involvement, cultural and linguistic differences, learner behavioural challenges, and broader community influences\. Discussion The study positions SMTs as institutional duty\-bearers who translate constitutional commitments to dignity into everyday school practice\. It contributes to scholarship on school leadership and human rights education by showing that dignity is not only a legal principle but also a leadership, governance, curricular, and relational practice\. The study recommends sustained human\-rights and dignity education for learners and staff, dignity\-focused staff development, clearer anti\-bullying mechanisms, restorative disciplinary procedures, and stronger home–school collaboration\.
