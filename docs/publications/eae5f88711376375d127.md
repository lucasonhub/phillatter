# Oligopeptide\-binding protein A is a potential target for combating Salmonella infection

**Autores:** Ken Yoshii; Koichiro Suzuki; Jun Adachi; Zhenyu Piao; Eri Node; Zilai Liu; Takahiro Nagatake; Koji Hosomi; Jun Kunisawa

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Salmonella infection remains a pervasive diarrheal illness, particularly affecting populations in developing countries\. The lack of an effective vaccine against non\-typhoidal Salmonella is attributable to antigenic diversity among serotypes, incomplete understanding of protective immune mechanisms, and the difficulty in identifying conserved antigens capable of eliciting broad cross\-protective immunity\. Methods To identify conserved Salmonella antigens, we established a Salmonella \-specific monoclonal antibody library using heat\-killed bacteria\. Candidate antigens recognized by monoclonal antibodies were characterized, and their functional relevance was evaluated through analyses of bacterial growth, respiratory activity, and protective effects in a murine Salmonella infection model\. Results We identified L2\-1H11, a monoclonal antibody that recognizes OppA, a conserved antigen present across multiple Salmonella serotypes\. L2\-1H11 was associated with suppression of bacterial growth and respiratory activity and attenuated enteritis pathology in Salmonella \-infected mice\. Conclusion These findings identify OppA as a conserved Salmonella antigen and suggest that it may represent a promising target for the development of subcutaneous and oral vaccines against Salmonella \.
