# Service adaptability assessment and coordinated planning and operation of PV\-rich low\-voltage active distribution network clusters

**Autores:** Shucan Zhou; Minjia Zheng; Xinyi Huang; Yixin Li; Yua Yue; Xin Huang; Kaiqing Liang; Yuting Huang; Lichen Yang; Wenhao Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

High rooftop photovoltaic \(PV\) penetration causes reverse\-power\-flow stress, voltage rise, and PV curtailment to vary across low\-voltage transformer service areas\. Fixed\-location flexibility cannot respond effectively when these stresses migrate over time\. This paper develops a coordinated planning and operation framework for PV\-rich low\-voltage active distribution network clusters\. The framework combines service\-adaptability assessment with mobile shared energy storage \(MSES\)\. First, an effective mobility service index \( M S I eff \) is constructed from the location and magnitude of reverse\-power\-flow exceedances in the no\-storage condition\. It identifies scenarios in which cross\-area resource reallocation provides additional value\. Second, stationary storage and MSES are optimized within a common power\-energy candidate domain\. The MSES model represents travel\-induced unavailability, traction energy consumption, connection preparation, interface limits, state\-of\-charge dynamics, and daily closure\. Across 30 parameterized PV\-load scenarios, MSES reduces PV curtailment in 16 scenarios and lowers equivalent annual cost in 15 scenarios\. It achieves both benefits in 13 scenarios\. M S I eff is strongly correlated with the realized reduction in PV curtailment, with Pearson and Spearman coefficients of 0\.867 and 0\.870\. In a representative high\-adaptability scenario, both schemes select 200 kW/800 kWh\. MSES reduces PV curtailment by 14\.54%, increases PV accommodation by 2\.02 percentage points, and lowers equivalent annual cost by 2\.04%\.
