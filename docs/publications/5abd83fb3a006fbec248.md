# Constructing the Golf Course Biodiversity Index: Adapting the City Biodiversity Index for Application at the Scale of Individual Golf Facilities

**Autores:** Jordan P\. Howell; Jordan Moore

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Golf courses occupy millions of acres globally and have documented potential to support biodiversity in fragmented urban and peri\-urban landscapes\. Yet no standardized measure exists for assessing biodiversity performance at the scale of an individual facility\. This paper develops the Golf Course Biodiversity Index \(GCBI\), a proposed 17\-indicator composite index adapted from the City Biodiversity Index \(CBI\)\. The GCBI organizes indicators across three domains \(Native Biodiversity on the Course, Ecosystem Services Provided by Biodiversity, and Governance and Management of Biodiversity\), and each indicator scored 0 to 4\. The index is deliberately dual\-resolution: it reports a single total score \(maximum 68 points\) that communicates overall performance to non\-specialist audiences, together with three domain scores that make the sources of that performance diagnostically transparent\. Indicators and scoring thresholds are grounded in peer\-reviewed ecology, conservation biology, and golf sustainability scholarship, with the evidentiary basis and any provisional assumptions for each threshold documented in the supporting supplementary file\. We position the GCBI as the first stage of a multi\-stage index\-development program and specify the empirical validation required before deployment\. We then discuss three prospective applications: a biodiversity\-specific supplement to golf certification programs, a sponsor\-accountability instrument aligned with nature\-related disclosure frameworks, and a consumer\-facing score intended to generate market incentives for biodiversity investment\.
