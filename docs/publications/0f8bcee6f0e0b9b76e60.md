# Research attitudes through the COVID\-19 pandemic

**Autores:** Sarah K\. Schlund; Dan K\. Hoang; Amy Kind; Joshua D\. Grill; Daniel L\. Gillen

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Identifying the factors that influence attitudes toward biomedical research is essential for improving public engagement and participation\. The COVID\-19 pandemic offers a unique opportunity to assess how a public health crisis shapes perceptions of clinical research\. We investigated whether attitudes toward biomedical research differed across phases of the COVID\-19 pandemic\. Methods We analyzed data from 5,098 adults enrolled in the UC Irvine Consent\-to\-Contact \(C2C\) registry, an online voluntary recruitment registry in Southern California, between June 2016 and August 2025\. Research attitudes were assessed with the validated 7\-item Research Attitudes Questionnaire \(RAQ\) at enrollment and annually thereafter\. Scores were analyzed continuously \(7 \-35\) and dichotomously, with &gt;28 defined a priori as high\. COVID\-19 periods were defined a priori as: P1 \(before March 13, 2020—the U\.S\. national emergency declaration\), P2 \(March 13 to December 11, 2020—prior to COVID\-19 vaccine approval\), and P3 \(after December 11, 2020—post\-vaccine approval\)\. Cross\-sectional analyses included all participants; longitudinal analyses were limited to 1,388 participants who enrolled before March 2020 and had ≥3 assessments\. Results Our sample of 5,098 adults was predominantly non\-Hispanic White \(70\.2%\), female \(65\.7%\), highly educated, and over age 50 \(66\.7%\)\. Participants enrolling during P2 had 60% higher odds of a high RAQ score \(&gt;28\) compared with P1 \(aOR 1\.60; 95% CI: 1\.07, 2\.40; p =0\.022\)\. Longitudinally, the mean within\-subject RAQ score was significantly higher during P2 versus P1 \(Est\. 0\.37; 95% CI: 0\.08, 0\.66; p =0\.011\), and the odds of a high RAQ score were 24% higher in P2 \(aOR 1\.24; 95% CI: 1\.09, 1\.39; p &lt;0\.001\) compared to P1\. Discussion These findings suggest that awareness of urgent medical issues, such as the COVID\-19 pandemic, may affect attitudes toward research\.
