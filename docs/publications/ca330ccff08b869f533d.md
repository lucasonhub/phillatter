# Renormalized volume fraction as a participation descriptor for composite thermal conductivity

**Autores:** Dimitrios E\. Anastasiou

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Classical models for predicting the thermal conductivity of composite materials rely on two components: the constitutive equation governing heat transport, and the descriptor representing reinforcement participation, conventionally the nominal geometric volume fraction \. This study tests whether prediction errors originate from this descriptor rather than from the constitutive equations themselves\. Here, is replaced by an effective volume fraction, , whose power\-law form is derived, not assumed, from scale invariance of transport participation\. Applied to five thermal\-conductivity models \(Voigt–Reuss–Hill, Maxwell, Bruggeman, Nan, and an Exponential geometric\-mean interpolation\) across 49 curated composite series \(243 datapoints\), this substitution, calibrated from one reference datapoint per series, reduces log\-space mean absolute prediction error by a factor of 2\.2 to 8\.5 \(1\.5 to 6\.5 on non\-calibration datapoints alone\) without altering any constitutive equation\. For the least\-idealized formulation, the fitted exponent&\#x27;s central tendency closely approaches an independently derived geometric baseline and shows robust, physically interpretable associations with dispersion quality, filler alignment, and filler scale and morphology in a machine\-learning descriptor analysis\. These results establish effective volume fraction as a physically grounded, renormalized descriptor of reinforcement participation in composite thermal transport\.
