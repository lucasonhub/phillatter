# VR\-FOCUS: evaluating eye tracking during a virtual reality N\-back task as a marker of cognitive load in chronic pain

**Autores:** Jordan Tsigarides; Jennifer O\. Bowler; Jack Dainty; Anthony J\. Bagnall; Alex J\. MacGregor

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Immersive virtual reality \(VR\) is a promising medium for adaptive pain therapeutics, but objective markers of cognitive load suitable for real\-time adaptation remain insufficiently characterised in chronic pain\. This study evaluated whether eye tracking embedded within a consumer\-available VR system provides signatures of cognitive load during a VR N\-back task\. A total of 84 participants \(42 healthy controls, 42 chronic musculoskeletal pain\) completed five levels \(Baseline to 4\-Back\) while ocular responses were recorded\. Performance declined and subjective workload increased with task level, confirming successful manipulation of cognitive demand, although performance plateaued beyond 3\-Back\. In mixed\-effects models, larger pupil diameter and higher blink rate were associated with task level and workload, without group differences\. Logistic regression using summary features discriminated 1\-Back from 4\-Back at near\-chance levels \(0\.51–0\.60\)\. Time\-series classifiers exploiting temporal structure performed substantially better in healthy controls \(0\.74–0\.88\) but were attenuated in chronic pain \(0\.58–0\.65\)\. Age alone did not appear to explain this difference, although a contribution cannot be excluded\. These findings support the feasibility of VR\-embedded eye tracking for cognitive load estimation in healthy adults, but performance in chronic pain is currently insufficient for reliable individual\-level inference; closed\-loop application in this population will require participant\-calibrated models and prospective validation\.
