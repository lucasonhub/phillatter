# A Study on Product Packaging and Its Influence on Consumer Purchasing Decisions

**Autores:** Gurkirat Singh; Devansh Modi; Shubham Raut

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Product packaging has evolved from a purely functional necessity into a strategic marketing tool that actively shapes how consumers perceive, trust, and ultimately purchase a product\. This study examines the influence of packaging on consumer purchasing decisions through a structured survey of 70 valid respondents, supported by a review of existing academic literature and a real\-world business case study\. The research finds that packaging measurably affects attention, trust, and purchase intent: 44\.9% of respondents said attractive packaging genuinely catches their attention, 46\.4% said good packaging builds trust, and 46\.4% said poor packaging would strongly discourage a purchase\. When shown two identical products differing only in packaging quality, with no price attached, 65\.7% of respondents chose the better\-packaged option, indicating that packaging alone carries substantial purchasing weight\. Design and layout emerged as the single most valued packaging element \(31\.4%\), ahead of material, eco\-friendliness, product information, shape, and colour\. A supporting case study of Tropicana&\#x27;s 2009 packaging redesign illustrates the commercial risk of poor packaging decisions, with the brand experiencing an estimated 20% sales decline within two months of an unrecognisable redesign\. The study also explores how artificial intelligence is beginning to reshape packaging design, testing, and quality control\. The study concludes that packaging is not a cosmetic afterthought but a measurable, quantifiable driver of consumer behaviour, and offers practical recommendations for businesses seeking to use packaging as a competitive tool\.
