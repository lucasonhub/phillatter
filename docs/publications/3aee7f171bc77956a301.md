# Alhosseini Almodarresieh, Seyed Alireza: The Three\-Level Ceiling: Empirical Evidence of Meta\-Cognitive Limits and Logical Cognitive Dissonance in System One vs\. System Two AI Models

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

We present a comparative empirical analysis of meta\-cognitive capabilities across three AI model architectures: TypeSafe AI’s Jev \(a “System One” decision\-only model\), Anthropic’s Claude 3\.5 Sonnet, and OpenAI’s GPT\-4o \(both “System Two” general\-purpose language models\)\. Using a novel evaluation framework—the Epistemic Meta\-Cognition Assessment Protocol \(EMCAP\)— we probe models with structured tasks involving epistemic nesting, self\-referential paradoxes, and category mistakes\. Our findings reveal three significant phenomena: \(1\) Jev exhibits a hard ceiling at approximately 3 levels of epistemic nesting, beyond which meta\-cognitive awareness collapses while confidence paradoxically inflates to 99%; \(2\) Jev and GPT\-4o display logical cognitive dissonance when processing the Card Paradox, simultaneously classifying it as undecidable and assigning a high decidability score; \(3\) Jev matches its own localized\-paradox performance \(95% accuracy on the Card Paradox\) but degrades sharply on distributed paradoxical structures, with depth\-estimation error rising to 57% at nesting level 7, compared to Claude’s 85% paradoxdetection probability at the same depth\. These findings suggest that meta\-cognitive capacity in AI may be constrained by architectural factors analogous to working memory limits in biological cognition, and that paradoxical inputs can induce internally inconsistent representations that current AI architectures do not always resolve\.
