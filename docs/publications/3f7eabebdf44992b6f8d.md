# ADAM17\-mediated CD16 shedding drives monocyte subset misclassification in ex vivo immunological assays

**Autores:** Soyeon Kwon; Seung Kak Shin; Seungwon Ryu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Human monocytes are classified as classical \(CD14\+\+CD16−\), intermediate \(CD14\+\+CD16\+\), and non\-classical \(CD14\+CD16\+\+\) subsets, but CD16 stability during ex vivo assays remains uncertain\. Here, we examined whether standard PBMC handling alters CD16\-based subset identity and whether ADAM17 inhibition can prevent this artifact\. In isolated PBMCs, intermediate and non\-classical monocytes rapidly lost surface CD16 during culture at 37 °C, with or without LPS stimulation, whereas CD16 expression remained stable at 4 °C and in unseparated whole blood\. Pre\- versus post\-incubation labeling and sorted\-cell experiments indicated that CD16 loss was not primarily due to receptor internalization or cell death, but instead produced a pseudo\-classical phenotype\. Public scRNA\-seq analyses showed enriched ADAM17 expression in monocyte/macrophage populations across blood, liver, and kidney datasets\. Pharmacological ADAM17 inhibition with TMI\-005 or TAPI\-1 preserved CD16 expression during LPS stimulation without broadly impairing monocyte cytokine production\. We therefore propose an ADAM17 inhibitor\-based protocol that combines surface staining, stimulation, and intracellular cytokine profiling to preserve monocyte subset identity during functional assays\. These findings identify a practical strategy to reduce CD16\-dependent monocyte subset misclassification in ex vivo immunological studies\.
