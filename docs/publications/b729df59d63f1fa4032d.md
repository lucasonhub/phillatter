# Teaching on demand: Exploring the role of AI in medical education podcasts

**Autores:** Payal Guha; Oscar L\. Oglina; Celia Brown; Courtney Krstić; Chukwuma N\. Uduku; Layla Thurston; Risheka Ratnasabapathy; Amir H\. Sam

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

INTRODUCTION: Artificial intelligence \(AI\)\-generated educational content holds promise for scalable medical education, but evidence comparing AI and clinician\-produced podcasts remains limited\. We investigated whether AI\-generated podcasts achieve comparable student satisfaction to clinician\-recorded podcasts, enabling broader curriculum coverage without overburdening clinical educators, and explored students&\#x27; attitudes towards AI\-generated podcasts\. METHODS: Medical students were randomised into two groups, with group 1 receiving clinician\-produced podcasts on type 1 diabetes \(T1DM\) and AI\-generated podcasts on type 2 diabetes \(T2DM\)\. Group 2 received AI\-generated podcasts on T1DM, and clinician\-produced podcasts on T2DM\. Students were blinded to the podcast types\. The outcome measures included students&\#x27; satisfaction \(measured using the Student Satisfaction with Educational Podcasts Questionnaire \(SSEQ\)\) and students&\#x27; attitudes to AI using an open\-ended survey\. A Bonferroni correction was applied \(α = 0\.025\)\. RESULTS: = 165\), 56% reported production method mattered, citing the sound of the voice, engagement of speaker, and ethical concerns as major issues with AI podcasts\. DISCUSSION: AI\-generated podcasts achieved non\-inferior satisfaction to clinician\-recorded podcasts\. However, students&\#x27; psychological preference for human teaching and negative perceptions of AI pose a barrier to scalable benefits, necessitating efforts to build trust and acceptability\.
