# Recent advances on MOF ligand engineering for CO2 hydrogenation

**Autores:** Kunli Song; Yichen Wang; Yajun Ma; Wei Lu; Yongquan Qu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Catalytic hydrogenation of CO 2 to value\-added chemicals, including carbon monoxide \(CO\), methane \(CH 4 \), methanol \(CH 3 OH\), and C 2\+ hydrocarbons/oxygenates, represents a pivotal strategy for mitigating carbon emissions and advancing a circular carbon economy\. Metal\-organic frameworks \(MOFs\) have emerged as highly promising platforms for CO 2 hydrogenation because ligand engineering enables precise control over active sites, electronic structure and nanoconfined environments\. Strategically modifying ligands permit atom\-level modulation of the electronic structure of metal nodes, precise tuning of pore geometry and functionality, and the fabrication of synergistic, multifunctional active sites, thereby directing the activation of CO 2 and H 2 , stabilization of key intermediates \( e\.g\. , ⁎ HCOO, ⁎ CO, ⁎ CH x \), and ultimately steering of reaction pathways toward target products\. This review provides a comprehensive and in\-depth analysis of ligand\-engineering strategies for MOF\-based CO 2 hydrogenation\. We systematically summarize the synthetic methodologies for ligand\-modified MOFs, delve into the fundamental mechanisms by which ligand engineering modulates catalytic properties \( e\.g\. , electronic effects, confinement, and cooperativity\), and detail its application in controlling selectivity toward CO \( via reverse water gas shift\), CH 4 \( via methanation\), CH 3 OH \( via the formate pathway\), and more complex C 2\+ products\. Our analysis highlights the unique advantages and structure\-performance relationships conferred by MOF architecture and ligand engineering while addressing prevailing research bottlenecks, such as stability under process conditions and scalability\. Finally, we outline persistent challenges and propose forward\-looking research perspectives, including the development of dynamic ligands, machine\-learning\-assisted rational design, and process intensification strategies\. This review aims to elucidate the transformative impact of ligand engineering in advancing MOF\-based CO 2 hydrogenation catalysis\. It serves as a comprehensive framework for understanding how precise ligand modulation can dictate reaction pathways\. By establishing structure\-performance relationships and identifying key frontiers such as dynamic stimuli\-responsive systems and rational stability engineering, this review aims to inspire the design of next generation catalysts\.
