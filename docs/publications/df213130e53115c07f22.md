# Dissociating task, semantic, and response conflict in cognitive aging: evidence from a low\-cognitive control Stroop paradigm

**Autores:** Eldad Keha; Maria Augustinova; Ludovic Ferrand; Eyal Kalanthroff

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Cognitive aging is often associated with increased Stroop interference, but it remains unclear whether this reflects a broad decline in inhibitory control or selective impairments in specific conflict\-resolution mechanisms\. The present study examined age\-related differences in task conflict, semantic conflict, and response conflict using a low\-cognitive\-control Stroop paradigm designed to amplify conflict effects\. Younger and older adults completed a manual color\-identification task including neutral shapes, neutral words, color\-associated words, non\-response\-set incongruent color words, and response\-set incongruent color words\. This design allowed us to dissociate response conflict, two components of semantic conflict, and task conflict within a single paradigm\. Older adults showed larger response conflict than younger adults, along with modest age\-related increases in both semantic relevance and semantic relatedness\. In contrast, task conflict did not differ between age groups\. These results suggest that age\-related increases in Stroop interference are not best explained by a single global inhibitory deficit\. Rather, under the present manual\-response, reduced\-control conditions, older adults showed greater semantic and response\-level conflict, whereas task conflict remained comparable across age groups\. This dissociation is consistent with a more fine\-grained account of cognitive control in aging\.
