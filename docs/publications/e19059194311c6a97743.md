# Whole\-exome sequencing reveals genetic etiology and pregnancy outcomes in fetuses with ultrasound\-detected abnormalities at a tertiary center

**Autores:** Meiying Cai; Na Lin; Hailong Huang; Meimei Fu; Yifang Dai; Liangpu Xu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Despite advances in prenatal diagnostics, the genetic causes underlying fetal ultrasound abnormalities remain poorly understood, with a significant proportion of cases lacking definitive molecular diagnoses\. Invasive prenatal testing was performed in 179 cases, followed by whole\-exome sequencing \(WES\) to identify genetic etiologies\. Among 179 abnormal fetuses, 132 had single\-system and 47 had multisystem abnormalities\. WES identified pathogenic \(P\) or likely pathogenic \(LP\) variants in 33 cases \(19 single\-system, 14 multisystem\), with skeletal \(7\), cardiac \(5\), and craniofacial defects \(2\) being most frequent\. Thirty\-four genes were involved, encompassing 41 variants \(15 missense, 10 frameshift, 6 splicing, 6 nonsense, and 4 in\-frame deletions\)\. WES yielded a P/LP rate of 17\.88% \(32/179\) for variants that explained the fetal phenotype, with an additional pathogenic finding \(overall P/LP rate: 18\.44%, 33/179\)\. Inheritance patterns were autosomal dominant \(19\), recessive \(12\), X\-linked \(1\), and mosaic \(1\)\. Family studies confirmed 27 variants \(18 inherited, 9 de novo \)\. Pregnancy outcomes included 60 terminations, 19 intrauterine deaths, and 8 postnatal abnormalities\. Most fetal ultrasound phenotypes matched the disease\-causing gene profiles\. Combining molecular testing with ultrasound helps clinicians identify genetic causes, assess recurrence risk, and inform parents, enabling personalized management for high\-risk families\.
