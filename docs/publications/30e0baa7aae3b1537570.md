# Improved HCV genotyping based on sequence encoding and feature integration

**Autores:** Ahmed M\. Fahmy; Muhammed S\. Hammad; Walid Al‐Atabany; Mai Said Mabrouk

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Genomic sequence classification is an important task for understanding viral genetic variability and supporting genotype identification, particularly for viruses such as the Hepatitis C Virus \(HCV\)\. In this study, we evaluate the effectiveness of different sequence encoding techniques, supplementary genomic descriptors, and class\-balancing strategies for HCV genotype classification under a stricter CD\-HIT\-EST cluster\-aware evaluation protocol designed to reduce sequence\-identity leakage\. Four encoding methods were investigated, namely Frequency Chaos Game Representation \(FCGR\), k\-mer encoding, one\-hot encoding, and label encoding\. These representations were evaluated using multiple machine learning models, including XGBoost, Random Forest, Multi\-Layer Perceptron \(MLP\), and K\-Nearest Neighbors \(KNN\)\. To address class imbalance, SMOTE was applied, while GC content and GC skew were included as supplementary compositional descriptors\. Overall, FCGR and k\-mer encoding outperformed one\-hot and label encoding\. The best result was achieved using k\-mer encoding with XGBoost and SMOTE, obtaining a macro F1\-score of 0\.890, accuracy of 0\.922, macro precision of 0\.866, macro recall of 0\.925, macro specificity of 0\.990, and MCC of 0\.893 under the CD\-HIT\-EST cluster\-aware evaluation setting\. The statistical analysis showed that SMOTE had a clear positive effect on classification performance, particularly for the Random Forest model, while GC\-based descriptors provided additional interpretability but did not show a statistically significant improvement in the k\-mer\-based experiment\. Overall, this study demonstrates the importance of selecting appropriate sequence encodings and class\-balancing strategies for machine\-learning\-based HCV genotype classification\. The findings provide a comparative basis for future work on sequence\-based classification tasks in virology and computational genomics\.
