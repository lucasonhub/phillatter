# Meta\-Bayesian Fusion Adaptation for Evacuation Prediction: A Hybrid XGBoost–Graph Neural Network Framework for the Mentawai Megathrust Scenario

**Autores:** Sularno Sularno; Wendi Boy; Putri Anggraini; Dio Prima Mulya; Rexcharles Enyinna Donatus

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Continuous monitoring of physiological conditions in intensive care units generates complex multivariate time\-series data that can support early clinical decision\-making\. However, conventional hybrid models combining Long Short\-Term Memory networks and XGBoost generally learn correlational patterns and may experience performance degradation under non\-stationary conditions, sensor artifacts, and changes in patient characteristics\. Moreover, these models are typically designed for outcome prediction rather than treatment\-effect estimation\. Therefore, a framework capable of integrating temporal forecasting, representation disentanglement, uncertainty estimation, and treatment\-response analysis is required\. Purpose: This study aims to develop a Causal Multi\-Task Fusion Engine \(CMTFE\) for forecasting future vital\-sign trajectories and estimating individualized treatment effects in IoT\-supported ICU monitoring\. Methods/Study Design/Approach: The proposed framework integrates a Temporal Convolutional Network for multivariate vital\-sign encoding, a β\-Variational Autoencoder for separating outcome\-relevant and confounding representations, a Transformer decoder for probabilistic trajectory forecasting, and propensity\-weighted XGBoost regressors for treatment\-effect estimation\. Experiments were conducted using 487,000 observation windows derived from 38,200 ICU stays in the MIMIC\-IV dataset\. The data were chronologically divided into training, validation, and testing sets at proportions of 70%, 15%, and 15%, respectively\. Forecasting performance was evaluated using Mean Absolute Error, Root Mean Squared Error, and Continuous Ranked Probability Score\. Treatment\-effect estimation was evaluated on a semi\-synthetic test set using Precision in Estimation of Heterogeneous Effect and Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve\. Results/Findings: CMTFE achieved an MAE of 0\.281, RMSE of 0\.390, and CRPS of 0\.218, outperforming the evaluated LSTM, TCN, Transformer, LSTM–XGBoost, and TCN–XGBoost baselines\. Under the constructed distribution\-shift scenario, the model exhibited a relative MAE increase of 4\.2%, compared with 9\.5% for TCN–XGBoost\. For semi\-synthetic treatment\-effect estimation, CMTFE obtained a PEHE of 0\.187 and an AUC of 0\.842\. The ablation analysis further indicated that representation disentanglement, contrastive regularization, propensity\-score weighting, and temporal convolution each contributed to the model’s performance\. Novelty/Originality/Value: The principal contribution of this study lies in integrating temporal forecasting, disentangled representation learning, uncertainty quantification, and propensity\-adjusted treatment\-effect estimation within a unified ICU\-monitoring framework\. Unlike conventional hybrid models that combine neural and tree\-based predictions through passive aggregation, CMTFE uses learned outcome\-relevant representations to support both forecasting and intervention\-response analysis\. Nevertheless, the treatment\-effect findings are based on semi\-synthetic outcomes and should be interpreted as experimental decision\-support evidence rather than validated clinical recommendations\. External, prospective, and clinician\-centered validation is required before real\-world deployment\.
