# The will to live, illness representations, hope, and heart failure self\-care

**Autores:** Carolyn Kleman; Kayla Alderson; Chris Evonko; Caroline Jenkins; Nia Lewis; Stephanie Turrise

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Heart failure \(HF\) remains one of the most prevalent cardiovascular diseases worldwide\. Effective management requires both medical treatment and patient engagement in self\-care activities\. Because self\-care can be challenging to sustain, understanding the factors that are associated with self\-care behaviors is essential\. Illness threat, coping resources, and self\-care efficacy may affect heart failure self\-care \(HFSC\)\. The will to live \(WTL\) and hope, as coping\-related resources, may motivate individuals with HF to engage in self\-care and strengthen their confidence in managing their condition\. Objective To examine the associations among illness threat, will to live, hope, and heart failure self\-care\. Methods Moderation analyses and multiple regression were conducted using survey data from 204 adults with heart failure\. Results Illness threat did not significantly influence HFSC and was not moderated by WTL or hope\. In contrast, both WTL and hope were positively associated with HFSC and self\-care efficacy\. Conclusion Across all HFSC domains—maintenance, monitoring, management, and self\-care efficacy—WTL and hope emerged as consistent and significant correlates\. Illness threat showed weak or non\-significant associations with HFSC and did not interact with WTL or hope in predicting self\-care behaviors\. These findings suggest that coping and dispositional resources may play a more central role in HFSC engagement than illness perceptions alone\. Interventions that focus primarily on emphasizing illness threat may be less effective than those that assess and strengthen patients’ hope, will to live, and sense of agency in managing their condition\.
