# Metal\-organic and covalent organic frameworks for precision drug delivery: a precision\-by\-design evidence framework for biologically accountable nanomedicine

**Autores:** Xinwei Li; Haonan Zhang; Ruidong Ding; Feng Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Metal\-organic frameworks and covalent organic frameworks offer a rare opportunity to design drug carriers from molecular building blocks upward\. Yet high loading capacity and stimulus\-responsive release alone do not constitute precision delivery\. Methods We critically synthesized evidence on MOF and COF pore\-level cargo interactions, framework and surface engineering, endogenous and externally triggered release, active targeting, biomimetic coatings, nanozyme activity and theranostic integration\. Precision delivery was decomposed into five evidence\-qualified dimensions: material, spatial, temporal, mechanistic and translational precision\. Results The evidence supports the complementary strengths of the two families across small\-molecule, protein, nucleic\-acid, gas and catalytic therapies\. Direct evidence is strongest for individual component technologies, selected antibody\-targeted systems and two\-component hybrids\. Recurrent evidence gaps include incomplete particle characterization in biological media, reliance on total\-tissue accumulation rather than cell\-specific delivery, and insufficient separation of carrier degradation from drug release\. Discussion A framework becomes precise only when it maintains a defined material identity, reaches a biologically justified compartment, releases an active payload at the intended place and time, and does so with measurable improvements in exposure, efficacy and safety\. This requires a precision\-by\-design framework that maps claims to minimum evidence and links material critical quality attributes to biological mechanisms, translational assays and development decisions\.
