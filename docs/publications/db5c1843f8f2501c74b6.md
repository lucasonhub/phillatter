# Perioperative management and neurological outcomes of traumatic spondyloptosis: A retrospective case series of 14 patients

**Autores:** Xing\-Sheng Yu; Zhao\-hu Mao; Cheng Qiu; Ling\-Zhi Zhang; Zheng\-Qi Chang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUNDTraumatic spondyloptosis is a severe, high\-energy spinal injury, and priority should be given to emergency resuscitation and vital\-sign stabilization during the perioperative period\. AIMTo analyze the clinical characteristics, perioperative treatment strategies, surgical protocols, and long\-term neurological rehabilitation outcomes of patients with traumatic spondyloptosis\. METHODSA retrospective analysis was conducted on 14 adult patients diagnosed with traumatic spondyloptosis who were admitted to our orthopedic department from January 2018 to January 2025\.Clinical data, including demographic characteristics, injury mechanisms, combined fracture sites, spinal injury segments, preoperative resuscitation measures, surgical approaches, postoperative rehabilitation protocols, and neurological function assessed by the American Spinal Injury Association \(ASIA\) impairment scale, were analyzed\.All patients completed at least 12 months of regular follow\-up to evaluate surgical efficacy, fracture healing and neurological recovery\. RESULTSAmong the 14 patients, 11 had polytrauma with extra\-spinal fractures involving the scapula, ribs, maxilla, skull and other parts, and three presented with hemorrhagic shock on admission and received emergency resuscitation\.Thirteen patients underwent posterior\-only spinal surgery, and one patient underwent combined anterior\-posterior surgery, with complete anatomical vertebral reduction achieved in all cases\.Preoperatively, all patients had severe neurological deficits \(ASIA A or B\)\.At the final follow\-up, eight patients achieved elevated ASIA grades, five showed obvious neurological recovery, three showed mild improvement, and six remained unchanged\.Postoperative complications included joint contractures, pneumonia, pressure ulcer, urinary tract infection, and deep vein thrombosis\. CONCLUSIONPosterior surgical treatment combined with individualized postoperative rehabilitation is useful and can achieve complete spinal reduction, relieve spinal cord and nerve root compression, promote fracture healing and improve neurological recovery\.
