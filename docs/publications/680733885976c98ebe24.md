# Exploring Potential Psychological, Behavioral, and Physiological Predictors of Human Flourishing in Division 1 Athletes: Toward a More Comprehensive Mental Health Screening

**Autores:** Derek Aaron Wheeler

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The National Collegiate Athletic Association \(NCAA\) has created numerous task forces and guidelines to address the rise in mental health challenges faced by student\-athletes\. However, these efforts have largely focused on identifying psychopathology rather than promoting flourishing\. The purpose of this study was to examine the predictive utility of self\-report and objective measures of human flourishing among NCAA Division I student\-athletes at initial screening \(i\.e\., preparticipation exam\)\. This retrospective, cross\-sectional, correlational study analyzed archival data from 92 student\-athletes from 13 different sports\. Measures included the Secure Flourishing Index \(SFI\), Patient Health Questionnaire\-9 \(PHQ\-9\), Athlete Sleep Behavior Questionnaire \(ASBQ\), and Power Points, an objective measure of Heart coherence\. Simple linear regression showed that Power Points significantly predicted SFI scores, explaining 4\.8% of the variance \(R² = \.048, p = \.037\)\. Multiple linear regression examined PHQ\-9, ASBQ, Power Points, sport type, and gender as predictors of SFI scores\. The model was significant and explained 29\.4% of the variance \(R² = \.294, p &lt; \.001\)\. Depression was the only significant individual predictor \(B = −\.124, p &lt; \.001\)\. The implications for these results are 1\) objective measures should continue to be researched for their predictive utility of human flourishing at initial screening and 2\) all universities should at a minimum use the PHQ\-9 to assess depression\. Finally, this study suggests using the SFI and an objective physiological marker as an adjunct to the initial health screening may provide a more comprehensive, data\-driven, strength\-based screening model that could potentially enhance early identification and intervention among elite student\-athletes\.
