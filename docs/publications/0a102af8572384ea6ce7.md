# Primary tracheal lymphoepithelioma\-like carcinoma in a young non\-smoking male: a case report highlighting the diagnostic value of flow\-volume loop

**Autores:** Shan Xiao; Sida Chen; Jintao Ou; Yan Shen

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Primary tracheal lymphoepithelioma\-like carcinoma \(LELC\) is an extremely rare malignancy \(&lt;2% of tracheal tumors\) strongly associated with Epstein–Barr virus\. Tracheal tumors can mimic asthma due to dynamic airway obstruction, often leading to diagnostic delay\. The flow\-volume loop provides critical clues to central airway obstruction but is frequently overlooked\. Case Presentation We report a 30\-year\-old non\-smoking male initially misdiagnosed with asthma, whose refractoriness to inhaled corticosteroids prompted spirometry\. The flow\-volume loop revealed a plateau pattern in both inspiratory and expiratory limbs, consistent with fixed upper airway obstruction which guided chest computed tomography \(CT\) and bronchoscopy to identify a tracheal polypoid mass\. Pathological examination confirmed LELC with positive EBV\-encoded small RNAs \(EBER\) in situ hybridization\. The patient underwent endoscopic resection followed by video\-assisted thoracoscopic surgery \(VATS\) tracheal resection\-anastomosis with lymphadenectomy\. Postoperative pulmonary function testing at 1\-year follow\-up demonstrated complete normalization of the flow\-volume loop morphology, with FEV 1 \(Forced expiratory volume in 1 s\) improved from 0\.91L to 3\.62L\(\+298%\) and PEF \(peak expiratory flow\) from 2\.03L/s to 6\.76 L/s \(\+233%\)\. The patient remained asymptomatic without recurrence at 1\-year follow\-up\. Conclusion This case underscores that, in young patients with asthma\-like symptoms refractory to conventional therapy, flow\-volume loop analysis should not be deferred, as it can unmask central airway obstruction and expedite the diagnosis of rare tracheal malignancies\.
