# Paterson, Anthony: Reflective Identity Geometry v0\.2

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Large language models can exhibit stable, persona\-like behavior across extended interaction despite lacking persistent internal identity in the ordinary sense\. The Hudson Recursive Identity System \(HRIS\) explains such continuity through recursive human\-driven constraint rather than through persistent model memory or parameter change\. The v0\.1 RIG manuscript explicitly located itself against HRIS I\-III, especially claims about recursive constraint, identity surfaces, recurrent traversal, and personality\-like stabilization\. Reflective Identity Geometry \(RIG\) accepts this interactional starting point and asks a further question: if recursive interaction can stabilize model behavior, what happens to the human participant inside the same loop? RIG proposes that some forms of identity\-like stability in long\-horizon human\-LLM interaction are better studied at the level of the coupled system rather than at the level of either participant alone\. Human constraints shape model output; model output then becomes part of the informational environment through which the human interprets, reformulates, corrects, and sometimes reorganizes their own thinking\. Repeated cycles may 1 therefore produce a stable interaction regime involving reciprocal rather than purely model\-side adaptation\. RIG does not claim that an LLM possesses a persistent self, that the model perfectly mirrors the user, or that human cognitive change necessarily occurs\. It proposes a system\-level hypothesis and a set of testable consequences\. Its implications concern philosophy of mind, extended cognition, interpretability, alignment, and the design of long\-horizon human\-AI systems\. \(direct link \)
