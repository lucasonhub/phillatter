# Wood\-Based Hybrid Construction and Circular Economy: A Three\-Level Framework for Component, System, and Building Design

**Autores:** Markku Karjalainen; Hüseyin Emre Ilgın; Teemu Hirvilammi; Sami Pajunen

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Hybrid construction is a key strategy for increasing the use of wood in contemporary buildings while meeting structural and regulatory requirements, particularly as building\-level carbon reporting and embodied\-carbon requirements are becoming increasingly important in Finland and other regulatory contexts\. This study aims to examine how wood\-based hybrid construction can be systematically structured to support low\-carbon and circular building practice, distinguishing single component level, system level, and building level strategies\. This research builds on a research, development, and education learning materials project at Tampere University and adopts a qualitative, non\-experimental methodology based on a structured synthesis of peer\-reviewed literature, technical sources, and documented application examples\. The main contribution of this study is a three\-level decision\-support framework that clarifies how wood\-based hybridization can be evaluated across different design scales in relation to structural performance, circular economy mechanisms, and early\-stage design decision\-making\. The results suggest that hybridization effectiveness is scale\-dependent, that higher hybridization levels may provide broader strategic opportunities for regulatory alignment and lifecycle\-oriented design when supported by appropriate system configuration, and that circular economy performance depends on connection logic, adaptability, and material separability rather than material choice alone\. The findings provide a practical reference for architectural design, professional practice, and education\.
