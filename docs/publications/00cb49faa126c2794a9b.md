# Online prediction of fresh concrete workability via machine learning\-based assessment of mixing power curves

**Autores:** Alexander Teichmann; M Haist

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Concrete production control relies largely on operator expertise and manual material testing, which delivers results only after mixing, when adjustments are very limited\. This approach becomes insufficient for low\-CO 2 mixes and recycled aggregates, whose variable water demand increases rejection risk\. Consequently, online quality assessment based on process information acquired during mixing is required\. A signal readily available in modern plants is the mixer’s power draw over time, i\.e\., the mixing power curve \(MPC\), which reflects rheology\-related transitions and homogenization behavior\. However, current practice reduces this to a single end\-of\-mixing value, ignoring the information contained in the temporal evolution\. This study therefore proposes a long short\-term memory \(LSTM\) model to predict slump flow from the MPC augmented by mix composition data before the end of mixing\. The model was trained on a synthetic dataset comprising 500 randomized mix designs with mixing curves generated according to an empirically supported six\-stage mixing model\. Slump flow was assigned using a heuristic rule combining empirical relationships with mix proportions and an MPC\-derived process\-state descriptor\. Across all evaluated scenarios, the LSTM maintained high accuracy \( R 2 &gt; 0\.925 \)\. Without water\-content information, it retains an MAE of 1\.2 cm, 4\.1 cm lower compared to a baseline regression model, highlighting the information contained in the MPC beyond mix proportions and single end\-of\-mixing power values\. Within the simulated framework, the results show that the LSTM can reliably estimate fresh concrete workability during mixing, supporting its use as a soft sensor for online quality assessment based on the already available mixing power signal\.
