# Estimating bilateral hand kinetics from upper\-limb kinematics in sprint front crawl: a subject\-wise validation of an artificial neural network

**Autores:** Tianyi Gao; Shang Qi; Jiawen Li; Dongxiang Huang; Kaiqi Zheng; 李海侠; Jie Gao

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective Hand force contributes substantially to front crawl performance, but measuring it requires hand\-worn hardware\. We tested whether an artificial neural network driven only by three\-dimensional upper\-limb kinematics can reconstruct bilateral hand kinetics recorded by a hand\-force device, and at which level of the signal \(waveform, discrete descriptors, between\-swimmer and inter\-limb differences\) the estimates agree\. Methods Twenty\-four male competitive swimmers performed maximal 50\-m front crawl without breathing while wearing eo SwimBETTER handsets; 14 upper\-body markers were tracked by underwater motion capture at 100 Hz\. For each swimmer, the underwater phase \(fingertip entry to exit\) of each hand was analysed\. A laterally mirrored multilayer perceptron using 197 trunk\-frame features estimated six targets per hand under subject\-wise six\-fold cross\-validation, with architecture, regularisation and smoothing selected inside each fold\. It was compared with four alternative learned models and two baselines \(a scalar group\-mean null model and a phase\-conditioned mean\-waveform template\), and uncertainty was estimated with participant\-cluster bootstraps\. Results For resultant force, root\-mean\-square error was 24\.5 ± 6\.1 N \(left\) and 23\.3 ± 9\.9 N \(right\), per\-window correlations were 0\.85 and 0\.84, and ICC\(A,1\) was 0\.70 and 0\.64\. The network was comparable to gradient boosting and coordinate\-only models but was not more accurate than the template \(template RMSE 21\.8 and 21\.2 N; left\-hand difference 2\.7 N, 95% CI 0\.9–4\.5\)\. Between\-swimmer correlations of measured and estimated peak force, impulse and mean force ranged from −0\.36 to 0\.27, and the estimated inter\-limb symmetry index did not agree with the measured one \(r −0\.23 to −0\.10; the dominant side was matched in 50%–58% of swimmers\)\. Left\-hand peak force was underestimated by 32\.6 N, and the absolute error in time to peak averaged 12%–13% of the underwater phase\. Modelling both hands in every window, a broader definition that includes each hand’s recovery and entry, inflated apparent accuracy \(left RMSE 21\.6 N, r 0\.89\)\. Conclusion In a device\-on protocol, kinematics\-driven estimation recovered the group\-typical force–time pattern but added little to a phase template and did not resolve differences between swimmers or between limbs\. It cannot yet replace hand\-force measurement for individual assessment\.
