# Evaluating cytokine\-based therapeutics in human 3D in vitro immune\-containing assembloids

**Autores:** Nina Maenhoudt; Mechthild Lütge; Melanie Obenloch; Aljona Caironi; Maria Kiratzis; Pol Jean\-Mairet; SAMUELE GRAZIANO; Philipp Fröbel; Tamara Hüsser; Emilio Yángüez; Maria Antsiferova; Adrian M\. Filip; Christian Müller; Stéphane Leclair; Stephan Gasser; Pablo Umaña; Sara Colombetti; Floriana Cremasco; Samuele Calabrό

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Cancer immunotherapies require accurate preclinical models that recapitulate the complex cellular interactions within the human tumor microenvironment\. While patient\-derived organoids have emerged as powerful tools for cancer research, standard cultures lack the stromal and immune compartments necessary to evaluate immunotherapeutic mechanisms\. This limitation hampers the development of next\-generation targeted cytokine therapies\. Methods We applied a 3D immune\-containing colorectal cancer assembloid platform integrating patient\-derived organoids, cancer\-associated fibroblasts and allogeneic peripheral blood mononuclear cells within a collagen matrix\. We characterized cellular responses to interferon\-gamma \(IFNg\) and engineered IFNg receptor agonists targeted to fibroblast activation protein \(FAP\) through single\-cell transcriptomics, immunohistochemistry, cytokine profiling, and functional T\-cell migration assays\. Results The assembloid model successfully recapitulated key features of IFNg signaling, including tissue growth suppression, enhanced chemokine secretion \(CXCL10\), and CD8 \+ T\-cell recruitment\. Single\-cell transcriptomics revealed that fibroblasts were the most responsive cell population, undergoing a phenotypic transition from myofibroblasts toward antigen\-presenting CAFs \(apCAFs\), characterized by robust upregulation of MHC class II genes\. FAP\-targeted IFNg molecules exhibited attenuated activity compared to recombinant IFNg, successfully avoiding upregulation of the severe immunosuppressive checkpoint molecule PD\-L1\. Discussion This 3D assembloid model provides a physiologically relevant platform for dissecting complex tumor\-immune interactions and optimizing targeted immunotherapies\. This approach enables the study of epithelial\-stromal\-immune crosstalk in cancer and offers a robust preclinical framework for screening and mechanistically validating next\-generation targeted cytokine therapies\.
