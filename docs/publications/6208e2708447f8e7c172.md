# Resample the Answer: Where to Spend Test\-Time Compute in Recursive Reasoning

**Autores:** Maxime Illouz

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Recursive reasoning models such as the Tiny Recursive Model \(TRM\) iteratively refine two latent states: an answer state y, decoded into the output, and a reasoning state z, which is never decoded\. Many of their failures have been attributed to bad basins, regions of latent space that trap the recursion on wrong answers\. Recent test\-time approaches perturb y, z or both to recover these failures, but the impact of this choice on performance, test\-time compute efficiency and scaling properties remains unclear\. Through controlled experiments on TRM, we find that a failed trajectory carries its failure in its answer state\. At equal compute, in depth and in width, resampling y recovers more failures than any perturbation of z we tested, and the performance gap widens as the test\-time compute budget grows\. Our approach, Gated Answer Resampling \(GAR\), pairs this resampling of the answer state with the model&\#x27;s own halting head and restarts only the puzzles that the head rejects\. With at most 64 restarts per puzzle and no training or hyperparameter search, GAR takes TRM from 85\.9% to 99\.4% on Sudoku\-Extreme and from 68\.8% to 85\.6% on Pencil Puzzle Bench validation\. On the same checkpoints, GAR also outperforms Probabilistic TRM \(PTRM\), which perturbs z at each step, even when PTRM uses 42 to 197 times as much compute\.
