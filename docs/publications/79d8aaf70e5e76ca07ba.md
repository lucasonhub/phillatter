# Quantifying behavioral contributions to diabetes risk using an explainable multiclass machine learning framework with BRFSS 2024

**Autores:** Sumit Barua; Sohaib Jabran; Charles Rodgers; Alvis C\. M\. Fong

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract This study developed an explainable multiclass framework to evaluate discrimination among non\-diabetes, prediabetes, and diabetes using 2024 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System data \( $$n=453\{,\}241$$ \)\. A dual\-feature\-set design compared behavioral/health\-status predictors alone with an expanded set including demographic and anthropometric variables across four classifiers\. Logistic Regression Model\-2 showed the strongest class\-balanced performance \(macro\-F1 = 0\.426; balanced accuracy = 0\.512\), while Decision Tree Model\-2 achieved the highest prediabetes recall \(0\.338\)\. XGBoost Model\-2 achieved the highest weighted F1\-score \(0\.775\) and macro ROC–AUC \(0\.765\), and showed better probability calibration than Logistic Regression, but failed to identify prediabetes in the held\-out test set\. SHAP analysis of XGBoost Model\-2 showed that demographic predictors accounted for 45\.21% of total diabetes\-class mean absolute attribution, behavioral/health\-status predictors for 42\.71%, and anthropometric predictors for 12\.08%\. Exploratory geographic SHAP aggregation showed variation in model\-attributed signals, but these patterns should not be interpreted as population\-level prevalence, comparative risk, or causal geographic effects\. Overall, the findings show that behavioral/health\-status information remained a substantial component of diabetes\-class model attribution after demographic and anthropometric predictors were included, while also highlighting the importance of class\-balanced and class\-specific evaluation in multiclass diabetes prediction\.
