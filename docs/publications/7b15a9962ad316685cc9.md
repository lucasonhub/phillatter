# Constructing the algorithmic gaze: viewer positioning in AI\-generated cinematic images

**Autores:** Seda Aktaş

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examines how cinematic gaze is reconstructed within generative artificial intelligence systems\. It argues that AI\-supported image generation processes do not merely produce visual content, but also establish algorithmic regimes of spectatorship that organize the viewer’s position within the scene\. To investigate this process, the same cinematic scene was regenerated through differently structured prompts using Midjourney V6, Leonardo AI, and Stable Diffusion based systems\. The research combines a practice\-based approach with comparative visual analysis\. The generated images were analysed through three main categories: viewer\-addressing gaze, viewer\-aligned gaze, and external observer positioning\. Particular attention was given to facial orientation, gaze direction, camera alignment, and spectator positioning\. The findings demonstrate that generative AI systems tend to privilege frontal, highly readable, and viewer centred compositions\. The study also reveals that different platforms respond to identical prompts through distinct visual organizations, suggesting that image generation is shaped not only by user input but also by model\-specific aesthetic tendencies and learned visual biases\. In several cases, the systems maintained dominant visual patterns even when prompts explicitly attempted to redirect spectator positioning, indicating forms of prompt resistance and algorithmic preference\. The article argues that AI generated imagery should be understood not simply as aesthetic output, but as a post\-cinematic visual regime that reorganizes perception, spectatorship, and visual orientation through algorithmic processes\. By bringing together film theory, digital visual culture, and generative AI studies, the study aims to establish a theoretical bridge between cinematic gaze and algorithmic visuality\.\.
