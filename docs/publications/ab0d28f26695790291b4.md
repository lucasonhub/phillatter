# Predicting

**Autores:** T1\.Faker 

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Speech comprehension relies heavily on predictive processing, enabling listeners to predict upcoming information before it is heard\. While extensive research has demonstrated predictive processing at the linguistic level, it remains unclear whether listeners also anticipate properties of the upcoming speaker during speech comprehension\. The present study investigated whether listeners pre\-activate phonological information and speaker\-related information using electroencephalography \(EEG\) and representational similarity analysis \(RSA\)\. Sixty\-two native Mandarin speakers listened to four\-character Chinese idioms produced by one male and one female speaker\. We constructed three types of trial pairs: \(1\) phonology\-related pairs \(pairs that predict similar phonological information from distinct speakers\), \(2\) speaker\-related pairs \(pairs that predict the same speaker despite different phonological information\), and \(3\) unrelated pairs \(different phonological forms produced by different speakers\)\. Prior to the onset of the idiom\-final segment, both phonology\-related pairs and speaker\-related pairs elicited greater neural pattern similarity than unrelated pairs, with phonology\-related effects emerging earlier than speaker\-related effects\. These findings showed that listeners pre\-activated both phonological information and speaker\-related information before the onset of upcoming speech, with the phonological similarity effect emerging earlier than the speaker\-related effect\. This suggest that anticipatory representations during speech comprehension may involve multiple types of information\.
