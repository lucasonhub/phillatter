# Moritz, Elan: The Primes Room Challenge: A Protocol for Testing Machine Anticipation

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

In 1843 Ada Lovelace wrote that the Analytical Engine could follow analysis but had &quot;no power of anticipating any analytical relations or truths\.&quot; The remark, made famous by Turing as &quot;Lady Lovelace&\#x27;s Objection,&quot; survives today in the claim that artificial intelligence can correlate existing data but cannot create new data\. Existing attempts to test it, notably the Lovelace Test and Lovelace 2\.0, tie originality to what a designer cannot explain or to an evaluator&\#x27;s judgment, and so either cannot be passed by any program or are passed too easily\. This paper specifies a protocol that tests Lovelace&\#x27;s claim in her own terms\. An agent is placed in a declared room: the axioms of arithmetic and a definition of primality \(the Classical Room\), or an invented structure generated after every tested model&\#x27;s training cutoff \(the Alien Room\)\. Agents enter at five nested levels of access to a frontier language model\. Every output is verified by a proof\-assistant kernel, compared by independent referees with an anticipation list that the architects sealed by hash commitment before the run, and checked against a named and dated corpus\. The resulting Room Lovelace Criterion grades outputs as agent\-novel, architect\-novel, or community\-novel\. Four predictions are stated in advance, together with what would confirm or refute each\. The protocol is offered for independent use; no experimental results are claimed\.
