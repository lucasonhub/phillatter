# Quercetin attenuates polystyrene nanoplastic\-induced intestinal barrier injury through modulation of macrophage inflammatory responses

**Autores:** Jian Kang; Heng Yang; Yan Wang; Xin Zhang; Chunyang Zhu; Yanhong Zhou; 李艳琴

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Polystyrene nanoplastics \(PS\-NPs\) are emerging environmental contaminants that may impair intestinal barrier integrity, but the involvement of macrophage inflammatory responses remains incompletely understood\. Quercetin is a natural flavonoid with well\-established anti\-inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties\. This study investigated the role of macrophage inflammatory responses in PS\-NP\-induced intestinal injury and evaluated the protective effects of quercetin\. Methods Male ICR mice were orally exposed to PS\-NPs for 4 consecutive weeks, and the effects of quercetin intervention on colonic injury, inflammatory responses, tight junction\-associated proteins, and macrophage polarization markers were evaluated\. RAW 264\.7 macrophages were used to further examine PS\-NP\-induced inflammatory responses and the effects of quercetin in vitro \. An indirect Transwell co\-culture system of RAW 264\.7 macrophages and MODE\-K intestinal epithelial cells was established to assess macrophage\-mediated epithelial barrier alterations\. NF\-κB and STAT1 signaling activation was also examined\. Results PS\-NP exposure induced dose\-dependent colonic histopathological injury, increased the expression of the pro\-inflammatory cytokines IL\-1β, TNF\-α, and IL\-6, and reduced the expression of the tight junction proteins ZO\-1 and occludin\. PS\-NPs were also associated with a shift toward a pro\-inflammatory macrophage phenotype, characterized by increased CD86 and decreased CD206 expression\. Consistently, PS\-NPs promoted inflammatory responses in RAW 264\.7 macrophages and increased NF\-κB p65 and STAT1 phosphorylation\. In the Transwell co\-culture system, MODE\-K cells co\-cultured with PS\-NP\-stimulated macrophages exhibited reduced tight junction\-associated protein expression and disrupted junctional integrity\. Quercetin treatment attenuated PS\-NP\-induced colonic injury and inflammatory responses, partially restored ZO\-1 and occludin expression, modulated macrophage polarization\-associated markers, and reduced NF\-κB and STAT1 activation\. Discussion These findings indicate that 50 nm PS\-NP exposure induces intestinal inflammatory responses and barrier\-associated injury accompanied by pro\-inflammatory macrophage activation and altered NF\-κB/STAT1 signaling\. The co\-culture findings further support the involvement of macrophage\-derived inflammatory responses in epithelial barrier impairment\. Quercetin attenuates PS\-NP\-induced intestinal injury, potentially in part through modulation of macrophage inflammatory responses accompanied by reduced NF\-κB and STAT1 activation\.
