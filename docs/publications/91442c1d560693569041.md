# Cognitive\-congestion language profiling in ADHD: lexical signal auditing and negative\-manifold specificity testing

**Autores:** Sieun Ju

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

High machine\-learning accuracy in classifying ADHD\-related writing does not necessarily establish an ADHD\-specific linguistic phenotype, as predictions may reflect diagnostic vocabulary, medication references, or community\-specific topics rather than cognitive organization\. This study evaluated an interpretable cognitive\-congestion language profile \(LCCI\) and a negative\-signature framework for detecting disorder\-specific linguistic distinctiveness\. A dataset of 25,000 Reddit\-derived posts from ADHD, OCD, depression, PTSD, and Asperger/autism\-related communities was analyzed using condition\-name masking and author\-grouped cross\-validation\. Twenty\-five features measuring discourse structure, uncertainty, urgency, lexical diversity, and coherence were compared with TF\-IDF classification\. The equal\-weight LCCI composite performed near chance \(ROC\-AUC 0\.506\), while the multivariable profile achieved ROC\-AUC 0\.654, increasing to 0\.681 with learned weights\. Individual feature differences were small\. TF\-IDF substantially outperformed LCCI \(ROC\-AUC 0\.940\), with negligible improvement from combining them\. An audit classified 59 of 60 high\-weight TF\-IDF coefficients \(98\.3%\) as topic\-related, without quantifying their contribution to predictive performance\. To test specificity independently of supervised classification, the negative\-signature framework excluded each psychiatric community in turn, constructed a reference language manifold from the remaining communities, and measured residual distinctiveness using PCA reconstruction, shrinkage Mahalanobis distance, and isolation forest\. ADHD exhibited no positive residual signal \(ROC\-AUC 0\.451, 0\.419, and 0\.429, respectively\), while OCD showed the greatest residual separability\. These findings distinguish predictive discrimination, interpretable linguistic association, and disorder specificity\. The principal contribution is a reproducible, generalizable falsification framework demonstrating that strong supervised classification does not establish an ADHD\-specific linguistic phenotype and providing a means to evaluate candidate linguistic biomarkers before clinical translation\.
