# Smart Agriculture Research in Date Palm \(Phoenix dactylifera L\.\) Production: A Comprehensive Bibliometric Study

**Autores:** Sajid Safeer; Adnan Nadeem; Sajid Maqsood; Cataldo Pulvento

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The date palm \(Phoenix dactylifera L\.\) is fundamental to food security, livelihoods, and agro\-ecosystem sustainability across the Arabian Peninsula, yet its production is increasingly constrained by water scarcity, salinity, climate change, and biotic stresses\. Smart agriculture technologies offer opportunities to improve crop monitoring, resource\-use efficiency, and decision\-making; however, their application in date palm production remains fragmented and insufficiently validated\. This study evaluates the development, geographic distribution, technological focus, research approaches, and thematic structure of smart agriculture research on date palms in the Arabian Peninsula and other major date\-producing countries\. A bibliometric analysis was conducted using peer\-reviewed English\-language articles and reviews indexed in Scopus and Web of Science from 2016 to 2026, with 2026 representing a partial year\. Following screening and duplicate removal, 164 published studies were retained\. Publication output increased gradually, although the trend was not statistically significant \(β = 1\.25 articles year−1, p = 0\.068; Mann–Kendall τ = 0\.489, p = 0\.060\)\. The literature was concentrated in the Gulf Cooperation Council \(GCC\) region, with Saudi Arabia contributing 54 publications \(32\.9%\)\. General agronomy/other topics accounted for 46\.3% of publications, while remote sensing/UAVs \(17\.1%\), GIS/geospatial technologies \(14\.0%\), IoT/sensors \(13\.4%\), and AI/machine learning \(9\.8%\) represented the leading specific technology categories\. Water and irrigation management dominated thematic applications \(46\.3%\), whereas Smart Farming/IoT accounted for only 11\.0%\. Observational and monitoring studies comprised 50\.0% of publications, compared with only 6\.1% involving field experiments, indicating limited field\-scale validation\. Internationally co\-authored studies received significantly more citations than domestically authored studies \(16\.55 vs\. 8\.68; p = 0\.0007\)\. Thematic analysis identified four major clusters: geospatial monitoring, water management, pest control, and sustainability, with AI/machine learning showing marked growth after 2022\. Altogether, the field is expanding but remains fragmented, highlighting the need for integrated IoT and sensor networks, rigorous field trials, AI\-driven decision\-support systems, and stronger regional and international collaboration to enable scalable, context\-specific smart agriculture for date palm production\.
