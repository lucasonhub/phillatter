# Effects of packaging materials and storage temperature on the microbiological and physicochemical quality of garlic cloves during storage

**Autores:** Hany S\. El‐Mesery; Mansuur Husein; Zicheng Hu; Bongolwethu Mabusela; Patrick Berka Njobeh; Amer Ali Mahdi

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Garlic cloves \( Allium sativum L \.\) are susceptible to significant quality deterioration during post\-harvest storage\. This study evaluated four packaging materials \(waxed paper, mesh, low\-density polyethylene, and perforated low\-density polyethylene bags\) under refrigerated \(4 °C\) and ambient \(25 °C\) conditions for 30 days\. At 25 °C, garlic exhibited weight loss exceeding 18%, a decrease in firmness of more than 45%, and high microbial growth \(6\.5 log CFU g⁻¹\)\. In contrast, refrigerated storage restricted weight loss to below 7% and maintained firmness across all packaging types\. Allicin and pyruvic acid levels declined throughout the storage period, with storage duration exerting a more pronounced effect than packaging type\. Multivariate analysis revealed significant correlations among moisture loss, firmness reduction, and microbial proliferation, accounting for more than 85% of the total variance\. These findings underscore temperature control as the primary determinant of garlic quality during storage, with packaging playing a secondary role\.
