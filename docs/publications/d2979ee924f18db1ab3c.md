# Niami, Morteza: The Deferred Life

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-19 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

In this book, I develop a philosophical account of deferred living within the framework of Finite Continuance\. I begin with the proposition that human beings frequently organize their present existence around the anticipation of a future in which life will finally become possible\. Happiness is postponed until circumstances improve, freedom until fear diminishes, and fulfillment until the conditions of existence become sufficiently favorable\. I interpret this recurrent structure as a temporal and ontological problem rather than merely a psychological disposition\. My central argument is that the indefinite preservation of future possibilities can progressively consume the finite capacities required for their realization\. A person may maintain functional continuity while losing time, energy, attention, relational openness, and the capacity to reorganize commitments\. I distinguish between preserving the conditions of life and preserving the capacity to live through them\. Drawing on the concepts of provisional stabilization, constitutive exchange, erosion, reconfigurative capacity, modal openness, and the threshold of sufficiency, I examine how deferred living emerges through individual decisions, interpersonal expectations, institutional arrangements, and cultural narratives of future fulfillment\. I argue that the ethical problem of postponement concerns the distribution of finite capacities across time and the preservation of meaningful alternatives\. I conclude that a defensible orientation toward finite existence requires neither the abandonment of future planning nor the unrestricted pursuit of immediate satisfaction\. It requires a form oftemporal responsibility through which I can preserve the conditions of future agency while allowing the present to become a genuine field of existence\.
