# When Sparsity Breaks Connectivity: Functional Reachability and Guided Topology Dynamics in Extreme Sparse Training

**Autores:** Mohammad Pasande; Mateusz Jaworski; Jeroen Rook; Elena Mocanu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Dynamic Sparse Training \(DST\) periodically prunes and regrows connections, inducing a discrete dynamical mechanism over network topologies\. At extreme sparsity \($\\ge 99\\%$\), this dynamic tends to destroy input\-output information pathways, and performance with standard magnitude/random updates degrades sharply\. We formalize forward, backward, and functional reachability on layered sparse graphs, derive closed\-form expectations for reachable neurons and edges, and show that Monte Carlo realizations match the theory\. Guided by this reachability\-based structure, we bias pruning toward non\-functional edges and regrowth toward functionally consistent candidates\. We introduce this reachability\-driven modification of topology evolution as Reachability\-guided in DST, referred to as guided DST\. On CIFAR\-10/100 and Tiny ImageNet, guided DST recovers large accuracy gaps versus unguided DST \(up to $\+14\.4$ points at $99\.9\\%$ sparsity on CIFAR\-100\), and on MLP\-Mixer guided DST remains trainable at $99\.99\\%$ sparsity while unguided random regrowth degrades sharply\. These results cast extreme\-sparse training as a connectivity\-preserving dynamical process\. Our implementation is publicly available at https://github\.com/mzjaworski/ea\_dst\.
