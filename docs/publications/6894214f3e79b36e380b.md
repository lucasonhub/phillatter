# Theatrical Gnoseology: Resolving the Performative Paradox through an Event\-Based Model of Cognition

**Autores:** Alexander V\. Markov; Оksana A\. Shtayn

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The article provides a comprehensive justiﬁcation for the project of Theatrical Gnoseology \(TG\) – a new epistemological model developed in response to the fundamental performative paradox of classical theory of cognition, identiﬁed through an analogy with the paradox of L\. Wittgenstein’s «Tractatus Logico\-Philosophicus»\. The paradox lies in the impossibility of legitimising the criteria of cognition using the cognitive apparatus itself without transcending the very boundaries declared by these criteria\. We show that historical \(I\. Kasten\) and contemporary \(H\.\-T\. Lehman, K\. Barad, B\. Latour, D\. Haraway\) concepts of performativity allow not for eliminating this paradox, but for recognizing it as a symptom of the event\-based nature of cognition\. Within TG, cognition is reimagined as a post\-dramatic event \(epistemic event\), unfolding within a heterogeneous network of human and non\-human actors\. The key principles of TG – the rejection of representation in favor of performativity, distributed subjectivity, multimodality, and processuality – receive both philosophical grounding through a synthesis of analytical and continental traditions, and partial formalisation using the apparatus of graph theory and game theory\. Consequently, knowledge is deﬁned not as a reﬂection of reality, but as a stable conﬁguration of coordination within a dynamic network of actors, and truth – as an equilibrium state of this network\. The heuristic power of TG for analyzing modern complex epistemic practices is demonstrated through the practical example of «Climatic Knowledge as Performance»\.
