# Apollo: Bridging magnitude and sign\-based optimization for robust deep learning

**Autores:** Ali Zeydi Abdian; Mohammad Masoud Javidi; Najme Mansouri

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Adaptive optimization lies at the core of deep learning, driving performance across both computer vision and natural language processing domains\. However, widely used optimizers such as AdamW and Lion exhibit inherent trade\-offs between cautious stability and aggressive convergence, lacking intrinsic mechanisms to dynamically balance these properties\. Consequently, they can exhibit sensitivity to hyperparameter variations and training instability, particularly when applied to complex architectures like Vision Transformers\. To address these challenges, we introduce Apollo, a geometry\-aware hybrid optimizer that dynamically fuses Lion and AdamW updates through a step\-wise, cosine\-similarity\-guided policy\. Built upon the decoupled weight decay principle, Apollo acts as an adaptive gradient filter, integrating directional smoothing via an exponential moving average to achieve seamless transitions between magnitude\-aware and sign\-based optimization steps\. Theoretically, we establish Apollo’s convergence to a first\-order stationary point for non\-convex objectives\. Empirically, extensive experiments on modern architectures, including ResNet\-18 and DeiT\-Small on CIFAR\-100, as well as BERT\-base and DistilBERT on AG News, demonstrate that Apollo achieves competitive predictive performance compared to state\-of\-the\-art baselines in fine\-tuning scenarios\. Notably, Apollo exhibits low seed\-to\-seed variance \(e\.g\., σ 2 ≈ 1 0 − 6 on DeiT\-Small\) and robust behavior across different smoothing hyperparameters, reducing the reliance on extensive grid searches\. By formalizing a geometric approach to optimizer fusion, Apollo offers a mathematically grounded and memory\-efficient alternative for contemporary deep learning tasks, balancing measurable computational overhead with enhanced training reliability\. Code and additional results are available at https://github\.com/Aliyar4061/ApolloV2Optimizer/tree/main \.
