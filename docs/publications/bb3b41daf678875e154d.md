# Sparse\-TD\-JEPA: Improving Zero\-Shot Reinforcement Learning With Sparse Latent Task Representations

**Autores:** Duaa Nakshbandi; Zhiyu An; Wan Du

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Successor\-feature\-based zero\-shot reinforcement learning methods pre\-train a task encoder and successor\-feature predictor on offline, reward\-free data, then recover a policy at test time by solving a closed\-form linear regression over a small labeled dataset\. We identify an underexplored limitation of this approach: when the task encoder produces dense representations, the inferred reward signal becomes diffuse across the state space, reducing the contrast between goal\-relevant states and the background and providing a weaker signal for the policy to follow\. We address this limitation by introducing an l1 sparsity penalty that concentrates reward\-predictive structure into a smaller effective subspace of the task encoder&\#x27;s latent space\. The penalty is applied only during training and requires no architectural change or modification to the closed\-form inference procedure\. We instantiate this approach as Sparse\-TD\-JEPA, a single\-line of code addition based on Temporal\-Difference Joint\-Embedding Predictive Architecture \(TD\-JEPA\) that significantly improved its performance in zero\-shot reinforcement learning\. We evaluate Sparse\-TD\-JEPA on 65 tasks across the DeepMind Control \(DMC\) Suite and OGBench, covering locomotion, navigation, trajectory stitching, puzzle solving, and robotic manipulation\. Sparse\-TD\-JEPA improves over TD\-JEPA in all 13 domain\-level comparisons\. On DMC, it increases the average return from 661 \+\- 6 to 714 \+\- 3\. On OGBench, it increases the average success rate from 38\.0 \+\- 0\.8 to 48\.4 \+\- 2\.3, with improvements of 13\.4\-18\.0 percentage points on the evaluated antmaze navigation and stitching domains\. Overall, Sparse\-TD\-JEPA achieves the highest mean performance among established zero\-shot baselines in nine of the 13 domains\. Analysis further shows that sparsity produces a sharper and more task\-informative inferred reward landscape\.
