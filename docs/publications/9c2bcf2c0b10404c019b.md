# On the Performance Boundary of Fourier\-Enhanced Physics\-Informed Neural Networks for High\-Order Nonlinear Oscillators: A MEMS\-Motivated Numerical Case Study

**Autores:** Yin Bing\-yi; Chun‐Hui He; Ji‐Huan He

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Numerical prediction of high\-order nonlinear oscillators using conventional physics\-informed neural networks \(PINNs\) is severely hampered by spectral bias, which prioritizes low\-frequency components and leads to significant errors in capturing multi\-harmonic vibration responses\. This work is a numerical\-method\-oriented investigation rather than an experimental MEMS validation study\. To mitigate spectral\-bias issues, we employ a Fourier multiscale physics\-informed neural network \(FMPINN\), which integrates random Fourier feature mapping with a parallel multi\-sub\-network architecture\. We note that the individual building blocks of FMPINN are existing components in scientific\-machine\-learning literature, and our core contribution is not architectural innovation\. While this enhancement yields substantial accuracy improvements—reducing relative L2 errors by one to two orders of magnitude under weak\-to\-moderate nonlinearity—the central contribution of this work lies in the quantitative characterization of its applicability boundary\. Through six systematically designed numerical cases of a fifth\-order Duffing oscillator reduced\-order model motivated by MEMS resonator dynamics, we demonstrate that the performance of FMPINN saturates under strong nonlinear excitation\. Contrary to intuition, simply increasing the Fourier\-feature dimension cannot resolve this saturation\. Spectral analysis reveals that this bottleneck stems from the initialization\-determined finite effective bandwidth of the fixed, frozen frequency mapping, which induces spectral\-folding\-dominated artifacts in the presence of dense high\-order harmonics\. This provides a plausible mechanistic interpretation supported by our numerical experiments, rather than a mathematically proven fundamental bound\. Our findings establish a practically useful numerical benchmark for the nonlinear stiffness threshold of Fourier\-augmented PINN frameworks and underscore the need for adaptive, rather than fixed\-scale, frequency embedding strategies\. All evaluations are conducted against synthetic noise\-free Runge–Kutta 45 benchmarks; experimental MEMS measurement data are not used in the present work\.
