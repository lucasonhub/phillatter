# Optimizing Flood Early Warning Sensor Networks Using Hydrological Modeling and Machine Learning: A Semi\-Arid Basin Case Study

**Autores:** Mahtab Helmi; Francesco Cappelli; Mehdi Dastourani; Manfred Kleidorfer; Salvatore Grimaldi

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Flood early warning systems \(FEWSs\) in data\-scarce and rapidly responding catchments require monitoring networks that provide sufficient forecasting information with minimal operational cost\. This study evaluates the applicability of a data\-driven sensor\-placement framework to the semi\-arid Torghabeh River Basin in northeastern Iran, where flood\-producing tributaries affect the city of Torghabeh and downstream developed areas\. A 2000\-year stochastic rainfall series was generated to represent a wide range of hydrological conditions and was used to drive a basin\-scale HEC\-HMS model parameterized from catchment physiographic characteristics and available hydrological information\. After applying the adopted flood\-event extraction and filtering criteria, 466 relevant synthetic events were retained for the sensor\-placement analysis\. Sub\-basin hydrographs were then used as predictors in a Random Forest forecasting model, while permutation feature importance, SHAP, and derivative\-based importance were combined to identify the most informative monitoring locations\. For a 1 h lead time, the progressive predictor\-selection analysis identified five of 24 candidate sub\-basins as a parsimonious configuration associated with the performance plateau, corresponding to an approximately 80% reduction in monitoring locations\. A different but similarly parsimonious configuration emerged for a 2 h lead time, demonstrating that optimal sensor placement is lead\-time\-dependent\. These results demonstrate the potential of explainable machine learning tools to support parsimonious FEWS monitoring\-network design under the evaluated synthetic modeling conditions, while operational application requires further validation using independent observed hydrological data\.
