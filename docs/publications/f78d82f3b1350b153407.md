# &quot;Deep lifts&quot;: Neural networks can generalize even with the dumbest optimizer

**Autores:** Sunil Madhow; Yu\-Xiang Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Given a labeled dataset $\\\{\(x\_i, y\_i\)\\\}\_\{i = 1\}^n$, consider drawing a parameter vector $\\hatθ$ uniformly at random from those that interpolate the data within a width\-$W$ depth\-$D$ ReLU architecture with continuous weights bounded within $\[\-M, M\]$\. We study the generalization properties of $\\hatθ$ in the case where the dataset can, in fact, be explained by a smaller $w$\-by\-$d$ ReLU network\. Despite the obliviousness of the selection rule for $\\hatθ$, it adaptively generalizes at the rate $\\tilde O\\left\(\\frac\{wdWD\}\{n\}\\right\)$, rather than being governed by the VC dimension of the full $W$\-by\-$D$ hypothesis class\. The reason is due to a particular inductive bias in ReLU networks: the $w$\-by\-$d$ parameter \*witnesses\* an exponential volume of $W$\-by\-$D$ interpolating parameters, which dwarfs the volume of poorly generalizing parameters \(conditioned on interpolation\)\. We explicitly construct this set of near\-equivalent $W$\-by\-$D$ parameters by a proof technique called \*deep lifting\*, and then tie the achievable volume to stability properties of the $w$\-by\-$d$ witness\. Examples show that these techniques allow us to convert approximation\-theoretic results into generalization guarantees for nonparametric classes\. We further show that generalization of uniform\-random interpolators can survive label corruptions\.
