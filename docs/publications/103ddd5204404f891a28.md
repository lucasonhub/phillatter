# Genetic and Epigenetic Aspects of Urate Urolithiasis: The Role of Urate Transporters

**Autores:** Olga Yu\. Nesterova; Olga A\. Georginova; Габбасова Ляля Адыгамовна; Tatyana N\. Krasnova; Armais Albertovich Kamalov

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Interest in studying uric acid stems from its central role in the pathogenesis of a large number of chronic non\-communicable diseases\. Elevated uric acid levels are directly linked to an increased incidence of gout, nephrolithiasis \(10%–20% of cases\), chronic kidney disease, and cardiovascular events, myocardial infarction and stroke\. Uric acid can have dual effects: as an antioxidant at physiological concentrations and as a trigger for inflammation and oxidative stress when its concentration increases, leading to increased manifestations of atherosclerosis and metabolic syndrome\. It is known that renal transporters maintain the balance of urate reabsorption and secretion, but the mechanisms of their interactions, the role of rare genetic variants, and regulatory factors such as microRNAs remain poorly understood, limiting the potential for personalized therapy and prevention\. The novelty of this search and analytical work lies in the systematization of modern data on the molecular mechanisms of urate transport, including the analysis of mutations associated with hyperuricemia and hypouricemia\. This review examines in detail the key renal transporters: URAT1 \(SLC22A12\) and GLUT9 \(SLC2A9\) as the main systems of urate reabsorption in the proximal tubules; OAT1, OAT3, OAT4, OAT10 \(SLC22 family\) and ABCG2, responsible for the secretion of uric acid from cells into the tubular lumen; NPT1 and NPT4 \(SLC17A family\), located on the apical membrane of the proximal renal tubules and responsible for sodium\-phosphate cotransport\. The operation of urate “transportosomes” of the proximal renal tubules, which ensure efficient transport and metabolism of uric acid, is described\. Particular attention is paid to genetic variants: a common ABCG2 polymorphism \(rs2231142\), which increases the risk of hyperuricemia and urolithiasis, and rare mutations in URAT1 \(G774A, c\.1400CT\) and GLUT9 \(c\.374CT\), which lead to hypouricemia types 1 and 2 with the risk of acute renal failure under exercise\. The roles of microRNAs \(miR\-143\-3p, miR\-4770 for GLUT9; miR\-301a/b for URAT1\) in post\-transcriptional regulation and GWAS loci \(GCKR, KCNK5, etc\.\) associated with urate homeostasis disorders are analyzed\. The review provides a deeper understanding of the paradoxical nature of uric acid, reflecting its dual biological role: a powerful antioxidant at physiological concentrations and a trigger for inflammation and oxidative stress when its serum concentration increases\. A comprehensive consideration of urate transport mechanisms will reveal gaps in our understanding of key mutations associated with urate urolithiasis, as well as present current approaches to the management of patients with gout, urate nephrolithiasis, and hyperuricemia\.
