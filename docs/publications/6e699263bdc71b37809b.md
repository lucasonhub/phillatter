# Precision Plant Breeding: Integrating Genome Editing with Artificial Intelligence\- A Review

**Autores:** B L Santhosh; Nakka Amulya; Madhuri Singh; C\. Varshitha; Biju Sidharthan; Vijay Kumar; Prabha Banjarey; Udit Prakash

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Precision plant breeding seeks to link biological target discovery with the development and evaluation of useful alleles in crop improvement\. Genome\-editing platforms, including CRISPR–Cas nucleases, base editors, prime editors, multiplex editing and cis\-regulatory editing, enable targeted genomic alterations; however, their successful use depends on appropriate target choice, editing performance and phenotypic validation\. Artificial intelligence \(AI\) offers complementary approaches for integrating pangenomic, multi\-omics, phenotypic and environmental data to prioritise genes and regulatory sequences, predict guide\-RNA activity and off\-target effects, and support selection across genetic backgrounds and environments\. This review examines the potential of combining these methods within a design–build–test–learn framework that connects edit design with molecular verification, high\-throughput phenotyping, genomic prediction and conventional breeding operations\. Examples discussed include susceptibility\-gene and promoter editing for disease resistance, regulatory interventions associated with stress responses, nutritional modification and the emerging application of AI\-designed nucleases in rice\. Although these advances demonstrate technical feasibility, widespread implementation remains constrained by plant\-specific data scarcity, differences among genotypes and environments, transformation and regeneration bottlenecks, complex trait architecture, unintended editing outcomes and regulatory diversity\. The review emphasises prospective model validation, causal interpretation, pangenome\-aware design, rigorous molecular characterisation and replicated field evaluation\. It concludes that AI\-guided genome editing can support crop improvement when used alongside quantitative genetics, breeder\-defined objectives and responsible governance, rather than as a substitute for empirical breeding evidence\.
