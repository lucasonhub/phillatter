# Model\-based identification of medication\-associated fall\-risk signals using EMG time\-series analysis: an exploratory mechanistic approach in polypharmacy

**Autores:** 정우진; 신민주; Jiyeon Jeon; Soyoung Lee; Youngjin Moon; Jung\-woo Chae; Hwi\-yeol Yun

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Falls are the leading cause of functional decline in older adults\. Existing fall\-risk assessments often overlook the contribution of medications, and emerging sensor\-based methods predict who is likely to fall but rarely explain why\. We present a model\-based approach for identifying medication\-related fall\-risk signals using high\-frequency time\-series electromyography \(EMG\)\. Fifteen young and 15 older adults performed perturbation\-based balance tasks, and nonlinear mixed\-effects modelling with bootstrap\-assisted covariate selection linked the EMG responses of seven lower\-limb and trunk muscles to common medication classes\. The simulation\-derived co\-contraction index \(CCI\) served as the outcome, compared against a benchmark derived from Falk et al\. Through stepwise covariate modelling followed by bootstrap\-based confirmation, two drug classes were retained as robust covariates: anticoagulants, associated with a higher co\-contraction index, and statins, associated with a lower co\-contraction index\. In model\-based simulations, anticoagulant exposure corresponded to significantly elevated odds of exceeding the CCI benchmark, whereas statins did not increase the simulated odds despite their consistent covariate signal\. This exploratory, signal\-generating framework foregrounds the identification of medication\-associated neuromuscular changes from EMG and may provide a mechanistic context that complements conventional epidemiology\. A companion web application illustrates this approach as a supplementary tool to support medication reviews during fall\-risk evaluations\.
