# From Imaging Gatekeeper to Catheterisation Laboratory Co\-Pilot: The Emerging Role of AI in Interventional Cardiology

**Autores:** Kyvan Irannejad; Hassoon Sarwar; Joseph Thomas; Matthew Jay Budoff

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: AI is rapidly reshaping cardiovascular medicine, particularly within imaging\-intensive fields such as interventional cardiology\. Advances in machine learning and deep learning have enabled automated analysis of complex cardiovascular datasets and opened new opportunities for decision support across the coronary care continuum\. Objectives: This review summarises current and emerging applications of AI in interventional cardiology, spanning pre\-procedural imaging, intraprocedural guidance, outcome prediction, and future technological developments\. Key findings: In the pre\-procedural setting, AI\-enhanced coronary CT angiography, AI\-derived fractional flow reserve, and automated plaque characterisation improve risk stratification and patient selection for invasive angiography\. Within the catheterisation laboratory, AI applications include automated angiographic interpretation, intravascular ultrasound and optical coherence tomography analysis, lesion classification, optimisation of stent sizing and deployment, emerging roles in structural heart interventions, and periprocedural haemodynamic monitoring\. Machine learning models are also increasingly used for risk stratification, predicting bleeding, contrast\-induced nephropathy, and mortality after percutaneous coronary intervention\. Despite these advances, important challenges remain, including regulatory oversight, algorithm validation, bias mitigation, data governance, clinician education, and unresolved liability issues\. Conclusion: AI is evolving from a diagnostic imaging adjunct to a comprehensive decision\-support platform in interventional cardiology\. Emerging technologies such as real\-time AI\-assisted catheterisation laboratory systems, multimodal data integration, digital twin modelling, and AI\-guided robotic interventions may further transform coronary intervention workflows, positioning AI as a procedural co\-pilot that augments rather than replaces physician expertise\.
