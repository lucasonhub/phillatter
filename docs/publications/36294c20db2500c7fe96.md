# Energy Consumption Modeling and Forecasting for Medical AI Systems in Nigerian Hospitals: A Climate\-Calibrated Simulation Framework

**Autores:** Howard Chioma Chinagorom

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Increasing adoption of artificial intelligence \(AI\) in healthcare raises energy\-demand questions that are especially acute in resource\-limited regions\. Because operational, application\-level energy\-metering data for medical AI systems in Sub\-Saharan African hospitals are not yet publicly available, this study develops a climate\-calibrated simulation framework that couples real facility profiles \(bed capacity for six Nigerian teaching hospitals, from institutional and published sources\) with real long\-term climate normals \(temperature and humidity for each hospital city\) and a transparently parameterized medical\-AI workload overlay \(diurnal, weekly, and holiday patterns informed by prior literature\)\. Statistical models \(ARIMA, SARIMA, Holt\-Winters exponential smoothing\) and machine\-learning models \(a purpose\-built LSTM and a hybrid LSTM\-ARIMA\) were evaluated on the resulting 58\-month \(January 2020\-October 2024\) simulated series using a consistent walk\-forward, one\-step\-ahead protocol\. Seasonal statistical models achieved the lowest forecast error \(Holt\-Winters MAPE=0\.36%, SARIMA MAPE=0\.37%\), outperforming the hybrid LSTM\-ARIMA \(MAPE=0\.51%\) and a standalone LSTM \(MAPE=0\.61%\), while the hybrid was most accurate at very short \(1\-day\) horizons\. Diurnal peak\-hour \(0800\-1400\) demand exceeded off\-peak demand by 35%, and dry/harmattan\-season demand exceeded the annual mean by 1\.4\-5\.3% depending on institution and local climate\. Rather than asserting a single best algorithm, this study&\#x27;s central contribution is the framework itself: a reusable, real\-data\-calibrated method for generating defensible medical\-AI energy\-demand scenarios where metered data do not yet exist, together with comparative evidence that the relative advantage of statistical versus machine\-learning forecasting depends on how strongly deterministic the underlying seasonal structure is\. The framework and comparative findings offer a starting point for energy planning and policy\-making for AI\-enabled healthcare across Sub\-Saharan Africa \(SSA\), pending validation against real operational meter data\.
