# University physical education course experience and resilience\-based psychological recovery capacity among students in Chinese higher education: indirect associations through emotion regulation, exercise self\-efficacy, and peer connectedness

**Autores:** Wendong Yang; Liangfang Meng

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

University students face increasing psychological pressure, yet formal mental health services often remain limited in reach and depend on active help\-seeking\. This study examines university physical education \(PE\) as an educational context whose perceived qualities may be associated with psychological resources relevant to adaptation under stress\. Using cross\-sectional survey data from 831 Chinese college students, we examined whether perceived PE course experience was associated with resilience\-based psychological recovery capacity and whether this association was indirectly linked through emotion regulation, exercise self\-efficacy, and peer connectedness\. The retained measurement specification represented PE course experience as a second\-order construct comprising teacher support, mastery climate, embodied engagement, emotional release, and peer interaction\. Structural equation modeling indicated that PE course experience was positively associated with resilience\-based psychological recovery capacity and with all three proposed indirect\-linking variables\. Bootstrapping results supported significant indirect associations through emotion regulation, exercise self\-efficacy, and peer connectedness, with exercise self\-efficacy showing the largest estimated indirect association\. The association remained significant after controlling for perceived stress and extracurricular exercise frequency\. These findings link perceived PE course qualities with resilience\-based adaptive capacity, conditional on the measured covariates\. They do not isolate curriculum effects from physical\-activity exposure\. Because the design is cross\-sectional, the findings should be interpreted as theoretically grounded associations rather than causal effects\.
