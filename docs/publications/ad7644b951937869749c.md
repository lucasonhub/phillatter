# Associated factors and cultivation strategies of positive social mentality among college students in the new era

**Autores:** Nanqiu Peng; Xiaoli Lv; Li He; Fenghua Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background In the context of the new era, college students’ positive social mentality plays a pivotal role in their personal development and social harmony\. Objective This study aimed to examine the current status and correlates of positive social mentality among medical students, with a focus on gender, academic year, and socio\-contextual factors\. Methods A cross\-sectional survey was conducted with 123 undergraduate medical laboratory students using a self\-developed questionnaire with strong psychometric properties \(Cronbach’s α = 0\.968; KMO = 0\.883\)\. The instrument assessed eight dimensions of positive social mentality \(optimism, psychological resilience, self\-efficacy, social responsibility, growth mindset, sense of belonging, value identity, and emotional regulation\) and six categories of associated factors \(family, school, online ecology, peer relationships, employment pressure, and policy perception\)\. Data were analyzed using t\-tests, ANOVA, and standardized multiple regression\. Results No statistically significant gender difference was found in the overall index or in any of the eight dimensions \(all p ≥ 0\.208\), and no dimension differed significantly across academic years \(all p ≥ 0\.127\); on average value identity scored highest and growth mindset lowest\. Standardized multiple regression revealed that school education was by far the strongest positive predictor \(β = 0\.68\), followed by peer relationships \(β = 0\.18\), family factors \(β = 0\.17\) and policy perception \(β = 0\.16\), while online ecology was negatively associated \(β = −0\.14\) and employment pressure was not a significant predictor \(β = −0\.09, p = 0\.140\); the six factors together explained 81% of the variance \(R 2 = 0\.81\)\. Conclusion Positive social mentality among medical students is associated with multiple interrelated environmental and relational factors rather than with gender or academic year\. Targeted, multi\-level interventions addressing these factors are needed to foster students’ psychological well\-being and civic engagement\.
