# O DESENHO UNIVERSAL PARA A APRENDIZAGEM \(DUA\) NO ATENDIMENTO EDUCACIONAL ESPECIALIZADO EM ESCOLAS RURAIS

**Autores:** Renata Picoli Takamori Funada

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This article analyzes the implementation of Universal Design for Learning \(UDL\) as a fundamental pedagogical strategy within Specialized Educational Services \(SES\) in rural education contexts\. Introduction: School inclusion in peasant and rural areas faces significant historical challenges related to precarious infrastructure and geographic distance, requiring the adoption of innovative methodologies that allow for curriculum flexibility and the valorization of local identity\. Therefore, the main objective of this study is to investigate, through a systematic literature review, how the principles of UDL, specifically within the context of rural schools, can enhance the inclusion of students with disabilities and promote genuine curricular and pedagogical accessibility\. Methodology: This is a qualitative, descriptive, and bibliographic research project\. To establish the theoretical framework, the Google Scholar and SciELO databases were consulted using specific descriptors in Portuguese combined with Boolean operators, covering the publication period from 2016 to 2026\. Inclusion criteria focused on academic studies addressing the intersection of special education, neuroscience, and rurality, with data being analyzed through the content analysis method\. Results and discussion: The results indicate that UDL, by offering multiple means of representation, action, and engagement, significantly reduces the need for subsequent individual pedagogical adaptations and integrates rural and traditional knowledge into the teaching\-learning process\. The discussion emphasizes that the successful application of UDL in rural contexts requires robust and continuous teacher training, as well as the strategic use of low\-cost assistive technology resources that are deeply aligned with the students&\#x27; daily realities and cultural backgrounds\. Final considerations: It is concluded that UDL represents an emancipatory and transformative approach for rural schools, as it allows human diversity to be the primary starting point for all educational planning\. Furthermore, this approach ensures that the fundamental right to education is exercised with equity and social justice, respecting peasant cultural roots and effectively eliminating historical learning barriers that have long marginalized rural students with disabilities\.
