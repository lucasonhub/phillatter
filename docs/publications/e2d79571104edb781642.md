# Human Oversight Is Not Enough: Rethinking Accountability in AI\-Assisted Clinical Decision\-Making

**Autores:** Yangyi Ren

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Governance of artificial intelligence \(AI\) in clinical care often places clinicians between AI outputs and care decisions, yet a human signature can coexist with weak practical control\. This Perspective asks why participation may fail to produce accountable oversight, how duties should be distributed, and which institutional conditions make review meaningful and correctable\. Drawing on human\-factors research, sociotechnical governance, public\-accountability theory, and recent oversight proposals, we argue that nominal involvement cannot establish accountability when clinicians lack relevant information, time, authority, or workable review routes\. We define Accountable Human Oversight \(AHO\) as a normative institutional arrangement linking task\-specific human review to capability, authority, traceability, and redress\. These conditions overlap with prior frameworks, including a 2026 model\. AHO’s narrower proposed contribution is to connect them to actor–forum answerability and accessible clinical reconsideration, rather than to claim new oversight dimensions\. We outline risk\-proportionate governance that assigns review ownership, evidence, triggers, and corrective actions across developers and model suppliers, healthcare institutions, clinicians, and regulators, while reserving case\-level review for settings in which it can add value\. AHO has not been empirically evaluated and is not evidence of improved patient outcomes\. Future research should examine its feasibility, workload, equity, and effects\.
