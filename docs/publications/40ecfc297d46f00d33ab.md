# Smart\-Contract\-Enabled Diminishing Musharakah in Bangladesh: A Doctrinal\-Functional Analysis and Six\-Layer Control Architecture

**Autores:** Md\. Shahed Alamm; Ummey Sumaiya; Niger Sultana; Md\. Aminul Islam; Md\. Baharul Hasan

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose: Diminishing musharakah, known in Bangladesh as hire purchase under shirkatul melk \(HPSM\), links home and asset finance to shared ownership and gradual transfer\. Its integrity depends on keeping partnership, lease, purchase undertaking and title transfer distinct\. This paper asks which parts of the product can safely be executed by smart contracts and which controls are needed to keep an automated version valid under Shariah and Bangladeshi law\. Design/methodology/approach: The study uses doctrinal\-functional analysis\. It reads the relevant AAOIFI Shari’ah Standards \(No\. 9 on ijarah and No\. 12 on sharikah\), IFSB standards, Bangladesh Bank instructions and Bangladeshi statutes against the smart\-contract and law\-and\-technology literature, and classifies each lifecycle function by whether it is deterministic, dependent on off\-chain facts, or dependent on judgment\. Findings: Full automation is neither required nor advisable\. Ownership\-ratio arithmetic, rent and unit\-purchase calculation, notices and evidence logging suit automation\. Unit pricing policy, asset condition, title, arrears, destruction, hardship and dispute resolution require authorized human decisions\. The paper proposes a six\-layer architecture \(legal prose, Shariah parameter registry, executable code, oracle and evidence service, governance, and remedy\) and a ten\-state transaction model with guarded transitions\. Originality/value: The paper connects specific Shariah standard clauses to code\-level controls and to Bangladesh’s registration, electronic\-records and Shariah\-governance rules, a link missing from existing Islamic FinTech reviews\. Implications: Shariah boards should audit deployed code and event logs, not only product manuals, and regulators should require parallel running and pause\-and\-correct capability before approving automated HPSM\.
