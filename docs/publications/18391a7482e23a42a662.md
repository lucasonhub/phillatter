# Latent class analysis identifies three biologically distinct phenotypes among patients undergoing endovascular thrombectomy: a derivation and temporal validation study

**Autores:** Li\-Li Yang; Chao Chen; Lili Sun; Mengxia Li; Li Han

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Despite successful vascular recanalization, approximately half of patients undergoing endovascular thrombectomy \(EVT\) fail to achieve functional independence at 3 months\. The biological determinants of this heterogeneity remain poorly characterized\. Methods Latent class analysis \(LCA\) was applied to 825 consecutive EVT\-treated patients \(development cohort, January 2021–December 2024\) using 12 routine post\-procedural clinical and laboratory indicators\. Phenotype associations with favorable 3\-month outcome \(modified Rankin Scale 0–2\) were assessed using multivariable logistic regression\. Reproducibility was evaluated in an independent temporal validation cohort \( n = 211\)\. Results LCA identified three phenotypes\. The Low\-burden phenotype \(Class 1, 44\.4%\) had the lowest inflammatory and coagulatory burden and achieved favorable outcome in 70\.8% of patients\. The Metabolic phenotype \(Class 2, 42\.7%\), characterized by hyperglycemia and elevated neutrophil\-to\-lymphocyte ratio, had a favorable outcome rate of 44\.9% and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage \(sICH\) rate of 7\.1%\. The hypercoagulable phenotype \(Class 3, 13\.0%\), defined by markedly elevated D\-dimer and atrial fibrillation, had a favorable outcome rate of 40\.2% and sICH rate of 8\.4%\. After multivariable adjustment, both Class 2 \(OR 0\.49, 95% CI 0\.35–0\.69\) and Class 3 \(OR 0\.41, 95% CI 0\.25–0\.67\) were independently associated with unfavorable outcome \(both p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Class distribution and outcome associations were reproduced in the validation cohort\. Conclusion LCA identified three biologically distinct phenotypes among EVT\-treated patients with divergent functional outcomes\. Multicenter prospective validation is needed before these findings can inform clinical practice\.
