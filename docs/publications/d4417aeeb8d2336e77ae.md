# Water and carbon fluxes in vineyards under climate change – A review of Eddy\-Covariance studies currently dominated by irrigated Mediterranean systems

**Autores:** Julien Giraudo; Julien Crétat; Mathieu Thévenot; George G\. Burba; Olivier Mathieu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Viticulture faces a double challenge under climate change: optimizing water use and contributing to the sequestration of atmospheric CO2\. This review synthesizes 25 years of advances in flux stations’ measurements of water vapour and carbon fluxes in vineyards using the eddy\-covariance \(EC\) method, based on a corpus of 92 articles focused on evapotranspiration \(ET\) and Net Ecosystem Exchange \(NEE\)\. Most studies show a strong influence of climate on ET and NEE annual cycle variability, especially global radiation and vapour pressure deficit\. Evapotranspiration\-based studies \(81% of the corpus\) mainly focus on ET partitioning in irrigated vineyard plots of arid regions\. These studies show that ET is primarily driven by transpiration, with contributions ranging from 56% to 86%\. Several studies indicate that precise monitoring of ET can help optimize irrigation and thereby reduce water stress\. A wide variety of instruments and models were validated via flux station ET measurements, but heat advection from neighboring areas, very common in viticultural regions, generates uncertainties in ET for many such alternatives\. Available NEE\-based studies \(13% of the corpus\) were mainly conducted in Italy\. Amongst them, to our knowledge, only five sites reported complete annual NEE, yielding an average of \-135\.7 g C m\-2 yr\-1\. This suggests that Mediterranean vineyards act as moderate carbon sinks, but the agricultural lateral fluxes of carbon, such as harvested grapes, need to be taken into account to reliably assess the vineyard&\#x27;s true potential for carbon uptake\. About 5% of the corpus focuses on the Water Use Efficiency \(WUE\), i\.e\., the ratio between biomass produced and volume of water consumed by the vineyard\. The WUE is particularly relevant for understanding the vine biophysical drivers of carbon and water fluxes, but the diversity of methods used to assess WUE impairs comparisons, calling for developing standardized approach\. The scientific community needs to build a vineyard flux network, an EC flux station network dedicated to vineyard research under different pedoclimate contexts, to further understand ET and NEE dynamics and drivers, as well as the influence of viticultural practices for identifying the best way for winegrowers to enhance carbon uptake while reducing vineyard water consumption\.
