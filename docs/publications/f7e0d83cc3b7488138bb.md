# Preliminary evaluation of ProSpare®, a rectal obturator for prostate bed radiotherapy within the randomised multicentre POPS trial \(NCT02978014\)

**Autores:** Sarah Stewart; Karen Thomas; Jenni Parmar; Alison Claire Tree; Chris C\. Parker; Helen McNair; Sophie Alexander; Julia Henderson; James Talbot; Christopher Scrase; Chee Goh; Reena Davda; Emma Gray; Peter Hoskin; Sarah J Needleman; Jenny Nobes; Kamalram Thippu Jayaprakash; Yvonne Rimmer; Ranachandran Venkitaraman; Anna Wilkins; Vincent Khoo; Julia Murray; David Paul Dearnaley

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background and purpose The introduction of volumetric modulated arc therapy \(VMAT\) has enabled dosimetric improvements during prostate bed \(PB\) radiotherapy; however, the planning target volume \(PTV\) margins remain substantially greater than for prostate radiotherapy\. New approaches are required to further optimise rectal dose and minimise target and organ motion\. ProSpare ® , a self\-inserted rectal obturator designed to facilitate daily image guidance, may enhance radiotherapy accuracy and reduce treatment\-related morbidity\. Materials and methods Patients scheduled to receive adjuvant or salvage PB radiotherapy after radical prostatectomy were randomised to either standard\-of\-care \(SOC\) PB\-IMRT using centre\-standard PTV margins and verification protocol \(SOC\) or PB\-IMRT with daily ProSpare ® \-guided radiotherapy using a reduced 5\-mm posterior margin\. Dose and fractionation schedules were selected by each centre according to protocol recommendations\. The primary endpoint is patient\-reported EPIC bowel function and bother at 2 years\. Secondary endpoints include acute Radiation Therapy Oncology Group \(RTOG\) and CTCAE toxicity, patient\-reported outcomes, device tolerability, and anorectal dosimetry\. We report the results from the prespecified analysis of these secondary endpoints\. Results Between 2017 and 2023, 162 patients were recruited; 158 began radiotherapy and were evaluable at 18 weeks\. Acute toxicity data were available for 146 patients \(SOC 76/81, ProSpare 70/77\)\. Compared with SOC, ProSpare ® was associated with lower 18\-week RTOG ≥grade 1 gastrointestinal \(GI\) and genitourinary \(GU\) toxicity \(39% vs\. 31% and 37% vs\. 29%, respectively\)\. The incidence of worst CTCAE grade 2 GI and GU toxicity was &lt;6%\. Of 51 responders, 50 found ProSpare ® tolerable and would recommend its use\. Use of ProSpare ® resulted in consistently lower ano\-rectal dose–volume parameters\. Conclusion ProSpare ® \-guided prostate bed radiotherapy \(PBRT\) resulted in lower acute RTOG bowel toxicity at all time points\. Patient\-reported tolerability was very favourable\. Use of ProSpare ® improved rectal and anal canal dosimetry\. Clinical Trial Registration ClinicalTrials\.gov , identifier NCT02978014\.
