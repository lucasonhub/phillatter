# Exponential advantage in continuous\-variable quantum state learning

**Autores:** Eugen Coroi; Changhun Oh

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

We study how entangled measurements, reflected states, and input\-state classicality determine the sample complexity of learning bosonic continuous\-variable quantum states through their characteristic function\. We first analyze a simple entanglement\-free protocol and show that its sample complexity is exponential in the number of modes n for general inputs\. We then prove that this exponential scaling is intrinsic through three complementary lower bounds: one excluding entangled measurements, one for reflection\-symmetric inputs, and one excluding reflected states even when entangled measurements on finitely many copies are allowed\. Next, we show that the classicality parameter s max quantitatively determines when this barrier can be softened: positive classicality can reduce the entanglement\-free sample cost, and for fully classical states heterodyne learning matches the ϵ \-scaling of a Bell\-measurement \(BM\) scheme\. Finally, combining reflected states with a two\-copy entangled measurement yields sample complexity independent of n \. These results identify when entanglement\-free strategies already suffice and when, within the resource models analyzed here, efficient continuous\-variable state learning requires the full BM resource combination of entangled measurements and reflected\-state access\.
