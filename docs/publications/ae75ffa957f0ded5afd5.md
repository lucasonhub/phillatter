# A comparative analysis of trade exposure and human capability in Indonesian growth and emissions dynamics

**Autores:** Muhammad Hasan Imaduddin; Soumya Basu; Hideyuki Okumura

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Understanding whether economic growth can be decoupled from environmental pressure is central to the energy–economy–environment nexus \(3E nexus\)\. Evidence remains limited for economies that combine developing\-country growth pressures, net energy\-exporting status, and structurally complex transition challenges, such as Indonesia\. This study compares two alternative macro\-structural configurations within a unified 3E system: an export\-inclusive specification containing energy exports and an HDI\-inclusive specification containing human development\. A Vector Error Correction Model is estimated using quarterly data from 2000Q1 to 2022Q4, complemented by reduced\-form impulse responses and Granger\-predictive tests\. In the main quarterly estimates, the GDP\-related level\-feedback term is positive in the export\-inclusive specification and negative in the HDI\-inclusive specification; the descriptive sign contrast indicates sensitivity to model specification\. Across both specifications, electricity and non\-electric energy use remain important emissions constraints\. In Indonesia, these constraints operate within a fossil\-intensive energy sector characterized by strong state involvement and regulation across electricity and fuel supply\. This combination resembles evidence from many fossil\-resource exporters and ASEAN peers, where capability gains can coexist with carbon lock\-in; some developed economies, by contrast, have recorded emissions declines alongside a lower fossil share of energy and reduced final energy use\. Policy should therefore channel capability gains, export revenues, and capital formation toward power\-sector decarbonization, reduced direct fossil\-fuel use, and cleaner productive investment, while accounting for Indonesia’s energy\-sector governance and infrastructure constraints\.
