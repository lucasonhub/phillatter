# Rethinking AI integration in African higher education: a mixed\-methods investigation of capacity, equity, and governance across sub\-Saharan Africa

**Autores:** J\. N\. A\. Aryee; P\. Davies; G\. A\. Torsah; M\. M\. Apaw; S\. M\. Mwando; C\. Kwisanga; C\. D\. Boateng; Eric Ouma Jobunga; D\. Mutale; Leonard  Kofitse Amekudzi

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The Revolutionizing Tertiary Education for Africa&\#x27;s Thriving AI Economy and Workforce \(RetAIn\) project presents a comprehensive, multi\-method investigation into the conditions, constraints, and catalysts shaping Artificial Intelligence \(AI\) adoption across African Higher Education Institutions \(HEIs\)\. Deploying both qualitative multi\-stakeholders Focus Group Discussions \(FGDs\) from three African countries \(Ghana, Rwanda, Namibia\) and a quantitative cross\-country survey across five Sub\-Saharan African nations \(Ghana, Rwanda, Namibia, Kenya, and Zambia\), this study produces a richly triangulated account of AI integration dynamics on the continent\. The FGD strand revealed a stark divergence in national AI trajectories: Rwanda operates under a strong top\-down policy vision hindered by institutional rigidity; Namibia exhibits pragmatic institutional adaptability constrained by geographic and economic smallness; and Ghana demonstrates emergent, bottom\-up innovation within a national policy vacuum\. The survey strand, drawing on 156 responses across five countries, corroborated these qualitative tensions, documenting widespread recognition of AI&\#x27;s strategic importance alongside limited structured engagement, persistent infrastructure deficits, and marked equity barriers, particularly for women, vulnerable groups, rural students, and part\-time workers\. From both methods, a unifying finding emerges: AI integration in African HEIs consistently fails when technical adoption outpaces social, pedagogical, and governance co\-evolution\. The study argues for context\-specific, socio\-technically grounded strategies that pair curriculum co\-design with robust industry partnerships, that centre equity and cultural relevance, and build governance frameworks capable of guiding, rather than chasing, technological change\.
