# Lightweight, interpretable acoustic emission diagnostics for multi\-mode damage in bonded CFRP joints using consensus\-guided machine learning

**Autores:** Yingshi Hu; Qian Li; Weiwei Li; Zongyang Liu; Dingcheng Ji; Baoning Chang; Marcus  H\. N\. Yio; Jing Lin

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Adhesively bonded carbon fibre\-reinforced polymer \(CFRP\) joints exhibit complex, multimode damage involving concurrent matrix cracking, debonding, delamination and fibre breakage, which challenges acoustic\-emission \(AE\)\-based diagnosis under industrial hit\-triggered acquisition\. This study is framed as a focused methodological investigation on a single\-lap bonded CFRP joint system, rather than as a claim of universal damage\-frequency thresholds\. The proposed workflow uses an offline consensus procedure to derive structure\-specific operational passbands from standard AE descriptors, then uses these fixed passbands to build compact band\-energy and entropy features for interpretable, online\-oriented classification\. AE waveforms from three nominally identical tensile tests are first represented by 18 conventional descriptors\. Multiple clustering families and random seeds are combined through a co\-association matrix to obtain a robust four\-cluster macro\-partition\. High\-confidence samples within each macro\-cluster are then used to define four operational passbands from central peak\-frequency quantiles\. These passbands are frozen and used to construct an extended 26\-dimensional representation that augments the baseline descriptors with band\-energy shares and entropy measures\. A second consensus step in the extended feature space provides 15 operational AE reference states that describe single\-dominant and spectrally mixed acquisition windows\. These states are used to train an XGBoost surrogate evaluated on a fully held\-out experiment\. The extended representation improves test accuracy from 0\.68 to 0\.91 and increases macro\-F1 and macro\-AUROC to about 0\.88 and 0\.90, respectively\. SHAP analysis indicates that the classifier relies primarily on peak frequency, passband energy and entropy descriptors\. The results demonstrate a trade\-off between offline robustness and online simplicity: the numerical passbands are system\-dependent and require commissioning for new structures, whereas the deployed decision path remains lightweight after calibration\.
