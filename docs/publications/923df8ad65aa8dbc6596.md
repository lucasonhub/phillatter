# Multi\-year temporal and seasonal abundance patterns of common bottlenose dolphins near Sarasota, Florida

**Autores:** Christina N\. Toms; Krystan A\. Wilkinson; Jason B\. Allen; Aaron A\. Barleycorn; Katherine McHugh; Randall S\. Wells

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Long\-term demographic monitoring is critical for understanding population stability and responses to environmental disturbance\. Bottlenose dolphins \( Tursiops truncatus \) inhabiting Sarasota Bay, Florida, have been studied by the Sarasota Dolphin Research Program since 1970\. While most dolphins in the resident community are individually identifiable via photographic\-identification \(photo\-ID\), direct census datasets are largely unavailable elsewhere\. A previous Sarasota study used capture\-mark\-recapture \(CMR\) surveys and robust\-design models to estimate abundance across four seasons in 2015\. This study updated those estimates and generated the first multi\-year abundance estimates for dolphins inhabiting Sarasota and Little Sarasota Bays using a robust design modeling framework\. Synoptic photo\-ID surveys conducted during 2013–2022 were used to estimate abundance, apparent survival, temporary emigration, and detection probabilities across 40 primary periods\. Under the original AICc analysis, model selection favored those incorporating seasonal variation in apparent survival; strong non\-random availability \(γ′ = 0\.89\); transience effects on temporary emigration \(presumed residents: γ″ = 0\.05; presumed non\-residents \(γ″ = 0\.32\)\); and strong heterogeneity in detection probabilities among primary periods and mixture groups \(i\.e\., presumed residents and non\-residents\)\. Sensitivity analyses assuming up to ĉ = 4 shifted support toward simpler models\. Temporary emigration and survival estimates remained stable but marked\-animal abundance estimates were sensitive to assumed overdispersion\. Based on AICc, N total varied from \~94 \(winter:2013; SE = 12\.68; 95% CI 72\-122\) to 204 individuals \(summer:2014; SE = 22\.85; 95% CI 164\-254\)\. Seasonal abundance generally increased from winter \(mean 139\.3, SD = 20\.1\) into spring \(mean = 159\.2, SD = 9\.2\) or summer \(mean = 167\.3, SD = 26\.5\) before declining again in fall \(mean = 159\.1, SD = 8\.2\)\. Annual means ranged from 123 in 2013 to 172 in 2020; the 10\-year mean was 156\.23 \(SE = 23\.38; 95% CI 117\-209\)\. Comparison with previous estimates indicated broad agreement in population size\. Results supported a relatively stable resident core community, with seasonal movement driving fluctuations in estimates of apparent survival and abundance\. Findings underscore the importance of accounting for movement and heterogeneity in CMR analyses and highlight the strengths and limitations of robust design approaches for understanding population dynamics in resident dolphin communities\.
