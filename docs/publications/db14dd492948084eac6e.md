# Fair Credit Decision Assurance: An Ethical Governance Framework For AI\-Based Credit Scoring in Bangladesh with Islamic Finance Duties

**Autores:** Sumaia Yasmin Keya; Niger Sultana; Md\. Baharul Hasan; Md\. Shahed Alamm; Md\. Aminul Islam; Ummey Sumaiya

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose: Machine\-learning credit scoring can extend formal credit to people with thin files, but alternative data can also turn social disadvantage into apparently neutral risk scores\. This paper develops a governance framework for AI\-based credit scoring in Bangladesh and adapts it to the duties of Islamic financial institutions\. Design/methodology/approach: The study is a structured integrative review combined with policy and doctrinal analysis\. It synthesizes peer\-reviewed research on algorithmic lending, fairness and explainability; international benchmarks \(the EU AI Act, U\.S\. adverse action guidance, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and the OECD AI Principles\); Bangladeshi instruments on e\-KYC, credit information, digital nano\-loans, e\-loans and data protection; and Islamic finance sources on maqasid al\-Shariah, gharar and Shariah governance\. Findings: Bangladesh now permits fully digital small\-value lending, yet its current instruments say little about model validation, bias testing, reason\-giving or contestation\. Predictive accuracy alone is an inadequate standard because a model can improve portfolio performance while concentrating false rejections on women, rural applicants or new\-to\-credit borrowers\. Originality/value: The paper proposes a Fair Credit Decision Assurance \(FCDA\) model with six gates: lawful data, feature relevance, fairness testing, explanation, human reconsideration and monitoring\. A Shariah overlay adds product\-level review and treats unexplained adverse decisions as a governance concern related to gharar\. Practical implications: The model gives banks, finance companies, mobile financial service providers and Bangladesh Bank a staged route to supervising algorithmic credit without halting innovation\.
