# Dietary elimination of gluten attenuates amyloid\-β and tau pathology in Alzheimer’s disease\-like in aluminum chloride\-induced rats: an experimental animal study

**Autores:** Ghada Abdelaziz; Abdel\-Rahman B\. Abdel\-Ghaffar; Dina Amr; Ahmed Mohamed Kamal Nada; Refaat M\. Gabre

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract This study evaluates the comparative neuroprotective efficacy of a multi\-component dietary pattern \(the Mediterranean\-DASH Intervention for Neurodegenerative Delay\) versus a single\-component elimination strategy \(gluten\-free diet\) in an AlCl₃\-induced rat model of Alzheimer’s disease\. Sprague–Dawley rats were randomly divided into six groups \(n = 6 each\) and fed a balanced diet, the MIND diet, or a gluten\-free diet, with or without AlCl₃ \(100 mg/kg/day\) administered orally for 8 weeks\. AlCl₃ administration significantly elevated Aβ, total tau, and p\-Tau S231 compared to non\-induced controls \( P &lt; 0\.001 for all AD effects\)\. Conversely, all three diets differed significantly from one another for total tau and p\-Tau S231 \( P &lt; 0\.05 for all comparisons\)\. Under AD\-inducing conditions, the MIND diet significantly reduced cortical Aβ and total tau by 41\.9% and 22\.7%, while the gluten\-free diet significantly reduced them by 50\.3% and 41\.6%, relative to the AD disease\-control group \(all P &lt; \.001\)\. Histopathologically, both diets preserved cortical and hippocampal architecture, but behaviorally, diet showed no significant effect on the novel object recognition discrimination index \( P = 0\.092\)\. Both the MIND and gluten\-free diets successfully attenuated Aβ accumulation, tau pathology, and neurodegeneration in AlCl₃\-induced AD models, with the gluten\-free diet yielding greater numerical reductions\.
