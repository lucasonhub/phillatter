# AI\-supported first consultation preparation in oncology: a real\-world clinical usability study

**Autores:** Josephine van Koevorden; Rachael Mertooetomo; David Neal; Bilge Çelik; Veerle Struben; Nanne Aben; Niels van den Hork; Vincent van der Noort; Marene Kroon; Luc H\. E\. Karssemakers; Ludi Smeele; Michel W\. Wouters; Richard J\. Dirven

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Large Language Models \(LLMs\) may support clinicians by facilitating information management, but their usability in real\-world oncology practice remains understudied\. This study evaluated the workflow performance of Artificial Intelligence\-based First Consultation Preparation \(AI\-FCP\), generating structured summaries from unstructured clinical documentation, co\-created with end users\. In a prospective single‑group pre‑post design, nine clinicians completed a person\-level baseline, followed by 33 consultations with AI\-FCP\. Case numbers varied by clinic scheduling and enrollment timing, potentially introducing survivorship bias\. Usability was evaluated using validated questionnaires and study\-specific instruments\. Post AI\-FCP, overall perceived workload \(NASA\-TLX\) was 28\.9% lower than baseline \(Hodges\-Lehmann estimate difference −12\.3; 95% CI –21\.7 to –3\.3\), with self\-reported average time savings of 3\.26 min \(SD = 2\.65\) per case\. Preparation succeeded in 84\.9% of consultations; 63\.6% of summaries were complete\. These exploratory findings suggest clinicians perceived lower workload and reported improved workflow efficiency with AI\-FCP\. Further research is needed to determine generalizability\.
