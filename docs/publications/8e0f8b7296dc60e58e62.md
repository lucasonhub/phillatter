# Building\-Held\-Out Validation of Thermal\-Sensation Prediction: Implications for Building Design and Operation Across 554 Buildings

**Autores:** Qian Zhang; Yikang Sun

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Machine\-learning models used to inform building thermal\-environment design and operation are often assessed by random record splits, whereas application in a new building needs an unseen\-building test\. We analyzed 49,231 thermal\-sensation records from 554 buildings in the ASHRAE Global Thermal Comfort Database II\. With fixed configurations, record\-wise five\-fold validation favored extremely randomized trees \(ET; record\-weighted mean absolute error \[MAE\] 0\.806\), while building\-held\-out validation favored histogram gradient boosting \(HGB; 0\.883 versus ET 0\.902\)\. For HGB, building\-held\-out MAE exceeded record\-wise MAE by 0\.061 sensation units \(95% building\-cluster bootstrap interval 0\.044–0\.081\)\. In a separate nested four\-candidate\-per\-model sensitivity analysis, building\-held\-out ET and HGB MAE were 0\.889 and 0\.885; their paired difference was 0\.004 \(95% interval −0\.003–0\.011\), and their building\-macro MAEs were likewise close\. PMV had MAE 1\.044 on 33,791 matched records, compared with 0\.899 for HGB retrained solely on PMV\-available training records; availability and study\-source concentration limit generalization\. An exploratory same\-building offset sensitivity found that 1–5 calibration labels worsened error; 20 labels offered only a modest mean benefit across eligible buildings, with a majority of buildings worsening\. The findings support matching validation units and evidence coverage to the intended building decision\. Energy savings, architectural design alternatives, and closed\-loop control were not evaluated\.
