## Resumo

BACKGROUND: The Huntington&\#x27;s Disease Integrated Staging System \(HD\-ISS\) provides a framework for staging HD but does not incorporate fluid biomarkers\. OBJECTIVE: The aim of this study was to examine neurofilament light chain \(NfL\) and mutant huntingtin \(mHTT\) and total tau across HD\-ISS stages and their associations with genetic burden, neuroimaging, and clinical measures\. METHODS: We performed cross\-sectional analysis of the Barcelona\-Huntington&\#x27;s Disease \(BCN\-HD\) cohort with external replication in the multicenter HDClarity study\. HD gene expansion carriers were classified as HD\-ISS stages 0 to 3\. NfL \(cerebrospinal fluid \[CSF\] and plasma\), CSF mHTT, total tau, striatal volumes, and Unified Huntington&\#x27;s Disease Rating Scale measures were quantified\. Group differences and associations were analyzed using adjusted models\. RESULTS: The BCN\-HD cohort included 78 participants \(50 HD gene expansion carriers \[HDGECs\], 28 controls\) and HDClarity included 218 participants \(166 HDGECs, 52 controls\)\. CSF NfL increased across HD\-ISS stages and discriminated premanifest from manifest individuals, as well as HD\-ISS stage 0 from stage 1\. Plasma NfL showed parallel increases and strongly correlated with CSF NfL \(ρ = 0\.88, P &lt; 0\.001\)\. More than half of HD\-ISS 0 individuals had NfL levels above the age\-adjusted 95th percentile \(P95\) and shorter predicted time to onset \(13 years for CSF and 10 years for plasma\)\. From HD\-ISS 1 onward, NfL levels exceeded P95 in most individuals\. Higher NfL concentrations were associated with genetic burden, striatal atrophy, and clinical measures \(all P &lt; 0\.001\)\. CSF mHTT was detectable from HD\-ISS 0 but showed weaker associations, whereas CSF total tau increased only from HD\-ISS 3\. CONCLUSIONS: NfL identifies biological activity across the HD\-ISS continuum and shows neuroaxonal injury in a subset of individuals classified as stage 0\. These findings suggest that fluid biomarkers may complement current HD\-ISS criteria and support biological stratification in observational studies and clinical trials\. © 2026 The Author\(s\)\.
