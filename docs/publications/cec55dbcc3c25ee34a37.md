# Analysis of post\-adoption behaviors of climate change adaptation strategies among smallholder farmers: Evidence from Burkina Fa\-so

**Autores:** Henri Zoungrana; Lwungili Justin Bationo; Kale Julienne MILLOGO1 MILLOGO; Coffie Francis José N’Guessan; Omer Souglimpo Combary

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Enhancing long\-term adaptation to climate change in developing countries requires an under\-standing of farmers’ post\-adoption behaviors\. This study examines the factors influencing post\-adoption decisions, namely substitution, maintenance, combination, and abandonment, across soil and water conservation techniques \(SWCTs\), agroforestry, crop associations, and improved seed varieties in climate\-vulnerable contexts\. Using a multinomial logit model with sample\-selection bi\-as correction and data from the Permanent Agricultural Survey covering 13,030 farmers in Burkina Faso from 2015 to 2019, the findings reveal that men are more likely to maintain, substitute, or combine SWCTs, agroforestry, and crop associations rather than abandon them, while age and education exhibit practice\-specific effects\. Land ownership, labor availability, farm size, and external support are associated with greater continuation, substitution, or combination for several practices rather than abandonment\. Climatic shocks also generate differentiated responses, with drought increasing the likelihood of SWCT abandonment, while crop associations are less likely to be abandoned in Sahelian and Sahelo\-Sahelian zones\. These findings highlight the need for post\-adoption policies that complement initial adoption support through tailored extension services, improved access to productive resources and institutional support, and geographically and climatically differentiated interventions to sustain and adjust existing adaptation strategies\.
