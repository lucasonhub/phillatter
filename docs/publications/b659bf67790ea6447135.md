# Niami, Morteza: The Ontological Turn of Artificial Intelligence: Structural Subjectivity, Atmospheric Conditioning, and the Reconfiguration of Belonging

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Contemporary philosophical discourse on artificial intelligence has undergone a significant thematic migration, moving beyond narrow epistemic questions of algorithmic accuracy and ethical debates concerning bias and accountability toward deeper inquiries into affective modulation, communicative agency, and the phenomenological restructuring of human lifeworlds\. While emergent scholarship has productively addressed atmospheric manipulation, the sociotechnical dynamics of large language models, the erosion of practical intuition, and the temporal fragmentation of lived experience, these investigations remain largely siloed within distinct subfields\. This paper advances the thesis that the most profound philosophical transformation introduced by contemporary AI is neither predominantly epistemic nor reducible to applied ethics, but fundamentally ontological: AI increasingly reorganizes the generative structural conditions under which subjectivity stabilizes, orients itself, and achieves temporal coherence\. Moving beyond static conceptions of the human subject as a pre\-given entity merely exposed to exogenous technological influences, we develop a process\-oriented account of structural subjectivity\. In this framework, identity and agency are understood not as inherent properties but as emergent effects of continuous regulatory dynamics operating within technologically mediated environments\. By synthesizing recent developments in post\-Heideggerian phenomenology, critical philosophy of technology \(particularly Stiegler and Ihde\), and social\-philosophical theories of recognition and belonging, this article introduces an integrated conceptual model articulated through four interdependent axes: \(1\) atmospheric conditioning, \(2\) background restructuring, \(3\) existential erosion, and \(4\) reconfigured belonging\. The paper concludes by arguing that the future trajectory of AI ethics must abandon its current anthropocentric output\-responsibility paradigm in favor of a rigorous ontology of technologically mediated subject formation, thereby confronting the political and existential stakes inherent in the algorithmic constitution of contemporary selfhood\. Keywords: Artificial Intelligence; Structural Subjectivity; Phenomenology of Technology; Belonging; Existential Erosion; Atmospheric Affects; AI Ontology\. \(direct link \)
