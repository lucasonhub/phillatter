# Potential Application of Carbon Nanotubes and Ag\-doped Carbon Nanotubes for Ethylene \(C2H4\) Adsorbent in Postharvest Agriculture

**Autores:** Art Anthony Z\. Munio

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Ethylene \(C2H4\) control technologies in postharvest agriculture are critical for minimizing wastage and maintaining the market value of climacteric fruits\. The present study reports molecular interactions between C2H4 and single\-walled carbon nanotubes \(SWCNTs\) and Ag\-doped single\-walled carbon nanotubes \(Ag\-doped SWCNTs\) using density functional theory\-based simulations\. The ground\-state calculations demonstrate that C2H4 is attached to the sidewall of the prototype single\-walled carbon nanotube \(SWCNT\) via weak physisorption, with bonding mediated by minor charge redistribution\. The interaction between C2H4 and the prototype nanotube can be enhanced by introducing an Ag dopant into the SWCNT sidewall, forming an Ag\-C2H4 π\-complex\. The Ag dopant can trap multiple C2H4 molecules, implying superior adsorbent performance\. The NVT molecular dynamics simulations at 300 K predicted that the presence of competing H2O and CO2 weakens C2H4 binding on the surface of pristine SWCNT, leading to its diffusion\. In contrast, the π\-complex structure of C2H4 with Ag\-dopant remains stable despite the presence of competing volatile gases\. The simulations predicted that Ag\-SWCNT is an effective adsorbent for C2H4 in H2O\- and CO2\-rich environments, such as in postharvest storage\. Overall, the findings presented herein will serve as a reference for the design of C2H4 control technologies in postharvest agriculture\.
