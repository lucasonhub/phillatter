# Parametric Resonance in Primal\-Dual Learning for Slowly Non\-Stationary Constrained MDPs

**Autores:** Maxime Baelde

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Primal\-dual methods for constrained Markov decision processes are analyzed under a two\-timescale assumption, with non\-stationarity entering through two channels\. In the additive channel, where the constraint level oscillates, the dual oscillation saturates at a floor set by the moving optimum and is amplified linearly by the timescale ratio\. In the parametric channel, where the constraint sensitivity oscillates, a coherent modulation carries no resonant forcing at any depth: the dual update&\#x27;s stiffness modulation cancels the primal update&\#x27;s damping modulation\. Instability instead comes from a mismatch between the constraint estimate used by the primal update and the measurement used by the dual update, producing a parametric resonance at twice the pair&\#x27;s natural frequency\. The tolerated mismatch grows with the square root of the forcing bandwidth and collapses with the square root of the dual\-to\-primal step\-size ratio\. These results hold on the tangent linear\-quadratic reduction around an active saddle, for one constraint and for $K$ constraints under a common modulation and an isotropy condition\. Driving both updates from the same estimate removes the resonant forcing but introduces a stationary offset equal to the estimator&\#x27;s bias\. In a Lagrangian soft actor\-critic agent, the multiplier locks onto the sub\-harmonic at the timescale ratios its identified linear loop predicts and a coherent cost critic suppresses the lock\-in, while a proportional \(PID\) term, predicted to leave the lock\-in in place, suppresses most of it through the projection of the multiplier\.
