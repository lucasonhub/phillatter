# Learning by Compression: Risk Estimates Attain the Law\-of\-Large\-Numbers Rate

**Autores:** Simone Garatti; Marco C\. Campi

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Compression schemes provide a general framework for deriving data\-dependent risk bounds for learning algorithms\. For learning rules based on preferent compression, existing results upper\-bound the risk in terms of a sample\-dependent complexity $\\boldsymbol\{k\}$, defined as the cardinality of the compression augmented by the training examples on which the reconstructed hypothesis is inappropriate\. These results, however, yield a margin of order $O\(\\sqrt\{\\ln N/N\}\)$ above $\\boldsymbol\{k\}/N$\. In this paper, we establish that the logarithmic factor is not intrinsic\. We prove that, for every fixed confidence level $1\-δ$, the risk is bounded with probability at least $1\-δ$ by $$ \\frac\{\\boldsymbol\{k\}\}\{N\} \+ O\\left\(\\frac\{1\}\{\\sqrt\{N\}\}\\right\), $$ uniformly over all possible values of $\\boldsymbol\{k\}$\. Thus, risk estimates for learning algorithms based on preferent compression attain the same law\-of\-large\-numbers rate as the empirical estimate of the probability of a fixed event\. We also show that the leading term $\\boldsymbol\{k\}/N$ is necessary and investigate its relation to the empirical risk\. In particular, we identify regimes in which the empirical risk retains the $O\(1/\\sqrt\{N\}\)$ convergence rate, as well as regimes in which it converges more slowly or ceases to be consistent, while the complexity\-based estimator preserves the $O\(1/\\sqrt\{N\}\)$ rate\.
