# Assessing the Use of Emerging Technologies for Digital Preservation of Indigenous Knowledge among Librarians in Bayelsa State Libraries

**Autores:** Benake\-ebide Christy Micah; Ufuoma Beauty Efe

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study assessed the use of emerging technologies for digital preservation of indigenous knowledge among librarians in Bayelsa State libraries\. The study was guided by two research questions that examined the emerging technologies available for digital preservation of indigenous knowledge and the extent to which librarians utilise these technologies in Bayelsa State libraries\. The study adopted a descriptive survey research design\. The population of the study comprised librarians working in selected academic and public libraries in Bayelsa State, Nigeria\. An online survey questionnaire titled Use of Emerging Technologies for Digital Preservation of Indigenous Knowledge Questionnaire \(UETDPIKQ\) was used for data collection\. A total of 127 responses were retrieved and used for the study\. The data collected were analysed using frequency counts, percentages, mean, and standard deviation\. The findings revealed that emerging technologies such as digitisation/scanning technologies, institutional repositories, cloud storage systems, digital archiving platforms, metadata applications, social media platforms, automated library systems, multimedia technologies, and internet/Wi Fi facilities were available in Bayelsa State libraries, with a grand mean score of 2\.75 on a 4\-point scale\. However, artificial intelligence tools were found to be inadequately available\. The study further revealed that librarians utilised emerging technologies for digital preservation of indigenous knowledge to a high extent, with a grand mean score of 3\.43 on a 5\-point scale\. Technologies such as social media platforms, internet\-based technologies, digitisation tools, institutional repositories, and cloud storage systems were widely used for indigenous knowledge preservation and dissemination\. These findings suggest that, although foundational digital tools are gaining ground in Bayelsa State libraries, deliberate investment in artificial intelligence capacity is needed if librarians are to sustain and advance current preservation practice\. The study concluded that although emerging technologies are increasingly available and utilised in Bayelsa State libraries, challenges relating to inadequate infrastructure, poor funding, and limited technological expertise continue to hinder effective digital preservation practices\. The study recommended improved funding for digital preservation initiatives, provision of advanced technologies, continuous ICT training for librarians, improved internet connectivity, and stronger institutional support for sustainable indigenous knowledge preservation
