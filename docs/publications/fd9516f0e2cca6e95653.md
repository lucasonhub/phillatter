# Learning Golf at University Through Gamification and Simulation

**Autores:** Chaoyang Song; Ming Xu; Lei Pan

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

University golf teaching asks novices to coordinate precise movement, visual attention, strategic choice, and repeated practice\. Game elements and simulation technologies may help, but evidence does not support a simple claim that making practice more entertaining or immersive reliably improves learning\. This narrative review argues that outcomes depend on the alignment of challenge, feedback, learner control, and task representativeness\. When these features support competence and preserve the perception–action demands of golf, they can improve motivation and create useful practice conditions; when points, screens, or real\-time cues displace meaningful decisions or bodily feedback, gains may remain confined to the training task\. Evidence from physical education, motor\-learning research, virtual reality, and golf\-specific studies is synthesised to explain why motivational outcomes are more consistent than skill transfer\. The review also identifies a marked evidence gap: most golf experiments use small samples of novice adults performing putting tasks, while university courses require broader technical and tactical learning over a semester\. A design framework and research agenda are proposed for comparative university sport pedagogy, including delayed retention, on\-course transfer, objective performance, psychological measures, accessibility, and cross\-cultural replication\. The central implication is that technology should be judged by what learners can do after its support is removed\.
