# Institutional capacity, FTA network breadth, and trade efficiency in China’s aquatic\-product imports: evidence from a true random\-effects stochastic frontier gravity model

**Autores:** Wei Yang; Kunlin Tang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

China’s rapidly expanding aquatic\-product imports raise an important economic question: how fully do established supplier relationships realize the trade capacity supported by market scale, production capacity, and institutional conditions? Using a balanced panel of 16 major suppliers over 2013–2022, this study estimates a true random\-effects stochastic frontier gravity model that separates persistent supplier heterogeneity from time\-varying trade inefficiency\. The results identify two distinct margins\. China’s fishery\-sector scale and supplier total aquatic\-animal production expand the conditional import frontier, while stronger China\-side logistics\-service competence and broader supplier external FTA networks are associated with lower trade inefficiency and stronger frontier realization\. The FTA\-network relationship remains particularly consistent across alternative time controls, LPI annualization, supplier economic and governance controls, and broader PPML samples\. Mean JLMS trade efficiency rises from 58\.00% in 2013 to 89\.21% in 2022\. In 2022, observed imports of USD 18\.41 billion correspond to a conditional frontier of approximately USD 20\.65 billion, leaving a USD 2\.24 billion conditional gap concentrated in a small number of large\-frontier relationships\. An efficiency–frontier matrix further distinguishes core high\-realization suppliers, large\-frontier relationships with greater room for realization, and efficient smaller suppliers that contribute diversification\. These findings show that trade\-capacity formation and trade\-capacity realization are complementary dimensions of resilient aquatic\-food supply chains\.
