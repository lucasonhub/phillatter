# EXPRESS: Perfusion Extent and Hypoperfusion Severity Determine Diaschisis Patterns in Acute Ischemic Stroke: A CT Perfusion Study

**Autores:** QI JIA; Nan Nan Sheng; Noémie Ligot; Anissa Abderrakib; niloufar sadeghi\.maybodi; Gilles Naeije

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: Diaschisis reflects remote functional suppression following focal ischemia and large\-scale network disruption\. We investigate the prevalence, overlap, and determinants of multiple diaschisis subtypes using acute CTP\. METHODS: We retrospectively analyzed 659 patients with hyperacute large\-vessel occlusion stroke\. Diaschisis was primarily assessed on CBV maps and classified into Crossed Cerebellar \(CCD\), Ipsilateral Thalamic \(ITD\), Ipsilateral Hippocampal \(IHD\), and CCD\+ITD\. Tmax\-based assessment was exploratory\. Multivariable logistic regression evaluated severe hypoperfusion extent \(Tmax&gt;6s\), hypoperfusion intensity ratio \(HIR\), and ischemic core volume \(CBF&lt;30%\)\. RESULTS: CCD was the most common subtype, followed by ITD and CCD\+ITD, whereas IHD was rare\. Greater severe hypoperfusion extent was independently associated with CCD, ITD, and CCD\+ITD, while higher HIR was additionally associated with ITD, and CCD\+ITD\. Ischemic core volume was not independently associated with diaschisis\. CCD was independently associated with left hemispheric stroke, while combined CCD\+ITD was associated with female sex\. Exploratory Tmax\-based analyses showed broadly similar associations\. Subtype overlap suggested involvement of multiple interconnected network nodes\. CONCLUSION: Hyperacute diaschisis reflects ischemia\-driven network dysfunction primarily related to severe hypoperfusion extent, with hypoperfusion severity contributing to specific subtypes\. These findings support CBV\-based CTP assessment for investigating large\-scale network vulnerability, while Tmax\-based may provide complementary information for future diaschisis research\.
