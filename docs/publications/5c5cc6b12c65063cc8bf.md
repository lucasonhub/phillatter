# Sleep as the Temporal Foundation of Energetic Sufficiency

**Autores:** Ari Ariel Marom

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Across the Informational Nosology corpus, a single condition determines whether the brain can sustain its operating mode: sufficient energetic headroom and the coupling capacity it supports\. An earlier monograph identified the blood–brain barrier as the spatial foundation of that supply\. The present work addresses the temporal foundation — the conditions under which the system can restore what the waking day expends\. Sleep is introduced not as one more state variable among others, but as the controller of the system’s capacity to acquire energy at all\. Short sleep, fragmented sleep, and hypoxic sleep are treated as convergent failures of a single nightly restoration process rather than as unrelated mechanisms\. Under ideal conditions the extended model remains conservative with respect to the earlier barrier framework\. Under chronic restriction it predicts a sharp loss of mode stability near twenty hours of wakefulness per day, in close agreement with established empirical thresholds\. A dedicated analysis of nocturnal oxygen deprivation shows how snoring, late eating, non\-dipping blood pressure, and anxiety can leave sleep duration intact while degrading what sleep is supposed to deliver\. The monograph closes by linking unfinished nightly restoration to the irreversible structural burden that later appears in aging\. The result is a unified account of sleep as the temporal condition of energetic sufficiency, continuous with the spatial account already developed for the blood–brain barrier, and preparatory for the corpus’s treatment of aging\. Keywords: sleep; energetic restoration; sleep homeostasis; nocturnal hypoxia; blood–brain barrier; Informational Nosology; mode stability; aging; restoration debt; systems neuroscience
