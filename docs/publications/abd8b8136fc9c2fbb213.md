# Capability or participation? A dimensional analysis of ocean digital twins and its implications for maritime spatial planning governance

**Autores:** Xavier Fonseca; Igor Stefan Mayer

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Ocean digital twins, Digital Twins of the Ocean \(DTOs\), are increasingly promoted as the decision\-support backbone for marine spatial planning \(MSP\), yet the term denotes at least four incompatible conceptions across communities that rarely cite one another, alongside a critical strand that interrogates the object rather than advancing a conception of it, and most marine decision\-support tools are never adopted in practice\. This paper asks whether, and how, a DTO can serve participatory marine governance\. Using a concept\-centric morphological analysis, we assemble a corpus of eleven DTO conceptions, one critical reading of the field, and fifteen deployed systems and decision\-support tools, and code each against six dimensions derived from the literature: three graded \(scope, capability, coupling\) and three categorical \(object, purpose, intended users\); the full coding matrix is provided as supplementary material\. Mapping the corpus reveals a structured capability–participation trade\-off rather than a uniform spread: the conceptions richest in analytical capability address the narrowest set of users, while those reaching non\-expert stakeholders sit lowest on the capability ladder; no continuously synchronised system combines both, and only an aspirational programme \(DITTO\) claims to\. Because MSP is a stakeholder\-driven governance process that demands both, this trade\-off diagnoses why capable ocean twins go unused in planning\. Setting the framework against the stages of the planning cycle sharpens this into practical guidance: continuous synchronisation, the property that distinguishes a twin from a decision\-support tool, is decisive at three of six stages and contributes little at the three where participation matters most, which gives authorities a basis for deciding whether a digital twin is what their planning problem requires\. We translate the analysis into governance\-relevant guidance: treating stakeholder\-usability as an explicit, evaluable requirement when MSP authorities specify, commission and assess DTOs, and decoupling a high\-capability predictive backbone from a simple stakeholder\-facing interface\. A descriptive definition, derived from the dimensions, gives marine managers a shared vocabulary for asking what any Digital Twin of the Ocean is for, and for whom\.
