# Automated Segmentation of Wound Bed Tissue from Clinical Photographs: A Benchmark of Five Deep Learning Architectures Under a Shared Protocol

**Autores:** David Reifs; Lorena Casanova\-Lozano; Jordi Solé‐Casals; Andrea Coda

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate characterisation of the wound bed, the relative proportions of granulation, slough and necrotic tissue described by the red\-yellow\-black model, is central to monitoring healing and guiding debridement in chronic\-wound care, yet in routine practice, it is judged visually and is poorly reproducible between clinicians\. We investigated automatic, pixel\-level segmentation of these three classes within the wound region of interest\. A corpus of 404 photographs was curated from a public wound image collection and densely annotated by wound care clinicians with a purpose\-built, cluster\-assisted labelling tool\. Five deep learning architectures spanning convolutional, transformer, hybrid and ensemble families were trained on one frozen data split under a largely shared pipeline, with limited, fully documented architecture\-specific tuning, and scored on a shared 62\-image test set\. The models fell within a narrow mean intersection\-over\-union band \(0\.748\-0\.818\)\. The strongest single model, a purely convolutional network, was statistically indistinguishable from the best ensemble, and simply averaging already\-trained convolutional and transformer models matched that ensemble at no additional training cost\. Slough remained consistently the hardest tissue\. Translated into clinical units, per\-wound tissue percentages were recovered with small mean bias \(within ± 5 percentage points\) but wide single\-image limits of agreement \(about ± 25\-50 percentage points\), so the measurement suits aggregate and longitudinal assessment rather than single\-image reading\. Within this dataset, split and training protocol, the choice among the strong hierarchical architectures was not decisive: a single strong model is the pragmatic option for clinical deployment, with an ensemble reserved for maximal accuracy and class balance\.
