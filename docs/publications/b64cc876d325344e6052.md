# Mask\-Resilient Multi\-Biometric Authentication for Healthcare

**Autores:** Siew\-Chin Chong; Caleb Yeo; Kian Ming Lim; Lee\-Ying Chong

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The fast integration of digital healthcare platforms, including telemedicine and electronic health records, has created a significant need for secure, contactless authentication methods\. Traditional login methods such as passwords and PINs are increasingly insufficient for ensuring the security of sensitive patient data\. This paper presents a robust, lightweight Healthcare Authentication System that five biometric modules to address major challenges in facial recognition: spoofing attacks, liveness detection, and facial occlusion due to mask\-wearing\. The system leverages RetinaFace for face detection, a MobileNetV2 model for mask classification, a lightweight CNN for blink\-based liveness verification, and MiniFASNet for spoof detection\. For identity matching, the system utilizes InceptionResNetV1 with ArcFace loss, enabling effective recognition of both masked and unmasked faces through a dual\-embedding strategy\. Each module was evaluated using benchmark datasets: CASIA\-FASD for spoofing, RMFD for mask detection, open/closed eye datasets for blink detection, and VGGFace2 \(with synthetic masking\) for recognition\. The system achieved 91% accuracy in blink detection, 85% in spoof detection, 97% in mask classification, and 96% in face recognition\. The complete pipeline achieved 88% overall accuracy on the evaluated dataset\. The proposed solution offers a practical and scalable approach for secure biometric authentication in healthcare environments, particularly in clinical settings where hygiene, user accessibility, and anti\-spoof measures are essential\. This work provides a foundation for future research in offline biometric verification, depth\-based spoof detection, and multilingual interface design, contributing toward more inclusive and secure healthcare technologies\. While we do not fine\-tune model backbones, our contribution lies in a clinically viable integration strategy that combines blink\-based liveness, anti\-spoofing, mask detection, and face verification under a fixed operating point with identity\-disjoint evaluation, achieving 88% end\-to\-end accuracy on public\-dataset inputs\.
