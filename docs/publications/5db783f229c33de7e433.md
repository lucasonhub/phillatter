# Shojaei Moghaddam, Robabeh &amp; Niami, Mahnaz: Responsible Suspension in Clinical Care: Finite Continuity, Reconfiguration, and the Temporal Ethics of Intervention

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Clinical care takes place under conditions of uncertainty, temporal constraint, bodily vulnerability, and the need for action\. Within these conditions, nurses must interpret present clinical situations while relying on prior knowledge, experience, patient history, and established patterns of recognition\. Such resources are indispensable, yet they can also produce premature closure when an earlier interpretation remains dominant despite emerging signs that the patient&\#x27;s situation has changed\. This theoretical\-conceptual article introduces responsible suspension as a proposed framework for addressing this tension\. Responsible suspension is defined as a deliberate, temporary, and purpose\-directed interruption of premature or overly fixed interpretation in order to permit renewed attention to the patient&\#x27;s present situation, while professional responsibility, safety obligations, and readiness to intervene remain active\. The concept is distinguished from Husserlian epoché, reflective practice, watchful waiting, and indecision\. The article further develops finite continuity to describe the patient&\#x27;s temporally extended clinical situation as continuous across past, present, and anticipated future while remaining constrained by bodily deterioration, clinical deadlines, institutional resources, and the reversibility of intervention\. Reconfiguration is then introduced as a process through which new signs do not merely add information to an existing interpretation but can reorganize the relations among history, bodily appearance, contextual meaning, uncertainty, and anticipated consequences\. Finally, the article proposes a temporal ethics of intervention, according to which ethical clinical judgment concerns not only what should be done but also whether one should pause, reassess, reconfigure understanding, or intervene at a particular moment\. The article argues that responsible suspension is neither passive observation nor a delay in care\. It is a temporally bounded form of responsible attention whose legitimacy depends on proportionality, clinical risk, uncertainty, reversibility, and the possibility of harm through continued delay\. The framework contributes a conceptual vocabulary for understanding how nurses can remain responsive without becoming prisoners of their first interpretation\. Keywords: phenomenology, nursing, clinical care, responsible suspension, clinical judgment, temporality, finite continuity, reconfiguration, embodied perception, ethical intervention \(direct link \)
