# Friluftsliv \(open\-air life\) as a cultural site of emotion socialization in early childhood in Norway

**Autores:** Evi Petersen; Natália Meireles Santos da Costa; Tuva Beyer Broch

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Emotional competence is often theorized as a set of individual capacities, while the cultural and institutional practices through which children learn to interpret, express, and regulate emotions receive less attention\. This Perspective paper examines Norwegian friluftsliv \(often translated to English as “open\-air life,”\) in early childhood education and care as a culturally situated setting of emotion socialization\. We propose conceptualizing friluftsliv as an institutional emotion niche: a configuration of repeated routines, material environments, social relations, and normative expectations that makes particular emotional experiences more likely and particular expressions more readily recognized as competent\. This creates a potential inclusion–exclusion paradox\. Friluftsliv can foster joy in nature, mastery, endurance, and belonging, but these outcomes are not guaranteed\. Children enter outdoor practices with different embodied histories, family practices, material resources, and sensory or physical needs\. Hesitation, withdrawal, or discomfort may consequently be misrecognized as deficits rather than as meaningful responses to particular situations\. We argue that children&\#x27;s emotional capacities must be distinguished from their institutional recognition and outline three directions for research: embodied environmental experiences, socialization into culturally valued emotional display rules, and identity formation and belonging through shared practices\. By situating institutional outdoor practices with children as culturally organized emotion niches, this paper contributes with conceptual and thematic innovation to emotion research and outdoor studies and offers directions for professional practice that support multiple forms of meaningful outdoor participation\.
