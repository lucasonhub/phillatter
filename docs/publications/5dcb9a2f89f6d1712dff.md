# From Audit to Continuous Control: Implementation Pathways, Governance, And Organizational Readiness For AI\-Enabled Compliance in Critical Infrastructure

**Autores:** Abolaji Adebayo

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Critical infrastructure \(CI\) operators face an escalating threat environment in which episodic, audit\-based compliance verification cannot keep pace with dynamic adversary behavior, converging information technology and operational technology \(IT/OT\) estates, and expanding regulatory obligations\. This article reports a systematic qualitative synthesis examining how artificial intelligence \(AI\)\-enabled security posture management can transform compliance with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework \(CSF\) from periodic audit into continuous control\. Using document analysis and qualitative content analysis of purposively sampled authoritative secondary sources \(standards, government publications, regulatory instruments, industry threat reports, and peer\-reviewed scholarship\), the study develops eight themes: AI applications in CI cybersecurity; continuous monitoring and real\-time compliance; CSF alignment; operational and security benefits; organizational and human factors; sector\-specific considerations; governance and regulatory implications; and research gaps\. Findings indicate that machine learning materially strengthens the Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover functions, compresses the interval between control failure and remediation, and improves audit readiness through continuously generated evidence\. However, benefits are contingent on data and telemetry foundations, workforce capability, mitigation of alert fatigue and automation bias, sector\-tailored integration with legacy OT, and governance structures that address model risk, explainability, and accountability\. The article contributes a five\-phase implementation pathway \(governance baselining, telemetry consolidation, supervised analytics, conditional automation with human oversight, and continuous assurance\) and offers recommendations for operators, policymakers, and researchers\. It argues that continuous, AI\-assisted compliance is best understood as a sociotechnical control system rather than a purely technological upgrade\.
