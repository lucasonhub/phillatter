# Evidentiary Admissibility and Physics Constrained Validation of Generative Artificial Intelligence and Deep Learning in Forensic Structural Failure Reconstruction

**Autores:** Mohamed Fayez\. Elnagar, Structural Consultant &amp; Certified Forensic Engineering Expert MSc, PMP, MCIArb, MASCE, MACI, M\.AISC, M\.ASTM, M\.FIDIC, PhD

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

The integration of generative artificial intelligence and deep learning algorithms into forensic engineering has allowed researchers to reconstruct highly complex collapse sequences with unprecedented speed\. However, a profound judicial and technical dilemma arises regarding the evidentiary admissibility of these AI\-generated models in expert witness testimony\. Because deep neural networks operate as mathematical &quot;black boxes,&quot; they generate predictive failure pathways based on statistical patterns rather than strict, deterministic adherence to the laws of continuum mechanics, fluid dynamics, or structural steel plasticity\. In a rigorous court of law, opposing legal teams can easily disqualify AI forensic reconstructions due to the inherent risk of digital &quot;hallucinations&quot; or minor geometric boundary errors that completely alter the attributed cause of collapse\. Forensic engineering currently lacks an open\-source, standardized verification protocol to test the physics\-constrained limits of machine learning paradigms\. Without mathematically proving that an AI model strictly enforces the conservation of mass, momentum, and energy during a simulated collapse, the judicial system cannot accept these advanced digital tools, rendering highly sophisticated computational research virtually useless for actual legal liability arbitration\.
