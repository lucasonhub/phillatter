Abstract Chomsky built two great things and never joined them\. In linguistics, he argued that humans are born with a language faculty — and he won that argument by refusing to accept a named capacity as an explanation\. In politics, he argued that authority must justify itself because humans have an instinct for freedom — and that instinct he simply took from Humboldt and Rocker, without derivation\. Asked for fifty years how the two halves connect, he answered honestly that the link is loose\. This paper takes that answer seriously\. It fixes eight criteria in advance, then scores Chomsky&\#x27;s programme and the Philosophy of Virtues in three domains\. Chomsky wins language and mind six to one, and the loss is reported rather than reframed\. In media, the Propaganda Model is shown to explain the channel but not the consenting subject, and a mechanism is offered to fill the gap, with one prediction that could fail\. In the third domain, freedom is derived rather than posited — but the ground it rests on cannot yet be tested\. The finding is a hole, and an offer to fill it\.
