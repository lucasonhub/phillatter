# Overexpression of the endogenous diguanylate cyclase gene dgcS enhances biofilm formation by Shewanella oneidensis MR\-1 on electrodes

**Autores:** Nanae Iwanami; Akiho Matsumoto; Ryotaro Yamamoto; Keisuke Tomita; Atsushi Kouzuma; Kazuya Watanabe

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In microbial electrochemical technologies, Shewanella oneidensis MR\-1 efficiently generates electric current via biofilm formation on electrodes\. A previous study demonstrated that the deletion of the diguanylate cyclase gene dgcS inhibits biofilm formation on electrodes and current generation in MR\-1\. However, the molecular mechanisms by which dgcS expression and subsequent cyclic dimeric guanosine monophosphate \(c\-di\-GMP\) signaling affect these processes remain unexplored\. Here, we investigated the effects of dgcS overexpression on current generation, electrode\-associated biofilm formation, and the intracellular concentration of cyclic AMP \(cAMP\), a second messenger that induces the expression of extracellular electron transfer \(EET\)\-related genes in MR\-1, to examine the potential coordinated regulation of intracellular c\-di\-GMP and cAMP levels\. In an electrochemical flow cell \(EFC\), the dgcS \-overexpressing \( dgcS \-OE\) strain exhibited significantly higher intracellular c\-di\-GMP concentrations than the vector control \(VC\) strain, forming more biomass\-rich biofilms on the electrode and generating higher current\. However, current generation normalized to electrode\-associated biomass did not differ significantly between the dgcS \-OE and VC strains, suggesting that dgcS overexpression did not increase EET activity per biomass\. Moreover, intracellular cAMP levels were not significantly different between the dgcS \-OE and VC strains, and the transcription levels of adenylate cyclase \(cAMP synthase\) genes and EET\-related genes in the dgcS \-OE strain tended to be reduced to 0\.3\- to 0\.6\-fold of those in the VC strain\. Collectively, these findings suggest that dgcS overexpression and the associated elevation of intracellular c\-di\-GMP levels enhance the electrogenic performance of MR\-1 primarily by promoting biofilm development on electrodes, while weakly repressing the transcription of genes related to cAMP signaling\.
