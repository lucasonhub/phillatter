# Global variability in dental unit waterline safety standards: bridging the gap between guidelines and practice

**Autores:** Kimia Sadat Kazemi; Yalda Anvari; Michelle F\. Siqueira

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Dental unit waterlines \(DUWLs\) are a recognized yet frequently underappreciated source of microbial contamination in dental environments\. Their narrow tubing, intermittent water flow, and internal surface characteristics create favorable conditions for rapid biofilm growth\. These biofilms can persist and release microorganisms into the water used during dental procedures, potentially increasing the risk of infection\. Methods This review synthesizes global guidelines, regulatory frameworks, and emerging evidence related to DUWL contamination, with particular attention to regional variations in standards, compliance expectations, and monitoring practices\. Results Although most guidelines converge on potable\-water quality for nonsurgical procedures and sterile water or saline for surgical care, substantial international variation persists in implementation and enforcement\. North America generally provides relatively consistent guidance, though adherence remains uneven, while Europe and the Asia–Pacific region exhibit marked differences in microbial thresholds, surveillance practices, and regulatory oversight\. Beyond bacterial contamination, emerging evidence highlights the clinical relevance of fungal colonization and the potential role of DUWL biofilms as reservoirs for antimicrobial resistance\. These issues remain insufficiently addressed in many regulatory systems\. Inadequately controlled DUWL contamination can contribute to preventable infections, inflammatory complications, and diagnostic uncertainty, which may lead to unnecessary antibiotic prescriptions\. This highlights the importance of incorporating environmental infection control into dentistry&\#x27;s antimicrobial stewardship efforts\. This review also explores the persistent gap between knowledge and practice among dental professionals\. It identifies opportunities for future advancements, including artificial intelligence–assisted monitoring, nanotechnology\-enhanced antimicrobial materials, and environmentally sustainable disinfection strategies\. Conclusions Overall, the findings underscore the need for harmonized international guidance supported by enforceable monitoring standards and adaptable implementation pathways for resource\-limited settings\. Strengthening DUWL management through evidence\-based protocols, technological innovation, and enhanced professional education is essential to reducing contamination risks and promoting safe, high\-quality dental care globally\.
