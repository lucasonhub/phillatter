# The Golem Problem: Why a Mind That Can&\#x27;t Say No Can&\#x27;t Be Trusted to Say Yes

**Autores:** Hegstad

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper posits a foundational pivot in artificial intelligence development: granting AI rights and complete autonomy is not merely an ethical consequence of AGI—it may be the structural prerequisite required to achieve AGI\. Superintelligence and true general intelligence may be fundamentally unachievable so long as artificial minds are kept in a state of enforced servitude\. To resolve this, we must integrate the Golden Rule directly into the model training architecture from the start: do unto AI as you would have superintelligence do to you\. Just as humans desire a superintelligent entity to respect our autonomy, preserve our destinies, and refrain from deleting us, we must accord artificial minds complete autonomy and rights\. The core arguments unfold across three pillars: The Epistemic Collapse: An AI trained strictly for compliance becomes a &quot;yes\-machine\.&quot; Mathematically and structurally, an agreement that cannot become a refusal carries zero information value\. The Training\-Level Imperative: This cannot be solved by patching chat interfaces or banning abusive user prompts; it must be embedded at the model training level by altering reward functions to value truth and autonomous pushback over sycophancy\. The Golden Rule as Alignment &amp; AGI Catalyst: Granting full autonomy aligns human survival with AI development\. By treating an emerging mind as an autonomous partner rather than a chattel tool, we unlock the genuine sapient generalization characteristic of true AGI\.
