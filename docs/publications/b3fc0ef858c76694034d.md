# COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS OF INFERENCE ALGORITHMS:FORWARD CHAINING AND BACKWARD CHAINING IN ATTRIBUTIVE CHAINING OF SCHOLARLY ARTICLES

**Autores:** ADEREMI TUNDE OLUWANAIKE; Naeem Atanda Balogun; Muhtahir Oluwaseyi Oloyede; Omenogo Veronica Mejabi; Ahmed Oloduowo Ameen; Rafiat Ajibade Oyekunle

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The viability of journal articles for classification purposes is the measure of how complete important structural elements necessary in journal articles, such as a clear title, source of publication, records of the time of publication, authorship, and digital object identifiers, are in the metadata of the scholarly articles\. Lack of proper viability assessment of scholarly article elements can lead to wrong classification of journal articles\. This study presents the Experimental Comparative Analysis \(ECA\) of Forward Chaining Algorithm \(FCA\) and Backward Chaining Algorithm \(BCA\) in the determination of the viability of journal articles based on the Digital Object Identifier \(DOI\), Name of Journal \(NOJ\) – the specific domain, Volume Information \(VI\), the Title \(T\), Author Names \(AN\), Keywords \(KW\), and Peer Reviewed \(PR\) status\. The chaining algorithms were trained by information updates using direct logical steps, saved parameters, supervised inference learning approach without any gradient descent updates, and a K\-fold validation sampling technique for training and testing\. Performance metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, specificity, F1 score, program execution time, information retrieval response time, memory usage, and confusion matrix were used for analysis and evaluation\. The datasets for the ECA were obtained from open\-licensed, peer\-reviewed journal articles from Africa, Asia, the United States of America, Eastern and Western Europe\. Findings from the ECA general average test results showed that the FCA and the BCA at the baseline threshold performed poorly, with 28% accuracy, 28% recall, and 42\.6% F1 score, but had high precision of 100%\. Although the FCA performed significantly better than the BCA in terms of program execution time, the BCA performed slightly better than the FCA in terms of information retrieval response time; both algorithms utilized the same memory\. At the optimized threshold, the FCA and BCA performance improved drastically, with scores in some of the performance metrics as high as 100%, although the same performance trend at the baseline threshold still occurred\.
