# The Reciprocal Longitudinal Associations Between Short\-Form Video Addiction and Cyberbullying Perpetration Among Vocational School Students

**Autores:** Danni Gui; Mingkun Ouyang; Yiwen Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Previous research has demonstrated the prevalence of short\-form video addiction and cyberbullying perpetration among adolescents\. Nevertheless, longitudinal evidence clarifying the directional associations between the two behaviors, particularly whether such associations reflect within\-person processes, remains limited\. Therefore, this study examined the reciprocal longitudinal associations between short\-form video addiction and cyberbullying perpetration using a three\-wave random intercept cross\-lagged panel model \(RI\-CLPM\) with 6\-month intervals, in a sample of 1,439 vocational school students \( M age = 17\.42 years, SD = 3\.94\)\. The results revealed significant positive between\-person associations, indicating that students reporting elevated short\-form video addiction engaged in more cyberbullying perpetration\. The within\-person effect from cyberbullying perpetration to short\-form video addiction remained significant across all time intervals, whereas the within\-person effect from short\-form video addiction to cyberbullying perpetration was only significant in the later interval\. These findings suggest that the longitudinal association between short\-form video addiction and cyberbullying perpetration involves both stable between\-person differences and dynamic within\-person processes\. This study advances understanding of the dynamic interplay between short\-form video addiction and cyberbullying perpetration among vocational school students and underscores the importance of considering both between\-person and within\-person processes in research on adolescent digital behaviors\.
