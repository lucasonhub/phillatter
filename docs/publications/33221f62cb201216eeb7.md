# Periprocedural body temperature and clinical outcomes after successful reperfusion in large vessel occlusion stroke

**Autores:** Hongting Yan; Qi Wang; Lu Zhang; Gao\-Peng Xing; Jiaqi Wang; Yue Wang; Hui\-Sheng Chen

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Functional recovery remains heterogeneous among patients with large\-vessel occlusion stroke, even after successful reperfusion\. Body temperature may constitute a potentially modifiable physiological factor associated with functional outcome\. In particular, whether clinical factors, including baseline stroke severity, modify this temperature\-outcome association remains uncertain\. Methods We retrospectively analyzed 219 patients with large\-vessel occlusion acute ischemic stroke \(LVO\-AIS\) who underwent EVT and achieved successful reperfusion, defined as a modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction score of 2b\-3\. Pre and post\-EVT body temperatures were recorded\. The primary outcome was favorable functional outcome, defined as a modified Rankin Scale \(mRS\) score of 0\-2 at 90 days\. Logistic regression analyses and exploratory subgroup analyses were performed to evaluate the association between body temperature and clinical outcomes\. Results Among the 219 included patients, 104 \(47\.5%\) achieved favorable functional outcome at 90 days\. Pre\-EVT temperature was not significantly associated with functional outcome\. In unadjusted analysis, each 1 °C increase in post\-EVT temperature was associated with lower odds of favorable functional outcome \(OR, 0\.40; 95% CI, 0\.20\-0\.79; P = 0\.01\) and higher odds of mortality \(OR, 2\.02; 95% CI, 1\.11\-3\.66; P = 0\.02\)\. However, these associations were attenuated after multivariable adjustment \(adjusted OR, 0\.47; 95% CI, 0\.19\-1\.15; P = 0\.10; adjusted OR, 1\.22; 95% CI, 0\.49\-3\.02; P = 0\.67\)\. In exploratory subgroup analyses, a potential association between higher post\-EVT temperature and lower odds of favorable outcome was observed in patients with baseline NIHSS ≤ 15 \(adjusted OR, 0\.29; 95% CI, 0\.08\-0\.98; P = 0\.047\), although the interaction was not statistically significant \( P for interaction = 0\.64\)\. Conclusion In this exploratory study, body temperature was not independently associated with functional outcome in the overall cohort\. Although a potential association between higher post\-EVT temperature and unfavorable outcome was observed in patients with baseline NIHSS ≤ 15, the interaction was not significant\. This finding is hypothesis\-generating and warrants prospective validation with pre\-specified subgroup analyses\.
