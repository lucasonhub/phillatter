# Monitoring Global Scale Surface Water Quality and Quantity Using Remote Sensing Techniques

**Autores:** Elisa Friedmann

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Rivers are dynamic and heterogeneous systems that distribute water, energy, and nutrients across ecosystems\. Monitoring fluvial behavior, especially at large scales, remains challenging but increasingly feasible with advances in satellite remote sensing and greater access to computational resources\. In this dissertation, we develop scalable methods to quantify river sediment, discharge, and carbon fluxes using satellite data and machine learning\. Chapter 1 overcomes long\-standing tradeoffs between cloud cover, resolution, and overpass that limit remote sensing of total suspended solids \(TSS\) applications by developing and demonstrating the utility of a continental scale satellite spatiotemporal data fusion reflectance product in Google Earth Engine\. Chapter 2 advances the global Surface Water Ocean Topography \(SWOT\)\-derived discharge product by scaling discharge estimation workflows and harnessing the satellite orbit configurations to assess spatial and temporal hydrologic regime capture\. Chapter 3 applies machine learning techniques to predict shallow groundwater carbon dioxide concentrations, a key unexplored source of riverine carbon evasion, and estimates the groundwater contribution to surface water greenhouse gas evasion across the continental United States\.
