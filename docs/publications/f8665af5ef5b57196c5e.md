# Artificial Intelligence Disruption and Structural Enrollment Decline in Economics and Business Higher Education: Longitudinal Evidence from Indonesia

**Autores:** Yohanes Sugiyanta

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid expansion of artificial intelligence \(AI\), digital transformation, and technology\-driven economic systems has fundamentally reshaped global higher education dynamics, particularly within conventional economics and business programs\. This study investigates the phenomenon of structural enrollment decline in economics and business higher education during the artificial intelligence era through a longitudinal institutional analysis of the Accounting and Management study programs at STIE Mulia Pratama, Indonesia, over the 1998–2025 period\. Employing a quantitative longitudinal approach, the study utilizes institutional enrollment data obtained from the Indonesian Higher Education Database \(PDDIKTI\)\. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, trend analysis, linear regression, forecasting analysis, and interpretative analysis to identify long\-term enrollment transformation and institutional sustainability challenges\. The findings reveal three major enrollment phases consisting of growth, fluctuation, and persistent structural decline\. Regression analysis confirms statistically significant negative enrollment trends within both study programs\. The Accounting program experienced an average annual decline of 3\.356 students, while the Management program demonstrated a stronger annual decline of 5\.056 students, indicating higher vulnerability to technological disruption and changing educational preferences\. The study further reveals that AI disruption, employability orientation, digital economic transformation, and competency\-based educational preferences substantially influence educational decision\-making among younger generations\. Consequently, conventional economics and business education increasingly faces sustainability challenges associated with declining institutional attractiveness and labor market transformation\. This study contributes to the higher education literature by positioning declining enrollment as a manifestation of structural transformation rather than temporary institutional fluctuation\. The integration of longitudinal institutional evidence, AI disruption perspectives, and sustainability analysis provides a broader analytical framework for understanding the future challenges of higher education within the digital economy era\.
