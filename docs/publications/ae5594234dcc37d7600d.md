# Evolutionary remodeling of the ventral retina enables aerial vision in the four\-eyed fish Anableps anableps

**Autores:** Louise Perez; Lina Shi; Alyssa Daspit; Keyla N\. Pruett; Carsyn Smith; Anna Prideaux; Josane F\. Sousa; Patricia N\. Schneider

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract The emergence of evolutionary novelties remains a central challenge in biology, particularly for complex organs such as the vertebrate eye\. Here, we use the four\-eyed fish Anableps anableps to investigate adaptation to simultaneous aerial and aquatic vision\. Single\-nucleus and spatial transcriptomics reveal that the ventral retina, which processes aerial light, is developmentally expanded and exhibits region\-specific opsin expression\. Ventral PR1 cones express opn1mw2 rather than the longer\-wavelength\-sensitive opn1lw paralogs expressed dorsally\. Machine\-learning predictions indicate that Opn1mw2 is red\-shifted relative to Opn1mw1 but remains blue\-shifted relative to dorsal Opn1lw pigments\. Dorsal\-ventral patterning markers further reveal an overrepresentation of ventral retinal identities\. Comparative spatial profiling of developing and adult Anableps and killifish indicates dorsal displacement of the retinal boundary, producing a proportionally larger ventral domain\. Thus, Anableps evolved aerial vision through developmental expansion of the ventral retina and regional reconfiguration of opsin expression within conserved cone subtypes\.
