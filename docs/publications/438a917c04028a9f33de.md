# Distinct host genetic determinants of HIV acquisition and disease progression: the case of classical HLA class I

**Autores:** Marion Amujal; James Bumba

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

HIV acquisition and disease progression appear to be influenced by largely distinct host genetic factors\. Whether infection becomes established is determined shortly after mucosal or parenteral exposure, before HIV\-specific CD8 \+ T\-cell responses become detectable\. Progression is shaped by immune responses acting after infection is established and is strongly influenced by classical HLA class I variation\. CCR5Δ32 affects both phenotypes, raising the possibility of shared determinants, but evidence for genetic overlap remains limited\. Here, we review evidence on whether classical HLA class I variation is associated with HIV acquisition across diverse exposure routes and study designs\. A large collaborative genome\-wide association study \(GWAS\) found no significant association between HLA class I variation and HIV acquisition after accounting for frailty bias, and candidate\-gene associations have not been independently replicated\. Future research should prioritize well\-characterized exposure cohorts and sequencing approaches capable of detecting structural variation and resolving complex loci such as killer\-cell immunoglobulin\-like receptors \(KIRs\), particularly in underrepresented African populations\.
