# Use of generative artificial intelligence in medical libraries: A scoping review

**Autores:** Antônio Carlos Picalho; Mateus Rebouças Nascimento; Luciane Maria Fadel

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose\. This study aims to map how health and medical libraries have used GenAI to support library services\. Methods\. Scoping review following PRISMA\-ScR\. Searches covered publications from 2020 to 2025 in PubMed, Embase, CINAHL, LISTA, Academic Search Premier, MEDLINE via Ovid, Scopus, Web of Science, ProQuest Dissertations &amp; Theses, and Google Scholar\. After deduplication, 106 of 250 records were screened in Rayyan by two independent blinded reviewers\. Findings\. Eleven studies were included: seven primary studies and four secondary or methodological papers\. Among primaries, five directly used GPT\-family models \(GPT\-4/ChatGPT/GPT\-4o or Perplexity layered on GPT\-4\) to answer clinical questions, support search\-strategy design, trial library tasks, and integrate source\-linked answers in a national portal\. One survey assessed intention to adopt generative chatbots; one case described a rule\-based chatbot\. GenAI accelerates workflows and extends services but requires human verification, transparency about limitations, privacy policies, and staff training \(including prompt engineering\)\. LLM\-generated search strategies exhibited variability and underperformed compared with human designs\. Implications\. In its current state, GenAI works best as a copilot useful for drafting, triage, and initial sense\-making, while librarians remain essential for high\-precision tasks \(performance\), quality control \(reliability\), and governance\. This suggests that its academic value lies not in automation alone, but in the mediated integration of AI into evidence\-informed services, where professional judgment, methodological transparency, and ethical accountability determine whether GenAI strengthens or weakens health information practices\.
