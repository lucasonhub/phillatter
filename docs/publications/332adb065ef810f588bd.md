# Birth\-quarter distribution, morphological characteristics, and team competitive performance in group rhythmic gymnastics

**Autores:** Jonatha Flávio Souza Lemos; Alicia Almeida de Souza Ruvieri; Lucas Savassi Figueiredo; Vivian de Oliveira; Luís Carlos Oliveira Gonçalves; Cláudia Marlise Balbinotti Andrade; Henrique de Oliveira Castro

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examined birth\-quarter patterns across competitive categories in group rhythmic gymnastics, their associations with morphological characteristics, and whether team\-level relative\-age composition, chronological age, and height were associated with competitive success\. The sample comprised 241 female gymnasts from the 2025 Brazilian Group Rhythmic Gymnastics Championship: Pre\-Juvenile \(n = 68\), Youth \(n = 51\), Junior \(n = 62\), and Senior \(n = 60\)\. Birth dates were classified into quarters \(Q1–Q4\), and anthropometric measures included body mass, standing and sitting height, leg length, and body mass index\. Birth\-quarter distributions were compared with population\-based expected frequencies derived from Brazilian female live\-birth data and weighted by birth year\. Morphological associations were tested using generalized estimating equations clustered by club and adjusted for competitive category and decimal age\. Exploratory associations with Top\-3 success were assessed using conditional logistic regression stratified by competitive event\. No category showed a significant departure from the population\-based expected birth\-quarter distribution after multiplicity adjustment \(Holm\-adjusted p ≥ 0\.251\), and the ordinal trend in first\-semester representation was nonsignificant \(OR = 0\.799, 95% CI 0\.637–1\.000, Holm\-adjusted p = 0\.251\)\. No morphological outcome remained significantly associated with birth quarter after adjustment for within\-club dependence and multiple testing \(Holm\-adjusted p ≥ 0\.083\)\. Exploratory team\-level associations with Top\-3 placement were nonsignificant and imprecise \(Holm\-adjusted p = 1\.000\)\. Overall, the study found no statistically supported evidence of birth\-quarter asymmetry or team\-level associations with competitive performance, although imprecision precludes excluding potentially meaningful team\-level effects\.
