# Creating partial disorder in spinel\-type cathodes for enhanced kinetics and cycling stability

**Autores:** Zhiyuan Hu; Wen Xu; Yi Qu; Fanjun Liu; Chengyu Jin; Dongdong Xiao; Peiyi Lin; Jia He; Ziyu You; Pan Zhengxi; Xiang Huang; Runze Yu; Zijian Cai; Zhengyan Lun

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The Mn\-based spinel\-type cathode material LiMn 2 O 4 \(LMO\) attracts widespread attention due to its low cost, eco\-friendly characteristics, ease of synthesis, and stable chemical properties\. However, its intrinsic two\-phase reaction leads to relatively lower practical capacity and poor cycling stability, restricting its application\. This study introduces partial disorder into LMO through high\-energy ball milling, synthesizing the fluorinated and lithium\-rich material Li 1\.5 Mn 1\.73 O 3\.8 F 0\.2 \(LMOF\)\. Structural characterization, including X\-ray diffraction, neutron diffraction, transmission electron microscopy, and X\-ray absorption spectroscopy, reveals cation disorder compared with ordered LMO\. The voltage profiles indicate a solid\-solution reaction in partially disordered LMOF, replacing the conventional sluggish two\-phase transition in fully ordered LMO\. The suppressed phase transitions contribute to improved cycling stability and rate capability\. Apparent lithium\-ion diffusivity is enhanced by approximately two to three orders of magnitude, with the difference also reflecting particle\-size\-related surface\-area effects, while impedance is reduced\. Electrochemical evaluation shows a high initial capacity of 337\.53 mAh g \-1 and capacity retention of 86\.54% after 100 cycles\. This work shows that tuning cation disorder offers a promising strategy to optimize spinel\-type cathodes for sustainable, high\-performance lithium\-ion batteries\.
