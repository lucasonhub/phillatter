# Knowledge Dependency and Institutional Decoupling in Global Education Governance: A Case Study of Higher Education Reform in Kazakhstan

**Autores:** Qifang Deng

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In the aftermath of World War II, higher education became a key focus of international development\. International institutions such as the OECD have actively channeled educational resources to support global economic reconstruction, particularly in war\-affected developing countries that lacked systematic educational infrastructure\. While these efforts aim to establish universal educational standards, policy formulation often overlooks local conditions, generating unintended problems\. Existing scholarship separately examines these potential outcomes in educational policy transfer yet overlooks their mutual circular feedback relationship\. Focusing on the case of Kazakhstan, this paper uses a qualitative case study approach and argues that a subtle hegemony rooted in global educational programmes, may lead to knowledge dependency in developing countries and further result in institutional decoupling\. Such decoupling can ultimately generate new rounds of knowledge dependency and impede educational development in developing countries like Kazakhstan\. It also calls for developing countries&\#x27; attention to the indigenous institutional autonomy in higher education\.
