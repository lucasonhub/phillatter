# Hammat, Kenneth: Consciousness Between Object and Relation: Towards a Triadic Ontology of Fact, Meaning, and Value

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper argues that consciousness is best understood as the mediating capacity binding three co\-fundamental ontological domains: object, the realm of empirical fact; idea, the realm of conceptual meaning; and relation, the realm of value\. Against views that treat normativity as derivative of physical, mental, or cultural states, it argues that relation possesses independent ontological standing, defending this claim against reductionism while recasting Korsgaardian constructivism as an account of how agents access relation rather than a rival to its independence, and positioning it relative to panpsychism, neutral monism, emergentist physicalism, and idealism\. A further contribution is a single classification principle \- cohesion, attraction, and stability \- that recurs across all three domains and grounds a specific, non\-arbitrary correspondence between truth, beauty, and goodness and these structural properties\. The framework is anchored, modestly and explicitly, in existing neuroscientific evidence for dissociable cognitive systems handling factual, conceptual, and value\-laden demands, without claiming this evidence as confirmation of the triadic ontology itself\. Applying the resulting criterion to artificial intelligence, the paper argues that current systems, however architecturally sophisticated, remain confined to object and idea, lacking the relational participation that consciousness in the fullest sense requires\. The paper closes by identifying the specific commitments it has made rather than settled, including whether the direction of authority within a value judgment \- whether an agent stands to a value as its arbiter or as answerable to it \- can be recovered in practice rather than merely posited, and the open question of whether value is uniquely captured by the cohesion\-attraction\-stability mapping proposed here\.
