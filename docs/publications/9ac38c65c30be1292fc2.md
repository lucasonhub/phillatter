# Machine learning\-based UCS prediction from wireline logs with empirical pseudo\-labeling: a case study of cross\-well transferability and one\-shot calibration in Western Australian basins

**Autores:** Youngsoo Song; Donghyeon Yoo; Jihoon Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Unconfined compressive strength \(UCS\) is essential for wellbore stability and sand\-production assessment, but laboratory measurements are sparse\. This study develops a machine\-learning framework for UCS prediction from wireline logs under data scarcity\. As the only well with a formation\-specific, laboratory\-calibrated UCS relationship, Waitsia\-3 provided the UCS–density\-derived\-porosity equation adopted without refitting; it reproduced three calibration measurements within 4\.3–7\.7% and generated 3,171 pseudo\-UCS labels\. Random forest \(RF\), support vector regression, and artificial neural network models were tuned by purged spatial cross\-validation and compared under random and contiguous depth\-based validation\. The selected RF achieved a mean absolute error \(MAE\) of 7\.80 MPa and an R 2 of 0\.792 under random splitting, versus 21\.06 MPa and 0\.387 on the depth\-based holdout, indicating optimistic performance when depth\-adjacent rows occur in both partitions\. SHapley Additive exPlanations \(SHAP\) attributed 70\.3% of RF importance to compressional slowness and log\-transformed deep resistivity, reflecting pore structure, fluid conditions, and rock\-frame stiffness\. Uncalibrated transfer to four wells produced MAEs of 43\.18–72\.70 MPa\. A SHAP\-weighted Kullback–Leibler score combining input divergence with RF reliance showed a strong but non\-significant association with well\-level mean absolute percentage error \( r = 0\.913, p = 0\.087, n = 4\); simpler metrics performed comparably\. Leave\-one\-out one\-shot calibration reduced Harvey\-3 MAE from 53\.65 to 29\.67 MPa but degraded the other wells\. Novelty lies in integrating empirical pseudo\-labeling, spatial validation, pre\-application shift screening, and conditional calibration within one cross\-well transfer workflow\. The framework supports first\-pass UCS profiling and targeted mechanical testing rather than stand\-alone operational decisions\.
