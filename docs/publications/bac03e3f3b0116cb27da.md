# Composition\-Controlled Hydrogen\-Bond Restructuring and Interfacial Transport in Glycerol–Ethanol Mixtures for Orange Essential Oil Separation: Experiments and Multiscale Modeling

**Autores:** Guoxiao Cai; Ruiyao Zhang; Fuqiang Qiu; Wei Xiong; Songlin Zhou; Fang Hao; Chang Yi Kong

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Hydrogen\-bonded glycerol–ethanol \(Gly–EtOH\) mixtures are promising biodiesel\-derived solvents for orange essential oil \(OEO\) separation\. However, how solvent composition governs equilibrium and transport properties and selectivity remains unclear\. We combined rotational rheometry, ReaxFF molecular dynamics \(ReaxFF\-MD\), density functional theory \(DFT\), COSMO\-RS, Redlich–Kwong equation of state \(RK EoS\), and machine learning to establish structure–property–separation relationships\. ReaxFF\-MD and RK EoS reproduced the composition\-dependent density behavior with AARDs of 4\.6% and 2\.0%, respectively\. Rheometry validated the ReaxFF\-MD\-predicted decrease in viscosity with increasing ethanol content\. ReaxFF\-MD and DFT reveal that ethanol reorganizes the glycerol\-dominated hydrogen\-bond network by altering O···O coordination and O···H correlations, while COSMO\-RS reveals a composition\-dependent shift toward weakly polarized surface segments\. Polynomial ridge regression and Gaussian process regression were used to interpolate density and viscosity, respectively\. Source\-separated and grouped cross\-validation showed that composition interpolation was more reliable than prediction at a completely unseen temperature\. Interfacial MD simulations further indicate that solute–solvent affinity, interfacial organization, and diffusivity contrast jointly govern linalool enrichment, with the highest simulated selectivity at x1 = 0\.5\. These findings establish a composition\-controlled mechanism connecting hydrogen\-bond restructuring, bulk transport, and interfacial separation, enabling rational design of sustainable biomass\-derived solvents\.
