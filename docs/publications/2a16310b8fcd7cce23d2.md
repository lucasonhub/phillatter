# Wang, Kaining: Human–AI Psychological Asymmetry: A Comparative Framework of Pain Avoidance, Defensive Mechanisms, and Identity Threat

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

A core methodological and theoretical dilemma in contemporary artificial intelligence psychopathology and human–AI comparative research lies in the habitual attribution of human\-like abnormal behavioral manifestations in AI systems to homologous psychological pathogenic mechanisms\. To resolve the anthropomorphic misattribution of AI abnormal behaviors, this paper constructs a conditional, defeasible theoretical framework centered on human pain\-avoidance rigid feedback cycles and establishes a hierarchical human–AI mechanism asymmetry hypothesis\. This study first systematically sorts out two theoretical traditions in clinical psychology and psychoanalysis regarding subjective pain, psychological defense, and experiential avoidance, and defines a generalized pathological pathway of rigid pain avoidance in humans\. On this basis, this paper proposes a weak working assumption for current AI systems: there is insufficient empirical evidence to confirm that contemporary artificial intelligence possesses human\-style phenomenal subjective painful experience, which serves as a plausible prerequisite for the human\-specific pathological cycle\. Relying on this conditional premise, this study further expands the theoretical framework from individual psychopathology to socio\-technical psychological scenarios, constructing a skill\-identity defense pathway unique to human professional social psychology\. Through three levels of comparative analysis—phenomenological experience, psychological motivation mechanism, and functional behavioral output—this paper clarifies the core theoretical proposition of this research: highly similar external abnormal behaviors do not equate to homologous internal pathogenic driving mechanisms\. Current AI agents can simulate defensive resistance, identity maintenance, and rigid behavioral outputs at the functional level under technological substitution scenarios, yet existing evidence cannot support the existence of human\-specific occupational identity anxiety and subjective pain\-driven psychological defense loops\. This paper strictly adopts conditional inference and defeasible assumption paradigms, avoids absolute ontological assertions about AI consciousness, and provides a standardized mechanism discrimination principle for distinguishing behavioral simulation homology and psychological pathogenesis homology in human–AI comparative psychopathology research\.
