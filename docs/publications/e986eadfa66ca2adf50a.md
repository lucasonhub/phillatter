# Influencing factors and genetic model of low\-resistivity shales: a case study of the Longmaxi Formation, Southern Sichuan Basin, China

**Autores:** Xin Wang; Zhe Cao; Zongquan HU; Zhenxue Jiang; Ranran Hao; Haocheng Shi; Weiyuan Gao; Dapeng Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Low\-resistivity anomalies are widely developed in the Wufeng–Longmaxi Formation marine shales of the Sichuan Basin, but their complex conductive mechanisms remain poorly understood, hindering accurate reservoir evaluation\. This study investigates the characteristics and formation mechanisms of low\-resistivity shales in southern Sichuan Basin by integrating well logging resistivity, geochemical analysis, X\-ray diffraction \(XRD\), X\-ray photoelectron spectroscopy \(XPS\), transmission electron microscopy \(TEM\), scanning electron microscopy \(SEM\), carbon dioxide and nitrogen adsorption \(CO 2 /N 2 adsorption\), high\-pressure mercury intrusion, and electrical experiments\. The results indicate that the studied shales can be classified into ultra\-low\-resistivity shale \(Zg &lt; 1\), low\-resistivity shale \(1 2\) types, which exhibit distinct differences in pore structure and conductive components\. Low\-resistivity shales are dominated by micropores and mesopores, while decreasing resistivity is accompanied by reduced total pore volume, mainly due to the loss of meso\-to macropores\. Organic matter graphitization is a critical factor controlling low resistivity in highly to overmature shales\. When equivalent vitrinite reflectance \(Ro,eq\) exceeds approximately 2\.9%, enhanced ordering of aromatic carbon structures promotes the development of graphite\-like electron conductive networks, resulting in significant resistivity reduction\. The 2\.9% value is an empirical division for the analyzed samples rather than a universal threshold\. At lower maturity stages, low resistivity is mainly controlled by ionic conduction associated with formation water and surface conductivity of clay minerals, whereas pyrite contributes as an auxiliary conductive phase\. This study establishes a maturity\-dependent model for low\-resistivity shale development and provides new insights into the electrical response mechanisms of complex marine shale reservoirs\.
