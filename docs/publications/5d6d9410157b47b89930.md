# Rapid bursts and long\-term stasis: phylogeny, evolutionary dynamics, and expression of B3 transcription factors in Arabidopsis, Cannabis, and Humulus

**Autores:** Matteo Toscani; Ainhoa Riera\-Begue; Nina Trubanová; Susanne Schilling; Rainer Melzer

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The B3 superfamily is one of the largest plant transcription factor gene families, yet its evolutionary dynamics in dioecious species like Cannabis sativa remain largely unexplored\. Here, we present a comprehensive phylogenetic, molecular evolution, and transcriptomic analysis of B3 domain\-containing genes in Arabidopsis thaliana , C\. sativa , and Humulus lupulus \. Using a gene mining pipeline, we identified 110, 101, and 88 B3 genes across the five established subfamilies \( ARF , ABI3 , HSI , RAV , and REM\), respectively\. We identified two distinct evolutionary trajectories with the B3 superfamily\. On the one hand the REM subfamily was by far the largest and characterized by massive lineage\-specific tandem duplications subjected to relaxed selective constraint, suggesting distinct or partially redundant roles in reproductive development\. On the other hand, specific non\-tandem clades remained highly conserved under strong purifying selection\. Detailed analysis of the candidate sex\-determination gene CsREM16 showed low dN/dS ratios, clear one\-to\-one orthology across studied species, and unique persistent sex\-biased expression across all six analyzed developmental stages in C\. sativa \. Comparative analysis of its ortholog HlREM16 confirmed a conserved female\-biased expression in H\. lupulus \. Together, these results demonstrate a dual\-mode evolutionary dynamic in the B3 superfamily and particularly in the REM genes and establish CsREM16 as a strong candidate for sex determination in C\. sativa , with a potentially conserved role in H\. lupulus \.
