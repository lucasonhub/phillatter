# Quantitative metabolism: data and mechanism as dual engines for a continuous, computable, and adaptive health framework

**Autores:** Yuchen Xu; Jieli Lu; Shijia Pan; Jingsheng Lin; Yufang Bi; Guang Ning; Weiqing Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Metabolism is a dynamic, multiscale system, yet metabolic medicine still relies largely on static, threshold\-based measurements that poorly capture longitudinal heterogeneity and early state transitions\. We propose an operational framework for quantitative metabolism that integrates high\-resolution measurements with mechanistically constrained computational models to represent metabolic states, predict trajectories, and guide intervention\. The framework comprises four interconnected layers: multi\-omics, defining individual molecular fingerprints; physiological and behavioral dynamics, capturing time\-resolved exposures and proximal responses; phenotypic function, representing integrated organ\- and system\-level consequences; and adaptive metabolic regulation, extending toward digital twins and model\-guided interventions such as digital therapeutics \(DtX\)\. Four sequential validation stages—quantifiable representation, predictive trajectory, operable intervention, and adaptive regulation—define the evidence required to advance from measurement to closed\-loop clinical application\. By replacing threshold\-defined categories with continuous, time\-resolved state representations, quantitative metabolism provides a structured research agenda for longitudinal metabolic assessment, mechanistically interpretable prediction, and prospectively validated personalized intervention across the life course\.
