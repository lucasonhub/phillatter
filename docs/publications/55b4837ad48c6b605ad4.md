# Recurrent mass shootings and the governance of insecurity in South Africa: towards a developmental security framework

**Autores:** Dee Khosa

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Between July 2025 and June 2026, Gauteng recorded a cluster of coordinated mass casualty shootings at unlicensed liquor outlets, an informal settlement and a minibus taxi\. This article treats that cluster as an occasion for theory building rather than as a sample and asks what account of governance best explains the pattern of siting, recurrence and response\. Using a structured comparison of four incidents selected on a stated threshold definition and four stated criteria, it argues that the attacks concentrate in interstitial spaces where regulatory, spatial, economic and intelligence authority are attenuated at the same time, and that the conjunction of those attenuations, rather than any one of them, produces the exposure on which coordinated attacks depend\. Drawing on human security, nodal and polycentric governance and the security and development nexus, and specifying what each leaves unaddressed, the article develops a developmental security governance framework organised around integration, prevention, inclusion and development\. The framework is set against nine rival explanations and presented as a model, as five propositions with stated disconfirming conditions, and as a sequenced programme, anchored in Sustainable Development Goal 16\. Its central implication is that accountability assigned by function will continue to leave the interstice ungoverned, and that a governing node accountable for the space itself is the institutional innovation the cluster demands\.
