# Application of a convolutional neural network for automatic cytological diagnosis of 7 common canine tumors on whole slide images: a proof\-of\-concept

**Autores:** M\. Attini; E\. Newing; I\. L\. Oikonomidis; T\. K\. Tsouloufi; L\. Ressel

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Artificial intelligence may help improve speed and consistency of diagnostic veterinary cytology, but validated tools remain limited\. Objectives To develop and evaluate a convolutional neural network \(CNN\) for automatic classification of 7 common canine tumors on cytology whole\-slide images \(WSIs\)\. Methods Ninety\-one May–Grünwald Giemsa–stained canine cytology cases were selected, representing carcinoma, histiocytoma, lymphoma, mast cell tumor, melanoma, plasma cell tumor, and soft tissue sarcoma, together with a background class\. Slides were tiled into 22,577 image patches \(512 × 512 pixels\) and manually labeled and split using a case\-wise 3\-fold design to avoid data leakage\. An ImageNet\-pretrained Inception\-v3 CNN was fine\-tuned for 8\-class classification\. Performance was assessed at tile level, Grad\-CAM was used for qualitative interpretability analysis, and majority\-vote prediction was further evaluated at ROI level using attention\-guided inference and at WSI level using attention\-free whole\-slide inference\. Results Mean test accuracy at tile level was 86\.3%, with mean macro balanced accuracy of 92\.0% and mean macro F1\-score of 85\.6%\. Grad\-CAM showed attention focused predominantly on cytologically relevant cellular regions\. At ROI level, attention\-guided inference achieved 100% Top1 accuracy \(28/28 cases\)\. At WSI level, attention\-free whole\-slide inference achieved 92\.9% Top1 accuracy \(26/28 cases\), and the correct diagnosis was included within the top 3 predictions in all cases\. Conclusion Inception\-v3 achieved high diagnostic performance on canine cytology WSIs\. The results support the feasibility of both AI\-assisted ROI\-based decision support and fully automated whole\-slide inference in veterinary cytology\. These findings are proof\-of\-concept evidence from a curated, single\-institution cohort of typical, well\-differentiated cases and require validation on diagnostically challenging and external cases before clinical use\.
