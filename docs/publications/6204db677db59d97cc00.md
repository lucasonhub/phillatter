# Niami, Morteza: Finite Continuance and the Modal Structure of Reconfiguration Counterparts, Persistence, and the Preservation of Future Possibility

## Descrição do feed

Modal metaphysics traditionally asks which ways things could have been and how individuals relate to their possible alternatives\. David Lewis&\#x27;s counterpart theory addresses the problem of de re modality by rejecting transworld identity in favor of counterpart relations between individuals in different possible worlds\. This paper develops a complementary problem that emerges when modality is considered from the perspective of finite and historically situated entities: what conditions preserve the capacity of an entity to reach, generate, or sustain future configurations that remain possible for it? Drawing on the ontology of finite continuance, the paper distinguishes modal possibility, reconfigurational accessibility, and continuational viability\. Modal possibility concerns what can obtain within an appropriate modal framework\. Reconfigurational accessibility concerns whether a determinate finite structure possesses a historically available pathway toward a configuration\. Continuational viability concerns whether such a transition can occur while preserving sufficient capacity for further reconfiguration\. The paper introduces the concept of modal meta\-capacity, defined as the capacity of a finite structure to preserve the conditions under which future configurations remain generable, accessible, and viable\. This concept extends the distinction between capacity and meta\-capacity developed within the ontology of finite continuance into the domain of modal metaphysics\. The resulting framework explains how a configuration may remain modally possible while becoming inaccessible to a historically determinate entity through erosion, temporal asymmetry, hysteresis, and loss of connectivity\. The paper argues that counterpart theory and finite continuance address different explanatory relations and can therefore be placed in systematic dialogue\. The proposed framework contributes to discussions of modality, persistence, identity, accessibility, and the metaphysics of change by shifting attention from the inventory of alternative states toward the preservation of the conditions under which future alternatives remain accessible\.
