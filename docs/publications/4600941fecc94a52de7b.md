# Computational modeling and optimized machine learning for multidrug solubility prediction in supercritical carbon dioxide

**Autores:** Nidal H\. Abu‐Hamdeh; Hasan H\. Hijji; Khalid H\. Almitani

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Reliable prediction of pharmaceutical solubility in supercritical carbon dioxide \(scCO 2 \) is complicated by strong compound\-specific behavior and response values\. This study investigated whether sparse measurements from a newly introduced drug can calibrate transferable machine\-learning predictions for computational estimation of solubility in wide range of parameters\. A curated workbook containing 1,618 observations for 60 drugs was modeled using molecular weight, melting point, temperature, and pressure\. Solubility was transformed as log₁₀\(S\), and XGBoost, CatBoost, and multilayer perceptron regression were each tuned using tree\-structured Parzen estimation, differential evolution, and particle swarm optimization\. Evaluation used five\-fold nested group cross\-validation, with complete drugs held out from training and tuning\. For each test drug, predictions were assessed before calibration and after 1, 3, or 5 randomly selected measurements, repeated ten times\. Within the present evaluation framework, the lowest\-log\-MAE zero\-shot configuration was XGBoost optimized by tree\-structured Parzen estimation \(log\-MAE = 0\.584; R² = −0\.087; RMSE = 1\.713 g/L\), showing that the four descriptors were insufficient for reliable extrapolation to unseen compounds\. Sparse calibration substantially improved transfer: the lowest\-log\-MAE one\-shot result was obtained by CatBoost–particle swarm optimization \(log\-MAE = 0\.418; R² = 0\.166\), whereas XGBoost–tree\-structured Parzen estimation had the lowest log\-MAE at three shots \(log\-MAE = 0\.298; R² = 0\.317\) and at five shots \(log\-MAE = 0\.251; R² = 0\.452; RMSE = 1\.223 g/L\)\. Median absolute relative deviation decreased from 82\.4% to 42\.9% between zero and five shots\. These results establish sparse, leakage\-controlled calibration as a more defensible use case than unconditional prediction for unseen drugs, while identifying the need for richer molecular descriptors and external prospective validation\. In practical terms, this few\-shot strategy can reduce the experimental burden for a newly introduced drug by using a small screening set to calibrate a global model before mapping the remaining pressure\-temperature domain\.
