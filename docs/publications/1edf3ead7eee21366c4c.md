# Temporal changes in dilated ascending aorta after transfemoral TAVR: a comparative analysis of bicuspid and tricuspid aortic valves

**Autores:** Qichen Xu; Zheng Xu; Xiaofu Dai; Liangwan Chen; Zhiqin Lin

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Transcatheter aortic valve replacement \(TAVR\) addresses valvular obstruction but leaves concomitant ascending aortic dilatation untreated\. Whether bicuspid aortic valve \(BAV\) morphology inherently accelerates post\-TAVR aortic expansion compared with tricuspid aortic valves \(TAV\) remains uncertain\. Methods This retrospective cohort study included 189 older adults \(BAV, n = 87; TAV, n = 102\) with severe aortic stenosis and ascending aortic dilatation \(≥4\.5 cm\) undergoing TAVR\. Annualized aortic expansion rates were evaluated utilizing linear mixed\-effects models derived from serial computed tomography angiography over a median 27\.0\-month follow\-up\. Results No ascending aortic dissection, rupture, or elective aortic interventions occurred during follow\-up\. The adjusted annualized expansion rate at the mid\-ascending aorta was low and comparable between the BAV and TAV cohorts \(0\.43 vs\. 0\.39 mm/year; interaction P = 0\.364\)\. Progression rates at the distal ascending aorta and sinuses of Valsalva similarly demonstrated no significant morphological differences\. No patient exhibited rapid progression \(&gt;5 mm/year\)\. Although moderate or greater paravalvular leakage occurred exclusively in BAV patients \(4\.8% vs\. 0\.0%; P = 0\.043\), its overall incidence was low \(2\.2%\); however, the extremely small number of cases precluded a definitive statistical assessment of its impact on longitudinal aortic expansion\. Conclusions In this selected cohort, dilated ascending aortas exhibited slow intermediate\-term expansion following transfemoral TAVR utilizing self\-expanding valves, with comparable rates between BAV and TAV morphologies\. These findings suggest that conservative management of concomitant ascending aortic dilatation may be a reasonable strategy in older TAVR candidates during intermediate\-term follow\-up, provided that strict blood pressure control and rigorous imaging surveillance are maintained\.
