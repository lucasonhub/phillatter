# Valence, Arousal, and Emotional Trajectories of Novice Investors in a Trading Simulation

**Autores:** Alain Finet; Kevin Kristoforidis; Julie Laznicka

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study examines how novice investors’ emotional states evolve during a stock market simulation, drawing on Russell’s dimensional model of valence and arousal\. It aims to identify the emotional trajectories reported by participants and to examine how these trajectories relate to their decision\-making orientations\. The empirical material consists of 15 semi\-structured interviews conducted after a 4\-hour stock market simulation\. A qualitative approach combining within\-case analysis and cross\-case comparison was adopted\. The findings reveal four emotional trajectories: positive arousal followed by loss recovery under tension; initial engagement evolving towards withdrawal or disengagement; stabilization through emotional regulation and the maintenance of control; and stabilization through precautionary withdrawal\. Two distinctive configurations further complement this structure\. The analysis shows that valence and arousal do not necessarily evolve in parallel\. Negative valence may remain associated with high arousal in narratives that also describe continued trading, whereas a decline in arousal may be associated with demoralisation, emotional regulation, or a shift towards greater security\. The study highlights that arousal indicates the degree of emotional mobilisation without determining the direction of subsequent decisions\. It contributes to a process\-based understanding of emotions by conceptualising them as trajectories rather than as isolated states\.
