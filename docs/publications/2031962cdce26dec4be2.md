# The Rise of Populism in the U\.S\.: A Study of Regional Wealth Inequality

**Autores:** Aryan Surendranath

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This paper investigates the role of regional wealth inequality in driving the rise of populism in the United States\. Distinct from prior studies that primarily focus on income disparities, this paper’s analysis examines wealth measures, specifically median wealth and the wealth Gini coefficient, to capture the deeper, structural economic divides that underpin political behaviour\. Using state\-level and county\-level voting data from the MIT Election Data and Science Lab alongside wealth data from the GEOWEALTH\-US database, the paper employs a series of ordinary least squares \(OLS\) regression models with demographic controls and state fixed effects, supplemented by instrumental variables \(IV\) analysis using historical state estate/inheritance taxes as an exogenous source of variation\. The results indicate that at the county level, higher median wealth is generally associated with lower populist vote shares, and increased wealth inequality within a county is positively correlated with populist support under the most robust preferred model specifications, with IV estimates confirming these relationships\. Overall, the findings suggest that long\-term wealth disparities may be a critical driver of populist sentiment, offering new insights into the political economy of contemporary electoral dynamics\.
