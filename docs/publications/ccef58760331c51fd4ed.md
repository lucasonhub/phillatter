# Knowledge\-practice decoupling in residential chemical indoor\-air pollution: a cross\-sectional survey in Lebanon

**Autores:** Wissam Ghach; Rina Salhab; Jana K\. Alwattar; Sara Abou Ibrahim; Nisreen H\. Alwan; Emran Alotaibi

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Residential chemical indoor\-air pollution arises from combustion, smoking residues, fragranced products, cleaning chemistry, pesticides, building materials and ventilation behavior, yet household\-level evidence from Lebanon remains limited\. This study assessed knowledge, preventive attitudes, household practices and symptom recognition, with particular attention to whether knowledge translated into exposure\-relevant behavior\. Methods This secondary cross\-sectional analysis used a non\-probability convenience sample of 495 completed self\-administered questionnaires collected between 8 November 2025 and 10 April 2026\. Evidence\-aligned scoring was used for knowledge and preventive attitudes, while practice items were separated into mitigation and source\-frequency indices\. Descriptive statistics, internal\-consistency estimates, Wilson 95% confidence intervals, Spearman correlations, robust linear models and robust logistic models were used\. Results Respondents were predominantly young, female and university educated\. Mean knowledge and preventive\-attitude scores were 47\.3 and 48\.8 on 0\-100 scales; the mean mitigation index was 66\.3 and the mean source\-frequency index was 58\.4\. Knowledge was moderately associated with preventive attitudes but only weakly associated with mitigation behavior\. Higher preventive\-attitude scores were associated with lower source\-frequency, lower odds of regular indoor smoking and a higher mitigation\-minus\-source difference score\. Symptom recognition was stronger for respiratory and neurological than for ocular, irritant and dermatological manifestations\. Discussion The findings indicate knowledge\-practice decoupling within this sample: mitigation behaviors coexisted with frequent source\-related activities, and knowledge alone did not explain household practice\. Because the study used a non\-probability, self\-reported cross\-sectional sample, results are sample\-specific and do not establish national prevalence or causal effects\. Source\-specific risk communication and representative studies incorporating objective exposure measures are warranted\.
