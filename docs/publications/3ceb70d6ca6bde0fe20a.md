# From Reactive Management to Trustworthy Autonomy: An Explainable, Digital\-Twin\-Assisted Framework for AI\-Native 6G Networking

**Autores:** Prof\. M\. S\. Diighe Kolekar Rushikesh Dnyaneshwar; Dr\. H\. B\. Jadhav Prof\. P\. A\. Sawant

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Future communication networks will need to manage increasingly diverse services, fluctuating traffic demands, and complex operating conditions with minimal manual intervention\. Artificial intelligence \(AI\) offers useful capabilities for predicting network behaviour, identifying faults, and recommending resource\-allocation decisions\. However, making a prediction is only one part of reliable network management\. Operators must also understand why a recommendation was produced, assess its possible consequences, and determine whether it is safe to apply\. This paper proposes an explainable, digital\-twin\-assisted framework for trustworthy AI\-native networking, particularly in the context of sixth\-generation \(6G\) communication systems\. The framework combines network telemetry, AI\-based prediction, digital\-twin simulation, explainable recommendations, policy validation, and monitored execution\. A virtual representation of the network is used to examine candidate actions before deployment, while the explainability component presents the factors influencing each recommendation\. A policy\-control layer checks operational restrictions and identifies actions requiring human approval\. The proposed design is informed by a focused review of research published during 2024, 2025, and 2026 on AI\-enabled digital twins, autonomous network management, generative AI, and AI\-native 6G architectures\. The review indicates that integrating prediction, simulation, explanation, and feedback offers a promising direction for more transparent network automation\. Nevertheless, the framework remains a proposed design and requires implementation and experimental validation\. The paper therefore defines research questions, evaluation metrics, and test scenarios for investigating its effectiveness, while discussing the challenges associated with model reliability, digital\-twin accuracy, cybersecurity, privacy, and computational overhead\.\.
