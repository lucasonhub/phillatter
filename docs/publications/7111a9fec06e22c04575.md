# Predicting neurodevelopmental delay among two\-year\-old infants in Northern Uganda

**Autores:** David Mukunya; Grace Ndeezi; Gertrude Namazzi; Thorkild Tylleskär; Brendah Nambozo; Milton Wamboko Musaba; Beatrice Mpora Odongkara; Vincentina Achora; Justin Bruno Tongun; Josephine Tumuhamye; Agnes Kasede Napyo; Vicent Ssentumbwe; Victoria Nankabirwa; James Kashugyera Tumwine

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: Data on neurodevelopmental disabilities in sub\-Saharan Africa are scarce\. Identifying children at the highest risk of neurodevelopmental delay could enable health workers to target these groups more effectively, improve resource allocation and programmatic focus\. OBJECTIVE: To develop and assess a model for predicting neurodevelopmental delay among two\-year\-old infants in Northern Uganda using easily obtainable predictors\. METHODS: We studied children born to women enrolled in a cluster randomized controlled trial \(January 2018\-March 2019\) evaluating the effect of peer counseling on health facility births\. At the trial&\#x27;s final visit \(day 50\), 1,804 children were enrolled\. In November 2020, we followed up 1,421 children \(78\.8%\) and assessed their neurodevelopment at 2 years using the Malawi Developmental Assessment Tool\. We used least absolute shrinkage and selection operator penalized regression with 10\-fold cross\-validation to select the best subset of predictors and assessed model performance using the area under the curve \(AUC\)\. RESULTS: Of the 1,421 children, 70 \(4\.9%; 95% CI: 3\.6\-6\.7%\) had neurodevelopmental delays\. Of the 19 candidate predictors, six were retained in the final model \(lambda = 0\.0076\): moderate wasting, prolonged neonatal hospitalization \(up to 28 days\), maternal education, place of birth, preterm birth, and absence of skin\-to\-skin contact at birth\. The model showed strong discriminative power \(AUC 0\.75\) and reasonable calibration \(calibration slope 1\.37; calibration belt 0\.536\-0\.727\)\. CONCLUSION: We developed a promising tool for identifying infants at increased risk of neurodevelopmental delay in low\-resource settings, which could help prioritize children for follow\-up and specialized interventions\.
