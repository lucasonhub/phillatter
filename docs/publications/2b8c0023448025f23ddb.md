# Naoto, Fujie: How Many Electrophysiological Bodies Can One Body Become? Sensorimotor Reweighting, Electrophysiological Control Reorganization, Mechano\-Electrical Transduction, and Fragility Transfer under the Xeno\-14 Body Principles

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper asks how many electrophysiologically distinct bodies a single biological body can become under changes in sensorimotor weighting, control organization, and mechano\-electrical coupling\. Rather than identifying the body with a fixed anatomical structure or a single neural control architecture, the analysis distinguishes bodily regimes by the non\-redundant relations through which sensory signals, motor outputs, postural constraints, electrical thresholds, mechanical loading, and recurrent feedback alter subsequent bodily possibilities\. Sensorimotor reweighting describes changes in the relative contribution of sensory and motor channels to ongoing control, while electrophysiological control reorganization tracks changes in the distribution and timing of electrically mediated coordination without assuming that anatomical change is required\. Mechano\-electrical transduction provides a critical interface through which mechanical deformation can modify electrical activity and electrical organization can alter later mechanical behavior\. The Xeno\-14 Body Principles are used as a structured intervention framework for testing whether changes in posture, gait, tension, breathing, balance, sensory attention, and movement initiation generate distinct but recurrent bodily regimes\. Fragility Transfer identifies cases in which increased stability or control in one regime relocates vulnerability to another bodily dimension, task, timescale, or future transition\. The broader claim is that one anatomical body can instantiate multiple electrophysiological bodies when changes in sensorimotor and electrical organization produce non\-redundant differences in control, reachability, adaptation, and future reconfiguration\. \(direct link \)
