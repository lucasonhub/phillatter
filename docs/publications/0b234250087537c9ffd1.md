# Do Emergent Misalignment Models Share a Circuit?

**Autores:** Muhammad Haseeb; Muhammad Tahir

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Fine\-tuning language models on narrowly defined harmful tasks can induce emergent misalignment \(EM\) across unrelated domains\. While prior work has identified shared misalignment\-associated representations across independently fine\-tuned models, it remains unclear whether these models rely on common computational components\. We investigate this question using three LoRA fine\-tunes of Qwen2\.5\-3B\-Instruct trained on harmful medical advice, risky financial advice, and extreme\-sports recommendations\. Using direction\-based activation tracing, we identify three attention\-head locations shared among the five highest\-ranked heads of all three organisms\. Replacing each organism&\#x27;s five selected head contributions with Base contributions reduces free\-generation EM from 38\.9% to 22\.9% for medical, 50\.7% to 12\.5% for finance, and 41\.7% to 11\.1% for sports, outperforming layer\-matched random interventions without reducing judged coherence\. Cross\-organism activation transfer further shows that the shared heads exhibit partially interchangeable effects on fixed\-response preference\. These findings provide evidence that representational convergence in EM extends to shared, causally relevant attention\-head components within a common model architecture\.
