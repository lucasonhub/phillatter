# Theory, practice, and perceived competence: Norwegian PETE students’ reflections on teacher education

**Autores:** Pål Lagestad; Knut Westlie; Ruben Vist Hagen; Arne Sorensen

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction This study investigates PETE students&\#x27; perceived competence and their reflections on the contribution of their education program, including potential gender differences and associations between perceived competence and program contribution\. Methods In total, 58 of all 61 PETE students at the end of their seventh semester answered the questionnaire, which gives a response rate of 95\.1%\. Results PETE students report uneven competence across domains, feeling most confident in teaching practical PE and least confident in theoretical sport instruction\. They also perceive their program as contributing more to practical teaching, especially at lower secondary level, than to disciplinary knowledge or theory\-based teaching\. Overall, programs appear to be stronger in developing practical skills than theoretical and subject\-specific competence\. The results also showed minimal gender differences in perceived competence and program contribution\. Male students felt slightly more competent as PE teachers, but most areas and perceived program contributions showed little variation by gender, suggesting broadly equal support despite some differences in self\-perception\. Perceived competence and program contribution were positively related, with stronger associations in theoretical domains and weaker ones in practical teaching\. Discussion Overall, the findings suggest that students perceive a need for stronger coherence within PETE programmes\. The programmes are experienced as contributing more strongly to practical than theoretical aspects of teacher preparation\.
