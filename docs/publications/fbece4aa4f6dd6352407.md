# Characterization of High\-Entropy Spinel Cobalt/Nickel Ferrite Nanomaterial for High\-Performance Energy Storage Applications

**Autores:** Jessica Fowler; Oday A\. Hammadi; Ali F\. Jasem; Said R\. Zainul Abdeen; Hayder G\. Fahad; Rand B\. Lutfi; Sohaib S\. Ahmed

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study presents an integrated methodology combining machine learning, atomic\-scale design, and experimental validation to develop high\-entropy spinel ferrite nanomaterials \(Co0\.5Ni0\.5Fe2O4\) for advanced energy storage applications\. Electrochemical evaluations revealed hybrid charge\-storage behavior merging capacitive and diffusion\-controlled mechanisms, with cyclic voltammetry showing a significant increase in current density at higher scan rates, confirming large surface area and quasi\-reversible redox reactions\. Galvanostatic charge\-discharge tests exhibited distinct voltage plateaus characteristic of Faradaic processes, with a specific capacitance strongly dependent on current density; Coulombic efficiency reached 95% at 1 A/g but declined to 55% at 10 A/g due to irreversible side reactions and ion trapping\. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy demonstrated a low charge\-transfer resistance \(\~4 Ω\) and a Warburg diffusion line with a 35° slope, indicating a porous, rough surface and battery\-type behavior rather than ideal supercapacitor performance\. The absence of a second semicircle confirmed electrical homogeneity and high surface activity\. Overall, the nanomaterial exhibited excellent electronic conductivity facilitated by cobalt and nickel ions within the spinel lattice, making it a promising positive electrode candidate for next\-generation batteries and supercapacitors\. This work underscores that merging machine learning\-driven predictions with precise experimental synthesis offers an efficient pathway to accelerate the discovery of advanced energy storage materials by correlating atomic\-scale structural distortions with macroscopic electrochemical performance\.
