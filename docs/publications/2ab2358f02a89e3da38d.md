# Niami, Morteza: Concepts, Pictures, and Ways of Seeing A Wittgensteinian Analysis of Capacity, Erosion, Reconfiguration, and Continuance

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This article examines the philosophical status of four central concepts in Morteza Niami’s ontology of Finite Continuance: capacity, erosion, reconfiguration, and continuance\. The central problem concerns their status as philosophical concepts\. Do they explain structural features of finite existence, or do they primarily provide pictures through which finite existence is seen in a particular way? The article develops a Wittgensteinian response that rejects the assumption that explanation and representation constitute mutually exclusive functions\. Drawing on Wittgenstein’s later discussions of language\-games, grammar, forms of life, aspect\-seeing, perspicuous representation, and philosophical pictures, together with interpretive work by Cavell, Diamond, Conant, Mulhall, Glock, and Hacker, the article argues that a philosophical picture can acquire explanatory force when it is disciplined into a conceptual grammar that establishes stable distinctions, constrained inferences, and revisable criteria of application\. The article develops four criteria for this transition: discriminatory power, inferential stability, cross\-contextual robustness, and corrigibility\. Capacity identifies a structured field of possible operation and future transformation\. Erosion identifies cumulative transformations affecting the availability and organization of that field\. Reconfiguration identifies structural transformation that alters subsequent possibilities\. Continuance identifies the persistence of sufficient organization and reconfigurative capacity across transformation\. Meta\-capacity specifies the higher\-order condition under which reconfiguration itself remains possible\. The article argues that Finite Continuance is most defensibly understood as a conceptual ontology rather than a metaphysical inventory of ultimate entities\. Its concepts operate simultaneously as explanatory instruments and perspicuous representations\. Their philosophical legitimacy depends upon their ability to organize experience without being mistaken for empirical mechanisms or metaphysical substances\. The article concludes that the distinctive contribution of Finite Continuance lies in making preservation of reconfigurative capacity the organizing criterion of finite continuance and in showing how a philosophical picture can become explanatory through disciplined conceptual grammar\. Keywords: Wittgenstein, Finite Continuance, capacity, erosion, reconfiguration, continuance, meta\-capacity, language\-games, grammar, form of life, perspicuous representation, aspect\-seeing, conceptual ontology, philosophy of language \(direct link \)
