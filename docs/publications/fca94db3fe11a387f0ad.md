# Characteristics of fume components and dynamic emission behavior of crumb\-rubber\-modified asphalt

**Autores:** Liaoliang Luo; Jiasheng Dai; Wenqiang Zhu; Changfeng Li; Long Yu; Yu Liu; Binqian Li; Zhenghao Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Crumb\-rubber\-modified asphalt \(CRMA\) facilitates the recycling of end\-of\-life tires but may release complex fumes during high\-temperature processing\. This study characterized the dynamic fume evolution and representative organic components of CRMA\. Methods An integrated system combining controlled fume generation, real\-time monitoring, and offline collection was developed\. Volatile organic compound \(VOC\) responses from four asphalt binders were monitored at 120–200 °C\. The H₂S and SO₂ responses of CRMA were quantified, and the detectable organic components in fumes from CRMA and its corresponding base asphalt were analyzed using gas chromatography–mass spectrometry\. Results The VOC concentration–time profiles were reproducible, with relative deviations below 10% during the principal response stages\. VOC responses increased with temperature, generally peaking before gradually approaching a slowly varying stage\. At 200 °C, the peak VOC concentration of CRMA reached 3,719\.1 mg/m³, approximately 5\.5 times that of the base asphalt\. As the temperature increased from 120 to 200 °C, the cumulative responses of H₂S and SO₂ increased from 265 to 1,035 ppm·min and from 3,785 to 5,715 ppm·min, respectively\. CRMA also exhibited more resolved chromatographic peaks, with isoprene\-related compounds, benzothiazole derivatives, benzothiophene, and dibenzothiophene forming representative crumb\-rubber\-associated signals\. Discussion Elevated temperatures intensified CRMA fume responses through the combined effects of volatilization, enhanced thermal desorption of rubber\-bound components, and mass transfer, providing a basis for processing\-temperature optimization and targeted fume control\. The reported concentrations are laboratory\-specific sensor\-equivalent values intended for comparative assessment rather than direct estimation of field emissions or occupational exposure\.
