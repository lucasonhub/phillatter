# Береза, Юрий Альбертович: ONTOLOGICAL LIMITS OF THE TRIAD OF DIALECTICAL LAWS: A CRITIQUE OF THE UNIVERSAL &quot;MECHANISM OF SELF\-DEVELOPMENT&quot; IN LIGHT OF THE PROBLEMS OF CONSCIOUSNESS, INFORMATION, AND TELEOLOGY

## Descrição do feed

The present work constitutes the first, methodologically critical chapter of a monograph dedicated to analyzing the limits of classical dialectics\. The object of this study is the law of the transition of quantity into quality, examined not as a universal mechanism of &quot;self\-development,&quot; but as a metaphorical construct insensitive to the ontological complexity of the world\. The aim of the chapter is to demonstrate the inadequacy of this law when confronted with three fundamental problems of modern science: 1\) the semiotic threshold \(the transition from chemical causality to the biological code\); 2\) the generation of complex specified information \(CSI\) in genetic systems; 3\) the &quot;hard problem&quot; of consciousness and the phenomenon of top\-down causation\. The research methodology is based on immanent critique and a comparative analysis of the conceptual apparatus \(&quot;dialectical leap,&quot; &quot;emergence,&quot; &quot;ontological crisis&quot;\) through the lens of the criteria of non\-deducibility, irreducibility, the appearance of new categories, and causal autonomy\. As a result of the analysis, the concept of an &quot;ontological crisis&quot; is introduced and substantiated as an alternative to the &quot;dialectical leap\.&quot; The crisis registers not merely the appearance of a new quality, but a change in the very type of causality and the ontological status of the system\. The chapter also provides an extended critique of related dialectical laws \(the unity and struggle of opposites, the negation of the negation\), demonstrating their inability to explain cooperative integration in biology and the &quot;interpretational closure&quot; of consciousness\. The conclusions of the chapter set the trajectory for the entire monographic study: they substantiate the necessity of transitioning from monistic dialectics to ontological pluralism and the recognition of the fundamental role of information and teleology \(final causality\) in the evolution of complex systems\.
