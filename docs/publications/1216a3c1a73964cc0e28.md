# Single\-cell and bulk transcriptomic characterization of the osteosarcoma microenvironment reveals lineage\-specific programs and inflammatory differences associated with metastasis

**Autores:** Zhiwen Zhou; Zhijun Li

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Osteosarcoma is a heterogeneous bone malignancy in which malignant, immune, stromal, and bone\-lineage cells form a complex microenvironment\. Clinical activity of immune\-checkpoint blockade has remained limited in advanced osteosarcoma, motivating cell\-type\-resolved investigation of the tumor microenvironment while avoiding mechanistic overinterpretation of cross\-sectional transcriptomic data\. Methods Single\-cell RNA\-sequencing data from primary osteosarcoma were obtained from GEO \(GSE162454; six patients\) and analyzed with Scanpy using quality filtering, normalization, highly variable gene selection, principal component analysis, neighborhood graph construction, UMAP, Leiden clustering, and marker\-based cell\-type annotation\. Seven transcriptional programs and ligand\-receptor communication scores were evaluated\. An independent bulk osteosarcoma cohort with metastatic status and overall survival annotation \(GSE21257; n = 53\) was used for differential expression, cross\-platform comparison, and survival analysis\. RT\-qPCR was performed in primary human osteoblasts exposed to MG\-63 osteosarcoma\-conditioned medium\. Results After quality control, 44,189 cells with 25,473 detected genes were retained\. Leiden clustering resolved 23 clusters annotated into 11 cell types; myeloid cells \(34\.0%\) and osteoblasts \(16\.6%\) were the largest populations\. Osteoblasts showed the highest osteogenic program scores, whereas myeloid cells showed the highest inflammatory and immune\-evasion scores\. Reclustering of osteoblasts, osteocytes, and mesenchymal stromal cells resolved seven stromal subclusters, but pairwise co\-expression between representative osteogenic and inflammatory genes was generally weak\. Ligand\-receptor analysis highlighted MIF\-CD74, SPP1\-CD44, and SPP1\-ITGAV as the strongest scored interactions\. In GSE21257, 333 genes differed between metastatic and non\-metastatic tumors at FDR&lt;0\.05; the inflammatory module was lower in metastatic tumors, whereas the osteogenic module was not significantly different\. The single\-cell osteoblast effect showed only modest concordance with the bulk metastatic effect \(Spearman rho = 0\.254\), and a 20\-gene osteoblast signature did not stratify overall survival \(log\-rank p = 0\.968\)\. In vitro , osteosarcoma\-conditioned medium reduced RUNX2 and SP7 and increased SOST and RANKL expression in primary osteoblasts\. Conclusion Integrated single\-cell and bulk transcriptomic analyses identify lineage\-specific osteogenic, inflammatory, and communication programs in osteosarcoma but also demonstrate limited cross\-platform generalizability of osteoblast\-centered signatures\. The RT\-qPCR experiment supports an osteoblast transcriptional response to soluble factors from MG\-63 cells, but it does not establish a specific immunosuppressive mechanism or prognostic biomarker\. These findings should therefore be considered hypothesis\-generating and require independent single\-cell, spatial, and functional validation\.
