# Why don&\#x27;t Graph Transformers attend to the same token?

**Autores:** Alexandre Bismuth

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

In large language models \(LLMs\), attention concentrates heavily on the first token\. This &\#x27;attention sink&\#x27; is believed to protect deep models against over\-mixing\. Even though graph transformers face a related pressure, we find that a SoTA graph transformer, GRIT, rarely forms sinks on molecules, and that it sinks less when trained on larger graphs, the opposite of the trend in LLMs\. In principle, a graph can prevent sinks: we prove and show empirically that its symmetry and locality limit the attention any node can receive\. On molecules, however, with distinguishable atoms and global attention, neither limit prevents sinks; what stops them lies in the model\. We find that the attention scoring function is the main cause\. Replacing GRIT&\#x27;s gated logit with a dot product raises its sink rate from 0\.09 to 0\.68 on identical graphs\. In Graph Transformers using dot\-product attention, removing the sink gives about the same error as removing a random node, while GRIT&\#x27;s most\-attended nodes matter to its predictions\. Attention sinks are therefore a property of dot\-product attention rather than of Transformers\. In our experiments, they form when dot\-product scoring has a distinguishable node to concentrate on\.
