# Management of Patients with Inoperable Malignant Bowel Obstruction: A Preliminary Report

**Autores:** Meenakshi V\. Venketeswaran; Daniel Raj Joseph Thangasamy; Thendral Ramasamy; Thirumoorthi Natarajan; Pradeep Jeyakumar; Ramakrishnan Ayloor Seshadri

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Malignant bowel obstruction \(MBO\) is a distressing complication of advanced cancer with poor survival and substantial symptom burden and often requiring intensive supportive management\. Data on its management in lower\-middle\-income countries \(LMICs\) are limited\. We retrospectively reviewed records of 25 adults with inoperable MBO managed by a palliative care \(PC\) unit at a tertiary cancer centre in India between November 2021 and October 2023\. Demographic, clinical, radiological and treatment data were collected; symptoms at admission and discharge, survival and place of death were recorded\. Eighteen patients \(72%\) were female\. Ovarian and gastric cancers were the most common primary malignancies \(6 each; 24%\), followed by rectal cancer \(5; 20%\)\. Abdominal pain \(17; 70\.8%\) and vomiting \(15; 62\.5%\) were the most common symptoms\. Nasogastric tubes were inserted in 13 patients \(52%\), of whom 8 \(61%\) had them removed before discharge\. Dexamethasone \(22; 88%\) and metoclopramide \(21; 84%\) were the most frequently used medications\. Symptom control at discharge was achieved in 20/23 patients \(87%\), and 19 \(79%\) tolerated oral liquids\. Nearly 80% died within 60 days of diagnosis\. Multidisciplinary medical management with PC involvement was associated with symptom control in most patients with inoperable MBO in this resource\-constrained setting\.
