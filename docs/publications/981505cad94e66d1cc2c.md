# Evaluating alternative trade network configurations under geopolitical risk: a multi\-scale systems approach

**Autores:** Jawad Chahine; Wang Guangda; Fadi A\. Ghosn; Lojain Suliman; Yamen Nissi

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Rising geopolitical risks and supply\-chain disruptions have increased interest in transport\-network diversification and alternative connectivity systems\. This article presents a scenario\-based comparison between a traditional maritime chokepoint configuration centered on the Strait of Hormuz and a prospective diversified multimodal corridor connecting the Middle East and Europe\. The study is grounded in an integrated theoretical framework that combines Geopolitical Risk Theory with a systems\-based approach, operationalized through a multi\-scale analytical framework \(macro–meso–micro\)\. At the macro level, the analysis captures global trade structures and geopolitical risk dynamics shaping route selection\. At the meso level, it examines corridor configurations, infrastructure networks, and regional connectivity patterns\. At the micro level, it focuses on operational performance, including flow efficiency, disruption response, and system adaptability\. This multi\-level perspective allows transport networks to be analyzed as complex systems shaped by both structural and geopolitical dynamics\. Methodologically, the research adopts a mixed\-methods design, integrating qualitative geopolitical analysis with a quantitative, scenario\-based assessment of trade flows, assumed capacity conditions, and disruption scenarios\. In addition, the study incorporates a technical approach through the development of a Python\-based program that enables real\-time computation and dynamic analysis, facilitating scenario modeling and performance evaluation of different transport routes under varying disruption conditions\. The analysis evaluates the potential resilience implications of reducing chokepoint dependency through a prospective corridor\-based configuration\. It does not seek to demonstrate that a global transition is currently occurring or to establish geopolitical risk as its primary causal driver\.
