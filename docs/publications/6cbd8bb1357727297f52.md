# Perioperative risk factors in elderly and senile individuals with fractures of the proximal femur

**Autores:** Шевалаев Геннадий Алексеевич; Aleksandr А\. Spasskov; А\.S\. Ermolenko; A\. H\. Salman Elattar

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Relevance\. Fractures of the proximal femur in elderly and senile patients represent a significant medical and social problem\. These injuries often lead to prolonged immobilization, loss of independence, and a considerable reduction in patients’ quality of life\. There is a steady trend towards an increase in the number of complications during treatment in this age group, which is caused by the physiological features of aging and the presence of multiple comorbidities\. The aim of the study: To systematize and summarize current literature data concerning the most significant perioperative risk factors in the treatment of proximal femoral fractures in elderly and senile patients, with a focus on comorbidity, cognitive impairments, and socio\-environmental conditions\. Research methodology: A systematic analysis of scientific literature, based on processing and integrating data from international and domestic sources over the past 10 years \(results of randomized clinical trials, meta\-analyses, cohort studies\) devoted to the study of perioperative risk factors in elderly and senile patients with proximal femoral fractures\. The results of the research: The analysis of literature data demonstrated that the leading perioperative risk factors include patient frailty and exhaustion \(including frailty syndrome\), cognitive and psycho\-emotional impairments \(including dementia and anxiety\-depressive disorders\), severe pain syndrome limiting early mobilization, and polymorbidity\. Inadequate preoperative assessment of patients emerges as a key factor hindering the implementation of early surgical treatment\. This not only increases the risk of postoperative complications \(pneumonia, thromboembolism, pressure ulcers, surgical site infections\) but also raises the mortality rate within the first year after injury, reaching 25–30 % according to some data\. Conclusion: Surgical treatment of elderly and senile patients with proximal femoral fractures should be carried out within a unified diagnostic algorithm based on a multidisciplinary approach to optimize preoperative preparation and minimize the risks of adverse outcomes\.
