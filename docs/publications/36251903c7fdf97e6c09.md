# A Leakage\-aware Cross\-dataset Evaluation Framework for IoT Intrusion Detection: BPSO Feature Selection, CORAL, and Bidirectional Transfer Analysis

**Autores:** Karrar Imran Ogaili; Sundos F\. Jabbar; Asraa A\. Abd Al\-Ameer

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Machine learning\-based intrusion detection systems \(IDS\) for the Internet of Things \(IoT\) are commonly evaluated using within\-dataset protocols that can conceal benchmark\-specific fitting, leakage, and failures under distribution shift\.This study presents a leakage\-aware, transfer\-aware, and reproducible evaluation framework combining duplicate and leakage auditing, train\-only preprocessing, mutual\-information \(MI\) ranking, binary particle swarm optimisation \(BPSO\), validation\-based threshold tuning, strict eight\-concept cross\-dataset mapping, optional correlation alignment \(CORAL\), and paired repeated\-seed analysis\.Five independent stratified holdout seeds were evaluated on resource\-bounded real\-data samples from Bot\-IoT and UNSW\-NB15\.On Bot\-IoT, near\-perfect accuracy and attack\-class F1 coexisted with much lower and unstable macro\-F1 because each test split contained only six or seven normal records; mean macro\-F1 ranged from 0\.6871 ± 0\.1314 for LR with MI\+BPSO to 0\.8590 ± 0\.0683 for RF\.On UNSW\-NB15, XGBoost \(0\.9219 ± 0\.0013\) and RF \(0\.9182 ± 0\.0031\) outperformed LR with MI\+BPSO \(0\.8738 ± 0\.0039\)\.No within\-dataset comparison was significant after Holm adjustment in the exploratory five\-seed Wilcoxon analysis\.Multiclass results further showed that the MI\-only feature pool outperformed the BPSO subset on both datasets\.Strict cross\-dataset macro\-F1 was substantially lower than within\-dataset performance\.Source\-only results ranged from 0\.3186 to 0\.4060 for Bot\-IoT to UNSW\-NB15 and from 0\.3009 to 0\.4457 in the reverse direction; CORAL improved macro\-F1 only for RF in the Bot\-IoT\-to\-UNSW\-NB15 direction and degraded macro\-F1 in the other five model\-direction comparisons\.Mean end\-to\-end single\-record latency was 8\.108 ms on Bot\-IoT and 5\.385 ms on UNSW\-NB15\.These findings support the methodological contribution: IoT\-IDS claims should be based on leakage\-controlled, class\-aware, uncertainty\-qualified, and transfer\-aware evidence rather than headline accuracy alone\.
