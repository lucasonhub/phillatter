# Essays on regulation, uncertainty and technological innovation in the green and digital eras

**Autores:** Xiaohan Yu

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

This thesis examines how institutional environments shape corporate innovation in two critical domains: green technologies and artificial intelligence\. It focuses on two key features of institutional environments, laws and different forms of uncertainty, to understand when and why regulation encourages firms to innovate\. The thesis addresses three interrelated questions: whether climate laws stimulate green innovation, how risk and Knightian ambiguity influence innovation across institutional contexts, and whether AI laws affect corporate AI innovation\. To address these questions, the thesis develops a unified framework that integrates regulation and uncertainty as complementary forces shaping innovation\. It combines global firm\-level data with patent and policy datasets and employs causal inference methods, including staggered difference\-in\-differences designs and selection models\. The first study demonstrates that climate laws significantly increase corporate green patenting and citations across 90 countries from 1984 to 2022, with stronger effects for high\-emission firms and in environments with robust legal enforcement or financial flexibility\. The second study shows that firms respond differently to uncertainty depending on institutional context: risk promotes innovation in G7 countries, while ambiguity stimulates innovation in non\-G7 countries\. The third study demonstrates that AI laws significantly increase corporate AI patenting across 86 countries from 2012 to 2021, with stronger effects in competitive markets and weaker effects under financial constraints\. Overall, the findings show that institutional environments shape innovation through two channels: directly, by providing clear rules that reduce policy uncertainty, and indirectly, by influencing how firms interpret and respond to uncertainty\. The thesis contributes by integrating institutional theory, the Porter Hypothesis, and the economics of uncertainty into a unified framework, and by providing the first global causal evidence on the innovation effects of climate and AI laws\.
