# Regenerative gastronomic tourism and short food supply chains: a SOR model approach

**Autores:** María del Carmen Avendaño\-Rito; Eduardo Cruz\-Cruz; Gabriela Maldonado\-Cruz; Enrique Cruz\-Domínguez; Olga Yaneth Chang Espinosa; Jorge Martín Guzmán Albores; Adrián Martínez\-Vargas

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Sustainable food systems depend not only on how food is produced but also on how consumers form trust, identity, and lasting relationships with local producers\. Regenerative gastronomic tourism, rooted in ancestral knowledge and place\-based culinary practices, has emerged as a catalyst for short food supply chains \(SFSCs\), yet the psychological mechanisms converting immersive culinary experiences into sustained demand remain poorly understood\. This scoping review synthesizes evidence published between 2016 and 2026 through the Stimulus–Organism–Response \(SOR\) framework, integrating consumption value and social identity perspectives\. Regenerative experiences, heritage workshops, farm visits, and digital stimuli activate internal consumer states, including perceived authenticity, territorial identity, groundedness, and multidimensional consumption value\. These states drive willingness to pay price premiums, post\-trip purchasing, and digital advocacy, with tourist\-generated word\-of\-mouth feeding back as stimuli for future consumers\. The discussion identifies critical gaps: longitudinal loyalty decay, digital mediation after the trip, community sovereignty risks, contextual diversity, and greenwashing resistance\. The SOR framework offers a conceptual pathway to inform the potential scaling of regenerative gastronomic tourism as an economic lever for sustainable food system transformation\.
