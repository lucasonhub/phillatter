# The clinimetric properties of an extended version of the brief approach/avoidance coping questionnaire in the Danish population: the BACQ\-14

**Autores:** Isabella P\. R\. Holst; Dorte Ejg Jarbøl; Sonja Wehberg; Tina Birgitte Wisbech Carstensen; Kirubakaran Balasubramaniam; Lisa Maria Sele Sætre

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective To describe the expansion of the three\-dimensional Brief Approach/Avoidance Coping Questionnaire \(BACQ\) from a 12\-item scale \(BACQ\-12\) to a 14\-item scale \(BACQ\-14\) covering four dimensions and to examine its clinimetric properties including reliability and validity\. Methods A thorough developmental process for the BACQ\-14 was performed\. Pilot tests were conducted to evaluate content validity\. A field test comprising both test and retest was performed\. Structural validity was evaluated using Exploratory Factor Analysis \(EFA\) and Confirmatory Factor Analysis \(CFA\)\. Internal consistency was evaluated by Cronbach’s alpha and item\-to\-rest Pearson correlation\. Hypothesis testing was based on health\-related variables, whereas test–retest reliability was evaluated using Bland–Altman \(BA\) plots\. Results The BACQ\-14 showed good content validity\. The EFA \( n = 150\) showed that both a three\- and a four\-factor\-model could be suitable for the BACQ\-14, but the CFA \( n = 360\) demonstrated that the four\-factor model had a substantial better fit\. Cronbach’s alpha for the dimensions in the four\-factor model ranged from 0\.66 to 0\.88\. The BA plots showed that the 95% limits of agreements were approximately \[−5;5\] for the four subscales, indicating large random variation\. Conclusion The BACQ\-14 covering four dimensions had slightly better clinimetric properties than the BACQ\-12 and is suitable for questionnaires where a brief assessment of coping is warranted\. However, its sensitivity to detect small changes in coping is constrained which may limit its utility in clinical practice\.
