# Multiple Imputation for Recurrent Events With Off‐Risk Periods

**Autores:** Szilárd Nemes

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

When observation of recurrent events ends before the planned follow\-up time, conclusions may depend on untestable assumptions about outcomes during the unobserved period\. This is especially challenging for recurrent outcomes such as hospitalizations or infections, for which each event creates a temporary period during which a new event cannot occur and at\-risk time stops accruing\. In such settings, multiple imputation must jointly determine future event counts and future at\-risk exposure\. This paper proposes an off\-risk\-aware extension of controlled multiple imputation that simulates post\-dropout trajectories in calendar time while allowing exposure to accrue only during on\-risk intervals\. Standard assumptions, including missing at random and jump\-to\-reference, are implemented by modifying only the post\-dropout rate assignment while leaving the exposure\-generation mechanism unchanged\. In simulation, missing\-at\-random imputation closely matched direct likelihood, whereas jump\-to\-reference progressively attenuated the treatment effect toward the null as missingness increased\. In the rhDNase illustration, missing\-at\-random imputation again yielded results very close to direct likelihood, while jump\-to\-reference produced only modest attenuation\. The method provides a practical sensitivity\-analysis framework for trials in which recurrent events temporarily remove participants from risk, helping align missing\-data assumptions, outcome generation, and analysis on the at\-risk time scale\.
