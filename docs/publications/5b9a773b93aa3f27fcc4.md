# Utilising wild genetic diversity for climate\-resilient moth bean \(Vigna aconitifolia \(Jacq\.\) Maréchal\) improvement through introgression breeding: A review

**Autores:** S S Kaviya; S\. Saravanan; S\. Juliet Hepziba; J Sheela; A\. Kavitha Pushpam

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Moth bean \(Vigna aconitifolia \(Jacq\.\) Maréchal\) is an underutilised, climate\-resilient grain legume well adapted to the arid and semi\-arid regions of the Indian subcontinent\. Despite its exceptional tolerance to drought and heat, its genetic improvement has been constrained by a narrow\-cultivated gene pool, limited genomic resources and the underutilisation of wild relatives\. As climate change increases the occurrence of abiotic and biotic stresses, broadening the genetic base of moth beans has become essential for developing resilient and high\-yielding cultivars\. This review critically examines the potential of introgression breeding as a strategic approach for incorporating beneficial alleles from wild Vigna species, including Vigna trilobata \(L\.\) Verdc\., Vigna stipulacea \(Lam\.\) Kuntze, Vigna indica T\.M\.Dixit, K\.V\.Bhat and S\.R\.Yadav and Vigna aridicola N\.Tomooka &amp; Maxted\. Previous reviews have primarily emphasised the nutritional value, genetic diversity and general breeding potential of moth bean, with limited focus on systematic wild relative utilisation and introgression pathways\. In contrast, this review integrates wild gene\-pool classification, donor\-trait relationships, reproductive barriers, linkage drag, pre\-breeding strategies and genomics\-assisted approaches within a unified breeding framework\. It further evaluates the application of marker\-assisted backcrossing, advanced backcross quantitative trait loci analysis \(QTL\), comparative genomics, genomic selection and emerging molecular tools for efficient trait introgression\. The synthesis indicates that combining systematic donor characterisation with reproductive barrier management, precision introgression and validated genomic tools can accelerate the deployment of adaptive alleles from wild relatives\. Future breeding efforts should prioritise multi\-environment validation, high\-throughput phenotyping, cost\-effective genotyping and targeted utilisation of wild germplasm to broaden the genetic base and develop climate\-resilient moth bean cultivars\.
