# Particle capture mechanism and friction\-reducing effect of vortex structures in continuous micro\-groove textures fabricated by ball\-end milling under lubricated conditions

**Autores:** Weimin Huang; Yaoyao Shen; Guijie Wang; Jixin Yang; Zhenhao Xue; Xiaomeng Liu; Zixuan Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Surface texturing has been widely used to improve the performance of friction pairs\. However, existing studies have mainly focused on the role of texture geometry in wear\-debris storage, whereas the mechanisms by which the internal flow field of textures regulates particle migration, retention, and friction reduction remain unclear\.In this study, continuous micro\-groove textures were fabricated by ball\-end milling\.Fluent numerical simulations and reciprocating friction tests were conducted to systematically investigate the capture behavior of micrometer\-sized solid particles induced by vortex structures inside the micro\-grooves and their effects on the frictionreducing performance of the textured surface\. The results show that the local geometric expansion at the micro\-groove inlet induces a stable vortex, which governs particle entry, migration, and retention through shear\-layer entrainment and recirculating transport\. The introduced particles do not merely act as passively accommodated objects; instead, they modify the spatial scale of the vortex and the velocity\-gradient distribution near the groove opening through particle\-fluid momentum exchange\.When the particle diameter is 2 μm and the mass fraction is 0\.7%, the vortex inside the groove is moderately weakened, internal recirculation dissipation is reduced, and the hydrodynamic wedge effect is enhanced, resulting in a 5\.7% increase in the loadcarrying capacity of the oil film compared with the particle\-free condition\. The friction test results indicate that the coefficient of friction first decreases and then increases with increasing particle diameter, reaching its minimum at 2 μm\. This study clarifies the vortex\-dominated particle capture and friction\-reduction mechanism in continuous micro\-grooves, providing a theoretical basis for the design of textured surfaces and the optimization of particle\-containing lubrication systems\.
