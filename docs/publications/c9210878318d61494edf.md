# Functional divergence of the synapsin family in glioblastoma: Synapsin III as a candidate differentiation\-restoring node

**Autores:** Xiangyu Dai; Yujie Ji; Zheng Cai; Haixin Zeng; Yi Li; Haining Zhou; Xu Zhang; Bing Li

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Glioblastoma \(GBM\) remains an aggressive adult primary brain tumor despite surgery, radiotherapy and temozolomide\-based chemotherapy, partly because glioblastoma stem cells \(GSCs\) retain self\-renewal capacity, lineage plasticity and therapeutic resistance\. Cancer neuroscience has shown that glioma cells receive activity\-dependent neuronal signals and exploit neural circuits to support tumor growth and invasion\. Synaptic proteins may therefore regulate both neuron\-glioma communication and tumor\-cell state\. Synapsin I, II, and III share conserved N\-terminal domains but differ in C\-terminal architecture, developmental expression and physiological function\. An emerging model of functional divergence in GBM is supported by a recent preclinical study comparing Synapsin III with Synapsin I and II\. In the tested GSC models, increasing Synapsin III promoted a neuronal\-like differentiated phenotype through a neuregulin 3 \(NRG3\)\-JAG1\-NOTCH1 mechanism, with reduced stemness and proliferative growth\. This review integrates structural, physiological and GBM\-focused evidence to examine how the developmental functions of Synapsin III may be repurposed in malignant stem\-like cells\. Independent replication, durable loss of tumorigenicity and tumor\-selective delivery are priorities for translation\. Synapsin III is a candidate differentiation\-restoring node whose clinical relevance remains to be established\.
