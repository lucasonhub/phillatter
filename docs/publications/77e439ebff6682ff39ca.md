# Making space for street sport: an exploration of public value creation through the GAME House concept in Danish municipalities

**Autores:** Signe Højbjerre Larsen; Evald Bundgård Iversen

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Street sports have gained increasing policy attention, challenging traditional sport infrastructures and governance models\. This article examines how the Danish GAME House concept—publicly funded, hybrid indoor–outdoor facilities for street sport and urban culture—creates public value for communities often marginalized within conventional sports provision\. The study draws on a qualitative case study of three GAME Houses in Denmark, combining document analysis with interviews with user groups, facility managers, and municipal actors\. The analysis is guided by Mark Moore&\#x27;s theory of public value, using the strategic triangle as an analytical framework\. The findings show that the GAME Houses generate public value by providing purpose\-built facilities that meet the practical and cultural needs of street sport communities, strengthen local activity\-based communities, and publicly recognize street sports as legitimate forms of participation\. However, tensions emerge around operational capacity, user involvement, and the institutionalization of informal practices\. The article concludes that public value creation in street sport facilities is context\-dependent and relies on flexible governance, local adaptation, and ongoing negotiation between institutional support and community autonomy\.
