# ON THE TRADE\-OFF BETWEEN BIAS MITIGATION AND UTILITY: FROM SINGLE\- TO MULTI\-TASKING

**Autores:** Junyi Chai

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Bias mitigation is a central objective in trustworthy machine learning, yet improving model trustworthiness may come at the cost of predictive utility\. This tension arises because bias\-mitigation objectives often restrict statistical information that is useful for prediction but undesirable from the perspective of trustworthiness\. This thesis studies the trade\-off between bias mitigation and model utility across different learning settings, progressing from algorithmic fairness in single\-task learning to spurious dependencies in multi\-task learning\.We first consider the in\-distribution single\-task setting, where bias is characterized using conventional group fairness criteria\. We propose an adaptive reweighting method that mitigates between\-group distributional disparities while assigning greater emphasis to error\-prone samples\. We further study fairness when sensitive attributes are unavailable during training through two complementary approaches: a self\-supervised fair representation learning framework and a knowledge\-distillation framework that implicitly reweighs samples through soft labeling\. Beyond algorithmic contributions, we characterize model\-specific Pareto frontiers between fairness and predictive utility\. Our analysis demonstrates that the nature of the fairness\-utility trade\-off depends on the fairness notion: improving Equalized Odds does not necessarily incur a substantial loss in accuracy, whereas increasingly stringent Demographic Parity constraints can lead to a more pronounced utility cost\.We then extend this perspective to the out\-of\-distribution setting by studying fairness under adversarial perturbations\. We characterize the relationship between adversarial attacks against fairness and accuracy and identify conditions under which fairness robustness and accuracy robustness are aligned\. Based on this connection, we develop fair adversarial training methods that improve robustness to fairness attacks while preserving predictive utility and can additionally benefit robustness against accuracy attacks\.Finally, we move beyond single\-task learning to multi\-task learning, where undesirable information may arise from spurious dependencies between task labels\. While correlations between related tasks can improve predictive performance, incidental correlations between training labels can become shortcuts that impair generalization\. We develop a framework for identifying such spurious inter\-task correlations through changes in task\-label correlations under class\-wise resampling and mitigate the identified dependencies through debiased adversarial training\. Across these settings, the thesis provides a unified perspective on bias mitigation as the problem of reducing reliance on undesirable statistical information while preserving the predictive structure that contributes to model utility\.
