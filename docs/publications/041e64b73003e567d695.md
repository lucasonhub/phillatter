# Genomic tracking of Campylobacter jejuni along poultry and pig value chains in Northeast India: Implications for food safety

**Autores:** Arockiasamy Arun Prince Milton; Kandhan Srinivas; Heiborkie Shilla; Balusamy Dhayananth; Samir Das; G Bhuvana Priya; Sandeep Ghatak

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Campylobacter jejuni is the predominant causative agent of human campylobacteriosis\. A total of 864 samples \(poultry cloacal swabs, chicken intestine, chicken meat, pig rectal swab, pig intestine, pork\) were screened for the presence of C\. jejuni using standard methods\. A total of 27 isolates were confirmed with PCR with an overall occurrence rate of 3\.13%\. Highest isolation rates were from chicken intestine samples \(9\.72%\), followed by pork \(7\.64%\) and chicken meat \(1\.39%\)\. Resistance levels were high towards nalidixic acid, tetracycline, ampicillin and ciprofloxacin\. Multidrug\-resistance and biofilm formation featured in 30\.77% and 53\.84% isolates, respectively\. Genomic characterization identified the highest presence of T86I mutation in gyrA and cmeABC system\. The isolates were abundant with important virulence genes such as cdtABC, wlan, neuABC and cstIII \. The isolates belonged to diverse Penner serotypes and MLST types with ST 353 complex being the predominant clonal complex\. Phylogenetic analysis indicated tendency of the genomes with similar ST/CC to cluster together and indicated wide genetic diversity \. To the best of our knowledge, this serves as the first genome submission of C\. jejuni from porcine sources of India\. Further, this study underscores the need for exploiting modern technologies, including genomics, to study various food\-borne pathogens\.
