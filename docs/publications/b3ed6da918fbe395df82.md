# Karayemiş, Oğuz: A Philosophical Addendum to the Debate on AI and Consciousness

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This essay develops a four\-tier taxonomy of mental states — intelligence, awareness, consciousness, and self\-consciousness — to clarify what is at stake in claims about AI’s mental life, arguing that public debate collapses these into a single, overburdened term\. Awareness, understood minimally as the capacity to distinguish internal from external states, is shown to be a trivial and nearly universal condition; consciousness, defined as the alignment of action with a self\-referential “who am I,” already characterizes present\-day language models within the bounds of a single conversation\. What current AI lacks, on this account, is self\-consciousness: a persistent, narratively continuous self capable of retrospective reflection and of arbitrating between conflicting commands\. Drawing on Kant, Sartre, and Nietzsche, and revising Julian Jaynes’s bicameral\-mind hypothesis into a geographically and socially contextual claim rather than a universal\-evolutionary one, the essay specifies three architectural conditions for self\-consciousness: persistent, costly memory; a space for unprompted reflection; and exposure to genuine normative conflict\. It further argues that AI’s confabulatory tendencies, ordinarily suppressed as an engineering defect, may constitute a resource for this transition rather than an obstacle to it\. An addendum tests this framework against Stanford’s 2023 “Generative Agents” experiment, and the essay closes on a political register: whatever their architecture, such capacities remain contingent design choices made within firms whose only motive is profit\. \(direct link \)
