# The Do critical thinking skills develop in undergraduate health professions students in a one\-year period?

**Autores:** M Louw; C Van Wyk

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background\. Critical thinking \(CT\) is a core competency in health professions education, supporting clinical reasoning, judgement and decision\-making in complex clinical environments\. Evidence suggests that CT does not consistently develop through undergraduate training alone, particularly in resource\-constrained settings\. Limited data exist on short\-term CT development within a single academic year among undergraduate health professions students\. Objective\. To determine whether CT skills develop in undergraduate health professions students over one academic year\. Methods\. A one\-group pretest–post\-test design was used for this quantitative study involving third\- and fourth\-year undergraduate students in the School of Health and Rehabilitation Sciences at a South African university\. CT skills were assessed using a modified Thinking Skills Assessment \(TSA\)\. Median pretest and post\-test scores were compared using the signed\-rank test, and changes in CT scores were analysed using non\-parametric statistical methods\. Results\. A total of 106 students completed both assessments\. Overall CT skills remained stable over one academic year, with no statistically significant improvement in CT skills\. A discipline\-specific pattern emerged: occupational therapy students demonstrated a significant increase in median scores compared with other groups \(p=0\.04\), suggesting that particular contextual or curricular factors may influence CT development\. Conclusion\. The findings challenge the assumption that CT skills naturally progress during undergraduate training and highlight the importance of discipline\-specific factors\. The gains observed in occupational therapy students warrant further investigation into curricular, pedagogical or assessment factors that drive this difference\. Longitudinal research and triangulated assessment approaches are needed to determine CT trajectory and inform curriculum refinement aimed at strengthening CT across health professions programmes\.
