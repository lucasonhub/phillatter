# Jack, Anthony I\. ; Robbins, Philip ; Friedman, Jared &amp; Roepstorff, Andreas: The genuine problem of consciousness

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

In Antonino Raffone, \_Consciousness: A comprehensive reference\_, 2nd edition\. forthcomingConsciousness science appears locked in a stalemate: major theories remain unchanged, empirical tests prove inconclusive, and philosophers embrace positions—from panpsychism to illusionism—that sit uneasily with any scientific framework\. We argue this impasse is itself a clue\. The persistence of the hard problem suggests the field has been focused on the wrong question\. Perhaps there is no missing ingredient; perhaps the problem reveals something about the minds trying to understand it\. Cognitive network neuroscience illuminates the mystery\. The brain&\#x27;s Task Positive Network \(analytic reason\) and Default Mode Network \(empathic reason\) employ incompatible representational formats and actively suppress one another\. Phenomenal concepts carry visceral mattering; physical concepts are stripped of it\. The act of comprehending an objective theory gets in the way of grasping what experience is like\. This same antagonism explains why introspective reports have seemed unreliable: researchers conflated tasks requiring analytic engagement with genuine phenomenological descriptions\. The sense that physical accounts leave something out is veridical—but what they leave out is a type of understanding, not an ingredient in nature\. The hard problem assumes this gap should close; neuroscience shows why it cannot\. Understanding why our cognitive architecture segregates these modes is explanatorily prior to questions about what phenomenal concepts refer to\. The field has been chasing an illusion of commensurability\. The genuine problem of consciousness is different\. Understanding the biological basis of the problem of consciousness illuminates a path for progress: building explanatory bridges between cognitively incommensurable perspectives through triangulation of neural, behavioral, and phenomenological evidence\.
