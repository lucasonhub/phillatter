# Associations Between Conventional Breast MRI Signal Characteristics and Immunohistochemical Biomarkers in Breast Cancer: A Retrospective Exploratory Study

**Autores:** Uğurcan Balyemez; Sinan Akay; Hatice Tuba Sanal; Mustafa Taşar

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND/AIMS:This study investigates whether quantitative magnetic resonance imaging \(MRI\)\-derived signal measurements are associated with immunohistochemical biomarkers in breast cancer, extending beyond morphological assessment to signal\-based imaging characteristics\. MATERIALS AND METHODS:The study retrospectively included 63 patients with 66 breast cancer lesions, selected from an initial cohort of 415 patients based on specific inclusion criteria\.MRI scans were performed using a standardized protocol, and quantitative MRI signal measurements were extracted and analysed\.Histopathological data\-including estrogen receptor \(ER\), progesterone receptor, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 status, and Ki\-67 proliferation index\-were also evaluated\.Statistical analyses, including correlation and regression tests, were conducted to assess associations between MRI features and immunohistochemical biomarkers\.RESULTS: Weak associations were observed between pre\-contrast T1 signal intensity \(SI\) and ER expression, and between T2 SI and the Ki\-67 proliferation index \(Spearman&\#x27;s ρ=\-0\.17 and 0\.25, respectively\); neither remained statistically significant after Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons nor after adjustment for within\-patient lesion clustering\.A more robust positive association was observed between the initial enhancement ratio \(IER\) and Ki\-67 \(ρ=0\.39,95% confidence interval: 0\.16\-0\.57,p=0\.001\), which remained statistically significant after Bonferroni correction \(corrected p=0\.016\) and in both a single\-lesion\-per\-patient sensitivity analysis and a cluster\-robust regression\. CONCLUSION:The association between IER and Ki\-67 remained statistically significant after correction for multiple comparisons and after accounting for within\-patient lesion clustering, suggesting that it may represent a comparatively robust exploratory imaging correlate of tumor proliferation\.Associations involving T1 SI and T2 SI were weak and did not survive correction\.These findings are preliminary and hypothesisgenerating, underscoring the need for further studies with larger datasets to clarify the clinical relevance of quantitative MRI analysis in breast cancer evaluation\.
