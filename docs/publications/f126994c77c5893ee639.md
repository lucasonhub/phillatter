# A fuzzy c\-means based dual\-layer flux\-blending framework for high\-resolution dam\-break simulations

**Autores:** Omid Seyedashraf

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Dam\-break flow modelling involves a trade\-off as resolving steep gradients requires high\-order discretisations, yet these tend to generate spurious oscillations near discontinuities\. Moreover, classical limiters used to suppress such oscillations rely largely on single\-parameter algebraic indicator rules\. This study replaces the procedure with a fuzzy c\-means flux\-blending framework for the finite\-volume solution of the shallow water equations\. To this end, an unsupervised classifier is trained on local hydraulic descriptors to assign each interface a shock\-membership grade, and a second fuzzy inference layer converts that grade into a weight that blends a high\-order flux with a dissipative low\-order flux\. The limiter\-based transition is thereby replaced by a data\-driven transition whose behaviour is determined by a measurable flow structure rather than a fixed algebraic rule\. Accordingly, dissipation is graded continuously with the local flow structure\. To validate the framework, one\- and two\-dimensional wet\-bed and dry\-bed dam\-break benchmarks, including circular and partial\-breach configurations, are considered\. Against the exact Stoker and Ritter solutions, the scheme attains $$\\:\{L\}\_\{1,h\}$$ errors of 0\.0664 and 0\.0257 for the one\-dimensional wet\-bed and dry\-bed cases, respectively, placing it between the first\-order Rusanov scheme and the Minmod\-TVD limiter\. In the two\-dimensional partial breach, the framework produces a qualitatively similar diffraction pattern to the Minmod\-TVD scheme without inducing non\-physical oscillations\. For the configurations tested, graded and interpretable flux control provides a viable alternative to limiter\-based transition\.
