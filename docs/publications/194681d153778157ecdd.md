# Criminalization of Indian Politics: Causes, Consequences, Electoral Trends, Judicial Responses and the Need for Electoral Reforms

**Autores:** Abhay Korde; Adv\. Tanoj Joshi

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The criminalization of politics has emerged as one of the most significant threats to India&\#x27;s democratic system\. The increasing participation of individuals with criminal antecedents in electoral politics undermines the principles of constitutional democracy, rule of law, transparency, accountability, and good governance\. Although India possesses an independent judiciary, an autonomous Election Commission, and comprehensive electoral laws, the persistence of candidates facing serious criminal charges in legislative bodies reflects systemic weaknesses in electoral governance and political accountability\. The phenomenon has expanded due to multiple factors, including excessive election expenditure, delayed judicial processes, political patronage, weak enforcement of electoral laws, identity\-based politics, and the growing nexus among politicians, criminals, bureaucrats, and business interests\. This study critically examines the historical evolution of criminalization in Indian politics and analyzes its constitutional, legal, political, and socio\-economic dimensions\. It evaluates the effectiveness of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, constitutional provisions, and the role of the Election Commission of India in addressing the issue\. The paper further examines landmark judgments of the Supreme Court that have strengthened electoral transparency through mandatory disclosure of criminal antecedents and immediate disqualification upon conviction in specified cases\. The study also presents a comparative analysis of political parties and states with respect to elected representatives having criminal backgrounds, using data compiled by the Association for Democratic Reforms \(ADR\) and the Election Commission of India \(ECI\)\. It explores the phenomenon of political horse trading, misuse of constitutional offices, and the erosion of democratic ethics resulting from criminal influence in politics\. The research concludes that criminalization has become institutionalized within India&\#x27;s electoral system due to structural deficiencies and political incentives\. Comprehensive electoral reforms, judicial efficiency, police reforms, transparent political funding, stronger accountability mechanisms for political parties, and enhanced voter awareness are essential to preserve the integrity of Indian democracy\.
