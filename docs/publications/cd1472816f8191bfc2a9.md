# Hybrid CNN–GNN Architectures for Hyperspectral Image Analysis in Kidney Cancer: A Structured Review of Foundations, Evidence, and Research Gaps

**Autores:** Jestin Joseph M; Shanthi T

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Renal cell carcinoma \(RCC\) accounts for most adult kidney cancers\. Its diagnosis relies on imaging and on histopathological subtyping and grading, both of which use only a small part of the optical information in tissue\. Hyperspectral imaging \(HSI\) records tens to hundreds of contiguous narrow bands per pixel and encodes biochemical contrast that RGB microscopy does not resolve\. Convolutional neural networks \(CNNs\) learn local spectral–spatial patterns\. Graph neural networks \(GNNs\) model relations between regions that are not neighbours on the pixel grid\. Hybrid CNN–GNN models that combine the two are an active line of work in hyperspectral remote sensing, and graph\-based models have already been applied to conventional RGB RCC histopathology\. This structured narrative review synthesises four bodies of literature: CNN and GNN methods for HSI, hybrid CNNGNN fusion, medical and renal HSI, and deep learning for RCC imaging and histopathology\. It also positions the work against existing RCC deep\-learning and HSI–GNN reviews\. In the literature located, all HSI studies of RCC tissue identified used non\-graph architectures, and graph\-based RCC models used RGB slides only\. The review then sets out a candidate CNN–GNN architecture for hyperspectral RCC tissue, defined as a testable hypothesis with an explicit evaluation protocol\. It identifies six research gaps: graph\-augmented HSI models for RCC, public data, tissue\-specific graph construction, transfer from RGB pathology graphs, interpretability, and computational feasibility\. It also identifies the main threats to validity in the current evidence: small single\-centre cohorts and the risk of data leakage when data are split at the pixel or patch level rather than the patient level\.
