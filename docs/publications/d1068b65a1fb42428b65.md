# Sedation and Gastrointestinal Adverse Event Reporting for Suzetrigine Versus Traditional Opioids: A Cohort\-Restricted Disproportionality Analysis

**Autores:** Dhruv Patel; Akshita Wadhwa; Gabrielle Norman; Juliana F De Camargo; Sonia Daryanani

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BackgroundSuzetrigine is a non\-opioid analgesic approved for moderate\-to\-severe acute pain\.Whether its early postmarketing adverse\-event reporting differs from traditional opioids is uncertain\.We tested a prespecified respiratory\-depression question and secondary sedation and gastrointestinal \(GI\) reporting domains\. Methods We analyzed US Food and Drug Administration Adverse Event Reporting System/Adverse Event Monitoring System quarterly American Standard Code for Information Interchange extracts from 2025 Q2 through 2026 Q1\.After latest\-version deduplication, the main cohort comprised adult or age\-missing US reports listing suzetrigine or exactly one of oxycodone, hydromorphone, morphine, or tramadol as the primary suspect; misuse/overdose\-related reports were excluded, but the cohort was not indication\-matched\.The primary endpoint was the exact MedDRA preferred term &quot;Respiratory depression,&quot; with minimum counts of 10 suzetrigine and 20 comparator reports\.Secondary domains were sedation and GI events\.Cohort\-restricted reporting odds ratios \(RORs\) were stratified by calendar quarter\.The prespecified acute/postoperative restriction was the most clinically aligned comparison; reporter\-occupation restrictions were post hoc\.No case\-level causality assessment was performed\. ResultsThe cohort included 894 suzetrigine and 2,365 opioid reports\.Respiratory depression appeared in 0/894 and 11/2,365 reports; the primary comparison was non\-estimable\.Sedation reporting was 33/894 versus 52/2,365 \(Mantel\-Haenszel ROR 1\.77, 95% confidence interval \(CI\) 1\.13\-2\.76;Holm\-adjusted p=0\.0244\), and GI reporting was 76/894 versus 157/2,365 \(ROR 1\.31, 95% CI 0\.98\-1\.74;adjusted p=0\.0688\)\.The most clinically aligned acute/postoperative restriction attenuated sedation to ROR 1\.03 \(95% CI 0\.56\-1\.89\)\.In post hoc reporter analyses, consumer\-only sedation occurred in 16 of 277 suzetrigine reports and 30 of 1,647 pooled\-opioid reports \(ROR 3\.13, 95% CI 1\.66\-5\.88\)\.Sedation administered exclusively by health care professionals occurred in eight of 295 suzetrigine reports and 17 of 481 pooled\-opioid reports, failing to meet the 20\-event comparator threshold\. ConclusionsThe respiratory comparison was non\-estimable\.Sedation reporting was elevated in the main cohort; consumer\-only estimates were elevated for sedation and GI events\.In the acute/postoperative restriction, both domain confidence intervals included 1\.These patterns are compatible with reporting bias or channeling but establish neither their cause nor clinical equivalence\.Zero respiratory reports do not establish safety\.Denominator\-based studies are needed\.
