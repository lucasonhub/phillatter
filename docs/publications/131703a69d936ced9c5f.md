# Longitudinal relationships between anxiety and fear of progression in post\-CABG patients: a cross\-sectional and cross\-lagged panel network analysis

**Autores:** Zhengwei Luo; Fenghui Chen; Huihui Li; Weilong Wang; Duohuan Chen; Guo Yongzhong; Juan Xing; Fang Liang; 陶建双

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Patients following coronary artery bypass grafting \(CABG\) frequently experience elevated anxiety and fear of progression \(FoP\), which has a high comorbidity and significantly increases adverse postoperative event risks\. This prospective study explored the dynamic longitudinal associations between anxiety and FoP in post\-CABG patients using network analysis methods\. Methods A prospective longitudinal design enrolled first\-time CABG patients from three tertiary hospitals in Urumqi, China\. Anxiety and FoP were assessed using the Generalized Anxiety Disorder\-7 \(GAD\-7\) and the Fear of Progression Questionnaire\-Short Form \(FoP\-Q\-SF\) at three time points: hospital discharge \(T1\), 1 month \(T2\), and 3 months \(T3\) post\-discharge\. Cross\-sectional Gaussian Graphical Models were constructed at each time point, and cross\-lagged panel network \(CLPN\) analysis was performed to examine longitudinal symptom associations\. Six a priori selected covariates \(age, gender, educational level, duration of CHD, chronic comorbidities, and NYHA functional classification\) were residualized prior to network estimation\. Results A total of 343 patients were included in the network analysis\. From T1 to T3, total GAD\-7 scores demonstrated a notable downward trend, whereas FoP\-Q\-SF scores exhibited a continuous upward trend\. The core node at both T1 and T2 was FoP11 “Worrying about what will become of the family if something should happen to the patient, “ which shifted to FoP2 “Being nervous prior to doctors’ appointments or periodic examinations” at T3\. In the T1\-T2 CLPN, the strongest cross\-lagged edge was from GAD7 “Feeling afraid as if something awful might happen” to FoP2 “Being nervous prior to doctors’ appointments or periodic examinations”\. In the T2\-T3 CLPN, the strongest cross\-lagged edge was from GAD2 “Not being able to stop or control worrying” to FoP6 “Being afraid of the possibility that the children could contract disease”\. In the T1\-T3 network, the strongest cross\-lagged edge was from GAD4 “Trouble relaxing” to FoP3 “Being afraid of pain”\. Anxiety nodes generally exhibited higher Out\-Expected Influence, while FoP nodes showed higher In\-Expected Influence\. Conclusion Anxiety levels gradually decreased during early rehabilitation, whereas fear of progression increased over time\. Early anxiety symptoms showed positive cross\-lagged associations with later fear of progression\. These findings suggest that clinical nurses should consider phased, precision\-based psychological interventions targeting stage\-specific symptoms during different rehabilitation stages\.
