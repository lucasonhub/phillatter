# AI\-Enabled Telerehabilitation for Chronic Nonspecific Neck Pain: Multicenter Randomized Controlled Trial

**Autores:** Yiwei Zhao; Yanbin Zhang; Ye Lin; D Liu; Jiamin Zheng; Siyi Cai

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Exercise therapy is effective in managing chronic nonspecific neck pain \(CNP\)\. Nevertheless, the high burden of CNP continues to place pressure on existing rehabilitation services, especially in remote or resource\-constrained settings where continuous and standardized physical therapy may be difficult to access\. By combining home\-based exercise with remote supervision and movement feedback, AI\-enabled telerehabilitation may broaden access to rehabilitation and support the continued management of CNP\. Objective: This study examined whether an AI\-enabled telerehabilitation program was noninferior to standard outpatient rehabilitation for improving neck\-related disability in individuals with CNP and compared the effects of the 2 interventions on pain, health\-related quality of life, and cervical range of motion\. Methods: This multicenter, prospective, parallel\-group randomized noninferiority trial was conducted at Peking Union Medical College Hospital and Beijing Jishuitan Hospital\. Patients with CNP of at least 3 months&\#x27; duration were randomized 1:1 to a telerehabilitation group \(TG\) or outpatient rehabilitation group \(OG\) using concealed, web\-based, variable blocks stratified by center and baseline Neck Disability Index \(NDI\) severity\. Both groups completed identical 24\-week strengthening and stretching programs \(3 sessions per wk; ≈30 min per session\) and were assessed at weeks 0, 4, 8, 12, and 24\. TG trained at home using sensor\-based movement recognition and threshold rules; OG received face\-to\-face outpatient rehabilitation\. Week\-24 NDI was the primary outcome; Numeric Pain Rating Scale \(NPRS\), EQ\-5D, and cervical range of motion were secondary\. Noninferiority required the upper 95% CI bound of the TG\-OG NDI difference to be &lt;10 percentage points \(5/50\)\. Analyses were intention\-to\-treat; assessors and statisticians were blinded\. Results: =\.15\) at week 24\. At week 24, the upper 95% CI bound was below the prespecified noninferiority margin of 10 percentage points on the 0 to 100 NDI scale, equivalent to 5 points on the original 0 to 50 scale, demonstrating noninferiority of TG\. Both groups showed clinically meaningful reductions in NDI, with comparable improvements in NPRS, EQ\-5D, and cervical range of motion\. No intervention\-related harms occurred\. Conclusions: Over 24 weeks, AI\-enabled telerehabilitation was noninferior to exercise\-matched outpatient rehabilitation for neck\-related disability, with broadly comparable improvements in pain, health\-related quality of life, and cervical range of motion\. AI\-enabled telerehabilitation may be a practical alternative for patients with CNP who are unable to attend outpatient rehabilitation\.
