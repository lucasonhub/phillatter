# Student Potential\-Based Educational Management: A Qualitative Case Study of Academic and Non\-Academic Ability Development in an Indonesian School

**Autores:** Trisnawati Trisnawati; Dwi Ratnawati; Raman Kumar

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose – This study examines how student potential\-based educational management integrates academic and non\-academic ability identification, differentiated educational services, institutional coordination, and assessment\-informed decision\-making at an Indonesian Islamic senior high school\. Method – A qualitative single\-case study was conducted at MAN 5 Tasikmalaya, Indonesia\. Participants were purposively selected from school leaders, homeroom teachers, subject teachers, and extracurricular coaches\. Data were collected primarily through semi\-structured interviews and analyzed thematically to identify patterns of educational planning, implementation, and evaluation\. Findings – Six interconnected themes emerged: multidimensional student potential identification, differentiated development programs, professional competencies, educational resource alignment, collaborative quality practices, and assessment\-informed evaluation\. These themes reflected three dimensions: student\-centered educational planning, institutional implementation, and continuous improvement\. Assessment information informed remedial instruction, enrichment, extracurricular coaching, and follow\-up decisions\. Evidence of data use was more explicit in instructional and extracurricular practices than in institutional management\. Novelty – This study proposes a context\-specific analytical framework connecting student potential identification, differentiated educational services, institutional coordination, and evaluative follow\-up, highlighting how student\-related information informs educational management practices\. Significance – The findings offer practical insights for school leaders, teachers, and educational policymakers seeking to strengthen student\-centered management through coordinated professional responsibilities, systematic documentation, and assessment\-informed educational interventions\.
