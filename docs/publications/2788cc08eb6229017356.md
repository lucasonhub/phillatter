# Potential to improve early detection of lung cancer through expansion of focused and opportunistic screening approaches: a scoping review focusing on potential expansion of screening risk factors and adoption of artificial intelligence models

**Autores:** Tony S\. K\. Mok; Christian Finley; Deniz Köksal; Molly S\. C\. Li; Elaine Shum; Jeffrey B\. Velotta; Pan\-Chyr Yang

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Low\-dose computerized tomography \(LDCT\) is the gold standard for lung cancer \(LC\) screening, but access is hindered by strict eligibility criteria and limited infrastructure in low/middle\-income countries\. A combination of approaches, including expanded screening eligibility criteria and artificial intelligence \(AI\), could improve detection of early LC in patients excluded by current screening criteria\. A scoping review was conducted \(PubMed, Cochrane library, congresses\) to explore two potentially complementary approaches to enhance LC screening\. The first identified LC risk factors and their potential inclusion in expanded screening criteria, allowing more people at risk to become eligible for screening; the second examined studies involving AI\-augmented imaging \(CXR or LDCT\) to improve workflow, triage, and opportunistic nodule detection\. Several robustly evidenced risk factors for LC were identified that are not included in current screening programs; widening of eligibility criteria could be considered based on local risk profile but requires further research to demonstrate benefit\. Opportunistic use of AI\-tools to assess incidental chest images may identify patients with pulmonary nodules requiring further investigation but their role in population\-level LC screening requires prospective validation in diverse healthcare systems\. Lung cancer accounts for one\-fifth of all cancer deaths\. Treatment is often more effective if the cancer can be caught early\. Several countries offer lung cancer screening involving a computerized tomography \(CT\) scan for people who meet set criteria, normally older people who are smokers\. This study aimed to explore ways that cancer screening could be expanded\. One option is to allow a wider variety of people who are at risk of lung cancer to access screening programs\. This study found several risk factors linked with higher rates of lung cancer\. An individual country’s healthcare system may want to consider the benefits of including people exposed to these risk factors in a lung cancer screening program\. A different option to expand screening services is to use artificial intelligence \(AI\) to analyze chest images that are taken for other medical reasons\. Several studies have explored whether AI can be reliably used to examine chest X\-rays for signs of lung cancer\. This study looked at the published evidence that explores this option and concluded that it may be possible to use AI with chest x\-ray to identify patients who may have early\-stage lung cancer\. This approach might be particularly useful in low/middle income countries where CT scans are less widely available\. Overall, the authors recommended that decisions on how to expand lung cancer screening need to be made on a country basis and that more research is needed\. This study reported a scoping review that explored two potentially complementary approaches to lung cancer \(LC\) screening and detection with a focus on but not limited to feasibility of adoption in low\- and middle\-income countries\.Firstly, a wide range of risk factors which increase the risk of LC were identified, most of which were not included in current screening programs\. Individual countries/regions may wish to research the prevalence of these risk factors in their populations to understand whether existing LC screening services may be enhanced, or to better understand where targeted screening services may be of greatest benefit, considering that including additional risk factors into screening eligibility criteria is dependent on population specific validation studies to demonstrate a net benefit\.Secondly, the use of AI\-assisted imaging with both low\-dose computerized tomography \(LDCT\) and chest X\-ray \(CXR\) was explored\. Several AI tools show promise for improving workflow, triage, and opportunistic nodule detection\.Emergence of AI\-CXR in particular, has potential to improve the detection of pulmonary nodules and identification of patients at risk for a subsequent diagnosis of LC in resource poor settings where LDCT is less widely available\. Accordingly, this approach could represent a valuable strategy for facilitating early LC diagnosis in some settings\. Moreover, it has the potential to expand diagnostic coverage to individuals beyond conventional high\-risk eligibility criteria for targeted screening, while enabling more efficient use of existing healthcare infrastructure \(i\.e\., costs and resources\) in settings without established national screening programmes\.Evidence remains preliminary, and as such the role of AI\-augmented chest imaging in population\-level LC screening requires prospective validation in diverse healthcare systems if these tools are to be more widely implemented\. Current evidence suggests that AI\-enhanced chest imaging should only be used as an opportunistic detection tool, or for referral enrichment, and not as an alternative to existing LC\-screening programs\. This study reported a scoping review that explored two potentially complementary approaches to lung cancer \(LC\) screening and detection with a focus on but not limited to feasibility of adoption in low\- and middle\-income countries\. Firstly, a wide range of risk factors which increase the risk of LC were identified, most of which were not included in current screening programs\. Individual countries/regions may wish to research the prevalence of these risk factors in their populations to understand whether existing LC screening services may be enhanced, or to better understand where targeted screening services may be of greatest benefit, considering that including additional risk factors into screening eligibility criteria is dependent on population specific validation studies to demonstrate a net benefit\. Secondly, the use of AI\-assisted imaging with both low\-dose computerized tomography \(LDCT\) and chest X\-ray \(CXR\) was explored\. Several AI tools show promise for improving workflow, triage, and opportunistic nodule detection\. Emergence of AI\-CXR in particular, has potential to improve the detection of pulmonary nodules and identification of patients at risk for a subsequent diagnosis of LC in resource poor settings where LDCT is less widely available\. Accordingly, this approach could represent a valuable strategy for facilitating early LC diagnosis in some settings\. Moreover, it has the potential to expand diagnostic coverage to individuals beyond conventional high\-risk eligibility criteria for targeted screening, while enabling more efficient use of existing healthcare infrastructure \(i\.e\., costs and resources\) in settings without established national screening programmes\. Evidence remains preliminary, and as such the role of AI\-augmented chest imaging in population\-level LC screening requires prospective validation in diverse healthcare systems if these tools are to be more widely implemented\. Current evidence suggests that AI\-enhanced chest imaging should only be used as an opportunistic detection tool, or for referral enrichment, and not as an alternative to existing LC\-screening programs\.
