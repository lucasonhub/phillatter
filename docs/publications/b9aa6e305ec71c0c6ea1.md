# Digital Skills and Human Capital Development: Bridging the Gap in Nigeria’s Workforce

**Autores:** Glory Chibuzo Agu; Kelechi Clara Anyanwu; Ogochukwu Nancy Nwedu; Kehinde Adedayo  Olajuwon

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Youth unemployment remains one of Nigeria&\#x27;s most pressing structural economic challenges, persisting against the backdrop of a rapidly expanding digital economy\. This study investigates the effect of digital skills on human capital development in Nigeria over the period 2000 to 2025\. Human capital development is the dependent variable proxied by youth unemployment with specific focus on three explanatory variables: digital literacy, internet penetration, and ICT contribution to real GDP\. Annual time series data were sourced from the World Bank World Development Indicators, International Labour Organization, the International Telecommunication Union, and the National Bureau of Statistics\. The Autoregressive Distributed Lag \(ARDL\) bounds testing framework was adopted, given the mixed order of integration \-I\(0\) and I\(1\) \- established by the Augmented Dickey\-Fuller unit root tests\. The bounds F\-statistic of 9\.594 substantially exceeds the upper critical bound at the 5% significance level, confirming a stable long\-run cointegrating relationship among the variables\. The error correction term is negative and significant at \-0\.729, indicating that approximately 72\.93% of short\-run deviations from equilibrium are corrected annually\. Long\-run estimates reveal that internet penetration and ICT contribution to real GDP exert negative and statistically significant effects on youth unemployment, conforming to a priori expectations\. Digital literacy, however, exerts a positive and significant effect in both the short and long run, reflecting a structural skills\-surplus condition whereby the supply of digitally trained youth outpaces the absorptive capacity of Nigeria&\#x27;s formal labour market\. Post\-estimation diagnostics confirm the absence of serial correlation, heteroskedasticity, non\-normality, and parameter instability\. The study recommends demand\-driven alignment of digital training curricula, accelerated broadband infrastructure rollout in underserved regions, and deliberate ICT sector deepening through targeted fiscal incentives as complementary strategies to reduce youth unemployment in Nigeria\.
