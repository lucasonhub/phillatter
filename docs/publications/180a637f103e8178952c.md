# Rethinking probiotics for honey bee health: host adaptation, strain specificity and ecological compatibility

**Autores:** Massimo Iorizzo; Dalila Di Criscio; Sonia Ganassi; Antonio Cristofaro

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Recognition of the honey bee gut microbiota as an important component of host physiology and health has stimulated increasing interest in probiotic supplementation\. However, experimental outcomes remain inconsistent, indicating that probiotic efficacy cannot be predicted solely from the taxonomic identity or host origin of supplemented microorganisms\. This Mini Review examines recent evidence through three interconnected concepts: host adaptation, strain specificity, and ecological compatibility\. Host\-associated microorganisms may possess advantages over non\-native or broadly used probiotic microorganisms in colonizing and interacting with the honey bee gut ecosystem, but native origin alone does not guarantee beneficial activity\. Likewise, substantial functional variation among closely related strains demonstrates that probiotic potential is fundamentally strain\-dependent\. Moreover, interactions with the resident microbiota, formulation, dose, delivery route, and environmental conditions can determine whether desirable microbial traits are expressed under colony conditions\. Importantly, successful colonization should not be considered synonymous with probiotic efficacy, while transient microorganisms may still exert measurable functional effects\. Current evidence remains limited by reliance on in vitro or short\-term experimental studies, restricted colony\-level replication, and insufficient validation across environmental contexts\. We therefore propose a tiered framework integrating host\-associated selection, strain\-level functional and safety characterization, consortium and formulation compatibility, in vivo functional assessment, and colony\-level field validation\. Within this framework, probiotic efficacy emerges as a context\-dependent property of a specific strain or consortium operating within a defined host, microbial, and environmental setting\. This perspective provides a basis for moving from empirical supplementation toward rationally designed and ecologically informed probiotic interventions for honey bee health\.
