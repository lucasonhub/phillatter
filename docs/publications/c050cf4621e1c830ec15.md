# Integrating Proximal and Remote Sensing to Improve Characterization and Mapping of Soil Organic Carbon Stocks under Contrasting Land Uses

**Autores:** Leonardo Augusto de Sousa; Fernanda Almeida Bócoli; Eduane José de Pádua; Luiza Maria Pereira Pierangeli; Junior César Avanzi; Bruno Montoani Silva; Carlos Victor Lima Albuquerque; Luiz Roberto Guimarães Guilherme; Marco Aurélio Carbone Carneiro; Nilton Curi; Sérgio Henrique Godinho Silva

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Soils are fundamental to support ecosystem functions\. Therefore, understanding soil organic carbon stock \(SCS\) as a strategic environmental component for climate regulation, sustainable agricultural practices, and maximizing fertilizer application is essential\. This study aimed to predict and map the SCS at Palmital Farm, Minas Gerais state, using land use, parent materials, soil classes, proximal and remote sensing data as predictor variables\. A total of 76 soil samples were collected in the surface \(A\) and subsurface \(B\) horizons using a regular grid design \(200 × 200 m\)\. Carbon content was determined using the Walkley–Black method\. Prediction models were created via Random Forest algorithm and validated through leave\-one\-out cross\-validation\. Environmental and anthropogenic influences on SCS were analyzed statistically and spatially\. The highest mean SCS values were found at the surface horizon under native vegetation and pasture areas \(\~ 33 Mg ha⁻¹\), due to continuous organic matter deposition and lack of disturbance\. Charnockite\-derived soils showed higher SCS \(31\.24 Mg ha⁻¹, surface\)\. Among soil classes, Argissolos stood out reaching the highest SCS \(35\.86 Mg ha⁻¹\)\. Remote sensing data and terrain attributes yielded the best SCS prediction \(R²=0\.43\)\. The main factors influencing SCS were land use/management and soil texture\. Also, strong correlations were observed between vegetation indices, some terrain attributes and SCS\. This approach, combining multiple variables, indicated a moderate potential to achieve accurate SCS predictions using these data, demonstrating the high variability and complexity of SCS spatial variation in detailed\-scale mapping\.
