# Tirzepatide versus other glucose\-lowering therapies and risk of COPD exacerbations in patients with comorbid type 2 diabetes: a retrospective cohort study

**Autores:** Wan\-Hsuan Hsu; Yang\-En Chan; Jheng\-Yan Wu; Yao\-Ming Chuang; Chia\-Yu Kuo; Bo\-Wen Shiau; Po‐Yu Huang; Chih\-Cheng Lai

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease \(COPD\) and type 2 diabetes \(T2D\) frequently coexist, sharing inflammatory and metabolic pathways\. Tirzepatide \(TZP\), a dual glucose\-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide and glucagon\-like peptide\-1 receptor agonist, has demonstrated metabolic and anti\-inflammatory effects\. However, its impact on pulmonary outcomes in patients with COPD and T2D remains uncertain\. Methods Using the TriNetX global research network, we conducted a retrospective cohort study of adults with coexisting COPD and T2D prescribed TZP, sodium\-glucose cotransporter\-2 inhibitors \(SGLT2i\), dipeptidyl peptidase\-4 inhibitors \(DPP\-4i\), or glucagon\-like peptide\-1 receptor agonists \(GLP\-1RA\)\. Propensity score matching \(1:1\) was performed separately for each comparison\. Primary outcome was COPD exacerbation; secondary outcomes included all\-cause mortality, acute respiratory failure, and lower respiratory tract infection\. Outcomes were assessed during a prespecified observation period from day one through day 365 after the index date\. Results Matched populations included 6,080 patients per group \(TZP–SGLT2i\), 4,684 \(TZP–DPP\-4i\), and 6,727 \(TZP–GLP\-1RA\)\. In the independently matched comparisons, median follow\-up was 232 versus 365 days for TZP versus SGLT2i, 235 versus 365 days for TZP versus DPP\-4i, and 233 versus 365 days for TZP versus other GLP\-1RAs; incidence rates were expressed per 1,000 person\-years\. Compared with SGLT2i and DPP\-4i, TZP significantly reduced COPD exacerbation risk \(hazard ratio \[HR\] 0\.73 \[95% CI 0\.64–0\.82\] and 0\.72 \[95% CI 0\.63–0\.82\], respectively\) and all\-cause mortality \(HR 0\.30 \[95% CI 0\.23–0\.38\] and 0\.24 \[95% CI 0\.18–0\.31\], respectively\)\. TZP also reduced acute respiratory failure and lower respiratory tract infection risks\. Outcomes were similar between TZP and GLP\-1RA, except TZP showed lower mortality \(HR 0\.55 \[95% CI 0\.42–0\.72\]\)\. Conclusion Among patients with COPD and T2D, TZP use was associated with lower risks of COPD exacerbations, respiratory complications, and mortality compared with SGLT2i and DPP\-4i, findings that warrant confirmation through randomized clinical trials\.
