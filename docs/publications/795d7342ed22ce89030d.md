# Integrated multi\-omics reveals plasma biomarkers of fatigue in HIV immunological non\-responders

**Autores:** Baixue Li; Xi Chen; Jibin Liu; Li Wen; Zhengnan Shen; Hang Zhou; Peijie Wu; Quansheng Feng

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective To characterize the metabolic and inflammatory signatures distinguishing fatigued from non\-fatigued HIV immunological non\-responders \(INRs\) and to explore candidate biomarkers and potential mechanistic pathways underlying HIV\-associated fatigue\. Methods We performed untargeted metabolomics and cytokinomics on plasma obtained from 18 fatigued and 17 non\-fatigued HIV\-INRs\. Mechanistic insights were explored via Protein\-Protein Interaction \(PPI\) networks and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes \(KEGG\) enrichment analyses\. Results Compared to non\-fatigued controls, the fatigued cohort showed significant up\-regulation of 4\-ketopimelic acid, cholesterol, and 1\-hexadecyl\-sn\-glycero\-3\-phosphocholine, with concurrent down\-regulation of DL\-arginine and D\-pipecolic acid \(P &lt; 0\.05\)\. Immunologically, the fatigued group displayed elevated macrophage migration inhibitory factor \(MIF\) but reduced interferon\-induced protein 10 \(IP\-10/CXCL10\) and interleukin\-18 \(IL\-18\) levels \(P &lt; 0\.05\)\. Functional enrichment analysis suggested the involvement of necroptosis, lipid metabolism, and atherosclerosis signaling pathways\. Conclusion Fatigue in HIV\-INRs receiving long\-term antiretroviral therapy was associated with distinct lipidomic and cytokinomic alterations\. These results underscore the potential roles of dysregulated fatty acid metabolism, oxidative stress, and chronic low\-grade inflammation in the pathophysiology of fatigue\. Cholesterol and IL\-18 emerged as candidate biomarkers associated with HIV\-associated fatigue; however, their combined discriminatory performance was not statistically validated in this exploratory study and requires confirmation in larger, independent cohorts\.
