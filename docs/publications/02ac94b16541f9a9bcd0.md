# The impact of digital technology on green economies

**Autores:** Botond Géza Kálmán; Zsuzsanna Szeiner; Szilvia Valéria Módosné Szalai

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Digital agriculture is regarded as an important instrument for achieving green economies, with the potential to raise productivity while reducing environmental impacts\. This study explores how digitalization in agriculture affects greenhouse gas emissions, pesticide use and productivity\. Methods The research uses a mixed\-methods design\. Quantitative analysis draws on international environmental and economic indicators for approximately 180 countries between 2000 and 2023 \(415 country\-year observations\), combining Spearman correlations, regression models with GDP interaction terms, and K\-means cluster analysis\. A qualitative case study of digital agriculture in Latin America complements the statistical findings\. Results Digital infrastructure is strongly associated with the adoption of agricultural digital technologies, and smart farming investment is associated with better economic performance of the agricultural sector\. Investment in digital agriculture shows a positive association with both emissions and pesticide use, while the interaction with GDP is negative and significant\. Cluster analysis confirms that countries with higher GDP and stronger digital infrastructure achieve more balanced environmental outcomes\. Discussion The environmental benefits of digital agriculture are conditional on economic capacity\. In economically advanced settings, digital technologies reduce resource use, waste and emissions; in economically constrained regions, higher output can offset the environmental gains\. Digital agricultural transformation therefore offers opportunities for sustainable development, but its success depends on equitable access to digital technologies and on policy support that mitigates the trade\-off between productivity and environmental protection\.
