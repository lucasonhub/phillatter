# Retrieval of Winter Rapeseed Total Photosynthetic Area Index Using Multi\-Dimensional Spatiotemporal Features from Sentinel\-1 SAR

**Autores:** Yong Jin; Xinbi Yue; Shangrong Wu; Yongli Guo

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The abrupt change of canopy structure at the flowering stage causes a long\-standing monitoring blind zone for SAR\-based inversion of winter rapeseed\. This study proposed a feature\-based inversion method for the Total Photosynthetic Area Index \(TPAI\) of winter rapeseed, in which a multi\-dimensional spatiotemporal feature framework was constructed by integrating radar\-derived features such as polarization interactions and spatial texture with acquisition\-date\-derived phenological priors including day\-of\-year harmonics and normalized z\-score features, and a regularized linear Ridge model was selected from six machine\-learning candidates\. Evaluated under a spatial five\-fold cross\-validation protocol with geographically disjoint validation folds, the model achieved an overall R2 of 0\.615 and an RMSE of 1\.033 m2 m−2, against an R2 of 0\.584 for a baseline encoding the growth calendar alone, indicating only a modest overall gain from the radar\-derived features; the radar\-derived features nevertheless delivered their largest within\-stage improvement over this baseline at the flowering stage, suggesting that they can partly alleviate—though not resolve—the sensitivity loss of conventional single\-polarization features during flowering\. Error magnitudes were insensitive to systematic bias of up to ±40% in the SAI scaling assumption, and full\-coverage TPAI mapping across five phenological stages, produced from seam\-corrected Sentinel\-1 mosaics, matched the observed growth trajectory\. Given that the SAI was estimated indirectly and the analysis relied on a single growing season at a single study area, these results should be regarded as site\- and season\-specific\. The framework therefore shows promise as a spatially validated, feature\-based pathway toward TPAI monitoring of winter rapeseed in cloudy\-rainy regions, with further validation across multiple seasons and regions required before operational application\.
