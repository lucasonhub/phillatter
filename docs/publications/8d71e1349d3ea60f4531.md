# MAPPING OF PERCEIVED FLOW AND KEY LOGISTICS BOTTLENECKS IN SERVICE ORDER FULFILLMENT IN REMOTE AREAS OF AMAZONAS STATE

**Autores:** Osmir Mello Soares de Souza Junior; Gilbert Breves Martins; Márison Luiz Soares; Daniel Nascimento\-e\-Silva

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study aimed to analyze the perceived workflow and key logistical bottlenecks impacting service order fulfillment for an organization operating in remote areas of the state of Amazonas\. This quantitative, descriptive, and exploratory study involved administering a structured questionnaire to 20 operations professionals\. The methodology comprised: a\) characterizing the respondents and operations; b\) mapping the perceived stages of the service order workflow; c\) identifying and classifying the main bottlenecks; d\) evaluating geographical and infrastructural conditions as well as operational resources; e\) analyzing frequencies, percentages, means, and cross\-tabulations between variables; and f\) using Spearman&\#x27;s correlation for exploratory purposes\. The results showed that: a\) travel was the critical stage in the workflow; b\) the main challenges were long distances, weather conditions, shortages of materials and personnel, and difficulties regarding transport and communication; c\) access conditions received the lowest average rating among geographical and infrastructural indicators; and d\) the bottlenecks were deemed significant for performance\. The study concluded that distance, accessibility, weather conditions, transport, and resource availability primarily drove the identified logistical bottlenecks\. It contributes to the field by systematizing—based on professional insights—the relationship between service order workflows, territorial conditions, operational resources, and logistical bottlenecks in remote areas\.
