# Solvation‐Equilibrium Locking Enables Ultrawide‐Temperature Operation of High‐Voltage Lithium‐Metal Batteries

**Autores:** Qingyu Dong; Nan Chen; Yang Li; Li Li; X\.J\. Liu; Feng Wu; Renjie Chen

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

ABSTRACT High‐energy lithium metal batteries are promising for space, polar, deep‐sea, and other extreme environments, but their operation is constrained by temperature‐sensitive ion transport, Li \+ solvation, and interfacial chemistry\. A solvation‐equilibrium‐locked electrolyte \(SELE\) is reported by combining dimethyl sulfite \(DMS\), a low‐freezing weakly solvating solvent, with Pyr 13 DFOB \(1‐methyl‐1‐propylpyrrolidinium difluoro\(oxalato\)borate\) as an ionic‐liquid co‐solvent\. Cooperative ion–dipole and weak C–H⋯O interactions between DMS and Pyr 13 \+ regulate the free‐solvent reservoir, while TFSI − \(bis\(trifluoromethanesulfonyl\)imide\) and DFOB − enrich the primary Li \+ coordination shell\. Variable‐temperature 7 Li NMR, Raman, and molecular dynamics simulations show that solvent‐separated ion pairs, contact ion pairs, and aggregates remain nearly invariant from −60°C to 60°C, unlike the strongly temperature‐responsive Pyr 13 DFOB‐free electrolyte\. This locked anion‐rich solvation structure reduces solvent coordination around Li \+ , mitigates solvent‐derived interfacial decomposition and gas evolution, and promotes LiF‐ and borate‐rich interphases on Li metal and NMC811\. Consequently, Li\|\|NMC811 cells deliver discharge capacity at −85°C and operate up to 120°C, while pouch cells remain functional after cooling to −122\.9°C\. A 6\.6 Ah Li\|\|Ni90 pouch cell further achieves 565 Wh kg −1 under lean‐electrolyte conditions, establishing solvation‐equilibrium locking as a molecular design principle for lithium metal batteries operating over a broad temperature range\.
