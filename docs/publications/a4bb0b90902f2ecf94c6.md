# Effects of intermittent theta burst stimulation on behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia in patients after cardiothoracic surgery

**Autores:** Jing\-Jing Ni; Wei Pan; Lin\-Han Feng

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND Older adults who have undergone cardiothoracic surgery are at high risk of behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia \(BPSD\), and available treatment options remain limited\.Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation, particularly intermittent theta burst stimulation \(iTBS\), may improve symptoms in patients with neurocognitive impairment; however, its efficacy and safety in this postoperative population remain unclear\.AIM To investigate the efficacy, safety, and potential mechanisms of left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex \(DLPFC\) iTBS for BPSD in older adults post\-cardiothoracic surgery\.METHODS This study initially identified 120 eligible older adults after cardiothoracic surgery at our hospital between January 2023 and December 2025, of whom 111 were included in the final analysis\.According to active iTBS treatment documented in the clinical records, patients were assigned to an iTBS group \(n = 55\) receiving iTBS over the left DLPFC, or a usual care group \(n = 56\) receiving conventional nursing and supportive care\.Both groups completed 20 sessions \(5 per week\) over 4 weeks; the usual care group received follow\-up and psychological support at the same frequency without magnetic stimulation\.Outcomes were assessed at baseline, 4 weeks, and 1\-month follow\-up\.The primary confirmatory outcome was the 4\-week change in Neuropsychiatric Inventory total score\.Secondary \(exploratory\) outcomes included Montreal Cognitive Assessment, Alzheimer&\#x27;s Disease Assessment Scale\-Cognitive Subscale, 17\-item Hamilton Depression Rating Scale, and Activities of Daily Living \(ADL\) scores\.Exploratory synchronization and reduced systemic inflammation but do not establish causality\.
