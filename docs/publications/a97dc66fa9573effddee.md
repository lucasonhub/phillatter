# Gender Sensitivity in Cyberspace: A Doctrinal Analysis of Cybercrime and Gender Justice in India

**Autores:** Loveleen Sharma; Dr\. Suman Paliwal

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rapid growth of digital technology has changed the way people communicate, work, study and participate in public life\. At the same time, cyberspace has created new opportunities for harassment, exploitation, surveillance and other forms of gender\-based harm\. Women are particularly vulnerable to cyberstalking, doxxing, online sexual harassment, image\-based abuse, morphing, deepfakes, cyber blackmail and sextortion\. Men and gender\-diverse persons may also experience online threats, harassment, identity misuse and other forms of digital abuse\. This paper examines the legal framework governing technology\-facilitated gender\-based cybercrime in India through a doctrinal and analytical approach\. It examines the Information Technology Act, 2000, the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the Information Technology \(Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code\) Rules, 2021, along with relevant constitutional principles and judicial decisions\. The study also examines the emerging challenge of content generated or manipulated by artificial intelligence, including deepfakes\. The paper finds that Indian law provides several legal remedies, but these remedies remain distributed across different statutes and institutions\. Emerging forms of cybercrime often fall between existing legal categories, creating difficulties relating to reporting, investigation, evidence, intermediary responsibility and victim protection\. The paper proposes a more coordinated, gender\-sensitive and technology\-responsive framework supported by stronger digital forensics, rapid content removal, victim\-centred mechanisms, cross\-platform cooperation and international collaboration\.
