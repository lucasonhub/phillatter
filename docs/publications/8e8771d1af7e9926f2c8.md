# Investigation of the effect of albatross bill impact on CFRP and GFRP wind turbine blades using Diomedea exulans collision scenarios

**Autores:** Ariyana Dwiputra Nugraha; Ahmad Mamba’udin; Benny Susanto; Ardi Jati Nugroho Putro; Yustinus Erwin Nugroho; Nugroho Karya Yudha; Abdul Syukur Alfauzi; Masaaki NISHIKAWA; Muhammad Akhsin Muflikhun

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Wind turbine blades are highly vulnerable to damage from transverse loads such as bird collisions, which can compromise structural integrity, reduce aerodynamic efficiency, and raise ecological concerns\. This study employed finite element simulations in Abaqus Explicit to examine the collision of a Diomedea exulans \(wandering albatross\) bill with Carbon Fiber Reinforced Polymer \(CFRP\) and Glass Fiber Reinforced Polymer \(GFRP\) blades\. The albatross bill was modelled in two configurations: a shell model representing the realistic hollow, lightweight anatomy based on β\-keratin properties, and a solid model serving as an extreme/conservative case to assess upper\-bound impact severity\. Impacts were simulated at three blade positions—tip \( IP \#1 \), mid\-span \( IP \#2 \), and root \( IP \#3 \)—with velocities of 57\.59, 47\.04, and 36\.60 m/s, respectively\. This study contributes to the existing literature by introducing an anatomically representative albatross bill impactor model based on β\-keratin properties and by systematically evaluating damage progression under repeated impacts\. Results indicate that CFRP blades provide superior impact resistance, showing peak von\-Mises stress of 1654 MPa \(CFRP\-B1\), maximum contact force of 46\.98 kN \(CFRP\-B2\), and energy absorption of 242\.33 J\. GFRP\-B1 exhibited the maximum displacement among all scenarios, with a value of 148\.79 mm\. While no major failure was observed, fiber damage was detected at IP \#2 in CFRP under repeated impacts, emphasizing the role of local stiffness\. albatross bird models, especially the solid bill, experienced severe deformation and fracture, underscoring ecological risks\. These findings provide a quantitative basis for improving blade design, including bioinspired coatings and bird deterrent strategies to enhance both durability and wildlife safety\.
