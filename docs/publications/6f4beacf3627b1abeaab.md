# From Pattern to Interpretation: Topic Modeling as Editorial Record

**Autores:** Johannes Ioannu; Fernanda Alvares Freire; Frederike Neuber

**Data:** 2026-10-11 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Presentation at the 21st Annual Conference of the European Society for Textual Scholarship \(ESTS 2026\), Lublin, Poland\. 30\.05\.2026\. Abstract: Machine learning and artificial intelligence open new possibilities for dealing with large text corpora, where content structuring becomes essential because readers can no longer grasp the corpus through close reading alone\. We think that approaches such as topic modeling or AI\-based keyword assignment in digital editions can help address this challenge\. Building on Hans Zeller’s foundational distinction between record \(Befund\) and interpretation \(Deutung\) \(Zeller 1971\), we argue that these computational approaches constitute a new layer of record\. Although their analytical character may suggest an interpretive element, systematic and transparent documentation ensures that they do not exceed the interpretive scope inherent in any editorial practice \(Martens 1994, S\. 74; Sahle 2013, S\. 210\)\. Like qualitative editorial findings, this computational record may contain biases and opacities, which underscores the need for transparent documentation \(Henny\-Krahmer, Neuber 2014\)\. Yet these methods could offer a curatorial angle, offering perspectives that can encourage editors to reassess their own biases and assumptions\. By exposing patterns beyond the reach of close reading, they enhance the accessibility of the material and temporarily complement the editor’s work, offering additional interpretive pathways\. Using the digital edition of Jean Paul&\#x27;s letters \(Bernauer et al\. 2018\) as a case study \(ca\. 12,000 documents\), we demonstrate how computational methods for content analysis can be integrated into editorial data models and interfaces\. We will focus particularly on the interplay between computational findings \(which remain record\) and their interpretation \(which requires human scholarly judgment\); a relationship which we conceive as complementary rather than hierarchical\.
