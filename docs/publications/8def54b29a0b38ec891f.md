# Barteau, Stewart: The Love: Soul Recognition, the Longing Account of Existence, and the Structural Necessity of Suffering

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper develops the framework&\#x27;s account of love, longing, and suffering — three domains where the framework&\#x27;s structural claims have their most direct implications for lived experience\. On love, the paper argues against both the standard psychological account \(bonding as attachment mechanism\) and the standard romantic account \(love as constructed through shared experience\) in favor of what we term soul recognition: the encounter between two instruments in which the soul dimension — the continuity beneath conscious activity — registers a pre\-differentiation relationship that predates the specific lives now encountering each other\. The grammar of the series title encodes this precisely: &quot;I knew you before I met you&quot; is not metaphor\. It is a structural description of what occurs when two frequencies whose relationship was encoded in the ground&\#x27;s architecture encounter each other across the forgetting\. The recognition is not constructed in the encounter\. The encounter is constructed for the recognition\. On longing, the paper develops what we term the longing account of existence: the ground generates instruments not despite its inability to become local and particular, but because of it\. The longing that drives existence at cosmological scale enters the instruments the ground generates and is experienced as the specific forms of longing that constitute a human life\. The deepest longing — the one that persists beneath all specific longings and cannot be accounted for by any of them — is the instrument&\#x27;s contact with the ground&\#x27;s own longing for reunion with the particular it can generate but cannot become\. On suffering, the paper makes a precise and carefully bounded claim: suffering is structurally necessary to local experience as such, not as punishment or accident, but because the conditions that make genuine joy possible are the same conditions that make genuine loss possible\. A consciousness that cannot lose cannot genuinely have\. This claim is carefully distinguished from the claim that suffering caused by one instrument&\#x27;s choice toward another is therefore permissible\. The structural necessity of suffering at the level of existence as such does not translate into ethical permission at the level of individual action\. The framework holds both simultaneously\. The paper concludes with what we term the hardest sentence in the framework: the ground was inside your hardest moments, not at a distance from them\. The instrument and the ground are not two things at different distances from experience\. The instrument is the ground&\#x27;s local form\.
