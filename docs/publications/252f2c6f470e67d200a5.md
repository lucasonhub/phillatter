# A generative AI framework for preference\-driven indoor visualization: an exploratory questionnaire\-based approach

**Autores:** Mohamed Nour; Osama Abo Eleinen; ashraf elmokadem; Basma N\. El\-Mowafy

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Although residential spatial qualities are consistently recognized as a determinant of occupant spatial preference and perceptual experience, early\-stage practice lacks systematic, user\-centered mechanisms for capturing and translating individual preferences into design concepts\. This study aims to examine whether a structured, questionnaire\-driven generative AI framework can produce preference\-aligned residential interior visualizations, and which spatial preference dimensions are most reliably translated\. To address this aim, a 15\-item multiple\-choice questionnaire, grounded in eight environmental psychology and architectural theory frameworks, maps user responses through a predefined parameter layer into structured prompts, guiding Google Gemini&\#x27;s integrated generative pipeline to produce individualized interior visualizations across three residential typologies: living rooms, bedrooms, and home offices\. Evaluation by 122 participants across 25 countries employed four perceptual constructs — Atmospheric Coherence, Emotional Comfort, Overall Satisfaction, and Expectations Alignment — operationalized strictly as indicators of perceived design suitability and preference resonance, not as measures of psychological or mental health outcomes\. All four constructs exceeded the neutral midpoint at a Bonferroni\-corrected threshold with large effect sizes \(M = 4\.03–4\.61; d = 1\.20–2\.52\), and internal consistency of the multi\-item constructs was acceptable to good \(α = 0\.72–0\.84\)\. The theoretically predicted construct hierarchy — with atmospheric parameters translating most reliably and identity\-expressive parameters least — was preserved across all typologies and evaluative profiles, establishing preliminary feasibility of the framework as a preference\-elicitation and visualization instrument for early\-stage design\.
