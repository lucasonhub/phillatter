# When is travel\-health AI inference transportable? Mobility\-conditioned admissibility for cross\-border disease risk and decision support

**Autores:** Adegoke Olusegun Adefolalu

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

BACKGROUND: Artificial intelligence is increasingly used for infectious disease surveillance, traveller risk assessment, outbreak forecasting and decision support\. Travel creates a distinctive transportability problem because people move between epidemiological, geographic, climatic and healthcare contexts in which the conditions supporting an inference may change rapidly\. METHODS: This conceptual/theoretical methodological\-development study was informed by a targeted narrative review of travel medicine, infectious disease surveillance, human mobility, border health, mass gatherings, antimicrobial resistance and health\-AI validation literature\. RESULTS: We develop a mobility\-conditioned admissibility framework that treats travel as movement across origin, transit, destination and return regimes\. Interpretation is conditioned on mobility state, epidemiological regime, exposure history, time\-dependent constraints and clinical or biological constraints\. Five proposed operational states are retained: supported inference, conditionally transportable inference, active monitoring, restricted interpretation and withholding\. The framework also distinguishes low probability from infeasibility and requires preservation of rare but epidemiologically feasible events\. CONCLUSIONS: Model availability and conventional predictive validity do not establish inferential transportability across changing travel contexts\. Mobility\-conditioned admissibility provides a complementary use\-boundary layer for travel health AI by making cross\-regime support explicit before outputs are interpreted or released\. Its incremental value requires prospective evaluation against calibrated uncertainty, external validation and dataset\-shift monitoring\.
