# CD23\-TARGETED IMMUNOTHERAPY FOR GIANT CELL MYOCARDITIS

**Autores:** Michael John Dochniak

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Giant cell myocarditis \(GCM\) is a rare, rapidly progressive cardiac disorder characterized by giant, multinucleated cells that contribute to severe myocardial damage\. Existing combination immunosuppressive therapies, although directed at immune\-mediated injury, lack precision and are associated with considerable adverse effects\. A CD23\-targeted intervention holds promise as a more selective, safer adjunctive treatment\. This review evaluates a strategy centered on the low\-affinity IgE receptor \(CD23/FcεRII\), predominantly expressed on alternatively activated \(M2a\) macrophages and B cells\. The proposed method uses engineered anti\-IgE–IgE complexes to engage CD23, inhibiting macrophage fusion—a defining feature of GCM—and fostering a more anti\-inflammatory immune milieu\. This therapy also aims to protect myocardial tissue by suppressing pathogenic T\-cell responses and slowing disease progression\. The review details the scientific basis for this approach, outlines laboratory testing protocols, suggests optimal delivery systems, and considers translational hurdles to clinical implementation\.
