# Clinical Performance of the IWGDF Risk Stratification System for Diabetic Foot Ulceration: A Gender\-Disaggregated Study of 713 Patients

**Autores:** Fatimah Z Alenazi; Thamer Alslamah; Basmah F\. Alharbi; Wafa Ibrahim Alotaibi; Metab Algeffari; Yasir Alsalamah; Faisal I\. Almohaileb; Mirna Awni Fawaz; António Raposo

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

INTRODUCTION: Diabetic foot ulceration \(DFU\) is a major complication of diabetes and is a leading cause of lower\-extremity amputation\. This study evaluated the cross\-sectional discriminative performance of the International Working Group on the Diabetic Foot \(IWGDF\) risk classification for prevalent active DFU, described gender\-disaggregated ulcer prevalence, and identified factors independently associated with moderate/high IWGDF risk in a specialist diabetes center\. METHODS: In this retrospective cross\-sectional study, 713 adults with diabetes mellitus who attended the diabetic foot service of a university diabetes center were classified according to the IWGDF risk system\. The prevalence of active DFU was compared across risk categories using the Cochran\-Armitage trend test\. Multivariable logistic regression was used to identify factors associated with moderate/high IWGDF risk and active DFU\. RESULTS: Active DFU was present in 21 patients \(2\.9%\), with no significant difference between men \(3\.8%\) and women \(2\.1%\)\. Overall, 17\.3% of the patients were classified as having moderate or high risk\. Ulcer prevalence increased progressively across the IWGDF categories, from 0\.0% in the very\-low\-risk group to 38\.5% in the high\-risk group \(p &lt; 0\.001\)\. The IWGDF classification showed excellent cross\-sectional discrimination for prevalent active DFU \(c\-statistic 0\.938\), with 90\.5% sensitivity and 99\.7% negative predictive value at the moderate/high\-risk threshold\. Older age and higher glycated hemoglobin \(HbA1c\) levels were independently associated with moderate/high IWGDF risk, whereas gender was not\. CONCLUSION: The IWGDF classification showed excellent cross\-sectional discrimination for prevalent active DFU\. Older age and poorer glycemic control, but not gender, were independently associated with moderate/high IWGDF risk\. These findings support routine IWGDF\-guided risk stratification in specialist diabetes care, although prospective studies are required to establish its performance in predicting future ulceration\.
