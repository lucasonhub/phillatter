# Novel Tool to Predict Primary Tumor Location in the Setting of Metastatic Cancer With Unknown Primary

**Autores:** Kerolous Eldeeb; Saad Badat; Haley Kopp Perlow; Atallah Baydoun; Prashant Vempati; Yifan Yang; Daniel Eidelberg Spratt; Ming Wang; Nicholas George Zaorsky

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

PURPOSE: This study aimed to develop and validate a tool to predict the primary tumor site in patients with metastatic cancer of unknown primary using demographic and clinical variables, including imaging\-derived sites of metastasis, without pathology or laboratory data\. METHODS: In this population\-based study, data were extracted from the SEER database\. Patients diagnosed between 2010 and 2020 with a known primary cancer site, metastatic staging, and metastasis in the bone, liver, brain, and lungs were included\. Patients were randomly split into training \(70%\) and/or testing \(30%\) sets\. A total of 27 primary tumor sites were included\. A gradient boosting model was trained to predict the primary tumor site, with random forest and classification tree models evaluated as comparators\. Random guess and frequency\-weighted guessing models were used for baseline comparison\. RESULTS: A total of 689,715 patients were included from 9,208,295 queried\. The most common primary cancer sites were non\-small cell lung cancer \(NSCLC\) \(n = 211,338\), intestines \(n = 64,187\), and pancreas \(n = 60,310\)\. The baseline models achieved Top\-5 accuracies of 0\.179 for a random guess and 0\.476 for a frequency\-weighted guess\. The gradient boosting model achieved Top\-1, Top\-3, and Top\-5 accuracies of 0\.40 ± 0\.001, 0\.680 ± 0\.001, and 0\.790 ± 0\.001, respectively, across 100 Monte\-Carlo replicates\. Top\-5 accuracy was highest for primary NSCLC \(0\.990 ± 0\.001\) and intestinal cancer \(0\.900 ± 0\.003\)\. Our model was most influenced by sex \(gain = 0\.27\) and metastasis to bone \(gain = 0\.20\)\. A web app was constructed: primarycancerpredictor\.netlify\.app\. CONCLUSION: This study developed and evaluated a gradient boosting machine learning model and a user\-friendly web tool to aid in the diagnosis of cancer of unknown primary\.
