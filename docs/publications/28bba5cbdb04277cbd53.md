# Interpretability as a leakage detector: a case study in machine\-learning\-based exoplanet detection with TESS

**Autores:** Alvaro Manuel Rodriguez\-Rodriguez; Eva Andrés; Gabriel Marín Díaz

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract The Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite \(TESS\) generates photometric time series for hundreds of thousands of stars, far more than can be vetted by hand, motivating machine\-learning triage\. We present a low\-resource, interpretable pipeline that classifies TESS light curves as transit\-bearing or not using a random forest trained on engineered summary features, treating feature\-importance analysis as a formal auditing step, and apply it to Sector 68\. The positive class comprises TESS\-confirmed planet hosts; the negative class comprises Sector 68 targets with no catalogued planet or candidate, assembled from a different source\. Auditing the model&\#x27;s feature\-importance ranking exposes a confound: the two dominant features, the flux energy and its logarithm, are for median\-normalized light curves essentially proportional to the number of cadences, that is, to observational baseline\. A transit\-blind ranking by this feature alone already separates the classes with an AUC of 0\.734, and because our train/test split cannot control for the classes&\#x27; differing provenance, the full model&\#x27;s held\-out ROC\-AUC of 0\.95 \(precision 0\.94, recall 0\.74, F1 0\.83\) is best read as performance under the current sampling procedure; isolating the confound&\#x27;s precise causal share is left as an open question for a dedicated ablation study\. Some evidence of genuine signal survives this caveat: applied to 400 held\-out candidates that share the negative class&\#x27;s baseline and so cannot benefit from the confound, the pipeline still flags 61 \(15%\) as probable planets, with the highest\-probability cases showing genuine periodic transit dips\. We give concrete recommendations for detecting and avoiding this failure mode: provenance\-matched class construction, baseline\-invariant features, controlled ablation, and routine interpretability\-based leakage audits\. The case study is offered as an instance of auditing scientific machine learning with its own tools, and a cautionary lesson for machine\-learning\-based exoplanet detection\.
