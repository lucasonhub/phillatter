# Zhang, Yu: The Unanswerable Arguer: Large Language Models, Argumentative Injustice, and the Structural Displacement of Epistemic Standing

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Topoi\_\. 2026Large Language Models \(LLMs\) introduce a novel form of argumentative injustice rooted in the structural absence of agential answerability\. Traditional theories of epistemic injustice explain credibility distortions in terms of identity prejudice, and thus presuppose human agency and mutual accountability\. Extending the analysis of argumentative injustice to non\-human systems reveals a different source of epistemic asymmetry: LLMs can produce linguistically competent outputs without comprehension and lack the moral receptiveness required for genuine accountability\. Recent accounts of generative epistemic injustice show how generative AI amplifies testimonial and hermeneutical injustices and undermines collective knowledge practices, but they remain focused on identity‑based harms rather than the standing of non‑agential systems as arguers\. Insofar as AI deployment creates a distributed agency that obscures responsibility, LLMs function socially as unanswerable arguers within practices of public reasoning\. I develop the concept of answerability‑void argumentation to diagnose how credibility excess for non‑agential systems generates a new agency‑based form of argumentative injustice\. I then introduce the notion of agential argumentative injustice to capture the harms inflicted on human arguers when structurally unanswerable systems are treated as legitimate knowers‑arguers\. Finally, I argue that addressing this displacement requires constructive epistemic governance, prioritizing institutional ecological control and rigorous norm design so that ultimate argumentative authority remains with accountable human agents\. \(direct link \)
