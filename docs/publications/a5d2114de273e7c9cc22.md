# Estimates of transition burden, geographic distribution, and risk\-stratified care for adolescent and young adult cancer survivors: A multi\-centre analysis from a low\- and middle\-income country

**Autores:** Gauri Kapoor; Rachna Kapoor Seth; Nishant Verma; Sandeep Jain; Payal Malhotra

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Improved survival in low\- and middle\-income countries is generating a rapidly expanding population of childhood cancer survivors \(CCS\) who will transition to adult care\. In India, the capacity and geographic distribution of long\-term survivorship care remain poorly characterized\. Methods We synthesized survivorship data from three tertiary pediatric oncology centres in North India\. Transition\-eligible survivors were those diagnosed before age 18 years, who completed cancer\-directed therapy between January 2016 and December 2025 and had attained age &gt;18 years by December 2025\. We quantified the transition\-eligible cohort, projected the transition burden through 2030, mapped adolescent and young adult \(AYA\) survivors by geographic distance from their treating centre, and risk\-stratified survivors for survivorship care planning as per published diagnosis and exposure based criteria\. Results Among 3,060 survivors, 398 \(13\.0%\) were transition\-eligible; 288 \(72\.4%\) were male\. Lymphomas \(34\.1%\), solid tumors \(29\.1%\), and acute lymphoblastic leukemia \(27\.8%\) were the predominant diagnoses\. The transition\-eligible cohort is projected to increase to 1,071 by 2030 \(\+169\.1%\)\. Approximately 58% lived &gt;50 km from their treating centre\. Overall, 387 \(97\.2%\) were classified as moderate \(44\.9%\) or high risk \(52\.3%\)\. Transition processes were absent \(56%\) or informal \(33%\); barriers included perceived lack of need \(78%\), unawareness of late effects \(67%\) and farther distance from treating centre \(56%\)\. Conclusion This study estimates that the transition\-eligible CCS population is projected to nearly triple by 2030 and is predominantly composed of moderate\- and high\-risk survivors\. A risk\-stratified, geographically distributed survivorship model incorporating tele\-support and shared care closer to home is urgently required\.
