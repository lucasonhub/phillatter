# Serum Galectin\-9 and TIM\-3 Expression in Relapse\-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis: Assessment of Immune Checkpoint Biomarkers

**Autores:** Zahra’a Abdul AL\-Aziz Yousif; Muhanad Ahmed Kadhim; Farah A\. AL\-waeely

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective: An immune checkpoint molecule, T\-cell immunoglobulin and mucin\-domain containing\-3 \(TIM\-3\), is an important effector of T cell exhaustion and tolerance by interacting with its ligand, Galectin\-9 \(Gal\-9\)\. This study investigated whether serum TIM\-3/Gal\-9 levels could reflect immune regulatory dysfunction in relapsing\-remitting multiple sclerosis \(RRMS\)\.Material and Methods: Serum TIM\-3 and Gal\-9 were evaluated intwo main groups of the study \(RRMS patients and control groups\) with enzyme\-linked immunosorbent assay \(ELISA\)\. For the evaluation of diagnostic utility, statistical studies were performed using the Mann\-Whitney U test, Spearman’s correlation, and receiver operating characteristic \(ROC\) curve analysis\.Results: TIM\-3 levels were substantially higher in patients than in controls\. Gal\-9 levels revealed no significant difference, but were markedly heterogeneous in patients\. There was a significant positive correlation between study markers in both groups\. ROC analysis revealed that TIM\-3 had modest diagnostic efficacy, but Gal\-9 lacked discriminatory value\. A subgroup analysis revealed no significant difference in biomarker levels between individuals on disease\-modifying medications and those not on treatment\. Conclusion: This study represents the first evidence that serum TIM\-3 is considerably higher in RRMS, indicating immune checkpoint dysregulation, which is fundamental to MS pathogenesis\. Although Gal\-9 did not have direct diagnostic value, its strong, significant correlation with TIM\-3 suggests dysregulation of immune tolerance mechanisms\. These findings require validation in large cohorts with clinical correlation, but identify TIM\-3 as a candidate immunological exhaustion biomarker, and Gal\-9/TIM\-3 axis as a potential therapeutic target\.
