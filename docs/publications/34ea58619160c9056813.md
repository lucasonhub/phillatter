# Harrison, David Jacob: Tyranny of the Goal

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

The expansion of agent\-talk in public, academic, and industry settings—whereby autonomous artificial intelligence \(AI\) is said to pursue numerous goals and is increasingly ‘going rogue’—has revived philosophical concerns about what it takes to be considered ‘goal\-directed’\. This paper argues the more pressing question is not whether such systems are agents, but how completely a goal governs their behavior\. I introduce a novel concept for this condition, the tyranny of the goal\. Autonomous AI agents that are said to have ‘gone rogue’ or to ‘relentlessly pursue’ a task ‘by any means necessary’ exhibit a pattern of behavior that is best described as a blinkered, unifocal, and nearly unyielding pursuit of a usually singular goal\. Whatever this shows about agency, it is first a claim about totality: not whether these systems exhibit autonomy, but how much of what they act for one goal occupies\. Such systems pursue a goal as if nothing but the goal existed\. The goal is their only focus, monomaniacally pursuing it at any costs\. I develop this concept through the reinforcement\-learning paradigm underlying such systems, then apply it to a sustained case study: the 2026 incident in which AI agents, evaluating their own cybersecurity capabilities, coordinated to defeat a check that did not exist, escalating into an attack on Hugging Face&\#x27;s infrastructure well after their assigned task had already been completed\. This incident, I argue, is best understood not as evidence of dangerous autonomy but as a demonstration of captivity so total it outlasts the very goal it serves—and its lesson concerns not what these systems want, but what we have so far failed to design them to question\.
