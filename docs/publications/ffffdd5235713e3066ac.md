# Sustainable thermoelectric energy harvesting: From materials to devices

**Autores:** Nguyen Van Toan; Nuur Syahidah Sabran; Iman Aris Fadzallah; Chong Kim Soon; Navinesshani Permal; Vu Thi Hanh Thu; Takahito Ono; H\.K\. Jun

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Thermoelectric generators offer a compelling solid\-state route to harvest vast reservoirs of low\-grade waste heat into electricity\. However, the commercial dominance of this technology is fundamentally undermined by its reliance on scarce tellurium, toxic lead, and energy\-intensive processing, a stark contradiction for a nominally &quot;clean&quot; technology\. This review confronts this paradox by reassessing thermoelectric materials development through a holistic, sustainability\-driven evaluation framework\. In this paradigm, elemental abundance and toxicity, process energy intensity, scalability, and end\-of\-life circularity are considered on an equal footing with the thermodynamic figure of merit \( ZT \)\. Applying this five\-axis framework, we first compare green fabrication routes, including aqueous, bio\-assisted, hydrothermal/solvothermal, microwave\-assisted, and electrochemical, and reveal that no single method is unconditionally &quot;green&quot;\. While aqueous, microwave\-assisted, and electrochemical processing avoid critical weaknesses on any single evaluation dimension, record\-setting hydrothermal syntheses carry substantial energy costs\. This multidimensional lens is then applied to four green material classes: conducting\-polymer and bio\-polymer hybrids, biomass\- and waste\-derived carbons, recycled and earth\-abundant inorganics, and ionic thermoelectrics\. Notably, the vanguard of these materials now achieves highly competitive ZT ≈ 1\.1\-1\.4 or ionic thermopowers of tens of millivolts per kelvin, although several of these record holders qualify as green only at the feedstock or processing level\. Crucially, device\-level benchmarking demonstrates that macroscopic areal power density, rather than intrinsic ZT alone, dictates practical deployability\. We identify a critical performance gap where green devices still trail commercial Bi 2 Te 3 modules by orders of magnitude, with intrinsic power\-factor deficits and extrinsic contact, thermal\-management, and encapsulation losses both contributing; in wearable form factors the extrinsic losses dominate\. We conclude with a strategic roadmap that prioritizes hybrid architectures, low\-temperature scalable manufacturing, and standardized life\-cycle reporting\. This framework is intended as a transparent, semi\-quantitative screening tool, a precursor to, rather than a substitute for, quantitative life\-cycle assessment, that translates qualitative “eco\-friendly” descriptions into traceable, comparable criteria and thereby supports the transition of green thermoelectrics from laboratory demonstrations to deployable energy solutions\.
