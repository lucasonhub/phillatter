# Media literacy as pedagogical practice: preparing secondary teachers to foster critical engagement with aggressive media portrayals

**Autores:** Ajit Ilangovan; Govindarajan P; Dr\.Savariah Xavier; Mohammed Shamsul Hoque

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Teacher education offers little guidance on how teachers might be prepared to support adolescents’ critical engagement with aggressive media portrayals\. This study examines how media literacy was constructed as a pedagogical practice by 30 secondary school teachers in Kerala, India, a regional context in which cinema carries considerable cultural weight, across an eight\-session professional learning \(PL\) sequence extending over one academic year\. A longitudinal mixed\-methods intervention design was adopted, combining an embedded qualitative strand generated during the sequence with a sequential explanatory strand that followed the post\-test\. Pre\- and post\-intervention surveys measured teachers’ beliefs about adolescents’ analytical capacity and their self\-efficacy for facilitating critical media analysis, while session artefacts, reflective journals, stimulated\-recall dialogues and post\-intervention interviews were used to trace changes in pedagogical reasoning\. An anonymised, school\-aggregated media\-consumption survey of 150 Grade 11 and 12 students served only as a discussion stimulus in Session 1; students were not research participants, and no student\-level data were analysed or reported\. Paired\-samples analyses indicated statistically significant within\-participant differences on both self\-report measures\. The qualitative evidence suggests a movement away from protectionist and deficit\-based framings of adolescents towards asset\-based orientations that position them as capable meaning\-makers, although this reorientation was uneven across participants\. Teachers also reported greater confidence in designing equity\-oriented analytical routines for texts depicting aggression\. Because the design lacked a comparison condition and relied on self\-report measures completed by volunteers, these findings are interpreted as teachers’ reported and perceived change over the period of the sequence, not as effects attributable to the PL sequence alone or as evidence of change in students’ learning\. The very large effect sizes obtained are treated as a methodological caution rather than as evidence of durable change\.
