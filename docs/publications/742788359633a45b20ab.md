# Dynamic interactions between multidimensional civilization development and urban–rural land use behaviors in China

**Autores:** Xiyu Chen; Ling Ling; Yuxuan Chen; Jia Kang; Wuyun Xia

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Understanding how civilization development interacts with land use transformation is critical for advancing sustainable human–land governance\. However, existing studies have largely examined land use transitions from socioeconomic or ecological perspectives, while the dynamic feedback mechanisms between multidimensional civilization development and land use behaviors remain insufficiently understood\. This study develops an analytical framework linking Agricultural Civilization Development \(ACD\), Industrial Civilization Development \(ICD\), and Ecological Civilization Development \(ECD\) with three urban–rural land use behaviors, including Production Expansion Behavior \(PEB\), Non\-agricultural Conversion Behavior \(NCB\), and Ecological Restoration Behavior \(ERB\)\. Using an unbalanced provincial panel dataset of China from 1993 to 2023, this study employs a first\-differenced panel vector autoregression model estimated by the generalized method of moments, supplemented by Granger causality tests, impulse response analysis, forecast error variance decomposition, regional heterogeneity analysis, and temporal breakpoint analysis around 2013\. The results reveal differentiated and asymmetric interactions between civilization development and land use behaviors\. In the baseline PVAR model, ACD is positively associated with subsequent changes in PEB, NCB, and ERB, while ICD is positively associated with NCB\. However, these development\-to\-land\-use linkages become substantially weaker after controlling for nationwide time effects, whereas the feedback relationships from land use behaviors to ACD remain relatively robust, with PEB showing a negative association and NCB showing a positive association\. ECD demonstrates comparatively limited dynamic linkages with land use behaviors\. Further analyses indicate that the interaction system is characterized by short\-lived responses, differentiated shock transmission patterns, and significant regional heterogeneity, with Eastern China exhibiting relatively stable adjustments, Central China showing stronger sensitivity, and Western China displaying greater volatility\. The temporal breakpoint analysis suggests that after 2013, China experienced a gradual transition from expansion\-oriented land use toward more coordinated production, construction, and ecological functions\. These findings extend land use transition research by incorporating civilization development perspectives and provide insights for regionally differentiated strategies of sustainable land governance\.
