## Descrição do feed

In Annette Zimmerman, Democratizing AI\. Manchester University Press\. pp\. 134\-146\. 2026This chapter critically examines Zimmermann’s claim that AI deployment constitutes agenda\-setting and should therefore be democratically controlled\. While broadly sympathetic to Zimmermann’s democratic playbook, the authors challenge the uniqueness of AI’s risks and argue that similar harms – such as monopolistic market structures, externalities, and public bads – exist across other sectors\. They propose that representative democracy, with its capacity to integrate expertise and public accountability, offers a more feasible and effective framework for AI governance than Zimmermann’s model of informed sub\-constituencies\. The chapter explores various harms posed by AI, including hallucinations, deepfakes, plagiarism, privacy violations, and mass unemployment, and evaluates which are best addressed through tort law, market mechanisms, or democratic deliberation\. It also highlights the global justice implications of AI\-induced job losses in developing economies\. While endorsing Zimmermann’s emphasis on unionization and countervailing power, the authors caution against over\-reliance on referenda and advisory councils, which may be vulnerable to capture or ineffectiveness\. Ultimately, they advocate for strengthening antitrust laws, campaign finance reform, and unionization as part of a robust representative democratic response to AI’s societal risks\. These measures, they argue, are better suited to tackle the asymmetries of power and influence in AI deployment while preserving innovation and democratic legitimacy\. \(direct link \)
