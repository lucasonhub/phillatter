# The autonomic\-ventricular coherence index: a novel single\-lead ECG method with acquisition\-dependent discrimination

**Autores:** R Jayasree; Chemmalar Selvi G\.

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Autonomic regulation and the heart&\#x27;s electrical activity are both central to normal cardiac function, yet there is currently no direct way to capture the interaction between them from a standard ECG\. This paper introduces the Autonomic–Ventricular Coherence Index \(AVCI\), a single\-lead ECG method that measures the beat\-to\-beat spectral coherence between variability in RR intervals and variability in QRS area\. Methods AVCI was developed and evaluated on 74 recordings from three PhysioNet databases \(MIT\-BIH Normal Sinus Rhythm, n = 18; BIDMC Congestive Heart Failure, n = 15; and MIT\-BIH Arrhythmia, n = 41\), with a further six recordings from the MIT\-BIH Long\-Term ECG Database used for preliminary independent validation\. Coherence was integrated over the high\-frequency band \(0\.15–0\.40 Hz\)\. Because the source databases differ in sampling rate and recording length, AVCI was examined both under each database&\#x27;s native recording conditions and after acquisition standardization, harmonizing all recordings to a common 128 Hz and 30\-minute duration, additionally upsampling to 360 Hz for 30 min under uniform acquisition, and applying a two\-tier duration\-stratified framework\. The analysis code is available at https://github\.com/JayaSreejai/AVCI\.git Results Under native conditions, AVCI demonstrated a high capacity to distinguish between congestive heart failure and normal sinus rhythm \(AUC 0\.844\)\. Like SDNN, it performed moderately in harmonized acquisition \(NSR vs\. CHF AUC 0\.647; NSR vs\. all\-disease AUC 0\.619\)\. Under native conditions, machine learning models trained on balanced datasets performed well \(KNN accuracy 79\.5%, ROC\-AUC 85\.7, SVM accuracy 79\.0%, ROC\-AUC 83\.0\), whereas under harmonized conditions, they produced average results\. R\-peak identification was consistent across QRS windows and accurate \(F1 0\.892\)\. These results show that AVCI&\#x27;s diagnostic performance depends on acquisition parameters, particularly sampling rate and recording duration\. Conclusions AVCI is a practical and understandable measure of autonomic\-ventricular coupling from a single ECG lead\. Combined with machine learning, it offers a promising path toward more robust and explainable cardiovascular diagnostics\. Standardized acquisition protocols are key to reliable clinical use\.
