# Artificial Intelligence in Epidemiology and Public Health: Measurement, Evidence, and Population Consequences

**Autores:** Pietro Corona

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** book

## Resumo

This scholarly monograph examines how artificial intelligence has developed within epidemiology and public health, which computational methods have been most frequently documented, how applications have evolved across scientific and geographical contexts, and what the available evidence establishes about their consequences for research, services and population health\. The investigation integrates historical reconstruction, quantitative evidence mapping, source\-based methodological appraisal, selected comparative analyses and conceptual synthesis\. Its analytical corpus comprises 4,752 retained research reports spanning 1993 to 2026, with the final year incomplete\. The study documents the coexistence and evolution of neural networks, tree\-based approaches, statistical learning and other computational methods, while examining geographical patterns, classification uncertainty and the emergence of contemporary AI\-enabled workflows and operational systems\. The monograph investigates applications across infectious\-disease surveillance and forecasting, genomic epidemiology, antimicrobial resistance, environmental exposure estimation, wastewater monitoring, population registries, organised screening and preventive health services\. Comparative analyses examine measurement validity, predictive performance, external validation, causal interpretation, screening completion, service productivity, implementation, economic consequences and equity\. Favourable outcomes are considered alongside null, adverse, mixed and uncertain findings, with explicit attention to study design, comparators, analytical populations and outcome definitions\. An original contribution is the identification and empirical investigation of underascertainment in the classification of environmental research functions\. The broader synthesis examines how AI changes the production of epidemiological knowledge, how observation and measurement shape scientific conclusions, and why improvements in prediction or operational performance cannot automatically be interpreted as attributable improvements in population health\. Organised into 18 chapters and five methodological appendices, with 44 original scientific illustrations and 25 tables, the book provides an integrated international perspective on the methodological evolution, documented contributions, limitations and emerging directions of artificial intelligence in epidemiology and public health\. The work is intended for epidemiologists, public\-health researchers, computational scientists, health\-services researchers, methodologists, policymakers and advanced students\.
