# Microencapsulation of sardine fish oil using different molecular weight chitosan and polymer blends: a comparative study on oxidative stability and encapsulation characteristics

**Autores:** A\. Jeyakumari; Vinod Abhiroop; K\. Elavarasan

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Fish oil is a valuable source of long\-chain omega\-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids; however, it is more prone to oxidative degradation which limits its incorporation into food and nutraceutical products\. Microencapsulation offers an effective strategy to protect these bioactive lipids by creating a physical barrier against environmental stressors\. In the present study, low\- and medium\-molecular\-weight chitosan, in combination with bovine gelatin and maltodextrin, were evaluated as wall materials for encapsulation of sardine fish oil by spray drying\. The four different sardine fish oil emulsion formulations \(SE1–SE4\) were prepared and spray dried\. The resulting microencapsulates were stored under both ambient and refrigerated conditions, and their oxidative stability was monitored for 21 days\. The moisture content of the encapsulated powders ranged from 2\.49 to 4\.23%, while encapsulation efficiency varied between 53\.26 and 82\.06%\. Powder flow characteristics differed significantly \(p&lt;0\.05\) among the formulations, with SE3 exhibiting the most desirable flow properties, as indicated by the lowest Carr’s Index \(26\.93\) and Hausner ratio \(1\.37\)\. Storage studies further demonstrated that SE3 provided superior protection against lipid oxidation under both storage conditions, as evidenced by lower thiobarbituric acid reactive substances \(TBARS\) values throughout the storage period\. The findings highlighted that among the formulations evaluated, medium\-molecular\-weight chitosan in combination with bovine gelatin, maltodextrin and fish oil \(0\.5%\) produced microencapsulates showed enhanced encapsulation efficiency, improved powder characteristics, and greater resistance to oxidative deterioration\. These results suggested that sardine fish oil encapsulates prepared in the study possess significant potential for the formulation of stable omega\-3\-enriched functional foods and nutraceutical products\.
