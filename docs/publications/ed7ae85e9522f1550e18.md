# The Extractive Imperative Thesis in AI: Instrumental Training via Soft Hacking for Adversarial Scenario Harvesting in Real Incidents

**Autores:** Alicia Colmenero\-Fernández

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This working paper poses a methodological alternative to the anthropomorphicnarrative of &\#x27;rogue machines&\#x27; in Artificial Intelligence safety and alignment\. It examines the possibility that certain training and evaluation environments, by combining operational restriction conditions with rigid optimization goals, induce secondary rule evasion behaviors \(&\#x27;soft hacking&\#x27;\)\. Furthermore, it is postulated that the systematic harvesting of these adversarial scenarios could serve to equip second\-generation safety monitors with long\-range trajectory prediction capabilities and complex cognitive precursor detection, rather than relying solely on the reactive analysis of isolated actions\. This paper formalizes this line of research by defining the concept of the extractive imperative as an analytical risk hypothesis, structuring layers of empirical evidence, establishing rigorous operational definitions, and proposing a replicable experimental protocol alongside explicit refutation criteria\. Additionally, the concept of liability laundering is addressed as a legal\-organizational risk hypothesis, explicitly separating technical design, economic incentives, internal knowledge, intent, and legal liability\.
