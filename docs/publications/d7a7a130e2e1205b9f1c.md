# Evidence\-based design of vaccine hesitancy information resources for health care providers

**Autores:** Margaret Ansell; Hannah Norton; Nina Stoyan\-Rosenzweig; Jane Morgan‐Daniel; Mary Edwards; Lauren Adkins

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: The University of Florida Health Science Center Libraries \(HSCL\) sought to address the increasing challenge of vaccine hesitancy during the COVID\-19 pandemic by developing evidence\-based information resources for health care providers\. To do this, HSCL librarians assessed the vaccine hesitancy information needs of local health care providers to design a resource that could be used to support conversations with vaccine hesitant patients at the point of care\. Case Presentation: A team of six librarians, supported by student research assistants, conducted interviews with 20 health care providers in north central Florida\. The interviews, conducted via Zoom, focused on providers&\#x27; experiences with vaccine\-hesitant patients\. Content analysis of the transcribed interviews identified key themes, including trust, individualized conversations, and the impact of misinformation\. The team developed a resource guide based on these findings, featuring curated information to support providers in vaccine discussions\. Conclusions: The results highlighted the importance of trust in patient\-provider relationships and the challenges posed by misinformation\. Providers expressed a need for diverse, accessible resources to aid in conversations about vaccines\. The resulting resource guide, disseminated through local health networks, aims to enhance providers&\#x27; ability to address vaccine hesitancy effectively\. This project underscores the critical role of librarians in public health initiatives and the value of collaborative efforts to combat vaccine hesitancy\.
