# Beyond Detection: Generative Artificial Intelligence for Autonomous Cyber Threat Hunting and Incident Response

**Autores:** Tunbosun Ajibola; Komeno Okpiyalele

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Autonomous cybersecurity requires a transition from isolated alert classification toward coordinated threat investigation, evidence\-grounded decisions, and measurable containment\. This study integrates a generative artificial intelligence \(GenAI\) multi\-agent threat\-hunting architecture with a six\-state nonlinear model of secure, exposed, compromised, and recovered assets and the evolving hunting and response capabilities\. The framework employs independent evidence verification and policy\-controlled response authorization, while model analysis uses a containment threshold, equilibrium stability and parameter sensitivity\. Across the three configurations reported in the supplied results, compromise fractions were 42\.71% for rule\-based defence, 32\.48% for machine\-learning\-supported defence, and 19\.16% for GenAI\-enabled defence; corresponding containment thresholds were 6\.860, 4\.857, and 3\.275\. These submitted outcomes indicate a 55\.1% lower modelled compromise fraction for the GenAI configuration than for rule\-based defence, although all thresholds remain above unity and therefore do not establish threat elimination\. Three author\-supplied three\-dimensional response surfaces describe the effects of propagation, assisted recovery, adversarial adaptation and hunting activation\. The investigation distinguishes these reported model\-based outcomes from unreported alert\-level accuracy benchmarks and from numerical bifurcations that require recorded AUTO\-07p continuation diagnostics\. The resulting contribution is a coherent framework linking generative investigation, bounded response capacity and nonlinear cybersecurity resilience, with explicit requirements for reproducible experimental validation and safe operational deployment\.
