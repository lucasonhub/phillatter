# Reference Hallucination in AI ‐Assisted Restorative Dentistry Education: An Educational Risk Assessment Using the Reference Hallucination Score Framework

**Autores:** Esra Yildirim Manav; Sıla Nur Usta

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

PURPOSE: Artificial intelligence \(AI\) chatbots are increasingly used by dental students and faculty for literature review, coursework preparation, and scholarly writing\. In restorative dentistry education, where evidence\-based decision\-making is fundamental, inaccurate AI\-generated citations may pose educational risks\. This study evaluated the bibliographic reliability of AI\-generated references in restorative dentistry topics and examined their potential implications for dental education\. METHODS: Five large language model \(LLM\)\-based chatbots were tested using ten standardised restorative dentistry prompts representing commonly assigned educational topics\. Each chatbot generated 100 references \(N = 500\)\. References were manually verified using indexed databases and evaluated using the Reference Hallucination Score \(RHS; range 0\-11\)\. Chatbot performance was compared using the Friedman test, followed by Dunn\-Bonferroni post hoc pairwise comparisons\. Binary bibliographic error parameters were analysed using generalised linear mixed models, with chatbot type, prompt complexity, and their interaction as fixed effects and prompt as a random intercept \(α = 0\.05\)\. RESULTS: A significant overall difference in RHS was observed among the evaluated AI chatbots \(p &lt; 0\.001\), with post hoc analyses identifying significant differences between specific model pairs\. ChatGPT\-5\.2 and Perplexity AI demonstrated the lowest hallucination severity, whereas Copilot and DeepSeek\-v3\.2 demonstrated the highest\. Prompt complexity did not significantly influence hallucination severity\. Errors most frequently involved author names, journal titles, and DOIs\. CONCLUSIONS: AI\-generated references in restorative dentistry demonstrated substantial variability in bibliographic accuracy across the evaluated AI systems\. These inaccuracies may pose potential risks to evidence\-based learning and academic integrity, highlighting the importance of systematic reference verification and AI literacy in dental education\.
