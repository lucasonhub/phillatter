# Iodide ion migration\-mediated resistive switching in 2D \(4,4\-DFHHA\) 2 PbI 4 perovskite memristors

**Autores:** Yuhan Fang; Hao Chen; Hudie Wei; Jie Jiang; Liang Chu

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Halide perovskites have emerged as promising memristor materials owing to their reversible ion migration characteristics, solution processability, and cost\-effectiveness\. While resistive switching \(RS\) mechanisms in two\-dimensional \(2D\) perovskites are still not fully understood, their quantum\-confined layered structures endow them with enhanced environmental stability, crystal structural plasticity, and defect tolerance compared to three\-dimensional \(3D\) counterparts\. In this work, 2D perovskite \(4,4\-DFHHA\) 2 PbI 4 memristors with a sandwich structure were fabricated, where iodide ion migration was the dominant RS mechanism\. The device exhibits a typical SET voltage of approximately 0\.35 V, indicating extremely low power consumption\. Incorporating a PMMA interlayer for interfacial passivation and ion confinement enables stable operation over 250 switching cycles, retention exceeding 10 3 s, and a consistent \~10 2 on/off ratio through 400 cycles\. Our findings elucidate the switching mechanism in 2D \(4,4\-DFHHA\) 2 PbI 4 memristors and demonstrate dual role effect of the PMMA interlayer in performance regulation, establishing a design framework for low\-power, stable perovskite memory devices\.
