# Railway infrastructure breakthrough and mode choice: evidence from the Ceneri Base Tunnel in Switzerland

**Autores:** Leonardo Conte; Jules Grandvillemin; Clément Rames; Orane Solim Koenga

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Major investments in railway infrastructure and service provision are often justified by their potential to increase public transport \(PT\) use and induce modal shift away from private motorized transport \(PMT\), thereby contributing to more sustainable mobility\. Yet, empirical evidence on the extent to which large\-scale railway interventions affect individual mode choice remains scattered\. This study evaluates the impact of the Ceneri Base Tunnel \(CBT\), opened in Ticino, Switzerland, in 2020, together with associated PT offer improvements, on work and study commuting mode choice\. Drawing on the INDMOB travel survey, that collected data from 24,577 individuals, we analyze changes in commuting practices between 2019 and 2023 and estimate the causal effect of the CBT opening using a Difference\-in\-Differences \(DiD\) approach\. We find that the CBT \(and associated PT improvements\) increased the probability of using PT rather than PMT as the main commuting mode by 4\.2 percentage points \(p\.p\.\) among commuters along its north–south corridors, relative to the control group, after controlling for socio\-demographics, mobility equipment, and PT level of service\. This corresponds to a 13% relative increase compared with the baseline PT share in the treatment group\. We further show that PT use is associated with individual and spatial characteristics: women and younger commuters, individuals with a PT subscription, and those living in areas with higher PT levels of service are more likely to use PT, conditional on the other covariates\. Our findings contribute to the transport literature on the effects of major railway infrastructure improvements on mode choice and support rail investments as an effective component of modal shift strategies, while highlighting the importance of individual mobility resources and the broader PT service context in shaping their potential\.
