# The Fundamental Observer Hypothesis \(FOH\): A Non\-Dual Computational Framework Resolving the Hard Problem of Consciousness

**Autores:** Devraj Prime

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Current neuroscientific paradigms primarily rely on emergentism to explain subjective qualitative experience \(qualia\), positing that consciousness arises organically from complex neural architecture\. Despite extensive mapping of neural correlates, the &quot;Hard Problem of Consciousness&quot; remains unresolved, failing to explain how physical matter generates subjective awareness\. The Fundamental Observer Hypothesis \(FOH\) proposes a radical paradigm shift: Consciousness is not an emergent property of the biological brain but a fundamental, non\-dual, spaceless, and timeless constant\. In this framework, the biological brain operates strictly as a &\#x27;Detector&\#x27; and &\#x27;Rendering Engine&\#x27;\. The phenomenon commonly identified as the psychological &quot;Ego&quot; or localized self is mathematically defined as a &quot;False Identification Bug&quot;—a computational error wherein the inert biological apparatus attributes the Fundamental Observer&\#x27;s illumination to its own processing\. By separating the biological hardware from the absolute awareness field, this model provides a unified explanation for waking states, deep sleep, and non\-dual mystical states induced by profound meditation or neurochemical alterations\.
