# Curvature\-Induced Electric\-Field Effects Govern Controllable N2 Electrocatalysis on Single\-Atom Catalysts

**Autores:** Nana Hu; Xingshuai Lv; Thomas Frauenheim; Shuwei Xia; Liangzhi Kou

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Electric field\-enhanced electrocatalysis represents an innovative strategy for improving the efficiency of electrochemical transformations\. However, quantitative evaluation of microscopic electric\-field effects under operando conditions remains challenging, limiting a rigorous, predictive understanding of field\-mediated reaction regulation\. Here, by integrating rigorous numerical methods, constant\-potential microkinetic simulations, and machine learning models, we establish electric\-field intensity as a quantitative descriptor that directly correlates local fields with catalytic performance\. Using curvature\-engineered M–N–C single\-atom catalysts as a model platform, we demonstrate that curvature\-induced electric fields modulate electron occupation at active sites and shift the potential of zero charge in a controllable manner\. This field\-driven electronic perturbation selectively tunes adsorbate binding energetics, thereby dictating limiting potentials, steering reaction pathways, and governing the thermodynamics and kinetics of interfacial proton\-coupled electron transfer in nitrogen electroreduction\. Notably, we uncover an inverted volcano–type dependence of catalytic activity on curvature, wherein optimal activity is achieved within a moderate field\-strength window\. This nonmonotonic trend underscores the necessity of balanced curvature regulation, rather than simply maximizing field intensity, in optimizing electrocatalytic performance\. These findings provide a quantitative framework for monitoring and regulating catalytic trends under realistic operating conditions via curvature\-induced electric fields, offering generalizable design principles for diverse SAC systems and electrochemical reactions\.
