# Threat factors to honeybees’ health and survival according to beekeepers in Burkina Faso

**Autores:** Souhaïbou Sawadogo; Oswald Gilbert Dingtoumda; Hugues Roméo Bazié; Zakaria Ilboudo

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Honeybees have been of significant socioeconomic value to human communities since prehistoric times\. Unfortunately, a collapse of honeybee colonies has been reported in several countries around the world, particularly in Europe and America\. The causes attributed to this phenomenon are numerous and interrelated, and their relative importance varies depending on the local context\. In Burkina Faso, data related to the health status of these insects remain scarce and limited\. Methods The objective of this study was therefore to identify and analyze the threats to the health and survival of honeybees in Burkina Faso, considering the opinions, perceptions, and experiences of beekeepers, who are considered key observers\. To do this, we conducted a mixed\-methods survey of 236 beekeepers selected using non\-probability “snowball” sampling in the country’s Sudanian \(SZ\) and Sudano\-sahelian \(SSZ\) climatic zones\. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics and comparative tests\. Results The results show that 21\.7% of hives are empty of bees in the SZ, compared to 28\.7% in the SSZ \( P &lt; 0\.05\)\. Honeybees have either abandoned these hives or simply never inhabited them\. Beekeepers believe that climatic factors \(43\.9% in the SZ and 32\.2% in the SSZ, P &lt;0\.05\) and health\-related factors \(22\.8% in the SZ and 22% in the SSZ , P &lt;0\.05\) contribute to increased mortality rates\. In both zones, more than 45% of respondents believe that bee populations are declining in their localities\. Regarding the sustainability and development of the beekeeping sector, stakeholders identified pest management \(69\.6% in SZ and 74\.4% in SSZ, P &gt; 0\.05\), the impacts of climate change \(76\.5% in SZ and 62\.8% in SSZ, P &gt; 0\.05\), and improvements in beekeeping equipment \(61\.7% in SZ and 44\.6% in SSZ, P &lt; 0\.05\) as the main challenges requiring attention\. Conclusion This participatory assessment provides a basis for concrete actions to protect honeybees\. This field experience could therefore serve as an indicator that potentially contributes to the effectiveness and acceptability of conservation measures\.
