# Flujo explicable para clasificar péptidos antimicrobianos con modelo pequeño de lenguaje de proteínas

**Autores:** Chavelys Fernández Quintero; Miguel Angel Fernández; Daniela Fuentes Morales; Christian David Castellanos Isern; Reydel Fanjul Evora; Daniel Alejandro Alfonso Bermúdez; Alejandro Cespón Ferriol; Daliana Cabrera Grau; Deborah Galpert Cañizares

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Antimicrobial resistance has driven the development of computational strategies to accelerate the identification of peptides with antimicrobial activity\. This study designed a reproducible and interpretable workflow for their binary classification based on vector representations derived from a protein language model\. The methodology integrated data preprocessing, embedding generation with ProtFlash, training of three architectures \(multilayer perceptron, one\-dimensional convolutional network, and bidirectional recurrent network\), external validation, and explainability analysis using integrated gradients and Shapley values\. The corpus used comprised 40,358 sequences, distributed across training \(19,548 sequences – 9,767 negative and 9,781 positive\), validation \(5,125 sequences – 2,561 negative and 2,564 positive\), and test \(15,685 sequences – 10,771 negative and 4,914 positive\) sets\. During internal validation, the architectures demonstrated competitive performance: the multilayer perceptron achieved the highest Matthews correlation coefficient \(0\.8247\), while the convolutional network led in accuracy \(0\.9112\) and the accuracy\-coverage balance \(0\.9086\)\. In the external validation, the recurrent configuration demonstrated greater resilience and the best overall balance \(Matthews correlation coefficient = 0\.6506\)\. The developed workflow demonstrated that the computationally efficient representation of the small protein language ProtFlash enabled the training of competitive classifiers and, when combined with explainability techniques, facilitated a transparent interpretation of the patterns associated with biological activity\. The integration of semantic representations with deep architectures and explainable artificial intelligence modules constitutes a robust and transparent strategy for the computational screening of bioactive candidates\.
