# Naoto, Fujie: Where Does the Category ‘Human’ Become Non\-Redundant After Its Removal?

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper asks where the concept of “human” becomes operationally non\-redundant after it has been removed from the initial description of biological, cognitive, technological, institutional, and social systems\. Rather than presupposing humanity as a natural kind, moral status, privileged cognitive form, or universal reference point, the analysis first describes configuration\-specific differences in embodiment, metabolism, memory, inference, communication, agency, responsibility, technological coupling, and institutional participation without using the category “human\.” The category is then reintroduced only where its absence produces a non\-redundant loss in prediction, explanation, intervention, legal or normative differentiation, or historical reconstruction\. The paper distinguishes biological lineage, species membership, personhood, rational agency, social identity, and institutional classification rather than treating them as interchangeable expressions of humanity\. It further examines whether artificial, hybrid, posthuman, or technologically transformed systems reproduce the operational differences for which the human category previously performed explanatory work\. The resulting method treats “human” neither as an indispensable starting point nor as a concept to be permanently eliminated\. Its status is configuration\-relative: the category becomes justified only within intervals where introducing it captures differences that cannot be recovered from lower\-level biological, operational, institutional, or inferential descriptions alone\. The broader claim is that the philosophical significance of the human should be tested by its residual explanatory work after removal rather than assumed from either human exceptionalism or antihumanism\. \(direct link \)
