# Effect of Waste Engine Oil Treatment on Moisture Absorption and Hygroscopic Performance of Selected Tropical Hardwoods

**Autores:** Enakem Benedict Agbonko; Adeniyi Emmanuel Daodu; Maria Kaka Etete Enoh; Michael Ikpi Ofem

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The moisture sensitivity of wood\-based reinforcement can restrict dimensional stability and durability in humid service environments\.This study examined the effect of waste engine oil impregnation on the moisture\-absorption behaviour of three tropical hardwoods\-White Safari \(Triplochiton scleroxylon\), Opepe \(Nauclea diderrichii\) and Bush Mahogany \(Khaya ivorensis\)\-considered for reinforcement in wood\-based composite applications\.Prepared specimens were separated into untreated controls and waste\-engine\-oil\-treated groups\.The treated specimens were immersed in filtered waste engine oil, drained and conditioned to stable mass before gravimetric moisture\-absorption assessment\.Moisture transport was interpreted using Fickian diffusion principles, while treatment effects were evaluated statistically at a 95% confidence level\.Waste engine oil treatment reduced equilibrium moisture absorption in all three species\.White Safari decreased from 11\.80% to 6\.20%, Opepe from 10\.50% to 5\.70%, and Bush Mahogany from 10\.30% to 5\.50%, corresponding to reductions of 47\.46%, 45\.71% and 46\.60%, respectively\.The treated specimens also showed slower moisture uptake and lower equilibrium values than the untreated controls\.These findings are consistent with partial occupation of pores and lumens by a hydrophobic oil phase and reduced accessibility of water\-binding sites\.The results demonstrate that waste engine oil impregnation can substantially reduce hygroscopic response in the selected hardwoods\.However, environmental safety, contaminant leaching, long\-term weathering and application\-specific durability require evaluation before engineering deployment\.
