# Machine learning\-based prediction of 90\-day tensile strength in geopolymer concrete under varying input\-variable scenarios

**Autores:** Parnian Mehravar; Hossein Bahrami; Hamidreza Ghazvinian

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate prediction of the long\-term tensile strength \(TS\) of geopolymer concrete remains a complex challenge owing to the nonlinear and interactive nature of the governing parameters\. This study develops and comparatively evaluates four machine\-learning models linear regression \(LR\), decision tree \(DT\), random forest \(RF\) and support\-vector regression \(SVR\) to predict 90\-day tensile strength from a suite of physicochemical and mixture\-related variables\. Eleven systematically designed input scenarios were formulated to quantify the influence of different feature combinations on predictive performance\. The results demonstrate a pronounced dependence of model accuracy on input configuration\. Early scenarios that employed limited variable sets yielded only moderate performance \(R² ≤ 0\.885, with most models below 0\.86\), whereas the optimal configuration \(S11\), which incorporates water absorption \(WA\), ground\-granulated blast\-furnace slag \(GGBS\), nano\-silica, pH and bulk density \(BD\), substantially enhanced predictive capability\. Among the models, DT achieved the highest test accuracy \(R² = 0\.984, RMSE = 0\.751\) together with the smallest generalisation gap \(training R² = 0\.988\), indicating strong predictive performance within the investigated dataset and limited evidence of overfitting under the applied validation procedures\. RF and SVR also delivered high predictive performance \(R² = 0\.964 and 0\.956, respectively\), while LR exhibited comparatively lower accuracy, underscoring the importance of nonlinear modelling approaches\. Permutation\-importance sensitivity analysis identified WA as the dominant influential parameter \(mean ΔR² = 0\.911\), followed by GGBS \(0\.588\) and nano\-silica \(0\.277\), indicating that WA and GGBS were the strongest predictors of TS within the trained model, consistent with though not a direct proof of their established physical roles in porosity and geopolymeric gel formation\. Collectively, the findings indicate that the integration of advanced tree\-based learning algorithms with comprehensive input characterisation provides a promising and computationally efficient framework for modelling the long\-term tensile behaviour of geopolymer concrete within the range of mixtures examined; broader industrial application will, however, require external validation on independent datasets and formal uncertainty quantification\.
