# Effects of Transcranial Photobiomodulation on Motor Functions in Healthy Individuals: A Randomized Controlled Trial

**Autores:** Milad Iravani; Maryam Moghaddam Salimi; Ali Jahan; Tohid Jafari Koshki; Abbas Ebrahimi Kalan

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objective This study investigated the effects of transcranial photobiomodulation \(tPBM\) targeting the supplementary motor area \(SMA\) on motor performance and neural activity in healthy individuals\. Methods In a randomized, double\-blind, sham\-controlled trial, 60 right\-handed participants received active tPBM \(850 nm, 60 mW/cm 2 , 36 J/cm 2 \) or sham stimulation over the SMA for four consecutive days\. Motor performance was assessed using a sequential finger\-tapping task for both hands\. Neural activity was evaluated using quantitative electroencephalography \(qEEG\) at rest\. Results Active tPBM significantly improved non\-dominant hand motor performance compared to sham, with reductions in reaction time and total movement time, and increased accuracy \(all p &lt; 0\.001 after Bonferroni correction\)\. qEEG showed significant increases in alpha and beta power at C3 and C4 electrodes in the active group \(all p &lt; 0\.001 after correction\)\. No significant changes were observed in the sham group\. Conclusion These findings suggest that short\-term tPBM targeting the SMA can enhance motor performance, particularly of the non\-dominant hand, and increase sensorimotor alpha and beta power in healthy young adults\. Further research is needed to clarify the underlying mechanisms and potential clinical applications\.
