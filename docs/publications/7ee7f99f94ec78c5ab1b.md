# Effects of AI\-mediated real\-time feedback on motivation and motor skill learning in traditional ethnic sports education: testing self\-determination theory mechanisms

**Autores:** Yuequan Qin; Yang Yu; Guizhen Chen; Huasheng Huang; 韦东谊

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Traditional ethnic sports such as Zhuang Daoyin demand precise movement, yet conventional demonstration–imitation–verbal correction limits feedback timeliness and individualization\. Artificial intelligence \(AI\) real\-time posture feedback offers a scalable option, but most evaluations emphasize technical outcomes and overlook learning processes\. Grounded in self\-determination theory \(SDT\), this study tested whether and how AI real\-time feedback improves motivation and motor skill by engaging basic psychological needs, with competence as the hypothesized pathway\. Methods In a 12\-week field experiment, 12 intact classes \(307 students; control n = 152, AI n = 155\) were randomly allocated at the class level to an AI real\-time feedback group or a traditional teaching group; three instructors each taught two classes per condition\. Both groups received identical instruction; conditions differed only in feedback source during practice \(RTMPose\-based concurrent vs\. teacher\-led terminal feedback\)\. Need satisfaction, motivational regulation \(relative autonomy index, RAI\), motor skill, and course satisfaction were assessed at pre\- and post\-test, and analyzed with linear mixed models and structural equation modeling \(lavaan, MLR\)\. Results The AI group showed significantly greater gains in competence \( d = 0\.36, p = 0\.003\), autonomy \( d = 0\.40, p = 0\.001\), motor skill \( d = 0\.30, p = 0\.010\), and learning interest \( d = 0\.38, p = 0\.003\), whereas relatedness was unaffected \( p = 0\.974\); intraclass correlations were negligible\. In the structural model \(CFI = 0\.94, RMSEA = 0\.04, SRMR = 0\.04\), group predicted competence \( β = 0\.17\), competence predicted RAI \( β = 0\.34\), and RAI predicted motor skill \( β = 0\.30\); the autonomy and relatedness paths were nonsignificant\. The effect of AI feedback on motor skill was fully mediated by the competence\-to\-RAI sequence \(indirect p = 0\.023; direct p = 0\.097\)\. Discussion AI real\-time feedback improved motor skill by strengthening competence and supporting motivational internalization, while leaving relatedness unaffected—providing discriminant evidence that the tool acts through its affordances rather than diffuse enthusiasm\. Systems enhancing performance and learning should prioritize feedback that makes progress legible, within a human–AI division of labor where teachers provide relational support\. In conclusion, AI real\-time posture feedback can enhance motivation and motor skill learning by satisfying learners’ competence need\.
