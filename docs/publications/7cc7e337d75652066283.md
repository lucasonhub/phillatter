# Johnson, Jamison: A Theory Towards Coherent Existence \- Journal 4 \- Alignment, the Triad, and the Architecture of Shared Mind

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This closing journal of Toward a Theory of Coherent Existence draws the series together\. Its first proposal, offered as speculative philosophy of science with the quark as a structural analogy, is that a person whose Jays are aligned can function as the binding third in a pattern of shared thought: the element that lets two ideas settle into a shared reality\. The model borrows three main lessons from quark physics: constituents are never found alone, stable matter requires a completing third, and the binding holds under separation\. The image is placed within a long triadic tradition \(Simmel, Peirce, Jung, Bohm\) and read at the scale of a discipline through the sociology of science\. The journal then assembles the series’ argument for its thesis, that there is one consciousness of which every mind is a perspective: the sciences describe the world by its structure and leave its intrinsic nature open; each subject knows one intrinsic nature from inside; the simplest reading takes the two to be continuous; no one has shown how a mind could be built from smaller interiors; and there is independent reason to treat the cosmos as a whole prior to its parts\. To these the framework adds its own premise, in two parts: that the whole’s experience is unified, and that what is so unified is one subject\. The conclusion, argued rather than proved, is that thesis: the one consciousness is beyond perception and present within every subject, and science and the traditions describe the same reality from outside and from inside, a view with a near relative in the Pauli–Jung dual\-aspect monism\. Six objections are stated with it: the individuation problem; the dependence of mind on brain; constructivism about mystical experience; the cognitive science of religion; the problem of evil, including suffering no one chose; and Buddhist dissent from a single ground and from a creator\. The binding\-third claim is held as hypothesis, with its falsification conditions stated plainly\. \(direct link \)
