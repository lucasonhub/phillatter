# Brown, Richard: Real Consciousness or Real Illusion? A Higher\-Order Approach to AI Consciousness

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Higher\-order theories hold that phenomenal consciousness requires not just first\-order representation of the world but some form of inner awareness of one’s own mental states\. This paper asks what higher\-order theories imply about the possibility of AI consciousness\. I argue that the answer depends on a distinction that is often collapsed in the literature: whether one holds a computational\-functionalist version of the higher\-order approach, on which the relevant inner awareness is a matter of the right kind of computation regardless of substrate, or a biological naturalist version, on which higher\-order theory is a way of picking out the biological states that consciousness turns out to be\. I also consider a third reading, interpretationalism, on which the question turns entirely on behavior\. Functionalist higher\-order theorists have real, if inconclusive, reason to take current large language models seriously as candidates for the beginnings of higher\-order representation\. Biological naturalist higher\-order theorists do not have to deny that AI could ever be conscious, but they are not committed to it either\. I argue that they should be illusionists about AI consciousness, should it ever arise, though not about our own\. A system with the relevant higher\-order self\-representation would have the kind of consciousness that illusionists think we have, a real, self\-representing kind that is not phenomenal consciousness\. It would be a zombie in the philosopher’s sense, lacking phenomenal consciousness, but not a mindless mimic\. There are two conditions here\. A system is a self\-representing mind, and not a mimic, only if its higher\-order representations are grounded in its own states\. On the biological reading, a further condition, the right biological realization, is needed for phenomenal consciousness\. A system that meets the first and not the second is what I call an AI illusionist\. If its higher\-order representations are like ours, it would also be subject to the illusion, representing a world as present to it when, on the biological reading, none is\. In short, if computational functionalism is true, a system with the right higher\-order structure would have real phenomenal consciousness\. If biological naturalism is true, illusionism is the right account of it\. Which of these is true is an empirical question, and the current evidence is preliminary\.
