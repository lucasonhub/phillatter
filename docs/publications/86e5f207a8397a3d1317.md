# Development and evaluation of the STOU BrainLadder: a board\-game intervention to enhance cognitive health among Thai elderly

**Autores:** Anusorn Koedsri; Thanyasinee Laosum

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Purpose This study aims to \(1\) design and develop the game and \(2\) evaluate the effectiveness of the developed game\. Design/methodology/approach Nine experts participated to examine the feasibility and the design concept and also to assess the prototype&\#x27;s suitability in two separate focus\-group sessions\. Another five experts subsequently evaluated the prototype\. Sixty elders participated in two experimental trials \(30 per trial\)\. Instruments included STOU BrainLadder, a board\-game evaluation form, the trail making test A/B \(TMT\-A/B\) – a memory\-screening test and a satisfaction questionnaire\. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, the Wilcoxon signed\-rank test, Friedman&\#x27;s Test, Kendall&\#x27;s W and content analysis\. Findings The board\-game comprised three mini\-games \(Simon, Matching and RuleMaster\)\. Experts rated it as appropriate, easy to understand and supportive of cognitive\-function training\. After 14 days, participants showed significant within\-cohort improvements \(p &lt; 0\.05\) in game\-embedded indicators related to processing speed, working memory, attention and executive function\. Overall satisfaction was high \(M = 4\.13, SD = 0\.94\), with suggestions to enlarge fonts and adjust audio volume for sensory accessibility\. Practical implications A well\-designed board game shall support cognitive\-health promotion and shall be integrated into community\-based health promotion\. Originality/value This study proposed an innovative game framework combining cognitive\-psychology and social\-cognitive learning to enhance elder cognitive functions in aging societies\.
