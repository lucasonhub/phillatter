# Jovanovic, Vladisav: The Philosophy of Structure: Local Form, Warranted Revision, and Reorganization Across Human, Institutional, and AI Systems

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper develops a philosophy of structure for examining how local forms arise, stabilize, carry functions and burdens, receive or resist warranted correction, and reorganize across human, technological, institutional, and complex\-system domains\. The proposal is meta\-theoretical rather than reductive: it does not claim that persons, large language models, institutions, and other complex systems share a single underlying mechanism\. Instead, it asks a common set of questions about what has stabilized, what sustains it, what it organizes, what pressures exceed its present capacity, and what kinds of evidence or consequence legitimately warrant revision\. The argument distinguishes field, structure, local form, and local structuration, then develops an account of stabilization, load, pressure, selective correction, and reorganization\. Complex\-systems concepts such as metastability, threshold, and scale are used as formal neighbors rather than as claims of mechanistic equivalence\. Human cases are treated through a non\-reduction principle: roles, relationships, identities, wounds, and self\-descriptions may organize a life without exhausting the person who inhabits them\. Large language models provide a contrasting case in which coherent relational form can produce real psychological effects without establishing human\-like subjectivity, while institutional analysis examines the divergence between declared purpose and actual steering\. The central claim is that mature structure is neither rigidity nor formlessness\. It is organization capable of maintaining enough continuity to function while remaining selectively revisable by sufficiently warranted correction\.
