# Toward modeling ecosystem responses to drought through coupled dynamical state representations

**Autores:** Junyi Liu; Xiang Zhang

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Increasingly frequent and persistent drought disturbances are reshaping terrestrial ecosystems, making Modeling Ecosystem Responses to Drought \(MERD\) increasingly important not only to quantify ecosystem impacts but also to understand the mechanisms governing ecosystem trajectories\. Advances in satellite observations, ecological monitoring, and artificial intelligence have greatly improved the ability within MERD to detect drought impacts and predict ecosystem responses across large spatial and temporal scales\. However, an important remaining challenge is to connect environmental observations, statistical relationships, and process\-specific understanding within representations that explicitly describe how coupled vegetation\-water interactions generate ecosystem trajectories through time\. Here, we argue that MERD would benefit from complementing observation\-centered descriptions with dynamical representations of ecosystem state evolution\. We propose that ecosystem drought responses can be represented as evolving coupled dynamical states, complementing analyses based on individual environmental observations\. Within this representation, observations constrain the estimation of ecosystem states, while ecological feedbacks provide the mechanisms governing their trajectories\. We further introduce a coupled vegetation\-water dynamical representation as a prototype for encoding ecological mechanisms into state evolution\. This perspective provides a unified dynamical language through which resilience, recovery, response thresholds, dynamical transitions, legacy effects, carbon loss, and model\-based counterfactual scenarios can be interpreted as different manifestations of ecosystem dynamics\. We envision that future developments in MERD will increasingly integrate Earth observations, geospatial information, artificial intelligence, ecological theory, and mechanistic dynamical representations to move beyond predicting drought impacts toward explaining how ecosystem trajectories emerge\.
