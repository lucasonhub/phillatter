# Gaze and Instrumental and Affective Communication in Doctor\-Patient Interactions

**Autores:** Nadine Bol; Debby Damen; Hugo P\. Aben; Carla Anne Roos; Rabia R\. Chhangur; Gijs A\. Holleman

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This wearable eye\-tracking study investigated the social and clinical context in which patients’ and a clinician’s gaze and verbal behaviours occurred during medical consultations at an outpatient neurology clinic\. We analysed gaze behaviour and instrumental and affective communication of patients and a neurologist in selected consultations \(n = 12\), representing different consultation compositions \(i\.e\., the social context: one\-on\-one, one\-on\-two, one\-on\-many\) and consultation activities \(i\.e\., the clinical context: discussing scan results, performing a neurological examination\)\. In\-depth analyses of mutual and joint gaze in relation to verbal utterances were explored in a smaller subset \(n = 3\), providing detailed descriptions of patient\-neurologist gaze and verbal communication while considering the social and clinical context of these consultations\. Results showed that both patients and the neurologist predominantly gazed at each other’s faces but also looked at other locations and objects\. Individual, mutual, and joint gaze patterns at faces, the screen, and companions varied across social and clinical contexts\. Instrumental and affective communication were intertwined and co\-occurred with patients’ and the neurologist’s mutual and joint gaze states\. These findings underscore that doctor\-patient communication is shaped by what is said and how attention is jointly managed within the broader social and clinical context of doctor\-patient communication\.
