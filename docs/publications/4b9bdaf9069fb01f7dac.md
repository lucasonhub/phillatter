# A statistical learning framework for explainable forest disturbance\-agent attribution with provable guarantees and theoretical foundations for spatial transfer

**Autores:** Khaleel Ahmad; Walid Abdelfattah; Malick Sallah; Jawaher Saud Alrashood

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract We develop the mathematical foundations of a framework that attributes a disturbance agent, namely wildfire, harvest, insect defoliation, windthrow, or drought, to each disturbance event detected in standardized satellite time\-series change metrics\. The attribution task is posed as a statistical decision problem on a feature space built from spectral\-temporal descriptors, object geometry, terrain, and climate\. Within this formulation we prove the Bayes optimality of the posterior\-maximizing rule and of its cost\-sensitive counterpart, a finite\-sample generalization bound for the empirical\-risk minimizer over a finite class, the variance\-reduction identity that governs ensemble estimators together with a bias\-variance decomposition, an ordering between the Gini and entropy impurities, the convexity and second\-order optimality conditions of gradient\-boosted trees, the gradient identities of the softmax and focal objectives, the efficiency, symmetry, and null\-player properties of the Shapley attribution used for explanation, the order preservation of temperature scaling, and the distribution\-free lower and upper coverage bounds of the marginal and class\-conditional conformal predictors used for uncertainty\. We further prove an unbiasedness and variance result for the design\-based accuracy estimator and a decomposition of the cross\-region transfer gap, together with an estimable form of that decomposition\. Many of these results are specializations of established statistical\-learning theory to the attribution setting; the contribution lies in their unified formulation on a standardized metric product, in the explicit statement of the assumptions under which each guarantee holds, and in a small number of new corollaries\. The paper is theoretical: all results are established by proof and illustrated with synthetic numerical examples, no empirical forest\-disturbance dataset is analysed, and the empirical transferability, calibration, and accuracy of an implemented system remain to be evaluated\.
