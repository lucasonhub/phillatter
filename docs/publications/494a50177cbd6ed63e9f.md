## Descrição do feed

In Anna Strasser, Anna&\#x27;s AI Anthology\. How to live with smart machines? Berlin: Xenomoi Verlag\. 2024In this paper, we argue for what we call the ‘AI\-Stance’ \(Strasser &amp; Wilby, 2023\) that is applicable to certain artificial systems when engaged in interactions with human partners\. The AI\-Stance is an epistemological toolbox involving an integrated suite of interpretative and predictive devices that allow us to understand not just the intentional and rational aspects of human\-machine interaction but also the normative, value\-laden aspects of those interactions\. On the view we develop, artificial systems can be considered genuine, albeit minimal, agents when their behaviour is autonomously geared towards finding novel means to human\-defined ends\. They can be thought to adopt a goal – specified and scaffolded by a human partner – and hence be seen to be acting intentionally with regard to that goal\. Further, they can be held responsible for negative outcomes of an interaction where the responsibility cannot be off\-loaded to anyone outside of the interaction \(e\.g\., the manufacturer\), nor wholly attributable to the human partner within the interaction\. We argue that by utilising the AI\-Stance, it is possible to bridge ‘responsibility gaps’ \(Matthias, 2004; Sparrow, 2007\) in cases where one can identify a fault with the means which were contributed by the artificial system\. Drawing on an influential account of moral blame by T\.M\. Scanlon \(2008\), we argue that such faults should be understood as moral rather than merely causal because taking the AI\-Stance allows for artificial systems to be judged blameworthy for their action and for future interactants with the system to modify their relations with that system in ways that are appropriate to that judgement\. \(direct link \)
