# E\-MDD: an evidence\-guided explainable framework for EEG\-based depression recognition

**Autores:** Huang Junfen; Zhuhuayang Zhang; YingYu Cao; Zhenxi Zhao; Liangfei Niu

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction An evidence\-guided explainable framework, E\-MDD, is developed to integrate high\-dimensional EEG representations, neurophysiological biomarkers, and large language models for the recognition of EEG\-based depression with concise and evidence\-based textual explanations\. Methods The proposed E\-MDD framework consists of three stages: classification, evidence construction, and evidence\-guided inference\. In the classification stage, two parallel feature extraction branches are employed to extract Electroencephalography latent features and neurophysiological biomarkers, which are subsequently fused and fed into a lightweight multilayer perceptron for the recognition of depression\. In the evidence construction stage, the latent feature dimensions of the EEG are aligned with neurophysiological biomarkers, and the model&\#x27;s decision basis is identified to construct structured evidence supporting the classification results\. In the evidence\-guided inference stage, structured evidence and the proposed evidence\-guided prompt are used to guide the large language model in generating concise textual explanations\. Results The experimental results on the Mumtaz dataset show that E\-MDD achieves an AUC of 0\.8472 and an F1\-score of 0\.8421\. Meanwhile, the generated short\-text explanations can effectively describe the basis of the model&\#x27;s decision\-making for its predictions, demonstrating a high degree of faithfulness\. The proposed framework also demonstrates consistent evidence\-guided explanation performance across EEGNet and EEGConformer\. Discussion Overall, this study proposes a neurophysiologically interpretable framework for EEG\-based deep learning models, which effectively enhances the interpretability of deep learning models for depression detection and provides a new perspective for understanding their decision\-making mechanisms\.
