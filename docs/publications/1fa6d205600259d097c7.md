# RETHINKING TRANSLATOR TRAINING AS A CROSS\-CUTTING TECHNOLOGICAL WORKFLOW

**Autores:** Yu\. V\. Kondratenko; L\. O\. Hastynshchykova

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Modern translator training faces a structural mismatch: technology is typically taught as a set of isolated modules, one tool per topic, while professional translation practice treats these same tools as an interconnected workflow in which a single instrument routinely performs functions attributed to several distinct stages\. This article reframes the transla\- tion process as a technological chain comprising four interrelated components – pre\-translation analysis, CAT\-assisted translation supported by an AI agent, quality management, and file preparation – and argues that these components are better represented as a network of overlapping actions than as a linear sequence of self\-contained stages\. Drawing on both theoretical models of translator competence \(PACTE’s instrumental competence, the EMT framework\) and empirical studies of specific tools and stages, the article identifies a gap between well\-established theoretical support for cross\- cutting technical competence and its still\-fragmented empirical and classroom validation\. The study demonstrates, through classroom examples – including the pre\-editing of auto\-generated video subtitles using regular expressions and a compar\- ative analysis of PDF\-document translation across several CAT environments – that a single technological action routinely crosses the boundaries between the four proposed stages, undermining the pedagogical logic of modular instruction\. The article proposes a network model of the translation technological chain, in which the four components function as condi\- tional nodes connected by feedback and cross\-cutting links rather than fixed sequential order, and grounds this model in the broader concept of a translator’s digital literacy\. The findings support a concrete pedagogical principle: technical com\- petence formation should be organized around cross\-cutting, project\-based assignments that require students to move across several nodes of the model, rather than around isolated tool\-specific modules\. The article concludes by identifying directions for further research, including the applicability of the proposed model to team\-based translation workflows and an unresolved gap between the documented professional importance of quality\-management tools and students’ compar\- atively modest perception of their value\.
