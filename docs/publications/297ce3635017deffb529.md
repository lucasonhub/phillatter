# Process mapping in clinical healthcare settings in low\-income and lower\-middle\-income countries: a scoping review

**Autores:** Judith Boegli; Karin Groß; Fenella Beynon; Leah F\. Bohle

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objectives To examine the use of process mapping \(PM\) in clinical healthcare settings in low\- and lower\-middle\-income countries \(LLMICs\)\. Methods A scoping review was conducted\. Full\-text empirical studies published from 2014 to 2024 were identified from MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, and CINAHL databases\. Results Twenty\-six articles were included\. Deductive and inductive content analysis was performed\. Applications have included process\- and quality\-improvement initiatives \(n = 12\), health system process analysis and assessment \(n = 7\), health systems tools design, implementation and evaluation \(n = 4\), and costing \(n = 3\)\. Observations, workshops and interviews were applied, with 65% using a single method \(n = 17\)\. PM was predominantly researcher\-led \(n = 17\), with local healthcare workers participating in the majority of studies \(n = 21\); none involved patients\. Gaps in reporting and documentation of PM activities were identified \(n = 16\)\. Conclusion PM demonstrated significant potential as a versatile and adaptable method for improving clinical processes\. Future PM studies should adhere to reporting guidelines, promote locally led PM activities and incorporate diverse perspectives, particularly those of patients\.
