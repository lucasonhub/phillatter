# Synergistic governance or institutional friction? Joint evidence from digital and green city pilots on corporate ESG performance

**Autores:** Xiaoning Cheng; Yiqi Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The transition toward sustainable development necessitates complex, multidimensional macroeconomic interventions\. Over the past decade, the Chinese government has orchestrated a staggered rollout of place\-based policies, most notably the Smart City pilots to propel digital infrastructure and the Low\-Carbon City pilots to enforce stringent emission constraints\. While isolated assessments of these spatial policies predominantly report positive environmental externalities, real\-world regional governance operates under conditions of extreme resource scarcity and multi\-tasking mandates\. When local administrations are concurrently subjected to rapid digitalization targets and rigid decarbonization quotas, the resulting policy superimposition may trigger unforeseen micro\-level distortions\. Addressing this critical gap, the present study constructs a joint\-evaluation framework to quantify the interaction effect of dual\-pilot policies on corporate Environmental, Social, and Governance \(ESG\) performance\. Drawing upon a comprehensive panel dataset of Chinese A\-share listed companies from 2008 to 2023—yielding 83,081 firm\-year observations—a multiple\-period Difference\-in\-Differences \(DID\) estimation is deployed\. Contrary to the idealized premise of policy synergy, empirical regularities herein uncover a robust institutional friction effect: while the standalone Low\-Carbon City policy substantially enhances corporate ESG performance, the superimposition of Smart City mandates generates a highly significant negative interaction effect\. This research departs from the conventional single\-policy evaluation paradigm, unpacking the black box of cross\-departmental policy compatibility\. Furthermore, the Multi\-tasking Agency Theory is enriched by demonstrating how capital\-intensive digital infrastructure projects cannibalize local credit resources, thereby crowding out corporate sustainability investments\. Integrating extensive heterogeneity and robustness checks, including unconfounded mechanism testing, robust causal inference is provided, setting a rigorous baseline for subsequent macro\-policy coordination evaluations\.
