# Integrated multi\-omics identifies distinct immune signatures with diagnostic and therapeutic implications in immune checkpoint inhibitor\-induced myocarditis

**Autores:** Shilong Zhang; Cai Qing\-qing; Jian Zhang; Yuan Liu; Hao Lu; Ye\-Rui Zhang; Yu\-Chen Xu; Hai\-Xiang Peng; Hui\-Yong Chen; Yan Wang; Leilei Cheng; Junbo Ge

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Immune checkpoint inhibitor\-associated myocarditis \(ICI\-myocarditis\) is a rare but frequently fatal immune\-related adverse event\. A comprehensive understanding of the cellular and molecular determinants driving ICI\-myocarditis is essential for improving diagnostic precision and developing targeted interventions\. We delineate the immune landscape of ICI\-myocarditis via scRNA\-seq, flow cytometry, Olink proteomics, and multiplex cytokine profiling\. We demonstrate that high levels of baseline ISG15⁺CD8⁺ T cells are linked to the proinflammatory features of monocytes and in vitro experiments confirm the existence of circulating ISG15⁺CD8⁺ T cells with a high potential to induce cardiomyocyte apoptosis, supporting their pathogenic potential for cardiomyocyte cytotoxicity\. Notably, intercellular crosstalk between these subsets is possibly mediated via the CCL2\-CCR2 axis, and its blockade attenuated ICI\-induced cardiac injury in vivo\. Based on the inflammatory signature in ICI\-myocarditis, we develop an 8\-protein custom panel with promising performance in distinguishing ICI\-myocarditis from other ICIs\-treated controls\. JAK\-STAT pathway activation promotes inflammatory programs in both ISG15⁺CD8⁺ T cells and monocytes\. Furthermore, JAK\-STAT inhibition ameliorates cardiac dysfunction and inflammatory infiltration while enhancing the response to anti\-PD\-1 therapy in mouse models\. In summary, this study implicates immune\-pathogenic features of ICI\-myocarditis and provides a possible basis for future therapies\.
