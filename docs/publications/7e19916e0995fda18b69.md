# Chis\-Ciure, Robert: Policy for an Artificial Consciousness Worth Attributing

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

In anyone but oneself, experience is always inferred, never observed\. This leads to the consciousness attribution problem\. The field’s working strategy to address it is comparative and tentative, extrapolating from the neurotypical adult human case to developmentally different or disordered brains, and then, with less confidence, to other animal clades\. In these cases, the extrapolation presupposes that several functional\-behavioral proxies covary with their neurobiological realizations, which are, at least partially, conserved due to evolutionary kinship\. The possibility of artificial consciousness, or synthetic phenomenality, collapses exactly this covariation: an engineered system can simulate or, in the future, perhaps even emulate an experiencer’s proxy profile while sharing none of the realizers that made those proxies worth trusting in the epistemically less problematic cases of neuroatypical humans and some metazoans\. I model the whole inferential difficulty along two axes of attribution\. On the horizontal axis lies a system’s proxy profile, which denotes the structured pattern it presents from the outside, across behavior, report, functional organization, computational architecture, and any other observer\-recoverable representation\. The vertical axis concerns the realization base, meaning the substrate\-specific and scale\-relative organization by which a system jointly generates and maintains the very functional\-behavioral proxies it exhibits\. While mostly entangled in the human and animal cases, the horizontal and vertical axes come apart in the experiencing machines scenario, revealing two symmetric errors of sufficiency\. To defend against both, I introduce a grain\-sensitive consciousness attribution policy that calibrates the evidential calculus across the two axes when their intersection is partial at best, theory\-laden, and revisable\. Applied to disembodied, stored\-program statistical computation such as LLMs, the policy yields a defeasible negative worth\-attributing verdict\. Nevertheless, whether a machina experiens is nomologically possible in other classes of engineered systems stays wholly open\. The lesson for the field is that proxy success increases, not cancels, the demand for a realizer story\. \(direct link \)
