# “I Didn’t Know That was Plagiarism”: EFL Students&\#x27; AI\-Giarism in a Pesantren\-Based Higher Education

**Autores:** Durratul Hikmah; Indah Riskiana; Wardatus Zahro

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The rise of generative AI \(Gen\-AI\) tools has blurred the ethical boundaries of academic writing, shifting the definition of plagiarism from traditional methods to AI\-based plagiarism \(AI\-giarism\)\. English as a Foreign Language \(EFL\) students are the most vulnerable people to fall into this practice due to second language writing anxiety, which drives dependency on AI as a shortcut\. This study aims to examine the levels of knowledge, attitudes, and self\-reported AI\-based plagiarism practices among EFL students at a pesantren\-based higher education institution\. Employing a quantitative cross\-sectional survey design, data were collected using a validated 51\-item questionnaire adapted from Phyo et al\. \(2023\), distributed to 99 English education students at a pesantren\-based higher education in East Java, Indonesia, via Google Forms\. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, Spearman’s correlation, multiple linear regression, and binary logistic regression\. Result revealed a moderate level of knowledge \(mean = 9\.63/17; 56\.6%\), with the greatest weakness in understanding that AI\-generated text passing Turnitin still constitutes plagiarism \(only 33\.3% correct\)\. Attitudes rejecting plagiarism were at a moderate level \(mean = 91\.24/135; 67\.6%\), with the self\-justification subscale scoring the lowest \(55\.5%\)\. A total of 50\.5% of respondents had engaged in at least one act of AI\-giarism\. Knowledge score emerged as the sole significant independent predictor of AI plagiarism behavior \(AOR = 0\.764;95% CI \[0\.62\-0\.94\]; p = 0\.012\)\. These findings reveal the “Pesantren Paradox”: a conflict between strong religious moral capital and limited digital\-ethical literacy, which creates a loophole for unintended AI\-giarism\.
