# Designing Rare Pediatric Disease Trials for FDA Review: A Mutation\-Agnostic Cell Therapy Strategy in Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy

**Autores:** Patricia Angelique Mallari; Peivand Pirouzi

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Clinical trial design in rare pediatric disease faces two compounding structural constraints that do not apply in the same way to common adult conditions: the eligible population for any single, mutation\-specific intervention is already a fraction of an already small disease population, and enrolling children in a placebo\-controlled trial for a fatal, progressive disease raises genuine ethical questions around consent and equipoise, pushing sponsors toward smaller trials, surrogate endpoints, or historical controls\. This article examines how a mutation\-agnostic allogeneic cell therapy for Duchenne muscular dystrophy \(DMD\) represents a different response to these constraints, trading the narrower population and accelerated\-approval pathway available to mutation\-specific exon\-skipping therapies for a broader eligible population and a correspondingly higher evidentiary burden on a traditional clinical endpoint\. The article develops this trial\-design tradeoff as a general framework applicable to other mutation\-agnostic rare\-disease programs, situates it against the product&\#x27;s documented regulatory history, and proposes a real\-world evidence strategy informed by registries already established for this disease area\. The article closes with recommendations for regulatory affairs professionals designing clinical programs for mutation\-agnostic therapeutics in rare pediatric populations\.Keywords: rare pediatric disease; mutation\-agnostic therapy; Duchenne muscular dystrophy; trial design; allogeneic cell therapy; regulatory artificial intelligence
