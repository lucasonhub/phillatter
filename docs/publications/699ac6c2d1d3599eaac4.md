# VLA\-GAUGE: Systematically Evaluating Generalization and Robustness in Vision\-Language\-Action Models

**Autores:** Jun Xiao; Rui Ye; Dong Cao; Jinyu Gu; Ying Wang; Zhiliang Zhu

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Vision\-Language\-Action models \(VLAs\) have achieved high success rates on familiar manipulation tasks, yet it remains unclear whether such scores reflect genuine zero\-shot generalization and perturbation robustness\. Existing evaluation frameworks provide limited capability\-level diagnosis and combinatorial perturbation coverage, often without connecting diagnosis to targeted model refinement\. We introduce VLA\-GAUGE, a diagnostic framework that unifies both evaluations under a shared executable task representation\. It combines \(1\) paired in\-distribution and out\-of\-distribution tasks across six capability suites, \(2\) single\-factor sensitivity analysis with combinatorial perturbation screening and evaluation, and \(3\) diagnosis\-driven refinement through targeted data augmentation and model fine\-tuning\. We evaluate 12 VLAs spanning different model sizes and action\-modeling paradigms under a unified protocol\. We find that \(1\) The mean Zero\-Shot Score on VLA\-GAUGE is only 22\.3%, with SimpleVLA\-RL scoring 8\.0% and ranking second to last among the evaluated models\. \(2\) Under three representative combinatorial perturbation configurations, the mean R\-Score is 9\.3\. \(3\) Diagnosis\-driven fine\-tuning improves $π\_\{0\.5\}$&\#x27;s zero\-shot generalization and robustness under a selected perturbation combination on held\-out scenarios by 72\.4 and 31\.8 scoring points, respectively; the latter exceeds the 10\.9\-point gain from LIBERO\-Plus single\-factor fine\-tuning despite fewer successful training trajectories\. These results show that high reported success rates on familiar manipulation tasks do not guarantee strong zero\-shot generalization to unseen tasks or robustness to combinatorial perturbations, while diagnostic evaluation provides actionable signals for targeted model improvement\.
