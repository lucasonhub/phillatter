# Digital transformation of outpatient care: physicians’ expectations and barriers to implementing telemedicine and artificial intelligence technologies\.

**Autores:** E\. N\. Morozova; Шинкарева Наталья Владимировна; N\. V\. Eckert; A\. V\. Ferjaoui; Р\. В\. Горенков

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background\. In the context of healthcare digital transformation, telemedicine \(TM\) and artificial intelligence \(AI\) are being integrated into clinical practice; however, the pace of their implementation outpaces the analysis of their impact on workflows, workload, and physicians’ psychological acceptance\. Studying systemic barriers and the human factor is essential for the successful scaling of digital tools in primary care\. Objective: to assess real\-world experience, identify key barriers, and determine the relationship between physicians’ expectations and practical challenges of implementing TM and AI in the activities of outpatient medical professionals\. Materials and methods\. A cross\-sectional study was conducted using an anonymous online survey of 897 medical professionals from outpatient clinics in the Moscow region\. The questionnaire covered socio\-demographic and professional characteristics, frequency and formats of TM, AI and EHR use, as well as perceived barriers and risks\. Statistical analysis employed descriptive statistics and Pearson’s χ² test \(p &lt; 0\.05\)\. Results\. Daily TM consultations are conducted by 49\.7% of physicians, predominantly in audio format \(46\.5%\)\. Despite recognizing TM effectiveness for chronic patient follow\-up \(63\.3%\), 30\.6% report increased workload, and 38\.3% consider no more than two consultations per day optimal\. The main TM barriers are technical failures \(29\.3%\) and low patient digital literacy \(16\.2%\)\. Regarding AI, a gap was revealed: readiness for use is 55%, while 60\.5% have no practical experience; 50\.1% fear degradation of their diagnostic skills\. Major systemic issues include insufficient interoperability \(43\.6%\) and lack of training \(22\.8%\)\. Statistical analysis confirmed significant associations between AI use and expectations \(p &lt; 0\.05\), and between training needs and AI readiness \(χ² = 23\.979; p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Conclusion\. A persistent gap was identified between positive perceptions of digital technologies’ potential and the real challenges of their integration\. The barriers – technical, organizational, and psychological – are systemic and require comprehensive solutions: specialized training, minimization of technical failures, ensuring information systems interoperability, and revision of workload standards considering the time spent on TM consultations\.
