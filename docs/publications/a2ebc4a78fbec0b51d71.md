# Intellectual property in design\-driven industries: A bibliometric and thematic analysis \(2000–2025\) with insights for the Jewellery Industry

**Autores:** Xia Du; Nor Azlina Mohd Noor; Khadijah Mohamed

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study aims to systematically examine the intellectual property \(IP\) research in design\-driven industries through a bibliometric and thematic analysis of publications from 2000 to 2025, and further derives implications for the jewellery industry\. With the rapid development of the creative economy and digital transformation, IP protection has become an important pillar for innovation and market competitiveness in design\-intensive industries\. However, current research lacks a systematic and holistic review of this interdisciplinary field, and evidence specific to the jewellery remains limited\. To fill this gap, a bibliometric and thematic analysis was conducted based on 359 articles retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection \(WoSCC\) database\. Descriptive statistics were processed using Microsoft Excel, while publication trends, global research patterns, thematic structures, and emerging research frontiers were analyzed using CiteSpace and VOSviewer\. The findings indicate that intellectual property research in design\-driven industries has maintained continuous growth and gradually formed an interdisciplinary integration trend\. Among the valid publications, &quot;Law&quot; is the dominant subject category, followed by &quot;Business&quot; and &quot;Management&quot;, with insufficient attention to &quot;Cultural Studies&quot; and &quot;Art&quot;\. Geographically, the USA and England are core hubs, with the USA leading in publications, citations and collaboration; China is an emerging contributor\. IP law journals dominate in publication volume like Journal of Intellectual Property Law, while interdisciplinary journals such as Strategic Management Journal exhibit higher citation impact\. Reliable keyword clustering identifies copyright, IP and innovation as core keywords, with three main thematic areas\. Accordingly, this study offers relevant implications for the jewellery industry, emphasizing integrated IP strategies and adaptive responses to digital and AI\-related challenges\. It provides a systematic knowledge map and supports future research and innovation in design\-driven industries\.
