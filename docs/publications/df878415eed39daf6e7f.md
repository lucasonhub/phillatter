# Designing a Post\-Approval Real\-World Evidence Registry for an FDA\-Regulated Oral Therapy: A Data Architecture Case Study in Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy

**Autores:** Shivangini Maheshbhai Limbachiya; Peivand Pirouzi

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The scientific case for a post\-approval real\-world evidence program is often well developed in regulatory literature, while the practical data architecture needed to execute that program, which variables a registry should actually collect, how they should be structured, and how comparability across a genetically and clinically heterogeneous population should be preserved, receives comparatively little attention\. This article addresses that operational gap directly, using an investigational oral fast skeletal myosin inhibitor for Duchenne muscular dystrophy \(DMD\) as a case study in designing a patient\-registry data architecture for a mutation\-agnostic therapy intended for use across a genetically diverse population with variable disease severity, concomitant treatment history, and prior exposure to other DMD\-targeted therapies\. The article develops the mechanism and regulatory status of this drug category briefly before turning to its principal contribution: a detailed, variable\-level registry design intended to support regulatory decision\-making rather than only descriptive monitoring\. The article closes with recommendations for regulatory affairs professionals responsible for translating a real\-world evidence concept into an executable data\-collection program\.Keywords: fast skeletal myosin inhibitor; Duchenne muscular dystrophy; patient registry; real\-world evidence data architecture; New Drug Application; regulatory artificial intelligence
