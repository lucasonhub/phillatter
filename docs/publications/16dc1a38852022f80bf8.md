# Social comparison influence on moral judgement change: test of the experimental design

**Autores:** Artem Oganyan; Inna F\. Deviatko

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

When individuals evaluate the moral culpability of others, do they adjust their judgments after learning how a peer has assessed the same situation — and does it matter whether that peer ranks higher or lower in the group&\#x27;s social hierarchy? This paper presents a novel experimental procedure that integrates four elements previously not combined in a single field design: vignette\-based elicitation of third\-party moral judgments, sociometric identification of referents within real academic groups, and face\-to\-face dyadic interaction as the mechanism of referent influence in a three\-wave pretest–intervention–posttest experimental design\. Across six student groups \(N = 35 dyads\) we test two hypotheses about moral conformity and its moderation by status asymmetry\. The first hypothesis is strongly confirmed: participants&\#x27; moral judgments shift systematically toward the referent&\#x27;s position in proportion to the initial discrepancy between them\. A significant negative effect of academic performance suggests that higher\-achieving students are less susceptible to referent influence, a finding that warrants dedicated follow\-up\. The second hypothesis that upward social comparison produces stronger conformity vs\. lateral comparison proved beyond the reach of the current design: the study was underpowered for detecting the interaction effect\. The paper documents the methodological lessons of this pilot and proposes specific design improvements for subsequent research\.
