# Novel AI\-derived muscle ultrasound biomarkers linking nutritional status and prognosis in cancer patients

**Autores:** Juan Jose Lopez\-Gomez; Lucía Estévez\-Asensio; Paloma Pérez\-López; Iván Rodríguez\-Marín; Noelia Martín\-Gómez; Olatz Izaola\-Jáuregui; Ángela Cebriá; Jaime González\-Gutiérrez; Rebeca Jiménez\-Sahagún; Puerto Bernoy\-González; Emilia Gómez\-Hoyos; Eduardo Jorge Godoy; Daniel Antonio de Luis Román

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Malnutrition and sarcopenia worsen prognosis in patients with cancer and require accessible diagnostic methods for assessing muscle quantity and quality\. Artificial intelligence \(AI\)\-assisted muscle ultrasound may improve the standardization and efficiency of body\-composition assessment\. This study evaluated the prognostic relevance of AI\-assisted rectus femoris ultrasound biomarkers within a comprehensive nutritional assessment of patients with cancer\. Methods This prospective longitudinal observational cohort study included 389 patients with cancer, most of whom had tumors of digestive origin\. Participants underwent longitudinal and transverse rectus femoris ultrasonography\. Images were analyzed using PIIXMED™ MSK, which applies U\-Net based anatomical segmentation followed by deterministic image\-processing procedures\. The evaluated variables included subcutaneous adipose tissue thickness \(SAT\), rectus femoris thickness \(RFT\) rectus femoris cross\-sectional area \(RFA\), pennation angle, mean echo intensity, and the percentages of low\- and medium\-echogenicity pixels within the segmented region of interest, reported as the Muscle Index \(Mi\) and Fat Index \(FATi\), respectively\. The muscle\-area index was calculated as AreaMi = RFAxMi\. Results The 389 participants had a mean age of 70\.6 \(10\.8\) years, 58\.9% were men, and 53\.7% \( n = 206\) were classified as geriatric oncology patients \(≥70 years\)\. Malnutrition was present in 75\.3% of participants, confirmed sarcopenia in 18\.8% and overall mortality during follow\-up was 26\.5%\. In univariate log\-rank survival analysis, reduced muscle\-area index \(MiArea&lt;1\.36 cm 2 ; p = 0\.014\) and low muscle quality \(Mi &lt; 46%; p = 0\.019\) were the strongest univariate predictors of significantly shorter survival\. In multivariate Cox proportional Hazards regression models adjusted for age, sex, digestive tumor location, and metastatic disease, larger rectus femoris area \(RFA: HR = 0\.77\. 95%CI: 0\.62–0\.95; p &lt; 0\.05\) and thickness \(RFT: HR = 0\.43, 95% CI: 0\.19–0\.99; p &lt; 0\.05\) remained independent protective factors against mortality\. Conversely, reduced muscle index \(Mi &lt; 47%: HR = 1\.60, 95%CI: 1\.05–2\.06; p &lt; 0\.05\) and the presence of myosteatosis \(HR = 2\.35, 95%CI: 1\.19–4\.62; p = 0\.01\) independently predicted an increased risk of mortality\. Conclusion In cancer patients, AI\-assisted rectus femoris ultrasound variables effectively predict mortality\. Greater muscle area and higher muscle index \(Mi &gt; 47%\) indicate a favorable prognosis\. Early ultrasound assessment detects structural alterations before overt deterioration, providing a crucial window for targeted nutritional interventions to preserve muscle integrity and prevent complications\.
