# Sudarsono, Pandi: The dual\-mode awareness framework \(DMAF\): toward epistemic sovereignty in AI\-mediated knowledge societies

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_AI and Society\_:1\-15\. forthcomingWhile cross\-checking a set of financial figures, I submitted the same data to a spreadsheet and to a conversational AI system and received two different answers\. The system, pressed to explain the discrepancy, eventually acknowledged that it had treated the numbers as language rather than as quantities to be computed—and had, in effect, been guessing\. Nothing in its response had indicated this\. This paper develops a conceptual framework for the structural condition that discrepancy revealed: operational opacity, the absence of any visible distinction, at the interaction surface, between AI outputs produced through probabilistic generation and those produced through reliable computation\. I argue that operational opacity differs from algorithmic opacity in the existing literature by relocating the problem from the internal architecture of AI systems to the moment of interaction itself, and that it differs from automation bias by naming a condition prior to evaluation: a culturally embedded assumption of cross\-domain competence—the omnicompetence assumption—that meets an uninformative interface and produces modality collapse, the inability to distinguish what kind of process generated a given response\. The consequence is capability illusion\. I develop the Dual\-Mode Awareness Framework \(DMAF\) around three analytically independent dimensions—modality perceptibility, verification effort, and stakes sensitivity—whose interaction, rather than any single dimension in isolation, determines the epistemic risk of a given exchange\. The framework&\#x27;s normative claims are grounded in Stiegler’s pharmacological account of technology and Habermas’s ethics of communicative transparency, selected because each names a feature of AI\-mediated exchange directly rather than by analogy\. DMAF does not prescribe interface redesign; it offers a diagnosis at the level of user awareness, on the strength of which I argue that primary responsibility for operational opacity falls on those who design and deploy AI systems rather than on the users who encounter them, while showing why this distribution does not require the framework to dictate how that responsibility should be discharged\. \(direct link \)
