# O\., T\.: The Gutenberg Moment: LLMs and the Irreversible Liberation of Formal Language

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_\. 2026Large language models \(LLMs\) are frequently compared to Gutenberg’s printing press as a metaphor for technological disruption at scale\. This paper gives that analogy mathematical content and identifies where it goes deeper than prior accounts have recognized\. We argue that LLMs do not constitute a threat to human intellectual agency because they lack the question\-sustaining structure \(Q\-structure\) that defines such agency — a result inherited from the companion paper The Phantom Intellect: Why AI Threat Discourse Misreads the Structure of Mind\. What LLMs do accomplish is structurally distinct and historically more precise: they dissolve the scarcity of formal\-language generation capacity, a capacity previously confined to specialists trained within institutional structures\. We formalize this dissolution via a Dissemination Closure axiom, prove that the resulting expansion of the generative capability set is irreversible under any operation short of total erasure of disseminated artefacts \(Theorem 4\.6\), and define a Gutenberg Moment as the structural condition under which such irreversibility obtains\. The Leiden Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics is read, in light of these results, not as a response to the emergence of mathematical intelligence in machines, but as a response to the collapse of the scarcity on which a specific institutional architecture depended — an architecture whose relationship to Q\-structure was never constitutive\. \(direct link \)
