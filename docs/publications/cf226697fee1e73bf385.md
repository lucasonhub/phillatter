# Human personality and place

**Autores:** Ling Xu

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** dissertation

## Resumo

Geographic variation in personality traits is a well\-documented phenomenon globally, characterized by distinct regional clustering, particularly for openness to experience, along the rural\-urban continuum\. This doctoral thesis investigates these psychological landscapes by evaluating regional personality variation in Estonia, a small Baltic nation, and examining the underlying behavioral mechanisms driving these differences\. By comparing individual\-level and aggregate\-level standardizations, the first phase of this research demonstrates that Estonia exhibits regional personality differences comparable in magnitude to much larger nations\. However, spatial correlations between regional personality and political, economic, social, and health \(PESH\) indicators across countries were small to modestly consistent\. This inconsistency highlights how spatial aggregation can obscure true individual\-level relations, underscoring methodological challenges like the modifiable areal unit problem \(MAUP\), Simpson&\#x27;s paradox, and the ecological fallacy\. To uncover the mechanisms behind these regional differences, the thesis scrutinizes the role of selective migration\. Rather than relying on traditional, binary &quot;stayer versus migrant&quot; categories, this research incorporates nuanced, functional\-level migration directions across villages, towns, cities, and international borders\. The observations reveal that combining distinct geographic trajectories washes out significant effects\. Crucially, individuals residing in identical destination types showed substantial personality differences based on their birthplaces\. This discovery challenges classic Person\-Environment fit theory, suggesting that individuals retain persistent psychological &quot;traces&quot; from their childhood environments that adult self\-selection does not completely erase\. Finally, a machine learning approach was employed to explore the limits of differentiating an individual&\#x27;s birth and current residence based on their personality profile\. While broad Big Five domains yielded modest predictive accuracy, adopting a highly granular item\-level approach significantly enhanced model performance\. Within Estonia, personality items differentiated current settlement type slightly better than birth settlement type, consistent with modest personality\-related residential sorting, whereas Big Five domains showed little difference\. In contrast, being born abroad was much more strongly differentiated than currently living abroad, suggesting that international birthplace retains a distinct personality\-related signal\. Overall, birth and current residence showed overlapping but not identical personality profiles, with these differences more visible at the item level than in broad domains\. Ultimately, this thesis advances geographic psychology by showing that regional personality variation persists independent of a nation&\#x27;s landmass and is associated with selective migration directions, while offering critical insights into the developmental childhood impact versus adulthood freedom of choice debate\.
