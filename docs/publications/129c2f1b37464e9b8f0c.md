# Moore, Terrence: The Natural Vow

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

This paper is Chapter 1 of Noura: Justice, Flourishing, and Commonwealth\. It address Hume’s guillotine\. Modern moral philosophy has long been haunted by David Hume&\#x27;s guillotine: the sharp distinction between is and ought, between factual description and normative prescription\. From this divide springs the persistent worry that no amount of empirical knowledge about the world—about human nature, social systems, or ecological interdependence—can ever logically compel a moral ought\. In the absence of divine command or self\-evident duties, the foundation of ethics seems to crumble into subjective preference or cultural convention\. This chapter of Noura: Justice, Flourishing, and the Commonwealth argues that the vow\-motive—the durable commitment to sustain and repair the conditions for flourishing—dissolves this divide\. It does not attempt to append a normative conclusion to a supposedly complete collection of value\-neutral facts\. It rejects the assumption that such a description of a human being could ever be complete\. Human beings are developmental and teleological creatures whose capacities have characteristic forms of integrated exercise\. The moral ought names the motives and actions expressive of that flourishing\. When interdependence is perceived without distortion—or lived within institutions that reveal it—care emerges as the coherent completion of human development rather than as an external imperative\.
