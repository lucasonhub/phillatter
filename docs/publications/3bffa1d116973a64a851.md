# The role of intelligence in crime prevention: A comparative analysis of intelligence\-led policing in South Africa and across countries

**Autores:** Mahlomola Mahlakwana; Radiakga Thabang Molokomme

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

In an era when information is power, the ability to anticipate crime has become more critical than merely responding to it\. Effective crime prevention increasingly depends on the strategic adoption of Intelligence\-Led Policing \(ILP\)\. This study examines the role of ILP as a proactive crime prevention strategy, comparing South Africa with the more established models of the United Kingdom, the United States, and Australia\. Grounded in Routine Activity Theory \(RAT\), this qualitative desktop study draws on fifteen purposively selected academic and policy sources, identified through a documented search and screening process\. The findings show that while ILP is well institutionalised in countries with mature policing systems, its implementation in South Africa remains fragmented, constrained by institutional fragmentation, limited analytical capacity, political interference, and resource shortages\. Comparative analysis underscores the importance of inter\-agency coordination, professionalisation of intelligence units, and context\-specific adaptation of ILP principles rather than direct replication of foreign models\. The study concludes that strengthening ILP in South Africa requires a unified intelligence framework that integrates community policing, builds institutional capacity, and embeds a culture of intelligence sharing and accountability, and it proposes a research agenda centred on evaluating pilot ILP interventions\.
