# Environmental Health Compliance Requirements for Urban Tuition Centres Operating in Renovated Shop Office Premises

**Autores:** Nur Khairul Faizah Mustafa; Adi Irfan Che Ani; Sharyzee Mohmad Shukri; Mohd Hayazi Agusi

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Adherence to environmental health standards is imperative to safeguard the well\-being of students, educators, and administrative personnel within private tuition centres\. Urban tuition centres operating in renovated shop\-office premises may encounter environmental health challenges because their original commercial layouts are not necessarily suited to sustained educational occupancy\. This study identifies the environmental health parameters relevant to tuition\-centre licensing in Kajang, Selangor, and proposes practical, evidence informed improvement measures\. A qualitative research design combined documentary analysis of the Ministry of Health Malaysia’s premises\-inspection checklist and 28 regulatory, technical and scholarly sources with one semistructured key\-informant interview involving an authorised environmental health inspector from the Hulu Langat District Health Office\. Kajang, defined as the geographical context without selected case study and field observations or premises inspections were conducted\. Directed qualitative content analysis and a six\-phase thematic procedure were undertaken manually using a structured coding matrix\. Five document informed themes were identified: lighting; indoor air quality and ventilation; sanitation, cleanliness and waste management; noise control; and building safety and maintenance\. Documentary frequencies indicate the extent to which the selected sources address each theme but do not represent centre level of noncompliance rates\. The inspector’s account provides contextual interpretation of inspection requirements rather than statistically representative evidence\. Appropriate responses include verifying classroom illuminance, ensuring adequate outdoor\-air ventilation, maintaining hygienic facilities, controlling intrusive noise, and keeping escape routes and safety equipment serviceable\. The findings provide a substantially contextualised framework for interpreting environmental health requirements in repurposed shop\-office tuition centres, subject to the limitations of a single key informant and the absence of primary premises level observations\. Keywords: Compliance, Environmental Health Standard, Parameters, Shop Office, Tuition Centre
