# The role of OSMRβ in inflammatory skin diseases

**Autores:** Qianqian Zhang; Guifang Qin; Xiaomin Huang; Zudong Meng; Yulin Zou

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

OSMRβ is a member of the IL\-6 cytokine receptor family and plays a critical role in pruritus and immune\-mediated skin diseases\. Upon inflammatory stimulation, skin immune cells release OSM and IL\-31\. These cytokines signal through the heterodimeric receptor complexes OSMRβ/gp130 \(type II OSM receptor\) and OSMRβ/IL\-31RA \(IL\-31 receptor\), which are expressed on keratinocytes, fibroblasts, and dorsal root ganglion \(DRG\) neurons\. Engagement of these receptors activates downstream JAK/STAT, MAPK/ERK and PI3K/AKT signaling cascades, leading to itch and inflammation that contributing to the pathogenesis of atopic dermatitis \(AD\), psoriasis \(PSO\), prurigo nodularis \(PN\) and familial primary localized cutaneous amyloidosis \(FPLCA\)\. Although a wide range of therapeutic options exist for these diseases, specific targeted treatments remain lacking\. Vixarelimab, an OSMRβ\-targeting monoclonal antibody, is currently under clinical evaluation for PN and AD\.
