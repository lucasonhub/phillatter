# Use of clarithromycin in the management of pediatric leprosy cases: an economic evaluation

**Autores:** Kelli Carneiro de Freitas Nakata; Ternize Mariana Guenkka; Priscilla Perez da Silva Pereira; Alytissa Cosme; Gilson Yugi Nakata; Zenóbia Quinderé Barreto; Luci Emília Grzybowski de Oliveira; Letícia Rossetto da Silva Cavalcante

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction: Leprosy remains a major public health challenge in hyperendemic regions, and new pediatric cases signal recent, active transmission\. Children who require substitution of a multidrug therapy \(MDT\) component because of adverse events, rifampicin resistance, or glucose\-6\-phosphate dehydrogenase \(G6PD\) deficiency currently lack approved second\-line therapeutic options\. Objective: To conduct an economic evaluation analyzing costs and estimating the budget impact of incorporating clarithromycin oral suspension as a second\-line therapeutic alternative for pediatric leprosy cases requiring MDT substitution in a Brazilian state\. Methodology: A cost\-minimization analysis and a budget impact analysis were conducted from the perspective of the Brazilian Unified Health System \(SUS\), using a decision\-tree model with a 12\-month and a five\-year time horizon, respectively\. The target population was children aged 6 months to 12 years\. Parameters were based on local epidemiological data, public procurement prices, and expert consensus obtained through the Delphi technique\. A univariate deterministic sensitivity analysis tested the robustness of the model\. Results: Incorporating clarithromycin resulted in an average incremental cost of US$ 185\.27 per case per year\. The annual budget impact was estimated at US$ 38,353\.44, with a cumulative five\-year impact of US$ 191,767\.21\. The sensitivity analysis showed that the annual budget impact could range from US$ 30,682\.75 to US$ 107,880\.94, depending on fluctuations in the drug price\. Conclusions: The incorporation of clarithromycin oral suspension 50 mg/mL is recommended as a second\-line option for pediatric leprosy cases requiring MDT substitution in the analyzed state\. To standardize access and promote the rational use of the medication, a clinical protocol should accompany its incorporation\.
