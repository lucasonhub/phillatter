## Resumo

Background/objective Induced molting is a well\-established model of nutritional restriction used to restore reproductive performance in laying hens\. However, limited information is available on how different molt induction programs \(MIPs\) coordinately influence the interactions among the reproductive, metabolic, and stress axes\. This study evaluated how different MIPs, representing distinct levels of physiological restriction, modulate the endocrine–metabolic responses associated with reproductive regression and post\-molt recovery in laying hens\. Methods Seventy\-five Rhode Island laying hens were randomly assigned to one of five MIPs: complete feed withdrawal, restricted commercial feed \(CF\), wheat bran \(WB\), alfalfa, or sorghum\. Plasma concentrations of estradiol, progesterone, prolactin, insulin, insulin\-like growth factor\-1 \(IGF\-1\), triiodothyronine \(T3\), and corticosterone, together with serum glucose, non\-esterified fatty acids \(NEFA\), and urea, were determined during the pre\-molt, molt induction, and post\-molt recovery phases\. In addition, MIPs were grouped into three physiological restriction categories: absolute restriction \(feed withdrawal\), moderate restriction \(CF and sorghum\), and nutrient dilution \(alfalfa and WB\)\. Results The MIPs induced distinct degrees of reproductive regression and metabolic challenge\. Feed withdrawal and alfalfa reached the target body weight loss more rapidly \(17\.27 and 18\.73 d, respectively\), whereas restricted CF required the longest period \(28\.87 d; P &lt; 0\.0001\)\. During molt induction, absolute restriction elicited the greatest endocrine suppression, characterized by lower estradiol \(76\.96 pg/mL\), progesterone \(0\.34 ng/mL\), insulin \(6\.58 µIU/mL\), IGF\-1 \(33\.82 ng/mL\), and T3 \(1\.84 ng/mL\) concentrations, together with higher corticosterone \(12\.21 ng/mL\), NEFA \(1\.24 mEq/L\), and urea \(8\.45 mg/dL\) concentrations \( P &lt; 0\.05\)\. Moderate restriction produced the mildest physiological alterations, whereas nutrient dilution induced intermediate responses\. Correlation analyses revealed a strong integration between reproductive and metabolic biomarkers \( r = 0\.35–0\.79\), with negative associations involving corticosterone and NEFA \( r = −0\.44 to −0\.71; P &lt; 0\.01\)\. Regression analyses further demonstrated distinct patterns of endocrine–metabolic coupling among the different restriction categories\. Conclusions Molt induction programs elicit distinct physiological responses that influence both reproductive regression and subsequent recovery\. These findings indicate that induced molting should be regarded as a coordinated process of endocrine–metabolic reorganization rather than merely a consequence of body weight loss\.
