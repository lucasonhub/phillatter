# Pavement vibration\-based estimation of vehicle speed and axle spacing for intelligent transportation infrastructure

**Autores:** Wanming Li; Dingwang Chen; YE Zhoujing; Songli Yang; Pengpeng Li; Haonan Zhang; Linbing Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Vehicle speed and axle spacing are key parameters that shape the pavement vibration response and serve as essential prior inputs for downstream models such as axle\-load estimation and hidden\-void detection\. Obtaining them from external systems such as video is vulnerable to weather and to time\-synchronisation errors between systems, whereas estimating them from the pavement vibration signal itself enables closed\-loop interpretation of traffic information and structural state within a single embedded system\. Using field data from embedded MEMS accelerometers, this study reconstructs the signal with complete ensemble empirical mode decomposition with adaptive noise \(CEEMDAN\) and dominant\-frequency screening, builds an instantaneous total\-energy curve via the continuous wavelet transform, and estimates speed and axle spacing by pairing prominent energy peaks across two sensors; the method is compared, under identical criteria, with a time\-domain envelope peak method\. For 12 field passes of a four\-axle truck \(25\.00–55\.62 t, 40–60 km/h\), both methods succeed in every case\. Over the ten cases evaluated against the 7750 mm total wheelbase, the two methods have similar median errors \(6\.5% vs\. 6\.8%\), whereas the wavelet energy method has a lower mean \(6\.2% vs\. 8\.8%\), a narrower interquartile range \(3\.3 vs\. 4\.8 percentage points\) and a much lower maximum \(10\.5% vs\. 33\.8%\); in the two cases evaluated against the 1350 mm rear\-axle spacing the errors are 2\.8%–3\.9% and 3\.2%–4\.5%\. The energy method was more stable under noise spikes \(6\.5% vs\. 33\.8% in the same case\), insensitive to the frequency band and to the wavelet scale grid, and required no case\-dependent parameter tuning, whereas the envelope method depends on a window length whose optimum drifts with speed\. The reconstructed signal also preserves waveform and spectral information, providing a reusable time\-frequency feature for downstream models\.
