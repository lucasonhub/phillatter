# Vogt, David Chelsom: AI\-generated Reason\-giving at the Courts

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Forthcoming in Routledge Handbook on the Ethics of AI and Criminal Justice \(2026\)\. This chapter discusses whether AI\-generated reason\-giving may satisfy the duty to give reasons for judicial decisions\. The first part of the chapter examines the legal and moral basis of the reason\-giving duty and distinguishes between two sets of norms regulating it: norms of reasons applying to the content of the reasons given, and norms of reason\-giving applying to the act of providing reasons\. It is argued that reason\-giving is constitutive of legal decisions in the sense that legal decisions necessarily provide directives and justifications\. The second part of the chapter considers arguments for why AI cannot give reasons in the same way that human judges give reasons\. AI systems cannot subject themselves to the norms that are constitutive of the speech acts that are involved in judicial reason\-giving\. Nevertheless, the institutional context in which AI is deployed may allow its outputs to be interpreted as providing justifications and directives on behalf of the justice system\. This requires an established human practice that fixes the illocutionary meaning of legal decisions—a framework that AI\-generated outputs can then operate within, without themselves being the source of that meaning\.
