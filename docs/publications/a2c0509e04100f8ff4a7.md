# AURELIANO \- A Human–AI Hybrid Operational Framework for Decision Intelligence Context Management and Human

**Autores:** Fabio Chiavolini

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The increasing availability of generative artificial intelligence \(AI\) systems has expanded the possibilities for developing personalized, context\-sensitive forms of human–AI collaboration\. The effectiveness of such collaborations depends not only on the capabilities of the underlying language model but also on the organization of interaction, the management of contextual information, the consistency of operational rules, the verification of outputs and the allocation of decision\-making authority\. This paper presents Aureliano, an experimental Human–AI Hybrid Hub \(HAHH\) framework designed to structure these dimensions within an existing generative AI environment\. Rather than introducing a new foundational model, Aureliano operates as a higher\-level cognitive and procedural framework that organizes the interaction between a human principal and a general\-purpose AI system\. Its central objectives are to improve contextual continuity, mitigate semantic drift and entropy, distinguish evidence from inference, support complex reasoning, maintain decision traceability and preserve human authority over consequential decisions\. The framework is examined through a conceptual architecture, an operational state\-management model, a structured epistemic classification scheme, a human\-in\-the\-loop validation mechanism and an exploratory verification approach\. Particular attention is devoted to the distinction between persistent information and autonomous memory, between behavioral consistency and model identity and between internally reported test results and independently reproducible empirical evidence\. The analysis suggests that a substantial part of the practical value of personalized AI collaboration may be produced by the systematic organization of the human–AI interface rather than by modifications to the underlying model\. Nevertheless, this proposition requires controlled empirical evaluation\. The paper therefore distinguishes the framework&\#x27;s specified properties, reported experimental observations and unresolved hypotheses\. The contribution is a transferable conceptual approach to the design and evaluation of human\-controlled, context\-aware AI collaboration, with implications for decision support, knowledge management, organizational analysis and responsible AI governance\.
