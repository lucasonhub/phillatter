# Lifetime exposure to nitrogen dioxide and ozone and pediatric asthma

**Autores:** Wenli Ni; Hilary L\. Zetlen; Sheryl L\. Rifas‐Shiman; Heike Luttmann Gibson; Emily Oken; Diane R\. Gold; Mary Berlik Rice

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Short\-term exposure to nitrogen dioxide \(NO2\) and ozone \(O3\) worsens pediatric respiratory health, but impacts of lifetime exposure on asthma development are less clear\. We examined associations of early\-life and cumulative NO2 and O3 exposure with asthma from mid\-childhood through mid\-adolescence among participants in Project Viva, a pre\-birth cohort enrolled in eastern Massachusetts, USA, from 1999 to 2002\. We estimated residential NO2 and O3 at 1×1 km resolution using geospatial models and calculated average concentrations in the first year of life and cumulatively through mid\-childhood and early teens\. Asthma was reported by questionnaire in mid\-childhood \(mean age 7\.9 years, n=1,174\), early teens \(13\.3 years, n=979\), and mid\-teens \(17\.8 years, n=665\)\. Using logistic regression adjusted for child age and sex, maternal education, and household smoking, we assessed odds of asthma per interquartile range \(IQR\) increase in exposure\. Higher NO2 in the first year of life \(IQR 10 ppb\) and cumulatively through midchildhood \(IQR 9 ppb\) was associated with higher odds of asthma in mid\-childhood \(odds ratio\[OR\] 1\.34, 95% confidence interval \[CI\]: 1\.05\-1\.71; OR 1\.32, 95% CI: 1\.01\-1\.73, respectively\)\. We did not observe associations for O3\. Even at relatively low levels, early\-life and cumulative NO2 exposure may increase childhood asthma risk\.
