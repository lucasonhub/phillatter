# Peripheral 5\-HT3A and DP2 targets as the basis for the analgesic action of the novel isoquinoline derivative G\-104

**Autores:** Daniil A\. Puzanov; Yu\.A\. Fomina; T\. Yu\. Kalyuta; A\. A\. Smolyak; С\. А\. Астафьева; Elena N\. Reshetova; Владимир Николаевич Стрельников; Alexander Fedonnikov

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Relevance\. The search for a potent and safe analgesic devoid of the disadvantages of opioids and non steroidal anti inflammatory drugs remains an unresolved challenge in pharmacology\. The synthetic isoquinoline derivative 6 \(3,3 dimethyl 3,4 dihydroisoquinolin 1 yl\)aminohexanoic acid \(G\-104\) has demonstrated marked analgesic activity in preclinical models; however, its molecular targets and mechanism of action have remained unidentified\. Objective\. To predict the potential peripheral targets 5\-HTЗА and DP2 and analgesic mechanism of this novel isoquinoline derivative using a comprehensive in silico analysis\. Methods\. Target prediction \(SwissTargetPrediction, SEA, TargetNet, PPB2\), molecular docking \(Glide\), and molecular dynamics simulations \(GROMACS\) were performed\. Results\. In combination with previously obtained experimental data, a hypothesis on the possible mechanism of G\-104 analgesia is proposed\. The serotonin 5\-HT ЗА receptor and the prostaglandin DP₂ receptor were selected as potential targets\. Docking revealed that G\-104 forms complexes with both receptors characteristic of antagonists, and molecular dynamics for DP₂ confirmed the stability of the binding pose throughout the simulation trajectory\. This dual peripheral mechanism—simultaneous inhibition of serotonergic pain modulation and pro inflammatory prostaglandin D₂ signaling—may be consistent with potent and prolonged analgesia observed in vivo and predicts a low risk of central and autonomic adverse effects\. Conclusion\. The obtained results position G\-104 as a potentially effective multitarget analgesic and pave the way for further preclinical investigations\.
