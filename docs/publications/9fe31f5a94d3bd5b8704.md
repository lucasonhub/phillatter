# Fujie, Naoto: Examining Sacilottian Operations: Representational Cognition, Natural Reason, Philosophical Operations, Autonomous Science, and Xenoscience

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper examines three interacting but distinguishable operational series: the operations theorized in Daniel Sacilotto’s Structure and Thought \(2024\), the philosophical operations performed by his own reconstruction, and the scientific operations that may emerge through autonomous and heterogeneous research configurations\. It reconstructs Sacilotto’s account of representational cognition, structural mapping, historical theoretical succession, and Natural Reason, with particular attention to the Rosenberg\-derived E1/E2 conditions and the six QST principles\. Sacilotto’s framework is treated as a historically revisionary materialist realism capable of accommodating structural generation, retrospective theoretical reconstruction, and non\-organic forms of cognition\. The analysis then examines how theoretical succession, material intervention, measurement revision, and operational continuation can become coupled, separated, and reconnected under specific configurations\. Constructed autonomous\-science cases illustrate how changes in instrumentation, variables, temporal conditions, external memory, and experimental authorization can reorganize subsequent scientific possibilities\. Drawing on operational\-shape realism and Xenoscience, the paper investigates the conditions under which philosophical distinctions and warranted theoretical comparisons may enter subsequent scientific operations and acquire material consequences\. It distinguishes inferential justification, physical implementation, actual effects, and cross\-medium propagation without presupposing a unified rational subject or shared global state space\. The resulting account identifies reciprocal constraints between Natural Reason and autonomous scientific operations while preserving the independent explanatory significance of Sacilotto’s philosophical project\. \(direct link \)
