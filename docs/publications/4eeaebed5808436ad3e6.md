# How Can Access to Justice in Criminal Proceedings Be Improved in Lithuania? Reforming Effective Defence, Lay Participation and Language Rights

**Autores:** Vladimir Komarnickiy

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: Access to justice in criminal proceedings, understood here as the effective ability of victims, suspects, and defendants to assert their position and to be protected against wrongful conviction, is bound up with the right to a fair trial under Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights \(ECHR\)\. Although the Republic of Lithuania ranks highly in international rule\-of\-law assessments and its Code of Criminal Procedure already secures numerous guarantees, practice reveals persistent gaps in the functional \(procedural\) dimension of that right, effective defence, equality of arms and effective participation\. Against the background of the contested 2023 reform proposals, this article asks how these procedural guarantees can be strengthened\. Method: The study applies doctrinal \(formal\-legal\), comparative\-legal, historical, and functional methods\. It analyses the Lithuanian Code of Criminal Procedure, Article 6 ECHR, and European Court of Human Rights case law\. It conducts a functional comparison with selected jurisdictions \(Germany, England and Wales, Denmark, the United States, Malta, Ireland, Argentina, and the Russian Federation\), chosen and justified in the methodology based on comparability of fair\-trial standards and transferability to the Lithuanian model\. Results and Conclusions: We advance four proposals: \(1\) an automated, randomised system for appointing state\-funded defence counsel; \(2\) reintroducing the \(non\-advocate\) public defender acting alongside a professional attorney, reframed by reference to modern analogues such as the English McKenzie friend and the German Beistand; \(3\) introducing trial by jury, at the defendant’s request, for serious offences; and \(4\) strengthening the interpreter’s role and the right to use one’s native language, including electronic delivery and self\-service translation of the case file\. The distinctive contribution lies less in restating the well\-known risks of prosecution\-influenced defence than in combining these measures, in particular the automated assignment system and the digital handling of language rights, into an integrated and transferable reform package, tested against the principal counter\-arguments, including the objection that lay participation is alien to the Lithuanian legal tradition\.
