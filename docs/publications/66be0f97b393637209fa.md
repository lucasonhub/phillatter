# Consumer perceptions of marketing mix in supermarkets: a cross\-cultural analysis of Croatia and Tanzania

**Autores:** Marko Šoštar; Robert Suphian; Gabrijela Garic; Josip Knežević

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The primary objective of this study was to examine whether significant differences exist in the perception of supermarket marketing mix elements between consumers in Croatia and Tanzania\. The analysis focused on five key dimensions \(product, price, distribution, promotion, and personnel\) with the aim of identifying the factors contributing to consumer satisfaction or dissatisfaction within distinct market contexts\. The research employed a quantitative methodology, gathering data from 991 respondents \(400 from Croatia and 591 from Tanzania\) through a structured survey based on a five\-point Likert scale\. The findings revealed statistically significant differences across all five marketing mix dimensions\. Tanzanian respondents consistently expressed more favorable perceptions, particularly in relation to promotional activities and personnel interactions\. Exploratory factor analysis suggested three broader dimensions, whereas confirmatory factor analysis provided stronger support for the theoretically specified five\-factor structure\. Multi\-group CFA further supported metric and scalar measurement invariance across the Croatian and Tanzanian samples\. The findings highlight the importance of adapting retail marketing strategies, particularly communication, service delivery, and promotional activities, to the specific characteristics of different national market contexts\.
