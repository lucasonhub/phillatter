# Community learning drives children’s digital literacy development: evidence for reconstructing broken education ecosystems

**Autores:** Judit Horgas; Eszter Salamon; Aristidis Protopsaltis

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Digital literacy development in children is widely assumed to occur primarily through formal schooling, yet growing evidence suggests that community learning—encompassing families, peers, and non\-formal organizations—has become the dominant pathway through which children acquire digital skills and competences\. This article focuses on the current distribution of digital literacy education responsibilities across formal and informal actors, examines how community\-based learning both mitigates and reproduces inequality, and analyses the implications of rights\-based frameworks for children’s digital participation and protection\. Drawing on quantitative survey data collected from primary and secondary school students, their teachers, school leaders, and parents in Hungary and the Netherlands within the framework of the EFFEct \(effective family and community engagement in technology\) horizon Europe project, qualitative and quantitative research from the EU\-funded DRONE \(digital literacy and combat of disinformation among vulnerable groups of adolescents\) and legal research in the SAILS \(supporting and advocating for inclusive learning spaces\) projects, and a systematic review of recent international scholarship, the study provides multi\-level empirical evidence on stakeholder roles in digital literacy education ecosystems\. across all four student groups surveyed, 100% of substantive responses indicated no perceived school\-based instruction on foundational digital citizenship and online safety skills, despite near\-universal device access, revealing a systematic curricular gap that contrasts sharply with children’s high levels of digital connectivity\. Parent data from 135 participants across six European countries in the DRONE project showed that families bear primary responsibility for teaching digital literacy to their children\. This finding is associated with both the limited availability and delayed implementation of structured school\-based programs, and the increasingly early engagement of children with digital technologies, through which digital skills and habits are formed prior to entry into formal schooling\. Addressing this fragmentation requires an urgent and systemic reconstruction of digital literacy education through coordinated multi\-stakeholder governance, curricular reform, teacher empowerment, and inclusive community\-based approaches that explicitly recognizes the central role of families and communities in children’s digital development\.
