# The Impact of AI\-Enabled Customer Interaction on Consumer Buying Intention towards Housing Properties in Bangalore: Evidence from Adoption, Regression and Correlation Analysis

**Autores:** Ajay Kumar Naik \.M; Dr\.Vaishaly Bopaiah G

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

AI\-enabled customer interaction, delivered through recommendation systems, chatbots and virtual assistants, is increasingly used by housing and real\-estate platforms to engage prospective buyers\. Housing is a high\-value, high\-involvement purchase in which trust and accurate information matter, yet evidence on how AI\-enabled interaction relates to buying intention in an Indian metropolitan market such as Bangalore remains limited\. This study examines the adoption and usage of AI\-enabled customer interaction tools among prospective housing buyers, their impact on and relationship with buying intention, and the challenges buyers report\. A quantitative, cross\-sectional survey was conducted with a structured five\-point Likert questionnaire, and 214 valid responses collected between 19 and 25 August 2026 were analysed with IBM SPSS Statistics and jamovi using descriptive statistics, Cronbach&\#x27;s alpha, one\-sample t tests, multiple linear regression, and Pearson and Spearman correlation\. Adoption was moderate\-to\-high \(item means 3\.26–3\.48; composite = 3\.373; α = \.849\)\. AI\-enabled interaction was significantly and positively correlated with buying intention \(Pearson r = \.337; Spearman ρ = \.349; p &lt; \.001\)\. A regression of the five AI items on the outcome was significant but explained a modest share of variance \(R² = \.056; F\(5, 208\) = 2\.490; p = \.032\), and no individual item was a significant unique predictor\. The most prominent challenges were a preference for human interaction at final negotiation \(M = 3\.36\) and technical issues \(M = 3\.27\); the challenge scale was reliable \(α = \.792\)\. Three of four hypotheses were supported and one was partly supported\. The study extends AI\-marketing research to the housing context and supports a hybrid AI\-plus\-human approach: AI for search and first enquiries, human contact for negotiation, and reliable technology throughout\. Because the design is cross\-sectional, the findings describe association rather than causation\. KEYWORDS: AI\-enabled customer interaction, Chatbots, Recommendation systems, Buying intention, Housing properties, Real estate, Bangalore\.
