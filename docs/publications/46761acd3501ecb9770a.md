# Conventional alumina to advanced functional materials: Evolution, surface engineering, and emerging applications in separation science and beyond

**Autores:** Krishna Radheshyam Gupta; Monali Rushi Dakhole; Amruta Selote; Milind Janrao Umekar

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Alumina \(Al₂O₃\) is a versatile inorganic oxide that has evolved from a conventional industrial material into an engineered functional platform for chromatography, catalysis, adsorption, environmental remediation, sensing, energy\-related technologies, and advanced ceramics\. Its broad utility arises from its diverse crystalline phases, tunable surface chemistry, high thermal stability, mechanical strength, controllable porosity, and capacity for surface modification\. Historically, activated alumina served as an important polar adsorbent and chromatographic stationary phase, while the Bayer process dominated industrial alumina production\. Advances in precipitation, sol–gel, hydrothermal, template\-assisted, and combustion methods subsequently enabled greater control over crystal phase, morphology, surface area, and pore architecture\.This review critically examines the evolution of alumina from conventional and industrial forms to porous, mesoporous, nanostructured, chemically functionalized, polymer\-coated, and hybrid materials\. Particular emphasis is placed on how polymorphism, surface hydroxylation, pore architecture, particle morphology, and functional groups govern adsorption and separation performance\. The development of alumina\-based chromatographic stationary phases, from activated alumina to chemically bonded and polymer\-modified systems, is discussed alongside emerging applications in catalysis, environmental remediation, sensing, and multifunctional composites\.The review further addresses sustainability challenges associated with conventional alumina production, including bauxite residue generation, and explores waste\-derived alumina and circular material strategies\. Key challenges include surface heterogeneity, reproducibility, phase stability, mass transfer, scalability, regeneration, and environmental impact\. Future directions encompass hierarchical pore engineering, application\-specific surface design, multifunctional hybrids, sustainable\-by\-design processing, advanced characterization, and data\-driven materials development\.
