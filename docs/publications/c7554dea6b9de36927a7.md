# Optimizing Drilling Fluid Rheology for Efficient Geothermal Drilling: A Machine Learning Approach

**Autores:** Ramesh Arunambigai; M Ramachandran; Sathiyaraj Chinnasamy

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Drilling fluid rheology plays a vital role in maintaining wellbore stability, transporting drilled cuttings, and improving the overall efficiency of geothermal drilling operations\. This study investigates the major drilling parameters influencing operational performance, including flow rate, weight on bit \(WOB\), rotational speed \(RPM\), and rate of penetration \(ROP\)\. Laboratory experiments and field investigations were conducted to examine the complex relationships among these variables and emphasize the importance of continuous, real\-time monitoring of drilling fluid properties\. Traditional offline rheological measurement techniques have limitations in modern intelligent drilling systems because they cannot provide sufficiently rapid measurements for immediate operational decisions\. Therefore, the study examines the limitations of conventional viscometers and evaluates the advantages of in\-line viscometers, particularly their automation capabilities and resistance to clogging\. The investigation also considers the effects of temperature, differential pressure, rock lithology, and lost circulation materials \(LCM\) on filtration performance, demonstrating that uniform ceramic discs may not accurately represent filtration behavior under actual drilling conditions\. Understanding these factors is essential for optimizing drilling fluid formulations and managing fluid\-related challenges in high\-temperature geothermal environments\.
