# Fano‐Engineered Au@Se Core–Shell Colloidal Pigments for Dual‐Mode Structural Color

**Autores:** YongDeok Cho; Yuwon Jeon; Min Kwon; Seungwoo Lee

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Colloidal structural colorants are attractive because they are substrate\-free and solution\-processable, but single\-component plasmonic or dielectric colloids usually exhibit broad visible resonances that limit chroma\. Here we report Au@Se core\-shell colloidal pigments in which a plasmonic Au core is embedded within a visible\-range Mie\-resonant Se shell\. The key signature is not the gradual red shift expected from passive dielectric loading, but an asymmetric hybrid feature with compressed linewidth, consistent with Fano\-like interference between the broad electric\-dipole response of the Se shell and the narrower Au\-core electric\-dipole response\. The hybrid mode yields Q factors up to ≈15 in freely dispersed colloidal pigments, producing narrow transmission windows and spectrally separated reflection bands from the same liquid dispersion\. By independently tuning the Au core diameter and Se shell thickness in a benchtop synthesis, we achieve broad visible color tuning in transmission and reflection and enlarge the accessible international commission on illumination chromaticity \(CIE\) area relative to single\-component Au or Se colloids\. The particles are formulated into ultraviolet \(UV\)\-curable resins and writing inks for 254\-ppi color filters, freeform paper deposition, and polarization\-selective readout\. This work establishes a colloidal design rule in which the shell acts as an active visible resonator rather than a passive refractive\-index coating\.
