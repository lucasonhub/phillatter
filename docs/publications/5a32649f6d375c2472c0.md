# Sergent, Brandon M\.: To Even Ask: How Questions About Experience Presuppose the Outside They Seek to Establish

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

Experiential Empiricism begins from the self\-proving occurrence expressed as ∃E: experience is\. A recurring response asks what causes, grounds, underwrites, generates, explains, or lies beneath experience\. These questions appear to continue inquiry beyond an allegedly premature stopping point\. This paper argues that they instead contain a concealed presupposition\. A relational question of the form “What R experience?” is not neutral with respect to the existence of R\. It grammatically positions experience as one relatum within a wider domain and leaves another position open for something distinct from experience to occupy\. When the existence or coherence of precisely such an exterior position is what is under dispute, the question has imported its answer into its syntax\. The error is clearest in “what causes experience?” but generalizes to grounding, underwriting, generation, explanation, containment, substrate, and meta\-level justification\. This does not prohibit causal inquiry, scientific modeling, or explanation among experiential patterns\. It distinguishes those legitimate internal relations from the unlicensed projection of the same relational machinery onto experience as such\. The result strengthens the distinction between recursion and exteriority developed in ∃E and the End of Meta\. One may continue asking questions indefinitely within experience\. No number of questions creates a standpoint outside the occurrence in which questioning itself takes place\. The problem is therefore not that inquiry has reached a dogmatic stopping point\. It is that a certain class of questions only appears open because its grammar silently manufactures a position that has never been populated\.
