# Intrinsically flexible inorganic piezoelectric subnanowire enables wireless wearable bioelectronics for non\-invasive multimodal human\-machine interfacing

**Autores:** Xin Xu; Xiaoya Wang; Yize Cui; Qingda Liu; Xun Wang

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Piezoelectric electronic skin \(PE\-skin\) offers a significant advantage in detecting minute forces, making it highly promising for human\-computer interaction in health management and medical rehabilitation\. However, PE\-skin often faces challenges in real\-time, wireless transmission of sensing data, which can compromise both data accuracy and user comfort\. Here, we report the synthesis of an inorganic piezoelectric polyoxometalate\-based subnanowire, ZnBiPMo12, which possesses intrinsic flexibility and chirality, a piezoelectric coefficient of 24\.9 pC/N, and notable ferroelectricity\. Leveraging its piezoelectric response, ZnBiPMo12\-based PE\-skin \(1\) demonstrates high elasticity and sensitivity to tiny pressures in the range of 0\.045\-0\.319 N\. Building on this, a wireless, wearable bioelectronic \(WBE\) device \(1A\) is fabricated through multi\-level assembly, enabling non\-invasive, multimodal monitoring of human physiological signals, including subtle muscle contractions, blinking, breathing, and all\-day heart rate\. By integrating a machine learning system, a 1\-based facial\-computer interface \(1B\) can accurately recognize nine emotional expressions and twelve spoken words\. This work not only introduces a typical inorganic piezoelectric material with intrinsic flexibility but also demonstrates scalable manufacturing from subnanomaterials to wireless WBEs, paving the way for non\-invasive, multimodal monitoring of human physiological signals and advanced human\-computer interaction\. Piezoelectric materials enable bioelectronic sensing by converting mechanical stimuli into electrical signals\. Xu et al\. introduce intrinsically flexible polyoxometalate\-based subnanowires and demonstrate their scalable integration into wireless wearable systems for human physiological monitoring\.
