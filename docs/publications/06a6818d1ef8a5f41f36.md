## Descrição do feed

\_Zenodo\_ 1\. 2026This paper argues that the question “Is the human a machine?” is incorrectly framed because it presupposes stable and mutually exclusive categories of human and machine before examining the conditions under which the relevant capacities emerge\. Rather than beginning from human exceptionalism or mechanical reductionism, the paper analyzes the human as a historically and materially organized configuration whose capacities depend on specific biological, technological, social, temporal, and informational conditions\. It distinguishes the operational shapes associated with life, consciousness, agency, memory, embodiment, and inferential organization, and asks under what configurations these capacities ignite, persist, transform, or disappear\. On this basis, the paper rejects both the claim that humans possess an irreducible metaphysical essence and the opposite claim that humans are simply machines in the ordinary technological sense\. The central issue is instead continuity and discontinuity across biological and artificial systems: which operational structures can be reproduced, transformed, or re\-instantiated in nonhuman substrates, and which remain dependent on particular configurations of embodiment, metabolism, temporal integration, environmental coupling, and social organization\. Machine continuity therefore does not imply human identity, nor does biological difference establish exceptionalism\. The paper concludes that human/machine comparison should be reconstructed as a problem concerning the emergence, persistence, and reconfiguration of capacities across heterogeneous systems rather than as a binary ontological classification\. \(direct link \)
