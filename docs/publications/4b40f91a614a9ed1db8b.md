# A CLOUD\-BASED GRID COLLAPSE PREDICTION AND DISASTER RECOVERY MANAGEMENT SYSTEM: A CASE OF THE NIGERIAN POWER SECTOR

**Autores:** Oni Olujimi Olakunle; Aderanti Aderemi Taiwo; Anih Ejiofor Matthew; Kazeem Oladimeji Leshi

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Nigeria’s power grid experiences recurring partial and total collapses that cause widespread electricity outages and socioeconomic disruption\. This study proposes a cloud\-based system that combines grid\-collapse prediction with coordinated disaster\-recovery management\. The predictive component uses a hybrid Convolutional Neural Network–Long Short\-Term Memory model\. The CNN identifies local patterns within multivariate grid data, while the LSTM captures temporal relationships that may indicate developing instability\. Disaster recovery is organised through a three\-horizon “Now, Next, and Later” framework\. “Now” focuses on immediate stabilisation, situational awareness, communication, incident coordination, and restoration of critical loads\. “Next” covers staged restoration, cause assessment, resource deployment, and a return to stable operation\. “Later” addresses predictive maintenance, infrastructure reinforcement, preventive investment, model improvement, and organisational learning\. It is designed to complement rather than replace existing protection and control\-room systems\. The framework was evaluated using simulated Nigerian power grid data\. The CNN\-LSTM achieved 95\.5% accuracy, 96\.0% precision, 95\.0% recall, an F1\-score of 95\.0%, and an ROC\-AUC of 0\.982\. Median inference latency was 184 milliseconds, and the false\-alarm rate was 2\.4%\. The recovery module correctly classified 98\.6% of 500 simulated actions into Now, Next, and Later\. A regional service\-failure exercise restored essential service in 7 minutes 42 seconds with a 46\-second recovery point\. These results show that the prototype provides a scalable, economically viable solution to enhance grid resilience in developing power systems with limited resources and infrastructure constraints\. The framework also shows how artificial intelligence, cloud computing, and structured disaster\-recovery planning can be integrated into a unified decision\-support approach for improving Nigerian grid resilience\.
