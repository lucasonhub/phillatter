# Machine\-Learning\-Assisted Tuning of Exchange–Correlation Functional Parameters for the eXact\-Integral Simplified Tamm\-Dancoff Approximation Method

**Autores:** Sheng\-Rui Wang; Dongyi Xiao; Qiu Fang; Xiang‐Yang Liu; Ganglong Cui

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Here, we improve the accuracy of eXact\-integral simplified Tamm–Dancoff approximation \(XsTDA\) excitation energies through reference\-guided and machine\-learning\-assisted molecule\-specific tuning of exchange–correlation functional parameters in the XsTDA response calculation\. Using DLPNO\-STEOM\-CCSD excitation energies for 55,624 organic molecules, we systematically tune one exchange–correlation functional parameter for four hybrid functionals: the exact\-exchange fraction ax for PBE0 and M06–2X, and the range\-separation parameter ω for CAM\-B3LYP and ωB97X\-D\. Two objectives are considered, targeting either the first 10 singlet excited states or only S1\. With the standard functional parameters, XsTDA yields 10\-state MAEs of 0\.61–0\.97 eV relative to DLPNO\-STEOM\-CCSD, whereas molecule\-specific tuning reduces these errors to 0\.19–0\.28 eV\. The two objectives reveal a clear one\-parameter trade\-off: S1\-specific tuning provides the highest S1 accuracy but compromises higher excited states, whereas 10\-state tuning gives more balanced multistate performance\. To enable practical application to new molecules, five machine\-learning architectures are trained to predict the tuned parameters directly from molecular structure, yielding held\-out\-test MAEs of 0\.21–0\.30 eV for the 10\-state objective and 0\.13–0\.16 eV for the S1\-only objective\. Among the models, a directed message\-passing neural network shows the strongest overall transferability; combined with ωB97X\-D, it yields MAEs of 0\.23 and 0\.24 eV for the 10\-state and S1\-only objectives on the 32\-molecule subset derived from the original XsTDA benchmark\. These results establish ML\-assisted molecule\-specific parameter tuning as a practical route to improve XsTDA excitation energies while retaining its computational efficiency\.
