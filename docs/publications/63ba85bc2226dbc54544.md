# A Lightweight Traffic Sign Recognition Network for Autonomous Driving: Integrating Dual\-Branch Attention and Weather\-Robust Augmentation

**Autores:** Ting Wang; Zhihao Weng

**Data:** 2026-10-18 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Traffic sign recognition is a key perception task in autonomous driving and advanced driver\-assistance systems, where accuracy and real\-time performance directly affect road safety\. In real\-world scenes, fog, rain, and low illumination reduce image contrast and introduce noise, degrading deep models, while in\-vehicle platforms impose strict limits on model size and computation\. We propose CAMSNet, a lightweight traffic sign recognition network\. Its architecture uses a lightweight residual backbone based on depthwise separable convolutions and a Dual\-branch Attention Calibration Module \(DACM\), whose parallel channel\- and position\-attention branches model channel dependencies and spatial location\. Their weights are fused elementwise and applied through residual reweighting to enhance sign features adaptively\. During training, a weather\-robust augmentation strategy based on atmospheric scattering randomly adds synthetic fog, Gaussian noise, and brightness changes\. This augmentation is applied only to training data, leaving inference as a lightweight, one\-way network\. On the German Traffic Sign Recognition Benchmark \(GTSRB\), CAMSNet achieves 98\.19% top\-1 accuracy with 0\.458 million parameters and 35\.88 million multiply\-accumulate operations \(MACs\), exceeding ResNet\-18, which has about 24 times as many parameters, and MobileNetV2, which has about five times as many\. CAMSNet with weather augmentation \(CAMSNet\+W\) reaches 98\.50% accuracy and 96\.34% under severe synthetic fog\. This is 36\.98 percentage points above ResNet\-18 and 36\.12 points above CAMSNet without weather augmentation\. CAMSNet\+W also shows the smallest accuracy drops under severe noise and low brightness\. Ablation and controlled comparisons indicate that attention improves robustness under degraded conditions while maintaining similar clean\-domain accuracy\. DACM is competitive with ECA and CBAM and offers a useful balance between parameter count and robustness\. In this single run, weather augmentation improved robustness without an observed loss in clean\-test accuracy\. Robustness conclusions are based on synthetic corruptions; generalization to real adverse\-weather scenes remains to be established\.
