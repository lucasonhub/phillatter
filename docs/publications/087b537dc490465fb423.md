# The Synthetic Imputation Approach: Using LLMs to Generate Synthetic Texts for Underrepresented Categories in Supervised Classification Tasks

**Autores:** Joan C\. Timoneda

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Annotated text data are central to many empirical applications in political science, yet many annotation tasks involve categories that are sparsely populated\. These low\- N categories pose problems for both encoder\-only and decoder\-only \(generative\) Transformer large language models \(LLMs\), leading to low performance, biased evaluation and measurement error in downstream analyses\. This article introduces the synthetic imputation approach, a targeted method for expanding sparse categories while remaining consistent with the category\-defining content of the human\-coded examples\. The approach uses a generative LLM to produce additional texts for underrepresented categories by conditioning on small, rotating subsets of human\-coded examples\. Rather than paraphrasing or replacing the original data, synthetic imputation adds informative variation only where data are thin\. I evaluate the approach using both encoder\-only and decoder\-only LLM classifiers across multiple political science applications\. The results show that synthetic imputation substantially improves performance and evaluation stability in sparse settings, recovering full\-sample performance once a modest number of true observations is available, with limited and predictable overfitting at very low levels of N \.
