# Open versus Laparoscopic Nephrectomy Before Kidney Transplantation in Patients with Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Disease: A Retrospective Study

**Autores:** Arthur Degani Ottaiano; Vinicius Camargo Achermann; Caio de Oliveira; Gabriel Chahade Sibanto Simões; C Andrade; Adriano Fregonesi

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Objectives: To evaluate the perioperative outcomes of pre\-transplant nephrectomy in patients with autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease, comparing open and laparoscopic approaches in patients undergoing nephrectomy due to large kidney volume to create space for the renal graft\. Methods: This retrospective study evaluated 13 patients who underwent pre\-transplant nephrectomy at a single institution over a 10\-year period\. Six patients underwent open nephrectomy and seven underwent laparoscopic nephrectomy\. Operative time, blood loss, transfusion requirements, length of hospital stay, and perioperative complications were analyzed\. Results: The laparoscopic group showed lower blood loss, lower transfusion requirements, and fewer postoperative complications compared with the open surgery group, although these differences did not reach statistical significance\. One patient undergoing laparoscopic nephrectomy required conversion to open surgery due to bleeding\. Large kidney volume was identified as an important factor contributing to technical difficulty and influencing surgical approach selection\. Conclusion: Laparoscopic nephrectomy is a safe and effective alternative to open surgery in patients undergoing pre\-transplant nephrectomy for large kidney volume\. However, extremely enlarged kidneys remain technically challenging and may increase the risk of conversion to open surgery\. Surgical approach should therefore be individualized according to patient anatomy and surgical team expertise\.
