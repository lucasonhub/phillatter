# Physics\-Data Co\-driven Reliability\-Based Robust Design for Rolling Bearings Under Small\-Sample Uncertainty

**Autores:** 张志斌; Weibin Guo; Juan Wang; Qilong Chu; Xiangdong He

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

This study proposes a physics\-data fusion framework for reliability assessment and reliability\-based robust design of rolling bearings under small\-sample uncertainty\. Hertzian contact theory and finite element analysis \(FEM\) are integrated using inverse\-variance weighted and Bayesian fusion strategies, followed by Gaussian Kriging surrogate modeling\. L\-moments are employed to characterize the unknown radial\-load distribution from limited measurements, while asymmetric Gauss\-Hermite quadrature is used to obtain the first four moments of the limit\-state function\. The Maximum Entropy Method \(MEM\) is then applied for reliability assessment, and a dual\-surrogate\-assisted NSGA\-II framework is established to simultaneously minimize bearing mass and reliability sensitivity under R ≥ 0\.999\. Results show that Bayesian fusion achieves R2 = 0\.9932 and an average relative error of 0\.359% relative to FEM\. Both Kriging models achieve R2 &gt; 0\.97 in leave\-one\-out cross\-validation\. The MEM results agree well with million\-sample Monte Carlo simulation\. The selected balanced compromise designs reduce bearing mass by 5\.00% and 6\.09% for weighted and Bayesian fusion, respectively, while satisfying R ≥ 0\.999 and substantially reducing reliability sensitivity\.
