# Production dynamics, trade behavior and spatial market integration of sesamum in India

**Autores:** Ramalingareddy Vijaya Kumari; SREENIVAS AKULA; Ramakrishna Gundu; VENKATESH PANASA

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background Sesamum \( Sesamum indicum L\.\), one of the oldest cultivated oilseed crops, occupies a significant place in Indian agriculture due to its rich oil content, nutritional benefits, and suitability to a wide range of agro\-climatic environments\. The crop is predominantly grown in states such as West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, and Rajasthan, which contribute substantially to national production\. Over the years, the acreage under sesamum has gradually contracted, reflecting changes in cropping patterns and production conditions; however, advances in yield performance have helped maintain production levels\. An assessment of long\-term production trends and market dynamics is crucial for designing strategies that support sustainable crop diversification, strengthen farmers’ livelihoods, and improve market efficiency through enhanced price transmission and integration across regions\. Methods This study analyzed the long\-term performance of sesamum cultivation in India by examining changes in area, production, and productivity over the period 1980–81 to 2025–26\. Wholesale price data from major sesamum\-producing states were also utilized to investigate price movements and the extent of market integration\. Growth performance and production dynamics were assessed using techniques such as Compound Annual Growth Rate \(CAGR\), decomposition analysis, instability measures, seasonal indices, and correlation analysis\. To examine the nature of price relationships across markets, econometric tools including the Augmented Dickey–Fuller \(ADF\) unit root test, Johansen co\-integration test, Granger causality analysis, Vector Error Correction Model \(VECM\), impulse response analysis, and forecast error variance decomposition were employed\. These methods facilitated an evaluation of both short\-run and long\-run price transmission mechanisms within the Indian sesamum market\. Results The study revealed that the expansion of sesamum production in India has largely resulted from gains in crop productivity, even though the area under cultivation has shown a declining trend over time\. Measures of instability indicated noticeable variations in cultivated area, output, and market prices across the major producing states\. Seasonal analysis demonstrated clear price movements, with lower prices during harvest and peak market arrivals and higher prices during periods of limited supply\. The presence of strong market linkages was confirmed through high correlations, significant long\-run co\-integration, and effective price transmission among markets\. Causality analysis identified Gujarat, Rajasthan, and Telangana as key markets influencing price movements in other regions\. Trade performance showed that India continues to hold an important position in global sesamum exports, while imports have also risen due to increasing domestic demand and periodic supply shortages\. These findings emphasize the need to improve productivity, strengthen market integration, and enhance trade competitiveness to support the long\-term development of the sesamum sector\. Conclusion Sustainable growth of the sesamum sector requires a strategic focus on enhancing productivity through the development and dissemination of improved, climate\-resilient cultivars suited to diverse agro\-ecological conditions\. Strengthening post\-harvest infrastructure, including storage, processing, and transportation facilities, will help minimize losses and improve product quality\. Efficient market information services, wider adoption of digital marketing platforms such as e\-NAM, and better market integration can enhance transparency and ensure remunerative prices for farmers\. In view of the expanding domestic and international demand for sesame and value\-added products, greater attention should be given to export promotion and value\-chain strengthening\. Furthermore, encouraging sesamum cultivation in rainfed and resource\-constrained regions through appropriate policy support and market incentives can foster crop diversification, improve farm profitability, and contribute to sustainable agricultural development\.
