# AI\-Driven Early Warning Systems for Flash Floods Using Satellite Imagery: A GeoAI\-Based Framework for Real\-Time Flash Flood Prediction, Risk Reduction and the Future of Intelligent Disaster Management

**Autores:** Vishwaditya Aazad; Dr\. S\. S\. Baghel

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract: Flash floods remain among the most destructive and least predictable hydrometeorological hazards, characterized by rapid onset, localized intense precipitation, and minimal warning time\. This paper critically examines whether the convergence of satellite Earth Observation \(EO\), Artificial Intelligence \(AI\), and hydrometeorological intelligence can transform flashflood early warning from a predominantly reactive, detectionoriented paradigm into a predictive, anticipatory, and progressively autonomous disaster intelligence system\. Through an interdisciplinary synthesis of remote sensing science, deep learning architectures, spatiotemporal modelling, and disaster risk reduction, we propose the AISatellite Flash Flood Early Warning Intelligence Framework \(AISFFEWI\)—a conceptual architecture that integrates multisource satellite data, groundbased observations, physicsinformed AI, uncertainty quantification, impact forecasting, and community feedback\. The study distinguishes carefully between flood detection and actionable early warning, and emphasizes that lead time, false alarm mitigation, and explainability are as critical as prediction accuracy\. The paper provides a comprehensive analysis of satellite data sources, AI model suitability, spatiotemporal deep learning, and the severe challenges posed by class imbalance, nonstationary climate conditions, and limited crossregion model transferability\. A dedicated Indiacentric perspective examines Himalayan and urban flash floods, while a futureoriented timeline \(2026–2050\) explores the gradual emergence of AIenabled digital twin watersheds and autonomous warning agents, always balancing technological promise against scientific caution, ethical accountability, and the imperative of communitycentred response\. The paper concludes that while satelliteAI systems hold substantial potential to enhance flashflood warning, they cannot replace hydrological understanding, institutional trust, and the human decisionmaking required to convert a probabilistic hazard forecast into lifesaving action\.
