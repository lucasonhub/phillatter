## Descrição do feed

Institutions, technologies, and knowledge systems increasingly remain operational while producing outcomes that are disconnected from the conditions they were created to address\. Metrics improve while underlying outcomes deteriorate\. Procedures reach completion without resolving the problems that initiated them\. Models become more coherent while losing responsiveness to the environments they describe\. These failures are difficult to diagnose because they do not initially appear as breakdowns\. The system continues to function according to its own internal criteria\. This paper proposes Reality Drift as a theory of representational misalignment in scaled symbolic systems\. Reality Drift is the progressive loss of corrective contact between a system’s representations and the real\-world conditions they are meant to track, describe, or govern, even while the system remains operational and internally coherent\. The defining paradox is operational continuity without reliable correction\. The paper introduces the Representation Stack as a reference model for examining how reality is transformed into measurements, metrics, operational representations, optimization targets, and narratives\. It then develops the Drift Principle, which holds that representations can be compressed, optimized, distributed, and reused faster than real\-world conditions can correct them\. Reality Drift becomes self\-reinforcing when corrective feedback remains present but loses the authority to revise the representations, objectives, and procedures governing the system\. The framework extends beyond metric distortion\. It describes how many forms of representation can acquire operational authority, reorganize the conditions they measure, and interpret those altered conditions as confirmation of their own validity\. The paper identifies the mechanisms, boundary conditions, diagnostic implications, and corrective requirements of this process across institutional, technological, and cognitive domains\.
