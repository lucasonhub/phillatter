# Predicting MCI\-to\-Alzheimer’s Conversion: A Systematic Benchmark of Classical, Machine Learning, and Deep Survival Models

**Autores:** Nathalia Arias Santa; Genaro Daza Santacoloma; Walter Serna Serna

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background/Objectives: Predicting time\-to\-conversion from mild cognitive impairment \(MCI\) to Alzheimer’s disease \(AD\) is a critical clinical challenge\. Most survival analyses rely on static baseline variables, ignoring the temporal dynamics of serial neuroimaging\. We benchmark seven survival model families using a novel temporal MRI descriptor pipeline on ADNI\. Methods: A cohort of 661 MCI patients \(250 conversion events, 37\.8%, median follow\-up 42\.0 months\) was constructed from ADNI\. FreeSurfer volumetrics of six brain regions were summarised into three time\-based descriptors each \(atrophy rate, trajectory variability σ, and time\-weighted cumulative mean μ\) under strict anti\-leakage constraints, yielding 18 neuroimaging features which, combined with six clinical variables \(age, sex, education, MMSE, ADAS\-13, and APOE\-ε4\), form a 24\-dimensional covariate vector\. Cox PH, Weibull AFT, Exponential, RSF, GBST, DeepSurv, and DeepHit were evaluated under stratified 5\-fold cross\-validation and assessed on an independent OASIS\-3 cohort \(n=78\) as an exploratory analysis\. Results: All seven models achieved C\-index 0\.853–0\.863 \(Δ=0\.010\), with Cox PH reaching 0\.863±0\.027 using only routine structural MRI\. Time\-dependent AUC ranged from 0\.856 \(12 months\) to 0\.938 \(48 months\)\. Risk stratification by quartile yielded highly separated Kaplan\-Meier curves \(log\-rank p&lt;0\.001\)\. On OASIS\-3 \(n=78, exploratory\), classical models showed preliminary cross\-cohort consistency \(Weibull AFT 0\.760±0\.063, RSF 0\.759±0\.049\), while deep learning degraded substantially \(DeepSurv 0\.551±0\.116\); these results are hypothesis\-generating and should not be interpreted as confirmatory given the small per\-fold sample size \(EPV ≈ 1\)\. Conclusions: The temporal MRI descriptor pipeline achieves a C\-index of 0\.863 using only routine structural MRI, without PET or CSF biomarkers, demonstrating strong discriminative performance on ADNI MCI\-to\-AD conversion\. The narrow performance band \(Δ=0\.010\) suggests that, within this feature\-engineering paradigm, no clear performance separation was observed among the evaluated model architectures; this convergence may reflect feature\-level rather than architecture\-level constraints, although the underlying cause cannot be established from discriminative performance alone\. Classical models showed more stable cross\-cohort behaviour under data\-scarce conditions; prospective validation on larger cohorts with full covariate parity is required before clinical translation can be considered\.
