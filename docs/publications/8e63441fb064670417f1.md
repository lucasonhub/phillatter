# Digital twin\-driven surrogate optimization for maximum power point tracking of photovoltaic systems under partial shading conditions

**Autores:** R\. Gohila; P\. Veena

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Photovoltaic systems under partial shading develop multimodal power\-voltage characteristics that render conventional gradient\-based MPPT algorithms ineffective\. Bio\-inspired metaheuristic approaches overcome local\-maxima entrapment but introduce a structural scan\-phase energy loss: each search iteration physically evaluates a candidate duty cycle on the PV hardware, dissipating real energy at every shading event\. This paper presents the Digital Twin\-Driven Surrogate\-Optimized MPPT \(DTSO\-MPPT\) model, which substantially reduces hardware scan losses by decoupling the global search from the physical PV system and limiting hardware interaction to VTC\-based verification\. The proposed four layer architecture has a physics calibrated Digital Twin with Recursive Least Squares, a surrogate PV surface builder to evaluate the calibrated model, an Orangutan Optimization algorithm to search the surrogate for the global optimum and finally a validation and transfer controller to verify the accuracy conditions\. The proposed model was implemented and numerically evaluated in MATLAB R2024b/Simulink 24\.1 under five partial\-shading conditions\. The simulation results exhibit the proposed model tracking efficiency as 99\.1%, GMPP convergence in 0\.14s, steady\-state power oscillation averaging approximately 0\.53 W and remaining within 0\.42–0\.58 W across the five evaluated cases\. The scan\-loss reduction energy efficiency metric of 99\.4% obtained by the proposed quantifies the reduction in scan\-loss, which represents the principal advantage over conventional hardware\-coupled MPPT methods\.
