# Corporate Fraud and Corporate Accountability in India: A Critical Study of the Companies Act, 2013

**Autores:** Khushi

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Corporate fraud has emerged as one of the most serious challenges to corporate governance and investor confidence in India\. The growth of the corporate sector, the increasing complexity of financial transactions, the separation of ownership and management, and the extensive delegation of managerial powers have created opportunities for corporate misconduct\. Corporate fraud may take several forms, including manipulation of financial statements, misappropriation of company assets, fraudulent related\-party transactions, falsification of accounts, concealment of material information and abuse of managerial position\. Such activities adversely affect shareholders, creditors, employees, investors and the economy as a whole\. The Companies Act, 2013 represents an important legislative attempt to strengthen corporate accountability and prevent fraudulent corporate practices in India\. Section 447 specifically provides for punishment for fraud, while Sections 211 and 212 establish the statutory framework for the Serious Fraud Investigation Office \(SFIO\) and the investigation of serious corporate fraud\. The Act further strengthens accountability through provisions relating to directors’ duties, auditor responsibilities, internal controls, audit committees, vigil mechanisms, related\-party transactions, investigation and class\-action remedies\. The statutory framework therefore adopts both preventive and punitive approaches towards corporate misconduct\. This paper critically analyses the concept of corporate fraud and the mechanisms of corporate accountability under the Companies Act, 2013\. It examines the role of directors, auditors, independent directors, audit committees, the SFIO and other regulatory mechanisms in preventing and investigating corporate fraud\. The study finds that the Companies Act, 2013 has significantly strengthened India’s corporate fraud regime, but challenges remain in relation to enforcement delays, complex corporate structures, auditor independence, inter\-agency coordination, whistle\-blower protection and technological forms of fraud\. The paper recommends stronger preventive governance, improved forensic investigation, enhanced accountability of corporate gatekeepers and more effective regulatory coordination\.
