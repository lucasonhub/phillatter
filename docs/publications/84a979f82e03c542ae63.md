# Johnson, Jamison: Faith \- The Fractalization of the Same Image

**Autores:** 

**Data de descoberta:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** unknown

## Descrição do feed

In this essay I set out my personal philosophy of religious symbolic interpretation within my Coherent Existence framework, and I argue that the world’s faiths are fractalizations of a single image: the same design refracted through history into many self\-similar renderings, each fractured in transmission and each still catching the same light\. The argument runs through three convergences\. I read the Golden Rule, traced across nine traditions, as the rule that follows once the other is recognized as a mirror of the self\. I read the stories of a people scattered for overreach \(Babel, Nimrod’s Babylon, the giants of Cholula, the asuras’ loss of speech, and the contested Sumerian passage whose preferred reading points to the gathering of tongues rather than their scattering\) with time removed, as the long dispersal in which one world became many views of itself, and I place the pride in Babel in rulers’ wish for one speech\. Only two of these accounts join the reach to a confusion of tongues, so I treat the shape of ego\-driven reach and dispersal as the parallel that carries weight\. And I read the kingdom within, with its disputed Greek, as the claim that heaven and hell are perceptions rather than places, unlocked by keys placed both within and in the neighbor\. Jung’s persona, ego, and Self supply the vocabulary for how the design is translated and mistranslated between people; play, the fading affect bias, the pyramid builders’ villages, the Hubble mirror, and Ibn Khaldūn’s three generations supply the illustrations\. I close on the conversation around gender as a present\-day instance of scattered tongues, and on kindness, toward which the evidence on acceptance points\. I read scriptures, histories, and sciences symbolically, for their meaning within the design, and where scholarship disputes a source, I say so openly\. \(direct link \)
