# Exploring trust in artificial intelligence in a problem\-based computer programming course

**Autores:** Christian Basil Omeh; Ndudi O\. Ezeamuzie; Chijioke Jonathan Olelewe

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Generative artificial intelligence is increasingly used in programming education, but evidence concerning its relationship with achievement and learner trust remains limited in Nigerian higher education\. This mixed\-methods, quasi\-experimental study examined whether integrating Google Gemini into problem\-based learning \(PBL\) was associated with undergraduate students’ programming achievement and trust in AI\. Sixty\-two students from intact classes participated in a pre\-test–post\-test design: 29 received Gemini\-enhanced PBL, and 33 received PBL without AI\. Programming achievement and multidimensional trust were assessed before and after the intervention, while eight experimental\-group students provided explanatory interview accounts\. Quantitative data were analysed using ANOVA and ANCOVA, supplemented by HC3 robust estimation and bias\-corrected bootstrapping; interview responses were coded thematically\. The experimental group recorded higher post\-test achievement than the control group\. The robust adjusted mean difference was 16\.42 points, 95% CI \[14\.16, 18\.67\]\. Post\-test trust scores were also higher in the Gemini\-enhanced condition, while gender and the instructional condition × gender interaction were not statistically significant\. Participants described Gemini as useful for problem clarification, algorithm development, debugging, collaboration, self\-directed learning and immediate feedback; trust was associated with transparent prompts, responsible data use and human accountability\. The findings suggest that structured Gemini support may complement PBL\.
