# The Schutte Self\-Report Emotional Intelligence Test in allied health sciences students: reliability, essential unidimensionality, and educational and behavioral correlates: a cross\-sectional study

**Autores:** Hamad Alhamad; Naser Alotaibi; Nasser N\. Hasan; Feddah M\. Ahmad; Ahmad Alenezi; Musaed Z\. Alnaser

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Emotional intelligence \(EI\) is an individual\-difference characteristic relevant to how health\-professions students learn, cope, and engage with support, making its valid measurement a prerequisite for research on student development in this field\. This study examined the psychometric performance and educational and behavioral correlates of the Schutte Self\-Report Emotional Intelligence Test \(SSEIT\) in allied health sciences undergraduates at Kuwait University\. Methods In this cross\-sectional study, the 33\-item SSEIT was administered in English, the language of instruction and assessment at the Faculty, through a confidential online survey\. Of 561 responses, 559 remained after duplicate removal and 489 after excluding 70 invariant \(straight\-line\) responders, following the screening pipeline of a companion study on the same administration\. Reliability, ordinal confirmatory factor analysis \(WLSMV\) with bifactor indices, polychoric parallel analysis, split\-half validation, and exploratory correlates with Holm correction were examined\. Results The total score was highly reliable \( α = \.96, 95% CI \[\.95, \.96\]; ordinal ω = \.97\)\. Under the primary ordinal\-appropriate estimator \(WLSMV\), all candidate structures fit acceptably \(one\-factor CFI = \.952; four\-factor plus reverse\-item method factor CFI = \.967, RMSEA = \.065\), and bifactor indices supported essential unidimensionality of the total score \(ECV = \.87; ω H = \.94; 29 of 33 items loading ≥ \.40 on the general factor\), a structure reproduced in split\-half validation\. Polychoric parallel analysis retained two factors, with a dominant first eigenvalue \(16\.96 vs\. 1\.93\)\. The three reverse\-keyed items remained misaligned after screening, consistent with a residual reverse\-wording method effect and possible second\-language response processes\. Among exploratory correlates, only prior EI\-workshop attendance \(d = 0\.56\) and marital status \(d = 0\.79, very small group\) survived Holm correction; GPA differences were nominal only, and a regression model explained modest variance \(adjusted R² = \.07\), robust to heteroscedasticity\-consistent estimation\. Discussion The SSEIT total score shows promise as a reliable, essentially unidimensional research measure of self\-reported EI in this population; facet scores, individual\-level screening, and wellbeing monitoring are not supported by the present evidence\.
