# Reframing US politics through African lenses: a cross\-continental dialogue on electoral systems and political thought

**Autores:** Patrick Maluki; Rabecca Kavithe Ndeto

**Data:** 2026-10-09 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Comparative democratic theory has long assumed that intellectual traffic flows from Western liberal models outward to other traditions\. This article reverses the direction\. It is, first and foremost, a work of comparative political theory: drawing on Ubuntu political philosophy, Pan\-African constitutional scholarship, and the comparative electoral\-systems literature, it examines four well\-documented structural failures of the American electoral order—the Electoral College, partisan gerrymandering, post\-Citizens United campaign finance, and duopolistic party competition—through lenses that African governance experience and African political thought uniquely sharpen\. A prior caveat is essential: this article does not argue that African democracy outperforms its American counterpart in aggregate\. The sub\-Saharan African average on the Perceptions of Electoral Integrity index sits at 44 out of 100, considerably below the United States’ 64\. The argument is more precise than that: specific African intellectual traditions and specific African institutional designs illuminate specific American pathologies in ways that the dominant US\-focused literature has not adequately registered\. The 2024 electoral cycles in Senegal, South Africa, Ghana, and the United States are used to illustrate the article’s core claims rather than to test them in any statistical or causal sense\. A dedicated section confronts the transferability problem directly; American democratic strengths are acknowledged at the outset rather than deferred to a closing caveat; and the article sets out what cross\-continental institutional learning, properly qualified, can and cannot deliver\.
