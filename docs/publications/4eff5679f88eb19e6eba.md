# How to Build a Benchmark That Actually Measures Taste

**Autores:** Xiaofan Wu; Xi Zeng; Miaoxia Chen; Peishan Chen; Shuyan Li; Chen Wang; Xiaohan Wang; Wanling Xu; Jiyun Yao; Hanyong Zhong; Jiahao Zhu

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** preprint

## Resumo

Expert\-aligned &quot;taste&quot; benchmarks should reward craft judgment, not the ability to game the test\. We introduce TasteBench, a pairwise good\-vs\-bad benchmark across visual, brand, book, and tech items under a frozen rubric with expert\-only gold \(panel n&gt;=3; percent agreement &gt;=0\.60 primary gate; weak engagement labels train/analysis\-only\), together with a control protocol that separates craft judgment from test\-gaming\. Applying the protocol to a Round\-1 pilot \(primary\_human\_expert; three biological human experts; n=250 pairs\) surfaces and quantifies three validity threats: two construction artifacts that inflate the trivial\-baseline layer, and a difficulty ceiling that inflates every score\. \(1\) Position: the preferred item is slot A with probability 0\.956, so an always\_item\_a policy scores 0\.956 \(fair chance 0\.5\)\. \(2\) Lexical leakage: a filename token cue scores 0\.912\. \(3\) Difficulty ceiling: craft domains show near\-ceiling human agreement \(book/brand/tech 1\.0\), and even under A/B randomization \(eval\_assignment\_seed7; gold\_side\_A \~0\.47\) all five VLM cards reach 1\.0 on craft, with only visual separating \(\~0\.84\-0\.91; a post\-hoc hard subset, n=107, leaves craft at 1\.0\)\. The protocol \(expert\-only gold, a weak\-label firewall, side randomization at construction, filename scrubbing, and hard filters\) makes each artifact measurable and, for the two construction artifacts, correctable; we release a reference eval harness and a side\-randomization tool, and show the tool removes the position skew \(0\.916 to 0\.495/0\.505\)\. The result is a calibrated view of what the benchmark does and does not yet measure: position and leakage are fixable, whereas the craft\-domain ceiling is a pair\-difficulty limit that Round\-2 must address\. We do not evaluate engagement, era/circle conditioning, or calibration here, and we claim no general taste SOTA\.
