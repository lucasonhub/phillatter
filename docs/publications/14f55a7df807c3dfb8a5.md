# Associations and Additive Interaction of Childhood Trauma and Psychological Resilience With Depression Among Vocational College Students

**Autores:** Jiacun Tian; Xiaoping Chen; Danwen Huang; Xiaoyan Qiu

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: To observe the effects of childhood trauma and psychological resilience on depression in vocational college students, as well as their additive and interactive effects\. Methods: From November 2023 to November 2024, 992 college students from a higher vocational and technical college in Shaoxing were selected using cluster sampling\. An online survey was conducted using the Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale \(CES\-D\), the 10\-item Connor\-Davidson Resilience Scale \(CD\-RISC\-10\), and the Childhood Trauma Questionnaire\-Short Form \(CTQ\-SF\)\. According to the presence or absence of depressive symptoms, the students were divided into a depressive symptom group and a non\-depressive group\. CTQ\-SF scores, CD RISC\-10 scores, and general characteristics of were compared between the two groups to analyse factors associated with depressive symptoms among higher vocational college students\. The additive interaction between CTQ\-SF and CD\-RISC\-10 scores in relation to depressive symptoms was also analysed\. Results: Among the 992 vocational college students, the incidence of depressive symptoms was 19\.86% \(197/992\)\. The CTQ\-SF score was higher in the depressive symptom group than in the non\-depressive group \(p &lt; 0\.05\), and the CD\-RISC\-10 score was lower in the depressive symptom group than in the non\-depressive group \(p&lt; 0\.05\)\. The depressive symptom group was older than the non\-depressive symptom group and had higher proportions of students with monthly living expenses &lt;1000 yuan, average or poor relationships with classmates, and divorced or widowed parents \(p &lt; 0\.05\)\. Multivariable logistic regression analysis showed that age \(odds ratio \(OR\) = 1\.179, 95% confidence interval \(CI\): 1\.072–1\.295\), average or poor relationships with classmates \(OR =3\.197, 95% CI: 2\.038–5\.015\), and having divorced or widowed parents \(OR = 3\.208, 95% CI: 1\.788–5\.756\) were risk factors for depressive symptoms among vocational college students \(p &lt; 0\.05\)\. Monthly living expenses \(OR = 0\.264, 95% CI: 0\.168–0\.416\), sexual orientation \(OR = 0\.740, 95% CI: 0\.551–0\.993\), and CD\-RISC\-10 score \(OR = 0\.306, 95% CI: 0\.187–0\.502\) were protective factors for depression symptoms among vocational college students \(p &lt; 0\.05\)\. The additive interaction analysis yielded relative excess risk due to interaction \(RERI\) =–0\.001, attributable proportion due to interaction \(AP\) =–0\.002, and synergy index \(S\) = 1\.003, indicating no additive interaction between CTQ\-SF and CD\-RISC\-10 scores in relation to depressive symptoms among vocational college students\. Conclusions: CTQ\-SF and CD\-RISC\-10 scores showed no additive interaction in relation to depressive symptoms among vocational college students\.
