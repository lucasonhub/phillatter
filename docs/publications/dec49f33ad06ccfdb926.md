# Planktonic Foraminifera Systematics:Current Status, Open Questions and Future Directions

**Autores:** Kirsty Marie Edgar; Tracy L\. Aze

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Planktonic foraminifera are one of the most important marine microfossil groups for biostratigraphy, palaeoceanography, and macroevolutionary studies because of their global distribution, environmental sensitivity, and exceptionally complete fossil record\. However, they have a complex evolutionary history with the planktonic mode of life originating from benthic ancestors’ multiple times since their first occurrence in the Early Jurassic\. They are a morphologically and ecologically diverse group whose systematics traditionally rely on test morphology, wall ultrastructure, and stratigraphic occurrence\. Technological advances in past decades in scanning electron microscopy, geometric morphometrics, micro\-CT imaging, and molecular genetics, have substantially helped to refine classifications and phylogenetic hypotheses, and enabled work towards a ‘total\-evidence’ framework for extant taxa\. Here, we review the historical development and current state of planktonic foraminiferal systematics and identify outstanding questions for future research\. These include uncertainties around the origin and early evolution of planktonic foraminifera, mechanisms for reconciling fossil and extant species concepts, under sampling of small\-size fractions that likely contain substantial undocumented diversity, and persistent temporal and spatial gaps in the fossil record that impact our understanding of their evolutionary history\. Answering these questions requires a combination of new and traditional techniques including new targeted sampling of under sampled intervals and regions, reanalysis of historical collections, integration of fossil and molecular datasets \(wherever possible\), expansion of community database approaches, and application of machine\-learning and trait\-based approaches\. Together, these developments offer a means of achieving a more stable and complete systematic framework for this globally significant clade\.
