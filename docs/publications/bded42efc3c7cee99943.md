# Empathy and emotional competence in deaf and hard\-of\-hearing and hearing preschoolers

**Autores:** Carolien J\. Rieffe; Brenda M\. S\. da Silva; Yung‐Ting Tsou; Claudia Alessandra Libbi; Johan H\. M\. Frijns; Guida Veiga

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Empathy is an important skill for social bonding, and thus can greatly affect children&\#x27;s daily social lives\. Due to various obstructions when entering a predominantly hearing environment, deaf and hard\-of\-hearing \(DHH\) children might face restricted opportunities for developing emotional competence, which in turn may affect their empathic skills\. The current study examined the extent to which the three main empathy layers that can be observed in young children \(i\.e\., emotion contagion, attention to others&\#x27; emotions, and prosocial actions\) were related to emotional competence \(emotion recognition, regulation and vocabulary\) in DHH and hearing preschoolers attending mainstream education \(kindergarten\) in Portugal\. A total of 181 preschoolers \(32 DHH and 149 hearing\) participated\. Caregivers rated children&\#x27;s empathy, and three indices for emotional competence \(emotion recognition, emotion regulation, emotion vocabulary\)\. Additionally, hearing and language related factors were collected in the DHH group and included as control variables\. One\-tailed independent samples t \-tests showed similar levels of empathy, emotion recognition and emotion regulation for both groups, but DHH children scored lower on emotion vocabulary than their hearing peers\. Additionally, hierarchical regression analyses showed that better skills in emotional competence \(all three indices\) were associated with more empathic behaviors \(prosocial actions\) toward a person in distress in both groups\. Outcomes are discussed in the context of social and emotional learning and the importance of equity in mainstream schools, addressing the special needs of DHH children or other children from marginalized groups\.
