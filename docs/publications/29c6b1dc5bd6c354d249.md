# A recall\-optimized LSTM–XGBoost ensemble model for river flood forecasting using multivariate time\-series data—case study in Sri Lanka

**Autores:** T\. H\. S\. Sankalpa; G\. H\. C\. Sandaruwan; A\. M\. Aslam Saja; N\. T\. M\. Sajith; M\. F\. M\. Ishqy

**Data:** 2026-10-16 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Riverine \(fluvial\) flooding remains a significant climate\-related disaster in many countries\. Extreme rainfall events caused by climate change cause significant damage and disruption to communities living near riverbanks or neighboring land areas, particularly when risk\-sensitive development is ignored\. This necessitates an effective mechanism to predict river flooding events and provide timely early warnings to the most vulnerable populations in high\-risk flood\-prone areas\. The dynamics of flood risks require a localized, automated, and scalable solution that can be centrally monitored\. This study presents a river flood forecasting system integrating artificial intelligence \(AI\) and Internet of Things \(IoT\)\. This system was deployed in one of the flood\-prone river basins in Sri Lanka to assess the operational feasibility of real\-time hydrological monitoring and flood\-risk classification\. The proposed system employed two machine learning models, Long Short\-Term Memory \(LSTM\) and Extreme Gradient Boosting \(XGBoost\), for flood\-risk prediction and IoT sensors \(rainfall and ultrasonic sensors\) for real\-time hydrological data collection\. The machine learning models were developed using rainfall, discharge rate, and river water level data to predict flood\-risk states\. A rule\-based ensemble strategy was implemented by combining the outputs of LSTM and XGBoost to prioritize positive\-class detection and reduce missed positive\-labeled observations\. Model performance was evaluated primarily using recall for the positive flood cases, while the overall accuracy was reported as the complementary metric alongside positive\-class recall, providing an overall assessment of classification performance\. Among the evaluated input window configurations using the same chronological holdout, the rule\-based ensemble model with a 1\-day input window showed the most favorable observed balance of performance metrics, achieving 100% positive\-class recall, with 98\.73% accuracy, 50\.00% positive\-class precision, and 66\.67% positive\-class F1\-score\. The 100\.00% recall was based on correctly classifying all four positive\-labeled observations in the held\-out test set \(4/4; 95% exact binomial CI: 39\.8%−100\.0%\) and these observations occurred consecutively from November 25 to 28, 2024, and represent one continuous positive\-labeled flood episode\. The proposed safety\-oriented AI\-IoT framework demonstrates the operational feasibility of integrating real\-time sensing with AI\-based flood forecasting; however, longer\-term field deployment, including actual flood events, is required to evaluate flood\-event prediction performance under real\-world flood conditions\.
