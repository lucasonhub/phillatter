# Use of augmented reality applications on smartphones as a tool for anatomy learning: perceptions of medical college students in Pakistan

**Autores:** Shamaila Hassnain; Muhammad Usama Bin Ali; Aleeha Chaudhry; Hanzala Shahid; Ahmad Asfand Yar; Zarnab Shaukat; Amna Choudary; Fatima Ali; Amna Hassnain

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background The teaching of anatomy is often perceived as one of the most challenging components of medical education\. Augmented reality \(AR\) has emerged as a transformative educational tool\. These applications superimpose digital information onto the physical world, offering students interactive, immersive experiences for understanding complex anatomical structures\. This research investigates medical students’ perceptions and self\-reported use of AR applications on smartphones to assess medical students’ familiarity with AR technology, engagement levels, usability challenges, and preferences between AR and traditional learning methods\. Methods A descriptive cross\-sectional study and a structured questionnaire were employed to collect data from medical students at Fatima Memorial College of Medicine &amp; Dentistry \(FMHCM&amp;D\), Lahore, Pakistan\. Data analysis was performed using SPSS 27\.00, employing descriptive statistics and chi\-square tests\. Kruskal–Wallis \(with Shapiro–Wilk and Dunn’s post hoc , Bonferroni\) was used to examine associations between the variables\. Results This study surveyed 496 medical students from FMHCM&amp;D, Lahore, Pakistan\. The mean age of participants was 21\.33 ± 1\.66 years\. 32% were males and 68% were females\. One hundred and forty\-two of 638 consenting students \(22\.3%\) had no prior AR app use and were removed before data entry\. Whereas 77\.7% \(496/638\) reported prior use of AR apps\. Only 70% of students agreed about the frequent use of AR apps\. 31% did not experience general usability difficulties \(e\.g\., navigation problems\), but 46\.8% reported performance issues \(e\.g\., app crashes, slow running, or battery drain\) while using AR applications\. These apps were perceived by 76\.4% of students as helpful for securing good marks in anatomy, and 70% agreed that AR apps reduced their reliance on traditional learning methods\. Furthermore, 81\.7% of students agreed that AR enhanced their enjoyment of learning anatomy\. Conclusion The findings of this study suggest that medical students perceive AR as a valuable tool that enhances their engagement and enjoyment, highlighting the need for controlled, outcome\-based research to establish AR’s actual impact on teaching methodology\.
