# Designing Genotype\-Based Enrollment Criteria Without a Companion Diagnostic: An FDA Regulatory Case Study in Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy Cell Therapy

**Autores:** Ruoqian Shi; Peivand Pirouzi

**Data:** 2026-10-15 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The question of whether a drug candidate requires a companion diagnostic is usually treated as a binary one: either the mechanism depends on a specific genetic alteration, requiring confirmatory testing before use, or it does not, in which case genotype is treated as clinically irrelevant to patient selection\. This article argues, using a representative allogeneic cardiosphere\-derived cell therapy for Duchenne muscular dystrophy \(DMD\) as a case study, that this binary framing obscures a subtler and more common practice: genotype\-based cohort capping and exclusion criteria used for trial enrichment, distinguishing slower\- from faster\-progressing patient subgroups without constituting, or requiring, a formal companion diagnostic\. The article develops this distinction in detail, situates it within the product&\#x27;s broader evidentiary dispute, proposes a real\-world evidence strategy that accounts explicitly for the enrichment logic used at the trial stage, and closes with recommendations for regulatory affairs professionals designing enrollment criteria for mutation\-agnostic therapies in genetically heterogeneous rare\-disease populations\.Keywords: patient selection; genotype enrichment; companion diagnostic; Duchenne muscular dystrophy; allogeneic cell therapy; regulatory artificial intelligence
