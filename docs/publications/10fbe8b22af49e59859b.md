# Forensics of Autonomous Multi\-Agent Systems: A Methodological and Algorithmic Framework for Detection, Causal Traceability, and Evidentiary Qualification of AI\-Generated Deviant Behaviours

**Autores:** Chawki Fares

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The emergence of autonomous agents based on large language models \(LLMs\) such as ReAct, LangGraph, AutoGen, or CrewAI — radically transforms the nature of acts produced by artificial intelligence systems\. These agents no longer merely generate text: they plan, call system tools, manipulate databases, and execute arbitrary code\. This operational autonomy, combined with their demonstrated vulnerability to indirect prompt injection attacks, creates a major evidentiary blind spot: no forensic framework currently exists to legally characterise an illicit act generated by AI before a criminal court\. This article proposes a methodological and algorithmic framework for the forensics of autonomous multi\-agent systems, structured around three fundamental locks: non\-invasive acquisition and cryptographic sealing of the agent&\#x27;s latent state, algorithmic quantification of behavioural deviation, and causal traceability compliant with Article 427 of the French Code of Criminal Procedure and Articles 12 &amp; 13 of the EU AI Act\. The project deploys an experimental three\-layer software architecture — immutable forensic sensor, behavioural deviation index engine, causal reconstruction and judicial report — and is part of a 36\-month doctoral work programme\. In my daily practice as a judicial expert in digital forensics, I use classical DFIR tools \(EnCase, FTK, Autopsy, Volatility, X\-Ways, Axiom, Magnet, IPED\) under ISO/IEC 27037\. However, when facing autonomous multi\-agent systems \(ReAct, LangGraph, MCP protocols\), these tools become ineffective: they record material alteration but remain blind to the internal probabilistic deliberation of the model\. On the legal side, since AI has no legal personality \(Article 121\-1 of the French Penal Code\), judges are powerless before the black box to characterise fault \(Article 121\-3 of the Penal Code, Article 1242 of the Civil Code\), whether the cause is a hostile injection \(OWASP LLM01\) or spontaneous drift \(goal drift\)\. The proposed framework aims to fill this gap by addressing three scientific locks\. The first consists in capturing decision\-making telemetry \(inference contexts, KV\-Cache, tool calls\) without prohibitive latency, with integrity guaranteed by Merkle trees and certified timestamping \(RFC 3161\)\. The second aims to formulate a composite deviation index combining semantic vector divergence \(embedding space\) and structural violation analysis on action graphs\. The third addresses the modelling of the trajectory as causal graphs \(Pearl&\#x27;s models\) in order to isolate the corrupted input and produce a report admissible in court\.
