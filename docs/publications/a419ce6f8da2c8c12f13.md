# Morphological Typology and Qualitative Anatomical Variants of the Adult Human Hyoid Bone: Forensic and Clinical Implications

**Autores:** Priti Singh; Arun Kumar; Sachin Sachin; Luv Sharma; Aarti Aarti

**Data:** 2026-10-17 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Background: The human hyoid bone demonstrates substantial macroscopic structural polymorphism\. Although classically described as a uniform U\-shaped structure, its morphological configurations vary considerably across populations\. Recognizing these qualitative anatomical variants is essential for forensic identification, medicolegal trauma interpretation, and surgical planning in head and neck procedures\. Aims &amp; Objectives: To evaluate the distribution and frequency of morphological shape typologies of the adult human hyoid bone, determine their correlation with biological sex and regional demography, and analyze qualitative anatomical variations\. Material &amp; Methods: A prospective post\-mortem anatomical investigation was carried out on 100 excised, cleaned, and dried adult human hyoid bones \(60 males, 40 females; age range: 40–83 years\)\. Specimens were systematically classified into distinct qualitative morphological variants: V\-shaped, U\-shaped, D\-shaped, Boat/B\-shaped, and Horseshoe \(HK\)\-shaped\. Associations between morphological typologies, biological sex, and residential background were evaluated using Pearson’s Chi\-square tests \(𝜒2\) and Fisher’s exact tests \(𝑝 &lt; 0\.05\)\. Results: V\-shaped \(42\.0%\) and U\-shaped \(42\.0%\) geometries predominated, accounting for 84\.0% of the entire sample, followed by D\-shaped \(7\.0%\), Boat/B\-shaped \(4\.0%\), Horseshoe \(HK\)\-shaped \(4\.0%\), and a rare atypical Htype variant \(1\.0%\)\. A highly significant association was identified between hyoid shape typology and biological sex \(𝜒2 = 38\.641,𝑝 &lt; 0\.001\)\. V\-shaped morphology predominated heavily in males \(66\.7% vs\. 5\.0% in females\), whereas U\-shaped morphology predominated in females \(67\.5% vs\. 25\.0% in males\)\. Rural and urban residential backgrounds showed no statistically significant divergence in shape distribution \(𝜒2 = 5\.24,𝑝 = 0\.387\)\. Conclusion: Qualitative macroscopic typology of the hyoid bone exhibits marked sexual dimorphism\. Morphological classification provides a rapid, accessible adjunct for forensic sex screening and offers critical anatomical awareness for head and neck surgeons\.
