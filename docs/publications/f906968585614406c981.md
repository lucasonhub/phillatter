# Real\-World Performance of an FDA\-Approved Artificial Intelligence Tool for Mammography in Patients with Prior Breast Surgery or Indwelling Biopsy Clips

**Autores:** Emily B\. Ambinder; Colin Paulbeck; Shuchi Zinzuwadia; Lisa Mullen; Kelly S\. Myers; Eniola Oluyemi; Babita Panigrahi

**Data:** 2026-10-10 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Abstract Background AI tools are increasingly used in clinical screening mammography, but their performance in patients with prior breast surgery or indwelling biopsy clips, who represent a substantial proportion of screening populations, has not been well characterized\. Purpose To evaluate the real\-world performance of an FDA\-approved AI mammography tool in patients with prior breast surgery or biopsy clips\. Methods and Materials We retrospectively reviewed all screening mammograms performed at our institution between 8/1/2024 and 2/28/2025 after implementation of a commercially available AI tool \(Transpara 1\.7\.4\-A, ScreenPoint Medical\), merging clinical outcomes with the AI risk score \(low, intermediate, or elevated\)\. Patients were stratified by procedural history into three groups: prior breast surgery, biopsy clip, and reference \(i\.e\. neither intervention\)\. Sensitivity, positive predictive value \(PPV\) and false\-positive rate \(FPR\) at the Elevated and Intermediate \+Elevated thresholds were compared using Fisher&\#x27;s exact tests presented as percentage \[95% confidence interval\]\. Results 24,427 screening mammograms were included \(mean age 61 ± 13 years SD\): 19,287 Reference, 2,647 Surgery, and 2,493 Biopsy clip\. AI sensitivity at the Intermediate \+ Elevated threshold was preserved \(Reference 92\.1% \[86\.9%, 95\.3%\], Surgery 90\.5% \[71\.1%, 97\.3%\], Biopsy clip 90\.0% \[69\.9%, 97\.2%\]; both p &gt; 0\.6\)\. The AI\-PPV of an Elevated exam was correspondingly reduced in altered\-breast patients \(Reference 13\.6% \[11\.1%, 16\.6%\], Surgery 5\.5% \[2\.9%, 10\.0%\], Biopsy clip 4\.9% \[2\.5%, 9\.4%\]; both p &lt; 0\.005 vs Reference\)\. The AI FPR was higher in both subgroups at the Intermediate \+ Elevated threshold \(Reference 35\.6% \[34\.9%, 36\.3%\] vs Surgery 47\.9% \[46\.0%, 49\.9%\] and Biopsy clip 47\.9% \[45\.9%, 49\.8%\]; both p &lt; 0\.001\) and more than doubled at the Elevated threshold \(Reference 2\.7% \[2\.5%, 2\.9%\] vs Surgery 5\.9% \[5\.1%, 6\.9%\] and Biopsy clip 6\.2% \[5\.3%, 7\.2%\]; both p &lt; 0\.001\)\. Conclusion AI examination\-level sensitivity for breast cancer was preserved in patients with prior breast surgery or indwelling biopsy clips\. However, an Elevated AI risk score was less likely to reflect a cancer in these patients than in the Reference group, with a lower AI\-PPV and higher AI\-FPR\.
