# A decision framework for selecting IMERG bias\-correction methods in complex tropical watersheds

**Autores:** Ery Suhartanto; Ussy Andawayanti; Rahmah Dara Lufira; Rizki Tri Utami

**Data:** 2026-10-12 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Accurate satellite precipitation estimates are critical for hydrological modelling and climate\-risk management in the tropics, where sparse gauges and complex storm regimes increase uncertainty\. This PRISMA\-guided systematic review synthesizes advances in bias correction for GPM\-IMERG across monsoonal, equatorial, orographic, and coastal basins\. Evidence is organized into bias controls, classical and distribution\-based corrections, machine\-learning/hybrid methods, and hydrological impacts\. Reported IMERG errors vary widely, exceeding 70% in high\-relief watersheds and approaching 200% in strongly convective regions\. Classical scaling reduces mean bias, whereas distribution\-based methods better reproduce variability and extremes\. ML models \(ANN, LSTM/Bi\-LSTM\) improve correlation, RMSE, and event detection, particularly at hourly scales\. Hydrological evaluations show improvements in streamflow, intensity\-duration\-frequency curves, and drought indices, with the Nash\-Sutcliffe efficiency increasing to approximately 0\.89 and peak\-flow estimates improving\. Despite improvements in IMERG V07, local correction remains essential; the choice of method should align with the regime, basin attributes, product version, and application objectives\.
