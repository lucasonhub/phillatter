# BALANCING GROWTH AND NATURE: UNDERSTANDING SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT IN KARACHI’S COASTAL AREAS: A CASE STUDY OF DHA PHASE VII–VIII AND THE MALIR RIVER DELTA

**Autores:** Muhammad Abrar Khokhar; Maaz Bin Qasim

**Data:** 2026-10-13 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Coastal areas give cities great economic, social, and environmental value, yet rapid building on them often damages the very nature that makes them valuable\. This paper studies sustainable development along the coast of Karachi, Pakistan, using the case of Defence Housing Authority \(DHA\) Phase VII–VIII and the neighbouring Malir River Delta\. The study follows the three\-pillar idea of sustainability environment, society, and economy and asks whether coastal growth in Karachi keeps these three in balance\. It uses a mixed approach: a review of recent research, comparison of land\-use maps from 1984 and 2017, field visits, and interviews with a marine scientist, estate agents, restaurant visitors, and fishermen from Ibrahim Haidri village\. The findings show a clear imbalance\. Economic growth from ports, luxury housing, and restaurants is strong, but land reclamation has cleared mangroves and marshlands, reduced fish stocks, cut public access to the shore, and raised the risk from storms and sea\-level rise\. Weak enforcement of environmental laws makes the problem worse\. The paper argues that development cannot and should not be stopped, but it must be made sustainable through balanced planning, mangrove restoration, protection of fishing livelihoods, marine spatial planning, and honest governance\. The Hong Kong Chek Lap Kok airport case shows that damaged coastal habitats can be restored when mitigation is taken seriously\.
