# Hearing shapes: the Bouba–Kiki effect in adult domestic dogs

**Autores:** Eleonora Biffi; G Pedretti; Simona Normando; Teddy Lazebnik; Maria Loconsole; Paola Valsecchi

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

The &\#x27;Bouba\-Kiki effect&\#x27; is an example of spontaneous association between auditory and visual stimuli, described in humans as a case of sound symbolism, a predisposition linked to language\. Recent evidence showed that 3\-day\-old chicks also exhibit a spontaneous association between round and spiky shapes and the sounds &\#x27;Bouba&\#x27; and &\#x27;Kiki,&\#x27; respectively, thus suggesting that the effect may rather be an instance of a more widespread cross\-modal correspondence\. We investigated whether the Bouba\-Kiki effect is present in adult domestic dogs \(Canis familiaris\) by investigating dogs&\#x27; spontaneous tendency to associate a spiky or a rounded shape to the &\#x27;Bouba&\#x27; and &\#x27;Kiki&\#x27; sounds\. Dogs looked significantly more at the rounded shape while &\#x27;Bouba&\#x27; was played, and they looked at and explored the spiky shape when &\#x27;Kiki&\#x27; was played\. Our results suggest that a spontaneous sound\-shape association akin to that of humans is also present in adult domestic dogs\. Further studies are needed to investigate the function and ontogenesis of this cognitive process\.
