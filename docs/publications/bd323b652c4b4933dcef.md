# High\-throughput methods for identification of ClpC N\-terminal domain bait ligands for antibacterial targeted protein degradation drug discovery

**Autores:** Helen E\. Erickson; Yahani P\. Jayasinghe; Zalak Sheth; Dylan B\. Wolfe; Julien Roche; Donald R\. Ronning

**Data:** 2026-10-14 · **Tipo:** article

## Resumo

Introduction Antimicrobial resistance, which can be attributed to overconsumption of antibiotics, as well as a shortage of new, pathogen\-specific drugs, is a continually expanding global health crisis\. In 2023, one in six bacterial infections is caused by an antibiotic\-resistant strain, according to the World Health Organization\. With the intention of channeling a fresh perspective through the antibiotic drug discovery pipeline, we harnessed the ClpC N\-terminal domain, a regulatory ATPase within the caseinolytic protease pathway, as a biomolecular target widely conserved across bacterial pathogens\. The Clp protease complex consists of the ATP\-dependent unfoldase, ClpC, and the ClpP protease\. The ClpC\-NTD recognizes proteins tagged with a phosphorylated arginine residue as substrates marked for proteolytic degradation, thereby initiating ClpC/ClpP complex formation and protease activity\. Methods Here we describe protein thermal shift screening assays, synthesis and utilization of a phospho\-arginine fluorescent probe for fluorescence polarization assay development, X\-ray crystallographic and computational docking experiments to characterize possible binding modes of ligands to ClpC homologs\. Results and discussion We developed a fluorescence polarization displacement assay using a phospho\-arginine\-based probe, with Kd values ranging from 0\.1 µM to 0\.8 µM for multiple ClpC\-NTD homologs from a variety of pathogenic bacteria\. Putative ClpC\-NTD binders were identified using a protein thermal shift assay and cross\-examined across multiple homologs using our displacement assay\. Additionally, we solved the X\-ray structures of Staphylococcus aureus and Chlamydia trachomatis ClpC\-NTDs and docked a panel of binders against three ClpC\-NTD homologs, offering structural insight into possible allosteric receptor\-ligand interactions\. Taken together, these results can be leveraged for screening and validating species\-dependent competitive or noncompetitive ligands of this prominent bacterial drug discovery target and ultimately advance the development of bifunctional molecules for targeted protein degradation\.
