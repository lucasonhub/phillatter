## [Symbol Grounding in Computational Systems: A Paradox of Intentions](../publications/6f719464ab8f7c296114.md)

Vincent C\. Müller

**Publicação:** 2009-11-01 · **Tipo:** article

The paper presents a paradoxical feature of computational systems that suggests that computationalism cannot explain symbol grounding\. If the mind is a digital computer, as computationalism claims, then it can be computing either over meaningful symbols or over meaningless symbols\. If it is computing over meaningful symbols its functioning presupposes the existence of meaningful symbols in the system, i\.e\. it implies semantic nativism\. If the mind is computing over meaningless symbols, no intentional cognitive processes are available prior to symbol grounding\. In this case, no symbol grounding could take place since any grounding presupposes intentional cognitive processes\. So, whether computing in the mind is over meaningless or over meaningful symbols, computationalism implies semantic nativism\.

**Relação com a pesquisa:** Computação e teoria computacional da mente: computationalism; Computação e formalização: computational; Representação simbólica e estrutura do pensamento: symbol grounding

## [From Maps to Metabolism: Historical Precedents and a Unified Critique of Computationalism](../publications/336f0183f737c6b7a3b4.md)

J\. Mark Bishop; Slawomir J\. Nasuto

**Publicação:** 2026-10-23 · **Tipo:** preprint

Alexander Lerchner&\#x27;s recent work on the &\#x27;abstraction fallacy&\#x27; argues that computational functionalism confuses physical processes with abstract descriptions, and that symbolic computation alone cannot instantiate consciousness\. Yet these claims were anticipated by Korzybski&\#x27;s map\-territory distinction, Searle&\#x27;s critique of strong artificial intelligence, Floridi&\#x27;s levels of abstraction, Spencer et al\.&\#x27;s observer\-relativity arguments, Winograd and Flores&\#x27;s account of computation as socially grounded practice, and Nasuto and Bishop&\#x27;s biological arguments\. Synthesizing these contributions, this paper advances a form of non\-computational physicalism: consciousness depends upon intrinsically organized, metabolically grounded physical dynamics rather than abstract computation, whilst leaving open the possibility of artificial consciousness\. An objection is that this critique targets an outdated computationalism\. Mechanistic computationalism, associated with Milkowski, Piccinini, Craver, Bechtel, and Kaplan, treats computation as a property of physically realized mechanisms rather than substrate\-independent formalism\. Although this represents an important advance, it does not dissolve the abstraction fallacy\. Computational attributions remain dependent upon choices of state individuation, system boundaries, and interpretive mapping that are not determined by physical facts alone\. Kaplan and Craver&\#x27;s &\#x27;model\-to\-mechanism mapping&\#x27; constrains computational descriptions without eliminating their dependence upon an external observer\. Grounding computation in neural mechanisms does not confer intrinsic significance or the normativity explored by Bickhard and Nasuto and Bishop\. The abstraction fallacy persists wherever computational descriptions are treated as constitutive of, rather than descriptive of, physical reality\.

**Relação com a pesquisa:** Computação e teoria computacional da mente: computationalism; Computação e formalização: computation, computational; Limites, críticas e alternativas ao computacionalismo: non\-computational; Alcance da computação e explicação dos fenômenos mentais: functionalism

## [On Computational Mechanisms for Shared Intentionality, and Speculation on Rationality and Consciousness](../publications/ed9d7dfe671354d9d4a9.md)

John Rushby

**Publicação:** 2023-06-03 · **Tipo:** preprint

A singular attribute of humankind is our ability to undertake novel, cooperative behavior, or teamwork\. This requires that we can communicate goals, plans, and ideas between the brains of individuals to create shared intentionality\. Using the information processing model of David Marr, I derive necessary characteristics of basic mechanisms to enable shared intentionality between prelinguistic computational agents and indicate how these could be implemented in present\-day AI\-based robots\. More speculatively, I suggest the mechanisms derived by this thought experiment apply to humans and extend to provide explanations for human rationality and aspects of intentional and phenomenal consciousness that accord with observation\. This yields what I call the Shared Intentionality First Theory \(SIFT\) for rationality and consciousness\. The significance of shared intentionality has been recognized and advocated previously, but typically from a sociological or behavioral point of view\. SIFT complements prior work by applying a computer science perspective to the underlying mechanisms\.

**Relação com a pesquisa:** Computação e formalização: computational; Intencionalidade e conteúdo semântico: intentionality; Alcance da computação e explicação dos fenômenos mentais: phenomenal consciousness
